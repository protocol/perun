This proposal violates fundamental principles of capital allocation and operational learning. The plan commits approximately $1 billion to fixed infrastructure before validating unit economics in a single market. This is not "thinking big"; it is negligence disguised as ambition.

The core failure is the sequence of operations. The proposal seeks to scale inputs (warehouses, trucks, cities) before validating the engine (margins, density, retention). Groceries are notoriously low-margin and operationally complex. A 30-minute delivery promise requires extreme local density to be profitable. Building capacity for density before proving demand exists creates a scenario where the company burns cash on empty warehouses and underutilized fleets.

The risks identified in the proposal description—capital intensity, thin margins, and lack of track record—are not merely hurdles; they are fatal flaws when combined with this execution strategy. If the unit economics in the first city reveal that the model loses money on every order (a likely scenario given the constraints), the company will be locked into a dozen cities with billions in sunk costs and no path to profitability. You cannot "learn" your way out of a structural deficit when your fixed costs are already maxed out.

True ambition here would be to dominate the first city, prove the model works with positive contribution margins, and then replicate that success. Speed matters, but speed without direction is just a faster crash. The venture capital raised provides an advantage, but it also creates a moral hazard to spend rather than learn. This plan chooses spending.

The correct approach is iterative validation:
1.  Master unit economics in the initial metropolitan area.
2.  Optimize the warehouse and delivery model based on real data, not projections.
3.  Expand to the next city only when the playbook is proven and repeatable.

Committing $1 billion now assumes the current unproven hypothesis is perfect. It is not. It is a guess. Betting the company on a guess is not strategy; it is gambling.

DECISION: NO-GO
