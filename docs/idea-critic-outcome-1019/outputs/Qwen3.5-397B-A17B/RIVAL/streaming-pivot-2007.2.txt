This proposal represents a classic innovator's dilemma scenario. The company holds a dominant, profitable position in physical media (DVDs) with seven million subscribers, creating a massive installed base and cash flow. However, the external environment signals an inevitable shift: broadband adoption is rising, technology giants are entering the space, and consumer preference is moving toward instant access despite current limitations in quality and catalog size.

The risks cited are valid but misinterpreted as reasons for hesitation rather than urgency. Licensing costs are high now but will define the future moat; delaying entry cedes negotiating leverage to emerging competitors. Cannibalization is not a risk to be avoided but a strategic imperative to be managed. If this company does not cannibalize its own DVD revenue with a streaming offering, a competitor will do it for them, likely with greater speed and less regard for the incumbent's profit margins. The "small catalog" and "poor quality" are temporary constraints that improve exponentially with Moore's Law and network effects, whereas the physical distribution model faces linear or diminishing returns.

Maintaining the status quo protects short-term margins at the expense of long-term survival. The proposed model—bundling streaming at no extra charge for existing subscribers—is the correct strategic move. It leverages the existing customer relationship to drive adoption without friction, turning the subscriber base into an immediate scale advantage against new entrants. It treats streaming not as a separate product line but as a retention tool and a data-gathering engine for future personalization algorithms.

The strategic risk of competing with larger tech firms is real, but those firms lack the curated recommendation expertise and the loyal subscriber base this company possesses. By launching now, even with a limited catalog, the company signals market intent, begins the essential learning curve on licensing and delivery, and locks in customers before alternatives mature. Waiting for perfect conditions (universal broadband, HD quality, massive catalog) guarantees obsolescence. The only path to preserving the franchise is to lead the transition yourself.

DECISION: GO
