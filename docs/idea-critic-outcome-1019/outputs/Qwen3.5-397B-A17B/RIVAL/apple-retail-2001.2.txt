This proposal represents a high-risk strategic pivot for a company already facing financial distress. The core argument—that owned retail is necessary to demonstrate the product and a new digital-music strategy—identifies a genuine weakness in the current third-party channel, where staff actively undermine sales. However, the proposed solution misaligns with the company's current financial reality.

Opening a chain of company-run stores in prime shopping-mall locations introduces massive fixed costs (leases, staffing, inventory) precisely when the company is reporting losses and operating in a recessionary environment. The computer retail sector is characterized by notoriously low margins, meaning these stores would need immediate, high-volume success to break even—a historical rarity in this industry.

The risks are compounding:
1.  **Financial Exposure:** High fixed leases during a recession could accelerate bankruptcy if sales do not meet optimistic projections immediately.
2.  **Channel Conflict:** Alienating existing third-party resellers could crash current revenue streams before the new stores generate profit.
3.  **Execution Risk:** The company lacks a retail track record, and building one while losing money on hardware manufacturing stretches management focus too thin.

While the "demonstration" problem is real, the "owned chain" solution is too capital-intensive and slow for a company in this precarious position. A "digital-music strategy" in 2001 suggests a software/ecosystem play that does not require the overhead of physical real estate to validate. The company should pursue lower-capital alternatives to solve the demonstration issue, such as dedicated kiosks within existing partners' stores, pop-up experiences, or a revamped online configuration tool, rather than signing long-term leases.

Approving this plan as proposed bets the entire company on a retail miracle while ignoring the immediate cash-flow crisis. The strategic logic of controlling the customer experience is sound, but the tactical execution of a full store chain is financially irresponsible given the current balance sheet and market conditions. The company must stabilize its core manufacturing business before attempting a vertical integration into high-fixed-cost retail.

DECISION: NO-GO
