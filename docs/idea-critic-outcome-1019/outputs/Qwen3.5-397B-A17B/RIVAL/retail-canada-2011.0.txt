This 2011 proposal represents a classic "boil the ocean" expansion strategy that ignores fundamental retail physics. The plan attempts to solve three distinct, high-difficulty problems simultaneously: market entry, supply chain construction, and technology deployment.

**The Pace Trap:** Opening 130–150 stores in two years means launching roughly 1.5 stores per week. This velocity leaves zero margin for error in site selection, staffing, or local merchandising calibration. In a new country, every store opening is a learning event; this schedule treats them as commodity deployments. If the first 20 stores reveal a fatal flaw in the value proposition or supply chain, the company is already contractually bound to roll out the remaining 110+ locations, compounding losses before the model is proven.

**The Systems Risk:** Launching a *new* inventory management system concurrently with a *new* cross-border supply chain is an operational gamble. Supply chains require stability to tune lead times and fill rates. Introducing unproven software into this fragile environment creates a blackout zone where visibility into stock levels will be unreliable. In discount retail, out-of-stocks on key value items destroy trust immediately. You cannot learn cross-border logistics while simultaneously debugging core inventory software.

**Competitive Reality:** Entering a market dominated by an entrenched competitor with higher local prices suggests a potential value arbitrage, but only if the supply chain works. The incumbent can temporarily discount specific baskets to blunt the newcomer's entry. If the new supply chain hiccups during this critical window, the retailer faces empty shelves against a fully stocked rival, cementing a reputation for unreliability before gaining traction.

**Verdict:** The strategic logic of expansion may be sound, but the execution plan is structurally unsound. The risk is not just failure, but catastrophic capital destruction at speed. The company must prove the cross-border supply chain and inventory system in a limited pilot (5–10 stores) before committing to the full lease portfolio.

DECISION: NO-GO
