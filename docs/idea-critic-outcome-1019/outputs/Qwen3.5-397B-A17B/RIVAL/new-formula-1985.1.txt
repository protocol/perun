The proposal contains a fatal flaw in its validation methodology that outweighs the positive taste test results. While the data confirms the new formula wins on sensory metrics in blind conditions, the testing protocol failed to simulate the actual market reality: the permanent removal of the original product.

Blind taste tests isolate flavor from brand equity, measuring only immediate sensory pleasure. They cannot measure grief, betrayal, or the loss of ritual. The proposal explicitly notes that participants were unaware the original would be withdrawn. This omits the single most significant variable: consumer reaction to loss. Behavioral economics demonstrates that loss aversion is psychologically roughly twice as powerful as the gain of an equivalent improvement. A "better" taste does not compensate for the removal of a cultural anchor.

The risks identified—emotional attachment, brand heritage, and lack of a fallback—are not minor side effects; they are existential threats. The brand's value proposition has shifted from purely functional (taste) to emotional (identity, nostalgia, continuity). By withdrawing the original entirely, the company converts loyal customers into refugees with nowhere to go within the portfolio. There is no "good enough" alternative offered to those who prioritize familiarity over sweetness.

Furthermore, the bottler network faces massive disruption without a transitional phase or a dual-product strategy. The absence of a fallback option removes all flexibility to respond to negative market signals post-launch. If the backlash occurs, the company has already burned its bridges, having declared the original formula obsolete.

A "better" product that ignites a consumer revolt is a commercial failure. The testing validated the liquid, not the strategy. The strategy assumes rational actors making taste-based swaps, ignoring the irrational, identity-based attachment to the legacy brand. Launching this plan as proposed bets the entire franchise on a hypothesis that was explicitly excluded from the testing parameters.

The prudent path is a dual-launch strategy or a phased transition that retains the original as a heritage line, allowing the market to self-segment. Replacing the flagship entirely based on incomplete data is reckless.

DECISION: NO-GO
