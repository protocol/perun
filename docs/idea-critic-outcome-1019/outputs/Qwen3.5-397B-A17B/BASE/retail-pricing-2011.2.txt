The proposal presents a radical strategic pivot for a struggling mid-market department store. The core objective is to shift from a discount-heavy, coupon-driven model to everyday low pricing and branded in-store shops. This strategy is championed by a new CEO whose past success was built in the branded electronics sector, a fundamentally different retail environment with distinct customer behaviors and product lifecycles.

The most critical flaw in this proposal is the complete absence of a pilot program. The plan dictates an immediate, chain-wide implementation without testing the hypothesis in a controlled environment. The existing customer base is explicitly described as "older" and "trained to wait for coupons," with historical sales heavily reliant on discounted items. Abruptly removing the primary mechanism these customers use to make purchasing decisions creates an extreme risk of immediate revenue collapse.

Furthermore, the CEO's previous success in electronics does not guarantee applicability to soft goods and department store retail. The pace of change proposed—altering pricing architecture and store layout across the entire network simultaneously—leaves no room for error or iterative learning. If the new pricing model fails to resonate, the company will have no operational buffer to revert or adjust before significant financial damage occurs.

The risks identified within the proposal itself are severe: alienating the core customer base, executing without a safety net via a pilot, and the sheer velocity of the rollout. Proceeding without a test phase ignores basic principles of risk management when dealing with a vulnerable business and a entrenched customer behavior pattern. The potential for catastrophic failure outweighs the theoretical benefits of the new model. Approval in the current form is irresponsible given the lack of validation data and the high probability of disrupting the remaining revenue stream.

DECISION: NO-GO
