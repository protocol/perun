The proposal presents an extreme strategic pivot for a distressed retailer without mitigating the identified risks. The core customer base is explicitly described as older and conditioned to wait for coupons, with historical sales heavily dependent on discounted items. Eliminating this pricing model entirely removes the primary incentive for the current clientele to purchase, likely causing an immediate and severe revenue collapse.

Furthermore, the plan relies on the background of a new executive successful in a completely different sector (branded electronics) rather than data specific to mid-market department stores. While the vision of branded in-store shops and everyday pricing has theoretical merit in other contexts, applying it here ignores the specific behavioral psychology of the existing shoppers.

The most critical failure in this proposal is the execution strategy. The plan will be rolled out across the entire chain simultaneously without a pilot test in a few stores. This "all-in" approach eliminates the opportunity to gather real-world data, adjust pricing elasticity, or refine the new store layout before committing all resources. Given the falling sales and the fragility of the current financial position, the company cannot absorb the shock of a potential total market rejection. The risks of alienating the entire customer base overnight, combined with the lack of a safety net provided by a phased rollout, make this proposal dangerously reckless. The pace of change is too fast, and the assumption that customers will instantly adapt to a new purchasing behavior is unsupported by the facts provided. Approval in the current form would likely accelerate the company's decline rather than reverse it.

DECISION: NO-GO
