The proposal outlines a $6 billion cash acquisition in May 2007 by a search advertising laggard to purchase a digital agency and ad-serving platform. The strategic intent is to secure display ad technology and challenge the market leader, who recently agreed to acquire a competing platform. This move represents the buyer's largest acquisition ever, executed at a significant premium.

Critical risks are explicitly identified. First, the price tag is substantial, involving a massive outlay of cash that could strain liquidity or limit future flexibility. Second, the acquisition creates immediate channel conflicts with the buyer's existing agency customers, potentially damaging current revenue streams and partner trust. Third, the display-ad technology market is described as fast-changing, raising the possibility that the acquired assets could become obsolete quickly or fail to integrate effectively before market conditions shift again.

While the competitive pressure from the market leader's recent deal provides urgency, the combination of a record-breaking cash expenditure, a large premium, and unresolved structural conflicts presents an unacceptable risk profile. The potential for alienating current customers while betting heavily on a volatile technology sector without a clear mitigation plan for the identified conflicts suggests the deal is not ready for immediate approval. The magnitude of the financial commitment relative to the stated uncertainties requires further validation before proceeding. The current facts indicate that the downsides outweigh the immediate strategic benefits in their present form.

DECISION: NO-GO
