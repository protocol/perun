The proposal presents a classic innovator's dilemma scenario where a market leader considers launching an inferior product that threatens its core revenue stream. In 2007, the identified constraints are severe: limited content, poor video quality, low broadband penetration, and high licensing costs. Furthermore, the threat of well-capitalized technology and retail giants entering the space creates a formidable competitive barrier. The risk of cannibalizing a highly profitable DVD business with seven million subscribers is immediate and tangible, while the benefits of the streaming option remain speculative and constrained by current infrastructure.

However, ignoring the shift toward digital distribution carries a greater existential risk. The entry of large competitors signals an inevitable industry transformation. If the company waits for broadband to become universal or for video quality to perfect itself, it cedes the strategic initiative to rivals who will define the new standard. By offering streaming at no extra charge to existing subscribers, the company leverages its massive installed base to gather critical data on user behavior without demanding immediate additional revenue. This approach turns the small, older catalogue into a testing ground for technology and licensing models rather than a primary profit center.

The high licensing costs and technology expenses are barriers to entry that also protect early movers who establish relationships with studios now. While cannibalization is a valid concern, it is preferable for the company to disrupt its own business model than to be disrupted by an external force that offers no path to recovery. The proposal does not require abandoning the DVD business but rather initiating a parallel track to ensure long-term relevance. Delaying this move to wait for perfect conditions or to revise the plan based on fears of competition allows competitors to capture the market narrative and customer mindshare. The strategic necessity of establishing a foothold in streaming outweighs the short-term financial risks and operational limitations described.

DECISION: GO
