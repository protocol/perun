The proposal seeks over $1 billion to launch a mobile-only subscription service featuring high-budget, short-form professional content with A-list talent. While the technology offering seamless vertical and horizontal viewing is innovative, the fundamental business case contains critical flaws based on the provided facts.

The market landscape in 2018 is already saturated with abundant free, ad-supported short-form video, including user-generated content. Introducing a paid subscription model in this environment directly contradicts established consumer behavior where similar content is available at no cost. Furthermore, the proposal explicitly acknowledges "subscription fatigue" as a rising concern, yet the solution is to add another paid tier rather than addressing this saturation.

The risks identified are severe and currently unmitigated. There is no proven demand for premium short-form content at a price point high enough to recoup production costs involving A-list talent and high budgets. The combination of unproven demand, high pricing pressure, and intense competition for user attention creates a precarious financial outlook. Investing over $1 billion without evidence that consumers will pay for short-form video, especially when free alternatives dominate, represents an unacceptable level of risk. The presence of major studio investors does not validate the consumer value proposition if the end-user refuses to pay.

The core assumption that high production values will drive subscriptions for content under ten minutes is untested and contradicts the prevailing trend of free consumption. Without a clear path to overcoming subscription fatigue or demonstrating willingness to pay, proceeding immediately is imprudent. The lack of proven demand makes the massive capital requirement unjustifiable at this stage.

DECISION: NO-GO
