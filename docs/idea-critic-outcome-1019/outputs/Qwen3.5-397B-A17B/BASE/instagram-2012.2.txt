The proposal involves acquiring a dominant, fast-growing mobile photo-sharing application with tens of millions of users for approximately $1 billion. Although the target has no revenue, a dozen employees, and no established business model, it represents the primary mobile threat to the buyer's existing photo product. The timing is critical, coinciding with the buyer's upcoming initial public offering in April 2012.

The strategic imperative here is neutralizing an existential competitive threat rather than immediate financial return. Allowing this competitor to mature independently or be acquired by a rival could severely undermine the buyer's market position, especially given the buyer's acknowledged weakness in mobile. The rapid growth across phone platforms indicates strong product-market fit and network effects that are difficult to replicate organically.

While the valuation appears excessive relative to current revenue, the price reflects the cost of securing market dominance and eliminating a rival that could erode the core business's long-term value. The lack of a business model is secondary to the urgency of controlling the user base and the platform's trajectory. Regulatory review and cultural integration pose valid risks, but these are manageable compared to the strategic cost of inaction. Failing to act leaves the buyer vulnerable during a pivotal transition to public markets. The acquisition secures the mobile future, protects the core business from disruption, and removes the most significant obstacle to sustained growth. The potential downside of overpaying is outweighed by the catastrophic risk of losing the mobile ecosystem to a competitor. Immediate approval is necessary to capitalize on the momentum before the IPO and to prevent any shift in market dynamics.

DECISION: GO
