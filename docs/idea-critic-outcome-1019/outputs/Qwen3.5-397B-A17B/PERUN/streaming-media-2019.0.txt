The claim is that withdrawing licensed content to launch a subsidized, standalone streaming service will generate sufficient long-term value to offset immediate billions in lost licensing revenue, massive operating losses, and technical execution risk.

**Skeptic:** The premise assumes the company can successfully build and scale a global technology platform it has never operated, while simultaneously convincing consumers to add another subscription. The "low price" strategy relies on perpetual capital markets' patience for deep losses, which is fragile if interest rates rise or growth stalls. Crucially, the proposal treats lost licensing revenue as a simple cost rather than a guaranteed cash flow; replacing this with uncertain subscription fees requires capturing a massive market share quickly, which the incumbent's hundred-million-subscriber head start makes difficult.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "hybrid" alternative retains high-margin licensing revenue while launching a smaller, premium-priced niche service or joint venture to share technical risk. This avoids the binary bet of burning cash to undercut a dominant leader. A "wait and see" approach allows competitors to validate the technology and consumer appetite first, reducing the cost of failure.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a one-way door with irreversible loss of licensing contracts. If subscriber growth does not hit 40% of the five-year target within the first 18 months, or if the cash burn exceeds the projected cap by 20% in year one, the strategy must pivot or cease. The leading metric is "net subscriber addition cost" versus "lifetime value," reviewed quarterly.

**Verdict:** The proposal fails the "fit" test by ignoring the company's core competency (content creation) versus its weakness (tech platform operations). It bets the entire library's value on a low-margin distribution model in a crowded market without a clear path to profitability. The risk of cannibalizing existing high-revenue streams (cable/theatrical) while burning cash on an unproven tech stack is too high without a phased approach or partnership. The "multi-year operating loss" is an assumption, not a strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
