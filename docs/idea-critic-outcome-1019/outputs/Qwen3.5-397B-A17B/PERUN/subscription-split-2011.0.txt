The proposal to split the DVD and streaming services into separate brands, accounts, and billing systems immediately following a 60% price increase is critically flawed.

**Steelman:** Separating the declining DVD asset allows the streaming unit to pivot faster without legacy drag, theoretically maximizing long-term shareholder value by distinct management focus.

**Attack:** The timing creates a compound shock. Customers have just absorbed a massive price hike; presenting them now with fragmented access (two logins, two bills, lost queues) feels not like strategic optimization, but like a "death by a thousand cuts" designed to force DVD churn. The loss of shared ratings destroys the perceived value of the combined ecosystem just as price sensitivity peaks. The rationale that DVD needs "different management" does not require a customer-facing brand schism this abruptly. A backend operational split could achieve management separation without alienating the user base during a pricing crisis. The risk of mass cancellation due to perceived hostility and friction outweighs the operational benefits of immediate brand separation. The proposal ignores the psychological impact of changing the rules of engagement right after raising the cost of entry.

**Better Way:** Maintain a unified brand and single sign-on temporarily. Implement internal management separation and distinct P&Ls without forcing customers into a fragmented experience. Communicate the strategic divergence gradually rather than via an abrupt structural split that mimics a service degradation.

**Kill Criteria:** If customer churn exceeds 5% in the first month post-split or if net promoter score drops below -20, the split must be reversed or paused. The leading metric to watch is the cancellation rate of dual-subscription holders in the first 30 days.

The current plan prioritizes internal organizational clarity over customer retention during a period of extreme price sensitivity. It solves a management problem by creating a massive customer experience problem. The friction of two bills and lost data, combined with the recent price hike, creates a high probability of a coordinated customer backlash that could damage the streaming brand's growth trajectory irreparably.

DECISION: NO-GO
