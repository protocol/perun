The proposal to split the DVD and streaming services into separate brands, accounts, and billing systems in September 2011 is fundamentally flawed due to timing and customer perception.

**Steelman:** Separating the declining DVD asset allows the streaming business to pivot faster without legacy drag, theoretically maximizing long-term shareholder value by distinct management focus.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the immediate context: a 60% price increase occurred just two months prior. Executing a brand split immediately after a massive price hike signals to customers that the company is not just raising prices, but actively dismantling the unified value proposition they just paid more for. This compounds "price shock" with "convenience shock" (two logins, two bills, lost recommendations).

**Better Way:** Maintain a unified brand and account interface while internally segregating management teams. This achieves the operational goal of distinct strategies without imposing friction on the customer or appearing to punish them for the recent price adjustment.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the split launches. Customers, already angry about the 60% hike, now face fragmented service and lost data continuity. The narrative shifts from "price increase" to "company breaking apart." Churn spikes dramatically as subscribers cancel both services rather than manage two relationships. The stock collapses not because of the strategy's long-term logic, but because the execution alienates the entire existing base simultaneously.

**Fit:** This move contradicts the north star of customer retention during a transitional pricing model. It treats the DVD decline as a reason to eject customers rather than a segment to migrate gently.

**Kill Criteria:** If customer confusion exceeds 5% of support tickets or churn increases by more than 2% month-over-month post-launch, the strategy fails. However, given the current sentiment, these thresholds will likely be breached immediately.

The cost of delay is low; the cost of immediate execution is catastrophic brand erosion. The company must stabilize the pricing perception before introducing structural friction. Do not proceed with the split while the price hike wound is fresh.

DECISION: NO-GO
