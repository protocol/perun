**Steelman:** A curated, single-store model with a 30% fee balances developer monetization and user trust, turning the CEO's previous web-app stance into a controlled native ecosystem that prevents fragmentation while ensuring quality.

**Attack:**
1.  **Contradiction:** The CEO publicly championed web apps; pivoting to native apps risks perceived inconsistency and alienates early adopters who bought the device for its web-centric philosophy.
2.  **Bottleneck:** Centralized review creates a scalability cliff. As submissions surge, approval delays will frustrate developers, driving them back to rival open platforms despite the security benefits.
3.  **Carrier Friction:** Carriers fear losing revenue from bypassed services (SMS, voice). Without a clear revenue-sharing model with carriers, they may block the device or restrict network access.
4.  **Polish Risk:** Even with review, bad native apps can crash the OS more severely than sandboxed web apps, threatening the brand's reputation for stability.

**Better Ways:**
*   **Hybrid Launch:** Start with a limited "invited-only" developer program to stress-test the review pipeline before a full public opening.
*   **Web-First Bridge:** Maintain strong web-app support as a secondary tier to appease the CEO's original vision and reduce immediate native load.
*   **Carrier Partnership:** Offer carriers a small percentage of app revenue involving their services to neutralize objections.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   If the review team cannot clear 90% of submissions within 7 days by Q3, the process is unsustainable.
*   If carrier pushback results in blocked distribution in two major markets, the model fails.
*   If crash rates attributed to third-party apps exceed 1% of total sessions, the approval process is insufficient.

**Verdict:**
The proposal is strategically sound but operationally fragile. The shift from web-only to native is necessary to compete, but the "single company-run store" creates a massive operational burden that is currently unproven. The concerns regarding carrier relations and review scalability are not theoretical; they are immediate blockers. The proposal lacks a phased rollout plan to mitigate the review bottleneck and no strategy to align carriers. Approving this *as proposed* invites chaos. It must be revised to include a phased launch and carrier revenue sharing.

DECISION: NO-GO
