If this fails, it will be due to cultural integration destroying the very creativity that drove growth, or regulatory blocks delaying the close until momentum stalls. This is a one-way door; once the cash and stock are transferred, the capital is gone. The cost of delay is likely total loss of relevance in mobile photos.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:**
The premise rests on the target being the singular mobile threat. Assuming the user growth metrics are accurate and the competitor's internal product cannot catch up organically, the strategic necessity outweighs the financial absurdity. The kill criterion for success is not immediate ROI, but the retention of the target's user growth rate and engineering talent for 24 months post-acquisition. If user growth flattens or key founders depart within year one, the thesis is broken.

**Verdict:**
While the price is staggering and the lack of revenue dangerous, the strategic cost of *not* acting is existential. The acquisition buys time and removes the biggest obstacle to mobile transition. The risk of overpayment is preferable to the risk of irrelevance.
