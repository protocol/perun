The claim is that a dominant DVD rental leader should immediately launch a free, limited streaming tier in 2007 despite high costs, poor quality, and inevitable cannibalization of its core profit engine.

The steelman: This is a defensive "two-way door" experiment to retain subscribers and gather data before giants lock up the market, treating the small catalog as a minimum viable product rather than a revenue source.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the specific proposal parameters. First, offering this at "no extra charge" against a backdrop of "high prices" for licensing creates an immediate, uncapped cash burn with no incremental revenue to offset it. Second, launching with "poor" quality and "older" titles while competing against incoming tech giants risks brand degradation; it signals weakness rather than innovation. Third, the proposal admits "broadband is not universal," meaning the addressable market for this specific offering is too small to justify the strategic distraction and licensing outlay at this precise moment.

The "do nothing" alternative is stronger: wait 12–18 months for broadband penetration to rise and licensing models to stabilize, then enter with a paid, distinct tier. The "cheaper path" is a separate, paid beta for a tiny user segment to test technology without cannibalizing the main revenue stream or inflating licensing costs prematurely.

The pre-mortem is clear: The company burns millions on licenses for a product users reject due to quality, accelerates DVD cancellations without replacing the margin, and enters the eventual streaming war with depleted cash reserves and a damaged brand perception of "low quality."

The cost of delay is low because the market infrastructure (broadband) is not yet ready for mass adoption. The proposal fails the "fit" test: it moves the north star (entertainment delivery) but threatens the vehicle (profitability) required to get there. A "go-if" structure is missing; this is an unconditional spend on a losing unit economics model.

DECISION: NO-GO
