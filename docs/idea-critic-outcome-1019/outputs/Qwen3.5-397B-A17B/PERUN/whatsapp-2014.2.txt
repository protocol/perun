**Steelman:** Paying $19B is the only way to instantly neutralize a existential mobile threat and capture hundreds of millions of users who have already rejected the buyer's core product.

**Attack:** The price implies a 12,600% increase in value in one year ($1.5B to $19B) for a company with 50 employees, minimal revenue, and an explicit anti-monetization stance ($1 fee, no ads). The unit economics are nonsensical: acquiring users at ~$40–$80 each without a path to recoup costs via ads or subscriptions violates standard venture logic. The "displacement fear" is real, but buying the displacer at a peak valuation without a integration plan for monetization or founder retention is a bet on hope, not strategy. If the founders leave or regulators block data merging, the asset becomes a costly, inactive shell. The "do nothing" alternative allows organic mobile adaptation; this purchase mortgages the company's future on a single, unproven hypothesis that these specific users are worth more than the entire historical value of the buyer's previous acquisitions combined.

**Kill Criteria:** This fails the "reversibility" test; $19B in cash and stock is a one-way door that cannot be undone if the cultural clash destroys the product. It also fails the "evidence" test: there is no verified monetization plan to justify the multiple. The cost of delay is lower than the cost of a failed integration that drains capital and management focus.

**Verdict:** The proposal lacks a mechanism to convert users to revenue, relies entirely on retaining founders who have shown independence, and pays a historic premium for an asset with no earnings. The risk of total capital loss due to regulatory blockage or user exodus post-acquisition outweighs the strategic benefit of buying growth.

DECISION: NO-GO
