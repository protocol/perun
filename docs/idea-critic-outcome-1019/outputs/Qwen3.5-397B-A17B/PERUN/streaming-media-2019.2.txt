The claim is that sacrificing billions in guaranteed licensing revenue to fund a speculative, low-priced streaming service will yield superior long-term value despite massive operating losses and execution risks in an unfamiliar technology sector.

**Skeptic:** The proposal assumes the market will switch to a inferior, smaller library solely based on price, ignoring that the leader's hundred-million-subscriber moat offers network effects and content depth a new entrant cannot immediately match. It treats "tens of millions" of subscribers as a linear function of price, neglecting churn and the immense cost of customer acquisition. The assumption that the company can successfully build a global tech platform from scratch, having never done so, is a critical single point of failure.

**Better-way:** The "do nothing" or "hybrid" option retains lucrative licensing deals while launching a smaller, premium-tier service to test technology and demand without burning cash on a loss-leading price war. Alternatively, partnering with an existing tech giant avoids the execution risk of building a proprietary platform.

**Kill-criteria:** If the company cannot secure a technology partner or hire a proven leadership team for the platform within six months, the execution risk is too high. If projected cash burn exceeds the company's liquid reserves divided by three years before reaching 20 million subscribers, the financial model collapses.

**Pre-mortem:** The venture fails because the licensing revenue hole is deeper than anticipated, the tech platform launches with critical outages, and the low price attracts low-value users who churn immediately when prices rise. The company is left with neither licensing income nor a viable streaming business, crippling its ability to fund theatrical releases.

**Decision:** The proposal relies on unproven technological execution and a pricing strategy that devalues the asset while destroying immediate, high-margin revenue. The "multi-year operating loss" is not a funded experiment but a bet-the-company gamble on entering a saturated market with a disadvantageous cost structure. The risks of cannibalization and execution failure outweigh the speculative upside of a price war against an entrenched monopolist.

DECISION: NO-GO
