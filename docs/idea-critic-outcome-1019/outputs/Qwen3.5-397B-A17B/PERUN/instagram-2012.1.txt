**Steelman:** Acquiring the category-defining mobile photo leader neutralizes an existential threat to the buyer's core business and instantly secures mobile dominance for $1B, a premium justified by strategic survival rather than current revenue.

**Attack:** The valuation is astronomically disconnected from financial fundamentals (no revenue, 13 employees). Paying $1B for a feature set creates a dangerous precedent, risks massive cultural integration failure, and may attract antitrust scrutiny given the buyer's market position. If the mobile trend proves fleeting or the app's engagement is a bubble, this becomes the most infamous overpayment in tech history. The buyer's own weak mobile product suggests a systemic inability to execute in this space, raising doubts about successful post-acquisition integration.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Save $1B but cede the mobile photo market to a competitor who might buy the target instead.
2.  **Build:** Attempt to replicate the app internally; high risk of failure given the buyer's existing weak mobile track record and the target's rapid head-start.
3.  **Partner:** Insufficient to neutralize the threat of the target being acquired by a rival.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of maintaining social networking dominance during the platform shift to mobile. However, it assumes the target's growth is sustainable and not a fad.

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition fails because the buyer's corporate culture suffocates the startup's creativity, causing key talent to flee and users to migrate to the next new platform. The $1B price tag becomes a millstone, distracting leadership while revenue never materializes.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a one-way door with irreversible capital outflow. The leading metric must be user retention and cross-platform growth post-acquisition. If key founders leave within 6 months or user growth stalls immediately, the strategic thesis is broken.

**Verdict:** While the financials are indefensible in isolation, the strategic cost of *not* acting (allowing a rival to own the mobile photo layer) is higher. The purchase is a defensive monopoly play, not an investment in current cash flow. The risk of cultural destruction is real but manageable compared to the risk of irrelevance.

DECISION: GO
