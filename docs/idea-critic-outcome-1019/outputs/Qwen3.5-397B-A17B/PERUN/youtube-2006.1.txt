**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant video platform eliminates the buyer's #2 status, captures the defining cultural shift of web media, and neutralizes a competitor that could otherwise partner with rivals. The $1.65B price is a premium for speed-to-scale that organic growth cannot match.

**Attack:** The valuation is detached from financial reality: zero revenue, massive operating losses, and existential legal threats make this a speculative bet on an unproven ad model. Copyright litigation could force the site's shutdown or bleed the buyer dry. Bandwidth costs may scale faster than monetization. Buying now locks in maximum risk before the legal landscape clarifies.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Wait for lawsuits to cripple the target, then acquire assets cheaply or let rivals fail first.
2.  **Organic Growth:** Improve the buyer's existing, smaller video service; slower but avoids inheriting immediate legal liability.
3.  **Partnership:** License content or technology without full acquisition; limits exposure.

**Fit:** Aligns with organizing the world's information, but the method (buying a lawsuit magnet) contradicts prudent capital allocation.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-acquisition, courts rule against fair use for user uploads. The platform is legally forced to remove 80% of its content, destroying user engagement. The stock price collapses as the $1.65B investment yields negative cash flow and endless legal fees.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "no meaningful revenue," "high bandwidth bills," and "threatened lawsuits." These are facts, not assumptions. The "largest destination" metric is vanity without a path to profit.

**Kill Criteria:** If a single major media company files a suit seeking statutory damages exceeding $500M within 6 months, or if ad CPMs remain below $5 after 12 months, the thesis fails.

**Verdict:** The risks are unquantified and potentially infinite. The price assumes success in a legal environment that currently suggests failure. This is not a calculated bet; it is a gamble with shareholder capital on an unresolved legal theory. The "do nothing" or "wait for clarity" options preserve capital while the market sorts out the copyright viability.

DECISION: NO-GO
