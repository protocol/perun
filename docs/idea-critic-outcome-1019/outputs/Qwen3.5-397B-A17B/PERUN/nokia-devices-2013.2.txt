**Steelman:** Acquiring the handset division secures the primary hardware reference for the OS, preventing defection to the rival platform and enabling tight integration to improve user experience, which is a prerequisite for attracting developers.

**Attack:** The core failure mode is not hardware supply but ecosystem gravity. The market leader dominates via app availability; owning a declining hardware asset (falling sales) does not automatically solve the "chicken-and-egg" developer problem. Integration risks are severe: absorbing a large, struggling workforce into a software-centric culture often destroys value rather than creating it. Furthermore, this move may alienate other handset manufacturers currently using the OS, pushing them toward the rival platform to avoid competing with the OS owner, thereby shrinking the total install base further. The €5.4 billion cost is a massive bet on a "two-sided market" dynamic where hardware ownership alone has historically failed to reverse network effects.

**Better Way:** Instead of full acquisition, a strategic partnership with strict exclusivity clauses or subsidized development funds could secure the manufacturer's loyalty at a fraction of the cost and risk. Alternatively, focusing resources on a cross-platform compatibility layer or a web-app strategy might bypass the native app deficit without the burden of hardware manufacturing.

**Kill Criteria:** If the combined entity cannot increase smartphone unit sales by 20% within 18 months or attract top-tier apps exclusively within 12 months, the thesis fails. The "one-way door" nature of this acquisition means failure results in permanent capital loss and cultural distraction.

**Verdict:** The proposal conflates hardware control with ecosystem vitality. Given the falling sales, the risk of alienating other partners, and the sheer difficulty of reversing network effects through vertical integration alone, the probability of destroying more value than created is high. The cost is too high for a hypothesis that hardware ownership fixes an app shortage.

DECISION: NO-GO
