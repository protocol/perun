The company’s 3‑5 % market share places it well short of a dominant position, yet it still commands a recognizable brand among enthusiasts. Recent financial statements, however, show a loss streak, indicating that cash flow is already constrained. Opening a chain of company‑run stores would add substantial fixed costs: prime‑mall leases, staffing, utilities, and inventory financing. In a retail sector where gross margins are historically thin—often single‑digit percentages—the added overhead would further depress profitability and increase break‑even volume dramatically.

The strategic rationale—showcasing the product and a new digital‑music ecosystem—relies on a consumer‑experience benefit that can be delivered through alternative, lower‑cost channels (pop‑up demos, partner‑led events, or dedicated brand corners within existing retailers). Existing third‑party electronics stores already provide exposure, albeit with price competition; leveraging those relationships with enhanced training or exclusive bundles could achieve the same brand‑visibility goals without the financial burden of full‑scale stores.

Risk factors are acute:

1. **Fixed lease commitments** during an economic downturn could become a cash‑drain, forcing the business to sustain under‑performing locations while the broader market contracts.
2. **Inventory risk**: stocking high‑value PCs and accessories ties up capital; unsold units would have to be discounted, eroding margins further.
3. **Channel conflict**: existing resellers may reduce orders or demand higher margins to compensate for perceived competition, jeopardizing a critical distribution network.
4. **Industry precedent**: many PC manufacturers have failed at proprietary retail ventures, citing poor return on investment and operational distraction.

Given the current loss trajectory, the low‑margin nature of PC retail, and the pronounced risks, the proposed rollout does not appear financially justifiable. A more prudent approach would be to reinforce the brand presence within existing retail partners, perhaps via dedicated brand‑in‑store experiences or limited‑time flagship pop‑ups, while preserving capital for product development and the digital‑music strategy.
