**Executive Summary**  
The retailer’s first overseas foray targets a neighboring market with 130‑150 leased sites, aiming to open most within two years using a brand‑new supply‑chain and inventory‑management platform. The market is led by a larger, entrenched competitor, and local pricing on several categories exceeds the retailer’s current levels. Success hinges on mastering cross‑border logistics, regulatory compliance, and bilingual labeling while deploying untested operational systems at scale.

**Key Risks**  
1. **Speed of rollout** – Opening >100 stores in 24 months demands simultaneous construction, staffing, and systems integration. Any delay multiplies cost and erodes momentum.  
2. **Unproven supply chain** – The new logistics network and inventory platform have no field data. Failure would cause stockouts or excess inventory, damaging brand perception.  
3. **Price perception** – Higher local prices on some goods reduce the retailer’s price‑advantage, risking a “premium” image that may not resonate with price‑sensitive shoppers.  
4. **Regulatory & labeling** – Bilingual packaging and cross‑border compliance add complexity and potential legal exposure.

**Mitigation Recommendations**  
- **Pilot phase**: Begin with 10‑15 stores in a single region, validate the supply chain and inventory system, and refine pricing strategy before scaling.  
- **Partnerships**: Leverage local distributors or third‑party logistics providers to reduce learning curve and regulatory risk.  
- **Staggered openings**: Phase store launches quarterly, allowing corrective actions without jeopardizing the entire rollout.  
- **Pricing analysis**: Conduct a granular price‑elasticity study to ensure competitive positioning, especially on categories where local prices are higher.

**Strategic Fit**  
International expansion aligns with long‑term growth goals, but the current plan’s aggressive timeline and reliance on untested infrastructure elevate execution risk beyond acceptable levels for a first‑time market entry.

**Conclusion**  
Approve a revised, phased approach that validates core capabilities before committing to the full 130‑150‑store program.

DECISION: NO-GO
