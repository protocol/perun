The proposal targets a market that is already fragmented and price‑sensitive. In early 2001 the company’s PC line holds only a small share, so the “only‑works‑with‑our‑software” restriction limits the addressable base to a few‑percent of potential buyers. Competing players—most of them flash‑based or using smaller hard‑drives—already exist at lower price points; their weak sales indicate limited demand for premium hardware when the ecosystem is not locked in.

A 5 GB hard‑drive player at $400 would be substantially above the price of rivals that offer comparable storage for less. Without a broadly licensed music‑download service, the product cannot deliver a compelling value proposition beyond “play ripped MP3s,” a use case already served by cheaper alternatives.

The company also lacks consumer‑electronics experience, which raises the likelihood of design, manufacturing, and quality‑control problems. Sourcing the required components (hard‑drive, LCD, battery, codec) within a sub‑year schedule is a material risk; any delay would erode margins further and jeopardize launch.

Given the combination of an insufficient addressable market, an unattractive price relative to competitors, and high execution risk on both supply‑chain and product‑development fronts, the proposal does not meet the criteria for a viable launch at this time.

DECISION: NO-GO
