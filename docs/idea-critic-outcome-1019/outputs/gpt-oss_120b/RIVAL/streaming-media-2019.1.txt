The plan aims to convert a lucrative licensing business into a low‑price, consumer‑facing streaming service. While the potential upside—building a direct‑to‑consumer audience and capturing long‑term subscriber value—is attractive, the proposal ignores several critical constraints:

* **Revenue trade‑off:** Pulling content from rivals eliminates a stable, high‑margin licensing stream that currently funds the company. The loss is not quantified, but it will likely exceed the early subscriber fees, especially when the target is only “tens of millions” versus the market leader’s >100 M users.
* **Cash‑burn exposure:** Funding the service with a multi‑year operating loss requires deep pockets and disciplined capital allocation. Without a clear path to profitability, the company risks depleting cash reserves needed for its core film and TV operations.
* **Execution risk:** The organization has never built a streaming platform. Developing, scaling, and maintaining the required technology stack introduces substantial operational uncertainty and potential for costly overruns.
* **Cannibalisation:** Existing cable and theatrical revenue could be eroded as content shifts to the new service, further weakening cash flow.

Given the magnitude of revenue loss, the unproven technology capability, and the aggressive subscriber goal relative to market leadership, the proposal is premature. It should be revised to include a phased rollout, clearer financial modeling of licensing trade‑offs, and a partnership or acquisition strategy to mitigate technology risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
