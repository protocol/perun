The proposal aims to leap from a licensed‑content library to a high‑cost, prestige original drama, releasing all 26 episodes at once in early 2011. This move would be the first for an online‑only service to launch a full‑season scripted series without a pilot, consuming a large portion of the content budget.

**Pros**
- Differentiates the service from competitors that rely solely on licensed titles.
- Leverages strong viewing‑data insights to argue that the audience will follow a marquee director and star.
- Potential to generate buzz, press coverage, and subscriber growth if the series succeeds.

**Cons**
- No pilot means no early market test; the audience’s appetite for a new, high‑budget drama is unproven.
- Production costs will dominate the content budget, reducing flexibility to acquire or retain other licensed titles.
- Early‑2011 streaming markets are still nascent; no precedent exists for a straight‑to‑series release, increasing execution risk.
- The “big‑budget” label raises expectations for production quality and marketing spend, further straining resources.

**Risk Assessment**
- **Financial exposure:** High – a single flop could erode cash flow and limit future licensing negotiations.
- **Strategic exposure:** Moderate – failure would signal that the platform cannot safely diversify into prestige originals, delaying future original‑content initiatives.
- **Mitigation options:** Conduct a limited‑scope pilot or a shorter “proof‑of‑concept” season; secure co‑financing or distribution partners to share cost; stage a staggered release to gather audience feedback.

**Recommendation**
Given the substantial budget share, lack of pilot de‑risking, and absence of market precedent, the prudent course is to postpone the full‑scale order. Pursue a smaller‑scale original project or a pilot‑style test to validate audience interest before committing to two full seasons.
