The acquisition would cost the buyer $19 billion—more than twelve times the target’s most recent $1.5 billion valuation and far beyond any cash flow the target currently generates. With only a $1‑per‑year subscription fee and “minimal revenue,” the deal relies entirely on future monetisation that has not been defined. The buyer’s stated motive—protecting its own services from a messaging threat—does not itself generate a clear return on the $19 billion outlay.

User‑base size (hundreds of millions of monthly active users) is attractive, but translating that scale into revenue will require a strategy that the target has not demonstrated. The lack of advertising and the modest subscription price leave a large gap between the user base’s potential value and the company’s actual earnings. Building a viable ad or premium‑service model would demand substantial product and sales investment, further eroding the economics of the deal.

Regulatory risk is also significant. A cross‑platform messaging service handling massive personal data will be subject to tightening privacy laws (e.g., GDPR, CCPA) and may face intense scrutiny after a high‑profile acquisition. Compliance costs and potential liability could materially affect the target’s future profitability.

Founder‑retention risk adds another layer of uncertainty. The founders’ willingness to stay and execute a post‑acquisition plan is unproven; losing them could jeopardise product continuity and user trust.

Given the extreme premium, undefined monetisation path, regulatory exposure, and founder‑retention concerns, the strategic upside does not outweigh the financial and operational risks.

DECISION: NO-GO
