**Executive Summary**  
The deal promises a global platform, brand complementarity, and a shared North‑American presence. However, the strategic fit is weak: the German premium brand relies on high‑margin luxury sales, while the US partner’s earnings are tied to a light‑truck cycle that can swing sharply with economic conditions. Cultural and labour‑relation differences between a German “co‑determination” model and an American unionized workforce add integration risk and could impair decisive management. The “merger of equals” claim is questionable because the US firm currently enjoys higher profitability and market share in its core segments, giving it leverage that could tilt governance in its favor.

**Key Risks**  
1. **Cyclical Revenue Exposure** – The US automaker’s cash flow is heavily dependent on trucks, minivans and SUVs, which are sensitive to fuel prices and economic downturns. A market slowdown could erode the combined entity’s earnings quickly.  
2. **Brand Dilution** – Merging a premium German marque with a mass‑market US brand may confuse brand positioning, risking loss of premium‑price power and loyal customers.  
3. **Cultural Integration** – German co‑determination and strong labour representation clash with the US firm’s more hierarchical, profit‑driven culture. Past cross‑border auto mergers have struggled to harmonize work rules, leading to costly strikes and productivity loss.  
4. **Governance Uncertainty** – Equal‑share structures often mask hidden power imbalances; without clear, enforceable parity mechanisms, the German side may become a minority stakeholder in practice.

**Potential Upsides**  
- Platform sharing could reduce R&D spend and accelerate technology rollout (e.g., electrification).  
- Combined scale may improve negotiating power with suppliers and regulators.  
- Access to a broader dealer network and diversified geographic revenue.

**Recommendation**  
Given the asymmetric risk profile, cultural integration challenges, and the questionable “equals” premise, the proposal should not be approved in its current form. The board should demand:

- A detailed integration plan that addresses labour‑law alignment and cultural onboarding.  
- Financial hedging or diversification strategies to mitigate the US partner’s truck‑cycle exposure.  
- Clear governance safeguards ensuring true parity (e.g., equal board voting rights, veto provisions).  
- A phased approach, perhaps starting with joint‑venture platform sharing before a full stock swap.

Only after these conditions are met should the merger be revisited.

DECISION: NO-GO
