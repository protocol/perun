The merger would instantly create a global automaker with a larger product portfolio and the ability to share platforms across premium and mass‑market segments. Past cross‑border auto alliances have struggled to align these systems, leading to costly integration delays and employee unrest.

3. **Brand positioning conflict** – The German premium brand’s value proposition relies on exclusivity and high‑margin pricing. Sharing platforms with mass‑market models could dilute brand equity and alienate core customers.

4. **Equity balance** – Although described as a “stock‑for‑stock merger of equals,” the relative market caps, earnings multiples, and governance structures suggest the U.S. firm may have a stronger bargaining position. Without clear safeguards, the deal could function as a de‑facto takeover, undermining the premise of partnership.
