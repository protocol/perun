**Executive Summary**  
The proposal seeks to leverage the company’s recent e‑reader and low‑priced tablet success by entering the flagship smartphone market in June 2014. The device will be sold exclusively through a single US carrier at a price comparable to leading flagship phones, feature a novel 3‑D‑like display using front‑facing cameras and head‑tracking, and offer a one‑touch product‑identification purchase flow. It will run a forked mobile OS with a reduced app store that lacks the dominant platforms’ mainstream apps.

**Key Strengths**  
- Existing brand recognition in digital reading devices could attract a niche of content‑focused users.  
- The 3‑D‑like display and head‑tracking are technically novel and could differentiate the product if the experience is compelling.  
- One‑touch product identification aligns with the retailer’s core commerce expertise.

**Critical Risks**  
1. **Price vs. Differentiation** – Flagship pricing demands a strong, broadly appealing value proposition. The 3‑D display is a niche feature; without a compelling ecosystem it may not justify the cost to mainstream consumers.  
2. **App Gap** – A forked OS with a smaller app store means users lose access to the dominant platforms’ essential apps (social, navigation, payments). This creates a severe usability deficit that can deter adoption.  
3. **Carrier Exclusivity** – Limiting sales to a single carrier reduces market reach, hampers brand visibility, and ties success to the carrier’s promotional priorities. In a saturated market, broader distribution is crucial for volume.  
4. **Market Saturation** – By mid‑2014, the smartphone market is dominated by two platforms with high consumer loyalty. Entering at the flagship tier requires significant brand pull or a breakthrough feature—neither is clearly established.  

**Strategic Fit**  
The proposal stretches the company’s core competency (content delivery) into a hardware segment that demands deep OS, app, and carrier partnership expertise. The required investments (R&D for 3‑D optics, OS forking, carrier negotiations, marketing) outweigh the modest incremental brand advantage from the e‑reader lineage.

**Recommendation**  
Given the high price point, limited app ecosystem, carrier lock‑in, and saturated competitive landscape, the plan presents a high probability of low adoption and poor ROI. The novelty of the 3‑D display does not offset the fundamental ecosystem deficits. The prudent course is to halt this smartphone launch and instead explore lower‑cost, carrier‑agnostic devices or strengthen the existing tablet line with incremental features that leverage the company’s content ecosystem.

DECISION: NO-GO
