**Executive Summary (ELI10)**  
The retailer wants to jump into a foreign market by leasing 130‑150 existing stores and opening almost all of them within two years. This means building a brand, a supply chain, and an inventory‑management system from scratch while competing against a much larger, well‑established rival. The plan also adds regulatory, bilingual‑labeling, and cross‑border logistics challenges.

**Key Risks & Why They Matter**  

| Risk | Impact if it materializes |
|------|---------------------------|
| **Speed of rollout** – 130‑150 stores in 24 months | Over‑stretched project teams, construction delays, staffing shortages, and rushed store‑fit‑outs that can damage brand perception. |
| **Unproven supply‑chain & inventory system** | Stockouts or excess inventory, higher operating costs, and inability to meet local demand patterns, leading to lost sales and margin erosion. |
| **Price perception vs. entrenched competitor** | If prices are higher than the local leader, shoppers may stay loyal to the incumbent, making the new stores unprofitable quickly. |
| **Regulatory & bilingual labeling** | Non‑compliance penalties, delayed openings, and costly re‑labeling efforts. |

**Completeness Assessment**  
The proposal covers market entry size, timeline, and high‑level logistics but omits: detailed cost‑benefit analysis, pilot‑phase validation, competitive pricing strategy, and contingency plans for supply‑chain failures. Coverage is roughly **5/10** – sufficient to outline ambition but lacking critical execution detail.

**Pros (✅) / Cons (❌)**  

- **A) Full‑scale launch as planned**  
  ✅ Rapid market presence could capture early‑adopter goodwill.  
  ✅ Leverages existing store locations, reducing site‑search time.  
  ❌ Massive capital outlay with limited proof of concept.  
  ❌ High exposure to supply‑chain and regulatory risks.

- **B) Staged rollout (pilot → scale)**  
  ✅ Allows testing of the new supply chain and pricing in a limited geography.  
  ✅ Reduces financial exposure and provides data to refine the model.  
  ❌ Slower market penetration may give the incumbent more time to react.  
  ❌ Requires additional planning and coordination for phased expansion.

**Recommendation**  
**Choose option B – a staged rollout** because it mitigates the three primary risks while still moving toward international growth. A pilot of 10‑15 stores would validate logistics, pricing, and regulatory compliance before committing to the full 130‑150‑store program.

**Net**: The current “all‑at‑once” plan is too aggressive given the untested supply chain, strong local competitor, and regulatory complexity. A phased approach balances ambition with risk control.

DECISION: NO-GO
