**Executive Summary**  
The proposal commits a large portion of the content budget to a high‑cost, two‑season original drama, released all at once without a pilot. This is a first for an online‑only service and departs from the industry norm of pilot testing. The only justification offered is internal viewing data, without external validation.

**Risks**  
1. **No pilot de‑risking** – Pilot episodes traditionally reveal audience interest and production issues. Skipping this step removes the primary safety net.  
2. **Uncertain audience** – The service’s data reflect past consumption of licensed titles, not the appetite for a prestige drama that may attract a different demographic.  
3. **Cost concentration** – Allocating a large share of the content budget to a single untested property threatens the ability to acquire or produce other titles, potentially eroding the broad catalog that currently drives subscriber growth.  
4. **Operational unfamiliarity** – No online‑only competitor has attempted a prestige scripted series; the team lacks proven processes for large‑scale, straight‑to‑series production.

**Potential Upside** – A successful prestige series could differentiate the brand, generate buzz, and attract new subscribers. However, the upside is speculative and hinges on unproven audience demand.

**Recommendation** – Given the high financial exposure, lack of pilot validation, and absence of comparable precedent, the prudent course is to **defer** the full two‑season order. Instead, commission a single‑episode pilot (or a limited‑run miniseries) to test audience response and refine production workflows before committing the full budget.

**Conclusion** – Approve a scaled‑down pilot approach; postpone the full two‑season commitment until pilot performance is validated.
