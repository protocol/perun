**Executive Summary**  
The automaker’s proposal to launch a mass‑produced petrol‑electric hybrid by 1997 is bold and technically pioneering, but the risk profile outweighs the strategic upside at this time.

**Key Drivers**  
1. **Technical Uncertainty** – The power‑split transmission and Ni‑MH battery pack have never been produced at volume. Battery durability is unproven, raising the likelihood of premature failures and costly warranty claims.  
2. **Cost Pressure** – Hybrid systems add significant material and engineering cost. With fuel cheap in the target export market, the price premium required to recoup these costs will likely suppress demand.  
3. **Market Signal** – Regulators have not mandated hybrids and no competitor is offering a production model, indicating weak near‑term consumer demand. Early adopters are a niche segment; mass‑market acceptance cannot be assumed.  
4. **Brand & Warranty Exposure** – Early reliability problems could damage brand reputation and generate large warranty liabilities, especially given the untested battery life.

**Strategic Considerations**  
- **First‑Mover Advantage** is attractive, but only if the product can be delivered reliably and profitably.  
- **R&D Leverage** could be captured by running a limited pilot program (e.g., low‑volume “technology demonstrator”) to validate the transmission and battery performance before committing to full‑scale production.  
- **Partnerships** with battery specialists or incremental sourcing from emerging suppliers could reduce risk and cost.  

**Recommendation**  
Delay the 1997 mass‑production commitment. Instead, allocate resources to a controlled pilot that validates the core hybrid technologies, quantifies real‑world durability, and gauges genuine market interest. Use the pilot data to refine cost models, warranty forecasts, and a realistic launch timeline. This approach preserves the strategic ambition while mitigating the high financial and reputational risks identified.

**Conclusion**  
Approve the strategic intent but reject the current 1997 mass‑production schedule until the technical and market risks are de‑risked through a pilot program.

DECISION: NO-GO
