The concept targets a niche that currently has abundant free, ad‑supported short video and a market already showing subscription fatigue. While premium, short‑form content could attract viewers seeking higher production values, there is no proven demand for a paid model at the proposed price point. The plan relies on very high production budgets and A‑list talent, which dramatically raises the cost structure without a clear path to recoup those expenses.

The technology claim—automatic orientation‑correct video—adds a modest differentiation, but it does not address the core market challenge: convincing users to pay for content they can already obtain for free elsewhere. Even with major studios and brands as investors, the competitive landscape for phone attention is extremely crowded, and the service would need to capture a sizable share of limited user time to be viable.

Risks highlighted in the proposal (unproven demand, pricing pressure, and attention competition) are material and cannot be mitigated simply by technology or talent. A more prudent approach would be to test the model with a lower‑cost pilot, perhaps using a hybrid free‑plus‑premium tier, and to gather concrete usage and willingness‑to‑pay data before committing a billion‑dollar budget.

Given the high financial exposure, lack of market validation, and intense competition for user attention, the proposal should be revised and de‑risked before proceeding.

DECISION: NO-GO
