The acquisition would give the buyer immediate control of the market‑leading code‑hosting platform, but the strategic fit is weak. The target is loss‑making and has been leaderless for months, indicating operational instability that will demand significant management resources after the deal. Community sentiment is openly hostile; developers are already considering a free alternative, so the most valuable asset—its user base—could erode quickly, undermining any revenue upside.  

A $7.5 billion stock purchase represents a steep price relative to current earnings, and without a clear path to profitability the buyer risks overpaying. Moreover, the buyer’s recent shift from anti‑open‑source to open‑source champion raises credibility concerns, making it harder to reassure existing users and retain talent.  

Regulatory and antitrust exposure is high. The target’s dominance in code‑hosting makes the transaction a clear candidate for scrutiny in multiple jurisdictions, potentially resulting in costly delays, divestitures, or outright blockage.  

Given the combination of operational risk, community backlash, financial overextension, and likely regulatory hurdles, the proposal does not meet a prudent risk‑adjusted threshold for approval at this stage.

DECISION: NO-GO
