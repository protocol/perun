**Executive Summary**  
A search‑engine firm is being asked to acquire a 20‑month‑old video‑sharing platform for $1.65 billion in stock. The target is the market’s largest video destination, but it runs on a tiny staff, carries massive bandwidth expenses, generates virtually no revenue, and hosts a flood of user‑uploaded copyrighted TV and film clips that have already provoked legal threats from media owners. The acquirer already operates a smaller video service.

**Key Risks**  

| Risk | Impact | Likelihood |
|------|--------|------------|
| **Over‑priced acquisition** – $1.65 B far exceeds the target’s cash‑flow‑free valuation and comparable market deals. | Dilution of existing shareholders; poor return on investment. | High |
| **Copyright litigation** – Ongoing infringement claims could lead to multi‑hundred‑million settlements or injunctions. | Direct financial loss; reputational damage; possible forced shutdown of the platform. | High |
| **Unproven ad model** – No proven monetisation beyond user uploads; ad revenue for online video was still nascent in 2006. | Uncertain path to recoup costs; continued cash burn. | Medium‑High |
| **Bandwidth cost at scale** – Existing high bills suggest exponential cost growth as traffic expands. | Ongoing operating loss; need for massive infrastructure investment. | Medium |
| **Integration complexity** – Merging two video services with differing architectures and cultures could stall execution. | Delayed synergies; additional engineering overhead. | Medium |

**Strategic Fit**  
The acquisition would instantly give the buyer the largest video audience, potentially unlocking network effects and a stronger position against emerging competitors (e.g., emerging social platforms). However, the strategic upside hinges on rapid development of a sustainable ad‑based revenue model and aggressive legal defence, both of which are uncertain and costly.

**Financial Outlook**  
Assuming a modest 5 % annual ad revenue growth, the target would require > $3 billion in annual ad spend to break even, well beyond current market expectations for online video in 2006. The bandwidth expense alone could consume > 30 % of any future revenue, eroding margins.

**Recommendation**  
Given the combination of an inflated purchase price, severe legal exposure, and an unproven monetisation pathway, the prudent course is to **reject** the deal in its current form. The buyer should either renegotiate a significantly lower valuation, demand indemnities for copyright liabilities, or seek alternative growth strategies (e.g., organic expansion of its existing service or partnership with content owners).

**Conclusion**  
The strategic benefits do not outweigh the financial and legal risks at the proposed terms.

DECISION: NO-GO
