The proposal targets a market that, in early 2001, is still nascent and fragmented. The company’s tiny market share and lack of consumer‑electronics experience make it unlikely to achieve the economies of scale needed for a $400 hard‑drive player, especially when competitors are already offering cheaper flash‑based devices. Pricing the product above those rivals will further shrink the addressable base, which is already limited to owners of the firm’s own computers—a segment too small to sustain volume sales.

Component sourcing for a 5 GB hard‑drive within a year adds supply‑chain risk; any delay would jeopardize the launch window, which is critical before the market consolidates around emerging standards (e.g., iPod, iTunes). Moreover, the music‑industry licensing ecosystem is not yet established, so the bundled software cannot guarantee legal content, reducing consumer appeal.

While entering the digital‑music space could be a strategic long‑term move, the current plan lacks mitigation for the three core risks—price, compatibility, and sourcing. A more viable approach would be to:

1. Reduce cost by using flash storage or a smaller capacity drive.  
2. Open the player to all major PC platforms, expanding the addressable market.  
3. Secure licensing agreements before launch or defer software bundling.

Without these adjustments, the venture is likely to incur significant losses and damage brand credibility.

DECISION: NO-GO
