The carrier’s objective—to create a mobile‑first media and advertising platform that can rival the two dominant online ad giants—requires both scale and expertise. The acquisition would add a legacy internet portal whose traffic and ad revenue have been in sustained decline for years, and whose leadership has turned over repeatedly. This signals a weak brand, eroding audience, and an uncertain future cash flow.

Financially, the $4.8 billion price tag implies a premium for a business that is losing market share. Without a clear path to reverse the revenue trend, the deal risks a negative return on investment, especially given the carrier’s lack of experience in consumer media and advertising. Integration risk is heightened by cultural mismatches: a telecom operator versus a media‑content company, each with different operating models, metrics, and talent pools.

Strategic upside exists—combining the carrier’s mobile reach with any remaining ad inventory could create a differentiated, data‑rich offering. However, this upside depends on rapidly stabilizing the target’s audience, rebuilding advertiser confidence, and building a competent media‑sales organization—capabilities the carrier does not currently possess. The time and additional capital required to acquire those capabilities are not accounted for in the proposal.

Given the target’s deteriorating performance, the carrier’s inexperience in the sector, and the sizable upfront cost, the acquisition presents a high‑risk, low‑certainty proposition. A more prudent approach would be to seek smaller, healthier media assets or to develop organic capabilities before committing such a large sum.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with this acquisition in its current form.
