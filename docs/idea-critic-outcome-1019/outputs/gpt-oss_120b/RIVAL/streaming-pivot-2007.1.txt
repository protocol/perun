**Analysis**  
The company’s core DVD‑mail business is profitable and already serves seven million customers. Adding a streaming service would:

* **Cost:** Licensing streaming rights is substantially more expensive than DVD rights, especially for older titles that command lower marginal revenue. The required technology stack (content delivery, DRM, streaming servers) adds further capital and operational expense.
* **Quality & Reach:** In 2007 broadband penetration is limited; many subscribers would experience poor video quality, reducing perceived value and potentially increasing churn if expectations are not met.
* **Cannibalisation:** Offering free streaming to existing DVD subscribers creates a direct substitute for the paid‑for DVD service, threatening the current profit engine without a clear revenue offset.
* **Competitive Landscape:** Large tech and retail players are poised to enter streaming with deeper pockets and stronger content deals. Entering now positions the firm as a low‑margin follower rather than a differentiated player.

**Recommendation**  
Delay the launch. Instead, pilot a low‑cost, invitation‑only streaming beta for a small subset of tech‑savvy users, funded by a modest surcharge or bundled with a higher‑tier DVD plan. This approach lets the company:

1. Test licensing negotiations and technology performance.  
2. Measure actual churn impact versus projected cannibalisation.  
3. Gather data to inform a future, potentially paid, streaming offering when broadband coverage and content costs improve.

Proceed only after a clear, data‑driven business case shows that streaming can complement, not erode, the profitable DVD model.

DECISION: NO-GO
