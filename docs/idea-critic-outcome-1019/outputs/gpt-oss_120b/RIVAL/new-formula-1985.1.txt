**Executive Summary**  
The company’s flagship cola has been losing market share to diet and non‑cola drinks. Blind taste tests show a rival’s sweeter formula is preferred, and an internally developed sweeter formula outperforms both the rival and the current product in taste tests, surveys, and focus groups. The proposal is to replace the century‑old formula with this new sweeter version and discontinue the original entirely.

**Key Benefits**  
- **Taste advantage**: Consistently higher scores across multiple consumer research methods.  
- **Potential market share gain**: Aligns the product with current consumer preference for sweeter profiles, which could reverse the decline.  
- **Simplified portfolio**: Eliminates a legacy SKU, reducing production complexity.

**Major Risks**  
1. **Emotional attachment & brand heritage** – The original formula is a cultural icon; removing it could alienate long‑time loyalists and generate negative press.  
2. **No fallback product** – If the new formula fails post‑launch (e.g., supply chain issues, taste fatigue), the brand lacks an alternative to re‑introduce quickly.  
3. **Bottler and distributor reaction** – Bottlers invested in existing equipment and marketing assets may resist a sudden switch, risking supply disruptions.  
4. **Regulatory & labeling changes** – A new formula may trigger re‑approval processes in some markets, adding time and cost.

**Mitigation Options**  
- **Dual‑track launch**: Introduce the sweeter formula as a “new” variant while keeping the classic as a limited‑edition or “heritage” line for a transition period (6–12 months).  
- **Pilot regional rollout**: Test the new formula in a few key markets first to gauge real‑world acceptance and bottler impact.  
- **Communication plan**: Proactively frame the change as an evolution honoring the brand’s history, offering nostalgia marketing for the legacy product.  
- **Supply‑chain buffer**: Secure a small reserve of the original formula for at‑risk bottlers during the transition.

**Recommendation**  
Given the strong taste data but significant brand‑heritage risk and lack of a safety net, the proposal should not be approved in its current “full replacement” form. Adopt a phased, dual‑track approach to preserve the legacy brand while testing market response to the sweeter formula.

**Decision**  
Approve a revised plan that launches the new sweeter formula alongside a limited‑edition version of the original for a defined transition period, with regional pilots and a robust communication strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
