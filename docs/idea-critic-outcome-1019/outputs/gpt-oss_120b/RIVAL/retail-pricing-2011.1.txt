The chain’s core revenue has been built on deep discounting and coupon‑driven buying habits. Shifting instantly to everyday low pricing (EDLP) removes the primary value proposition that customers expect and that drives foot traffic. Without a pilot, the company cannot measure how existing shoppers will react, how sales volume and margin will change, or whether the new in‑store shop concept will attract enough traffic to offset the loss of promotional lift.

Key concerns:

1. **Customer behavior** – Decades‑long coupon reliance creates a psychological expectation of price cuts. An abrupt move to EDLP risks alienating shoppers, increasing churn, and accelerating the sales decline the plan aims to stop.

2. **Revenue impact** – Most sales have historically come from discounted items. Removing those discounts removes a proven demand driver. The plan offers no data on margin improvement or substitution effects, leaving a large upside‑down risk.

3. **Operational scale** – Rebuilding every store at once is a massive capital and change‑management effort. Even if the new shop format is attractive, the logistics of redesign, staff retraining, and supply‑chain adjustments could cause widespread disruption.

4. **Risk mitigation absent** – The proposal does not include a controlled test, phased rollout, or contingency plan if early stores underperform. The “no‑pilot” approach eliminates the only low‑cost way to validate assumptions before committing the entire network.

A more prudent path would be:

- Run a pilot in a representative subset of stores (different market segments, sizes, and regions) to gauge customer response, sales lift, and operational challenges.
- Use the pilot data to refine pricing algorithms, promotional cadence, and shop layout.
- Phase the rollout over multiple years, allowing the brand to adjust messaging, train staff, and manage cash flow.

Given the magnitude of change and the lack of empirical validation, approving the full‑scale rollout now would expose the company to a high probability of significant revenue loss and brand damage.

**Recommendation:** Hold the current plan, conduct a limited pilot, and develop a staged implementation roadmap before committing the entire chain.
