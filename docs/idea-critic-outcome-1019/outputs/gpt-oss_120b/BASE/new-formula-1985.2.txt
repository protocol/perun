The data show a clear consumer preference shift: blind taste tests, surveys and focus groups all rank the new, sweeter formula above both the incumbent product and the rival’s sweeter drink. From a pure‑product standpoint, the new formula offers the strongest lever to halt the ongoing loss of market share to diet and non‑cola categories.

However, the proposal also eliminates the legacy formula entirely. The brand’s century‑old flagship is a cultural touchstone; emotional attachment and heritage are core assets that have historically insulated the company from short‑term product‑taste fluctuations. Removing the original without a fallback eliminates a safety net should the new formula encounter unforeseen market resistance, supply‑chain issues, or regulatory challenges. Moreover, bottlers—critical partners in distribution and brand stewardship—may resist a sudden, total switch, potentially jeopardising shelf space, promotional support and overall sales velocity.

A more resilient approach would retain the original as a heritage or “classic” line (limited‑time, niche, or regional), allowing the new formula to become the primary growth engine while preserving brand equity and providing a contingency. This dual‑track strategy also gives bottlers a clear transition path and mitigates consumer backlash.

Given the strong consumer preference for the new formula but the significant heritage and partner risks associated with a full withdrawal, the current all‑or‑nothing plan is premature.

DECISION: NO-GO
