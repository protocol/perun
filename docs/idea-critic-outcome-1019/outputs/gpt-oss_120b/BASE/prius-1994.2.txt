The proposal targets a breakthrough product – a volume‑produced petrol‑electric hybrid – at a time when the underlying technologies (power‑split transmission, nickel‑metal‑hydride batteries) have not yet been demonstrated in large‑scale automotive applications. The 1997 launch date leaves roughly two years for development, validation, tooling, and supply‑chain qualification, a schedule that is aggressive even for an established manufacturer.

**Key Strengths**

* First‑mover status: No competitor offers a production hybrid, giving a potential brand‑leadership and learning‑curve advantage.
* Favorable fuel economics in the primary export market reduce immediate cost pressure for buyers.
* Early exposure to hybrid technology could position the company advantageously if future regulations or fuel‑price shifts favor electrified powertrains.

**Critical Weaknesses**

* Battery durability and lifecycle cost are unproven; warranty exposure could be significant if early units fail prematurely.
* The power‑split transmission is a novel, complex system that will require extensive reliability testing; any failure will damage brand reputation.
* No clear near‑term demand signal: customers have not expressed willingness to pay a premium for an untested hybrid, especially when fuel is cheap and no regulatory mandate exists.
* The compressed timeline risks cost overruns, rushed engineering, and supply‑chain bottlenecks.

**Risk Mitigation Options**

1. Initiate a limited‑run pilot program (e.g., 5,000 units) to validate battery life and transmission reliability before committing to full‑scale production.
2. Partner with a battery specialist or secure a long‑term supply agreement to share technical risk.
3. Conduct market research and pre‑order campaigns in target regions to gauge price sensitivity and demand elasticity.

**Recommendation**

Given the high technical uncertainty, tight schedule, and lack of a demonstrable market pull, proceeding directly to mass production by 1997 is premature. A phased approach that validates core technologies and assesses genuine consumer interest should precede any full‑scale rollout.
