The acquisition offers a fast‑track entry into the core infrastructure of modern software development, turning a formerly adversarial relationship with open‑source communities into a strategic asset. Running the target “independently” could preserve its brand and developer trust, while the buyer gains a platform that fuels its own ecosystem and creates cross‑selling opportunities for cloud services.

However, the risks outweigh the upside at this stage:

* **Community hostility** – The buyer’s historic antagonism toward open source has left a deep‑seated distrust. Even with an independent operating model, many developers are already vocal about migrating to alternatives (e.g., GitLab, free self‑hosted solutions). Early defections would erode the network effect that underpins the target’s value.

* **Leadership vacuum** – The target has been without a permanent CEO for months, indicating governance instability. This makes integration, cultural alignment, and execution of a post‑acquisition roadmap uncertain.

* **Financial mismatch** – The target is loss‑making; a $7.5 bn stock price far exceeds current revenue. Without a clear path to profitability, the buyer risks diluting shareholder value, especially if developer churn reduces future monetisation.

* **Regulatory and antitrust exposure** – The acquisition would create a de‑facto monopoly over the dominant code‑hosting service, likely attracting intense scrutiny from U.S. and EU competition authorities. The buyer’s size and market power amplify the probability of a protracted review or a forced divestiture.

* **Strategic alternatives** – The market already offers viable, free competitors. If the buyer cannot swiftly rebuild trust, the platform’s relevance may decline, turning the purchase into a sunk‑cost liability.

Given these factors, the prudent course is to pause. The buyer should first secure stable leadership at the target, develop a concrete plan to win back the developer community, and model a realistic path to profitability. Simultaneously, a detailed antitrust risk assessment and contingency strategy are essential before committing $7.5 bn in stock.

Proceeding now would expose the buyer to significant financial, reputational, and regulatory damage.

DECISION: NO-GO
