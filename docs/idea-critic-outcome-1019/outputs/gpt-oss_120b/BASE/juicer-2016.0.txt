The startup’s capital base suggests it can absorb early‑stage losses, but the business model hinges on three cost drivers that together create a steep barrier to adoption.

1. **Device price** – At $699 the juicer sits above the premium tier of most countertop appliances (e.g., high‑end blenders typically range $300‑$500). Without a compelling, differentiated experience, price‑sensitive consumers will compare it to lower‑cost alternatives that can achieve similar results with fresh produce.

2. **Subscription cost vs. DIY** – The model replaces a one‑time produce purchase with recurring bag fees. Even if the per‑bag price is modest, the cumulative monthly cost will likely exceed the expense of buying whole fruit and vegetables and juicing them at home, especially for households that already own a basic juicer or blender. The value proposition must therefore rely on convenience, freshness verification, and a perceived health premium that is not demonstrated in the proposal.

3. **Internet dependency** – Requiring Wi‑Fi for operation (e.g., freshness checks) adds friction for users who prefer offline kitchen tools, raises privacy concerns, and introduces a point of failure that could render the device unusable if connectivity is lost. This further narrows the target audience to highly connected, tech‑savvy early adopters.

4. **Market size** – The intersection of consumers willing to spend $699 on a kitchen gadget, commit to a subscription for pre‑packed produce, and maintain constant internet connectivity is a relatively small segment. Scaling beyond this niche would demand price reductions, broader bag offerings, or an offline mode—none of which are addressed.

Given the high upfront cost, uncertain subscription economics, reliance on connectivity, and limited addressable market, the proposal carries significant commercial risk. Without adjustments to pricing, flexibility in bag sourcing, or an offline capability, the venture is unlikely to achieve sustainable adoption at the suggested price points.

**Recommendation:** Revise the model before proceeding.

DECISION: NO-GO
