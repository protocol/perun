**Executive Summary**

The target is the market‑leading video‑sharing platform, now 20 months old, with a massive user base but negligible revenue, a tiny operating staff, and escalating bandwidth expenses. The acquisition price of $1.65 bn in stock values the site at roughly 30 × its (non‑existent) earnings and far exceeds comparable media‑tech deals of the era. The buyer already operates a smaller video service; the acquisition would provide instant scale, brand recognition, and a captive audience, but also inherits severe liabilities.

**Key Risks**

1. **Litigation Exposure** – The platform hosts a high volume of copyrighted TV/film clips. Media companies have already threatened lawsuits; past experience shows such actions can generate multi‑hundred‑million‑dollar judgments and injunctions that could cripple the service.
2. **Revenue Model Uncertainty** – No proven ad‑supported model exists for online video at this scale. Monetisation will require building a robust ad‑tech stack, negotiating rates, and proving CPMs that cover bandwidth and royalty costs.
3. **Bandwidth & Infrastructure Costs** – At current traffic levels, bandwidth alone consumes a large portion of operating cash flow. Scaling to the buyer’s global footprint will dramatically increase these expenses unless negotiated peering or CDN agreements are secured.
4. **Over‑valuation** – Paying $1.65 bn in stock dilutes existing shareholders and sets a high integration hurdle. The price assumes rapid monetisation and cost‑control that are not yet demonstrated.

**Potential Upsides**

- Immediate access to the largest online video audience, accelerating the buyer’s strategic pivot to video.
- Cross‑selling opportunities with the buyer’s existing content and advertising relationships.
- Possibility of leveraging the platform’s brand to negotiate premium advertising and licensing deals.

**Mitigation Recommendations**

- **Price Adjustment** – Renegotiate to a lower cash‑plus‑stock mix, tied to performance milestones (e.g., revenue targets, litigation resolution).
- **Litigation Shield** – Secure indemnification from the seller for pre‑acquisition infringement claims; allocate a dedicated legal fund for settlement or licensing negotiations.
- **Revenue Blueprint** – Require the seller to present a detailed, data‑driven ad‑sales plan, including projected CPMs, inventory fill rates, and partnership pipelines.
- **Cost Controls** – Conduct an independent audit of bandwidth usage; negotiate volume discounts with CDN providers before closing.
- **Integration Plan** – Develop a 90‑day integration roadmap that aligns engineering, content‑policy, and sales teams to address compliance and monetisation simultaneously.

**Conclusion**

While the strategic rationale is compelling, the current terms expose the company to outsized financial, legal, and operational risks that outweigh the immediate market advantage. The deal should be re‑structured before any commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
