**Assessment of the proposed split**

**Strategic fit**  
The core driver is the divergent trajectories of the two businesses: DVD‑by‑mail is in long‑term decline, while streaming is still expanding and requires rapid, technology‑focused decisions. Keeping them under a single brand forces a one‑size‑fits‑all pricing and product roadmap that penalises the growing segment (the 60 % price hike for combined users). A clean separation lets each unit adopt the pace, pricing, and promotional tactics that suit its market dynamics without being constrained by the other’s legacy cost structure.

**Customer impact**  
The biggest downside is friction for existing dual‑subscribers. They will have to manage two accounts, two logins, and two bills, and will lose the convenience of shared ratings and recommendations. This could trigger churn, especially among price‑sensitive users who just faced the steep combined‑plan increase. However, the price hike already signals that the current bundle is unsustainable; customers who value both services may be willing to accept extra effort if the price differential is justified. A well‑timed communication campaign—explaining the rationale, offering a transition discount, and providing a simple “link‑accounts” feature—can mitigate confusion.

**Financial implications**  
Separating the DVD line allows its cost base, inventory, and fulfillment processes to be managed independently, potentially improving margins or enabling a quicker wind‑down if needed. Streaming can pursue aggressive subscriber acquisition (e.g., lower entry price, device partnerships) without being held back by the higher DVD cost structure. The split also creates a clearer financial picture for investors, making it easier to allocate capital to the higher‑growth segment.

**Operational risk**  
Launching a new brand and website introduces short‑term execution risk (technology rollout, customer support scaling). Yet the DVD business already requires distinct logistics; creating a dedicated platform aligns with its operational reality. The risk is manageable if the split is staged, with the new site going live for a pilot group before full migration.

**Recommendation**  
Proceed with the split, but accompany it with a targeted migration plan: (1) offer a limited‑time discount for dual‑subscribers who move to separate accounts, (2) provide a single‑sign‑on bridge for the first three months, and (3) retain a “cross‑recommendations” export tool so users can manually import their ratings. This balances strategic clarity with customer experience safeguards.

DECISION: GO
