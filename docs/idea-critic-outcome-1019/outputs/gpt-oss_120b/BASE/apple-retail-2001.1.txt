The company holds only 3‑5 % of the PC market and has been posting losses. Its current distribution relies on third‑party electronics retailers that favor lower‑priced competitors, limiting the firm’s ability to showcase premium features and the new digital‑music platform. Opening a chain of company‑run stores would give direct control over the customer experience and could serve as a live demo venue for the music strategy, potentially strengthening brand perception and allowing higher‑margin accessories and services to be sold.

However, the retail model for PCs is characterized by thin margins, high inventory requirements, and substantial fixed costs. Prime‑mall leases in 2001 are expensive and would lock the firm into long‑term obligations just as the broader economy is slipping into recession. With recent losses, the company’s cash flow is already constrained; adding significant lease and staffing expenses would increase the risk of further cash‑burn.

Channel conflict is another serious concern. Existing reseller partners are already pressured by price competition; introducing company‑owned outlets could alienate them, prompting reduced shelf space or even loss of the current distribution network. Given the firm’s limited market share, the loss of any reseller support would be disproportionately damaging.

The PC industry’s history with proprietary retail chains is poor—most attempts have failed or been quickly divested. The firm lacks retail operating experience, and building a competent store‑operations team would require additional time and capital. Inventory risk is heightened by rapid product cycles and the need to stock a broad range of configurations, increasing the chance of obsolescence and markdowns.

A more prudent approach would be to strengthen the existing channel partnership, perhaps by creating dedicated demo kiosks within current retailers, offering exclusive in‑store experiences, or piloting a limited number of flagship stores in low‑cost locations to test the concept before full rollout.

Given the current financial strain, macro‑economic headwinds, and the high fixed‑cost, low‑margin nature of PC retail, the proposal introduces more risk than strategic benefit at this time.

DECISION: NO-GO
