The proposal tackles a clear market shift: the DVD‑by‑mail segment is contracting while streaming is expanding. Keeping the two services under a single brand forces a one‑size‑fits‑all pricing and product strategy that no longer fits either business. Separating the DVD line into its own brand would let it be managed, priced, and marketed independently, while streaming can pursue growth‑focused initiatives without being held back by the legacy DVD economics.

**Strengths of the split**

* **Strategic focus** – each unit can adopt a roadmap appropriate to its growth trajectory (e.g., aggressive content acquisition for streaming, cost‑efficiency for DVD).
* **Pricing flexibility** – the DVD brand can introduce lower‑cost tiers or promotional bundles that would be impossible under the current combined‑price model.
* **Operational clarity** – distinct teams can be held accountable for metrics that truly matter to their product (e.g., churn for streaming, fulfillment cost for DVD).

**Key risks**

1. **Customer confusion** – two separate logins, bills, and interfaces may frustrate existing subscribers.
2. **Loss of cross‑service recommendation value** – the recommendation engine that leverages viewing and rental history will be fragmented, potentially reducing engagement.
3. **Backlash to the recent 60 % price hike** – customers already upset by the increase may view the split as a “nickel‑and‑diming” tactic and churn.

**Mitigations that must be in place before launch**

* **Unified authentication** – implement single‑sign‑on so a user can access both sites with one credential, reducing friction.
* **Clear communication plan** – a phased announcement that explains the why, the benefits (e.g., more tailored pricing), and the timeline, using email, in‑app messaging, and support scripts.
* **Transition offers** – provide existing combined‑plan members a limited‑time discount or a bundled “add‑on” option that lets them keep both services under one bill if they prefer.
* **Data bridge for recommendations** – maintain a shared recommendation service that can ingest both streaming watch data and DVD rental data, even if the UI is separate, preserving personalization value.
* **Pilot and feedback loop** – roll the new DVD brand to a small geographic or demographic segment first, measure churn, support tickets, and NPS, then adjust before full rollout.

Given the strategic necessity of separating a declining business from a high‑growth one, the proposal is sound **provided** the above mitigations are committed to execution. Approving the split now enables the streaming unit to move quickly while the DVD unit can be restructured without being held hostage by the combined price increase.
