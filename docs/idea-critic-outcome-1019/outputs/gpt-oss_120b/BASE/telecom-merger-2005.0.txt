The proposed $36 billion merger would give the larger carrier immediate access to a niche business segment that values push‑to‑talk (PTT) services, a capability the buyer currently lacks. That customer base could generate incremental revenue and strengthen the combined firm’s position in construction and enterprise markets.

However, the two networks are built on fundamentally different, incompatible technologies. The target’s system is optimized for voice‑centric PTT and offers limited data capacity, whereas the buyer’s network is designed for broadband data services. Integrating these platforms would require either:

1. Maintaining a legacy network alongside the modern one, incurring ongoing operational and maintenance costs, or  
2. A costly, time‑consuming migration of the target’s customers to the buyer’s platform, risking service disruption and loss of the very PTT users the acquisition is meant to capture.

The buyer would also assume a substantial goodwill balance, reflecting the target’s brand value and customer relationships, but without a clear path to protect or monetize that goodwill given the technology mismatch.

Given the high integration risk, the likely need for significant capital investment to harmonize the networks, and the uncertainty of preserving the target’s core PTT value under a new technological regime, the proposal as presented does not meet a prudent risk‑adjusted return threshold.

**Recommendation:** Hold the transaction pending a detailed integration plan, cost‑benefit analysis of technology migration, and a strategy to safeguard the PTT customer base.
