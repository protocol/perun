The plan targets a niche that has not yet demonstrated commercial viability: premium short‑form (≤10 min) content delivered via a mobile‑only subscription model. While the production quality and A‑list talent could differentiate the service, the core hypothesis—viewers will pay a recurring fee for brief, high‑budget episodes—conflicts with two strong market trends.

1. **Subscription fatigue** – Consumers already juggle multiple SVOD services (Netflix, Disney+, HBO Max, etc.) and are increasingly selective about adding new paid tiers, especially for content that can be accessed for free on platforms like TikTok, YouTube Shorts, and Instagram Reels.

2. **Abundant free alternatives** – The short‑form space is saturated with user‑generated and brand‑sponsored videos that are instantly available, algorithmically curated, and cost‑free. Convincing users to replace that experience with a paid offering would require a clear, compelling value proposition beyond production budget.

The proposed $1 billion capital raise is massive for an untested format, raising the financial exposure for investors. Even with major studios and brands on board, the risk‑adjusted return profile is weak unless the service can achieve rapid scale and high ARPU (average revenue per user). The technology that adapts video orientation is a modest differentiator; it does not address the fundamental demand question.

Given the absence of market validation, the high cost structure, and the competitive pressure from free short‑form platforms, the proposal should not be approved in its current form. A more prudent approach would be to pilot the concept with a limited content slate, test pricing elasticity, and gather user data before committing to a billion‑dollar rollout.

**Recommendation:** Hold the proposal for revision and a staged validation plan before proceeding.

DECISION: NO-GO
