**Assessment**

1. **Strategic Fit**  
   • Acquiring the dominant code‑hosting platform aligns with the buyer’s recent pivot to open source, giving it immediate credibility and a foothold in a critical developer‑infrastructure market.  
   • Ownership would allow integration of the platform with the buyer’s cloud and developer tools, creating cross‑selling opportunities and a potential revenue engine beyond the current loss‑making status.

2. **Financial Considerations**  
   • Purchase price of ≈ $7.5 bn in stock values the target at a premium to its current market (private) valuation, but the platform’s revenue is still modest relative to that price.  
   • The target’s loss‑making status implies a need for continued investment to reach profitability; the buyer must budget for cash burn and possible restructuring costs.  
   • A stock‑based deal dilutes existing shareholders; the buyer should model dilution versus expected incremental revenue from integration and from retaining the platform’s large user base.

3. **Leadership Gap**  
   • Absence of a permanent CEO for months suggests operational uncertainty. The buyer must install experienced leadership quickly (ideally a CEO with SaaS and community‑centric experience) to stabilize operations and reassure both employees and the developer community.

4. **Community Trust & Defection Risk**  
   • The developer community’s distrust is a core risk. Past hostility to open source fuels fear of “vendor lock‑in” or policy changes.  
   • Mitigation: commit contractually to keep the platform independent, preserve existing open‑source licensing, and maintain transparent governance (e.g., a community advisory board). Publicly guarantee data portability and continued support for competing tools.  
   • Early engagement with key community influencers and a clear roadmap can reduce defection; however, some migration to free alternatives (e.g., GitLab) is likely inevitable.

5. **Regulatory & Antitrust Exposure**  
   • The acquisition consolidates a critical piece of developer infrastructure under a major cloud vendor, raising antitrust concerns, especially in the U.S. and EU.  
   • The buyer should prepare a robust antitrust filing, emphasizing the platform’s continued independence, open‑source commitment, and the existence of viable competitors.  
   • Timing (June 2018) predates heightened scrutiny of big‑tech acquisitions, but the buyer must still anticipate prolonged review.

6. **Operational Independence**  
   • The proposal to run the target independently helps allay community and regulator worries, but requires clear governance structures, separate budgeting, and autonomy over product decisions.

**Conclusion**  
The strategic upside is compelling, yet the high price, loss‑making status, leadership vacuum, and pronounced community and antitrust risks demand decisive mitigation. If the buyer can immediately appoint a proven CEO, lock in commitments to platform independence, and allocate sufficient resources to bring the business to profitability while preparing a solid antitrust case, the acquisition can proceed.

**Decision:**  
DECISION: GO
