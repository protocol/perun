**Assessment of the proposal**

1. **Market evidence** – Blind taste tests, surveys and focus groups show a clear preference for a sweeter formula that out‑performs both the current flagship and the rival’s product. The data indicate a genuine taste‑driven opportunity to regain market share from diet and non‑cola competitors.

2. **Scope of the change** – The plan calls for a complete replacement of the century‑old flagship and the total withdrawal of the original product. This is a radical brand move that eliminates any safety net.

3. **Key risks**  
   * **Emotional attachment & heritage** – The flagship has been sold for a generation; many consumers have strong nostalgic links. Removing it outright could trigger a backlash that outweighs the taste advantage.  
   * **Testing blind to removal** – Participants were not told the original would disappear, so their stated preference may not translate into purchase behaviour once the original is gone.  
   * **No fallback product** – If the new formula fails to resonate in the real market (e.g., due to perception of “tampering” with a classic), the company would have no alternative to re‑engage dissatisfied customers.  
   * **Bottler and retail reaction** – Bottlers have invested in the legacy product line; a sudden discontinuation could strain relationships, disrupt supply chains, and increase costs for re‑tooling.

4. **Strategic options**  
   * **Dual‑track launch** – Introduce the sweeter formula as a new “variant” (e.g., “Classic‑Plus”) while retaining the original as a “Classic” offering. This preserves heritage, provides a fallback, and allows the market to self‑select.  
   * **Limited geographic pilot** – Roll out the new formula in selected markets to validate real‑world acceptance and monitor bottler response before a national decision.  
   * **Communications plan** – Frame the change as an evolution, not a replacement, to mitigate emotional resistance.

5. **Recommendation** – The taste advantage is compelling, but the all‑or‑nothing approach carries disproportionate brand‑heritage and supply‑chain risks. A phased, dual‑product strategy offers a safer path to capture the taste‑driven market while protecting the brand’s core equity.

**Decision:** **DECISION: NO-GO**
