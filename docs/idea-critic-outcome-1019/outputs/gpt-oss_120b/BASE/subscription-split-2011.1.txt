The proposal correctly identifies that the DVD‑by‑mail segment is on a long‑term decline and that its operational priorities differ from those of the streaming service. Splitting the two businesses will allow each to be run with strategies and cost structures that match its market realities, and it will shield the streaming brand from the backlash generated by the 60 % price hike applied to the combined plan.

However, the plan as written creates several immediate customer‑experience problems that could outweigh the strategic benefit:

* **Dual login and billing** – forcing existing combined‑plan members to manage two separate accounts will likely increase support tickets, generate frustration, and accelerate churn, especially among price‑sensitive users who have just faced a large increase.
* **Loss of cross‑service recommendations** – the current recommendation engine leverages data from both formats to surface titles. Removing that synergy reduces the perceived value of each service and may lower engagement.
* **Brand confusion** – a sudden introduction of a new name and website for a service that many customers have used for years risks mis‑perception that the company is abandoning DVD customers or that the two offerings are unrelated.
* **Reaction to price increase** – while the split isolates streaming from the price hike, the DVD‑by‑mail customers will still feel the impact, and without a clear migration path or incentive, many may abandon the service entirely.

To move forward safely, the proposal should incorporate mitigation steps: a single‑sign‑on bridge, an optional combined‑billing option for legacy users, a phased rollout with extensive communication, and a limited period of shared recommendation data to preserve value during transition.

Because these critical risk‑reduction measures are absent, approving the split in its current form would likely generate more churn and support costs than the strategic gains justify.

**DECISION: NO-GO**
