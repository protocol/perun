The company’s market position—3‑5 % share and a recent string of losses—means cash flow is already constrained. Opening a company‑run chain in prime‑mall locations would add substantial fixed costs (high‑rent leases, staffing, utilities) at a time when the broader economy is slipping into recession. Retail margins for personal computers are historically thin; the added overhead would further erode profitability and could accelerate the loss trend.

Inventory risk is another critical factor. Owning stock in multiple stores ties up capital that could otherwise be used to fund product development or marketing. Unsold units would have to be discounted, deepening margin pressure. The proposal also creates direct channel conflict with existing third‑party retailers, whose staff already favor lower‑priced competitors. Alienating these partners could shrink the already modest distribution footprint, reducing overall unit volume.

The strategic rationale—showcasing the product and the new digital‑music platform—can be achieved through lower‑cost alternatives: dedicated brand kiosks inside existing retailers, pop‑up experience spaces, or an enhanced e‑commerce presence with virtual demos. These options preserve the partner relationship, avoid large lease commitments, and keep inventory risk manageable.

Given the company’s fragile financial state, the macro‑economic outlook, and the high‑cost, high‑risk nature of the proposed retail rollout, proceeding as outlined would likely worsen the loss trajectory rather than generate the intended brand uplift. The prudent course is to defer the full‑scale store launch and explore lower‑investment experiential channels first.

DECISION: NO‑GO
