The proposed $36 billion merger pairs a national carrier with a smaller operator whose push‑to‑talk (PTT) service commands a strong niche among construction and business users. The target’s customer base is indeed valuable: high‑margin, loyal accounts that could augment the buyer’s enterprise revenue and provide cross‑selling opportunities for data and voice services.

However, the core obstacle is technical. The two networks rely on incompatible radio standards, and the target’s platform is limited in data capacity. To realize any synergies, the combined firm must either:

1. Maintain both legacy networks in parallel, incurring ongoing operational expense and complexity; or  
2. Undertake a costly, time‑consuming migration of the target’s customers onto the buyer’s more advanced infrastructure, risking service disruption and potential churn of the very business customers the deal seeks to retain.

Both paths increase integration cost substantially and expose the combined entity to execution risk. Moreover, the buyer would assume a large goodwill balance, reflecting the premium paid for the target’s brand and customer relationships. If the technical integration falters, that goodwill could be impaired quickly, eroding earnings.

Strategically, the acquisition could be justified only if the buyer has a clear, funded roadmap to harmonize the networks—e.g., a phased migration plan with guaranteed service levels for PTT users, and a concrete timeline to retire legacy equipment. Absent such a plan, the merger threatens to dilute the buyer’s financial position and distract management from core growth initiatives, especially as data demand accelerates industry‑wide.

Given the information provided—significant technology incompatibility, undefined integration costs, and exposure to goodwill impairment—the proposal carries more risk than immediate strategic benefit. A prudent course is to defer approval until a detailed integration blueprint and cost‑benefit analysis are presented.

DECISION: NO‑GO
