The target is a six‑person startup building an open‑source mobile OS in a market where the dominant platforms (Symbian, Windows Mobile, Palm) already have carrier‑backed ecosystems. Handset makers have little incentive to adopt a new, unproven stack, and carriers—who control which devices reach consumers—are historically reluctant to back software that could reduce their own revenue streams (e.g., carrier‑specific UI layers, bundled services).

The acquiring company’s core competency is search advertising, not hardware integration, supply‑chain management, or carrier negotiation. Without those capabilities it will rely entirely on the startup’s ability to persuade manufacturers and carriers to ship devices running the new OS. That is a long‑term, high‑cost effort that the acquirer is not positioned to execute.

The strategic rationale—driving mobile web usage to boost search‑ad revenue—assumes a rapid shift from feature phones to data‑centric smartphones. In mid‑2005, data plans are expensive, mobile browsers are limited, and consumer adoption of mobile web is modest. Even if the OS gains traction, the revenue uplift to search ads would be indirect, delayed, and difficult to quantify. There is no defined monetisation path for the OS itself (licensing, services, or app marketplace), leaving the investment without a clear cash‑flow bridge.

The proposed price of at least $50 million for a six‑person team translates to >$8 million per employee, a valuation far above typical early‑stage software ventures, especially given the absence of any existing product, customers, or revenue. The risk‑adjusted return horizon is multi‑year, while the company’s shareholders likely expect nearer‑term growth from its core advertising business.

Given the mismatch between the acquirer’s strengths and the acquisition’s execution challenges, the uncertain market dynamics, the lack of a direct revenue model, and the premium valuation, the proposal carries a high probability of failing to deliver the anticipated strategic benefit within an acceptable timeframe.

DECISION: NO-GO
