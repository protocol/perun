The company’s DVD business is currently cash‑generating and the subscriber base is large enough to absorb a modest experiment. Adding streaming now would give the brand a foothold before the market matures, signaling relevance to tech‑savvy customers and creating a barrier to entry for later competitors. However, the proposal as written forces the firm to bear the full cost of streaming licences and infrastructure while offering a low‑quality, limited catalogue for free. In 2007 broadband penetration is uneven, so many subscribers would derive little value, yet the company would still incur the same licensing fees. The high licensing cost combined with a small, older‑title catalogue will likely increase churn among the most profitable, DVD‑focused segment, eroding the margin that currently funds the business.

Strategically, the move is a defensive play against future entrants, but the cost structure is unsustainable without a clear path to scaling the catalogue, improving video quality, or monetising the service (e.g., a tiered fee). A free‑to‑all rollout also removes any price‑signal that could help the company negotiate better terms with studios. The risk of cannibalising the core DVD revenue outweighs the uncertain benefit of early streaming exposure under the current parameters.

A more prudent approach would be a limited pilot (geographically or by subscriber segment), a modest subscription surcharge for streaming, or a partnership that shares licensing costs. Until those mitigations are in place, approving the full‑scale, free streaming launch would likely damage profitability and expose the firm to a costly competitive battle it is not yet equipped to win.

**Decision:** The proposal should not be approved as presented.
