**Steelman claim** – Acquiring the six‑person startup for ≥ $50 M will give the search‑advertising company a foothold in mobile OS development, spurring open‑source adoption, expanding mobile web usage and ultimately increasing search‑ad revenue.

**Skeptic attacks**  
- **Adoption assumption:** Handset makers have little incentive to ship a new, unproven OS; historically they stick with iOS, Android, Symbian, or Windows Mobile.  
- **Carrier control:** Carriers own the distribution pipeline and have historically blocked platforms that jeopardize their own services or revenue shares.  
- **Revenue model gap:** The OS is free; without a clear monetisation path (e.g., app store fees, licensing, data services) the acquisition cannot recoup cost.  
- **Payback horizon:** Even optimistic mobile‑web growth would require several years before any measurable lift in search‑ad clicks appears, far exceeding typical M&A integration cycles.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
1. Form strategic partnerships or licensing deals with existing OS vendors to embed search‑ad widgets, avoiding a $50 M cash outlay.  
2. Invest in a mobile‑web optimization team to capture ad revenue directly, a lower‑cost, higher‑speed path.  
3. Acquire a company with proven mobile‑ad technology or a larger OS player that already has carrier agreements.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**  
- No carrier distribution deal signed within 24 months → abort.  
- No handset‑maker adoption within 36 months → abort.  
- Cumulative net loss > $100 M by year 5 → abort.  

**Concrete failure scenario** – Carriers reject the OS, preventing any handset from shipping it. The startup’s technology remains unused, the $50 M purchase yields zero revenue, and the search‑advertising business sees no lift in mobile ad spend, breaching the first kill‑criterion.

Given the high strategic risk, lack of a clear monetisation model, and the existence of lower‑cost alternatives, the proposal should not be approved as presented.

DECISION: NO-GO
