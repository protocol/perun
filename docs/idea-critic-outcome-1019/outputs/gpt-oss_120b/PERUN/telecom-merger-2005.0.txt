**Steelman:** The merger would create the nation’s largest wireless carrier, combining a broad consumer base with a niche, high‑value business segment. By acquiring the smaller carrier, the buyer gains immediate access to construction‑and‑business customers who rely on push‑to‑talk, potentially boosting revenue and market share. The $36 billion deal is positioned as a strategic move to dominate both consumer and enterprise markets.

**Skeptic (assumptions & risks)**  
1. **Technology incompatibility** – The two networks use different radio standards; integrating them will require costly dual‑network operation or a full rebuild of one side, extending rollout timelines and increasing CAPEX.  
2. **Data‑service limitation** – The target’s network is weak for data, a core growth driver in 2005 onward; retaining it could erode the merged entity’s competitive edge in data‑centric services.  
3. **Goodwill burden** – The buyer must absorb substantial goodwill, which could impair balance‑sheet strength and limit future investment flexibility.  
4. **Integration cost & timeline** – Industry precedents (e.g., past carrier mergers) show integration overruns of 20‑30 % and 12‑24 months delays, risking customer churn, especially among business users who need reliable service.

**Better‑Way (alternatives)**  
- **Do nothing** – Preserve capital, avoid integration risk, and continue organic growth in data services.  
- **Partial acquisition** – Purchase only the business‑customer contracts and brand, leaving the legacy network to be phased out, reducing technology overlap.  
- **Strategic partnership** – Form a roaming or wholesale agreement to tap the business segment without full network integration.

**Kill‑Criteria (pre‑mortem)**  
- If integration cost exceeds 15 % of deal value (> $5.4 B) → abort.  
- If projected data‑service coverage falls below 80 % of current network within 12 months → abort.  
- If goodwill impairment > $2 B within first fiscal year → abort.

**Fit with Strategy** – The buyer’s long‑term vision emphasizes data‑centric growth and network uniformity; a legacy, data‑weak network conflicts with that direction.

**Conclusion:** Given the high integration risk, technology mismatch, and financial burden, the merger does not meet the required risk‑adjusted return thresholds without a clear mitigation plan.

DECISION: NO-GO
