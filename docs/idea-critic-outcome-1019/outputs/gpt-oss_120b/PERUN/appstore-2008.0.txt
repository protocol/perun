**Steelman:** The company will launch a curated app store, letting third‑party developers sell native apps while the firm retains a 30 % commission and approves every submission. This aims to expand functionality, generate revenue, and keep control over quality and security.

**Skeptic checks**
- **Security & stability:** Unvetted native code can crash the OS or expose vulnerabilities. The proposal relies on a manual review process that historically struggles to catch sophisticated exploits.
- **Carrier objections:** Apps that bypass carrier‑provided services (e.g., billing, messaging) may provoke carrier resistance, risking market access or revenue‑share agreements.
- **Review workload:** A single‑company store will need a sizable, continuously staffed QA team; scaling to thousands of apps quickly becomes untenable.
- **Product polish:** Poorly designed apps can tarnish the brand’s reputation for a seamless, polished experience, especially when the phone is still early‑generation.

**Better‑way alternatives**
1. **Phased rollout:** Start with a limited “beta” store for vetted partners, using sandboxed APIs to contain risk.
2. **Carrier‑co‑managed channels:** Offer carriers a white‑label storefront for carrier‑approved services, preserving relationships.
3. **Open distribution with revenue‑share SDK:** Allow developers to publish elsewhere but embed a mandatory SDK that routes purchases through the company, reducing review load while still earning commissions.

**Kill‑criteria**
- Any security breach that compromises user data or device integrity within the first 90 days.
- Formal carrier protest leading to loss of distribution agreements.
- Review backlog exceeding 30 % of incoming submissions for more than two weeks.
- Measurable drop (>5 pts) in device NPS attributable to third‑party apps within six months.

**Remaining risk:** Even with sandboxing, unknown zero‑day exploits may surface; carrier negotiations may delay launch; the review team must scale faster than anticipated.

Given the high‑impact, low‑reversibility risks and the existence of lower‑cost, lower‑risk alternatives, the proposal should not be approved as‑is.

**DECISION: NO-GO**
