**Steelman** – The service commissions a 26‑episode original drama, straight‑to‑series, released all at once, to reduce reliance on rising licensing fees, leveraging strong viewing data and the prestige of a known director and star.

**Skeptic attack** –  
- **Budget share**: “large share” of the content budget could jeopardize the licensing of other popular titles that currently drive subscriber growth.  
- **No pilot**: without a pilot the team cannot validate audience interest, narrative quality, or production costs.  
- **All‑at‑once release**: binge‑release removes the ability to adjust marketing or creative direction based on early episode performance.  
- **Unproven model**: no online‑only service has yet delivered a prestige scripted series; the risk profile is higher than any historical internal data can confirm.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
1. **Pilot‑first approach** – Produce a single episode (or a short 4‑episode arc) to test audience reaction, then scale if metrics meet targets.  
2. **Co‑production** – Partner with another studio or network to share production risk and cost, while still gaining original content.  
3. **Incremental original slate** – Start with lower‑budget, genre‑specific originals (e.g., comedy, documentary) that require less upfront spend and can be released gradually.

**Fit with strategy** – The proposal aligns with the goal of reducing licensing spend, but conflicts with the core metric of maintaining a broad, high‑engagement catalog. The risk‑adjusted ROI is unclear.

**Pre‑mortem** – If the series underperforms, the service loses a substantial portion of its budget, may need to cut other licenses, and could see subscriber churn. The decision is a two‑way door only if a pilot or co‑production is used; the current all‑or‑nothing plan is a one‑way door.

**Kill‑criteria** – Proceed only if:  
- Production cost ≤ 15 % of total annual content budget,  
- Projected first‑month viewership ≥ 30 % of the platform’s average series launch,  
- A contingency clause allows termination after the first 6 episodes if targets aren’t met.

Given the absence of pilot data, the high budget exposure, and no proven precedent, the proposal does not satisfy the kill‑criteria.

**Decision** – The project should be re‑scoped to include a pilot or risk‑sharing arrangement before full commitment.  

DECISION: NO-GO
