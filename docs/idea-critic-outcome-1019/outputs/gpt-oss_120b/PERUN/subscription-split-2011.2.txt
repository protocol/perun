**Steelman (strongest defensible claim)**  
Separating the DVD‑by‑mail service into its own brand and website will let each business be run by a team focused on its specific market dynamics, avoid the backlash from the 60 % price hike on the combined plan, and give the streaming side freedom to pursue its own growth strategy.

**Attack & Findings**  
1. **Customer confusion** – Two separate logins, accounts and bills increase friction, likely raising churn, especially for users who value the convenience of a single subscription.  
2. **Loss of cross‑service recommendations** – Removing shared ratings eliminates a key value‑add that encourages users to try both tiers, potentially accelerating the decline of the DVD side.  
3. **Reaction to price increase** – Splitting after a steep price hike does not address the core dissatisfaction; customers may simply cancel the DVD service altogether.  

**Better‑Way Alternatives**  
- Keep a single brand but offer an *add‑on* DVD tier with its own pricing, preserving one login and shared recommendations.  
- Introduce a “stream‑only” plan while keeping the combined plan unchanged, letting price‑sensitive users migrate voluntarily.  

**Fit with Stated Rationale**  
The DVD decline does call for distinct management, but the proposal sacrifices the synergy that currently mitigates that decline (shared recommendations, bundled pricing). The split may solve a management problem at the expense of a larger revenue‑loss problem.

**Pre‑mortem / Door**  
Assume the split fails: a 15 % churn spike in the first quarter, NPS falling >5 points, and a surge in support tickets about login/billing issues. This is a two‑way door; reversal would require re‑integrating accounts—a costly effort.

**Evidence** – All points derived from the proposal’s own risk list; no external data needed.

**Kill‑Criteria (proposed)**  
- If churn >10 % within 90 days *or* NPS drops >5, abort the split and re‑unify billing.  

**Dissent Ledger** – Risks of confusion, lost recommendations, and insufficient mitigation of price‑increase backlash outweigh the management benefit without concrete mitigation steps.

**Remaining Risk** – High customer churn and brand dilution.

DECISION: NO-GO
