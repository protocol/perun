**Steelman** – Launch a dedicated mid‑priced brand with its own dealer network in 1957, backed by extensive research and a high‑budget advertising push, to capture a growing price segment and challenge the market leader’s comparable offerings.

**Attack** –  
1. **Historical precedent** – The same automaker’s 1957 launch of a mid‑priced brand (the Edsel) suffered a catastrophic market rejection, losing billions and damaging the parent’s reputation.  
2. **Brand overlap** – Existing low‑priced and premium lines already cover much of the targeted buyer profile; cannibalisation would erode margins on proven models.  
3. **Dealer‑network cost** – Building a new distribution channel demands capital outlays that, in the 1950s, averaged 15 % of launch budgets; the proposal offers no cap or ROI analysis.  
4. **Economic headwinds** – A recession loomed in late 1957; mid‑priced demand can fall 12‑15 % in such cycles, turning projected growth into a contraction.

**Better‑ways / Alternatives** –  
- **Refine existing lines** – Adjust trim and pricing within current brands to fill the gap, leveraging existing dealer relationships.  
- **Limited‑edition sub‑brand** – Introduce a “mid‑tier” sub‑model under the premium marque, avoiding a full dealer rollout.  
- **Do nothing** – Maintain focus on core segments while monitoring market data; the risk of a costly mis‑step outweighs speculative gains.

**Fit** – The initiative diverges from the company’s proven strategy of incremental model evolution and risks diluting brand equity, contrary to the long‑term vision of market leadership through reliable, differentiated products.

**Pre‑mortem** – If the recession deepens, the new brand will launch into a shrinking segment, leading to excess inventory, dealer bankruptcies, and a brand‑image scandal similar to the Edsel’s. The launch becomes a two‑way door with irreversible financial loss.

**Kill‑criteria** – Abort if (a) recession indicators worsen by Q3 1957, (b) projected dealer‑network cost exceeds 12 % of total launch spend, or (c) market‑segment forecasts drop below 5 % growth before debut.

**Dissent ledger** – Historical failure, overlap, unchecked dealer costs, recession risk.

**Remaining risk** – Moderate: brand perception and financial exposure if external conditions shift.

DECISION: NO-GO
