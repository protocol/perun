**Steelman:** Launch a low‑cost hybrid console that can dock to a TV or be used handheld, powered by a modest mobile chip, with detachable motion controllers. Price it on par with more powerful rivals and back it with a modest first‑year lineup of games from the maker’s own studios.

**Attack / Risks**
1. **Hardware weakness** – Using a modest‑power chip will make performance noticeably lower than rivals, risking negative reviews and consumer backlash.
2. **Brand confusion** – The previous home console failed and was confusingly launched; repeating a hybrid with unclear positioning could repeat that mistake.
3. **Third‑party ecosystem** – Limited support from external developers means the game library will rely heavily on internal titles, reducing long‑term appeal.
4. **Supply chain** – A new form factor and detachable controllers introduce additional manufacturing complexity, increasing the chance of shortages at launch.
5. **Recovery window** – The company has a short timeframe to recoup losses; a slow‑selling product could deepen financial strain.

**Alternatives**
- **Do nothing**: Focus on strengthening the handheld line and mobile partnerships, avoiding capital risk.
- **Higher‑spec console**: Invest in a more powerful chip and accept a higher price point to compete on performance rather than novelty.
- **Co‑development**: Secure early commitments from key third‑party studios before launch to guarantee a broader launch library.

**Fit with Strategy** – The goal is to revive the home‑console business, but the proposal leans heavily on internal games and price parity rather than differentiated value, misaligned with a recovery‑focused strategy that typically prioritizes proven revenue streams.

**Pre‑mortem (One‑Way Door)** – If early sales are weak, the product can be discontinued with limited sunk cost; however, the brand damage and inventory risk are irreversible.

**Kill Criteria** – Abort if:
- First‑six‑month sales < 500 k units, **or**
- Fewer than 5 third‑party titles ship at launch, **or**
- Supply shortages exceed 20 % of forecasted units.

Given the high hardware and ecosystem risks, the short recovery horizon, and the lack of concrete mitigation for third‑party support, the proposal does not meet a defensible risk/reward balance.

**Decision:** The executive team should not approve the plan as presented.

DECISION: NO-GO
