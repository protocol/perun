**Steelman:** Launch a premium smartphone (June 2014) sold only through one U.S. carrier, priced like flagship phones, featuring a 3D‑like display driven by front‑facing cameras and head‑tracking, plus a one‑touch product‑identification system. The device runs a forked OS with a reduced app store and lacks the dominant platforms’ mainstream apps.

**Skeptic attack:**  
- **Platform lock‑in:** With only two platforms controlling >95 % of apps, a reduced store will alienate users who expect services such as Google Maps, WhatsApp, and iMessage.  
- **Differentiation vs price:** A 3D‑display gimmick does not solve a core consumer need; at flagship pricing the value proposition is weak.  
- **Carrier exclusivity:** Restricts the addressable market to a single carrier’s subscriber base (~30 % of U.S. phones) and hampers brand visibility.  
- **Market saturation:** Early‑2014 smartphone sales were already plateauing; entering with a niche feature adds little to total volume.

**Better‑way alternatives:**  
1. Release a co‑branded Android device on the same carrier, retaining access to the full Play Store.  
2. Add the 3D‑camera module as an accessory for the existing tablet, leveraging the current user base.  
3. License the head‑tracking tech to existing OEMs rather than building a full phone.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem):** Abort if first‑year shipments <5 % of flagship volume, or if the app catalog remains <500 titles, or if carrier‑only sales fall below 10 % of the carrier’s total smartphone shipments.

**Dissent ledger:** The proposal’s core risk—lack of mainstream apps at a premium price—alone outweighs its novelty. Without a clear path to app ecosystem growth or broader carrier support, the bet is unlikely to meet revenue or market‑share targets.

DECISION: NO-GO
