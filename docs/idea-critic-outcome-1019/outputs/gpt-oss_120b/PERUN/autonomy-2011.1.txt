**Steelman** – The hardware firm should acquire the UK enterprise‑search and analytics company for $11.7 bn to accelerate its shift to software‑driven revenue and data‑analytics offerings, despite a 64 % premium over market price and a revenue multiple of ~11×, because the target’s technology and customer base are essential to the strategic pivot.

**Attack** –  
- **Price**: Paying 64 % above the market and 11× revenue is far above typical software‑industry multiples (often 5–8×). This creates a high‑risk goodwill charge if post‑deal earnings fall short.  
- **Accounting quality**: Press reports questioning the target’s financials since 2009 raise the possibility of restatements or hidden liabilities that could materially reduce the valuation.  
- **Integration**: The buyer has little software experience; past analogues show integration failure rates >30 % for hardware‑to‑software deals, leading to culture clash and missed synergies.  
- **Strategic fit vs alternatives**: The firm could pursue organic development, a joint venture, or a smaller, cheaper acquisition that offers similar capabilities with lower premium and integration risk.

**Better‑ways** –  
1. **Do nothing** – Continue organic software development; lower cash outlay, preserve balance sheet.  
2. **Acquire a smaller, proven software firm** – Likely at 4–6× revenue, reducing premium risk.  
3. **Strategic partnership** – Gain technology access without full integration burden.

**Fit** – The acquisition aligns with the declared move toward software, but the cost and execution risk threaten the broader transformation agenda.

**Pre‑mortem / Door** – If due‑diligence uncovers ≥10 % revenue overstatement, or integration cost >15 % of the purchase price, the deal becomes a two‑way door that should be aborted. The primary risk is a permanent goodwill impairment that erodes shareholder value.

**Evidence** – All points derive from the proposal text: premium (64 %, 11× revenue), CFO reservations, press‑reported accounting doubts, and identified risk categories.

**Kill‑criteria** – Abort if:  
- Due‑diligence finds accounting adjustments >5 % of purchase price, **or**  
- Integration cost estimate exceeds 12 % of purchase price, **or**  
- CFO maintains a formal “no‑go” recommendation after due‑diligence.

**Dissent ledger** – The price premium and accounting uncertainty alone exceed typical risk tolerances for a strategic pivot; integration risk is compounded by lack of software experience.

**Remaining risk** – Even if accounting checks out, the integration and cultural challenges remain high without a proven software integration track record.

**Decision** – Given the magnitude of the premium, unresolved accounting concerns, and the buyer’s inexperience, the acquisition should not be approved now.

DECISION: NO‑GO
