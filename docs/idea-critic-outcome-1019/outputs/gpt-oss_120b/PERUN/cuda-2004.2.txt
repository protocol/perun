**Steelman claim:** The chipmaker will embed general‑purpose compute units and a full software stack into every consumer GPU, raising per‑chip cost but creating a universal platform for scientific computing that could seed a future market and give the company a first‑mover advantage.

**Skeptic attack:** 1) Cost impact: without any committed buyers, the added silicon and toolchain will increase unit cost on *all* products. Even a modest 5 % price rise can erode competitiveness against the rival’s cheaper, performance‑focused GPUs, risking loss of market share in the core gaming segment. 2) Market uncertainty: academic users are still hacking existing APIs; no commercial workload exists to justify the expense. 3) Engineering distraction: resources diverted to a speculative feature set may delay critical performance improvements needed to stay ahead of the rival.

**Better‑way alternatives:** - Do nothing now and continue to dominate the gaming market while monitoring emerging GPGPU demand. - Introduce optional, higher‑priced “pro” GPUs with the extra features, targeting early adopters without raising the cost of the entire product line. - Partner with universities to develop a limited‑run research chip, gaining data without a fleet‑wide cost increase.

**Kill‑criteria:** If the added silicon raises the bill‑of‑materials cost by >5 % and there are no pre‑signed purchase agreements for the new capability within 12 months, the programme must be halted. The metric to watch is the price‑elasticity impact on quarterly market‑share loss; a 2 % drop triggers a stop‑go review.

Given the high upfront cost, lack of a paying market, and the risk of weakening the core competitive position, the proposal should not be approved in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
