**Steelman claim** – Raise > $1 billion to launch a mobile‑only subscription service offering professionally produced, sub‑10‑minute episodes with A‑list talent and orientation‑agnostic video, targeting users tired of subscription fatigue.

**Attack** – The core hypothesis (“premium short‑form will attract paying subscribers”) is unsupported: no market data shows willingness to pay for high‑budget, brief episodes when free, ad‑supported short clips already saturate phones. High production costs create a cost structure that can only be justified by very high ARPU, yet the proposal offers no pricing model or breakeven analysis.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
1. **Do nothing** – Preserve capital and monitor emerging demand signals.  
2. **Partner with an existing platform** – Leverage its subscriber base and distribution, reducing launch cost and risk.  
3. **Hybrid model** – Offer a lower‑priced, ad‑supported tier to build audience before converting to premium.

Both alternatives cost far less, are reversible, and test demand before committing billions.

**Fit** – The proposal does not advance a known strategic north‑star (e.g., scalable, low‑cost growth). It introduces a high‑burn, low‑evidence product that conflicts with the goal of minimizing subscription fatigue.

**Pre‑mortem** – Likely failure points: (a) subscriber acquisition cost far exceeds LTV; (b) churn > 20 % within months; (c) inability to differentiate from free short‑form content; (d) unsustainable cash burn leading to early shutdown.

**Kill‑criteria** – If CAC > $30 or 6‑month churn > 15 % or revenue < $200 M after 12 months, the project should be halted.

Given the absence of demand evidence, the massive upfront capital requirement, and cheaper, testable paths, the proposal should not be approved as presented.

DECISION: NO-GO
