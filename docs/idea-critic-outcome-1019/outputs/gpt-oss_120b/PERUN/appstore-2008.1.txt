**Steelman** – The phone maker should launch a curated native‑app store, letting any developer publish paid or free apps after a company‑run review, taking a 30 % commission, to expand the device’s capabilities, capture a new revenue stream, and keep pace with rivals that already host thriving app ecosystems.

**Attack & Alternatives**  
1. **Do nothing** – Remain web‑only. This avoids security, carrier, and review‑load risks but cedes the emerging app market to rivals, likely shrinking market share and developer goodwill.  
2. **Open distribution** – Allow developers to sideload apps without review. Cuts review workload and carrier friction but exposes users to malware, undermines product polish, and erodes the brand’s quality reputation.

**Fit** – The company’s stated goal is to “deliver a premium, secure, integrated experience.” A curated store aligns with that, while still addressing the competitive pressure from rival platforms.

**Pre‑mortem** – Failure modes: (a) a malicious app leaks user data, (b) carrier partners block the store over revenue‑sharing concerns, (c) review backlog exceeds 30 days, delaying critical updates, (d) a flood of low‑quality apps dilutes the brand. All are two‑way doors; each can be reversed by tightening review criteria, pausing new submissions, or temporarily disabling the store.

**Evidence** – The proposal already cites three concrete concerns; no external data needed. The 30 % cut mirrors successful models elsewhere, providing a proven revenue baseline.

**Kill‑criteria** – Stop the store if any of the following occurs within the first 6 months: (i) > 5 % of released apps are flagged for security violations, (ii) average review time > 45 days, (iii) carrier complaints lead to ≥ 2 major contract renegotiations, or (iv) net promoter score drops > 10 points after launch. Each trigger mandates an immediate pause on new app approvals and a post‑mortem.

**Remaining Risk** – Moderate: initial review load and carrier negotiations, but mitigable with clear SOPs and a small dedicated review team.

**Decision** – The strategic upside outweighs the manageable risks, provided the kill‑criteria are enforced.

DECISION: GO
