**Steelman:** Launch a low‑priced, direct‑to‑consumer streaming service, funded by sustained operating losses, to capture tens of millions of subscribers within five years and shift revenue from licensing to subscription.

**Key Risks & Concrete Failure Scenarios**  
1. **Licensing revenue drop > $X billion:** If 30 % of current license contracts are terminated within year 1, cash flow turns negative faster than projected, jeopardizing other business units.  
2. **Technology platform failure:** Without prior streaming experience, a platform outage affecting > 5 % of users in month 6 triggers churn > 15 %, making the subscriber target unreachable.  
3. **Cannibalisation:** Pulling content from rivals cuts cable‑and‑theatrical ancillary revenue by an estimated 10 % in year 2, eroding profit margins and reducing funds available for the new service.

**Better‑Way Alternatives**  
- **Hybrid model:** Retain licensing while gradually rolling out a premium tier on an existing, proven platform (e.g., partner with a cloud streaming provider). This limits cash burn and preserves licensing cash flow.  
- **Phased geographic launch:** Start in a single market, validate technology and subscriber acquisition cost before scaling globally.  
- **License‑to‑own transition:** Negotiate profit‑share deals with existing streaming partners to test brand pull‑through without full platform build.

**Fit with Strategy**  
The move aligns with a long‑term goal of direct consumer relationships but conflicts with the short‑term objective of maintaining steady cash flow and protecting cable/theatrical revenue streams.

**Pre‑mortem & Door Test**  
If subscriber growth stalls at < 5 % of target after two years, the initiative becomes a two‑way door: cease further investment and revert to licensing. The cost of continued loss is bounded by a $500 million cash‑burn ceiling set for year 3.

**Kill Criteria**  
- **Subscriber KPI:** < 3 million net new subs by end of year 2 → halt rollout.  
- **Cash‑burn limit:** > $500 million cumulative loss by year 3 → terminate.  
- **Tech reliability:** > 2 % monthly downtime in any quarter → pause expansion.

**Decision:** The proposal’s upside is outweighed by unmitigated revenue loss, technology risk, and insufficient kill criteria. A revised, phased approach is required before committing full resources.  

DECISION: NO-GO
