**Steelman** – The acquisition would give the sports‑drink maker a fast‑growing premium RTD tea/juice brand, instantly adding a high‑margin, health‑focused portfolio. Leveraging its existing supermarket distribution and mass‑market advertising could accelerate the brand’s reach, diversify revenue away from sports drinks, and create cross‑selling opportunities.

**Attack & Risks**  
1. **Price** – $1.7 bn cash is a large premium for a brand whose sales base is still niche; without clear cash‑flow forecasts the pay‑back period is uncertain.  
2. **Distributor loyalty** – The target relies on a quirky, independent distributor network. Shifting to the buyer’s centralized supermarket channel may alienate those partners, risking a supply‑chain disruption and loss of shelf space.  
3. **Brand dilution** – The cult‑like image that fuels consumer loyalty could be eroded by corporate mass‑marketing, reducing the premium pricing power that justifies the acquisition price.  
4. **Competitive pressure** – Large soft‑drink firms are launching their own tea lines; the target could quickly lose market share if it cannot differentiate under a corporate umbrella.

**Better‑Way Alternatives**  
- **Do nothing** – Preserve cash and avoid integration risk; continue organic growth in the sports‑drink core.  
- **Strategic partnership or licensing** – Gain distribution upside without full ownership, keeping the independent distributor model intact.  
- **Acquire a smaller, less‑integrated brand** – Lower cash outlay, easier cultural fit, and still achieve diversification.

**Fit with Strategy** – Diversification aligns with the company’s long‑term vision of a broader “active‑lifestyle” portfolio, but the move diverts focus from the core sports‑drink business and may strain resources.

**Premortem** – If distributor pull‑back cuts sales >20 % within 12 months, brand perception drops (NPS falls >10 points), or competition captures >15 % of the category, the acquisition would become a drag on earnings.

**Kill‑Criteria** – Abort if (a) post‑close EBITDA contribution falls below 8 % of purchase price within two years, or (b) the brand’s market share declines >10 % YoY after integration.

**Recommendation** – The upside is attractive but the risks—especially price, distributor loyalty, and brand dilution—are insufficiently quantified. Proceed only after a deeper financial and channel‑integration diligence phase.

DECISION: NO-GO
