**Steelman:** The buyer should acquire the mobile photo‑sharing app for $1 billion in cash and stock now, to secure a fast‑growing user base, eliminate a competitive threat, and instantly gain a market‑leading mobile product that can be integrated into its own photo portfolio.

**Attack & Assumptions:**  
- *Valuation:* $1 bn for a pre‑revenue company implies a price‑to‑sales multiple of > 100× any future revenue, far above comparable tech deals.  
- *Business model:* No proven monetisation; growth alone does not guarantee profitability.  
- *Buyer’s mobile capability:* The buyer’s own mobile photo offering is weak, suggesting integration risk and possible cannibalisation without clear roadmap.  
- *Regulatory:* A $1 bn cash‑stock deal will attract antitrust scrutiny, potentially delaying or blocking closure.  
- *Culture:* A small, founder‑led team may be disrupted by a large corporate owner, risking talent loss and user‑experience degradation.

**Better‑Ways (Alternatives):**  
1. **Strategic partnership or minority stake** – share technology and data while preserving culture, at a lower cash outlay.  
2. **Earn‑out structure** – tie a large portion of the price to achieving defined revenue or user‑engagement milestones, reducing upfront risk.  
3. **Wait for revenue traction** – monitor the app’s monetisation rollout for 12 months; if successful, a later acquisition would command a lower multiple.

**Fit with Strategy:** The buyer’s north‑star is to dominate mobile photo sharing. Acquiring the app could accelerate this, but only if the deal does not jeopardise financial health or regulatory approval.

**Pre‑mortem / Kill Criteria:**  
- If post‑close revenue < $50 m in 12 months, trigger a divestiture clause.  
- If antitrust review exceeds 6 months, abort.  
- If > 30 % of the founding team departs within 3 months, consider the cultural risk realised and unwind.

**Conclusion:** The proposal’s upside is outweighed by valuation, model, and integration risks. A phased or performance‑based approach would mitigate exposure.

DECISION: NO‑GO
