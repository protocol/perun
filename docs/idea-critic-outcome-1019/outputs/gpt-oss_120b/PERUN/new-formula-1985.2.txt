**Steelman** – Replace the century‑old flagship cola with a sweeter formula that outperforms both the current product and the rival in blind taste tests, surveys and focus groups, aiming to stop market‑share loss.

**Attack & Better‑Way**  
1. **Historical precedent** – In 1985 the same company launched “New Coke” and withdrew the original; the move triggered massive consumer backlash, brand‑heritage damage and a sharp sales dip before “Coke Classic” was re‑introduced. The proposal repeats that error.  
2. **One‑way vs two‑way door** – Removing the original is a one‑way door for existing loyal customers; while the formula can be re‑produced, the brand‑trust loss is hard to quantify and may be permanent.  
3. **Fallback missing** – No parallel product means no safety net if the new taste is rejected. A dual‑line launch (new formula alongside the classic) would let the market self‑select, limit exposure, and preserve heritage.  
4. **Bottler risk** – Bottlers have long‑term contracts tied to the classic brand. Sudden withdrawal could breach agreements, cause supply chain disruption, and raise legal costs.  
5. **Emotional attachment** – Brand heritage is a core intangible asset; removing it removes a differentiator that many consumers still value, especially in niche segments (e.g., “original taste” enthusiasts).

**Kill‑Criteria** – Approve only if:  
- Pre‑launch pilot shows ≤ 5 % drop in sales in test markets within 3 months;  
- Bottler contracts remain intact (no legal disputes); and  
- Consumer sentiment surveys stay ≥ 80 % positive for the new formula.

**Recommendation** – Do not approve the full withdrawal now. Instead, launch the sweeter formula as a secondary product while retaining the classic, run controlled market tests, and establish clear rollback triggers.

**Decision** – The risks outweigh the potential upside without a fallback and without proven acceptance beyond blind tests.

DECISION: NO‑GO
