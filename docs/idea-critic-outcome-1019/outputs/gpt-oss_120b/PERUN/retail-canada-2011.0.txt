**Steelman (strongest defensible claim)**  
The retailer will lease 130‑150 existing sites in the neighboring country, open almost all within two years, and operate them with a newly built cross‑border supply chain and a brand‑new inventory‑management system, aiming to capture market share from the entrenched local leader despite higher local prices.

**Key assumptions**  
1. Sufficient demand exists at price points comparable to the U.S. model.  
2. The new supply‑chain and inventory system can be deployed at scale within 12 months.  
3. Regulatory, customs and bilingual‑labeling requirements will not cause costly delays.  

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Phased rollout:** start with 20‑30 pilot stores, validate logistics, then scale.  
- **Acquisition or joint venture:** buy an existing local chain to inherit proven logistics and brand recognition.  
- **Hybrid model:** use a mix of owned and franchised locations to reduce capital exposure.

**Fit with corporate strategy**  
International growth is a stated objective, but the aggressive two‑year “all‑at‑once” schedule conflicts with the company’s risk‑averse capital‑allocation policy.

**Pre‑mortem (worst‑case scenario)**  
Supply‑chain bottlenecks cause frequent stock‑outs, eroding the retailer’s reputation. Regulatory fines arise from labeling errors. Delayed openings increase cost per square foot, while the incumbent sustains price‑lead advantage, resulting in <2 % market share after the first year and a sunk‑cost exposure of >$500 M.

**Kill‑criteria (go/no‑go triggers)**  
- >10 % of stores miss the 12‑month opening deadline.  
- Supply‑chain KPI (on‑time delivery) falls below 85 % during the first six months.  
- Average basket price is >5 % higher than the incumbent, leading to <3 % market share after 12 months.  

**Recommendation**  
Given the unproven logistics platform, the regulatory learning curve, and the high price‑perception risk in a market dominated by a larger competitor, the proposed “launch‑all‑stores‑within‑two‑years” plan exceeds the company’s acceptable risk threshold. A phased pilot or acquisition strategy would mitigate these risks.

**Decision**  
DECISION: NO-GO
