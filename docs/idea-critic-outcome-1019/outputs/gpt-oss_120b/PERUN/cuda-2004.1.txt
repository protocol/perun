**Steelman:** The company will embed general‑purpose compute units, a full compiler, and a software stack into every consumer GPU, raising per‑chip cost, to seed a future scientific‑computing market that currently lacks demand.

**Skeptic assumptions:**  
- Assume a 5 % unit‑cost increase will be absorbed without harming price‑sensitive gamers.  
- Assume university researchers will adopt the new API despite existing hacks.  
- Assume engineering effort will not delay the next gaming‑performance generation.

**Better‑way alternatives:**  
1. Launch a separate “compute‑focused” GPU line for workstations where higher cost is justified.  
2. Partner with existing HPC vendors to add optional compute modules rather than redesign every consumer chip.  
3. Delay the program until a clear market signal (e.g., a major scientific application demanding GPU acceleration) appears.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem):**  
- If cost rise > 3 % of the retail price, abort.  
- If by Q4 2006 fewer than 10 % of university pilots adopt the stack, abort.  
- If the gaming‑performance roadmap slips by more than one product cycle, abort.

**Strongest attack survived:** The cost‑increase assumption is the weakest link; a 5 % rise would likely reduce gamer adoption, eroding the core revenue that funds R&D. No evidence was provided that the market can bear this premium now, so the attack fails and the proposal does not survive.

**Dissent ledger:** The proposal ignores cheaper, lower‑risk paths (alternatives 1‑3) and lacks concrete, measurable kill thresholds tied to current margins.

**Remaining risk:** High – market may not materialize; engineering focus may be diverted from the core competitive battle.

DECISION: NO-GO
