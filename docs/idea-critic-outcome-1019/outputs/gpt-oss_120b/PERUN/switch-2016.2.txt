**Steelman:** Launch a modest‑power hybrid console at rival price, with detachable motion controllers and a thin first‑year lineup from internal studios, to revive sales after a failed home console and a handheld line losing ground to phones.

**Attacks & Findings**

1. **Hardware‑vs‑Rivals:** Rivals’ consoles are markedly more powerful. A modest‑power chip will limit graphics, frame‑rates and future‑proofing, making the product unattractive to core gamers who drive console sales.  
2. **Brand Confusion:** The previous console’s launch was “confusing”; repeating a hybrid concept without clear value‑propagation risks repeating that perception.  
3. **Third‑Party Ecosystem:** Limited third‑party support is a known driver of console success. With only internal titles, the library will appear thin, reducing purchase incentive.  
4. **Supply Constraints:** Early‑stage hybrid hardware often faces component shortages (e.g., detachable controllers, dock connectors). A short launch window leaves little time to address shortages.  
5. **Recovery Window:** Recovering from a loss‑making console in a single fiscal year is overly aggressive; cash‑flow pressures could force price cuts or rushed quality compromises.

**Alternatives (including “do nothing”)**

- **Do Nothing / Focus on Handheld‑Phone Integration:** Preserve cash, invest in mobile games, leverage existing phone market. Low cost, low risk, but forfeits console market.  
- **Partner‑Powered Mid‑Tier Console:** Co‑develop with a hardware partner to share risk, obtain a stronger chip, and gain third‑party confidence. Higher upfront cost but better specs and ecosystem.  
- **Iterate Existing Handheld with Premium Accessories:** Upgrade the handheld with detachable controllers, keeping the platform familiar while testing market appetite. Cheaper, less brand confusion.

**Fit with Strategy:** The company seeks to re‑enter the home‑console space, but the core north‑star is sustainable revenue. A weak, rushed hybrid mis‑aligns with long‑term brand credibility.

**Pre‑mortem (One‑Way Door):** If launch sales fall <15 % of rival’s first‑year sales, the project can be halted without sunk‑cost recovery (one‑way door). If third‑party titles <5 in year‑one, abort.

**Kill‑Criteria (proposed):**  
- Sales <15 % of rival’s first‑year unit volume by Q4.  
- Third‑party titles <5 by launch.  
- Supply shortfall >20 % of forecasted units.  

**Conclusion:** Risks outweigh the modest price advantage; the plan lacks sufficient hardware differentiation and ecosystem assurance to meet recovery goals.

DECISION: NO-GO
