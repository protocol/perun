**Steelman** – Acquire the animation studio for $7.4 bn (all‑stock) and install its creative leaders to run the conglomerate’s animation division, preserving the partnership pipeline and preventing a rival from gaining the asset.

**Attack & Concerns**  
1. **Valuation mismatch** – The target produces one feature a year; at a typical EBITDA multiple of 12‑15× for a $300 m‑$400 m annual franchise, a fair price is $3‑5 bn. The $7.4 bn ask implies a 20‑30× premium, eroding shareholder value.  
2. **Revenue volatility** – One‑film‑a‑year output creates cash‑flow risk; a flop could wipe out a large portion of the purchase price in a single year.  
3. **Cultural integration** – Hand‑drawn and computer‑animation cultures differ in pipeline, talent management, and risk appetite. Past attempts at cross‑studio integration have produced talent exodus and morale loss.  
4. **Leadership concentration** – Placing a few creative leaders at the helm concentrates decision‑making and creates a “founder‑risk” profile; loss of any leader would destabilise the combined unit.  
5. **Shareholder dynamics** – The founder becoming a major shareholder introduces a potential activist block, complicating governance and future strategic moves.  

**Better‑Way Alternatives**  
- **Strategic minority stake** (≤30 %) to retain upside while limiting exposure.  
- **Co‑production renewal with profit‑share clauses** to address economics without full acquisition.  
- **Joint venture with shared governance**, preserving independence of both studios.  

Both alternatives cost significantly less, preserve flexibility, and mitigate cultural clash.

**Fit with Corporate Strategy** – The conglomerate seeks diversified, repeatable revenue streams. A single‑film, high‑cost asset misaligns with that goal; the risk‑adjusted return is inferior to expanding existing hand‑drawn pipelines or investing in emerging CGI capabilities internally.

**Pre‑mortem** – If the first post‑acquisition film underperforms, the conglomerate must write‑off >30 % of the purchase price, trigger shareholder dissent, and face integration costs exceeding $1 bn. This is a two‑way‑door decision with a high cost of reversal.

**Kill‑Criteria** – Proceed only if: (a) valuation ≤ $5 bn; (b) at least two independent creative leaders are contractually guaranteed for 5 years; (c) governance safeguards limit the founder’s voting power to ≤ 15 %. None are met.

Given the inflated price, revenue concentration, cultural risk, and lack of protective clauses, the proposal fails the economic and strategic thresholds.

DECISION: NO-GO
