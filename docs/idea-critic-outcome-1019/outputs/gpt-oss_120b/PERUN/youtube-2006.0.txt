**Steelman:** Acquiring the 20‑month‑old video‑sharing platform for $1.65 B in stock gives the buyer instant market dominance, a massive user base, and a content library that can be leveraged for advertising revenue, while eliminating a direct competitor to its own smaller service.

**Skeptic attacks**
- **Valuation:** $1.65 B exceeds any reasonable multiple for a company with zero meaningful revenue and only a thin staff. Comparable early‑stage internet firms were valued far lower.
- **Litigation exposure:** The site hosts massive amounts of copyrighted TV/film clips; ongoing threats from media companies could translate into multi‑hundred‑million dollar settlements or injunctions that cripple the service.
- **Bandwidth cost:** At scale, bandwidth alone could consume a large portion of cash flow, especially without a proven monetisation model.
- **Integration risk:** Merging a high‑traffic platform with the buyer’s smaller service may create technical and cultural friction, potentially alienating existing users.

**Better‑way alternatives**
1. **Build internally:** Continue expanding the buyer’s own video service; it avoids the litigation baggage and lets the company test ad models on a controllable scale.
2. **Strategic partnership/licensing:** License the larger site’s technology or user base for a revenue‑share, limiting exposure while gaining market insight.
3. **Do nothing:** Preserve capital and wait for the market to mature; many early video sites failed under similar pressures.

**Kill‑criteria**
- Any lawsuit that results in a settlement > $200 M.
- Bandwidth costs exceeding 30 % of projected cash flow within 12 months.
- Failure to generate ≥ $100 M annual ad revenue by the end of year 2.

Given the high price, unquantified legal risk, and lack of revenue, the deal fails multiple kill‑criteria and cheaper, lower‑risk paths exist.  

**Conclusion:** The proposal should not be approved in its current form.  

DECISION: NO-GO
