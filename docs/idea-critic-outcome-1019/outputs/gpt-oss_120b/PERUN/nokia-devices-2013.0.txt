**Steelman** – Acquire the handset maker for €5.4 bn to unite hardware and software, lock the OEM into the company’s OS, and halt its drift toward the dominant rival platform.

**Skeptic attacks**  
1. **App‑ecosystem problem** – Owning hardware does not create apps; developers choose platforms based on user base and revenue potential. The proposal offers no mechanism to attract or retain them.  
2. **Integration risk** – Merging a large, declining handset operation brings cultural, operational, and cost‑overrun hazards that historically erode value.  
3. **Competitor reaction** – Other OEMs may accelerate migration to the rival OS, shrinking the company’s remaining market share further.  
4. **Financial upside** – Falling handset sales give little upside to recoup a €5.4 bn outlay; without a clear path to revenue growth, ROI is doubtful.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Developer incentives** – Directly fund SDKs, app‑store subsidies, and cross‑platform tools to grow the app catalog.  
- **Strategic partnership** – Share risk through a joint venture or revenue‑share agreement with the OEM rather than full ownership.  
- **Niche focus** – Target enterprise or emerging‑market devices where the OS can command a premium without needing mass‑market hardware.

**Fit** – The core competence is software; diverting resources to a large hardware business misaligns with the company’s strategic north‑star.

**Pre‑mortem** – Integration stalls, costs exceed budget, handset sales continue to fall, and the OS share drops, leading to a multi‑billion‑euro write‑down.

**Kill criteria** – If handset sales decline >10 % YoY within 12 months post‑close *or* app‑store growth <5 % YoY, trigger divestiture.

Given the high integration risk, lack of a clear app‑ecosystem solution, and cheaper, lower‑risk alternatives, the proposal should not proceed as presented.

DECISION: NO-GO
