**Steelman** – The company will design, source, and ship a 5 GB hard‑drive pocket music player in <12 months for $400, bundling only its own PC music software and targeting its small‑share computer owners.

**Attack**  
1. **Addressable market** – With <5 % PC market share, the compatible base is ≈200 k units (assuming 4 M PCs sold). Even a 10 % penetration yields 20 k units, far below the 100 k+ volumes needed to amortize tooling and HDD inventory.  
2. **Price vs. competition** – Competing flash‑based players sell for $150–$250. A $400 HDD device offers no clear advantage (larger capacity is irrelevant for most users) and will be perceived as overpriced.  
3. **Component risk** – Early‑2001 HDDs for sub‑inch devices have long lead times and high failure rates. Any supply delay pushes the launch past the 12‑month window, inflating cost and eroding market relevance.  
4. **Software lock‑in** – Requiring proprietary software eliminates the large pool of users who already own iTunes‑compatible MP3 players, further shrinking demand.

**Better‑Way** –  
- Use flash memory (≈256 MB–1 GB) to cut cost, size, and supply risk.  
- Release a driver for Windows 98/2000 (and possibly Mac) to broaden the user base.  
- Partner with an established music‑software vendor to add value beyond the OS bundle.

**Kill‑Criteria** (pre‑mortem): abort if any of the following occurs before launch:  
- Launch delayed >12 months.  
- Retail price >$350.  
- Forecasted addressable units <50 k.

**Remaining Risk** – Even with flash, the company still lacks brand credibility in consumer audio and must compete against entrenched players. Without a clear differentiation or licensing ecosystem, the venture is likely to lose money.

Given the high price, tiny compatible base, and component‑sourcing risk, the proposal does not meet a viable business case.

DECISION: NO-GO
