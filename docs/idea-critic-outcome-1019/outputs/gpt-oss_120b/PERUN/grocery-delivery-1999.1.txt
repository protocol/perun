**Steelman:** The startup will spend ~ $1 billion on massive, highly automated regional warehouses and a delivery‑truck fleet, opening dozens of new metros quickly, before it has validated the unit economics of its first market.

**Attack:**  
- **Capital intensity vs. thin margins:** Grocery delivery operates on 1‑3 % margins; a $1 bn outlay will generate massive cash‑burn before breakeven.  
- **Customer‑density requirement:** A 30‑minute slot demands dense order volumes; without proven density the warehouses will be under‑utilized, raising per‑order costs dramatically.  
- **No operating track record:** No data exist on order size, pick‑rate, or delivery cost in the pilot city, making any long‑term ROI forecast speculative.  
- **Rapid expansion risk:** Scaling before learning creates a “build‑then‑learn” trap; mis‑steps will be amplified across all sites, threatening the entire capital base.

**Better‑way alternatives:**  
1. **Lean pilot:** Lease a modest fulfillment space in the first city, use existing third‑party logistics, and iterate on slot pricing and density targets.  
2. **Phased rollout:** Commit only to a single additional city after the pilot meets a predefined profitability threshold (e.g., > 5 % contribution margin).  
3. **Asset-light model:** Partner with regional warehouses to avoid upfront construction costs while still testing market demand.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem):** Halt further build‑out if, after 12 months in the first metro, (a) average contribution margin remains < 2 %, (b) order density is < 15 orders / km² / day, or (c) cash‑burn exceeds 30 % of total capital.

Given the extreme financial exposure without validated economics, the prudent course is to defer the billion‑dollar build‑out until the pilot proves sustainable.

DECISION: NO-GO
