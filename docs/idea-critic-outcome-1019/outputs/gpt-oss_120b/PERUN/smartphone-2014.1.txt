**Steelman:** Launch a high‑end smartphone (unsubsidised price comparable to flagship devices) sold only through a single U.S. carrier, featuring a 3D‑like display using front‑facing cameras and head‑tracking, plus a one‑touch product‑identification system. The device runs a forked OS with a reduced app store, omitting the dominant platforms’ mainstream apps.

**Attack & Failure Scenario:**  
If the phone’s price matches flagship levels but its only unique selling point is a gimmicky 3D display, early adopters will compare it against established Android and iOS devices that offer mature ecosystems, proven cameras, and broader carrier availability. A concrete failure would be <5 % market share in the carrier’s handset sales within six months, driven by (a) price‑to‑value mismatch, (b) missing essential apps (e.g., major social, navigation, and payment apps), and (c) limited distribution (single carrier). The company would incur sunk‑costs in hardware, OS fork, and carrier partnership without recouping R&D.

**Better‑Way Alternatives:**  
1. **Software‑only approach:** Release the 3D display and product‑ID features as an app for existing Android/iOS devices, leveraging their app stores and user bases.  
2. **Carrier‑agnostic hardware:** Partner with multiple carriers or sell unlocked, lowering distribution risk while keeping the OS fork minimal.  
3. **Focus on accessories:** Offer a detachable 3D camera module for existing phones, testing market appetite with lower capital outlay.

**Fit with Strategy:** The retailer’s core competency is content delivery, not handset manufacturing. Diverting resources to a full‑stack smartphone diverges from its proven e‑reader/tablet niche and strains cash flow.

**Kill Criteria (pre‑mortem):** Abort if (i) carrier sales contribution <5 % of handset volume by Q2 2015, (ii) net‑present‑value of the project <0 after 12 months, or (iii) critical app gaps identified by a user‑testing panel exceed 3 major categories.

Given the high financial exposure, weak differentiation, and ecosystem deficiencies, the proposal should not proceed as written.

DECISION: NO‑GO
