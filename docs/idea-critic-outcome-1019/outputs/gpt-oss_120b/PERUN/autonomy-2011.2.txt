**Steelman:** The acquisition would instantly give the hardware firm a mature analytics platform, a foothold in enterprise software, and a revenue stream that could offset declining hardware sales. A premium is justified to secure market share quickly and to signal a decisive shift to shareholders.

**Attack:**  
- **Price:** A 64 % premium (≈ 11× revenue) far exceeds typical software‑industry multiples (≈ 3–5×). Even a high‑growth target rarely commands > 6×.  
- **Accounting quality:** The target’s financials have been questioned since 2009; unresolved restatements could materially alter earnings and cash flow.  
- **Integration risk:** The buyer lacks software integration experience; past hardware‑to‑software pivots have shown > 30 % failure rates due to cultural clash and product overlap.  
- **Strategic fit:** A single large bolt‑on may distract from building internal capabilities and could lock the buyer into legacy codebases.

**Better‑way alternatives:**  
1. **Strategic partnership or minority stake** – lower cash outlay, shared risk, and a test‑bed for integration.  
2. **Smaller, proven software acquisition** – e.g., a niche analytics firm at 2–3× revenue, reducing premium and integration complexity.  
3. **Organic development** – leverage existing R&D budget to build analytics tools, preserving capital and aligning culture.

**Kill‑criteria (must be satisfied before proceeding):**  
- Premium ≤ 30 % above market price *or* clear ROI > 15 % within 3 years.  
- Clean audited financials with no material restatements.  
- Demonstrated integration plan with a dedicated software leadership team and a ≤ 20 % projected synergy risk.  

**Current status:** Due diligence is still weeks away; the key kill‑criteria are unmet. Proceeding now would lock in a high‑cost, high‑risk transaction without sufficient evidence of value.

**Conclusion:** The proposal should not be approved in its present form.

DECISION: NO-GO
