**Steelman** – Launch a company‑wide social network integrated across search, mail, video and photo services, using invite‑only beta, friend‑group circles and video hangouts; executive bonuses linked to adoption.

**Attack & Gaps**  
1. **Network‑effect barrier** – The incumbent already has hundreds of millions of users; without a critical mass, adoption will stall. No clear path to bootstrap users beyond invites.  
2. **Historical failure** – Three prior social attempts failed; the same product‑market mismatch is likely to repeat unless the core value proposition is fundamentally different.  
3. **Forced integration** – Embedding a social layer into existing services risks alienating current users who value those services for productivity, not social interaction.  
4. **Incentive misalignment** – Executive bonuses tied to adoption may pressure teams to inflate metrics or push unwanted features, increasing churn.  
5. **Differentiators weak** – “Friend‑group circles” and “video hangouts” are now standard on most platforms; they do not constitute a sustainable moat.

**Alternatives**  
- **Do nothing** – Preserve focus on core search and mail strengths.  
- **Partner or acquire** – Leverage an existing niche social platform to gain users without building from scratch.  
- **Limited feature rollout** – Introduce social widgets only where they add clear value (e.g., collaborative document sharing) rather than a full network.

**Fit** – The proposal diverts resources from the company’s core competency (search) and conflicts with the strategic emphasis on user trust and privacy.

**Pre‑mortem** – If launch fails, the brand suffers credibility loss, user churn from existing services, and wasted engineering effort. The effort is a two‑way door only if the integration can be cleanly rolled back, which is unlikely given deep product coupling.

**Kill‑criteria** – Abort if early beta adoption <5 % of invitees or if churn in core services exceeds 2 % within three months.

Given the high risk, weak differentiation, and misaligned incentives, the proposal should not be approved in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
