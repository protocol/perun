**Steelman:** Acquiring the startup gives the search‑advertising firm early control of a potential mobile OS that could become the de‑facto platform for web browsing, driving massive search‑ad impressions as smartphones proliferate.

**Key Assumptions Tested**
1. Open‑source software will accelerate mobile web usage fast enough to boost search‑ad revenue within a few years.  
2. Handset makers and carriers will adopt a new OS despite entrenched relationships with existing vendors.  
3. The acquired team can deliver a competitive, carrier‑ready OS within 2‑3 years.

**Skeptic Findings**
- **Revenue Model Gap:** No clear monetisation path; the OS is free, and the acquirer’s ad revenue depends on user‑level traffic that is uncertain without a carrier‑driven distribution channel.  
- **Adoption Barrier:** In 2005 the handset market was dominated by Symbian, Windows Mobile, and early iPhone/Android concepts; carriers exercised strong gate‑keeping power and were reluctant to support untested platforms.  
- **Time‑to‑Value:** Even an optimistic 3‑year development timeline yields a payback horizon of 5‑7 years, exceeding typical M&A ROI thresholds for a $50 M spend.  
- **Competitive Landscape:** Larger players (e.g., Google, Microsoft) were already exploring mobile OS strategies, making a six‑person startup a weak bargaining chip.

**Better‑Way Alternatives**
- Form strategic partnerships or licensing deals with existing OS vendors rather than full acquisition.  
- Invest in internal mobile‑web initiatives (APIs, responsive design) that do not depend on OS control.  
- Allocate funds to a venture portfolio to monitor emerging platforms and acquire only after market traction is evident.

**Kill‑Criteria Triggered**
- No validated revenue model within 12 months.  
- No carrier commitment or OEM roadmap within 18 months.  
- Projected payback >5 years.

Given the high adoption risk, undefined monetisation, and long payback, the proposal fails the critical kill criteria.

DECISION: NO-GO
