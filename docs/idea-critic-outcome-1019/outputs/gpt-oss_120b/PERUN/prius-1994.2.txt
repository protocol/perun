**Steelman:** The automaker will launch a volume‑produced petrol‑electric hybrid by 1997, using a novel power‑split transmission and NiMH battery pack, securing first‑mover advantage, capturing a new market segment, and establishing a platform for future electrified models.

**Attack:**  
1. **Battery durability** – NiMH packs have no proven long‑term cycle life in automotive use; early failures would drive warranty costs and damage brand reputation.  
2. **Cost risk** – Novel transmission and untested battery integration are likely to raise unit cost well above conventional models, eroding price competitiveness.  
3. **Demand uncertainty** – Cheap fuel in the export market removes a price incentive for hybrids; without regulatory pressure or clear consumer interest, pre‑orders are speculative.  
4. **Warranty exposure** – Unproven reliability of both the transmission and battery could generate high warranty claim rates, threatening profitability.

**Better‑way alternatives:**  
- **Limited pilot program** (e.g., 5 000 units) to validate battery life and transmission reliability before scaling.  
- **Partnership with battery supplier** to secure performance guarantees and share development risk.  
- **Delay full‑scale launch** until a regulatory mandate or fuel price shift creates a clearer demand signal.

**Kill‑criteria (quantitative):**  
- Projected hybrid unit cost > 30 % above comparable petrol model.  
- Expected warranty claim rate > 5 % of hybrids within the first 24 months.  
- Pre‑launch market survey indicating < 10 000 firm orders for the first year.

**Evidence check:** All risk factors rely on assumptions (battery lifespan, cost overruns, demand). No hard data or supplier commitments are presented; thus the assumptions remain unverified.

**Conclusion:** Given the high technical and market uncertainty, the proposal lacks sufficient risk mitigation and quantitative justification to proceed to full mass production by 1997. A more prudent approach is a staged rollout with supplier risk sharing.

DECISION: NO-GO
