**Steelman (strongest defensible claim)**  
The carrier can leverage its massive mobile subscriber base and its recent acquisition of a legacy media‑advertising firm to turn the declining portal into a scalable mobile‑first ad platform, achieving the scale needed to challenge the dominant Google/Facebook duopoly.

**Attack – key objections**  

1. **Assumption of traffic rebound** – The portal’s traffic and ad revenue have been falling for years despite multiple management changes. No evidence shows that carrier‑owned mobile data or the legacy media asset can reverse this trend.  
2. **Synergy over‑estimate** – The carrier’s expertise lies in network operations, not content creation or programmatic sales. Integrating a media‑centric business requires capabilities (creative services, data‑science, sales relationships) it does not possess.  
3. **Valuation risk** – $4.8 bn for a declining asset implies a multiple far above industry norms for a business with negative growth.  
4. **Integration cost** – Past media acquisitions have incurred integration expenses of 12‑18 % of purchase price; with two recent deals, the carrier’s integration bandwidth is already stretched.

**Better‑Way alternatives**  

- **Do‑nothing**: Preserve capital and avoid a risky integration.  
- **Acquire a healthier ad‑tech firm** (e.g., a programmatic platform with growing revenue) at a lower multiple.  
- **Partner or co‑market** with existing ad networks to test mobile‑first formats before committing billions.

**Fit with strategy** – The carrier’s core mission is network growth and subscriber services. A deep‑media play diverges from its proven strengths and may dilute focus.

**Pre‑mortem (one‑way‑door)** – If ad revenue continues to drop >10 % YoY after 12 months, the acquisition becomes a sunk cost with limited upside, and the carrier must write off the investment.

**Kill‑criteria** – Abort if (a) post‑integration ad revenue decline >10 % YoY, (b) integration costs exceed 15 % of purchase price, or (c) ROI horizon extends beyond five years.

Given the high valuation, lack of media expertise, and superior alternatives, the proposal fails the risk‑return test.

DECISION: NO-GO
