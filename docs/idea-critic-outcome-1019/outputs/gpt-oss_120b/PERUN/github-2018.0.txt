**Steelman:** Acquire the code‑hosting service for $7.5 bn in stock, keep it independent, and leverage its community to accelerate the buyer’s open‑source pivot.

**Skeptic attacks**  
1. **Valuation vs cash‑flow:** The target is loss‑making; paying a premium in stock assumes future profitability that is unproven. Without a CEO, turnaround risk is high.  
2. **Community backlash:** Vocal distrust can trigger mass migration to free alternatives, eroding the very network the buyer seeks.  
3. **Regulatory exposure:** Antitrust bodies have recently challenged large tech acquisitions; a dominant host changes market dynamics, raising the probability of a block or costly divestiture.  
4. **Integration uncertainty:** Even with an “independent” carve‑out, stock‑based deals create governance entanglements that can slow decision‑making and dilute culture.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- Form a strategic partnership or minority investment, preserving the target’s autonomy while testing integration.  
- Contribute resources to open‑source tooling, building goodwill without a costly acquisition.  
- Acquire a smaller, profitable niche host to gain assets without the antitrust spotlight.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**  
- If developer activity falls >30 % within 12 months post‑close, unwind the deal.  
- If regulatory review imposes conditions that increase cost >15 % of the purchase price, abort.  
- If the target’s quarterly burn exceeds $200 M without a roadmap to profitability, trigger a divestiture clause.

**Remaining risk:** Even with safeguards, the combination of community defection, uncertain turnaround, and antitrust risk makes the upside speculative relative to the $7.5 bn price tag.

**Decision:** The proposal should not proceed until the buyer secures a clear path to profitability, resolves leadership vacuum, and obtains firm regulatory clearance.

DECISION: NO-GO
