**Steelman claim**: Acquiring the handset division for €5.4 bn will give the OS company full control of both hardware and software, lock the manufacturer into its platform, and create a vertically integrated ecosystem that can reverse the decline in market share and protect against migration to the rival platform.

**Skeptic attacks**  
1. **App‑ecosystem mismatch** – The core weakness is a sparse app catalog, not hardware ownership. Buying a handset maker does not automatically attract developers or increase app availability. Without a clear plan to grow the ecosystem, the acquisition will not solve the strategic problem.  
2. **Integration cost and cultural clash** – Merging a large, possibly demoralized workforce into a software‑centric company is costly and risky. The €5.4 bn price likely underestimates post‑deal restructuring, tooling, and retention expenses.  
3. **Competitive retaliation** – Other handset makers may accelerate their shift to the rival platform, reduce component supply, or launch price wars, eroding any marginal gain from the purchase.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- Form a strategic partnership or joint venture with the handset maker, sharing risk while preserving flexibility.  
- Invest directly in developer incentives, SDK improvements, and cross‑platform app tools to grow the app base without hardware acquisition.  
- Build a new, lean flagship device internally to showcase the platform, avoiding the baggage of a declining division.

**Kill‑criteria**  
- If post‑acquisition market‑share growth is <2 % within 12 months, abort further integration investment.  
- If integration costs exceed €2 bn beyond the purchase price, trigger a divestiture clause.  
- If competitor retaliation causes a >5 % revenue dip in the next fiscal year, cease further hardware expansion.

**Remaining risk** – High: ecosystem stagnation, integration overruns, and market backlash could outweigh any marginal hardware control benefits.

Given the lack of evidence that hardware ownership fixes the app‑ecosystem deficit and the sizable integration and competitive risks, the proposal should not be approved as presented.

DECISION: NO-GO
