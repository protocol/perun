**Steelman claim** – Splitting the DVD‑by‑mail operation into its own brand and website will let the declining physical‑media business be run independently, while the streaming service can pursue a faster, separate strategy without being tied to the 60 % price hike.

**Skeptic checks (assumptions)**  
1. **Customers will accept two logins/bills** – assumes churn stays low despite added friction.  
2. **Loss of shared ratings/recommendations won’t matter** – assumes each service can sustain engagement without cross‑recommendation boost.  
3. **Streaming can grow without DVD cross‑selling** – assumes the DVD channel no longer drives acquisition for streaming.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Option A:** Keep a single account but add a “DVD‑only” tier with its own pricing; retain shared recommendations and a single bill.  
- **Option B:** Offer a “bundle‑discount” for customers who keep both services together, softening the 60 % increase while still separating management internally.  
- **Option C:** Pilot the brand split in one geographic market, measure churn and support load before a full rollout.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**  
- If projected churn exceeds **10 % of dual‑subscribers** within six months, abort the split.  
- If net revenue per user falls **>5 %** after the change, revert to the integrated model.  
- If support tickets about “login confusion” surpass **2 %** of active users, pause the rollout.

**Concrete failure scenario** – A long‑time subscriber logs into the streaming site, expects to see their DVD queue, finds none, assumes the DVD service has been discontinued, contacts support, and cancels both services. Scaling that behavior to 5 % of the 2 million dual‑subscribers would erase roughly $12 M in annual revenue—far outweighing any operational benefit of brand separation.

**Recommendation** – The proposal carries high risk of customer churn and loss of cross‑service network effects. A safer path is to test a limited‑scope split or introduce a separate DVD‑only tier while preserving shared recommendations and a single billing experience. Until such pilots prove negligible churn, the full brand separation should not be approved.

DECISION: NO-GO
