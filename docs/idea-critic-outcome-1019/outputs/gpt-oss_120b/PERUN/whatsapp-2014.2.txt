**Steelman:** Buying the messaging app for $19 B will instantly give the buyer a massive, active user base, protect its core services from being displaced, and provide a platform for future monetisation and data‑rich services.

**Attack:**  
- **Price:** $19 B is >12× the $1.5 B valuation a year earlier. Even generous growth assumptions cannot justify this premium without a clear revenue upside.  
- **Monetisation:** Current revenue is negligible (a $1 annual fee). No ad model or proven in‑app monetisation exists, so upside is speculative.  
- **Regulation:** Messaging apps face tightening privacy laws (GDPR, CCPA). Integration could expose the buyer to costly compliance work and liability.  
- **Founder‑Retention:** With only ~50 staff, losing founders could cripple product continuity and user trust.

**Better ways:**  
1. **Do nothing** – focus on organic messaging features; lower risk, no cash outlay.  
2. **Strategic partnership or minority stake** – test integration and monetisation before a full buy‑out.  
3. **Acquire a smaller, profitable messaging firm** – lower price, proven revenue.

**Fit with strategy:** The buyer’s goal is to prevent user loss, but the acquisition’s cost‑to‑benefit ratio is unfavorable and misaligned with a disciplined growth approach.

**Pre‑mortem:** If integration stalls, the buyer loses $19 B with no revenue, faces regulator fines, and may see user churn if the app’s experience degrades.

**Kill criteria:** Any scenario where projected incremental revenue < $2 B in 3 years, or where regulatory compliance costs > $500 M, should halt the deal.

Given the disproportionate price, unclear monetisation, regulatory exposure, and founder‑retention risk, the proposal is not justified.

DECISION: NO-GO
