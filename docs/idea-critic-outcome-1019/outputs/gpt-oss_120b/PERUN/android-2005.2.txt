**Steelman claim** – Acquiring the six‑person OS startup for ≥ $50 M will give the search‑advertising company control of an open‑source mobile platform, spur mobile Web usage, and ultimately boost search‑ad revenue.

**Skeptic attacks**  
1. **Adoption barrier** – In 2005 the handset market is locked by a few OS vendors (Symbian, Windows Mobile, Palm) and by carriers that dictate which devices reach consumers. Convincing handset makers to ship a brand‑new OS without a proven ecosystem is improbable.  
2. **Revenue model vacuum** – The startup has no monetisation path; the acquiring firm would have to invent one (e.g., pre‑installed search, app store fees) from scratch, adding product‑development risk and cost.  
3. **Carrier resistance** – Carriers control distribution and have historically rejected platforms that threaten their control or require costly integration.  
4. **Pay‑back horizon** – Even optimistic forecasts assume a 3‑5 year horizon before any ad‑revenue uplift materialises, far exceeding typical M&A pay‑back windows for a $50 M outlay.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Strategic partnership / licensing** with existing OS vendors to embed search without the $50 M sunk cost.  
- **Invest in carrier‑friendly services** (mobile search APIs) that leverage existing platforms, preserving cash and reducing integration risk.  
- **Wait for industry consolidation** (e.g., emergence of a dominant open‑source platform) before committing large capital.

**Kill‑criteria**  
- No carrier or handset‑maker commits to ship the OS within 24 months.  
- No demonstrable revenue stream (e.g., pre‑installed search deals) signed within 18 months.  
- ROI < 2× after 5 years.

Given the high adoption risk, unclear monetisation, carrier gatekeeping, and long pay‑back, the proposal does not meet acceptable risk/reward thresholds.

**Decision** – The acquisition should not proceed as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
