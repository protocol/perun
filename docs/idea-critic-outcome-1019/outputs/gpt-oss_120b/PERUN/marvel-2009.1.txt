**Steelman (best defensible claim)**  
The $4 B cash‑plus‑stock acquisition gives the family‑entertainment group direct ownership of a comics publisher with a deep library of girl‑ and child‑focused characters, enabling cross‑media expansion, tighter creative control over film production, and a long‑term revenue stream beyond licensing fees.

**Skeptic (critical checks)**  
1. **Price vs. cash capacity** – $4 B is a large outlay; without disclosed financing terms we cannot confirm it is affordable or accretive.  
2. **Film slate risk** – The target’s first self‑financed film succeeded, but the pipeline is short and unproven; future box‑office performance is highly volatile.  
3. **Licensing encumbrances** – Key flagship characters are already tied to rival studios under long‑term deals, limiting the acquirer’s ability to exploit its most valuable IP.  
4. **Creative‑control disputes** – Past attempts by similar conglomerates to steer comic‑book studios have led to leadership turnover and culture clash.  
5. **Cyclical market** – Comics‑based films have shown boom‑bust cycles; a single hit does not guarantee sustained profitability.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Strategic licensing partnership** with the publisher to co‑produce films while preserving cash.  
- **Minority stake** to test integration without full exposure.  
- **Acquire a smaller, cash‑flow‑positive publisher** whose IP is not heavily encumbered.

**Kill‑criteria (stop‑conditions)**  
- Acquisition price exceeds 3 × the target’s EBITDA.  
- Existing licensing contracts block use of ≥ 30 % of the top‑10 characters for > 5 years.  
- Projected cash burn > $500 M before any synergy realization.  
- Integration timeline > 12 months with no clear governance plan.

**Dissent ledger**  
The proposal’s upside hinges on future film hits and the ability to renegotiate entrenched licensing deals—both highly uncertain. The price appears premium given the limited, unproven film pipeline and the loss of core characters to competitors. Safer, lower‑cost paths exist to access the IP portfolio.

**Remaining risk**  
High financial exposure with unclear path to monetize the most valuable characters; integration and creative‑control risk remain inadequately mitigated.

DECISION: NO-GO
