**Steelman claim**  
The retailer will acquire leases for 130‑150 existing sites in the neighboring country, open almost all within two years, and operate them with a brand‑new cross‑border supply chain and inventory‑management system, despite a dominant local competitor, higher local price levels, and the need for bilingual labeling.

**Critical attack (skeptic)**  
1. **Speed of rollout** – Opening >130 stores in 24 months requires ~6–7 stores per month, a pace rarely achieved even by seasoned operators. Any delay cascades through the supply‑chain build‑out, jeopardizing opening schedules.  
2. **Unproven supply‑chain & inventory system** – Deploying a brand‑new logistics network and IT platform concurrently with store openings removes the safety net of a phased learning curve. Early‑stage bugs can cause stock‑outs, eroding the retailer’s reputation before brand awareness is built.  
3. **Price perception** – The target market already faces higher prices; a discount retailer must win on price *and* convenience. If the new supply chain cannot sustain lower cost structures, the retailer will be perceived as “expensive” relative to the entrenched competitor, limiting foot‑traffic.  
4. **Regulatory & bilingual labeling burden** – Cross‑border compliance and dual‑language packaging add operational complexity and cost, further squeezing margins.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Pilot phase**: Start with 20–30 stores in a single region, validate the supply chain, then scale.  
- **Acquisition or joint venture** with a local player to gain immediate logistics capability and brand recognition.  
- **Leasing fewer flagship sites** and using existing distribution hubs to reduce initial capital outlay.

**Kill‑criteria (stop‑conditions)**  
- If fewer than 50 % of the planned stores open on schedule (i.e., <65 stores by month 12).  
- If the new inventory system’s on‑time delivery rate falls below 90 % during the pilot period.  
- If average basket size is ≤5 % lower than the domestic market after the first six months, indicating price perception failure.

**Remaining risk** – Even with a pilot, the retailer faces a high‑risk, low‑reversibility bet: large capital commitment, brand exposure, and a dominant incumbent. The proposed “all‑at‑once” strategy lacks sufficient mitigation.

**Decision** – Given the aggressive timeline, untested logistics, and strong incumbent, the proposal should not be approved in its current form. A phased rollout or partnership approach is required first.  

DECISION: NO-GO
