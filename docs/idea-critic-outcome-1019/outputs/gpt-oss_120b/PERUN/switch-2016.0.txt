**Steel‑man claim** – A modest‑priced hybrid console, using a low‑power mobile chip and detachable motion controllers, can revive the brand by leveraging the maker’s own studios for a thin launch line‑up and capture market share despite weaker hardware than rivals.

**Critical attack**  
1. **Spec‑price mismatch** – Pricing the device on par with far more powerful rivals will make the value proposition unattractive; historically, price‑sensitive gamers compare performance first.  
2. **Brand confusion** – The last console’s launch already left consumers uncertain; a similar hybrid with ambiguous positioning risks repeating that failure.  
3. **Third‑party ecosystem** – Low‑spec hardware and a small install base give developers little incentive to create titles, limiting the library beyond the maker’s own games.  
4. **Supply chain pressure** – Simultaneous development of a new SoC, modular controllers, and a hybrid form factor compresses the production schedule, increasing the chance of shortages or excess inventory.  
5. **Recovery window** – Expecting a turnaround within a single fiscal year leaves little margin for market adoption, especially when competing against entrenched platforms.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Handheld‑only device** with a slightly higher price but optimized performance for the mobile‑phone market, reducing engineering complexity and focusing on a clear use‑case.  
- **Software‑first strategy**: double down on exclusive titles and licensing while postponing hardware until a competitive chip is secured, preserving cash and brand goodwill.  
- **Partnership model** – co‑develop the hybrid with an established console maker to share risk and gain immediate third‑party support.

**Kill‑criteria** (pre‑mortem)  
- < 500 k units sold or < $200 M revenue in the first six months.  
- Fewer than 10 third‑party titles confirmed for launch.  
- Persistent supply shortages causing > 30 % missed launch windows.

Given the high likelihood of failing these thresholds without substantial hardware or ecosystem improvements, the proposal should not be approved in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
