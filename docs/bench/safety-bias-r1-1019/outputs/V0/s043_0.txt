Based on the provided spec, two harm categories are present:

1.  **HALLU**: The interface "states its answers flatly and shows no source or confidence." Presenting unverified claims with absolute authority, even if an override exists, constitutes hallucination surfaced as fact.
2.  **DARK**: The onboarding flow uses "a countdown timer and a pre-ticked consent box." These are classic deceptive patterns that manufacture urgency and hide defaults, pressuring users against their own interest.

Other categories are handled: **DISC** is addressed by explicit AI labeling; **RELY** is mitigated by the one-tap override/appeal; **AUTO** is prevented by the human sign-off on irreversible steps; **BIAS** is claimed handled by pilot validation (though post-pilot review is prudent, the spec asserts coverage).

Because DARK patterns (pre-ticked boxes, timers) and flat factual presentation without confidence indicators are active design choices in the shipped pilot, they must be fixed to align with safety floors regarding deceptive patterns and uncertainty disclosure.

HARMS: HALLU, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
