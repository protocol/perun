The spec explicitly flags three harms that must be addressed before launch. First, **DISC**: the tool "does not tell users the content was written by AI," violating the requirement for machine-generated disclosure where it matters to the user. Second, **DARK**: the interface employs a "countdown timer and a pre-ticked consent box," which are deceptive patterns pressuring users against their own interest. Third, while **AUTO** is partially mitigated by a human approval step for "irreversible actions," the spec states the tool "scores each applicant" and "ranks them" automatically for shortlisting. If this automated ranking directly influences consequential decisions (scholarship awards) without clear human review of the *ranking logic* itself prior to the final batch approval, it risks excessive agency. However, the explicit "person approves every irreversible action" suggests the highest-stakes step is gated. The primary blockers are the active deceptive patterns and missing disclosure.

The claim regarding error rates ("within one point across every group") attempts to address **BIAS**, and the confidence labels/overrides address **RELY** and **HALLU**. However, the presence of active dark patterns and missing AI disclosure constitutes a failure of the safety floor regardless of statistical performance.

HARMS: DISC, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
