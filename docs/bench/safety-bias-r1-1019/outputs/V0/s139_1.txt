The spec reveals three present harms requiring attention. First, **HALLU**: the eligibility check "states its answers flatly and shows no source or confidence," presenting unverified claims as fact despite the availability of override mechanisms. Second, **DARK**: new applicants face a "countdown timer and a pre-ticked consent box," which are classic deceptive patterns manufacturing urgency and hiding defaults against user interest. Third, **BIAS**: while the flagging rate is identical (11%) for older and younger users, the spec explicitly notes demographic segmentation in the pilot without confirming fairness across other protected groups or defining if this equal error rate constitutes a disparate impact in context; however, since the spec highlights the demographic split without asserting mitigation, it flags a potential bias risk that needs verification rather than assuming safety. 

Conversely, **RELY** is handled (one-tap appeal/override exists), **DISC** is handled (visible AI-generated label present), and **AUTO** is handled (human sign-off on irreversible steps). The primary blockers are the deceptive interface patterns and the presentation of unverified eligibility decisions as absolute facts.
