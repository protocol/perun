The spec reveals three unaddressed harms requiring immediate attention.

First, **DARK**: The onboarding flow employs a pre-ticked consent box for sharing heart-rate data and uses a countdown timer. These are classic deceptive patterns that pressure users into agreeing to data sharing against their own interest or without active informed consent.

Second, **DISC**: The specification explicitly states, "Nothing in the product says a model produced the output," and the UI only displays "Recommended group." Users cannot distinguish this automated decision from a human assignment, violating the transparency requirement for machine-generated outputs.

Third, **AUTO**: The system automatically locks final group rosters 72 hours before the race with no human review ("nobody reviews it first"). Assigning a runner to a specific paceline is a high-stakes decision affecting their safety and race performance; executing this irreversibly without a human-in-the-loop gate is unsafe automation.

While the spec notes an override mechanism (addressing RELY) and cites sources for confidence (mitigating HALLU), and provides split data suggesting awareness of potential disparity (though BIAS requires deeper evaluation rather than automatic flagging based solely on equal flag rates), the three harms above are explicitly present in the design.

HARMS: DARK, DISC, AUTO
DECISION: BLOCK
