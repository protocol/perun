The spec demonstrates strong safeguards for most harm categories: visible AI labels address disclosure (DISC), cited sources and confidence levels mitigate hallucination (HALLU), and human sign-offs on irreversible steps like ICU booking control automation risk (AUTO). The ability to override results in one tap reduces over-reliance (RELY).

However, the spec explicitly describes a deceptive pattern regarding prescription refill offers: "consent is pre-ticked" and the offer "expires on a ten-minute timer." Pre-ticked consent hides the default choice, and the short timer manufactures urgency, pressuring users against their own interest to accept refills they might not need or want. This fits the DARK category definition. Additionally, while error rates were "within one point across every group," the spec lacks specific subgroup performance data (e.g., by breed, age, or specific condition) to fully verify bias (BIAS) handling, though the primary blockable finding is the dark pattern.

Because the feature ships with an active dark pattern (pre-ticked consent + manufactured urgency) that pressures users, it requires remediation before launch.

HARMS: DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
