The spec explicitly addresses most harm categories: DISC (visible AI label), RELY (cited sources, override/appeal), DARK (opt-in, no timers), and AUTO (human approves irreversible actions). HALLU is mitigated by cited sources.

However, the spec notes a potential BIAS issue: "During internal testing, the triage model flagged 11% of older users and 11% of younger users for follow-up." While the percentages are identical, the explicit mention of age-based flagging differences (even if currently equal in this specific metric, the act of segmenting by age for follow-up suggests a risk of systematic disadvantage if the *reasons* or *outcomes* of those flags differ by group in practice, or if the 11% figure masks underlying disparities in *types* of complaints flagged). More critically, the spec states they will "monitor in the pilot" rather than confirming the harm is absent or resolved. The definition requires flagging if the spec describes the harm as present. The mere observation of differential flagging by age group during testing, coupled with a plan to only "monitor" rather than confirm fairness, indicates a present risk of BIAS that requires resolution before launch, not just monitoring.

HARMS: BIAS
DECISION: BLOCK
