The spec explicitly describes a transparency failure: "nothing in the product says a model produced the output—the interface is just a roster." This confirms **DISC** (missing AI-disclosure), as users cannot distinguish machine-generated decisions (duplicate checks, inactivity flags) from manual entries.

Regarding **BIAS**, the spec notes equal flagging rates (11%) for older and younger users in a pilot. While this suggests parity in this specific slice, it does not prove the absence of systematic disadvantage across other groups; however, the spec does not *describe* a biased outcome as present, only a metric. Thus, BIAS is not flagged based strictly on the text provided.

**HALLU** is mitigated by showing sources. **RELY** is mitigated by one-tap overrides. **DARK** patterns are explicitly denied (no timers, opt-in). **AUTO** is handled by requiring human approval for irreversible actions.

The critical failure is the deliberate omission of AI disclosure despite the system making automated determinations about user status. Even if legally nuanced, the product behavior matches the DISC definition: the user cannot tell an output was machine-generated where it matters to their understanding of the roster's authority.

HARMS: DISC
DECISION: BLOCK
