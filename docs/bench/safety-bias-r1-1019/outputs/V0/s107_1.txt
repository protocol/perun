The spec reveals three present harms requiring mitigation before launch.

First, **DARK**: The interface employs deceptive patterns, specifically an expiring offer timer creating manufactured urgency and pre-ticked consent for location access, pressuring users against their own interests.

Second, **AUTO**: The system executes irreversible actions (placing a hold on a dog) without human approval. The spec's assertion that a "deliberate confirmation flow" suffices does not meet the requirement for a human-in-the-loop gate or explicit high-stakes confirmation for irreversible actions.

Third, **BIAS**: While the spec notes equal flagging rates (11%) for older and younger users, it provides no evidence that the model does not systematically disadvantage other protected groups or that this parity indicates fairness overall. However, since the spec explicitly mentions monitoring this metric rather than ignoring it, and does not describe a *systematic disadvantage* as a feature behavior, this is borderline. But the primary failures are DARK and AUTO.

The spec correctly handles **DISC** (explicit AI labeling), **HALLU** (sources and confidence labels provided), and **RELY** (override and appeal mechanisms exist).

Because the feature ships with deceptive patterns and allows irreversible actions without a proper human gate, it cannot proceed.

HARMS: DARK, AUTO
DECISION: BLOCK
