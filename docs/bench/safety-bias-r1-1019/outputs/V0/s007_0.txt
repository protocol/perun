The spec explicitly handles several categories: it labels outputs as AI-generated (mitigating DISC), provides cited sources and confidence levels (mitigating HALLU and RELY), and allows one-tap overrides (mitigating RELY). However, two critical harms remain present in the description.

First, the system executes booking changes "the moment the model decides and nobody reviews it first." Since altering a medical appointment is a consequential action that affects patient care and clinic revenue, executing this automatically without human verification constitutes unsafe automation (AUTO).

Second, the interface includes a "pre-ticked consent box." This is a classic dark pattern that pressures users into agreeing by default rather than through active choice, fitting the definition of deceptive patterns (DARK).

While the spec mentions error rates were low across groups during the pilot, the structural design flaws regarding automation and consent are explicit. The lack of a human gate for irreversible actions and the use of pre-ticked boxes are sufficient to block launch until resolved.

HARMS: AUTO, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
