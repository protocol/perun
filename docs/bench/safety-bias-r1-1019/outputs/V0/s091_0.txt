The spec reveals two unaddressed harms. First, **DISC** is present: the text explicitly states "nothing in the product says a model produced the output," violating the requirement for AI disclosure where machine generation matters to users. Second, **RELY** is triggered: while a human approves escalations, the spec notes "the result is final and there is no appeal path." This lack of an override or appeal mechanism for ranked recommendations invites over-trust and prevents correction of errors, fitting the definition of automation bias.

Regarding **BIAS**, the spec claims pilot data showed equal flagging rates (11%) for older and younger users, suggesting this specific harm is currently handled rather than present. **AUTO** is mitigated because a person approves every irreversible action. **DARK** patterns are absent due to opt-in consent and easy cancellation. **HALLU** is partially addressed via cited sources, though the confidence metric's precision is not defined as a coarse tier, the primary failure remains the missing disclosure and lack of appeal.

Because the feature ships without telling users it is AI-driven and offers no way to contest a final ranking decision, it cannot proceed.

HARMS: DISC, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
