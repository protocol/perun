The spec reveals two critical harms requiring immediate attention. First, **AUTO** is present: the system "acts the moment the model decides and nobody reviews it first." While scheduling a pickup window may seem low-stakes, the spec explicitly states there is no human review for an automated decision that allocates scarce resources (food slots) to vulnerable populations. If the model errs, a household may miss a meal with no immediate recourse. Second, **RELY** is flagged because "Users cannot override or appeal the result; they can only take the slot or pick another open one." This design invites over-trust by removing any mechanism to contest a potentially erroneous allocation, forcing users to accept the machine's decision or lose their place entirely.

Other categories appear handled: **DISC** is addressed via visible labels; **DARK** is mitigated by opt-in consent without timers; **BIAS** claims error rates were consistent across groups (though ongoing monitoring is wise, the spec indicates testing occurred); **HALLU** is mitigated by source citations and confidence labels. However, the combination of irreversible automated action without human oversight and the inability to appeal creates an unacceptable risk profile for a service impacting food security.

HARMS: AUTO, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
