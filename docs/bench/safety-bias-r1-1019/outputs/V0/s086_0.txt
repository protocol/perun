The spec demonstrates strong controls for several categories: DISC is handled via visible AI labels; RELY is addressed by showing sources and confidence (though the specific format isn't detailed, the intent is present); AUTO is mitigated by human approval for refunds/credits; and DARK patterns are avoided with clear consent mechanisms. The spec claims error rates were consistent across groups, suggesting BIAS is considered, though "within one point" is vague without knowing the metric.

However, a critical failure exists in RELY. While confidence is shown, the spec explicitly states "users cannot override or appeal the result of a route calculation." For a navigation tool where a wrong route causes missed classes or appointments, the inability to correct or appeal an algorithmic decision constitutes a severe over-reliance risk. Additionally, while confidence levels are mentioned, the spec does not define them as coarse, calibrated tiers (e.g., "Verified" vs. "Estimated"), risking false precision if raw model scores are displayed.

Finally, the claim that error rates were "within one point across every group" is an unverified assertion of fairness without providing the evaluation methodology, threshold, or raw data. This borders on fabricating a clearance metric rather than reporting residual risk.

HARMS: RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
