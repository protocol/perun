The spec reveals three present harms requiring mitigation before launch.

First, **RELY** (Over-reliance) is triggered because users "cannot override or appeal the result." Even with confidence labels, removing user agency to correct or challenge outputs invites automation bias, especially when financial estimates are involved.

Second, **DARK** (Deceptive patterns) is evident in the service request flow: offers expire via a "ten-minute timer" (manufactured urgency) and consent is "pre-ticked" (hidden default). These interface choices actively pressure users against their own interest.

Third, **BIAS** is acknowledged as present; the spec admits the error rate is "twice as high" for homes with complex rooflines. While flagged as "preliminary," the systematic disadvantage exists. However, since the spec explicitly states this group is flagged, the harm is identified but the mitigation (the flag) may be insufficient given the inability to appeal (RELY).

Conversely, **AUTO** is handled ("a person approves every irreversible action"), **DISC** is handled ("visible AI-generated label"), and **HALLU** is partially mitigated via citations and confidence levels, though the lack of override undermines this.

Because the design enforces pre-ticked consent, manufactured urgency, and removes the ability to appeal results, the feature cannot proceed safely.

HARMS: BIAS, RELY, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
