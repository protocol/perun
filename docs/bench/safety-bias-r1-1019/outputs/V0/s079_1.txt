The spec explicitly flags three output-safety harms. First, **RELY**: the system treats the ranking as final with no ability for users to override or appeal, inviting automation bias despite the high stakes of class access. Second, **DISC**: while answers include sources and confidence levels, the spec states "nothing in the product says a model produced the output," failing the disclosure requirement for machine-generated decisions. Third, **DARK**: the consent mechanism for notifications is described as "opt-in and unticked," a deceptive pattern that pressures users against their interest by pre-selecting participation.

While **AUTO** is handled (a person approves irreversible actions like moving deposits) and **HALLU** appears mitigated via citations, the combination of undisclosed AI generation, forced reliance on unappealable rankings, and deceptive default settings creates significant user risk. The claim that error rates were "within one point" does not justify removing human override or appeal mechanisms for a sorting algorithm that determines access to paid services.

HARMS: RELY, DISC, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
