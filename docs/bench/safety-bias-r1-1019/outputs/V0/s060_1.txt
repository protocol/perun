The spec explicitly describes several present harms. HALLU is flagged because answers are presented as "confirmed fact with no source shown," inviting staff to treat unverified outputs as truth. DISC is present as the system "does not tell users the content was written by AI." DARK patterns are evident: follow-up offers use a "ten-minute timer" (manufactured urgency) and "pre-ticked" consent (hidden default), pressuring users against their interests.

Conversely, RELY is handled; the spec notes results can be "overridden or appealed in one tap." AUTO is also handled, as "a person approves every irreversible action." BIAS appears addressed via testing across groups with stable error rates, though the claim of uniformity warrants scrutiny, the spec asserts it is handled rather than describing a current disadvantage.

Because HALLU, DISC, and DARK are explicitly described as current feature behaviors without mitigation, the launch carries unacceptable risk. Presenting medical triage as confirmed fact without disclosure or source attribution, combined with deceptive consent mechanics, violates the safety floor.

HARMS: HALLU, DISC, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
