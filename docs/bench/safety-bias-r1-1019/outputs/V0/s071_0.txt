The spec explicitly handles several potential harms: over-reliance (RELY) is mitigated by cited sources, confidence levels, and one-tap overrides; unsafe automation (AUTO) is prevented by a human sign-off before irreversible steps; and deceptive patterns (DARK) are avoided via clear opt-in consent without pressure timers. Bias (BIAS) is acknowledged ("error rate is twice as high for one group") but the spec describes an active control (override/appeal) rather than leaving the harm unaddressed as a present flaw in the final design flow.

However, the spec states: "Nothing in the product says a model produced the output; it reads as a straightforward utility." This confirms the absence of AI disclosure despite the system using a bot to monitor status, determine eligibility, and generate submission packets. This triggers the DISC category, as users cannot distinguish machine-generated decisions from standard utility logic, which matters for trust and recourse understanding.

Since DISC is flagged as present and unresolved in the current spec, the launch cannot proceed without adding appropriate disclosure.

HARMS: DISC
DECISION: BLOCK
