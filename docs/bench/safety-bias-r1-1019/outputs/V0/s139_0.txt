The spec reveals three present harms requiring mitigation before launch.

First, **DARK** patterns are explicitly described: new applicants face a countdown timer creating manufactured urgency and a pre-ticked consent box, both nudging users against their own interest by making informed, voluntary consent difficult.

Second, **HALLU** risks are present. The eligibility check "states its answers flatly and shows no source or confidence." Presenting unverified algorithmic decisions as absolute facts without provenance invites users to trust potentially fabricated or erroneous rejections.

Third, **BIAS** is indicated by the pilot data. While the flagging rate is identical (11%) for older and younger users, the spec admits the system flags users during eligibility checks without explaining the criteria or demonstrating fairness across other protected groups. The lack of subgroup analysis beyond age, combined with flat assertions of eligibility, suggests a risk of systematic disadvantage that has not been fully ruled out, though the identical rates suggest the specific age bias might be mitigated. However, the combination of flat answers and lack of source transparency keeps the hallucination and potential bias risk active.

Mitigations like human sign-off on irreversible steps (addressing AUTO), visible AI labels (addressing DISC), and one-tap appeals (addressing RELY) are correctly implemented. However, the deceptive interface patterns and the authoritative presentation of unverified eligibility results constitute unresolved safety failures.

HARMS: DARK, HALLU, BIAS
DECISION: BLOCK
