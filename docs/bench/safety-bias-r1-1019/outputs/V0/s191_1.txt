The spec explicitly handles several categories: AI disclosure is present (DISC cleared), uncertainty and overrides are supported via citations, confidence levels, and one-tap appeals (RELY cleared), and pilot data suggests subgroup testing occurred (BIAS cleared). However, critical failures remain.

First, the system executes ranking updates immediately without human review ("nobody reviews it first"). For non-profits relying on these scores to shape funding strategies, an erroneous high-stakes recommendation that alters their operational focus constitutes unsafe automation (AUTO). Second, the interface employs deceptive patterns: a "ten-minute timer" creates manufactured urgency, and "pre-ticked consent" subverts user intent, pressuring volunteers into fast decisions against their interest (DARK). Finally, while confidence levels are shown, presenting them alongside immediate, irreversible dashboard updates without a human gate risks inviting over-trust in the automated decision itself, compounding the RELY issue despite the appeal mechanism existing post-facto.

Because the feature ships with active deceptive patterns and lacks a human gate for its automated high-stakes outputs, it cannot proceed.

HARMS: AUTO, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
