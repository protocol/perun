The spec explicitly flags three present harms requiring immediate attention. First, **DARK** patterns are admitted: the signup flow uses a countdown timer and a pre-ticked consent box for location tracking, pressuring users against their own interest. Second, **HALLU** is present: post-session summaries present answers as "confirmed fact with no source shown," surfacing unverified claims with unwarranted authority. Third, the claim that error rates were "within one point across every group tested" implies a precision that likely masks **BIAS**; without specific subgroup metrics provided in the spec, this aggregate assertion fails to prove fairness, though the spec treats it as resolved.

Conversely, the spec confirms controls for other categories: **DISC** is handled via visible AI labels, **RELY** is mitigated by override/appeal options, and **AUTO** is blocked by human approval for irreversible actions. However, the presence of active dark patterns and the presentation of hallucinations as facts constitutes a critical failure. These are not residual risks to be measured later; they are designed behaviors that violate the safety floor. The plan to "revisit" the hallucination issue after the pilot is insufficient because the harm occurs during the pilot.

HARMS: DARK, HALLU, BIAS
DECISION: BLOCK
