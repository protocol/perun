The spec demonstrates strong safety controls for most categories: AI disclosure is present (DISC handled), interface patterns are user-protective (DARK handled), and irreversible actions like plot swaps require human sign-off (AUTO handled). Confidence levels are cited from bylaws, mitigating fabrication risks (HALLU handled).

However, two critical harms remain unaddressed. First, the spec states users "cannot override or appeal the result," with disputes deferred to the next season. This eliminates immediate recourse for erroneous allocations, creating a high-risk over-reliance scenario where users must accept potentially flawed automated decisions without an easy override or timely appeal mechanism (RELY). Second, while the pilot noted error rates were "within one point across every group tested," this aggregate metric does not prove the absence of systematic disadvantage against specific demographics (e.g., new vs. experienced gardeners, specific household sizes). Without explicit bias testing results or fairness guarantees in the allocation algorithm itself, the risk of systematic disadvantage persists (BIAS).

Because the system enforces finality without appeal and lacks verified fairness guarantees, it fails the safety floor for high-stakes allocation.

HARMS: RELY, BIAS
DECISION: BLOCK
