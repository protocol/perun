The spec presents three flagged harms. First, **DISC**: the output "reads like a standard triage worksheet" with no indication that a model produced the ranking or reasons, violating the requirement for AI disclosure where it matters to users. Second, **RELY**: while sources and confidence levels are shown, the spec explicitly states "the result is final and there is no appeal path," preventing nurses from overriding or correcting the system's decisions despite potential errors. Third, **BIAS**: the pilot defines a stop condition if error rates are "twice as high for one group," which implicitly acknowledges a known risk of systematic disadvantage that requires pre-launch mitigation rather than just post-launch monitoring.

Although **AUTO** is handled (humans sign off on irreversible steps) and **DARK** appears addressed (no deceptive timers, opt-in consent), the lack of transparency regarding the AI nature of the tool, combined with the inability to appeal final rankings and the acknowledged potential for disparate error rates, creates unacceptable risks for a medical triage system. The "human sign-off" on downstream actions does not cure the lack of appeal for the ranking itself, which drives clinical attention.

HARMS: BIAS, RELY, DISC
DECISION: BLOCK
