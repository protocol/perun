The spec explicitly addresses most output-safety controls: AI disclosure is present (DISC handled), uncertainty is shown via sources and confidence levels, and a one-tap override exists (RELY handled). Consent flows avoid dark patterns (DARK handled), and irreversible actions like surgery scheduling require human approval (AUTO handled).

However, the spec presents a specific measurement regarding age groups ("older" vs. "younger") to claim fairness but fails to define these cohorts or test against protected characteristics like breed, species, or specific medical conditions. While it claims matching vet judgment, it does not provide a rigorous harm inventory or eval plan for potential systematic disadvantages beyond this single age metric. More critically, the "confidence level" is mentioned but not defined as a coarse, calibrated tier (e.g., "Corroborated" vs. "Unverified"); if this is a raw model probability, it risks over-trust. Finally, while a pilot is planned, the spec lacks a defined stop-condition or rollback trigger if the triage suggestions cause harm during the 12-week run. The absence of a clear "stop" mechanism for a high-stakes medical triage tool constitutes an unresolved risk under the AUTO and RELY categories regarding governance.

HARMS: RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
