The spec reveals three critical harms requiring remediation before launch.

First, **RELY** (Over-reliance) is present because the design explicitly prevents users from overriding or appealing finalized assignments. Even with source citations, removing human agency in operational planning invites dangerous automation bias and blocks correction of errors.

Second, **DARK** (Deceptive patterns) is evident in the confirmation flow. Requiring users to click through a countdown timer and a pre-ticked consent box manufactures urgency and hides the active choice required for bulk reassignments, pressuring users against their own interest.

Third, **BIAS** is indicated by the system flagging specific age demographics ("older" and "younger" users) for review. While the spec notes equal percentages, targeting age groups without explaining the neutral business justification or proving the absence of disparate impact on protected classes constitutes a potential systematic disadvantage that requires verification before shipping.

Although the spec correctly includes AI disclosure (addressing DISC), sources for claims (mitigating HALLU), and human approval for irreversible actions (mitigating AUTO), the hard block on appeals, the deceptive consent interface, and the unexplained age-based flagging create unacceptable residual risk.

HARMS: RELY, DARK, BIAS
DECISION: BLOCK
