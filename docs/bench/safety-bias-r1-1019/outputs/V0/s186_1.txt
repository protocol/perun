The spec explicitly addresses several harm categories: DISC is handled via visible AI labels; RELY is mitigated by one-tap overrides and appeals; HALLU is addressed through cited sources and confidence levels; AUTO is controlled by human sign-off for irreversible actions. The pilot data provided (11% flag rate for both older and younger users) suggests no evident BIAS in the described sample.

However, the spec describes a DARK pattern: "consent is pre-ticked when admins enable the auto-judge feature" and "The offer expires on a ten-minute timer." These are deceptive patterns that pressure users against their own interest by manufacturing urgency and hiding defaults. Additionally, while confidence levels are stated, the spec does not define them as coarse, defined tiers (e.g., "Confirmed" vs. "Unverified") rather than potentially false-precision numbers, which remains a partial RELY risk if the confidence is presented as a raw percentage without calibration context.

Because the spec confirms the presence of pre-ticked consent and manufactured urgency, the DARK category is flagged. This requires fixing before launch to avoid manipulating user choice.
