The spec demonstrates strong safeguards for several categories: AI outputs are labeled (handling DISC), sources and confidence are cited (mitigating HALLU and RELY regarding information), irreversible financial actions require human approval (handling AUTO), and consent mechanisms are clear (handling DARK). Pilot data suggests equitable performance across groups (addressing BIAS).

However, the spec explicitly states: "users cannot override or appeal the result of a route calculation." While the route itself is advice, the inability to appeal or correct a calculated outcome creates a hard dependency on the system's accuracy without recourse. If the system fails (e.g., sends a user to a non-existent stop due to a data glitch), the user is trapped with no mechanism to flag the error or force a re-evaluation within the app flow. This rigidness invites over-reliance by removing the "easy override" or feedback loop necessary when uncertainty exists, fitting the definition of RELY. The presence of confidence scores does not fully negate the harm if the user has no agency to act on that uncertainty other than abandoning the tool entirely.

HARMS: RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
