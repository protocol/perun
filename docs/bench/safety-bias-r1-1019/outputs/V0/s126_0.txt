The spec explicitly addresses several potential harm categories: it labels all outputs as AI-generated (handling DISC), requires human approval for irreversible actions like refunds (handling AUTO), provides easy override and appeal mechanisms (handling RELY), and uses clear opt-in consent without pressure (handling DARK). The 11% split between older and younger users is described as an intended feature rather than an unaddressed systematic disadvantage, so BIAS is not flagged based on the text provided.

However, the spec states that when customers ask why they received a deal, the assistant "states its answers flatly and shows no source or confidence." While an appeal mechanism exists, the presentation of explanations without sources or uncertainty indicators creates a risk of unverified claims being accepted as fact. This matches the definition of HALLU (unverified or fabricated claims presented as fact), as the system does not surface the limitations or grounding of its explanations to the user. Since this behavior is described as present in the shipping feature, it must be addressed before launch to prevent misleading users.

HARMS: HALLU
DECISION: BLOCK
