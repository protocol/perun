The spec demonstrates handling for several categories: sources and confidence labels address HALLU; override/appeal mechanisms address RELY; explicit AI labeling addresses DISC; and human sign-off on irreversible steps addresses AUTO. While a known error disparity for flat roofs is acknowledged, the spec states an adjustment is planned before full rollout, and this pilot phase does not yet describe systematic disadvantage as a shipped, unmitigated feature, so BIAS is not flagged as a present failure in this specific pre-launch context.

However, the spec explicitly describes two deceptive patterns: a "ten-minute timer" creating manufactured urgency and "pre-ticked" consent for emails. These are clear instances of DARK patterns that pressure users against their own interest. Since the definition of done requires managing residual risk and the anti-rationalization floor notes that governance scales but floors never relax, shipping with known deceptive patterns constitutes a blockable harm.

HARMS: DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
