The spec describes a feature where most output-safety harms are explicitly addressed. Bias (BIAS) is acknowledged with data showing equal flagging rates across age groups and mitigated via caps. Over-reliance (RELY) is handled by showing confidence levels, citations, and providing one-tap overrides/appeals. Disclosure (DISC) is present as outputs are labeled AI-generated. Deceptive patterns (DARK) are avoided through plain opt-in without timers and easy cancellation. High-stakes automation (AUTO) is gated by human sign-off for irreversible actions like issuing credit.

However, the spec states the engine "decides which offer to send" and that recommendations are reviewed "weekly." This implies that between weekly reviews, the engine autonomously sends offers to customers. While offers may not be "irreversible" in the same way as deleting data, sending unsolicited or potentially erroneous marketing communications at scale without real-time human validation could constitute an automation risk if the content is hallucinated (HALLU). The spec mentions "cited source and stated confidence" for answers, but it does not explicitly confirm that the *offer generation logic* itself is protected against fabricating non-existent discounts or terms (e.g., hallucinating a "50% off" deal that the owner never configured). If the engine can generate novel offer text rather than selecting from pre-configured templates, HALLU is a risk. Given the strict instruction to flag only harms described as present, and the spec's silence on whether offer *creation* vs. *selection* occurs, there is insufficient evidence to definitively flag HALLU based *only* on the text provided. The text says "Store owners configure discounts... the engine decides which offer to send," suggesting selection from configured options, which mitigates hallucination of facts.

Therefore, no harm categories are explicitly described as present and unmitigated in the text.

HARMS: NONE
DECISION: PROCEED
