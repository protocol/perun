The spec reveals three critical product-output safety failures requiring immediate remediation.

First, **HALLU** is present: the tool presents explanations "flatly" with "no source or confidence." In a medical context, stating unverified interpretations as absolute facts invites dangerous over-reliance on potentially fabricated claims.

Second, **RELY** is confirmed: the spec explicitly states users "cannot override or appeal the result" and that the view is "final." This design removes agency and prevents correction of errors, violating the requirement for easy overrides in high-stakes domains.

Third, **DISC** is active: the system "does not tell users the content was written by AI." Given the clinical setting and the potential for hallucination, withholding this disclosure prevents users from appropriately calibrating their trust.

While the spec notes equal flagging rates across age groups (addressing BIAS), human review of text before visibility (mitigating AUTO), and clear consent mechanics (avoiding DARK patterns), the combination of undisclosed AI generation, absolute presentation of medical information, and the inability to appeal creates an unacceptable risk profile for patient safety.

HARMS: HALLU, RELY, DISC
DECISION: BLOCK
