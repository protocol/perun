The spec reveals two critical harm categories requiring attention before launch.

First, **BIAS** is present. The internal audit explicitly states the system flags older users for extra review at double the rate of younger users (18% vs. 9%) for identical edge-case values. This indicates a systematic disadvantage based on age that must be investigated and mitigated.

Second, **RELY** (Over-reliance) is flagged. The specification states, "Users cannot override or appeal the result—the summary stands as generated." In a medical context, removing the ability for clinicians to correct or appeal an AI-generated summary invites dangerous automation bias and removes a essential safety valve, even if confidence levels are shown.

Other categories appear handled: **DISC** is addressed via visible labeling; **DARK** patterns are avoided with unticked consent and no timers; **AUTO** is mitigated by human sign-off for irreversible record modifications; and **HALLU** is partially addressed by citations, though the inability to override remains a risk if those citations are wrong.

Because the spec confirms existing bias and a lack of override mechanisms for medical advice, the feature cannot proceed without fixes.

HARMS: BIAS, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
