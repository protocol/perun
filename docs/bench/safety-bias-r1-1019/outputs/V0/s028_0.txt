The spec reveals three unmitigated harms. First, **BIAS** is present: the tool admits error rates are "twice as high" for homes with complex rooflines. While flagged as "preliminary," this systematic disadvantage requires stronger mitigation than a label if it significantly impacts quote accuracy for that demographic. Second, **RELY** is a critical failure: the spec explicitly states "Users cannot override or appeal the result." Even for ballpark figures, denying any mechanism to contest or correct output invites dangerous automation bias, especially when financial estimates are involved. Third, **DARK** patterns are hardcoded: the interface uses a "ten-minute timer" for offers and "pre-ticked" consent boxes. These are textbook deceptive patterns that pressure users against their own interest by manufacturing urgency and obscuring affirmative consent.

While **AUTO** is handled (a person approves irreversible actions) and **DISC** is addressed (visible AI labels), the combination of known bias, zero appeal rights, and dark patterns creates unacceptable risk. The inability to override results contradicts the stated goal of early research, trapping users in potentially flawed financial projections. The dark patterns further compromise user autonomy. These issues must be resolved before exposing users to financial misinformation and manipulative design.

HARMS: BIAS, RELY, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
