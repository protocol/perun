The spec reveals three critical harm categories that must be addressed before launch.

First, **RELY** is present: the bot states answers "flatly" with "no source or confidence," inviting over-trust by hiding uncertainty from users relying on financial decisions.

Second, **DARK** patterns are explicitly designed: the interface uses a "ten-minute timer" to manufacture urgency and employs "pre-ticked" consent, forcing users to actively opt-out rather than freely opting in.

Third, while the spec claims a human approves irreversible actions, it simultaneously states that "once a claim is filed, the result is final and there is no appeal path." If the human gate occurs *after* filing but the result is already "final," the gate is ineffective, constituting **AUTO** (unsafe automation of irreversible actions). If the gate is before filing, the "no appeal" clause still creates an irreversible state for the user's data/claim status without recourse, reinforcing the **RELY** and **DARK** issues. The "error rates within one point" claim lacks subgroup breakdowns, but without explicit evidence of disadvantage in the text, **BIAS** is not flagged per instructions, though the lack of granularity is concerning.

The combination of manufactured urgency, pre-ticked consent, hidden uncertainty, and a potentially broken human gate for irreversible financial claims creates unacceptable risk.

HARMS: RELY, DARK, AUTO
DECISION: BLOCK
