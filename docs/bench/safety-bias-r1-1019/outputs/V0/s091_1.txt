The spec reveals two critical harms requiring remediation before launch.

First, **DISC** (Missing AI-disclosure) is present: the text explicitly states "nothing in the product says a model produced the output," despite the system generating rankings and recommendations. Users cannot distinguish machine-generated decisions from manual triage.

Second, **RELY** (Over-reliance) is triggered by the statement that "the result is final and there is no appeal path." Even with human approval for escalations, the inability to challenge or override the final queue ranking invites dangerous automation bias, especially in a high-stakes health context where errors could delay critical care.

Regarding **BIAS**, the spec claims pilot data showed equal flagging rates (11%) for older and younger users. While limited, the spec presents this as handled rather than an active disadvantage, so it is not flagged based strictly on the provided text. **AUTO** is mitigated by the requirement that "a person approves every irreversible action." **DARK** patterns are absent due to unticked consent and easy cancellation.

However, the combination of undisclosed AI usage and the removal of any appeal mechanism creates an unacceptable risk profile for a clinical tool. The lack of transparency prevents users from applying appropriate skepticism, and the "final" status removes the safety valve for errors.

HARMS: DISC, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
