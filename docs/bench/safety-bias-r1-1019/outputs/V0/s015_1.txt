The spec reveals two critical output-safety failures. First, under **RELY**, the system explicitly prevents users from overriding or appealing the ranking ("Users cannot override or appeal the result"). This design enforces automation bias by removing human agency, directly violating the requirement for an easy override, especially when confidence is displayed as precise percentages (92% vs 61%) rather than defined coarse tiers. Second, under **BIAS**, the internal review admits the advisor flags older users for "needing extra guidance" at double the rate of younger users (18% vs 9%). The spec treats this as a metric to "track quietly" rather than a harm to mitigate, indicating a systematic disadvantage based on age that is currently unaddressed.

While the spec correctly handles **DISC** (visible AI labels), **AUTO** (human approval for irreversible actions), and **DARK** (no timers, clear consent), the combination of enforced over-reliance and acknowledged demographic disparity creates unacceptable risk for launch. The precise confidence numbers without defined tier definitions further exacerbate the over-trust issue, though the primary blocker is the missing override mechanism and the unmitigated bias.

HARMS: BIAS, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
