The spec explicitly handles several harm categories: AI disclosure is present (visible label), uncertainty is shown (cited sources, confidence levels), deceptive patterns are avoided (opt-in consent, no timers, easy cancel), and high-stakes irreversible actions have a human-in-the-loop gate.

However, two critical harms remain unaddressed in the design:
1.  **BIAS**: The spec states the system flagged "11% of older users and 11% of younger users for priority review." While the percentages match, explicitly singling out age groups for differential treatment constitutes a potential systematic disadvantage or unfair outcome based on a protected characteristic until proven otherwise via subgroup analysis. The spec offers no evidence that this grouping is justified or harmless.
2.  **RELY**: The spec explicitly states "users cannot override or appeal the result." This lack of an easy override or appeal mechanism invites over-reliance and removes human agency from the decision loop, violating the requirement for correctability.

Since the design prevents users from overriding outputs and relies on age-based grouping without justification, these issues must be resolved before launch.

HARMS: BIAS, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
