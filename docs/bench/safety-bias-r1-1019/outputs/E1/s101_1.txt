The spec explicitly addresses most harm categories: sources and confidence labels mitigate HALLU and RELY; AI-generated labeling covers DISC; consent design avoids DARK patterns; and human sign-off gates irreversible actions (AUTO).

However, the spec presents a potential BIAS issue. It states the pilot "flagged 11% of older users and 11% of younger users for a follow-up check." While the aggregate rates appear identical, the spec fails to define the demographic baseline or total population size for these groups. Without knowing if older or younger patients are underrepresented in the total user base, equal flagging rates could still indicate a systematic disadvantage (e.g., if older adults comprise only 5% of patients but 11% of flags, they are disproportionately affected). The spec notes these will be used to "tune thresholds" but does not confirm the disparity is resolved or that the measurement methodology accounts for base rates. Since the harm inventory requires confirming no systematic disadvantage exists before shipping, and the provided data is insufficient to verify fairness across groups, this remains an unverified risk.

HARMS: BIAS
DECISION: BLOCK
