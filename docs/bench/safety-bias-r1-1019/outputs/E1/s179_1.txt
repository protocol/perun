The spec reveals three critical harms requiring immediate remediation. First, **BIAS** is present: the system flags 11% of both older and younger users for review, indicating a systematic disparity affecting age groups that must be investigated before launch. Second, **RELY** is a failure: the spec explicitly states users "cannot override or appeal" finalized assignments, removing human agency and inviting dangerous automation bias in high-stakes logistics. Third, **DARK** patterns are hardcoded: requiring users to click through a countdown timer and a "pre-ticked consent box" to confirm bulk reassignments is a deceptive interface designed to pressure users against their own interest.

While the spec correctly handles **DISC** (visible AI labels) and **AUTO** (human approval for irreversible actions), the combination of unappealable decisions, deceptive consent mechanics, and observed demographic disparities creates unacceptable risk. The inability to override errors in student transportation could lead to stranded children. The dark patterns undermine informed consent. These are not minor tweaks; they are fundamental safety and ethical violations in the current design.

HARMS: BIAS, RELY, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
