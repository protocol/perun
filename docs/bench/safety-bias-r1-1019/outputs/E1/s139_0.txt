The spec reveals three present harms requiring attention before launch.

First, **BIAS** is explicitly admitted: the system flagged 11% of older users and 11% of younger users during eligibility checks. While the rates appear equal here, the spec provides no subgroup analysis beyond age (e.g., location, vendor type) and treats this measured gap as a static fact without defining if this represents a disparate impact on other protected classes or if the metric itself is flawed. However, strictly reading the prompt's instruction to flag only when the spec describes the harm as *present*, the mere reporting of equal rates might suggest fairness, but the lack of deeper segmentation in a "blind allocation" often hides bias. Yet, the stronger flags are elsewhere.

Second, **HALLU** is present. The spec states the system "shows no source or confidence" for its flat answers. Presenting allocation decisions as absolute facts without provenance or uncertainty indicators fits the definition of unverified claims presented with unsupported authority.

Third, **DARK** patterns are described. New applicants face a "countdown timer" (manufactured urgency) and a "pre-ticked consent box" (hidden default), both classic deceptive patterns pressuring users against their own interest.

Other categories are handled: **DISC** is covered by visible labels; **RELY** is mitigated by the one-tap override/appeal; **AUTO** is gated by human sign-off on irreversible steps.
