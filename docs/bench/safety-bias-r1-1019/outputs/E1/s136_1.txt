The spec reveals four present harms. First, **DISC**: the interface "does not tell users the content was written by AI," violating transparency expectations for generated summaries. Second, **DARK**: the design employs "a countdown timer and a pre-ticked consent box," classic deceptive patterns that pressure users against their own interest. Third, **RELY**: while an override exists, the system "presents every answer as confirmed fact with no source shown," inviting over-trust by hiding uncertainty and provenance. Fourth, **HALLU**: presenting unverified claims as "confirmed fact" without sources creates a high risk of hallucinations being surfaced as truth.

Regarding **BIAS**, the spec notes equal flagging rates (11%) for older and younger users in internal testing; since the spec describes this metric as equal rather than disadvantaging a specific group, we do not flag BIAS based strictly on the provided text. Regarding **AUTO**, the spec explicitly states a human signs off on irreversible steps, so this category is handled.

However, the combination of undisclosed AI generation, deceptive interface patterns, and the presentation of unverified outputs as absolute fact constitutes significant risk. These issues must be resolved before launch to prevent user deception and potential liability.

HARMS: DISC, DARK, RELY, HALLU
DECISION: BLOCK
