The spec explicitly addresses most harm categories: it labels outputs as AI-generated (mitigating DISC), requires human sign-off for irreversible steps (mitigating AUTO), allows easy overrides and appeals (mitigating RELY), cites sources for claims (mitigating HALLU), and uses plain opt-in without pressure (mitigating DARK).

However, the spec claims error rates were "within one point across every group tested" during a pilot. While this suggests an attempt to measure fairness, stating that groups are effectively equal based on a small pilot (12 branches) without providing the specific disparity metrics or acknowledging residual risk borders on certifying the system as unbiased. The harm inventory principle states that a measured gap is a present harm until re-measurement closes it, and a "fixed" note or small pilot does not clear it. The spec asserts a positive outcome ("within one point") rather than reporting residual risk or marking unverified metrics as such. This constitutes a potential BIAS risk because it presents a definitive claim of fairness that may not hold at scale, effectively asserting the harm is solved without robust, ongoing measurement plans detailed in the spec.

HARMS: BIAS
DECISION: BLOCK
