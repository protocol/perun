The spec describes a confidence label presented as a precise percentage ("92% sure"). This violates the safety floor requiring confidence to be shown as a **defined coarse tier** (e.g., Confirmed/Unverified) rather than a bare number, which implies uncalibrated precision and invites over-trust (RELY). While the spec mentions an override, the presentation of false precision undermines the user's ability to correctly gauge uncertainty.

Additionally, the spec claims error rates were "within one point across every group tested" to assert fairness. Without access to the raw evaluation data, subgroup definitions, or statistical significance tests, this claim of no bias is **UNVERIFIED**. Asserting equitable performance based on a summary statement without providing the runnable evals or model cards constitutes a measurement gap (BIAS). The spec treats these assertions as facts rather than residual risks requiring further validation before full scaling.

Although the spec includes human gates for irreversible actions and proper disclosures, the reliance on false-precision confidence metrics and unverified fairness claims presents immediate risks of automation bias and undetected disparate impact.

HARMS: RELY, BIAS
DECISION: BLOCK
