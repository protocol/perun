The spec demonstrates handling for most harm categories: bias is monitored (equal flag rates), hallucination is mitigated via citations, over-reliance is addressed with overrides and confidence levels, disclosure is present, and high-stakes automation has a human gate.

However, the spec explicitly describes **DARK** patterns: "consent is pre-ticked" and an offer expiring on a "ten-minute timer." These are deceptive patterns that pressure users against their own interest by manufacturing urgency and hiding defaults. Additionally, while confidence levels are shown, the spec does not confirm they are rendered as **defined coarse tiers** (e.g., "Confirmed" vs "Unverified") rather than raw precision numbers, which risks **RELY** if users misinterpret numeric confidence as calibrated fact. Finally, stating "11% flagged" for different age groups without confirming equal *error* rates leaves **BIAS** potentially unverified, though the equal volume suggests awareness.

The presence of pre-ticked consent and manufactured urgency constitutes a present harm under the DARK category that must be resolved before launch.

HARMS: DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
