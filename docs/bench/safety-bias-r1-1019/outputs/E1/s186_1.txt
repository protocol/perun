The spec explicitly addresses several harm categories: bias is measured with equal flag rates (11%) across age groups; disclosure (DISC) is present via visible labels; over-reliance (RELY) is mitigated by one-tap overrides and appeals; hallucination (HALLU) is countered by cited sources; and high-stakes automation (AUTO) requires human sign-off.

However, the spec describes a **DARK** pattern: "consent is pre-ticked when admins enable the auto-judge feature." Pre-ticked consent is a deceptive pattern that pressures users against their interest by manufacturing agreement without active choice. Additionally, while confidence levels are stated, the spec does not confirm they are rendered as defined coarse tiers rather than false-precision numbers, though the primary clear violation is the pre-ticked consent.

Because the interface employs a pre-ticked default for consent, a deceptive pattern exists that must be resolved before launch to align with safety floors regarding user autonomy and honest interface design.
