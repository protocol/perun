The spec reveals three present harms. First, **DISC**: the interface omits any indication that a model generated the output, presenting itself as a standard workflow tool. Second, **DARK**: the design employs deceptive patterns, specifically a manufactured urgency (ten-minute expiration) and pre-ticked consent, pressuring users against their own interests. Third, **RELY**: while sources are shown for claims, failed applications are final with no appeal path, removing the ability to correct or override the system's decision despite the high stakes of license denial.

Although the spec notes human sign-off on irreversible steps (mitigating **AUTO**) and claims consistent age performance in a small pilot (addressing **BIAS** concerns preliminarily), the lack of transparency, coercive interface elements, and absence of an appeal mechanism constitute unaddressed risks. The claim of "consistent performance" based on a 40-person pilot is insufficient to fully verify **BIAS** absence, but per instructions, we only flag harms explicitly described as present; the spec describes the *absence* of disclosure, appeal, and fair consent mechanics directly.

HARMS: DISC, DARK, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
