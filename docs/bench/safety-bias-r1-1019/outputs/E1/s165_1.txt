The spec explicitly addresses several potential harms: it labels outputs as AI-generated (mitigating DISC), cites sources and allows overrides (mitigating RELY), uses plain consent without pressure (mitigating DARK), and gates irreversible actions behind human approval (mitigating AUTO). It also claims pre-pilot testing showed consistent error rates across groups, suggesting an attempt to address BIAS, though the sample size (40 volunteers) is small.

However, the feature presents unverified confidence as a precise number ("90% confident"). Presenting a raw model confidence percentage implies a calibration precision that likely does not exist, inviting over-trust despite the override option. This violates the principle of showing uncertainty as a defined coarse tier rather than false-precision numbers. Additionally, while error rates were "within one point," the spec does not confirm if this constitutes a systematic disadvantage for any specific group, leaving BIAS as a potential residual risk requiring deeper measurement before full launch. The precise confidence score is a present harm under the RELY category because it misrepresents uncertainty.

HARMS: RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
