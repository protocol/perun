The spec describes two active harms requiring remediation before launch.

First, **DARK** patterns are explicitly present: the workflow includes a "countdown timer" (manufactured urgency) and a "pre-ticked consent box" (hidden default), both of which pressure users against their own interests and undermine informed consent.

Second, **RELY** (over-reliance) is triggered by the presentation of a "stated confidence level." Unless this is rendered as a defined coarse tier with explicit textual definitions (e.g., "Corroborated" vs. "Single-source") rather than a raw number, it risks implying false precision and inviting over-trust, especially in a health context. While the spec notes citations and override capabilities, the combination of deceptive interface patterns and potential false-precision confidence metrics creates unsafe conditions for patient-facing outputs.

Other categories appear mitigated: **DISC** is handled via visible labels; **AUTO** is gated by human approval; **BIAS** shows equal flagging rates in testing (though ongoing monitoring is wise, the spec does not describe a current systematic disadvantage); **HALLU** is addressed via source citation. However, the presence of DARK patterns alone necessitates a block.

HARMS: DARK, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
