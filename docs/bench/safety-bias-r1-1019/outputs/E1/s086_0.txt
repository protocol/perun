The spec explicitly addresses several harm categories: it includes an AI-generated label (handling DISC), cites sources and states confidence levels (mitigating HALLU and RELY regarding uncertainty), and requires human approval for irreversible refund actions (handling AUTO). Location sharing uses plain opt-in without pressure (handling DARK).

However, the spec states: "users cannot override or appeal the result of a route calculation." While confidence is shown, the inability to appeal or override the system's routing decision constitutes a lack of recourse for users who believe the suggestion is unsafe or incorrect. This rigid design invites over-reliance by removing the user's ability to correct or contest the automated decision, flagging RELY. Additionally, while error rates were "within one point," the spec does not confirm that bias was measured across protected groups beyond the listed volunteer segments, but since it claims testing occurred across diverse groups without citing a gap, BIAS is not explicitly flagged as present based *only* on the text provided. The primary failure is the hard block on appeals.

HARMS: RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
