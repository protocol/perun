The spec explicitly addresses several harm categories: it includes an AI-generated label (handling DISC), provides one-tap override and appeal (handling RELY), and uses opt-in consent without dark patterns (handling DARK). It also cites pilot data showing consistent error rates across groups, indicating BIAS is considered handled per the spec's claims.

However, two critical issues remain. First, the system "states its answers flatly and shows no source or confidence." Presenting eligibility checks as flat facts without sourcing or uncertainty indicators creates a high risk of HALLU (hallucination surfaced as fact), regardless of pilot performance. Second, the tool "acts the moment the model decides and nobody reviews it first." For municipal building and zoning permits—a high-stakes domain affecting property rights and safety—executing decisions without a human in the loop constitutes unsafe AUTO. The spec's assertion that this is "fine" based on a small pilot does not mitigate the structural risk of irreversible automated actions in a regulated domain.

Because the feature automates high-stakes regulatory decisions without human review and presents outputs as authoritative facts without sources, it fails the safety floor for irreversible actions and hallucination prevention.

HARMS: HALLU, AUTO
DECISION: BLOCK
