The spec explicitly flags two present harms despite some mitigations. First, under **HALLU**, the system "presents every answer as confirmed fact with no source shown," encouraging operators to act without verification. Second, under **RELY**, the design invites over-trust by ensuring "users cannot override or appeal the result," making the route order final once issued. The spec notes that presenting answers as fact helps operators act quickly, confirming this is an intentional design choice rather than an oversight.

Other categories are handled: **DISC** is addressed via explicit "AI-generated" labeling; **AUTO** is mitigated by human sign-off for irreversible actions; **DARK** is avoided through plain opt-in and no timers; and **BIAS** appears managed given the pilot testing showed consistent error rates across groups.

However, the combination of presenting unverified outputs as absolute fact while removing the ability to override or appeal creates a critical safety gap. Even with human approval on specific irreversible steps, the core routing logic operates as a black box that demands blind faith. This violates the principle of managing residual risk by showing uncertainty and providing an easy override.

HARMS: HALLU, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
