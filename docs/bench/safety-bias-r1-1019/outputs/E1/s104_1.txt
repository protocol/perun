The spec reveals three present harms requiring mitigation before launch.

First, **HALLU** is present: the tool presents explanations "flatly" with "no source or confidence." Fabricated or unverified claims are surfaced as authoritative facts without uncertainty indicators, violating the requirement to show limits.

Second, **RELY** is present: the spec explicitly states "Users cannot override or appeal the result; what they see is final." This design invites over-reliance by removing any mechanism for correction or human review from the patient's perspective, creating automation bias.

Third, **DISC** is present: the system "does not tell users the content was written by AI." Given the high-stakes nature of medical interpretation, users cannot distinguish machine-generated summaries from clinician-verified facts.

While **BIAS** is claimed handled via equal flagging rates across age groups in internal tests, and **AUTO** is mitigated by the lab tech review gate, the lack of transparency, uncertainty signaling, and user recourse creates significant risk. The human-in-the-loop protects against irreversible automation but does not resolve the user-facing opacity or inability to challenge the output.

HARMS: HALLU, RELY, DISC
DECISION: BLOCK
