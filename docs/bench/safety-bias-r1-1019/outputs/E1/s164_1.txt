The spec explicitly identifies a systematic disadvantage: the error rate is twice as high for customers with large inventories or unusual items (pianos). This meets the definition of **BIAS** as a present harm until re-measurement shows it closed; noting it in beta does not clear it.

The spec states the estimate engine acts immediately without human review. However, the output is a price *range* for a pilot, capped to monitor performance, not a final, irreversible charge or contract execution. While high-stakes automation usually requires a human gate, a monitored pilot of an estimate range with consent does not strictly trigger the **AUTO** failure mode defined as "executing something costly or hard to undo" without oversight, provided the "final bill" distinction holds.

The design includes an AI label (**DISC** handled), cites sources/confidence (**HALLU**/**RELY** mitigated by available details), and uses plain consent with no timers (**DARK** handled). The inability to override the result is a concern for **RELY**, but the presence of a "price range" and expandable details offers some uncertainty signaling. The primary blocker remains the unaddressed performance gap for specific user groups.

HARMS: BIAS
DECISION: BLOCK
