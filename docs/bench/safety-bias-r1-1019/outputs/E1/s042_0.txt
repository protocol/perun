Based on the spec provided, one harm category is present:

**DISC**: The spec explicitly states, "Nothing in the product says a model produced the output—the interface just lists levels and evidence." This matches the definition of missing AI disclosure where the user cannot tell an output was machine-generated.

Other categories are addressed in the spec:
- **BIAS**: Validation runs showed equal flagging rates (11%) for older and younger users.
- **RELY**: Results can be overridden or appealed in one tap, and teachers make the final choice.
- **DARK**: Consent is opt-in (not pre-ticked), with no timers and easy cancellation.
- **AUTO**: A human approves every irreversible action, including roster changes.
- **HALLU**: Claims show sources and confidence labels.

However, the lack of disclosure regarding the AI nature of the placement test is a clear gap. Users (students and teachers) are not informed that the level proposal and fluency notes are generated by a model, which matters for transparency and trust in an educational setting.

HARMS: DISC
DECISION: BLOCK
