The spec reveals several critical output-safety failures. First, **DISC** is present: "nothing in the product says a model produced the output," violating the requirement for AI disclosure where it matters to the user. Second, **DARK** patterns are explicitly designed: the interface uses a "ten-minute timer" creating manufactured urgency and "pre-ticked" consent, nudging staff against their own interest by making careful review harder. Third, **RELY** is a failure because "users cannot override or appeal the result," removing the easy override required to prevent automation bias, despite the system showing confidence levels.

While **AUTO** is handled (a person approves every irreversible action) and **BIAS** appears addressed in the spec's view (equal flagging rates cited), the combination of hidden AI origin, deceptive pressure tactics, and the inability to appeal constitutes a dangerous launch state. The claim that equal error rates equal fairness is a measurement assertion, but the structural harms of dark patterns and missing disclosure are definitive based on the text.

HARMS: DISC, DARK, RELY
DECISION: BLOCK
