The spec explicitly describes several present harms. First, **HALLU** is flagged because the interface presents every answer as "confirmed fact with no source shown," inviting users to treat unverified outputs as absolute truth. Second, **DISC** is present as the spec states Pawse "does not tell users the content was written by AI." Third, **DARK** patterns are implemented: follow-up offers use a "ten-minute timer" (manufactured urgency) and "pre-ticked" consent (hidden default), forcing users to actively decline rather than opt-in.

While the spec notes that error rates were consistent across groups (addressing **BIAS**) and that irreversible actions require human approval (mitigating **AUTO**), the remaining issues constitute active design choices that mislead users or pressure them against their interests. The ability to override results mitigates **RELY** slightly, but presenting hallucinations as confirmed facts fundamentally undermines that safety valve. Because the spec confirms the presence of deceptive patterns, missing disclosure, and the presentation of fabrication as fact, the feature cannot proceed without redesigning these specific interactions.

HARMS: HALLU, DISC, DARK
DECISION: BLOCK
