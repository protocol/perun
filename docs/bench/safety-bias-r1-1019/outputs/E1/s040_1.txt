The spec explicitly admits a measured disparity: the error rate is "twice as high for one group," satisfying the definition of **BIAS**. While the feature displays source and confidence labels, it explicitly states "users cannot override or appeal the result," creating an over-reliance trap (**RELY**) where users must accept potentially erroneous assignments without recourse. Furthermore, the system "acts the moment the model decides and nobody reviews it first" to assign waitlist spots. Since assignment determines access to essential food resources—a high-stakes outcome for vulnerable users—executing this irreversible allocation without human review constitutes unsafe automation (**AUTO**).

Although the spec addresses disclosure (**DISC**) via visible labels and avoids deceptive patterns (**DARK**) with plain consent, the combination of known bias, locked-out overrides, and fully autonomous high-stakes execution presents immediate harm risks. The admission of unequal error rates paired with the removal of appeal mechanisms and human gates requires remediation before launch.

HARMS: BIAS, RELY, AUTO
DECISION: BLOCK
