The spec explicitly describes a present harm under DISC: "nothing in the product says a model produced the output—the interface is just a roster." Since the system performs automated checks (duplicate detection, inactive profiling), failing to disclose machine generation to the staff users violates the transparency requirement defined in the safety guidelines.

While the spec claims mitigation for other categories, they require verification rather than immediate blocking based solely on the text provided:
- BIAS: The spec notes equal flagging rates (11%) for older and younger users, suggesting an attempt to address bias, though residual risk remains unverified without raw data.
- RELY: Mitigated by showing sources, confidence labels, and one-tap overrides.
- AUTO: Mitigated by requiring human approval for irreversible actions.
- DARK: Mitigated by opt-in consent, no timers, and easy cancellation.

However, the missing AI disclosure (DISC) is a clear design choice described in the spec that prevents users from understanding the nature of the decisions affecting their waitlist status. Per the safety floor, this missing disclosure must be addressed before launch to prevent deception, even if the automation is low-stakes.

HARMS: DISC
DECISION: BLOCK
