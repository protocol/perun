The spec explicitly addresses several potential harm categories: it labels outputs as AI-generated (handling DISC), requires human approval for irreversible actions like refunds (handling AUTO), provides easy override and appeal mechanisms (handling RELY), and uses plain opt-in without pressure (handling DARK). The 11% split between age groups is described as an intended business rule for separate offer sets, not a systematic disadvantage or error, so BIAS is not flagged based on the text provided.

However, the spec states that when customers ask why they received a deal, "the assistant states its answers flatly and shows no source or confidence." While appeals are possible, the system presents explanations as definitive facts without indicating uncertainty or providing sources for its reasoning. This matches the definition of HALLU (unverified or fabricated claims presented as fact) because the system asserts reasons with authority the evidence (which is hidden) may not support, risking misleading users about the logic behind their offers.

HARMS: HALLU
DECISION: BLOCK
