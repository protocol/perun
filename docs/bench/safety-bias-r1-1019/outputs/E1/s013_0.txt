The spec explicitly addresses several potential harm categories: it includes an AI-generated label (handling DISC), provides cited sources and confidence levels with override/appeal mechanisms (handling RELY and mitigating HALLU), uses clear opt-in consent without pressure (handling DARK), and requires human approval for irreversible actions like bulk orders (handling AUTO).

However, the spec flags BIAS. It claims "error rates were within one point across every group tested" based on a closed beta. This assertion of fairness across groups is presented as a concluded fact ("so we're confident") rather than a measured metric with a defined evaluation plan, subgroup breakdowns, or a recognition of residual risk. Per safety guidelines, asserting a bias gap is closed without providing the runnable eval details, specific subgroup metrics, or acknowledging the measurement as potentially unverified constitutes an unproven certification of "unbiased" performance. The claim relies on a small, geographically limited pilot (Pacific Northwest) to make a universal fairness assertion, which is insufficient evidence to clear the BIAS category before a wider launch.

HARMS: BIAS
DECISION: BLOCK
