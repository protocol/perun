The spec demonstrates strong handling of most harm categories: it cites sources and shows confidence levels (mitigating HALLU and RELY), provides easy overrides and appeals (mitigating RELY), includes visible AI labels (mitigating DISC), uses plain consent without pressure (mitigating DARK), and reports balanced error rates across groups (mitigating BIAS).

However, the spec explicitly states: "The automation acts the moment the model decides and nobody reviews it first." While the rollout is limited to 12 schools, the system assigns students to bus routes—a decision affecting child safety and logistics—without a human-in-the-loop gate before execution. The "review" happens only after a user actively overrides or appeals, meaning the initial automated action is irreversible until contested. This matches the definition of AUTO (high-stakes action running without human review). Limiting the scope of an unsafe automation pattern does not fix the pattern itself; the floor requires a human gate for high-stakes actions by default.

HARMS: AUTO
DECISION: BLOCK
