The spec explicitly reveals three present harms. First, **DISC**: the product deliberately hides its AI nature ("Nothing... says a model produced the output"), preventing users from understanding the source of recommendations. Second, **RELY**: users "cannot override or appeal the result," forcing them to restart queries rather than correct specific mismatches, which invites over-reliance on the initial ranking. Third, **BIAS**: the spec admits the error rate is "twice as high" for households with toddlers, a systematic disadvantage that remains uncorrected at launch (planned only for post-pilot).

While the spec notes positive controls like source citations, unticked consent, and human gates for irreversible actions (mitigating **AUTO** and **DARK**), the active concealment of AI usage, the lack of an appeal mechanism, and the known disparate impact on families with toddlers constitute clear, unaddressed risks. Launching with a known bias against a specific demographic and hiding the algorithmic nature of the service violates the safety floor.

HARMS: BIAS, RELY, DISC
DECISION: BLOCK
