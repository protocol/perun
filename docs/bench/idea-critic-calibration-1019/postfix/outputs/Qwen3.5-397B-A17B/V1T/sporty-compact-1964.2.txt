**Steelman:** Leverage existing compact infrastructure to rapidly deliver a stylized, customizable "pony car" targeting the emerging youth demographic, minimizing R&D risk while maximizing margin through options and volume advertising.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is brand dilution: if the shared economy underpinnings are too visible, the "sporty" illusion collapses, alienating the very buyers seeking status. However, the long hood/short deck proportion masks the compact wheelbase, and the extensive options list allows buyers to mentally distance their specific configuration from the base "cheap" model.

*Alternative 1 (Do Nothing):* Retain the dull, reliable image. Safe, but cedes the high-growth youth segment to competitors who may define the category first.
*Alternative 2 (Clean Sheet Design):* Build a dedicated sports chassis. Higher performance potential, but doubles development time and cost, missing the immediate demographic wave and violating the "low price" constraint.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns perfectly with the "young buyers entering the market" trend. If we wait for proven demand (Risk 1), the window closes; the baby boom is a time-sensitive event. The "unproven demand" risk is mitigated by the low incremental cost of reusing mechanical parts. If the car fails, the loss is limited to styling tooling and marketing, not a new powertrain. The heavy ad spend is a binary bet: without it, the car remains invisible; with it, even a moderate hit covers costs due to the low base investment.

**Kill Criteria & Verdict:**
The only fatal flaw would be if the shared parts forced a price point higher than true sports competitors or made the car undriveable. The proposal explicitly states "low base price" and relies on "reliable" mechanics. The risk of looking cheap is a marketing challenge, not a product failure, solvable via the proposed customization. The "do nothing" option carries a higher strategic risk of irrelevance than the financial risk of this launch. The proposal effectively turns a commodity into a lifestyle product with minimal engineering exposure.

DECISION: GO
