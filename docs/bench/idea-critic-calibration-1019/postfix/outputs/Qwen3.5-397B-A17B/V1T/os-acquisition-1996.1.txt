The claim is that acquiring a small, unproven OS vendor with a difficult founder will rescue a failing giant. Steelman: This is the only available modern, object-oriented core that can be integrated quickly enough to stop market share bleeding, and the founder's unique vision is required despite past friction.

**Skeptic:** The target has sold few units; scaling from niche to mass market is unproven. The founder's history suggests high integration risk and potential cultural toxicity. The price is disproportionate to current revenue, implying a bet on future potential rather than current asset value.

**Better-way:** Licensing an established vendor's system reduces execution risk and avoids cultural integration issues. Continuing internal development, though failed twice, avoids the distraction of M&A. Doing nothing preserves capital but accelerates decline.

**Kill-criteria:** If the founder refuses a strictly advisory role without operational control, the deal fails. If the technical audit reveals the OS cannot scale to the hardware portfolio within 12 months, the deal fails. If the cost exceeds 15% of cash reserves, the deal fails.

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition closes, but the founder alienates the existing engineering team, causing a brain drain. The OS requires a total rewrite to support legacy applications, delaying launch by two years. The company runs out of cash during the transition.

**Decision:** The proposal relies on a "savior" narrative that ignores the high probability of cultural collapse and technical debt. The alternative of licensing provides the modern core without the existential risk of integrating a volatile founder and an unproven codebase at a premium price. The risks of migration and founder conflict are not mitigated by the "advisor" title, given the history. The failure to validate the technical scalability against the specific hardware portfolio before commitment is a fatal flaw. The cost is unjustified by the revenue, making this a speculative gamble rather than a strategic repair.

DECISION: NO-GO
