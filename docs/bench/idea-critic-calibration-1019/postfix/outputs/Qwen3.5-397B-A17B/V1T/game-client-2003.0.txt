**Steelman:** A mandatory client solves critical update and cheating failures while creating a future distribution monopoly, outweighing short-term friction.

**Attack:** The proposal assumes universal broadband in 2003, which is false; many target users rely on dial-up or limited connections, making mandatory large downloads a hard blocker for sales. Retail partners, currently the primary revenue channel, will likely reject boxed goods that require online activation, causing immediate revenue collapse before the digital store matures. Furthermore, forcing a buggy, unproven client on users risks alienating the loyal base needed to fund the platform's growth.

**Better Ways:** A hybrid model allowing boxed games to function offline with optional online features for updates/anti-cheat preserves retail relationships and respects bandwidth limits. Alternatively, patch via existing web downloads without a mandatory persistent client reduces friction while solving the update issue.

**Pre-mortem:** The client launches with critical bugs and high system requirements. Broadband adoption lags projections. Retailers drop the games in protest. Sales plummet 40% in Year 1. The company runs out of cash before the third-party store gains traction, leaving them with no retail presence and a failed digital platform.

**Fit & Evidence:** The strategy aims to control distribution, but the execution ignores the 2003 infrastructure reality. No evidence is provided that the installed base has sufficient bandwidth or that retailers will accept the terms. The risk of "early bugs" is admitted but not mitigated; a mandatory gateway amplifies any bug into a total service outage.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a one-way door with high capital risk. If retail partners signal rejection or beta testing shows >20% failure rates on average user connections, the project must stop. The proposal lacks a phased rollout or an offline fallback, making the risk of total market exclusion unacceptable at this stage. The "mandatory" nature is the fatal flaw; it forces a transition the market cannot yet support.

DECISION: NO-GO
