The claim is that acquiring a larger, declining department-store chain will rescue a smaller, post-restructuring discount chain by 2005. The steelman posits that rapid consolidation of corporate functions and cross-selling brands will generate sufficient synergy to offset two decaying revenue streams before big-box rivals dominate.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the "declining plus declining equals growth" arithmetic. Both entities suffer from falling sales and underinvested, aging real estate. Merging them combines two liabilities rather than creating an asset, likely accelerating cash burn during the critical post-restructuring stabilization phase. The proposal assumes cost savings from merging functions will outpace revenue erosion, yet explicitly admits these savings are "uncertain." Furthermore, the customer bases differ significantly; forcing a merger of distinct formats risks alienating the core discount shoppers without guaranteeing the departure of department-store patrons.

The "better way" analysis suggests that doing nothing avoids the massive integration risk and capital outlay required to close a deal in 2005. A cheaper alternative would be to focus solely on revitalizing the existing discount chain's balance sheet, which just emerged from court supervision, rather than taking on the debt and operational drag of a larger, failing competitor. The pre-mortem is clear: the combined entity runs out of cash within 18 months as integration costs spike and same-store sales continue to drop against growing big-box competition.

This is a one-way door with high irreversibility; once the brands are commingled and stores converted, separating them is prohibitively expensive. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed merger that could drag the restructured entity back into insolvency. There is no evidence provided that the older chain's brands have any pull in discount stores, making the cross-selling strategy an unproven assumption. The proposal lacks a concrete kill criterion or a pilot test to validate the format conversion before a full-scale acquisition.

DECISION: NO-GO
