The claim is that a radical minimalist redesign, removing the primary shelf cue (the orange with a straw), will succeed despite an older, loyal demographic. The steelman: modernizing the visual identity will attract new buyers without alienating the core base if supported by heavy advertising.

However, the skeptic identifies a fatal flaw in the "all stores at once" launch strategy. The proposal admits the old image is the "main shelf cue" and the buyers are "older." Removing this cue universally creates a high probability of immediate sales collapse before advertising can retrain recognition. The "better way" is a staged rollout or A/B testing in select regions to measure recognition failure rates before committing to a nationwide print run. The current plan lacks any kill criteria or pilot phase; it bets the entire brand equity on a single, irreversible switch.

The pre-mortem reveals that shoppers, unable to instantly locate their usual product on the shelf, will substitute a competitor's brand. Once lost, regaining these habitual buyers is significantly more expensive than retaining them. The cost of delay (waiting to test) is negligible compared to the cost of a failed national launch. The evidence provided (loyal, older base; reliance on the old image) directly contradicts the proposed execution method.

This is a one-way door with no safety net. The proposal fails to address how it mitigates the specific risk of non-recognition among its most vulnerable demographic. Without a pilot to validate that the "cap shaped like an orange" and "vertically rotated name" are sufficient new cues, the risk of catastrophic revenue loss is unacceptably high. The plan must be revised to include a phased introduction with clear metrics for shelf recognition before full deployment.

DECISION: NO-GO
