**Steelman:** Leverage existing compact mechanics to rapidly deliver a stylish, customizable coupe/convertible at a low entry price, targeting the emerging youth demographic with massive advertising to create immediate market pull.

**Skeptic & Better-Way Analysis:**
The primary risk is that sharing parts with a "dull" economy car undermines the sporty image. However, the proposal mitigates this through distinct styling (long hood, short deck) and a psychological pricing strategy: a low base price attracts attention, while the extensive options list allows buyers to upgrade performance and aesthetics, effectively distancing the final product from its economy roots. This is superior to designing a new chassis (high cost/slow) or waiting for proven demand (missing the demographic wave).

The "unproven demand" risk is addressed by the low marginal cost of using existing mechanicals. If the car fails, the financial exposure is limited to marketing and body tooling, not a new powertrain. The "heavy launch spending" is a calculated necessity to define a new category; without it, the car remains just another compact. A "do nothing" approach cedes the youth market to competitors who may soon identify this gap.

**Pre-mortem & Kill Criteria:**
If the car launches and sales are low despite the ad spend, the failure mode is likely that the "economy" stigma was too strong to overcome via styling alone. The kill criterion should be sales volume relative to ad spend in the first 90 days. However, given the low production risk (shared parts) and the clear demographic shift, the potential upside of creating a new market segment ("pony car") vastly outweighs the downside of a modest flop. The proposal effectively balances innovation with cost control.

**Verdict:**
The proposal turns a constraint (cheap parts) into a feature (affordable entry) and addresses the image risk through design and customization. The market timing is critical, and the financial risk is contained.

DECISION: GO
