The proposal describes a high-stakes turnaround: acquiring a modern OS from a small vendor to replace a failing internal system, despite the target's low sales, high price relative to revenue, migration risks, and the founder's contentious history.

**Steelman:** This is not a product purchase but a talent and technology rescue. The target's code is the only viable modern foundation available; buying it secures the intellectual property and the specific visionary leadership required to unify the ecosystem, which licensing cannot achieve. The "high price" is actually an investment in survival, where the cost of inaction (continued market share collapse) dwarfs the acquisition cost.

**Better Way Analysis:**
1.  *Do Nothing:* Guarantees continued decline and eventual irrelevance as the dated OS cannot support modern applications.
2.  *License Another Vendor:* Surrenders control of the core platform, marginalizing the hardware maker to a commodity builder with no differentiation.
3.  *Internal Build (Attempt 3):* History proves this fails; the organization lacks the current capability to execute alone.

**Pre-mortem & Kill Criteria:**
Assume failure: The integration stalls because the engineering cultures clash, or the founder's advisory role becomes disruptive, causing key talent to flee. The migration takes too long, burning cash before new products ship.
*Door Type:* One-way door (acquisition is hard to unwind without massive loss).
*Cost of Delay:* Catastrophic. Every quarter without a modern OS accelerates the exodus of developers and users.

**Evidence & Assumptions:**
The proposal assumes the target's object-oriented architecture is superior and portable. It assumes the founder's "difficult history" can be managed through a restricted advisory role rather than operational control. The "few hundred million" is justified only if it stops the bleeding immediately.

**Verdict:**
The risks are real but manageable compared to the certainty of failure under the status quo. Licensing is a slow death; internal builds have already failed twice. The acquisition consolidates the only viable path forward. The "high price" is irrelevant if the alternative is zero market value. The founder's involvement is necessary but must be strictly scoped to prevent internal friction.

DECISION: GO
