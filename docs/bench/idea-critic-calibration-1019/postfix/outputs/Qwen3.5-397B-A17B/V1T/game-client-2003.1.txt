**Steelman:** A mandatory client centralizes update delivery and anti-cheat enforcement, creating a defensible moat against piracy while establishing a future high-margin distribution platform, despite short-term friction.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is retail channel collapse combined with low broadband adoption in 2003, stranding customers who cannot download patches or authenticate. However, "do nothing" guarantees continued revenue leakage from cheating and unsold boxed inventory due to version fragmentation. Physical patch discs are logistically unsustainable and slow. A hybrid approach (optional online features) fails to stop cheating effectively, as cheaters will simply avoid the optional component.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the industry's inevitable shift from physical to digital ownership. If the client launches with critical bugs, trust evaporates instantly; therefore, the "one-way door" risk is high. Yet, the cost of delay is ceding the platform war to competitors who might secure exclusive developer deals first. The risk of partners dropping boxed games is real but manageable if the digital value proposition (instant updates, anti-cheat) is marketed aggressively to consumers, forcing retailers to adapt.

**Evidence & Calibration:**
While 2003 broadband penetration is incomplete, the trajectory is exponential. The proposal addresses the core existential threats: cheating and update latency. The "mandatory" nature is the only way to ensure a unified, cheat-free environment. Risks regarding user dislike are valid but represent a transitional friction, not a fatal flaw, provided the client offers tangible value (stability, fair play).

**Kill Criteria:**
Proceed only if the initial client release guarantees <5% crash rate and includes an offline fallback for single-player modes to mitigate broadband gaps. If retail partners drop more than 20% of shelf space within six months, pivot to a voluntary model.

**Verdict:**
The strategic necessity of controlling the distribution pipeline and enforcing game integrity outweighs the transitional friction of mandatory adoption. The alternative is slow obsolescence.

DECISION: GO
