The claim is that a $1,500 head-worn display with a camera, voice control, and small screen should launch via an invitation-only program in 2013 as an everyday consumer product. The steelman posits that exclusivity will drive hype among early adopters, validating the form factor before mass production.

However, the skeptic hat identifies fatal flaws in the "everyday consumer" positioning. First, the $1,500 price point excludes the mass market required to normalize a socially awkward device; it is a luxury prototype, not a daily driver. Second, the raised risk of "privacy objections" is not merely a hurdle but a potential deal-breaker for public adoption in 2013, where no social norm exists for constant recording by strangers. Third, "no clear everyday use" contradicts the core requirement for an everyday product; without a killer app, the device remains a novelty.

The better-way analysis suggests that positioning this as an "everyday consumer product" immediately is the weakest path. A superior alternative is to position it strictly as an enterprise developer kit or a niche tool for specific industries (e.g., logistics, medicine) where the camera and hands-free operation solve defined problems, ignoring the "social acceptance" and "privacy" landmines of the general public. The "do nothing" option (delaying public launch until battery life improves and use cases mature) dominates the proposed plan on risk and reversibility.

The pre-mortem reveals that launching as an everyday product leads to public backlash over privacy, poor retention due to lack of utility, and brand damage when the battery fails to last a full day. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed public rollout that cements the device as "creepy" rather than "innovative."

This proposal fails the fit test: it attempts to skip the necessary evolution from niche tool to consumer staple. The risks are not just high; they are existential to the product's long-term viability. There is no kill criterion offered to mitigate the privacy backlash once it occurs.
