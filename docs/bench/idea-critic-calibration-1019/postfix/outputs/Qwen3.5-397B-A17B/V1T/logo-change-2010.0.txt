The claim is that an untested, unannounced, immediate total logo replacement will successfully modernize a mature brand without triggering destructive backlash. The steelman: a decisive, clean break signals contemporary relevance faster than a phased approach, avoiding the "uncanny valley" of a half-changed brand.

However, the proposal fails the pre-mortem. Assuming the rollout has already occurred and failed: the cause was the removal of the primary recognition heuristic (the dark blue square) without customer validation, triggering an identity crisis among loyalists that social media amplified into a boycott. The "no advance announcement" strategy removes the ability to manage the narrative or gauge reaction before full commitment. This is a one-way door; once the old logo is gone from every channel, the brand cannot easily revert without admitting failure, cementing the negative perception.

Alternatives were not adequately weighed. "Do nothing" preserves equity while other modernization efforts proceed. A "pilot" in specific digital channels or a limited product line would test the new aesthetic with lower risk. A "phased rollout" with communication allows the customer base to adapt. The proposal explicitly rejects testing, which is fatal for a change targeting emotional brand attachment rather than functional utility.

The evidence provided highlights that the old square is the core recognition element. Removing it entirely, instantly, based on internal design preference rather than market data, violates the basic principle of preserving brand equity during evolution. The risks (backlash, loss of recognition) are high probability and high impact. The cost of delay (waiting to test or phase) is negligible compared to the cost of a botched rebrand that alienates the core revenue base.

There is no kill criterion or rollback plan mentioned. If sales drop 20% in week one due to confusion, the proposal offers no off-ramp. The absence of testing makes the outcome a gamble, not a strategy. A sound modernization strategy must validate that the new mark actually conveys "contemporary" to the target audience before burning the bridges to the past.

DECISION: NO-GO
