**Steelman:** Differentiate via novel interaction and software breadth rather than raw power, targeting a blue-ocean demographic at a lower price point while maintaining legacy support.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is developer abandonment due to architectural complexity, rendering the second screen a liability. However, the "gimmick" risk is mitigated by the explicit strategy to keep the older, proven handheld on sale, preserving the core revenue stream while the new device tests the market. Matching the rival's graphics ("do nothing" relative to strategy) guarantees a specs war against a well-funded opponent with superior media capabilities; this is a likely loss given the proposer's current hardware disadvantage. A "cheaper single-screen" alternative fails to solve the differentiation problem.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This moves the north star from "technical supremacy" to "accessible play." If this fails, it will be because first-party software did not demonstrate the touch interface's value quickly enough, not because the hardware was impossible to build. The cost of delay is high; waiting allows the rival to cement mindshare as the sole premium option.

**Evidence & Calibration:**
The proposal accepts the premise that graphics are inferior but argues they are non-decisive for the target "broad" audience. The risk of developer confusion is real but manageable if the platform holder provides robust first-party titles that define the genre. The "two screens add cost" objection is countered by the requirement to be *lower* price than the rival; this implies the rival's media/Graphics bill of materials is significantly higher, leaving room for a dual-screen, lower-power design to win on margin and retail price.

**Verdict:**
No fatal flaw exists. The risks (developer confusion, gimmick perception) are inherent to innovation but are covered by the kill criteria implicit in the strategy: if touch games do not sell, the device fails, but the company retains its legacy business. The alternative (direct competition) is strategically weaker. The proposal represents a valid asymmetric strategy.

DECISION: GO
