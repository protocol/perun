Steelman: A premium, keyboard-less touchscreen phone with integrated music and web, sold via an exclusive carrier partnership that cedes design control and shares revenue, can disrupt a market dominated by business-centric keyboard phones and carrier-controlled handsets.

Skeptic: The claim assumes consumers will abandon physical keyboards for touch interfaces despite no prior market proof. It assumes a single carrier will accept reduced control and share revenue for an unproven device from a company with zero phone experience. The lack of third-party apps at launch creates a "empty platform" risk, potentially making the premium price unjustifiable compared to established competitors.

Better-way: The safer alternative is licensing the music player software to existing phone manufacturers, avoiding hardware risk and carrier negotiations entirely. Another option is a phased entry: launch a lower-cost device with limited features to test the market before committing to a premium, exclusive model. "Do nothing" preserves current music player dominance while monitoring competitor integration.

Kill-criteria: If the exclusive carrier fails to meet agreed-upon marketing spend or network quality targets within six months, the partnership must be renegotiated or terminated. If pre-orders do not reach 500,000 units in the first quarter, production must be scaled back immediately. The absence of a clear path to a third-party app ecosystem within 12 months should trigger a strategic pivot.

Pre-mortem: The device fails because enterprise users reject the lack of a keyboard and physical security features, while consumers find the price too high for a device lacking apps. The exclusive carrier underperforms, limiting reach, and competitors quickly replicate the music integration on cheaper, keyboard-equipped phones.

Despite significant risks, the proposal addresses a clear strategic threat (music player erosion) and leverages core competencies (design, music, user experience). The exclusive deal mitigates carrier interference, a known industry pain point. The risks are high but calculable; the potential to redefine the market justifies the gamble.

DECISION: GO
