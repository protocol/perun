The claim is that committing to a massive, unproven wide-body jet will secure market dominance and meet a specific customer's cost-reduction needs. The steelman: this specific design (raised cockpit, high-bypass engines) creates a dual-use asset (passenger/freighter) that hedges against the predicted shift to supersonic travel, while the single anchor customer provides sufficient initial demand to justify the unprecedented capital outlay.

However, the failure hypothesis is catastrophic insolvency. The development and factory costs exceed all prior company history, creating a "bet the firm" scenario. Relying on a single airline order for a vehicle of this scale violates basic portfolio diversity; if that carrier falters or cancels, the specialized factory becomes a stranded asset with no alternative buyers. Furthermore, the core engine technology is unproven; failure there grounds the entire fleet. The threat of supersonic replacement is not merely a risk but a potential market obsolescence event; if supersonic travel becomes viable quickly, this subsonic giant becomes an expensive relic before it pays back its investment.

The "do nothing" alternative preserves capital and allows competitors to bear the R&D risk of large aircraft. A better way would be to stretch existing narrow-body designs incrementally or wait for multiple launch customers to share the development burden. The proposal lacks a kill criterion: there is no defined threshold for engine performance or additional orders that would trigger a stop before total commitment. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of failure; waiting for engine validation and a second customer reduces existential risk without forfeiting the long-term opportunity.

The strongest attack survived is the freighter conversion feature, which mitigates the supersonic obsolescence risk by ensuring utility even if passenger demand shifts. Yet, this does not solve the immediate cash-flow crisis caused by the massive upfront spend with only one buyer. Without a second anchor customer or a government backstop, the financial exposure is fatal.

DECISION: NO-GO
