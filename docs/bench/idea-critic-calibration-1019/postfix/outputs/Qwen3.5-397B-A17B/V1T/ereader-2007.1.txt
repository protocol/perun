The claim is that a non-hardware bookseller should launch a subsidized, cellular-enabled e-reader to unlock digital sales despite piracy fears and cannibalization risks.

**Steelman:** The device is not a hardware profit center but a loss-leader distribution channel. By bundling free cellular connectivity and underpricing content, the company removes friction (no PC needed) and piracy incentives, creating a closed ecosystem where convenience outweighs the risk of print cannibalization.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Capability Gap:** Building reliable hardware and negotiating cellular data deals is outside core competency. *Counter:* Partner with an existing manufacturer (ODM) rather than building factories; treat connectivity as a bulk data purchase, not a telecom venture.
2.  **Cannibalization:** Low-price ebooks will destroy high-margin print sales. *Counter:* Digital offers zero inventory/shipping costs. Even at lower prices, margins per unit may remain healthy, and the format captures customers who currently buy *nothing* due to immediacy needs.
3.  **Market History:** Previous e-readers failed. *Counter:* Prior failures lacked seamless connectivity and a massive existing catalog. The "free cellular" feature is the differentiator that shifts the value proposition from "gadget" to "instant library."
4.  **Do Nothing:** Competitors will eventually digitize. Waiting cedes the emerging digital market and leaves the company dependent on physical logistics forever.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if the device is too expensive, the screen is poor, or publishers withhold titles. The proposal mitigates this via a "single low price" model to assure publishers of volume and using paper-like screens for readability.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot shows hardware costs cannot be subsidized by projected lifetime book purchases per user within 18 months, or if major publishers refuse participation, the project must pivot to a software-only app strategy.

**Verdict:** The risks of inaction (obsolescence) outweigh the execution risks. The "cannibalization" fear is a false negative; it is better to cannibalize one's own print sales than to let a competitor digitize the customer base. The proposal correctly identifies that the value lies in the ecosystem, not the hardware margin.

DECISION: GO
