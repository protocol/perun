**Steelman:** A search company can disrupt mature email by offering 100x more storage than incumbents, monetizing via contextual text ads, and using scarcity (invites) to drive demand despite high initial infrastructure costs.

**Skeptic:** The core assumption is that storage costs will drop fast enough to sustain free gigabyte-scale accounts before ad revenue scales. If hardware prices stall, the unit economics collapse. Privacy scanning is a binary reputational switch; one leak or policy shift kills the model.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" option preserves capital but cedes the strategic high ground of user data and daily engagement. Cheaper alternatives like 500MB storage fail to create the "never delete" psychological shift required to displace incumbents. The invitation model is already the cheapest customer acquisition strategy available.

**Kill-Criteria:** This is a two-way door; if ad click-through rates on email content fall below search benchmarks within 12 months, the model fails. However, the cost of delay is catastrophic: waiting allows competitors to lock in users with similar storage leaps. The risk of privacy backlash is mitigated by clear opt-in terms and the novelty of the utility provided.

**Calibration:** The "fatal flaw" test requires a scenario where the product launches and immediately cannot recover costs or faces immediate legal blockage. Current 2004 hardware trends suggest storage costs are plummeting, making the gigabyte bet a leading indicator rather than a sunk cost. The privacy objection is a known variable, not an unknown killer; it can be managed via transparency. The lack of direct revenue at launch is standard for platform plays funded by long-term ad potential. No concrete scenario exists where the specific combination of massive utility, viral invite mechanics, and existing ad infrastructure guarantees immediate insolvency. The strongest attack (unit economics) survives because the trajectory of hardware costs supports the volume play.

DECISION: GO
