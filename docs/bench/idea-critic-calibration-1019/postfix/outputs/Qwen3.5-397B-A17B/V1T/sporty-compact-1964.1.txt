**Steelman:** Leverage existing compact mechanics to rapidly deliver a stylish, customizable coupe/convertible at a low entry price, targeting the emerging youth demographic with massive advertising to create immediate market pull.

**Skeptic:** The core risk is brand perception; sharing "cheap" parts may alienate buyers seeking status, not just style. Additionally, unproven volume demand for sporty small cars makes the heavy upfront ad spend a potential financial sinkhole if the cultural moment is misread.

**Better-Way:** Alternatives include a limited regional pilot to test demand before national ad spending, or a slightly higher trim level to distance the image from the economy donor car. However, "doing nothing" cedes the youth segment to competitors. A staged launch reduces risk but sacrifices the "cultural phenomenon" impact essential for this specific demographic. The proposed "long options list" effectively mitigates the "cheap" risk by allowing buyers to upgrade perceived value.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This aligns with capturing the postwar boom's coming-of-age spending. If it fails, it will be because the "economy" stigma outweighed the styling, or the ad spend failed to ignite the cultural fire. The one-way door is the ad budget; the two-way door is production volume (adjustable post-launch).

**Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks explicit stop-loss metrics. Success depends on the "sporty" narrative overriding the "cheap" reality. The risk of looking cheap is mitigated by the customization options. The unproven demand is the bet; the large ad campaign is the method to prove it instantly rather than gradually.

**Decision:** The strategy correctly identifies a demographic shift and uses existing assets to lower barriers to entry while using customization to manage brand perception. The high ad spend is risky but necessary to define a new category quickly. The risks are real but manageable through the options mix and are outweighed by the first-mover advantage in a nascent segment.

DECISION: GO
