**Steelman:** A simultaneous, unannounced rebrand shocks the market into perceiving the retailer as modern, bypassing the inertia of incremental change.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "do nothing" and "pilot" tests. Replacing a decades-old, high-equity asset (the dark blue square) without testing violates basic brand stewardship. The "no advance announcement" strategy removes the ability to manage narrative, guaranteeing that social media backlash will define the launch. Loyal customers, the revenue backbone, are treated as obstacles rather than stakeholders. The risk of alienating the core base for an unproven "contemporary" look is asymmetric: the downside (revenue drop, brand confusion) vastly outweighs the upside (perceived modernity).

**Better Way:** Run a controlled pilot in a single channel or region. Test the new wordmark against the legacy square with actual customers to measure recognition and sentiment. If data supports the shift, phase the rollout with a communications campaign explaining the evolution, honoring the brand's history while signaling change.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales have dipped 15%. Social media is flooded with images of the "old, real logo" vs. the "generic new one." Competitors mock the bland redesign. The company is forced to revert, having spent millions on a failed experiment that damaged trust.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed without quantitative customer validation showing the new design retains at least 90% of current brand recognition and improves "modernity" scores by a statistically significant margin. Do not proceed without a phased rollout plan.

**Verdict:** The proposal relies on hope rather than evidence, ignores the value of existing brand equity, and invites unnecessary catastrophe by refusing to test or communicate. It is a high-cost, high-risk gamble with no safety net.

DECISION: NO-GO
