The claim is that offering 1,000x more storage than incumbents, funded by content-matched ads, will disrupt a mature email market despite high infrastructure costs and privacy risks.

**Steelman:** By eliminating the user's need to delete messages and providing instant retrieval, the service creates a unique value proposition that justifies ad-scanning privacy trade-offs, allowing rapid user acquisition via exclusivity to offset initial lack of direct revenue.

**Skeptic:** The premise assumes storage costs are low enough to sustain 1GB/user indefinitely on a free tier. If hardware costs remain high or ad click-through rates on private communications are lower than search queries, the unit economics collapse. Privacy objections could trigger regulatory backlash or brand toxicity, causing enterprise users to ban the tool.

**Better Way:** Instead of a massive 1GB launch, a tiered approach (e.g., 500MB) reduces initial capital exposure. Alternatively, licensing the search technology to existing providers avoids the immense operational burden of running an email service. However, "doing nothing" guarantees ceding the data-rich email layer to competitors who might integrate search later.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a two-way door; if ad revenue per user does not exceed storage/operational costs within 18 months, the service must pivot to paid tiers or shut down. The pre-mortem failure mode is a privacy scandal destroying trust before scale is reached.

**Decision:** The proposal bets on a paradigm shift where storage becomes cheap and search becomes the primary interface for email. The risks (cost, privacy) are real but manageable compared to the strategic risk of ignoring the value of indexed personal data. The invitation-only launch mitigates server load while creating demand. The "fatal flaw" test fails; no single objection guarantees failure if execution is precise. The potential to redefine user expectations of storage and monetize attention within communication outweighs the conservative "mature market" argument.

DECISION: GO
