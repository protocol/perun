**Steelman:** Dominate the portable market by prioritizing affordability, battery endurance, and software depth over graphical fidelity, targeting a mass audience rather than tech enthusiasts.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is consumer rejection of monochrome graphics when color is available. However, color screens drain batteries rapidly and increase costs. A color alternative at $89.95 is technically infeasible in 1989 without sacrificing playtime or build quality. "Do nothing" cedes the entire portable sector to the rival, allowing them to establish an ecosystem lock-in. A high-end handheld ($150+) limits the install base, reducing software attachment rates which drive long-term profit.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a strategy of market expansion through accessibility. If this fails, it will be because third-party developers ignore the platform due to perceived hardware weakness, or the marketing fails to distinguish "long battery life" as a superior feature to "color." The one-way door risk is the $20M marketing spend; if the product flops, hardware inventory becomes dead weight. However, the low unit cost limits downside exposure compared to a premium device.

**Evidence & Calibration:**
The proposal relies on the assumption that gameplay duration and price are the primary purchase drivers for the mass market, not screen technology. The bundled puzzle game is critical; it must demonstrate addictive potential to overcome the visual deficit. Margins are thin, but the model depends on volume and software licensing, not hardware profit.

**Verdict:**
The risks are real but manageable. The monochrome screen is a feature, not a bug, enabling the 10+ hour battery life that color rivals cannot match. The price point undercuts the competition significantly, lowering the barrier to entry for children and impulse buyers. The bundled software mitigates the "single-game" stigma of previous devices. No fatal flaw exists; the trade-off favors volume and ecosystem growth over spec-sheet superiority.

DECISION: GO
