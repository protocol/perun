The claim assumes clear cola can sustain nationwide demand as a "purer" alternative despite historical linkage between cola flavor and dark color. The steelman is that clarity signals a distinct, premium category capable of retaining novelty buyers without eroding the flagship's core identity.

However, the "purer" positioning fights a deep-seated consumer heuristic: dark color equals authentic cola taste. If the flavor profile does not radically differ, the visual dissonance will cause rapid trial dropout once the novelty fades. Test markets notoriously overstate demand for visually novel items because early adopters buy for the gimmick, not the taste. The proposal acknowledges cannibalization and copycat risks but offers no mitigation other than a "large marketing campaign," which increases sunk costs if the product fails to retain users.

A better way is a staged regional rollout with a capped marketing spend to verify repeat purchase rates before committing to a nationwide launch. The current plan treats a major brand extension as a binary switch rather than a learning loop. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed nationwide launch that dilutes brand equity and invites rivals to exploit the misstep.

The strongest attack survived is the fear of cannibalization; if the clear cola attracts only new users or lapsed drinkers seeking "purity," the flagship remains safe. Yet, without verified repeat-purchase data beyond the initial test market spike, this remains an assumption. The proposal lacks a specific kill criterion for repeat purchase rates or a timeline to pivot if novelty sales collapse. Launching nationwide without these guards turns a testable hypothesis into an irreversible bet on a visual gimmick.

Given the high risk of novelty decay and the lack of a staged approach to validate long-term retention, the proposal as stated is premature. It requires a defined pilot phase with strict retention metrics before nationwide commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
