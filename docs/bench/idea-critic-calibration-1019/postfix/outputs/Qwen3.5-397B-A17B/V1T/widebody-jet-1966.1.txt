**Steelman:** Building a massively larger, wide-body freighter-capable jet secures market dominance against supersonic threats and meets urgent airline demand for lower seat costs, despite unprecedented financial and technical risks.

**Skeptic:** The premise relies on a single customer order to justify an outlay larger than any previous company investment. If that airline cancels or if supersonic transport (SST) renders subsonic giants obsolete before ROI, the company faces insolvency. The "unproven" high-bypass engine introduces a critical single point of failure; if it fails certification or performance targets, the airframe is useless.

**Better-Way:** Alternatives include stretching existing narrow-body designs (lower risk, lower capacity gain) or waiting for SST market clarity. However, neither addresses the specific "cost per seat" demand of the largest customer nor the freighter conversion potential. Doing nothing cedes the heavy-lift market to competitors and ignores the immediate economic pressure of growing travel volumes.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Assuming failure: The factory debt crushes cash flow while SSTs capture premium routes, leaving the wide-body too expensive to operate and too large to fill. This is a one-way door; the factory cannot be easily repurposed. However, the cost of delay is losing the primary customer and the strategic high-capacity segment entirely. The raised cockpit design specifically mitigates obsolescence by ensuring freighter utility if passenger demand shifts.

**Evidence & Calibration:** The proposal admits the risks (single buyer, unproven engine, SST threat) but offers a specific architectural mitigation (freighter conversion). No fatal flaw exists because the risks are known variables, not unknown unknowns. The "fatal" financial exposure is the necessary price of maintaining market leadership in a growth phase. The single anchor customer provides sufficient initial validation to proceed, provided the engine development is tightly coupled with the supplier.

**Decision:** The strategic necessity of controlling the high-capacity segment and the built-in freighter fallback outweigh the financial terror. The risks are severe but managed through design flexibility and customer alignment.

DECISION: GO
