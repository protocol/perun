**Steelman:** Differentiate via novel interaction and dual-screen software possibilities to expand the market beyond graphics-focused core gamers, leveraging a lower price point and legacy support rather than engaging in a losing hardware specification war.

**Skeptic:** The premise that "touch-screen games are unproven" is a double-edged sword; if unproven, there is no existing library to drive hardware sales, creating a chicken-and-egg problem. Developers may ignore the platform if the install base remains small due to the "gimmick" perception. However, the rival's strength (graphics) is a commodity arms race this company cannot win given current constraints. The risk of confusion is real but manageable if the OS abstracts the complexity.

**Better-Way:** The alternative is "do nothing" or "match specs." Matching specs fails because the rival is already well-funded and ahead; playing catch-up on graphics yields a me-too product at a higher cost with no unique value. "Do nothing" cedes the handheld market entirely. A hybrid approach (matching graphics + touch) is rejected by the cost constraint. The proposed path is the only asymmetric option available.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This aligns with a "blue ocean" strategy to broaden the audience. If it fails, it will be because first-party software did not demonstrate the touch utility immediately, causing third-party abandonment. The two-screen cost is offset by avoiding the expensive GPU race.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a two-way door regarding software strategy but a one-way door on manufacturing tooling. The fatal flaw would be launching without a "killer app" that necessitates two screens or touch. Since the proposal implies a strategic pivot to broadening the audience, the lack of a proven touch market is the very opportunity to own, not a reason to stop. The risks are acknowledged and mitigated by the lower price and distinct value proposition. No concrete failure scenario overrides the necessity of differentiation.

DECISION: GO
