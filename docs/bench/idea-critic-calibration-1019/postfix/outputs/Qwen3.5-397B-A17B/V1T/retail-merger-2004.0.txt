The claim is that acquiring a larger, declining department store will rescue a newly restructured discount chain. The steelman: combining forces creates immediate scale, allows cross-selling brands, and eliminates redundant corporate costs to offset falling sales.

However, the "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw. The proposal pits two failing models against each other. Both entities suffer from declining sales, aging infrastructure, and underinvestment. Merging them does not fix the core product-market fit; it merely combines two leaking buckets. The "cost savings" from merging corporate functions are speculative and likely insufficient to offset the massive capital expenditure required to renovate the acquired chain's old stores. Furthermore, the customer bases differ significantly; forcing a department store model into discount locations risks alienating the price-sensitive core of the discount chain without guaranteeing loyalty from the department store shoppers, who are already fleeing to growing big-box rivals.

The pre-mortem is clear: by 2006, the combined entity is bloated with double the number of underperforming assets, drained of cash from the acquisition, and still losing ground to agile big-box competitors. The "do nothing" alternative—focusing on restructuring the existing discount chain—is safer and cheaper. A "go-if" condition cannot save this because the fundamental premise (that two declining trends create an upward trajectory) is economically unsound without a proven turnaround plan for the acquired assets, which is absent.

The strongest attack is the lack of a viable differentiation strategy. With big-box rivals growing, this merger creates a distracted, debt-laden hybrid that competes on neither price nor experience. The uncertainty of cost savings and the high risk of cultural and operational clash between distinct customer bases make this a one-way door to insolvency. There is no kill criterion that can mitigate the inherent weakness of buying a larger, failing business when you are barely stable yourself.

DECISION: NO-GO
