The claim is that an annual membership eliminating per-order friction will increase lifetime value despite short-term margin compression. The steelman: locking in loyalty via upfront fees creates a psychological and economic moat, driving frequency that offsets shipping losses through volume leverage and reduced churn.

However, the "heavy buyer" risk is existential. If the top 20% of customers double their order frequency, variable logistics costs could vastly exceed the $79 fixed revenue per user, creating a negative marginal contribution on every additional order. Unlike digital subscriptions, this model has high variable costs that scale directly with the success metric (frequency). The proposal admits the economics are "unproven," yet seeks approval for a hard-to-reverse commitment. Once sold, revoking free shipping breaches trust and invites immediate churn, making the "one-way door" risk severe.

Alternatives exist: a tiered loyalty program based on spend (not flat fee), free shipping thresholds per order, or a pilot limited to a specific geographic zone or customer segment to validate unit economics before a full launch. The "do nothing" option preserves current margins while allowing competitors to test the waters.

The pre-mortem reveals a cash-flow crisis: upfront fee revenue is recognized over 12 months, but shipping costs are incurred immediately. If adoption spikes, the retailer faces a liquidity crunch funding logistics before earning the revenue. The cost of delay is low; the cost of a failed nationwide launch is catastrophic.

Without a capped pilot, verified unit economics showing positive contribution margin at 2x frequency, and a clear kill criterion based on early loss ratios, this is a gamble, not a strategy. The proposal lacks the data to prove the "heavy buyer" scenario won't bankrupt the initiative. It fails the calibration test: the risk of unlimited variable costs against fixed revenue is a fatal flaw without a bounded test.

DECISION: NO-GO
