**Steelman:** Launching now leverages the recent acquisition to establish a foothold before the OS becomes obsolete, betting that heavy marketing and developer incentives can rapidly close the app gap despite current weaknesses.

**Skeptic Analysis:** The proposal ignores critical structural deficits. The operating system has "almost no market share," implying a lack of ecosystem gravity that advertising alone cannot fix. Shoppers already view the product as "unproven." Launching with a "small app catalog" against a dominant leader creates a negative feedback loop: users won't buy without apps, and developers won't build without users. The "leadership transition" introduces strategic instability exactly when consistent long-term vision is required to court developers. Pricing "in line with the market leader" removes the only viable advantage (cost) for an inferior product.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" alternative regarding an immediate full launch is superior. A staged approach focusing solely on securing flagship apps before hardware release, or positioning the device as a low-cost niche alternative rather than a direct premium competitor, reduces capital exposure. Rushing to match the leader's price without matching value is a path to inventory write-downs.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch failed six months post-release. The cause: consumers rejected a premium-priced device with limited utility (apps), and the marketing spend failed to overcome the "unproven" stigma. The leadership transition caused mixed messaging to developers, stalling the very outreach the proposal relies on. The company burned cash on ads for a product the market did not want at that price point.

**Kill Criteria:** This plan lacks a trigger to stop. If developer sign-ups do not triple within the first quarter, the "heavy investment" in outreach is throwing good money after bad. Without a mechanism to pivot pricing or delay launch based on app availability, the risk is catastrophic.

**Conclusion:** The proposal asks to enter a winner-take-all market with an inferior product at a premium price during internal instability. It relies on hope (marketing overcoming ecosystem deficits) rather than leverage. The mismatch between price, value (app catalog), and market perception constitutes a fatal flaw.

DECISION: NO-GO
