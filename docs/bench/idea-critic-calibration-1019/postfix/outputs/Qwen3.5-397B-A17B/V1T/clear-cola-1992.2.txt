The claim assumes clear color signals "purity" to mass markets and that test market enthusiasm predicts national retention. The steelman is that this extension captures a distinct "health-conscious" segment without eroding the flagship's core identity, creating a net revenue lift.

However, the "better way" analysis reveals a critical flaw: doing nothing or a limited regional rollout dominates a nationwide launch. The proposal ignores the fundamental consumer heuristic that cola flavor is intrinsically linked to dark color; violating this may cause cognitive dissonance rather than perceived purity. Test markets are notoriously prone to novelty bias, where early adopters try the product once for the gimmick but fail to repeat purchase. A nationwide campaign locks in massive sunk costs before validating repeat rates.

The pre-mortem shows a likely failure mode: the product launches with high initial trial due to marketing spend, then crashes as novelty fades, leaving the brand diluted and retailers skeptical. Worse, it cannibalizes the flagship by confusing the brand promise without bringing in enough new loyalists to offset the loss. The risk of rivals launching copies is secondary to the risk of self-cannibalization and brand erosion.

The evidence provided admits shoppers link flavor to color and that novelty buyers may not repeat. These are not minor risks; they strike at the core mechanism of the product's value proposition. Launching nationwide bypasses the necessary step of proving long-term viability beyond the initial curiosity spike. A staged approach (e.g., expanding test markets to measure 6-month repeat rates) is a strictly superior alternative that reduces downside risk while preserving upside potential.

The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed national launch. Without data proving that "clear" converts to "loyal" over time, the assumption that this will succeed as a major brand extension is unsupported. The proposal fails to define kill criteria for the test phase, treating a positive initial response as sufficient validation for full scale.
