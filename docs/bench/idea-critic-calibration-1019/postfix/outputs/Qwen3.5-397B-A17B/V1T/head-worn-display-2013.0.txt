The claim is that a $1,500 head-worn display with a camera, launched via an invitation-only program in 2013, will succeed as an everyday consumer product. The steelman is that early adopters will tolerate high costs and social friction for novel utility, creating a beachhead for mass adoption.

However, the "better-way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the sequencing and positioning. The proposal attempts to skip the essential "prosumer/enterprise" validation phase. In 2013, the identified risks—privacy objections, lack of clear everyday use, and social acceptance—are not merely hurdles; they are market rejection vectors for a general consumer positioning. A "better way" would be to position the device strictly as an enterprise tool or developer kit first. This lowers the social stakes (uniforms/contextualize usage), justifies the $1,500 price point through productivity ROI rather than novelty, and isolates privacy concerns to controlled environments.

The pre-mortem is clear: launching directly as an "everyday consumer product" invites immediate cultural backlash ("Glassholes"), regulatory scrutiny, and retail rejection because the value proposition ("clear everyday use") is undefined for the average person. The invitation-only model creates artificial scarcity but does not solve the fundamental utility gap. The cost of delay to refine the use case in an enterprise setting is negligible compared to the brand damage of a failed consumer launch.

The strongest attack survived is the argument that early adopters drive trends. While true, early adopters in 2013 cannot overcome the "social acceptance" barrier for the general public if the device is perceived as a surveillance tool without a compensating, obvious utility. The proposal fails to address how the device transitions from a "creepy camera" to a "necessary tool" for the mainstream. Without a defined killer app for the everyday user, the high price and privacy risks dominate the decision matrix.

This is a one-way door for brand reputation. Once labeled a privacy violator, recovery is nearly impossible. The proposal lacks a pivot strategy if consumer sentiment turns negative immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
