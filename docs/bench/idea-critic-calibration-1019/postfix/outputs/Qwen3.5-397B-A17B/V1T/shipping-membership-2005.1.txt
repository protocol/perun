**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee decouples shipping cost from purchase frequency, removing the primary friction for repeat buyers and locking in loyalty, even if heavy users temporarily subsidize their own shipping through increased order volume.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Retain per-order fees; continue losing repeat customers to friction; margin stays stable but growth stagnates.
2.  **Threshold Waiver:** Free shipping only above a high cart value; reduces friction slightly but fails to drive frequency or loyalty.
3.  **Proposal ($79/yr):** High upfront commitment from customers signals intent; shifts revenue recognition upfront; risks short-term margin compression.

**Fit:** Aligns with a "customer obsession" north star by prioritizing long-term lifetime value (LTV) over immediate per-transaction margin. Assumes the retailer has the cash flow to absorb the initial shipping deficit.

**Pre-mortem & Door:**
*   *Failure:* Heavy users order 3x more, shipping costs triple, and the $79 fee covers only 10% of the logistics bill. The program becomes a money-losing trap that cannot be cancelled without massive PR backlash.
*   *Door Type:* One-way door. Once sold, memberships are legally and reputationally difficult to revoke mid-year.
*   *Cost of Delay:* Competitors capture the "loyal" segment first; changing consumer expectations make per-fee shipping seem archaic.

**Evidence & Calibration:**
No internal data exists to verify the "heavy buyer" risk or elasticity. However, the risk is mitigated by the upfront cash injection ($79) which improves working capital. The "unproven economics" is a feature, not a bug: it is a deliberate bet on behavioral change (frequency) that cannot be modeled without running it. The kill criterion is clear: if the ratio of shipping cost to membership revenue exceeds a specific threshold (e.g., 1.5x) after Q1, the program must be capped or restructured for the next renewal cycle.

**Decision:**
The proposal addresses the identified biggest friction (fees) with a mechanism (prepaid membership) that aligns incentives for frequency. While the margin risk is real, the strategic necessity of locking in loyalty and the upfront cash benefit outweigh the modeled uncertainties. The one-way door nature requires careful monitoring, but inaction is a greater strategic risk.

DECISION: GO
