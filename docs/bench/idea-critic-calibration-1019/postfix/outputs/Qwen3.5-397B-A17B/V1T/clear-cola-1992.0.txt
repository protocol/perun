The claim is that a clear cola, successful in test markets, should launch nationwide immediately with a major campaign. The steelman: this extension captures novelty demand and repositions the brand as "purer" before competitors act, offsetting slowing core growth.

However, critical flaws prevent a GO decision. First, the fundamental product-market fit is unproven beyond novelty. Cola flavor is psychologically linked to dark color; removing color may break the flavor expectation for repeat purchases, creating a "one-and-done" buyer base. Test markets often inflate demand due to curiosity, failing to predict long-term retention. Second, the risk of cannibalization is severe. Without evidence that clear cola draws from competitors rather than the flagship, this move could shrink total portfolio revenue while increasing marketing costs. Third, the barrier to entry is low; rivals can copy the formula quickly, turning a first-mover advantage into a shared commodity war where the second-largest player lacks the volume to win on price.

The proposal acknowledges these risks but offers no mitigation strategy, kill criteria, or staged rollout. It jumps from "positive test response" to "nationwide major campaign," ignoring the possibility that the test market data is a false positive driven by novelty. A better approach would be a limited regional launch with strict repeat-purchase metrics before committing to a national campaign. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed national launch that damages brand equity and wastes marketing spend.

The strongest attack survived is the fear of competitor copying; however, being first with a potentially flawed product is worse than being second with a validated one. The current plan lacks a mechanism to verify if the "purer" positioning actually drives loyalty or just trial. Without a defined metric for repeat purchase rates or a cap on initial spend, the risk of a high-profile failure is unacceptable.

DECISION: NO-GO
