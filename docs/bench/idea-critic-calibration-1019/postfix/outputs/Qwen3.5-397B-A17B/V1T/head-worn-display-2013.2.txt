The claim is that a 2013 head-worn display with a camera, voice control, and small screen should launch via an invitation-only program at $1,500, then expand to the general public as an everyday consumer product.

**Steelman:** An exclusive, high-price entry allows early adopters to normalize the form factor and refine use cases before a mass-market push, mitigating initial social friction.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "everyday consumer" fit test against its own evidence. In 2013, the identified risks are not merely hurdles; they are fundamental blockers to the stated positioning.
1.  **Value Proposition Gap:** The proposal admits "no clear everyday use for most people." Launching a general consumer product without a defined daily utility contradicts the definition of a consumer staple. Early adopters may tolerate novelty, but the bridge to the "general public" is undefined.
2.  **Social & Privacy Hostility:** A wearable camera in 2013 invites immediate regulatory and social backlash (e.g., bans in restaurants, cinemas, and private spaces). Positioning it as "everyday" ignores that the device makes the wearer a social pariah in many contexts.
3.  **Economic Mismatch:** $1,500 is a luxury price point for a device with "short battery life" and limited utility. This pricing restricts the user base too severely to generate the network effects needed to solve the social acceptance problem.
4.  **Premature Scaling:** The plan moves from "invitation-only" to "general public" without a mechanism to resolve the core utility or privacy deficits. An invite program tests logistics, not market fit for a product lacking a use case.

**Better Way:** Pivot to an enterprise/industrial pilot (manufacturing, logistics, medicine) where the hands-free camera has immediate, billable utility, privacy is controlled, and price is secondary to productivity. Abandon the "everyday consumer" angle until battery life improves, costs drop, and a killer app emerges.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed to the general public until a specific, non-novelty daily use case is validated by data showing >40% daily active usage among early adopters over 6 months. Without this, the general launch will result in high returns and brand damage.

The proposal attempts to force a niche prototype into a mass market without solving the primary friction points. It violates the basic requirement of product-market fit.

DECISION: NO-GO
