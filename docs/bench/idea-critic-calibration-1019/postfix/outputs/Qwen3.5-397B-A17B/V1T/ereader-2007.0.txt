The claim is that a non-hardware bookseller should launch a subsidized, cellular-enabled e-reader to unlock digital sales despite piracy fears and past market failures.

**Steelman:** Only a frictionless, purpose-built device with ubiquitous connectivity can create a mainstream e-book market, justifying hardware losses through long-term content margin expansion and ecosystem lock-in.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Capability Gap:** Designing reliable consumer hardware is distinct from retail. Failure here destroys brand trust. *Alternative:* License existing hardware or partner with an OEM (cheaper, faster, lower risk).
2.  **Publisher Relations:** Aggressive low pricing threatens publisher margins, risking content withdrawal. *Alternative:* Launch a software app first to prove demand without hardware capex.
3.  **Cannibalization:** Shifting high-margin print to low-margin digital reduces immediate profit. *Alternative:* Do nothing; wait for competitors to validate the market (do nothing is safer given prior e-reader failures).
4.  **Cost Structure:** Free cellular data creates a massive, variable operating expense per user. If usage spikes, losses compound.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches with buggy firmware and slow page turns (hardware inexperience). Publishers refuse bestsellers due to price pressure. The cellular subsidy burns cash faster than content sales replenish it. The project is canceled after two years, leaving stranded customers and a bruised balance sheet.

**Fit:** This moves the "north star" of selling more books only if the hardware succeeds perfectly. It is a one-way door with immense capital commitment. The proposal lacks a pilot, a specific kill criterion for hardware defects, or a capped budget for the cellular subsidy. The assumption that "free connectivity" drives adoption enough to offset hardware inexperience is unproven.

**Verdict:** The risks of executing outside core competencies (hardware) while subsidizing a new cost center (cellular) without a validated path to publisher cooperation are fatal in the current state. The proposal must first prove the content model via software or a limited pilot before committing to full hardware manufacturing and network subsidies.

DECISION: NO-GO
