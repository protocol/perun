The claim is that a full rewrite favoring publishers, deployed via a "big bang" migration, will reverse slowing traffic without destroying the community. The steelman is that publisher content drives scale, and a clean break is the only way to modernize quickly against a growing rival.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in this specific context (2010 social news). First, the core mechanic (voting) is the community's primary value; altering it to favor publishers directly antagonizes the loyal base responsible for current traffic. Second, a "full rewrite" introduces massive engineering risk with no proven benefit over iterative improvement. Third, and most critically, moving all users at once eliminates the ability to rollback. If the new algorithm depresses engagement or the rewrite contains bugs, the site fails instantly with no recovery path while a rival is actively growing.

The "do nothing" or "iterative" alternative is superior: A/B test the new surfacing logic on a small segment while keeping the core voting mechanic intact for the majority. This validates the publisher-content hypothesis without betting the entire company. The cost of delay (losing some traffic to the rival) is far lower than the cost of a catastrophic, irreversible launch that alienates the entire user base simultaneously.

The proposal fails the pre-mortem: Assume the launch happened and traffic collapsed by 50% within 24 hours. The cause is the alienation of core voters and untested code scaling issues. Because the deployment is "all users at once," the only remedy is a full revert, which is impossible if the old system is decommissioned or the database schema changed incompatibly. The lack of a staged rollout or a preserved legacy path makes this a one-way door with a high probability of failure. The risk of destroying the existing community to chase an unproven publisher model, with no safety net, is unacceptable.

DECISION: NO-GO
