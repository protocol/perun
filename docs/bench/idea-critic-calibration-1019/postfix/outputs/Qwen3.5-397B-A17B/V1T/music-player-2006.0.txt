**Steelman:** A well-funded entrant can disrupt a complacent monopoly by prioritizing superior user experience (wireless sharing, screen size) over legacy copy-protection constraints, betting that consumers value convenience over industry-standard DRM.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores critical ecosystem lock-in. In 2006, the dominant player controls the supply chain; labels are unlikely to license content to a new store lacking established DRM, rendering the device content-barren at launch. Dropping copy-protection alienates existing partners without a guaranteed consumer migration path. "Large marketing budget" cannot compensate for a hollow catalog. The "late to market" status is fatal if the value proposition relies on content availability that competitors exclusively control. Wireless sharing is a feature, not a platform killer, if the library is empty.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid massive CAPEX burn in a losing battle; wait for the incumbent to stumble or regulations to shift.
2.  **Licensing Play:** License the dominant player's OS or store integration rather than building a walled garden from scratch.
3.  **Niche Hardware:** Build the superior screen/player as an accessory for the existing ecosystem, not a competitor.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Assuming the goal is market share, this fails the "content availability" test immediately. If labels restrict sharing or deny licenses due to the dropped DRM standard, the device launches as a pretty brick. The cost of delay is high, but the cost of failure is catastrophic brand damage and financial loss.

**Kill Criteria:** Proceed only if binding contracts with major labels are secured *before* hardware tooling begins, explicitly permitting the new sharing model. Without verified content rights, the marketing budget is wasted.

**Verdict:** The proposal assumes content will follow hardware quality, contradicting the 2006 reality where hardware follows content licenses. The risk of launching with an empty store is unmitigated. The plan to drop industry-standard DRM without confirmed label buy-in is a fatal strategic flaw.

DECISION: NO-GO
