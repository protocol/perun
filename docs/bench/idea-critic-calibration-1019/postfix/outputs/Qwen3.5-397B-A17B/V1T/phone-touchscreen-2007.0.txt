The claim is that a computer and music player manufacturer can successfully enter the mobile phone market by launching a premium, keyboard-less touchscreen device with exclusive carrier partnership and revenue sharing, despite lacking industry experience and third-party apps.

Steelman: By controlling the full stack (hardware, software, services) and securing a revenue share, the company avoids carrier commoditization and creates a differentiated user experience that justifies a premium price, leveraging its existing brand loyalty in music and computing.

However, critical flaws threaten viability. First, the "no physical keyboard" assumption contradicts the 2007 market reality where business users (the primary high-margin segment) demand physical input for email; removing it risks immediate rejection by the core target demographic. Second, the "single carrier exclusivity" strategy severely limits market reach during the critical adoption phase, relying entirely on one partner's sales performance while competitors access the entire market. Third, the lack of third-party applications at launch creates a barren ecosystem compared to emerging rivals, reducing long-term stickiness. Finally, the revenue-share model assumes the carrier will accept lower per-unit margins for a device with unproven demand, a difficult negotiation without a track record in phones.

Better ways exist: licensing the OS to multiple manufacturers to gain immediate scale, or launching with a hybrid input method to mitigate keyboard risks. The "do nothing" option preserves current high-margin music player sales without risking capital on an unproven, highly competitive venture.

The pre-mortem reveals failure via poor initial sales due to keyboard absence and limited distribution, causing the carrier to drop support before the ecosystem matures. This is a one-way door with massive sunk costs in R&D and marketing. The cost of delay is low; waiting to validate touchscreen typing adoption or secure multi-carrier support reduces existential risk.

Without a proven demand for keyboard-less phones, a guaranteed multi-carrier distribution, or an app ecosystem, the risks outweigh the potential rewards. The proposal relies on changing consumer behavior and carrier economics simultaneously without evidence either will shift in the company's favor immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
