**Steelman:** Transform an invisible component into a consumer demand driver by subsidizing OEM ads featuring a new brand logo, forcing buyers to request the chip by name and locking out clones.

**Skeptic:** The premise that end-users care about internal components is unproven and historically unprecedented for this industry. If consumers ignore the logo, the company burns a massive portion of its marketing budget with zero sales lift. The claim that "buyers do not know which chip is in their computer" is the very problem to solve, but assuming they *will* care once branded is a dangerous leap without pilot data.

**Better-Way:** Before committing the entire strategy to a consumer campaign, test the value proposition with a smaller, targeted segment or a direct rebate program to gauge sensitivity. Alternatively, strengthen relationships with OEMs through exclusive technical partnerships rather than risking resentment by dictating their ad spend. The "do nothing" option maintains current B2B stability without financial exposure.

**Kill-Criteria:** This is a one-way door; once the brand promise is made to the public, retreating admits failure and confuses the market.
*Pre-mortem:* The campaign launches, OEMs comply grudgingly, but consumers remain indifferent. Result: 40% of the marketing budget is wasted, clone makers gain share due to lack of B2B defensive spending, and OEM relationships sour.
*Tripwire:* If a limited pilot in two major markets does not show a 5% increase in premium-tier unit sales within six months, the program must be terminated immediately.
*Leading Metric:* Consumer recall of the logo and specific "ask-by-name" incidence at the point of sale, not just ad impressions.

**Verdict:** The risk of alienating the only customers (OEMs) while betting the marketing budget on an unproven consumer behavior is too high to approve as proposed. The proposal lacks a staged entry or a verified mechanism to ensure OEM buy-in beyond financial coercion. It requires a pilot phase with clear exit criteria before full rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
