The proposal outlines a vertical integration strategy where a software firm with a minor mobile operating system share acquires its primary hardware partner to prevent defection to a dominant rival. The core objective is securing the hardware channel to sustain the software platform, which currently suffers from a lack of applications and developer support compared to the market leader.

However, the fundamental issue identified is an ecosystem deficit, not a distribution bottleneck. The market leader's dominance stems from a robust application library that attracts users and developers in a self-reinforcing cycle. Owning a struggling hardware division with declining sales does not automatically generate the missing software appeal. If consumers reject the platform due to limited apps, controlling the device manufacturer will not reverse this trend; it merely converts a variable cost into a fixed liability.

The financial commitment of €5.4 billion is substantial, particularly given the target's falling sales trajectory. This acquisition introduces significant execution risks, including the complex integration of a large workforce and potential retaliatory measures from other handset manufacturers who may abandon the software platform to avoid supporting a competitor. These reactions could accelerate the very decline the deal aims to prevent.

Furthermore, the premise that hardware ownership solves an app-ecosystem problem is unproven. History suggests that platform success relies on developer incentives and user adoption, factors not guaranteed by merging with a distressed hardware entity. The deal addresses a symptom (potential loss of a hardware partner) rather than the disease (lack of compelling software services). Without a clear path to revitalizing the app ecosystem independent of hardware ownership, this expenditure threatens to drain resources without securing long-term viability. The risks of cultural friction, alienating remaining partners, and inheriting a sinking business outweigh the theoretical benefits of vertical control in this specific context.

DECISION: NO-GO
