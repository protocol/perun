The proposal seeks over $1 billion to launch a mobile-only subscription service featuring high-budget, short-form professional content with A-list talent. While the technology offering seamless vertical and horizontal viewing is innovative, the fundamental market assumptions present critical flaws. The landscape in 2018 is already saturated with abundant free, ad-supported short-form video, including user-generated content, which directly competes for the same limited phone attention.

Introducing a premium paid model amidst rising "subscription fatigue" creates a significant barrier to entry. Consumers are increasingly resistant to adding another monthly fee, especially when comparable content formats are available at no cost. The proposal admits there is no proven demand for premium short-form content; launching a venture of this magnitude without validated market interest is financially reckless.

Furthermore, the strategy relies on high production budgets and A-list talent, which drastically increases the burn rate before revenue stability is achieved. Competing for phone attention against entrenched free platforms requires more than just superior video orientation technology; it demands a compelling value proposition that justifies the price point. Currently, the combination of unproven demand, high costs, and a hostile pricing environment suggests a high probability of failure. The risks regarding price sensitivity and competition outweigh the potential benefits of the proposed technological feature. Approving this now would expose the organization to substantial financial loss without a clear path to profitability or user adoption. The lack of evidence supporting consumer willingness to pay for this specific format makes the investment untenable in its current state.

DECISION: NO-GO
