The proposal outlines a strategic pivot for a phone maker in 2008, shifting from a web-app-only stance to a curated native app ecosystem. The model involves a single company-run store where developers set prices, the company retains a 30% revenue share, and every app undergoes mandatory review. While rival platforms already possess open distribution ecosystems, this proposal introduces a controlled gatekeeping mechanism.

The identified concerns are substantial: security vulnerabilities from untrusted code, potential instability affecting device performance, carrier friction over bypassed services, the operational burden of reviewing every submission, and the risk of diluting the product's signature polish. However, the proposed mitigation strategies directly address these risks. By mandating that the company reviews and approves every app, the plan creates a firewall against malicious or unstable software, directly protecting security and stability. This curation also preserves the user experience and product polish, differentiating the device from rivals with open, potentially chaotic distribution.

The 30% revenue share establishes a sustainable business model that incentivizes the company to maintain the infrastructure while allowing developers to set prices, fostering a vibrant market. Although carrier objections regarding bypassed services are valid, the control exerted through the review process allows the company to negotiate or filter specific functionalities that might trigger severe carrier backlash, rather than rejecting the entire concept. The workload of reviews is a necessary operational cost to ensure the high-quality standard required to compete.

Ignoring the shift to native apps in 2008, while rivals already have ecosystems, poses a greater existential threat than the operational challenges of managing a store. The web-app argument is becoming obsolete as user demand for deeper hardware integration grows. The controlled approach balances innovation with the brand's reputation for quality. The benefits of creating a sticky, monetizable ecosystem outweigh the logistical hurdles, which are manageable through the proposed review workflow. Delaying this transition would cede critical market ground to competitors.

DECISION: GO
