The proposal outlines a high-stakes pivot from a lucrative licensing model to a direct-to-consumer subscription service. While the goal of capturing tens of millions of subscribers is ambitious, the strategy relies on sustaining multi-year operating losses to undercut the market leader. This approach immediately eliminates billions in guaranteed licensing revenue, creating a massive financial hole that must be filled by unproven subscription income.

The execution risks are severe. The company has no history of running a technology platform at this scale, introducing significant operational vulnerability. Simultaneously, the plan invites cannibalization of existing profitable streams, including cable and theatrical releases, without assurance that new subscribers will compensate for these losses. The market leader already commands over a hundred million subscribers, establishing a high barrier to entry that a low-price strategy alone may not overcome.

Funding years of losses while building an audience requires immense capital resilience. If subscriber growth lags behind projections, the company faces a compounding crisis of shrinking revenue and expanding debt. The risks of technological failure, revenue erosion, and prolonged cash burn outweigh the potential long-term benefits of owning the customer relationship. Without a clearer path to profitability or a phased approach to mitigate immediate revenue loss, proceeding now is financially imprudent.

DECISION: NO-GO
