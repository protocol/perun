The proposal outlines a high-stakes strategic pivot: sacrificing immediate, guaranteed licensing revenue to build a direct-to-consumer streaming platform. The core logic relies on accepting multi-year operating losses to capture tens of millions of subscribers, undercutting the market leader on price. However, the risks are substantial and immediate. The company has no prior experience running a technology platform of this scale, introducing significant execution risk that could derail the launch or degrade user experience. Furthermore, the plan explicitly invites cannibalization of existing lucrative revenue streams from cable and theatrical releases while burning cash to fund operations.

The competitive landscape presents a formidable barrier. The market leader already commands over a hundred million subscribers, creating a massive moat of content depth and brand loyalty. Attempting to dislodge this dominance solely through lower pricing is dangerous; it compresses margins before scale is achieved and assumes price is the primary driver for consumers, ignoring content exclusivity and platform stability. The target of reaching tens of millions of subscribers within five years is ambitious but unproven, especially given the simultaneous loss of the steady licensing income that currently funds the business.

Crucially, the proposal asks the executive team to commit to a path where the company becomes a competitor to its own partners, instantly turning former revenue sources into adversaries. Without a proven tech infrastructure or a clear roadmap to profitability beyond "building an audience," the financial exposure is extreme. The combination of untested operational capabilities, the certainty of near-term revenue decline, and the intensity of competition suggests the current plan is too fragile. The potential for catastrophic cash burn without a guaranteed payoff outweighs the theoretical long-term benefits in their current form. A more measured approach, perhaps involving a phased rollout or strategic partnerships to mitigate tech risk, is necessary before committing to full-scale withdrawal from rivals. As it stands, the risk profile is unacceptable.

DECISION: NO-GO
