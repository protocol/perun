The proposal outlines a vertical integration strategy where a software company with a low single-digit mobile OS share acquires the primary hardware manufacturer running that system for €5.4 billion. The stated goals are to unify hardware and software control and prevent the manufacturer from defecting to the dominant rival platform. However, the factual context presents severe structural hurdles. The target's smartphone sales are already declining, suggesting the asset is losing market relevance rather than gaining traction. Furthermore, the market leader possesses a decisive advantage in applications and developer support, which is identified as the core ecosystem problem.

The proposal explicitly questions whether owning hardware can resolve an app-ecosystem deficit. History and market dynamics suggest that developer adoption follows user base size and engagement, not just hardware ownership. Acquiring a shrinking hardware division does not automatically attract developers away from a platform with established dominance. Additionally, the risks of integrating a large workforce and provoking retaliatory actions from other handset makers could further fragment the already small ecosystem. If other manufacturers perceive the software owner as a direct competitor rather than a neutral platform provider, they may accelerate their own shift to the rival platform, effectively isolating the proposer.

Spending €5.4 billion on a declining asset to solve a software ecosystem problem appears misaligned. The capital expenditure is massive relative to the low single-digit market share, and the strategic logic relies on the assumption that hardware control dictates software success, despite evidence that the app gap is the primary barrier. The risks of alienating remaining hardware partners while absorbing a losing business unit create a high probability of accelerating decline rather than reversing it. Without a clear mechanism to instantly reverse the app deficit or stop the sales fall through this acquisition alone, the move threatens to drain resources without securing the necessary market position. The fundamental mismatch between the problem (lack of apps/users) and the proposed solution (buying falling hardware) warrants rejection in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
