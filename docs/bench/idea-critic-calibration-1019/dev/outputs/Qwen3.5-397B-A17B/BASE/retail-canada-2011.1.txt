The proposal outlines an aggressive international expansion for a US discount retailer into a neighboring market in 2011. The strategy involves acquiring 130 to 150 existing store leases and opening nearly all locations within two years. This approach relies on establishing an entirely new supply chain and inventory-management system from scratch. While the target market offers opportunities due to higher competitor prices on certain goods, it is dominated by a larger, entrenched rival.

Significant operational hurdles exist. The company lacks experience in cross-border logistics, local regulations, and bilingual labeling requirements. The proposal explicitly acknowledges critical risks: the rapid pace of simultaneous store openings, the unproven nature of the new supply chain systems, and potential mismatches in price perception.

Executing this plan introduces extreme vulnerability. Launching over 100 stores before validating the new supply chain and inventory systems creates a high probability of systemic failure. If the logistics network falters, shelves will remain empty across the entire new region simultaneously, damaging the brand's reputation for value and reliability before it can gain traction. Furthermore, attempting to master complex regulatory and labeling landscapes while managing a massive construction and hiring rollout stretches organizational capacity too thin.

The presence of a dominant local competitor means there is little room for error. A slower, phased approach would allow the company to test its supply chain, refine its pricing strategy, and adapt to local regulations with a smaller footprint. By committing to such a vast scale immediately, the company risks catastrophic financial loss and operational collapse if the unproven systems cannot handle the volume. The combination of untested technology, unfamiliar regulatory environments, and an accelerated timeline makes the current proposal untenable. The risks outweigh the potential rewards in their current configuration.

DECISION: NO-GO
