The proposal involves acquiring the dominant online video platform for $1.65 billion in stock despite the target having zero meaningful revenue, a tiny staff, and massive operational costs. The timing is October 2006, meaning the target is less than two years old. The primary asset is market leadership in user-generated content, but this comes with severe liabilities: skyrocketing bandwidth bills and imminent copyright lawsuits from major media companies regarding unauthorized TV and film clips.

The acquiring company already operates a smaller, internal video service, suggesting this purchase is a defensive move to eliminate a rival and capture the market instantly rather than build organically. However, the financial mechanics are deeply concerning. Paying such a colossal premium for an entity with no proven advertising model creates an enormous burden on the buyer's balance sheet. The risks are not theoretical; they are immediate and existential. Copyright litigation could result in crippling damages or an injunction that shuts down the core service. Furthermore, scaling bandwidth costs without a corresponding revenue stream could drain cash reserves rapidly.

While being the largest destination offers network effects, the path to monetization is unproven. The proposal admits the ad model is uncertain. Betting the company's stock on the hope that advertising revenue will eventually outpace legal settlements and infrastructure costs is a speculative gamble of the highest order. The lack of files, network, or tools prevents deeper due diligence, but the facts presented paint a picture of an asset that is legally toxic and financially unsustainable at the proposed price. The combination of high acquisition cost, legal exposure, and operational losses outweighs the strategic benefit of market share. Approving this now exposes the executive team to unacceptable liability without a clear roadmap to profitability. The prudent course is to reject the deal in its current form until legal risks are mitigated and a viable revenue model is demonstrated.

DECISION: NO-GO
