The all-stock structure mitigates immediate cash flow strain, though it does dilute current ownership. The risk of the famous founder becoming a major shareholder is significant but manageable; their continued alignment serves the conglomerate's interests better than their departure to a rival. Culture clash and dependence on key individuals are valid concerns, yet the alternative—allowing the partnership to lapse—guarantees a loss of market share and creative momentum. Given the recent failures of the internal unit, external intervention is necessary. The cost of inaction exceeds the financial premium of the deal. Securing this asset prevents a competitor from gaining a decisive advantage and provides an immediate turnaround mechanism for the conglomerate's animation division. The strategic necessity of controlling this content engine outweighs the identified financial and operational risks.
