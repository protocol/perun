Steelman: Rapidly acquiring 130–150 sites leverages existing lease opportunities to achieve immediate scale, deterring competitor retaliation before the new supply chain and inventory systems are fully proven in a cross-border context.

However, this claim fails the pre-mortem test. The proposal bundles three unproven, high-risk variables into a single irreversible launch: (1) an untested international supply chain, (2) a new inventory management system, and (3) a "big bang" rollout of nearly 150 stores within two years. In retail, supply chain friction is the primary killer of margin; introducing a new, unproven system while simultaneously scaling to 150 locations creates a single point of catastrophic failure. If the inventory system glitches or logistics stall, 150 stores will face stockouts simultaneously, destroying brand reputation in the new market instantly.

The "do nothing" or "pilot" alternative is superior. Opening 5–10 stores first would allow the retailer to debug the cross-border logistics, validate the inventory system under real load, and calibrate bilingual labeling without exposing the entire capital outlay. The entrenched competitor can easily absorb a small entrant but would struggle to react to a sudden, massive saturation—*if* the operations work. Since the operations are explicitly "not yet proven," the scale is premature.

The price perception risk compounds this; if the supply chain fails to maintain low costs due to inefficiencies, the core value proposition vanishes. The proposal assumes the new systems will work at scale immediately, a dangerous assumption given the complexity of cross-border regulation and logistics. There is no kill criterion or phased approach to limit exposure. The cost of delay (waiting to prove the system) is negligible compared to the cost of failing at 150 locations.

This plan prioritizes speed over viability, ignoring the necessity of operational proof before capital commitment. The risk of total market exit due to systemic operational collapse is too high to approve as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
