The claim is that a pre-revenue grocery startup should immediately commit $1 billion to build automated warehouses in a dozen cities before validating unit economics. The steelman argues that speed and infrastructure dominance are necessary to secure market share and deter competitors in a winner-take-all landscape.

However, this proposal fails the fundamental test of startup strategy: learning before scaling. Groceries operate on razor-thin margins where density and operational efficiency dictate survival. The proposal explicitly admits there is no operating track record and unit economics are unknown. Committing irreversible capital ($1 billion) to highly specialized, automated infrastructure across multiple markets assumes the business model works at scale without evidence it works at all. This violates the "two-way door" principle; once built, these warehouses cannot be easily repurposed if the model fails.

Better ways exist: lease existing space, use manual picking to learn workflows, and dominate a single neighborhood before expanding to a city, let alone a dozen. The cost of delay here is negligible compared to the cost of building the wrong system. The pre-mortem is clear: the company burns through its venture capital constructing empty warehouses in cities where customer density never reaches the break-even point required to support 30-minute delivery slots.

The strongest attack is the mismatch between "unknown unit economics" and "billion-dollar fixed commitment." No amount of venture capital insulates against a broken model multiplied by twelve cities. The risks of capital intensity and thin margins are not mitigated by speed; they are exacerbated by it. Without a proven pilot demonstrating that a single warehouse can be profitable, scaling to a dozen is not strategy; it is gambling with other people's money.

The proposal lacks any kill criteria or phased approach. It demands an all-in bet on unproven assumptions. The only prudent path is to validate the model in the current metropolitan area, achieve positive unit economics, and then expand.

DECISION: NO-GO
