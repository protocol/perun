The claim assumes taste preference is the sole driver of cola sales, ignoring that brand value often resides in heritage and emotional identity rather than sensory metrics alone. The steelman posits that maximizing immediate sensory satisfaction will reverse market share loss. However, this ignores the "endowment effect," where consumers value the existing product higher simply because they own the tradition.

The better way is to launch the sweeter formula as a new variant alongside the original, capturing the taste-test winners without alienating the core base. The proposal to withdraw the original entirely creates a one-way door with catastrophic reversibility costs; once the old formula is gone, the brand severs its historical contract with loyalists.

The pre-mortem reveals a specific failure mode: the market reacts not to the taste of the new drink, but to the *loss* of the old one. Because testing did not disclose the withdrawal, the data is fundamentally flawed; it measured addition, not replacement. Participants preferred the sweeter taste in a vacuum but were never asked if they would accept the deletion of their current favorite. This invalidates the primary evidence supporting the total swap.

The cost of delay is low compared to the risk of immediate, irreversible brand damage. A "go-if" condition requiring a test where participants are explicitly told the original will vanish was not met. Without a fallback product, the company bets the entire franchise on unverified emotional resilience. The lack of a phased rollout or a permanent slot for the legacy formula violates basic portfolio risk management. The strongest attack—that removing the original triggers a backlash disproportionate to taste gains—survives because the provided data explicitly excluded this variable.

There is no kill criterion defined for consumer outrage, nor a leading metric to track sentiment beyond sales volume initially. Proceeding now ignores the verified risk that the product is not just a beverage but a cultural icon.

DECISION: NO-GO
