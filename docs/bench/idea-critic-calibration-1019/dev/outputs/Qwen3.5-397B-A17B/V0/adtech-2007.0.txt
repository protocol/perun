The claim is that a $6 billion cash acquisition of an ad-serving platform in May 2007 is the optimal path to compete with a search leader who just acquired a rival.

**Steelman:** Buying the market's leading independent platform instantly grants scale, technology, and inventory access, preventing the search leader from monopolizing the emerging display market.

**Skeptic:** The price represents a massive premium with no proven ROI in a volatile, fast-changing sector. Paying entirely in cash depletes reserves needed for organic R&D or other opportunities. The "conflict" with existing agency customers is not a minor hurdle; it threatens the core revenue stream of the buyer's current business model if those agencies flee to competitors to avoid fueling a rival.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "partner" option avoids immediate capital destruction. Instead of a $6 billion outlay, the company could build internal capacity or form exclusive licensing deals with multiple smaller networks, diversifying risk rather than betting the company on a single, expensive asset that may become obsolete as technology shifts.

**Kill Criteria & Pre-mortem:** Assume the acquisition fails in 18 months. Why? Because the integrated technology proved incompatible with the buyer's culture, the premium paid made profitability impossible, and key agency clients defected due to conflict of interest. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a botched integration. The market is moving too fast for a rigid, expensive purchase to remain advantageous.

**Decision:** The proposal fails the fit and risk assessment. The cash cost is excessive relative to the unproven long-term value of the target in a shifting market. The conflict with existing customers introduces an immediate, unquantified liability that could erode the buyer's current standing. The strategic necessity does not justify the financial recklessness of an all-cash deal at a large premium without a clear, immediate path to neutralizing customer conflicts.

DECISION: NO-GO
