**Steelman:** Leveraging existing, spiky infrastructure to sell unused compute capacity creates a high-margin revenue stream that subsidizes core retail capital expenses, turning a cost center into a profit center before competitors realize the model's viability.

**Skeptic:** The claim fails on operational reality. Retail reliability standards differ fundamentally from developer platform expectations; intermittent outages acceptable for internal batch jobs will destroy credibility with external paying customers. The organization lacks the specific DNA for developer relations, support, and SLA enforcement. Investors viewing heavy capex as a negative will likely view this expansion as dangerous distraction rather than efficiency, increasing the cost of capital.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" option preserves focus on the core retail margin battle. A safer alternative is licensing the internal virtualization tools to existing hardware vendors rather than operating the service directly, capturing value without assuming operational risk or brand exposure.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 24 months: The platform suffers a major outage during a retail peak event because resources were oversold to external tenants, causing retail revenue loss. The brand is damaged, investors revolt over the dual failure of retail performance and cloud losses, and the unit is shut down.

**Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits the market is "unproven" and the company has "no experience." There is no evidence that thin retail margins can absorb the inevitable initial losses of a platform business. The strategy relies on a "build it and they will come" assumption in a market with entrenched incumbents.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a one-way door with massive irreversible capital allocation. Without a committed anchor tenant, verified unit economics showing margin accretion within two quarters, and a ring-fenced budget that cannot impact retail operations, the risk of catastrophic distraction is too high. The proposal offers none of these safeguards.

**Verdict:** The proposal asks to enter an unproven market, against incumbents, without relevant experience, using capital investors already question, risking the core business. It fails the "better-way" test against focus and the "pre-mortem" test on operational conflict.

DECISION: NO-GO
