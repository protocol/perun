**Steelman:** Paying $19B secures the dominant mobile messaging layer to prevent existential displacement of the core social network, buying time to integrate users before a competitor does.

**Skeptic Attack:** The price is 12x the prior valuation with zero revenue and no monetization path. The target explicitly rejects advertising, clashing with the buyer's core model. Privacy regulations could fragment the user base post-acquisition. Founder retention is uncertain given the cultural clash between a fee-based privacy focus and an ad-based data model. If the founders leave or the user base migrates due to policy changes, the asset becomes worthless instantly.

**Better Way:** Build a competing messaging feature internally or acquire a smaller, compatible platform for a fraction of the cost. Alternatively, partner via API instead of full ownership to test integration risks.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a one-way door; reversal is impossible once cash/stock is spent. The cost of delay is high if competitors move first, but the cost of error is catastrophic ($19B loss).

**Evidence & Assumptions:** The proposal assumes user loyalty transfers to the parent company and that monetization can be forced later without churn. It assumes the $1 fee is a barrier to entry for competitors rather than a limit on scale. No data supports the claim that $19B is the minimum required to secure this position.

**Pre-mortem:** In 24 months, regulators block data integration, founders quit, users revolt against new terms, and the app dies, leaving a $19B goodwill impairment.

**Decision:** The valuation relies entirely on unproven assumptions about future monetization and retention under new ownership. The lack of a revenue model, combined with explicit cultural resistance to the buyer's business model, creates an unacceptable risk profile. The price exceeds any reasonable discounted cash flow analysis based on current facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
