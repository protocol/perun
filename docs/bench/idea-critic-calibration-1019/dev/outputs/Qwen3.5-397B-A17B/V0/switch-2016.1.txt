The claim is that a hybrid console with modest specs, priced at parity with powerful rivals, and a thin first-party launch will succeed where a previous home console failed and handhelds are eroding.

**Steelman:** The unique "play anywhere" form factor creates a new market category, making raw power comparisons irrelevant. First-party software quality compensates for quantity, and the hybrid nature justifies the price despite weaker specs.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Price/Performance Mismatch:** Pricing a modest mobile chip equal to far more powerful stationary rivals is a critical vulnerability. Consumers often equate price with performance; this invites direct comparison where the proposal admits defeat. *Alternative:* Price significantly lower to reflect hardware reality or delay until specs improve.
2.  **Confusing Positioning:** After a prior confusing launch, introducing a "both/and" device risks alienating the core base further without a crystal-clear message. *Alternative:* Focus strictly on handheld or home, not both, to simplify marketing.
3.  **Software Risk:** A thin first-year lineup relies entirely on immediate hits. If one stumbles, there is no depth to retain users. *Alternative:* Delay launch to build a stronger software buffer.
4.  **Supply Constraints:** Hardware novelty (detachable controllers, dock) increases manufacturing complexity, risking stockouts during the critical holiday window.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to curiosity but fails to retain users due to poor third-party support (driven by weak specs) and consumer confusion over its identity versus price. Sales miss targets, compounding previous financial losses.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders do not exceed the previous console's launch week by 20% within the first month, or if attach rate for software drops below 2.5 units per console in Q1, the strategy is failing.

**Verdict:** The proposal ignores the fundamental economic disconnect between "modest power" and "rival pricing" while betting the company on a confusing form factor and a thin software slate. The risks of repeating past financial failures are too high without a clearer value proposition or price adjustment.

DECISION: NO-GO
