**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only viable path to demonstrate the integrated hardware-software music strategy, as third-party staff actively undermine the premium value proposition, causing continued losses despite product viability.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is fixed-cost collapse: high mall leases during a recession could accelerate bankruptcy before brand equity shifts. Channel conflict may trigger immediate revenue drops if resellers retaliate by de-prioritizing shelf space. Alternatives include:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Continue losses; brand dilution worsens as competitors control the narrative.
2.  **Shop-in-Shop:** Dedicated branded zones within existing retailers. Lower cost, but fails to solve the staff-incentive problem or fully control the experience.
3.  **Online Direct:** Avoids leases but cannot demonstrate the music ecosystem physically, missing the core strategic pivot.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with a "differentiation" north star, rejecting the commodity trap. However, assuming a two-year ramp-up ignores the cash-burn reality of a loss-making entity. If the music strategy fails to resonate, these stores become unexitable liabilities. The industry's poor retail track record suggests operational incompetence is a systemic risk, not just a market condition.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:**
The proposal lacks a capped financial runway. Without a strict stop-loss, this is an unlimited bet. The "demonstration" argument holds only if the conversion rate in-store vastly exceeds current channels, yet no pilot data exists to prove this unit economics shift.

**Verdict:**
The strategic logic is sound (control the narrative), but the financial structure is fatal for a loss-making company in a recession. Opening a full chain immediately violates basic capital preservation. The company must prove the unit economics work in a single location before scaling to "prime shopping-mall locations" nationally. Approving the full chain now ignores the "fixed lease cost" risk that could kill the firm within 18 months.

**Decision:**
Do not approve the full chain rollout. Require a single flagship pilot with a hard 12-month kill criterion based on gross margin per square foot and verified incremental sales (not cannibalized from resellers).

DECISION: NO-GO
