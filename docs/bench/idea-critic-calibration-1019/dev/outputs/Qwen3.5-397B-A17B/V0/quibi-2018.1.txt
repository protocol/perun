The claim assumes a massive untapped market for premium, short-form subscription video despite abundant free alternatives and rising subscription fatigue. The steelman posits that high-production value and exclusive A-list talent in a mobile-native format (vertical/horizontal adaptive) create a unique value proposition sufficient to overcome price sensitivity and attention competition.

However, the proposal fails the "better-way" and "fit" tests critically. First, it ignores the dominant market reality: free, ad-supported short-form video (UGC and professional) already saturates user attention on existing platforms. Asking users to pay a premium for what they currently get for free, merely with higher budgets, is a weak value proposition without proven demand. Second, the "technology feature" of adaptive orientation is a commodity expectation, not a differentiator; major platforms already handle this. Third, the capital requirement ($1B+) for content creation in a crowded attention economy presents an asymmetric risk: if the "premium short-form" hypothesis is false, the entire investment is lost with no pivot path, as the content library would have little residual value outside the failed subscription model.

The pre-mortem is clear: the service launches, acquires a small initial base due to brand name, but fails to retain subscribers against free competitors, leading to a rapid burn of capital and shutdown. The cost of delay is negligible; waiting for proven demand signals is safer than betting a billion on an unproven format. The proposal offers no kill criteria or measured pilot; it demands a full-scale launch based on executive intuition rather than data. The existence of free substitutes and the lack of a verified willingness to pay for this specific format make the risk unacceptable.

DECISION: NO-GO
