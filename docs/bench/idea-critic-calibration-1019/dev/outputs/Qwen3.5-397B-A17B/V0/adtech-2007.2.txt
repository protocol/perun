Steelman: Acquiring the agency and platform instantly grants critical scale and technology to challenge the search leader's recent move, preventing permanent market exclusion. However, the $6 billion cash price represents a massive premium in a volatile, fast-changing sector. The "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw: paying a premium for an agency business model creates immediate conflicts with the buyer's existing agency customers, who may flee to competitors to avoid funding a rival. Furthermore, owning heavy infrastructure in a rapidly shifting technology market risks rapid obsolescence compared to lighter, organic development or smaller, tech-only tuck-ins. The pre-mortem indicates that integration friction and customer churn could destroy the acquired value within two years, leaving the buyer with a bloated cost base and no market share gain. The cost of delay is low; waiting allows the market to clarify which ad-serving technologies actually survive, avoiding the risk of buying the "wrong" standard at the peak price. The evidence suggests the premium pays for current revenue that is at high risk of evaporating due to the very conflicts the acquisition creates. A capped, time-boxed pilot partnership or a smaller technology-only acquisition would test the waters without exposing the company to catastrophic balance sheet damage. The kill criterion is clear: if the acquisition cannot guarantee retention of 90% of existing agency clients within six months, the strategic premise collapses. Given the unchecked conflict risk and the extreme price in a nascent market, the proposal fails the risk-reward test.

DECISION: NO-GO
