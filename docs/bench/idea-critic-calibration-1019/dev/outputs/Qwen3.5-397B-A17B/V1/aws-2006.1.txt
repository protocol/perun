The claim is that a retailer should sell infrastructure as a service despite having no relevant experience, facing entrenched competitors, and operating under investor scrutiny regarding capital intensity. The steelman is that existing spiky infrastructure represents sunk capacity that, if productized, could generate high-margin revenue from a nascent but potentially massive market, diversifying revenue beyond thin-retail margins.

However, the skeptic hat identifies fatal flaws in the current proposal. First, the "unproven market" for hourly server rental combined with "no experience" in developer platforms creates a high probability of building a product that fails to meet enterprise reliability standards, damaging the core retail brand. Second, the proposal ignores the massive cultural and operational shift required; selling to developers is fundamentally different from retail logistics. Third, with investors already questioning capital spending, doubling down on infrastructure for an unproven external market increases financial risk without a guaranteed return.

The "better-way" analysis suggests that "doing nothing" or licensing the internal tools to a single partner is safer than a full public launch. The cheapest alternative is to validate demand via a private beta with known partners before committing to a distinct business line. The cost of delay is low because the market is undefined; being first matters less than being reliable.

The pre-mortem reveals that the most likely failure mode is a reliability outage during a retail peak event caused by external load, leading to lost retail revenue and investor panic. The proposal lacks specific kill criteria, a phased rollout plan, or evidence that the internal team can support external SLAs. The risks of distraction and margin dilution are not mitigated by the current plan; they are merely acknowledged. Without a pilot program to prove the technology and market fit, and without a clear path to addressing the competency gap, the proposal is too speculative given the financial constraints.

DECISION: NO-GO
