The claim is that launching a new mid-priced division in 1957 will capture growth and fill a portfolio gap. The steelman: a dedicated brand prevents cannibalization while targeting a proven, expanding demographic with tailored styling.

However, the "better-way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the timing and infrastructure strategy. The proposal admits a recession risk and a potentially shrinking segment, yet commits to a massive, irreversible fixed-cost expansion (new dealer network) right before the debut. History shows mid-priced segments are the first to collapse in downturns as consumers trade down to low-priced reliability or hold existing vehicles. Building a standalone dealer network creates immense overhead that cannot be scaled down quickly if the market contracts.

The "do nothing" or "cheaper alternative" option is to badge-engineer a mid-priced model within the existing low-priced or premium dealer networks. This captures the same customer intent without the catastrophic fixed costs of new real estate and staff if the recession hits. The current plan bets the company's liquidity on a specific market timing (1957 growth) that the proposal itself flags as uncertain.

The pre-mortem is clear: a 1958 recession causes the new division to miss volume targets immediately. With high fixed costs from the new dealer network and low margins from discounted inventory, the division bleeds cash rapidly, dragging down the parent company's core profitable lines. The "kill criterion" here is the recession risk itself; proceeding with full infrastructure build-out before economic certainty is a one-way door with asymmetric downside.

The strongest attack survived is the branding necessity, but the execution method (new dealers) fails the risk/reward test given the admitted recession probability. The proposal must be revised to utilize existing distribution channels or delay the network build until economic indicators stabilize. Approving this as proposed exposes the firm to existential financial risk based on optimistic timing in a volatile cycle.

DECISION: NO-GO
