**Steelman:** Paying $19B secures the dominant mobile messaging layer to prevent existential displacement of the core business, buying time to integrate a network effect that cannot be built organically.

**Attack:** The price implies a 12,600% increase in value in twelve months ($1.5B to $19B) for a company with negligible revenue, no monetization strategy, and an explicit anti-advertising stance. The unit economics are undefined; paying $380 per employee or roughly $60–$100 per user (estimates vary on exact MAU) assumes the target can pivot to monetization without destroying the engagement that makes it valuable. Founder retention is a single point of failure; if the founders exit post-vesting, the asset may become a hollow shell. Regulatory scrutiny on privacy and monopoly power could block integration or force divestiture, rendering the strategic "moat" useless. The "do nothing" option risks slow erosion, but this bet risks immediate, massive capital destruction for an unproven hypothesis.

**Better Way:** A minority stake with a board seat allows observation of monetization experiments without full balance-sheet exposure. Alternatively, build a competing feature set leveraging existing cash flows, accepting slower growth but retaining capital discipline.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a one-way door with irreversible capital outflow. The cost of delay is the risk of user migration accelerating, but the cost of error is a $17.5B write-down. There is no pilot possible at this scale.

**Verdict:** The proposal lacks a verified path to ROI. The valuation relies entirely on the assumption that "preventing displacement" justifies any price, ignoring that overpaying can itself cause the decline it seeks to prevent by starving other innovation. The lack of a monetization plan combined with the target's ideological refusal of ads makes the revenue model speculative. Without a concrete integration plan that respects the target's culture while extracting value, the risk of culture clash and talent flight is extreme. The price creates an impossible hurdle for future returns.

DECISION: NO-GO
