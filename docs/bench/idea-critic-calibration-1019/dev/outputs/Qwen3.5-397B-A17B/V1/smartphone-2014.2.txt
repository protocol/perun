The claim is that a differentiated 3D display and visual search justify launching a flagship-priced smartphone on a forked OS with a limited app store and single-carrier exclusivity in a saturated 2014 market.

**Steelman:** Unique hardware features and seamless shopping integration create a niche premium device that bypasses the need for a full app ecosystem, leveraging the retailer's brand loyalty to succeed despite platform limitations.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "fit" and "options" tests. In 2014, smartphone utility is defined by app availability, not display novelty. A forked OS lacking mainstream apps is a fatal flaw for a flagship-priced device; users will not pay premium prices for a broken ecosystem. The "3D-like" feature is a gimmick with no proven demand, while the "one-touch" shopping can be delivered via a software app on existing dominant platforms at a fraction of the cost. Carrier exclusivity severely caps total addressable market just as the category matures. The "do nothing" option (focusing on the successful e-reader/tablet or releasing a shopping app) offers higher ROI with zero hardware risk.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. The cause is the app gap: consumers reject the hardware because their essential daily apps are missing, rendering the 3D feature irrelevant. The price point accelerates rejection.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the market is saturated and dominated by two platforms, yet offers no strategy to overcome the network effect of their app stores. It relies on unproven hardware differentiation to override established user behavior.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a one-way door with massive inventory risk. There is no pilot path mentioned; it is a full launch. The lack of a "go-if" condition regarding app developer commitment or pre-order validation makes this reckless. A safer alternative is to license the visual search technology to existing platform users.

**Verdict:** The combination of a premium price, a crippled app ecosystem, and carrier exclusivity in a saturated market presents a fatal flaw. The differentiation does not compensate for the loss of core utility.

DECISION: NO-GO
