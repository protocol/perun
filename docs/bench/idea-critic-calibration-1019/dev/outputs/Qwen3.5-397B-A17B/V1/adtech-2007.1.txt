The claim is that a $6B cash acquisition of an ad-serving platform is the necessary and sufficient move to catch the search leader. The steelman: owning the full stack prevents the competitor from monopolizing display inventory, which is critical as search growth saturates.

However, the "better way" analysis reveals fatal flaws. First, the price is extreme for 2007, representing a massive premium in a nascent, unproven display market where technology shifts rapidly. Second, the proposal ignores the immediate destruction of value via channel conflict: the buyer's existing agency customers will flee to competitors rather than share data with a platform owned by their rival. Third, the "do nothing" or "partner" option is safer; building or licensing avoids the integration risk and cultural clash of buying a massive agency.

The pre-mortem is clear: integration stalls, key talent leaves due to culture clash, agencies defect, and the $6B cash reserve (needed for other R&D) is depleted with no ROI. The cost of delay is low because the display market is not yet mature enough to justify a "winner take all" bet at this valuation. The evidence suggests the technology is too volatile to lock in via acquisition now.

This fails the kill criteria: there is no observable metric proving this specific target can overcome the channel conflict or justify the premium compared to organic growth. The risk of alienating the current customer base is a concrete failure scenario not mitigated by the plan. The proposal assumes the market will freeze in its current state, allowing time for integration, which contradicts the "fast-changing" nature of the sector.

DECISION: NO-GO
