The claim is that a $1B+ mobile-only, premium short-form subscription service will succeed despite market saturation and subscription fatigue. The steelman: high-production, orientation-agnostic video fills a gap between amateur clips and long-form TV, leveraging A-list talent to overcome discovery friction.

However, the proposal fails the "do nothing" and "cheaper alternative" tests. Free, abundant short-form video already dominates mobile attention; user-generated content and ad-supported models have proven scale where subscription models struggle for short durations. The "orientation-agnostic" feature is a hygiene factor, not a moat, as major platforms already support both modes. The core assumption—that users will pay a premium for professional short content when free alternatives exist—is unsupported by 2018 market data, which shows declining willingness to add more subscriptions ("fatigue").

The pre-mortem reveals a fatal flaw: the business model relies on acquiring subscribers in a zero-sum attention economy against entrenched free competitors, with no clear path to profitability given the high burn rate of A-list talent and production costs. The cost of delay is low; waiting for clearer demand signals or testing with a capped, lower-budget pilot would reduce the $1B exposure. The proposal ignores the "Chesterton's fence" of why existing players haven't solved this with a paid model: the unit economics likely don't support it.

The strongest attack is the lack of proven demand for *premium* short-form. The proposal offers no evidence that "professional production" drives willingness to pay for <10 minute episodes, especially when competitors offer similar content for free. The kill criterion—subscription growth against free alternatives—is not addressed with a realistic acquisition strategy.

This is a one-way door with massive capital risk and no validated product-market fit. The safer, smaller alternative is a staged pilot with a fraction of the budget to test pricing and retention before scaling. As proposed, the plan commits real money without a validated hypothesis.

DECISION: NO-GO
