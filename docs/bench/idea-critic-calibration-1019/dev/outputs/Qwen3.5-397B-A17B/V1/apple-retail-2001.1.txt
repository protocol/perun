**Verdict: REVISE (Functionally NO-GO as proposed)**

The claim that company-run stores are *needed* to demonstrate the product and digital-music strategy fails the "do nothing" and "cheaper alternative" tests. The proposal assumes physical presence in prime malls is the only way to solve the demonstration problem, ignoring that third-party retailers already have the foot traffic; the issue is their incentive structure, not the lack of a venue.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The argument that third-party staff actively push cheaper competitors is valid; without a controlled environment, the premium product loses. However, the proposed solution (high-fixed-cost malls during a recession) is a disproportionate response to an incentive problem.

**Better Ways:**
1.  **Shop-in-Shop:** Lease small, branded kiosks *inside* existing major retailers. This solves the "staff push competitors" issue by controlling the immediate sales environment and demonstration area without bearing full mall lease overhead or inventory risk.
2.  **Incentive Realignment:** Pay third-party staff directly for every unit sold or demonstration completed, turning them into advocates. This is variable cost, not fixed.
3.  **Pop-up Tours:** Temporary locations in high-traffic areas during peak seasons to demonstrate the music strategy without long-term leases.

**Kill Criteria & Pre-mortem:**
If the company proceeds with full malls:
*   **Failure Scenario:** A recession deepens. Sales volume does not immediately cover high fixed leases. The company burns cash rapidly, accelerating losses. Channel conflict causes resellers to drop the brand entirely, shrinking total reach before stores can compensate.
*   **Metric:** Cash burn per store must not exceed X% of monthly marketing budget within 6 months.
*   **Stop Condition:** If any single store fails to meet 80% of projected unit economics by Month 9, the entire rollout halts.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal commits to the highest-risk, highest-fixed-cost option without testing lower-cost alternatives that address the core incentive mismatch. The "digital-music strategy" can be demonstrated in a kiosk or pop-up; it does not require a full mall footprint immediately. The risk of channel conflict is real but manageable via incentives before resorting to cutting out partners.

**Recommendation:** Reject the full mall rollout. Approve a pilot of 5 "shop-in-shop" locations and a revised reseller incentive program. Re-evaluate in 12 months.

DECISION: NO-GO
