The claim is that acquiring the studio for $7.4 billion is the only viable path to secure its creative output and reverse the conglomerate's animation decline. The steelman posits that without ownership, the studio will inevitably partner with a rival, causing catastrophic long-term value loss that far exceeds the acquisition premium.

However, the "better-way" analysis reveals fatal flaws in the binary framing. The proposal assumes a partnership extension is impossible, yet the breakdown is over economics, not strategy. A structured profit-participation deal or a minority stake with first-look rights could secure content without the $7.4 billion equity risk, culture clash, or dilution from issuing stock to the founder. The "do nothing" option is not status quo; it is pursuing a non-ownership commercial alliance, which preserves capital and avoids integrating incompatible hand-drawn and CGI cultures.

The pre-mortem highlights a specific failure mode: the valuation relies on the studio maintaining its hit rate. With only one film a year, a single box-office disappointment post-acquisition would crater the stock price, making the $7.4 billion price tag look grotesque in hindsight. Furthermore, placing external creative leaders in charge of the existing unit invites immediate internal revolt and talent flight from the legacy hand-drawn division, destroying the very asset the buyer hopes to revitalize. The dependence on a few creative leaders is a key-person risk that ownership exacerbates rather than mitigates; if they leave, the buyer owns an expensive, empty shell.

The evidence provided shows the partnership talks failed on economics, implying the studio is willing to deal but at a higher price. Paying $7.4 billion effectively capitulates to those demands while adding massive integration risk. The proposal fails to demonstrate why a cheaper, reversible commercial agreement cannot achieve the primary goal of content security. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a botched integration.

DECISION: NO-GO
