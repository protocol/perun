**Steelman:** Capturing the growing mid-priced segment with a dedicated brand prevents customer defection to competitors and maximizes lifetime value across the corporate portfolio.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on linear extrapolation of a cyclical market. The "growing" segment is highly sensitive to disposable income; a recession (explicitly noted as a risk) would collapse this specific tier first, leaving the new division with high fixed costs and zero volume. Furthermore, building a parallel dealer network from scratch introduces massive capital expenditure and channel conflict just before a potential economic downturn. The "extensive research" likely reflects consumer sentiment during an economic peak, creating a dangerous lag between data collection and market reality at the 1957 debut.

**Better Way:** Utilize the existing premium or low-priced dealer networks to sell a "bridge" model under a sub-brand or distinct badging. This tests the price point with minimal infrastructure risk. Alternatively, delay the standalone network launch until the business cycle proves resilience against a recession.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If a recession hits in 1957-58 (a plausible post-Korea war cycle), the new division becomes a cash incinerator, dragging down the parent company's liquidity. The cost of delay is lost market share, but the cost of failure is existential financial strain. The requirement for a new dealer network is a one-way door that cannot be easily reversed without massive write-downs.

**Evidence & Calibration:** The proposal admits the segment "could shrink" and cites "recession risk" yet proceeds with maximum capital intensity (new brand, new dealers). This contradicts prudent capital allocation in the face of identified macro-headwinds. The kill criterion—market contraction—is treated as a footnote rather than a gate. A sound strategy would stage the infrastructure investment, not commit it fully before the economic outlook clears.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the reversibility and risk-adjusted return test. It bets the farm on a specific economic continuity that the proposal itself doubts. The existence of a known, high-impact risk (recession) combined with a high-fixed-cost execution strategy (new dealers) mandates a halt to the current plan.

DECISION: NO-GO
