**Steelman:** Acquiring the startup secures a free, open mobile OS to prevent search monopolization on emerging devices, betting that ubiquitous mobile web access will expand ad inventory enough to justify the $50M cost despite no direct hardware revenue.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is irrelevance: if handset makers and carriers reject the OS to protect existing licensing fees or control, the $50M buys only a dormant codebase with no path to influencing search volume. The "open source" lure may not outweigh the integration costs and carrier resistance to unproven platforms.
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve $50M cash; risk being locked out if a competitor acquires this tech or builds a walled garden that blocks your search engine.
*   **Partnership/Licensing:** Attempt to license the tech without acquisition. Risk: The tiny startup lacks the capital to fight legal/technical battles against giants, likely failing before maturity.
*   **Internal Build:** Build an OS from scratch. Risk: Too slow; the mobile window is opening now.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the north star of "organizing the world's information" by ensuring access extends to pockets. However, assuming a pre-mortem where the project fails in 3 years: the cause is likely carrier blocking and lack of flagship handset adoption, leaving the OS fragmented and unused. The cost of delay is ceding the mobile interface to rivals who will default users to their own search.

**Evidence & Calibration:**
The proposal admits no revenue model and high adoption risk. The $50M price tag is speculative for a six-person team with no product. The "free" model relies on altruistic adoption by competitors (handset makers) who have inverted incentives to maintain the status quo. Without a committed flagship partner or carrier subsidy, the "build it and they will come" strategy is a fatal flaw in a controlled distribution market. The risk of total capital loss with zero influence on search volume is unmitigated.

**Verdict:**
The proposal lacks a concrete mechanism to force adoption against entrenched carrier/handset incentives. Buying the tech does not solve the distribution bottleneck. The $50M bet relies on hope rather than a leveraged path to market.

DECISION: NO-GO
