The claim is that a retail company should launch an unproven, capital-intensive cloud computing business in 2006 despite lacking relevant experience, facing investor skepticism, and confronting established competitors.

Steelman: Leveraging existing spiky infrastructure to sell excess capacity creates a high-margin revenue stream that diversifies the business beyond thin-retail margins, turning a cost center into a profit center.

However, the skepticism is severe. The company has zero operational experience running a developer platform at scale, a fundamentally different business model requiring distinct reliability SLAs and support structures. The market is unproven; developers may not trust a retailer with their core infrastructure over established hardware vendors. The capital requirements are massive, directly conflicting with investor concerns about heavy spending. The risk of distraction is existential: failing to meet enterprise reliability expectations could damage the core retail brand.

Better ways exist. The company could first validate demand by selling limited, non-critical batch processing capacity to internal teams or select partners before a public launch. Alternatively, they could partner with an existing infrastructure provider rather than building a competing platform from scratch. Doing nothing preserves capital and focus on the core retail business, which is already under investor scrutiny.

The pre-mortem reveals a likely failure mode: a major outage for early external customers triggers a PR crisis, causing the retail stock to plummet and forcing an immediate shutdown of the initiative after wasting hundreds of millions in CapEx. This is a one-way door; once the brand is associated with unreliable infrastructure, it is hard to recover. The cost of delay is low; the market is unproven, so waiting a year to see if competitors succeed or fail carries minimal opportunity cost compared to the risk of being the first to fail spectacularly.

The proposal lacks a pilot, a defined kill criterion for the pilot, and a plan to address the specific lack of platform expertise. The risks of margin dilution and distraction are not mitigated, only acknowledged. The evidence suggests the company is betting the farm on an unproven hypothesis without a safety net.

DECISION: NO-GO
