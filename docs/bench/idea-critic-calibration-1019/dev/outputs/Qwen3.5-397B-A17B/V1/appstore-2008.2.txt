The claim is that a closed, curated native app store will succeed despite prior executive skepticism of native apps and existing open rival ecosystems. The steelman: a single controlled store balances developer reach with the device's core promise of stability and polish, turning security from a constraint into a premium feature while capturing revenue via a 30% cut.

Alternatives include maintaining the status quo (web apps only), which risks ceding the emerging mobile software market to rivals; or an open sideloading model, which invites immediate security failures and carrier backlash. Doing nothing is the highest risk: it validates the "web-only" stance while competitors lock in developers with native tools.

Assumptions tested: (1) Developers will accept a 30% fee and review latency for access to a polished user base. (2) Carriers will tolerate bypassed services if the phone drives sufficient subscriber growth. (3) A central team can scale review without becoming a bottleneck. These are plausible given the device's rapid adoption trajectory and the lack of viable native alternatives at launch.

Pre-mortem: If the review process slows to weeks, developers flee to open platforms. If a malicious app slips through, trust evaporates. However, the proposal explicitly includes company review, mitigating the security risk. The "polish" concern is addressed by curation, not rejection. The 30% cut aligns incentives: the platform succeeds only if developers sell.

The strongest attack is that the 30% fee and closed nature stifle innovation compared to open rivals. This fails because the primary value proposition is not "maximum freedom" but "reliable, high-quality user experience." Early adopters and premium developers prioritize reach and trust over total openness. The gatekeeping is the feature, not the bug.

Kill criteria: If developer sign-ups miss targets by 50% in six months, or if a security breach occurs due to review failure, the model must pivot to sideloading or reduced fees. Leading metric: Number of approved apps and attach rate per device.
