**Steelman:** Acquiring the startup secures a free, open mobile OS to commoditize handset software, forcing widespread adoption of mobile web browsing and locking in future search-ad dominance before competitors establish walled gardens.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is ecosystem rejection: handset makers and carriers, currently dominant, have zero incentive to adopt a new, unproven platform that threatens their control. Without pre-committed hardware partners, the OS remains a ghost town. The "open source" lever assumes developers will flock to a platform with no users, while users need apps they don't have yet.

*Do Nothing:* Preserve capital. The mobile web in 2005 is nascent; waiting for the market to mature reduces the risk of backing the wrong horse.
*Alternative:* License existing Java ME or Symbian tools to optimize search on current phones rather than building an OS from scratch. This captures immediate ad revenue without the massive overhead of an OS war.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the north star of "search everywhere," but the path is indirect and fraught. If we assume the role of platform provider, we enter a brutal war of attrition against established players (Nokia, Microsoft, Palm) who view us as a threat, not a partner. In a pre-mortem, the project fails because carriers block pre-loads, handset makers demand licensing fees we refused to pay, and the "free" model fails to overcome switching costs. The $50M price tag is a lottery ticket on a multi-year burn with no guaranteed revenue model.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:**
The proposal admits "no revenue model" and "carrier resistance." These are not risks; they are current facts. The bet relies entirely on the assumption that "free" breaks distribution logics that have held for decades. There is no verified path to installation base growth.

**Verdict:**
The claim that this acquisition directly drives ad revenue is unsupported by the current market structure. The gap between "owning an OS" and "serving ads" is a chasm filled with hostile incumbents. Without a signed commitment from at least one major handset maker or carrier prior to acquisition, the probability of total loss is unacceptably high. The proposal lacks a concrete mechanism to force adoption.

DECISION: NO-GO
