The claim is that replacing a coupon-driven model with everyday low pricing (EDLP) and branded shops will reverse sales declines for a mid-market department store chain. The steelman: eliminating discount complexity reduces operational costs and attracts a broader demographic tired of "fake" sales, while branded shops modernize the experience.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the specific context provided. The customer base is explicitly described as "older" and "coupon-driven," with most historical sales occurring at a discount. This indicates deep behavioral conditioning; these customers do not view the regular price as the "real" price. Abruptly removing coupons without a transition period or education campaign effectively tells the core revenue source that the store no longer wants their business.

Furthermore, the proposal comes from an executive successful in *electronics*, a category with different margin structures and purchase frequencies than general apparel/home goods typical of department stores. What worked for branded electronics may not translate to soft goods where comparison shopping is easier.

The decision method highlights a critical failure in the "do nothing" vs. "go" comparison. The risk of immediate, chain-wide implementation is catastrophic revenue collapse if the hypothesis is wrong. There is no pilot to validate whether the existing base will migrate to full-price buying or simply leave. The cost of delay (waiting for a pilot) is negligible compared to the cost of a failed chain-wide rollout. The pre-mortem is clear: sales plummet because the loyal base abandons ship before new customers arrive, and the brand loses its value proposition entirely.

The lack of a pilot is the strongest attack. A sound strategy would test the new pricing in 5-10 stores to measure elasticity and churn before committing the entire chain. Proceeding without this data ignores the specific constraints of the customer demographic and the unproven transferability of the CEO's previous success. The proposal fails the calibration test: the risk of total alienation of the core base is a concrete failure scenario not mitigated by the plan.

DECISION: NO-GO
