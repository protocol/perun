**Steelman:** Acquiring the agency and platform instantly grants critical scale and technology to challenge the search leader's recent move, preventing permanent market exclusion.

**Attack:** The $6 billion cash price represents a massive premium in a fast-changing market, creating immense downside if display technology shifts again. More critically, buying an agency directly conflicts with the buyer's existing agency customers, who may flee to competitors to avoid funding a rival's media arm. This risks cannibalizing the core business to chase a speculative display future. The "fast-changing" nature of the sector suggests the acquired tech could be obsolete quickly, making the premium unjustifiable.

**Better Way:** Instead of a vertical integration that alienates partners, the company could license the technology or form a non-exclusive partnership. This achieves the tech goal without the $6 billion cash outlay or the customer conflict. Doing nothing preserves cash for organic development or a later, cheaper acquisition once the market stabilizes.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If the market shifts away from current display standards within two years, the company is left with a bloated agency business and angry former clients. The one-way door of spending such liquidity limits future strategic options. The conflict with existing customers is a structural flaw, not a solvable integration issue.

**Kill Criteria:** The deal fails if it triggers a net loss of existing agency clients exceeding 5% within the first year or if the technology requires a rewrite within 18 months. The leading metric must be the retention rate of current agency partners, not just ad revenue growth.

**Verdict:** The structural conflict with existing customers and the excessive premium in a volatile market create a fatal flaw. The risk of destroying the current partner ecosystem outweighs the potential gain in display share. The proposal does not beat "do nothing" or a non-exclusive licensing alternative when customer retention is weighted heavily.

DECISION: NO-GO
