**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio secures the only proven hit-making engine before a rival does, replacing a failing internal unit with immediate market leadership, justifying the $7.4B premium as the cost of survival and future cash flows.

**Skeptic:** The valuation relies entirely on continuing the current hit rate (one film/year) without degradation. If the creative leaders leave or the culture clash stifles output, the asset becomes worthless relative to the price. The "all-stock" deal heavily dilutes existing shareholders if the synergy fails. **Better-Way:** Alternatives include a revenue-sharing partnership without equity, acquiring a smaller emerging studio, or investing heavily in the internal unit with new leadership. However, given the urgency of the expiring contract and the internal unit's track record of underperformance, these alternatives likely result in lost market share. "Do nothing" guarantees the asset goes to a rival.

**Kill-Criteria & Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if: (1) Key creative talent departs within 18 months; (2) No new hits are released within 3 years; (3) Integration costs exceed 20% of projected synergies. The pre-mortem assumes the culture clash causes the creative team to disengage, resulting in delayed releases and box-office bombs, rendering the $7.4B expenditure a massive write-down.

While the price is steep, the strategic necessity of controlling the IP pipeline outweighs the financial risk. The risks (talent retention, culture) are manageable only if the creative leaders are given genuine autonomy, as proposed.
