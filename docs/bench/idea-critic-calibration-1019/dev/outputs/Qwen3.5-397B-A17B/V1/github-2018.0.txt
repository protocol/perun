The claim is that acquiring the dominant code-hosting service for $7.5B will secure the buyer's open-source pivot despite cultural hostility and target instability.

**Steelman:** Only immediate ownership prevents a competitor from locking up the world's largest developer graph, making the premium a strategic necessity rather than a financial investment.

**Skeptic Attack:** The core failure mode is community defection. If developers perceive the acquisition as an existential threat, they will migrate to the free competitor mentioned. Since the target is already leaderless and loss-making, the buyer inherits operational chaos while paying a premium for an asset whose primary value (network effect) evaporates upon the very act of purchase. The "run independently" promise is historically untrustworthy given the buyer's past hostility.

**Better Way:** The alternative is deep partnership or waiting. The target lacks a CEO and is bleeding money; time favors the buyer, not the seller. Waiting 6–12 months could lower the price significantly or allow the free competitor to fracture the market, reducing the need for a risky, high-cost rescue.

**Kill Criteria & Pre-mortem:** Assume the deal closes and developer churn hits 15% within year one due to distrust. The $7.5B price tag implies massive growth; if the community shrinks, the ROI is negative forever. Regulatory scrutiny could also force a breakup, creating maximum distraction for zero gain. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a botched integration that triggers a mass exodus.

**Verdict:** The proposal relies on the fragile assumption that a hostile buyer can instantly reassure a distrustful community while paying a top-tier premium for a leaderless, unprofitable asset. The risk of triggering the very defection the deal aims to prevent is too high. The existence of a viable free alternative gives developers an immediate exit ramp if trust breaks. The financials (loss-making, high multiple) and cultural friction create a fatal flaw that "independent operation" cannot mitigate given the buyer's history.

DECISION: NO-GO
