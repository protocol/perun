**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only viable channel to demonstrate the integrated hardware-software music strategy, as third-party staff actively undermine the premium value proposition.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is fixed-cost collapse: high mall leases combined with low PC margins and a recession could accelerate losses before brand equity builds. Channel conflict may trigger immediate revenue drops if resellers retaliate. "Do nothing" preserves cash but guarantees continued margin erosion and strategic irrelevance as the music initiative fails in impersonal big-box environments. A "shop-in-shop" alternative reduces lease risk but fails to solve the staff-incentive problem.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the north star of an integrated digital lifestyle, not just unit volume. However, assuming a two-way door is fatal; retail leases are long-term liabilities. If the music strategy does not drive hardware attachment within 12 months, the stores become pure cost centers. The industry's poor retail track record suggests operational inexperience is a hidden killer.

**Evidence & Calibration:**
No current data verifies the specific attachment rate needed to break even on prime real estate. The proposal lacks a capped, time-boxed pilot; it jumps straight to a chain. Without a verified metric linking store visits to profitable hardware sales, this is a bet on hope, not math. The "demonstration" need is real, but the "chain of stores" solution is unproven and disproportionately risky given the company's recent losses.

**Kill Criteria:**
A fatal flaw exists: the plan commits to high-fixed costs without a proven unit-economic model for the new retail format. The risk of draining remaining cash during a recession outweighs the potential channel benefits. The proposal must first prove the concept in a single, controlled location with a clear pivot/stop metric before scaling.

**Verdict:**
The strategic intent is sound, but the execution plan is financially reckless given the company's fragile state. Approving a full chain rollout now ignores the necessity of validating the retail model's profitability before incurring massive fixed obligations.

DECISION: NO-GO
