**Steelman:** Betting the company's margin on a future where parallel processing becomes ubiquitous, transforming a niche gaming component into the engine of scientific discovery before competitors realize the shift.

**Attack:** The proposal demands raising unit costs and diverting engineering resources from a fierce, immediate war for gaming market share against a dominant rival. There are zero committed customers, no proven revenue model, and the target market is currently non-existent. If the "general purpose" vision fails to materialize quickly, the company bleeds margin on every unit sold while losing the core gaming battle due to distraction. The risk is total: bankruptcy from high costs without offsetting revenue.

**Better Way:** Wait for the market to prove viability. Let academic hackers continue using existing APIs until a clear commercial demand emerges, then add specific features to high-end cards only. This preserves cash and focus.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** The strategy assumes a massive, latent demand for parallel compute that contradicts current market signals. If the software ecosystem (compilers, tools) fails to gain traction due to complexity, the added silicon area becomes wasted die space, directly lowering profitability in the core business. The "one-way door" is the irreversible allocation of R&D budget away from gaming features during a critical competitive window.

**Decision Method:**
1.  **Evidence:** Current revenue is 100% gaming/workstation. No paying GPGPU customers exist.
2.  **Cost of Delay:** Low; the market isn't ready anyway.
3.  **Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks a pilot, a capped budget, or a specific metric to validate demand before full-scale rollout. It bets the farm on an unproven hypothesis.

**Verdict:** This is a high-stakes, low-reversibility bet with no validated learning phase. It violates the principle of staging investment against evidence. The "fatal flaw" is the lack of a staged approach; it proposes full commitment before proving the "better way" (waiting) is insufficient. The risk of collapsing the core business outweighs the potential of a non-existent market.

DECISION: NO-GO
