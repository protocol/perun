The claim is that acquiring the dominant code-hosting service for $7.5B will secure strategic positioning despite cultural hostility and target instability. The steelman: only immediate ownership prevents a competitor from locking up the world's largest developer network, and the buyer's resources can stabilize the target faster than organic growth.

However, the "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the timing and price. The target is leaderless, loss-making, and actively bleeding trust to a free alternative. Paying a premium ($7.5B) for a distressed asset while the community is in open revolt creates a high probability of mass defection post-announcement. The "do nothing" or "wait six months" alternative is superior: let the current leadership vacuum resolve or let the free competitor struggle with monetization, then acquire a stabilized asset for significantly less.

The pre-mortem is clear: announcement triggers a developer exodus to the free rival, validating the community's distrust. The $7.5B price tag becomes a sunk cost anchor, forcing the buyer to monetize aggressively (raising prices), which accelerates the exodus. The regulatory risk is secondary to the market risk; if the developers leave, the asset is worthless regardless of antitrust approval.

The proposal fails the "fit" test because it ignores the primary value driver: community goodwill, which is currently at an all-time low. Buying now crystallizes the maximum risk at the maximum price. The lack of a permanent CEO at the target suggests internal chaos that the buyer cannot instantly fix without appearing as an occupying force.

The strongest attack survived? None. The argument that "we must buy now or lose them forever" collapses when the target is currently unmanaged and losing users. Waiting is not losing; it is letting the dust settle. The specific failure scenario is a 20% user drop within 90 days of closing, rendering the ROI impossible. There is no kill criterion offered to mitigate this immediate cultural collision.

DECISION: NO-GO
