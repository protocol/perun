The claim assumes a market exists for high-cost, short-form premium video despite abundant free alternatives and rising subscription fatigue. The steelman posits that professional quality and technical convenience (orientation adaptation) justify a premium price where user-generated content fails. However, the "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw: the proposal ignores the dominant economic reality of 2018. Free, ad-supported short video was already saturating the market, and consumers were explicitly expressing fatigue with adding more subscriptions. The cost structure (A-list talent, high production budgets) requires massive scale to break even, yet the value proposition (short episodes, orientation flexibility) is insufficient to displace entrenched free habits or justify another monthly fee.

The pre-mortem is clear: the service launches with high burn rate, fails to convert users away from free platforms due to lack of exclusive "must-watch" long-form libraries, and collapses under content costs before achieving necessary scale. The "orientation" feature is a commodity, not a moat. The proposal attempts to solve a non-existent problem (poor vertical video playback) with an expensive solution (premium production) in a market moving toward aggregation and free access.

There is no evidence provided that users will pay for short-form content when high-quality short clips are freely available on social platforms. The risk of competition for phone attention is understated; the service competes not just with other streamers, but with infinite free scrolling. The "do nothing" or "wait" alternative is superior: let the market validate short-form monetization models with lower capital exposure before committing $1 billion. The kill criterion is the lack of proven demand; proceeding without a pilot or lower-cost validation mechanism violates basic investment hygiene for an unproven format. This is a classic case of over-investing in production before validating distribution and willingness to pay.

DECISION: NO-GO
