**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio secures a proven hit-making engine, prevents a rival from gaining it, and revitalizes the conglomerate's struggling animation division by installing superior creative leadership.

**Attack:** The $7.4 billion valuation relies entirely on the continued output of a small group of creatives producing only one film annually. If these leaders depart or their streak ends, the acquisition becomes a catastrophic overpayment with no tangible asset base to justify the cost. The "all-stock" structure dilutes existing shareholders significantly, and handing control to the target's leaders risks alienating the conglomerate's legacy workforce without guaranteeing cultural integration.

**Better Way:** Instead of a full buyout, negotiate a profit-sharing distribution deal or a minority stake with first-look rights. This captures upside while capping downside and avoiding the integration risk of a total culture clash.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the deal fails in three years. The cause: the creative leaders chafe under public market scrutiny, leave to start a new independent venture, and the stock price collapses as the market realizes the "asset" walked out the door. The one-way door nature of this spend means recovery is impossible.

**Fit:** While this moves the north star of "dominance in animation," it does so by betting the company's liquidity on a single, non-scalable production pipeline. The reliance on a specific founder becoming a major shareholder creates a governance conflict where one individual holds disproportionate sway over the conglomerate's strategic direction.

**Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks a defined exit strategy if the creative team underperforms. There is no mechanism to protect against the specific risk of talent flight, which is the single point of failure. Without a contractual lock-in that survives the acquisition or a lower entry price to buffer against talent loss, the risk profile is unacceptable.

**Verdict:** The price is too high for a talent-dependent service business masquerading as an IP factory. The risk of the key assets (the people) leaving post-acquisition renders the $7.4 billion valuation speculative and dangerous.

DECISION: NO-GO
