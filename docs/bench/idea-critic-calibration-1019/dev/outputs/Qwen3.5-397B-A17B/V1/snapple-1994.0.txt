**Steelman:** Acquiring the tea brand instantly captures premium growth and loyal customers, leveraging the buyer's scale to accelerate distribution and marketing reach beyond what independents can achieve.

**Attack:** The core value lies in the brand's "cult image" and quirky independent distributor loyalty. The proposal explicitly plans to replace these with mass-market advertising and supermarket-focused distribution—the exact playbook used for the sports drink. This is a category error. Sports drinks compete on utility and availability; premium tea competes on exclusivity and narrative.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" alternative (organic growth) preserves brand equity. A superior alternative is a "hands-off" acquisition: maintain independent distributors and niche marketing to protect the premium pricing power that justifies the $1.7 billion price tag.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 24 months. Cause: The brand is perceived as "sold out" and ubiquitous. Supermarket saturation dilutes exclusivity; mass advertising alienates the core demographic; independent distributors defect to competitors due to policy changes. Revenue collapses as the brand becomes just another SKU in a crowded shelf, unable to command premium pricing against soft-drink giants who now have their own tea lines.

**Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits risks to "cult image" and "distributor loyalty" yet prescribes the exact actions that trigger those risks. There is no evidence provided that this specific consumer segment responds positively to mass-market treatment; historical data in premium beverages suggests the opposite (commoditization kills margins). The plan treats the brand as a logistics problem rather than a cultural asset.

**Kill Criteria:** The plan lacks a "stop" condition if brand sentiment drops post-integration. It assumes the buyer's operational model is universally applicable, ignoring the unique mechanism (exclusivity) driving the target's value.

**Decision:** The proposal identifies the fatal flaws but proceeds with the strategy that guarantees them. Integrating a cult brand into a mass-market machine without a pilot or protected lane is a one-way door to value destruction. The $1.7 billion investment is at risk of immediate erosion upon implementation of the proposed distribution and marketing shifts.

DECISION: NO-GO
