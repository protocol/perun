The claim is that replacing the flagship formula with a sweeter alternative, validated by blind taste tests, will reverse market share loss. The steelman: sensory superiority drives purchase frequency, and removing the old formula forces migration to the superior product, maximizing efficiency.

However, the attack reveals a fatal flaw in the evidence methodology. The blind taste tests isolate flavor from brand identity, measuring only immediate sensory gratification (sips) rather than long-term satisfaction (cans) or emotional equity. The proposal explicitly notes that testing did not inform participants of the original's removal. This creates a false positive: consumers chose "a better tasting drink" assuming the original remained an option, not "the only drink available."

The "better way" analysis fails because the proposal rejects a staged approach. A superior strategy would introduce the new formula as a line extension while retaining the original, allowing market data to validate migration without triggering loss aversion. The current plan ignores the "Chesterton's Fence" of brand heritage; the original formula is not just a recipe but a cultural artifact. Removing it entirely converts a product decision into an identity crisis.

The pre-mortem is clear: if launched as proposed, loyalists will perceive the removal as a corporate betrayal rather than an upgrade. The emotional backlash will outweigh the sensory gains, causing a boycott that blind tests cannot predict. The lack of a fallback product eliminates reversibility, turning a two-way door into a one-way cliff. The risk of alienating bottlers and core consumers is not merely "emotional attachment" but a fundamental misunderstanding of the value proposition. The proposal treats the brand as a commodity defined solely by sweetness, ignoring the non-fungible value of tradition.

Because the evidence ignores the variable of permanent removal and the plan lacks a safety net or phased rollout, the risk of catastrophic brand damage is unmitigated. The flaw is fatal: the test conditions do not match the deployment reality.

DECISION: NO-GO
