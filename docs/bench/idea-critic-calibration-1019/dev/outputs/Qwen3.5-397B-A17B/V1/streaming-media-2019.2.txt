**Steelman:** Sacrificing immediate, guaranteed licensing revenue to build a owned distribution channel is necessary to survive a market shifting decisively toward direct-to-consumer models, where controlling the customer relationship outweighs short-term profitability.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on three unverified assumptions that create a fatal flaw. First, it assumes the company can successfully execute a global technology platform despite having zero prior experience running one; history shows media companies often fail at tech infrastructure, leading to poor user experience and churn. Second, it treats licensing revenue as replaceable, ignoring that removing content from rivals strengthens competitors' own libraries while the new service struggles to gain traction. Third, the "multi-year operating loss" funded by existing cash flows risks starving the core theatrical and cable divisions, which currently generate the billions required to fund this burn.

**Better Way:** A hybrid approach retains selective licensing to non-competing regions or windows while launching a smaller, premium-tier service first. This validates the technology stack and content appeal without betting the entire library or accepting unlimited losses immediately.

**Pre-mortem:** In five years, the service has 15 million subscribers (missing the target), the tech platform is unstable causing high churn, and the core business has shrunk due to lost licensing deals and internal cannibalization. The company is forced to sell assets to cover debt incurred during the burn.

**Fit & Evidence:** The proposal targets "tens of millions" against a leader with "over a hundred million." Without a clear path to differentiate beyond price (which is already set below market), the customer acquisition cost will likely exceed the lifetime value, making the "multi-year loss" a permanent state rather than an investment phase. The risk of execution failure in an unproven domain (tech platform) combined with the certainty of lost licensing revenue creates a negative expected value.

**Kill Criteria:** The plan lacks a specific stop condition for the cash burn or a metric to validate the tech platform's viability before full library withdrawal. Proceeding without a pilot or phased rollout violates basic capital allocation discipline for a company entering an unfamiliar operational model.

DECISION: NO-GO
