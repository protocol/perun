The claim is that a dominant DVD rental leader should immediately launch a free, limited streaming tier in 2007 despite high licensing costs, poor infrastructure, and imminent competition from tech giants.

Steelman: Offering free streaming now acts as a defensive moat, retaining subscribers who might defect to future competitors by locking them into the existing ecosystem before alternatives mature, even if the immediate unit economics are negative.

However, the proposal fails the "do nothing" and "cheaper alternative" tests. The streaming catalog is small and old, quality is poor, and broadband penetration is low; this creates a high risk of brand dilution without delivering genuine user value. Launching a subpar product signals weakness rather than innovation. Furthermore, paying high licensing fees for a feature that cannibalizes the highly profitable DVD core, without a clear path to monetization or scale, destroys shareholder value prematurely. The strategic risk is not just competing with larger entrants, but exhausting capital reserves before the market is ready.

A better way is to wait 12–18 months for broadband penetration to increase and licensing costs to normalize, or to launch a separate, priced experimental tier to validate demand without endangering the core revenue stream. The current plan lacks a kill criterion for licensing spend or a metric to measure cannibalization impact against retention gains.

The strongest attack is that this move accelerates the decline of the cash cow (DVDs) to fund a service that cannot yet compete on quality or selection, effectively subsidizing the transition for future competitors who will enter with deeper pockets and better technology. The proposal assumes early adoption will drive loyalty, but evidence suggests early adopters in 2007 will be disappointed by the quality, potentially accelerating churn rather than preventing it. Without a capped budget or a specific pivot trigger, this is an open-ended burn of profitable capital.

DECISION: NO-GO
