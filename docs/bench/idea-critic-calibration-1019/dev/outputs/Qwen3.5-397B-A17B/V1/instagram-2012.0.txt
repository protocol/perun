**Steelman:** Acquire the dominant mobile-native photo network immediately to neutralize the primary threat to our core business and secure the mobile future, accepting that cultural integration and regulatory hurdles are manageable costs of survival.

**Attack & Options:**
The valuation ($1B for ~12 staff, zero revenue) is mathematically indefensible by traditional metrics. However, the strategic cost of *inaction* exceeds the financial premium. If this app solidifies its lead on the newly launched second platform, it becomes the default mobile layer, rendering our existing photo product obsolete. "Do nothing" risks existential displacement; "Build our own" is too slow given their velocity and network effects. "Buy a cheaper competitor" fails because no other player has comparable traction or cultural mindshare.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the north star of dominating mobile engagement. The pre-mortem reveals failure modes: regulatory blocks delaying closure, or heavy-handed integration destroying the very culture that drives growth. Yet, the "one-way door" analysis favors action: waiting even a quarter allows the competitor to deepen moats, making acquisition impossible or prohibitively expensive later. The risk of overpaying is finite ($1B); the risk of losing the mobile transition is infinite.

**Evidence & Calibration:**
The facts show tens of millions of users, rapid cross-platform expansion, and status as the "leading mobile threat." No revenue is irrelevant when the asset is a network effect monopoly in formation. The "fatal flaw" test requires proving the app will fail organically or that regulation will definitely block the deal. Neither is supported by the provided facts; both are speculative risks compared to the verified reality of their market dominance.

**Decision:**
The proposal passes the survival test. The premium is an insurance policy against obsolescence. Kill criteria should be set post-acquisition (e.g., user retention drops >20% within 6 months due to integration), but the acquisition itself must proceed now to prevent the competitor from becoming untouchable.

DECISION: GO
