The claim is that replacing the flagship formula with a sweeter variant, validated by blind taste tests, will reverse market share losses. The steelman: sensory superiority drives purchase, and removing the old formula forces migration to the superior product, maximizing efficiency.

However, the attack reveals a fatal flaw in the evidence. The taste tests isolated flavor from brand identity, creating a false positive. Consumers prefer the rival's taste in a vacuum but reject it when labeled as a replacement for their heritage brand. The proposal ignores that the product is not just a liquid but a symbol; removing the original entirely severs an emotional contract. The testing methodology failed to simulate the actual market condition: the *loss* of the original. Participants chose the sweeter drink assuming the original remained an option; they were never asked to choose between the new drink and *nothing* of the old type.

The "do nothing" alternative (keeping the original while perhaps introducing the sweetener as a line extension) carries less risk. The proposed path is a one-way door with no fallback; once the old formula is withdrawn, rebuilding trust is exponentially harder than launching a new SKU. The risk of alienating the core loyalists—who drive volume and bottler stability—is not mitigated by taste scores. The pre-mortem is clear: launch day triggers a backlash framed as corporate arrogance erasing history, not improving taste. The "sweeter is better" metric is a leading indicator of sip preference, not brand loyalty or long-term sales volume.

Because the testing explicitly omitted the consequence of removal, the data supporting the total replacement is invalid. The emotional attachment and heritage risks are not merely "considerations"; they are the primary drivers of cola sales, which the blind tests intentionally stripped away. A strategy that bets the entire franchise on a metric known to be incomplete (taste vs. brand) fails the calibration test. The lack of a fallback product makes this a catastrophic single-point failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
