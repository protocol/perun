The claim is that acquiring the dominant but unprofitable video platform for $1.65 billion creates sufficient strategic value to justify extreme financial and legal risk. The steelman posits that video is the inevitable future of search, and owning the de facto standard now prevents a competitor from locking up the market, while the buyer's scale can solve the target's bandwidth and monetization inefficiencies.

However, the skepticism is severe. The target has no meaningful revenue, massive operating costs, and an existential threat from media lawsuits that could result in statutory damages exceeding the purchase price. The "unproven ad model" is not a minor gap; it is the entire basis of valuation. If ads cannot support high-bandwidth video, the asset is a money pit. Furthermore, buying a lawsuit magnet when the buyer already has a smaller, safer video service introduces massive liability without immediate cash flow to offset it. The "do nothing" alternative allows the market to mature and legal precedents to settle before committing capital.

The better way is to license technology or wait for the valuation to correct post-litigation clarity. Paying a premium for a company whose core content library may be illegal constitutes a failure of due diligence. The pre-mortem is clear: six months post-acquisition, major studios win injunctions, ad rates fail to cover bandwidth, and the stock price collapses under the weight of integration costs and legal fees.

There are no kill criteria or tripwires in the proposal; it is an unconditional bet on a hypothesis that video ads will work and courts will favor fair use for commercial platforms. Given the lack of revenue, the certainty of expensive litigation, and the availability of a smaller internal alternative, the risk profile is unacceptable. The price assumes success in areas where the target has currently failed.

DECISION: NO-GO
