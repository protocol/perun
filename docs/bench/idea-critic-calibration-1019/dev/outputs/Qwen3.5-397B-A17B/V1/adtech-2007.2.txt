**Steelman:** Acquiring the agency and platform instantly grants critical scale and technology to challenge the search leader's recent move, preventing permanent market exclusion.

**Skeptic:** The $6 billion cash price is enormous for 2007, representing a massive premium with unproven ROI in a volatile display market. Paying entirely in cash depletes reserves needed for organic R&D or other opportunities. The "fast-changing technology" risk suggests the acquired assets could become obsolete quickly, turning a premium purchase into a stranded investment. Furthermore, owning an agency creates direct conflicts with the buyer's existing agency customers, potentially driving them to competitors and eroding the core business base.

**Better-Way:** Instead of a $6 billion cash outlay, the company could license ad-serving technology, form strategic partnerships, or build a smaller, focused solution internally. A "do nothing" stance preserves capital while monitoring the competitor's integration risks. A staged acquisition or earn-out structure would mitigate the risk of overpaying for rapidly depreciating technology.

**Kill-Criteria:** This is a one-way door with high irreversibility due to the cash expenditure and integration complexity. The cost of delay is losing market share, but the cost of error is catastrophic capital misallocation. The proposal lacks specific kill criteria for customer churn rates or technology obsolescence timelines.

**Decision:** The proposal fails the calibration test. The conflict with existing customers threatens the core revenue stream, and the all-cash premium in a fast-moving sector lacks sufficient safeguards. The risks of customer attrition and technological obsolescence are not adequately addressed by the current plan. The sheer size of the bet relative to the uncertainty demands a structured, lower-risk approach or clear tripwires that are absent here.

DECISION: NO-GO
