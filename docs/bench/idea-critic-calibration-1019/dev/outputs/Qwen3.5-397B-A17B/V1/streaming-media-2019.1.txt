**Steelman:** Withdrawing content to launch a discounted proprietary streaming service will secure long-term direct-to-consumer dominance, outweighing short-term licensing losses and cash burn.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on two unverified assumptions: that price alone drives migration from an entrenched competitor with 100M+ subscribers, and that the conglomerate can execute complex streaming technology without prior experience. The "multi-year operating loss" creates a fixed cost burden while simultaneously eliminating the high-margin licensing revenue currently funding operations. If subscriber acquisition lags due to content fragmentation (users unwilling to subscribe to yet another service) or technical failures, the company faces a liquidity crisis with no immediate revenue bridge. The "low price" strategy devalues the library permanently, making future price hikes difficult without triggering churn.

**Better Way:** A hybrid approach: license content selectively to maintain cash flow while launching a higher-priced, premium-tier service bundled with existing cable assets to reduce churn risk. This avoids the "binary" bet of pulling all content immediately.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Assuming the goal is market share, this strategy ignores the "do nothing" option's value: guaranteed billions in licensing fees. If the tech platform fails at launch (high probability for a first-time operator), the brand suffers irreparable damage. The one-way door is pulling content from rivals; once lost, those distribution channels may not reopen on favorable terms.

**Evidence & Calibration:** No data is provided on customer willingness to switch services solely for price, nor on the specific technical gap. The projection of "tens of millions" in five years is an assumption, not a verified forecast. Without a capped, time-boxed pilot or a verified technical readiness assessment, the risk of catastrophic cash burn is unmitigated. The proposal lacks a kill criterion for the operating loss or a fallback if subscriber targets are missed by 50%.

**Verdict:** The plan commits to irreversible revenue loss and massive capital expenditure based on unproven execution capabilities and optimistic adoption curves. It fails to beat "do nothing" on risk-adjusted returns without a staged rollout or technical validation.

DECISION: NO-GO
