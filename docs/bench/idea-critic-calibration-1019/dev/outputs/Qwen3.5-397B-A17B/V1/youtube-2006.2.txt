**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant video platform eliminates the buyer's largest competitive threat, secures the primary destination for user-generated video traffic, and converts a litigation target into a strategic asset that can be defended with superior legal and financial resources.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is existential: copyright lawsuits could force the platform offline or incur damages exceeding the purchase price. The "do nothing" alternative allows the competitor to potentially monetize first or be bought by a rival (e.g., Yahoo, Microsoft), cementing the buyer's second-place status. Building a rival internally has already failed to gain traction. The "cheaper" alternative of waiting for litigation to crush the target is a gamble; if the target survives or settles cheaply, its valuation will skyrocket, making future acquisition impossible.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the north star of organizing the world's information, shifting from text to video. If we assume the lawsuits succeed, the $1.65B is lost. However, the "one-way door" risk is mitigated by the buyer's deep pockets to fight lengthy legal battles and the ability to implement content-filtering technology (which the target lacks). The cost of delay is ceding the video medium entirely to a competitor during a pivotal internet shift.

**Evidence & Calibration:**
The target's dominance in traffic is the only verified metric that matters; revenue is secondary at this inflection point. History shows platform winners monetize later. The copyright risk is real but manageable for a giant compared to a startup. The price is high, but the strategic value of owning the category leader outweighs the cost of building a failed competitor or losing the market.

**Kill Criteria:**
Proceed only if legal counsel confirms no immediate injunction is likely pre-closing. The leading metric is user growth retention post-acquisition; if traffic drops >20% due to content removal, the thesis fails.

**Verdict:**
The risk of inaction (losing the video war) exceeds the financial risk of acquisition. The buyer is the only entity capable of solving the legal and infrastructure bottlenecks.

DECISION: GO
