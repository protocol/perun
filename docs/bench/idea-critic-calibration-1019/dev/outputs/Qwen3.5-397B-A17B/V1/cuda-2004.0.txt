**Steelman:** Embedding general-purpose compute into mass-market GPUs creates a ubiquitous, low-cost supercomputer network, unlocking scientific markets that CPUs cannot serve efficiently, securing a decade-long moat before rivals react.

**Attack:** The proposal violates basic unit economics: it raises costs on 100% of units to serve a 0% revenue market. In 2004, no software ecosystem exists to utilize these features; academic hacking proves interest, not willingness to pay. Diverting engineering from the fierce gaming rivalry risks losing the core cash cow that funds this experiment. The "market" is hypothetical; the cost is immediate and universal.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Maintain gaming dominance; let academics continue hacking APIs until a real market emerges.
2. *Targeted High-End:* Add programmability only to expensive workstation cards where scientific users already buy, avoiding mass-market cost penalties.
3. *Wait:* Let CPU makers or rivals attempt this first; copy only if adoption hits 5%.

**Pre-mortem:** By 2006, the chip ships with unused transistors, increasing die size and heat. Gaming performance lags the rival due to divided engineering focus. Market share drops in the core segment. The scientific toolchain is unfinished and incompatible with academic hacks. The company misses a quarterly earnings target, stock tanks, and the program is canceled as a costly distraction.

**Fit:** This contradicts the current North Star: winning the gaming performance war. There is no verified customer demand, only speculative research. The cost of delay is zero because the market does not yet exist. The cost of action is immediate margin compression.

**Kill Criteria:** Without a committed enterprise customer pre-paying for the toolchain or a verified projection showing >10% of revenue from non-gaming within 3 years, this is a gamble with the company's primary engine. The proposal offers no pilot, no phased rollout, and no protection for the core gaming business.

**Verdict:** The risk of destabilizing the core business for a non-existent market is fatal. The unit economics are inverted.

DECISION: NO-GO
