**Steelman:** A premium, integrated device leverages a loyal niche to establish a beachhead in a stagnant market, betting that superior user experience and future ecosystem expansion will outweigh initial hardware limitations and high cost.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The claim fails the "do nothing" test. The addressable market is mathematically insufficient: a small-share computer maker targeting only its own users creates a ceiling too low to recoup R&D for custom hardware and a 5GB drive. Cheaper competitors exist; while they sell poorly, their failure signals weak consumer demand for the category itself, not an opening for a premium entrant. The "walled garden" restriction (compatible only with its own software) is a fatal friction point when the value proposition is portability.

**Pre-mortem:**
Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: The device remains a niche peripheral for existing computer owners rather than a standalone cultural phenomenon. The music industry refuses to license content without a larger installed base, creating a chicken-and-egg deadlock. Component costs for miniaturized drives remain high, preventing price reduction. The brand becomes associated with an overpriced toy rather than innovation.

**Evidence & Calibration:**
No evidence supports the assumption that computer buyers will pay a 2x premium for a music player restricted to their current ecosystem. The "tiny addressable base" is a verified constraint, not a risk. There is no verified path to cross-platform compatibility or music licensing within the proposed timeline. The proposal relies on the unproven hypothesis that hardware alone can create a market where software and content are absent.

**Kill Criteria:**
The proposal lacks a mechanism to expand the addressable market beyond current computer owners immediately. Without a confirmed path to Windows compatibility or pre-negotiated music licensing, the project cannot achieve the volume necessary for sustainability. The high price point with no content ecosystem is a fatal flaw.

**Verdict:**
The plan ignores the primary barrier to adoption (content availability and cross-platform utility) and doubles down on a shrinking hardware niche. It bets on a future ecosystem that does not exist yet, with no bridge to get there.

DECISION: NO-GO
