**Steelman** – Introduce a console‑wide, paid online platform (annual fee, broadband required) that gives every game a single player identity, friends list, voice chat and matchmaking, launching in 2002 for the 2001 console.

**Attack** –  
1. **Broadband availability**: In 2002 only a small minority of homes have broadband; most users rely on dial‑up. Requiring broadband excludes the majority of the installed base.  
2. **Price sensitivity**: Competing consoles let publishers decide whether to charge, and many offer free online play. A mandatory subscription will push price‑conscious gamers toward those rivals, shrinking the console’s player pool.  
3. **Operational unknowns**: The company has never run large‑scale hosting or support. Building, staffing and maintaining a reliable service adds a high, unquantified cost and risk of service outages that could damage the brand.

**Alternatives** –  
- **Do nothing** – keep the status quo, avoid new costs and preserve current user experience.  
- **Partner with an existing ISP/online service** – share infrastructure and risk, possibly subsidise access.  
- **Freemium model** – provide basic matchmaking free, charge only for premium features (voice chat, enhanced friends list). Each alternative reduces the barrier to entry and leverages existing expertise.

**Fit** – The proposal seeks immediate revenue but conflicts with market expectations (free online play) and the company’s current operational capabilities. It does not advance the core goal of growing the console’s user base.

**Pre‑mortem / Door** – If adoption falls below 5 % of console owners in the first year, the service becomes a sunk‑cost liability (two‑way door). The cost of building the platform could exceed projected subscription revenue, leading to a financial loss and brand damage.

**Kill‑criteria** – Adopt a hard stop: abort if <5 % adoption or if operating costs exceed subscription revenue by >20 % after six months.

Given the low broadband penetration, price barrier, and untested operational demands, the proposal is too risky to launch as‑is.

DECISION: NO-GO
