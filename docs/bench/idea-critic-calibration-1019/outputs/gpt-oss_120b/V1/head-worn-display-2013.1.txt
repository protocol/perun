**Steelman claim** – Launch an invitation‑only early‑adopter program for a $1,500 head‑worn display with camera, voice control and screen, then expand to the mass market as an everyday consumer product.

**Skeptic checks**  
- **Price**: $1,500 is well above comparable devices (e.g., early smartphones, tablets) in 2013; price‑elastic demand will be low.  
- **Use case**: No compelling daily utility is demonstrated; most users lack a need for a wearable camera.  
- **Privacy**: A constantly‑on camera invites legal and social backlash; regulators may impose restrictions.  
- **Social acceptance**: Visible wearables were still novel in 2013; early adopters risk stigma, limiting word‑of‑mouth growth.  
- **Battery**: Short runtime (<2 h) restricts practical use, further eroding value.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
1. **Enterprise pilot** – Target field‑service or media professionals who value hands‑free video; price can be justified by productivity gains.  
2. **Developer kit** – Sell at a reduced price to developers to build an ecosystem before consumer launch.  
3. **Delay launch** – Wait for battery and sensor improvements, then price closer to $800, reducing barrier to entry.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**  
- **Privacy incident** → >50 % of pilot participants withdraw, <20 units sold.  
- **Battery <2 h** → >70 % of users report unusable daily.  
- **Revenue < $30k in first 6 months** → abort consumer rollout.  

**Evidence** – No market data or pilot metrics are provided; all risk factors are assumed.

**Conclusion** – The proposal’s high price, unclear everyday utility, privacy exposure, and limited battery life constitute fatal flaws under the outlined kill‑criteria. A safer path is to re‑target the product to enterprise use or postpone until technology improves.

DECISION: NO-GO
