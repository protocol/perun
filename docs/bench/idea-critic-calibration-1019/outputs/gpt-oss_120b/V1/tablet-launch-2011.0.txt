**Steelman claim** – Launch a tablet built on the newly‑acquired mobile OS, priced competitively with the market‑leading tablet, and compensate for a thin app catalog by investing heavily in developer outreach and advertising.

### Assumptions challenged
1. **Price parity drives sales** despite a sparse ecosystem. History shows customers trade off price for app variety; the iPad’s premium price was justified by its catalog.  
2. **Developer outreach quickly yields a robust catalog**. Building a meaningful app base typically takes years; a short‑term “heavy” push rarely reaches critical mass.  
3. **Advertising can overcome “unproven” perception**. Brand trust in tablets was nascent in 2011; ads alone did not shift early adopters toward unknown platforms.  
4. **Leadership transition will not impair execution**. New executives often reprioritise, risking delay or budget cuts.  
5. **Market still open to new entrants**. By 2011 the iPad held >70 % share; Android tablets were emerging but required an existing OS ecosystem.

### Better‑way alternatives
- **Delay launch** until the app ecosystem reaches ≥30 % of the leader’s catalog, using the interim to ship a developer‑focused device.  
- **Partner with an established OS** (e.g., Android) to leverage its app store while differentiating on hardware.  
- **Target a niche segment** (education, enterprise) with a lower‑priced, purpose‑built tablet, avoiding direct price competition with the iPad.

### Kill‑criteria (one‑way door)
- If after 6 months the app count is <30 % of the market leader’s, halt further investment and discontinue the product line.  
- Review sales versus advertising spend at the 6‑month mark; a <5 % conversion rate triggers shutdown.

### Pre‑mortem
Failure likely stems from an insufficient app ecosystem, high unit cost, and leadership instability, leading to poor sales and sunk marketing spend.

**Decision** – The risks outweigh the mitigations; the proposal should not proceed as written.  

DECISION: NO-GO
