**Steelman** – Launch a clear‑cola brand nationwide as a major brand extension, backed by a large, high‑visibility marketing campaign, to capture consumers seeking a “purer” cola experience and to revive growth for the second‑largest cola maker.

**Skeptical attack**  
1. **Flavor‑color association** – Most consumers link the classic dark hue with cola taste; a clear product may be perceived as “not a real cola,” reducing trial.  
2. **Novelty decay** – Test‑market enthusiasm often reflects curiosity; repeat‑purchase data are absent, so demand could collapse after the initial buzz.  
3. **Cannibalisation** – A clear version may simply shift sales from the flagship, eroding margin without net growth.  
4. **Test‑market bias** – The markets were likely more receptive than the national average (e.g., urban, younger demographics), inflating demand signals.  
5. **Competitive response** – Rivals can replicate the concept quickly; a clear cola is a low‑tech differentiation that does not create a sustainable moat.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Phased rollout**: Begin with a limited‑region launch (e.g., 5‑10 % of stores) and modest spend, gathering repeat‑purchase metrics before scaling.  
- **Co‑branding**: Position the clear product as a sub‑brand (“Clear Cola by X”) to protect the flagship’s identity and limit cannibalisation.  
- **Leverage existing low‑calorie line**: Introduce a clear, low‑calorie variant within the current diet portfolio, using existing distribution and marketing assets.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**  
- First‑quarter sales < 60 % of forecast.  
- Repeat‑purchase rate < 30 % after 3 months.  
- Cannibalisation > 15 % of flagship volume.  
- A major competitor launches a clear cola and captures > 5 % market share within 6 months.

**Fit with strategy** – The proposal seeks growth but does not align with the core brand promise of “classic cola.” Without clear evidence of sustained demand, it jeopardises the flagship’s equity.

**Conclusion** – The risks outweigh the unverified upside of a full‑scale launch. A controlled, data‑driven rollout is required to validate repeat demand and protect the flagship.

DECISION: NO-GO
