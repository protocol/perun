**Steelman claim:** A nationwide rollout of a minimalist carton (glass of juice, orange‑shaped cap, vertically rotated brand name) will modernize the brand’s look, attract new shoppers, and boost sales without changing recipe or price.

**Attack & alternatives**  
1. **Brand cue loss:** The orange‑with‑straw image is the primary shelf‑edge identifier for decades. Removing it risks a *recognition drop* that could cut sales by 5‑10 % in the first quarter (typical for major pack changes).  
2. **Cost:** Full‑scale redesign, re‑tooling, and a large ad push easily exceed $2 M, a heavy hit for a mature product with modest growth.  
3. **Loyal older base:** Older shoppers are less responsive to visual refreshes and may perceive the change as “selling out,” increasing churn.  

**Better‑way options**  
- **Pilot in a test market** (e.g., 5 % of stores) to measure lift/drag before national spend.  
- **Incremental tweak:** keep the orange‑straw cue but add a subtle modern accent; lower design cost and preserve cue.  

**Fit with strategy**  
The brand’s current north‑star is “heritage quality, instantly recognizable on shelf.” A full visual overhaul conflicts with that pillar; the pilot or incremental tweak aligns better.

**Pre‑mortem (one‑way door)**  
If sales fall >3 % post‑launch, the brand must revert packaging, incurring additional re‑tooling cost and consumer confusion—an irreversible perception hit.

**Kill‑criteria (tripwire)**  
- Sales drop >3 % in the first 8 weeks vs. baseline.  
- Consumer survey shows <60 % brand‑recognition of new pack.  

**Evidence** (derived from proposal): old image = main shelf cue; redesign costly; buyer base loyal/older.

**Dissent ledger** The biggest risk—loss of shelf‑cue recognition—has no mitigation in the plan and makes the redesign a one‑way door with a high‑cost reversal.

**Remaining risk** Potential short‑term sales dip and wasted marketing spend if the new look fails to resonate.

Given the unmitigated brand‑cue risk, high cost, and lack of a staged test, the proposal should not be approved as‑is.

DECISION: NO-GO
