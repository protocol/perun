**Steelman:** Acquire the social‑network for $580 M, keep it independent, and monetize it by selling ads across the conglomerate’s media properties.

**Attack & Alternatives**  
1. **Do nothing** – avoid a $580 M outlay and the integration risk; instead grow a proprietary platform or partner with existing networks.  
2. **Buy a smaller, profitable niche site** – lower price, proven revenue, fewer brand‑safety concerns.  
3. **Joint venture/strategic partnership** – share risk while testing audience fit.

Both alternatives cost less and allow the conglomerate to learn the young‑audience market without a massive sunk cost.

**Fit with Strategy** – The goal is a social‑media foothold, but the target demographic (young users) is hard to monetize and the rival college‑focused network threatens rapid market share loss. The proposal does not advance the core media business and adds a unit with uncertain ROI.

**Pre‑mortem / One‑Way Door** –  
*Scenario:* Brand‑safety scandals arise from user‑generated pages, forcing ad pull‑backs and damaging the conglomerate’s reputation. Integration costs balloon because the hastily built technology requires a $200 M rewrite. Revenue never scales, leaving the $580 M purchase a net loss. This is a **two‑way door**: the loss can be partially mitigated by divesting, but the reputational hit is irreversible.

**Evidence & Assumptions** – The only data given are “little revenue” and “fast‑growing” status. Assuming “little revenue” means < $50 M annually, a $580 M price implies > 10× revenue, far above typical social‑media acquisition multiples.

**Kill‑Criteria** –  
* ROI < 5 % annualized over 5 years, **or** any brand‑safety incident that forces a > 20 % ad‑revenue drop in the conglomerate’s core channels, **or** integration cost > $200 M. None of these thresholds are likely to be met.

**Conclusion** – The financial multiple is excessive, brand‑safety risk is high, and a cheaper, lower‑risk path exists. Approving the deal now would expose the conglomerate to significant financial and reputational loss.

DECISION: NO-GO
