**Steelman claim:** Launch a paid, console‑wide online service in 2002 with a single player identity, friends list, voice chat and matchmaking for all games, requiring broadband and an annual subscription.

**Assumptions challenged**  
1. **Broadband availability** – In 2002 only ~10 % of U.S. households had broadband; many markets were still dial‑up only.  
2. **Player willingness to pay** – Early online gaming (e.g., Dreamcast, PC) was largely free; a mandatory fee could deter adoption.  
3. **Operational capacity** – The company has never run large‑scale hosting or support; building that from scratch adds huge fixed cost and risk.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Free tier** with optional premium add‑ons (voice chat, matchmaking) to lower entry friction.  
- **Revenue‑share model** where each publisher pays per‑user or per‑session, reducing the need for a blanket subscription.  
- **Pilot in regions with higher broadband penetration** (e.g., Japan, South Korea) before global rollout.

**Kill‑criteria (must be met to proceed)**  
- Broadband penetration ≥ 20 % of the console’s installed base in the launch region.  
- Forecasted subscription uptake ≥ 5 % of console owners (≈ 50 k users) to cover hosting, support and development costs within 12 months.  
- A concrete hosting‑cost model showing ≤ 30 % of projected subscription revenue consumed by infrastructure.

**Premortem** – If broadband remains scarce, the service will see <2 % adoption, leading to large sunk costs, negative brand impact and an operational burden the company cannot sustain.

**Conclusion** – The current proposal fails the broadband‑penetration and adoption kill‑criteria, and a less risky, tiered approach exists. Approving the plan now would likely result in financial loss and reputational damage.

DECISION: NO-GO
