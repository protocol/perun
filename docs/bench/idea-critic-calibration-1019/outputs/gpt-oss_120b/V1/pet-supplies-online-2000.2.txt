**Steelman** – Approve the IPO, raise capital, launch a national TV blitz, acquire the biggest rival, expand warehousing, and deliberately price many items below cost to capture market share quickly.

**Attack (skeptic)**  
1. **Negative gross margins** – Selling below cost creates a cash‑drain that must be funded indefinitely. Even with a large cash infusion, the burn rate will outpace any realistic revenue growth because low‑margin pet goods are heavy and expensive to ship.  
2. **Customer‑acquisition cost (CAC)** – National TV ads in the early‑2000s cost tens of millions. With a CAC far above the lifetime value of a pet‑supply customer (especially when margins are negative), the payback period stretches beyond a year, jeopardizing liquidity.  
3. **Capital‑market risk** – An IPO in early 2000 sits at the peak of the dot‑com bubble; a market correction could freeze equity financing when the company still needs cash to cover operating losses.  
4. **Acquisition integration** – Buying the largest competitor adds debt and integration risk while the combined entity still suffers negative unit economics; any delay magnifies cash burn.  
5. **Warehouse expansion** – Additional fixed costs increase the break‑even volume needed; with low margins, the required sales volume may be unattainable.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Do‑nothing / delay IPO**: Continue operating privately, prove a sustainable gross margin on a core product line, and improve unit economics before seeking public capital.  
- **Targeted marketing**: Use digital channels (email, early search ads) with measurable CAC, avoiding the high upfront cost of national TV.  
- **Selective acquisition**: Acquire only technology or niche inventory assets, not the whole competitor, reducing debt and integration complexity.

**Kill‑criteria / pre‑mortem**  
- If gross margin remains negative after 6 months post‑IPO, trigger a strategic pause and divest non‑core inventory.  
- If CAC exceeds 150 % of projected customer lifetime value, halt further TV spend.  
- If the S&P 500 or Nasdaq falls >15 % within 30 days of filing, postpone the IPO.

**Evidence** – The proposal itself admits negative margins and high CAC; no counter‑data provided. No financial model or runway analysis is attached.

**Remaining risk** – Even with kill‑criteria, the plan relies on an optimistic market rally and rapid margin improvement that are not substantiated.

Given the unchecked cash‑drain, market timing risk, and lack of a realistic path to profitability, the proposal should not be approved in its current form.

DECISION: NO‑GO
