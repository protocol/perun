**Steelman** – A high‑visibility consumer campaign plus a co‑funded advertising program could make the chip’s brand known to end‑users, creating demand‑pull that forces computer makers to highlight the chip, potentially increasing market share and pricing power.

**Attack & Alternatives**  
1. **Do nothing** – stay in the proven B2B channel; low cost, no consumer confusion.  
2. **OEM‑only co‑marketing** – fund computer‑maker ads that already mention the chip, without a consumer‑facing logo; cheaper and respects OEM autonomy.  
3. **Limited pilot** – run the logo program in one region or product line to test consumer response before a global roll‑out.

**Fit** – The goal of brand awareness aligns with a desire for higher market penetration, but it conflicts with the chip maker’s historic B2B focus and the risk‑averse culture of OEM partners.

**Pre‑mortem** – If consumers still cannot identify the chip, the budget is wasted and OEMs may resent being told how to advertise, possibly reducing future cooperation. A failed campaign could also damage the chip’s perceived premium image.

**Evidence & Assumptions** – No market research is cited to show that end‑users care about the internal processor brand, nor any cost‑benefit analysis of the proposed budget share. The claim that “buyers will ask for the chip by name” is unverified.

**Kill‑criteria (one‑way‑door)** – Abort if, after a 6‑month test, unaided brand recall is <5 % or if more than 30 % of OEM partners refuse to display the logo.

**Decision** – The proposal carries a large, untested budget commitment, uncertain consumer demand, and a high risk of OEM push‑back. A controlled pilot with clear metrics is required before a full launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
