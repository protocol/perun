**Steelman claim** – Rewrite the entire site from scratch, redesign the front page to surface stories by favoring publishers, and switch all users to the new version in a single launch.

**Key assumptions**
1. Existing community will accept a fundamentally different surfacing algorithm.  
2. Publisher‑driven ranking will increase traffic enough to offset possible churn.  
3. Engineering can deliver a full rewrite without overruns.  
4. No rollback is needed because the new version will be an improvement.

**Better‑way alternatives**
- **Incremental redesign**: refactor the front‑page UI and ranking logic in the current codebase, preserving the voting mechanic, then roll out gradually.  
- **A/B testing / phased migration**: launch the new ranking to a small user segment (e.g., 5 % of traffic) and monitor churn, engagement, and publisher revenue before scaling.  
- **Dual‑site approach**: keep the legacy site live while offering the new version as an opt‑in, allowing users to choose and providing a safety net.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**
- If net active users drop ≥ 10 % within two weeks of launch, abort and revert.  
- If community sentiment (survey or support tickets) scores < 3/5 on “satisfaction with story discovery”, halt rollout.  
- If critical bugs affect > 1 % of page loads, trigger rollback.  
- If engineering schedule slips > 30 % beyond the committed deadline, pause the project.

**Evidence & risk assessment**
- The community’s core value is the democratic voting system; past changes that reduced voting weight have historically caused spikes in churn on similar platforms.  
- Full rewrites historically have a 30–50 % chance of missing schedule or budget (industry data).  
- A rival site is gaining users; a cautious, data‑driven migration offers a better chance to win back traffic without alienating the base.

**Conclusion**
The proposal’s all‑at‑once launch ignores proven mitigation strategies (phased rollout, A/B testing) and lacks concrete stop‑loss thresholds. The risk of irreversible community loss outweighs the speculative gain from publisher‑favoring ranking.

**DECISION: NO-GO**
