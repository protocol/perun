**Steelman:** Acquire the social‑media platform for $580 M, keep it as an independent unit, and monetize it by selling advertising across the conglomerate’s existing media channels.

**Attack:**  
1. **Price vs. revenue** – Paying $580 M for a “little‑revenue” asset implies a valuation many times current earnings, creating a long pay‑back horizon. Without disclosed revenue numbers, the price appears unjustified.  
2. **Competitive pressure** – A college‑focused rival is gaining traction; its demographic overlap threatens user growth and could siphon advertisers.  
3. **Brand‑safety risk** – User‑generated pages are prone to extremist or harmful content, exposing the conglomerate to reputational damage and costly moderation.  
4. **Monetization of a young audience** – Younger users have lower ad spend and higher ad‑block usage, making revenue per user uncertain.  
5. **Technical debt** – The platform was built “quickly and unevenly,” suggesting hidden engineering costs for scaling, security, and integration with the conglomerate’s ad‑tech stack.

**Better‑ways / Alternatives:**  
- **Do nothing** – Preserve capital and avoid brand‑risk exposure.  
- **Partner or minority stake** – Gain access to the audience without full acquisition cost, limiting exposure while testing integration.  
- **Acquire a more mature platform** – Target a site with proven revenue and robust tech, even at a lower price, to ensure quicker ROI.

**Fit with strategy:** The conglomerate seeks digital expansion, but the proposal offers a high‑cost, high‑risk entry point that does not clearly accelerate the north‑star goal of profitable digital reach.

**Premortem / Kill‑criteria:** Failure would likely stem from (a) inability to reach breakeven within three years, (b) a brand‑safety incident causing a PR crisis, or (c) engineering overruns exceeding $150 M. No concrete thresholds or mitigation plans are presented.

**Evidence:** All concerns derive from the proposal’s own risk statements; no financial forecasts, growth targets, or cost‑control measures are supplied.

**Decision:** The proposal lacks quantifiable kill‑criteria and presents a disproportionate price‑to‑revenue ratio, making the risk unacceptable without revision.

DECISION: NO-GO
