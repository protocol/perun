**Steelman** – The chip maker wants to create a consumer‑facing brand for its CPU, funding computer‑maker ads that display a new logo. By making the logo visible, end users will learn the chip’s name and demand it, driving higher volumes and allowing the chip maker to capture a larger share of the market.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
1. **Do nothing** – Continue selling anonymously; avoid costly brand spend and partner friction.  
2. **Co‑marketing only** – Offer joint promotions without a mandatory logo, preserving maker autonomy while still raising awareness.  

Both cost less and carry lower risk; the “do‑nothing” baseline preserves cash flow.

**Fit with strategy** – The 1991 market prized OEM relationships; shifting to consumer‑direct branding contradicts the established channel‑first strategy and could alienate key partners.

**Pre‑mortem / One‑Way Door** – Failure scenario: computer makers reject the logo, pull advertising, and publicly criticize the chip maker, causing a loss of OEM contracts (a one‑way door). The cost of the program (large share of marketing budget) is unrecoverable if sales do not rise quickly.

**Evidence / Assumptions** – Assumes consumers will care about CPU brand and that awareness will translate to purchase decisions within a short cycle. No data from comparable component branding exists; the claim is unverified.

**Kill‑criteria** –  
- **Partner backlash**: >10 % of OEMs refuse participation.  
- **ROI**: Marketing spend >30 % of quarterly profit without a measurable lift in shipments after two quarters.  

If either threshold is met, the program must be halted.

**Remaining risk** – High: partner resentment, uncertain consumer demand, large budget exposure, and no proven precedent.

Given the lack of evidence, the one‑way‑door risk to OEM relationships, and the availability of lower‑cost, lower‑risk alternatives, the proposal should not be approved in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
