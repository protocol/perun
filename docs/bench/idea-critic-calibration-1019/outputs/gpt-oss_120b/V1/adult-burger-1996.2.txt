**Steelman claim**  
The chain should launch a premium adult‑focused burger, price it above the core menu, and back it with the industry’s largest advertising and promotion budget to capture adult customers and boost revenue.

**Key assumptions**  
1. Adults will overlook the chain’s child‑centric image if presented with a premium product.  
2. A massive ad spend can quickly reshape brand perception.  
3. Price elasticity for the new burger will be low enough to sustain higher margins.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Pilot rollout** in a few markets with modest advertising to validate demand before national launch.  
- **Gradual brand shift** by expanding existing adult‑oriented items (salads, specialty sandwiches) and using them as stepping stones toward a premium offering.  
- **Co‑branding** with a recognized premium meat supplier to lend credibility without huge ad spend.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**  
- If after 12 weeks the premium burger accounts for <5 % of total sales, discontinue the product.  
- If advertising ROI (incremental profit ÷ ad spend) stays below 1:1, halt further spend.  
- If brand‑perception surveys show a ≥10 % decline in “family‑friendly” rating, roll back the campaign.

**Risk assessment**  
The proposal is a two‑way door: the burger can be removed, but the advertising budget is sunk. Launching with the largest ad spend before any demand signal creates a high‑cost, low‑reversibility gamble. Historical cases (e.g., fast‑food “premium” launches in the 1990s) show that brand mis‑alignment often leads to poor sales and lasting brand dilution.

**Conclusion**  
Proceeding without a demand test exposes the chain to significant financial loss and potential brand damage. The prudent path is a limited pilot with measured spend and clear kill‑criteria.

DECISION: NO-GO
