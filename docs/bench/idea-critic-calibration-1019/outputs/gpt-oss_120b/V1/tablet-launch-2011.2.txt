**Steelman claim** – Launch a tablet running the newly‑acquired mobile OS at flagship‑level price, despite a tiny app catalog, by pouring money into developer outreach and advertising.

**Skeptic checks**
- **Market dominance:** The incumbent tablet (iPad) controls >70 % of sales; price parity offers no competitive edge.
- **App ecosystem:** <5 % of the leader’s catalog translates to a weak value proposition for consumers and developers.
- **OS market share:** Near‑zero presence means brand‑recognition and carrier/enterprise support are lacking.
- **Leadership transition:** New executives often delay execution and shift priorities, raising execution risk.
- **Consumer perception:** “Unproven” products historically see <10 % of early‑adopter sales in this segment.

**Better‑way alternatives**
1. **Delay launch** until the app ecosystem reaches a critical mass (e.g., 20 % of competitor’s catalog) or a niche market (education, enterprise) is secured.
2. **Price‑differentiate** the tablet below the market leader to attract cost‑sensitive buyers while the ecosystem matures.
3. **License the OS** to an existing hardware partner rather than building a full‑stack tablet, reducing capital exposure.

**Kill‑criteria (one‑way‑door)**
- If app count < 500 within 12 months, cease further advertising spend.
- If tablet sales < 5 % of forecast after Q2, halt production and re‑allocate budget.

**Dissent ledger** – The proposal’s upside hinges on rapid ecosystem growth that historically takes 2‑3 years; the current plan forces a large, irreversible spend now with minimal differentiation, breaching the kill‑criteria risk ceiling.

Given the high probability of sunk‑cost loss and lack of a clear exit trigger, the plan should not be approved in its present form.

DECISION: NO-GO
