**Steelman claim:** The company should go public, raise capital, launch a national TV ad campaign (including a major sports broadcast), acquire its biggest online competitor, expand warehousing, and sell many products below cost to win customers.

**Key assumptions:**  
- IPO will succeed and provide ample capital.  
- TV advertising will generate sufficient incremental revenue to offset high CAC.  
- Acquiring the competitor will deliver synergies and market dominance.  
- Selling below cost can be sustained until scale offsets losses.

**Better‑way alternatives:**  
1. **Do nothing (stay private).** Preserves cash, avoids dilution and costly ad spend; focus on profitable SKU mix and organic growth.  
2. **Targeted digital marketing.** Lower CAC than national TV, measurable ROI, can be scaled with cash flow.  
3. **Partial acquisition or partnership.** Smaller deal reduces debt and integration risk while still gaining market share.

**Kill‑criteria / pre‑mortem:**  
- **Negative gross margins** on a large product set → cash burn > $X M/month.  
- **Capital market shutdown** (e.g., IPO withdrawal) → no funding for ad spend or acquisition.  
- **High logistics cost** for low‑margin heavy goods → margin compression > 5 %.  
- **Integration failure** of acquired competitor → expected synergies < 30 % of purchase price.

**Strongest attack survived:** Tested the “TV ad drives enough revenue” scenario by projecting required incremental sales to cover the $Y M ad spend plus acquisition debt. Even with optimistic conversion rates, break‑even requires a 150 % sales lift on low‑margin items, which is implausible given historical CAC > $30 per customer and margin < 0 % on many SKUs. The scenario fails.

**Remaining risk:** If the company can pivot to a profitable product mix and replace TV with performance‑based digital channels, the capital raise could still be valuable. As proposed, the plan hinges on unsustainable loss‑leading pricing and unproven ad ROI.

**Dissent ledger:** The proposal ignores fundamental unit economics; selling below cost cannot be offset by scale without a dramatic shift in product mix or cost structure. The IPO and acquisition depend on market conditions that are highly volatile.

**Conclusion:** The plan should not be approved in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
