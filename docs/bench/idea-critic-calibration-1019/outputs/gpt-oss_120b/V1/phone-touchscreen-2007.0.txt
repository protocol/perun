claim: Company X should launch a premium touchscreen smartphone with an integrated music player, sold exclusively through a single carrier, while retaining full control of hardware and software design and receiving a share of monthly service revenue.
steelman: Launch a high‑end touchscreen phone without a physical keyboard, featuring a full web browser, email, and the company's music player, sold at a premium price through one carrier that grants the company hardware/software control and revenue sharing, in exchange for several years of exclusivity.
hats_run: skeptic, better-way, kill-criteria
assumptions:
  - Premium pricing will be accepted despite the absence of a physical keyboard in a market dominated by keyboard phones.
  - A single carrier partnership will provide sufficient market reach to meet revenue targets.
  - Integration of the existing music player alone will be a strong enough differentiator to drive sales.
  - The company can develop competitive phone hardware and software without prior phone‑industry experience.
better_ways:
  - Partner with an established smartphone OEM to co‑develop the device, leveraging their hardware/software expertise.
  - Release the phone on multiple carriers to broaden market exposure and reduce reliance on exclusivity.
  - Adopt an open mobile OS (e.g., Android) to enable a third‑party app ecosystem from launch.
  - Introduce an initial lower‑priced model with a physical keyboard to test market acceptance before moving to a premium, keyboard‑less design.
kill_criteria:
  - No third‑party applications available at launch, resulting in projected ARPU below $15/month.
  - Carrier exclusivity limits first‑two‑year unit sales to fewer than 500,000 units.
  - Development costs exceed $500 M without a clear path to profitability.
strongest_attack_survived: The absence of a third‑party app ecosystem makes a premium, keyboard‑less phone unjustifiable; without apps the device cannot compete with emerging smartphones (e.g., iPhone, Android) and will likely capture <10 % market share, violating revenue and ARPU assumptions.
questions_parent_must_resolve:
  - What minimum third‑party app ecosystem is required to achieve the target ARPU?
  - Can the company realistically design and manufacture phone hardware/software within budget and timeline constraints?
  - What are the projected unit sales given the exclusivity limitation?
user_question: NONE
dissent_ledger: The proposal overlooks the essential role of a third‑party app ecosystem for a premium smartphone; launching without it is a fatal flaw that undermines market viability.
remaining_risk: High risk of market rejection due to ecosystem gap and limited carrier reach.
decision: NO-GO
