The claim is to launch a dual‑screen, touch‑enabled handheld at a lower price than the rival, keeping the existing device on sale to broaden the audience.  

**Assumptions** – that a lower price will offset weaker graphics, that developers will adopt a new form factor quickly, and that touch‑screen games will prove popular. None of these are verified; no market or cost data are provided.  

**Better ways** – an incremental upgrade to the current handheld (adding a touch overlay or modest graphics boost) would reduce risk, preserve developer familiarity, and keep the price advantage while still differentiating from the rival.  

**Kill criteria** – if projected development cost raises the retail price above the rival, if fewer than 10 % of third‑party titles are ready for touch within 12 months, or if early sales forecasts fall short of the rival by >20 %, the project should be halted.  

**Premortem** – the device could be dismissed as a gimmick, leading to poor developer support, weak launch titles, and inventory of the older model. The dual‑screen adds hardware complexity and cost, potentially eroding the intended price advantage.  

**Fit with strategy** – expanding beyond core gamers aligns with the company’s growth goal, but the plan risks cannibalizing the existing line and damaging the brand if the product fails to deliver compelling experiences.  

Given the unverified assumptions, the availability of a lower‑risk incremental path, and the lack of concrete kill‑criteria thresholds, the proposal should not be approved as‑is. Further analysis of development costs, developer commitment, and a phased pilot is required before committing to full production.

DECISION: NO-GO
