**Steelman** – Sell the head‑worn display (camera, voice control, small screen) to early adopters at $1,500 via invitation‑only program, then launch to the mass market as a daily‑use consumer gadget.

**Attack** – The strongest objection is the absence of a compelling everyday value proposition at a price that far exceeds comparable devices (smartphones, tablets). Without a clear “must‑have” use case, adoption will be driven by novelty rather than utility, leading to rapid churn.

**Better ways** – 1) Target enterprise or professional niches (field service, remote assistance) where camera and voice add measurable productivity, allowing a higher price point. 2) Reduce price or bundle with content services to improve value perception. 3) Launch a limited‑feature “privacy‑first” version that disables the camera by default and includes a hardware shutter.

**Kill criteria** – If early‑adopter sales < 10 units/month, or if privacy complaints exceed 5 % of feedback, the program must be halted. Battery life below 4 hours of continuous use also triggers cancellation.

**Fit** – The proposal does not align with the company’s core competency of advertising‑driven data services; it diverts resources to hardware without proven revenue streams.

**Pre‑mortem** – Likely failure modes: (a) media backlash over covert recording, (b) mass rejection due to social stigma of “camera glasses,” (c) poor ROI because price forces early adopters into a niche that quickly loses interest.

**Evidence** – Comparable products (e.g., Google Glass) launched in 2013 faced identical barriers and were pulled from consumer market within a year.

**Dissent ledger** – Privacy risk, price/value mismatch, unclear daily utility, social acceptance, battery life.

**Remaining risk** – High market rejection risk; mitigable only by redefining target segment and price.

DECISION: NO-GO
