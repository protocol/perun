**Steelman:** Launch a $89.95 handheld with a monochrome screen, modest processor, long battery life, bundled puzzle game and a $20 M marketing push, expecting sufficient market uptake to become profitable despite competing against a color‑screen rival.

**Attack – concrete failure scenario:**  
Assume unit cost (hardware + game) = $45, shipping = $5 → $50 total. Profit per unit = $90 – $50 = $40. To recoup the $20 M marketing spend alone requires 500 000 units sold. Add $5 M for game licensing and overhead → break‑even rises to ≈ 625 000 units. If the rival’s color screen convinces even price‑sensitive consumers, first‑year sales could fall to 300 000 units, yielding a $4 M loss (300 k × $40 = $12 M revenue vs $20 M marketing). Thin margins at $89.95 leave little cushion for price cuts or unexpected costs, making the project financially fragile.

**Better‑way alternatives:**  
1. Delay launch until a low‑cost color LCD becomes affordable, preserving price parity while offering a superior visual experience.  
2. Release a modular platform: start with the monochrome device but design hardware slots for future color upgrades, spreading R&D risk.  
3. Expand the software bundle beyond a single puzzle game to increase perceived value without raising price.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem):**  
- **Sales threshold:** < 500 000 units in 12 months → project termination.  
- **Margin breach:** average profit per unit < $30 after first‑year → abort.  
- **Competitive shift:** rival reduces price below $80 within 6 months → stop marketing spend.

Given only the supplied facts, the proposal hinges on an optimistic sales assumption without evidence that price and battery life will outweigh the clear disadvantage of a monochrome display versus a color competitor. The thin margin leaves little room for error, and the kill‑criteria are easily triggered.  

**Decision:**  
DECISION: NO-GO
