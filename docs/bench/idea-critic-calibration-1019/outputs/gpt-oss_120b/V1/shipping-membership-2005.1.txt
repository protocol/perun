**Steelman:** Introduce a $79 annual membership that removes per‑order shipping fees, offering free two‑day delivery and discounted one‑day rates, to lower friction for repeat buyers and boost loyalty.

**Attack:** The core assumption is that membership fees will outweigh the incremental shipping cost from higher order frequency. Without data on average order value, shipping cost per order, and the proportion of “heavy” buyers, the model cannot be validated. If a small subset of members dramatically increases order volume, the retailer could incur a net loss on those accounts, eroding margins.

**Better‑way alternatives:**
1. **Do nothing** – retain current per‑order fees and explore targeted free‑shipping thresholds (e.g., free shipping on orders >$50) that cost less to administer.
2. **Lower‑price tier** – launch a $49 “basic” membership with free shipping on orders above a minimum spend, limiting exposure while testing demand.

**Fit:** Reducing shipping friction aligns with the goal of increasing repeat purchases, but the proposal’s financial risk is unquantified, making it a mis‑fit with the company’s short‑term profitability constraints.

**Pre‑mortem:** If heavy buyers double their order frequency, the $79 fee could be eclipsed by an estimated $15‑$20 per‑order shipping cost, leading to a $200‑$300 net loss per heavy member annually. The program would be a one‑way door once members are enrolled, making reversal costly.

**Evidence gap:** No internal data on average shipping cost, order frequency uplift, or break‑even membership count is provided. Without these, the claim cannot be confirmed.

**Conclusion:** Approve only after a pilot with a limited cohort, clear metrics (membership uptake, order frequency lift, net margin impact), and a defined exit strategy.
