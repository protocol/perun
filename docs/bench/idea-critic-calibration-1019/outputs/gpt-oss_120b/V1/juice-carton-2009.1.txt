**Claim** – Replace the current orange‑with‑straw carton nationwide with a minimalist design (glass of juice, orange‑shaped cap, vertically rotated brand name), keep recipe and price, and launch with a large advertising blitz.

**Steel‑man** – A fresh, modern look will attract younger shoppers, signal innovation, and potentially boost sales while preserving product quality and price.

**Attack**  
1. **Shelf cue loss** – The orange‑with‑straw image is the primary visual cue; removing it risks a drop in instant recognition, especially for the brand’s older, loyal buyers.  
2. **Cost** – Full‑nation redesign and a big campaign demand significant upfront spend with no proven ROI.  
3. **Consumer inertia** – Older demographics are less likely to switch to a new look without strong reassurance.  
4. **No test** – Rolling out everywhere at once gives no chance to measure impact or adjust.

**Better‑ways / Alternatives**  
- **Do nothing** – Retain proven packaging; avoid risk and cost.  
- **Phased rollout** – Pilot the new design in a few regions, monitor sales and brand‑recall metrics, then expand if successful.  
- **Minor refresh** – Keep the orange image but simplify graphics or update typography; lower cost and preserves cue.

**Fit** – The brand’s north‑star is to grow market share while protecting existing loyalty. A wholesale visual change mis‑aligns with the “protect core, grow new” strategy.

**Premortem / Kill criteria** –  
- Sales dip >3 % QoQ in the first quarter after launch.  
- Brand‑recall score falls >10 % in consumer surveys within 2 months.  
- Redesign & campaign spend exceeds 115 % of budget.  

If any trigger occurs, the redesign must be rolled back and a remediation plan enacted.

**Evidence** – No market‑test data, cost breakdown, or consumer‑research results are provided; the proposal rests on assumptions.

**Decision** – The risks outweigh the unproven benefits. A controlled pilot or lighter refresh is advisable before a full‑scale launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
