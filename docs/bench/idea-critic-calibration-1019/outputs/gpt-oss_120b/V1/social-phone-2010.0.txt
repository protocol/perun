**Steelman claim**: Launch two teen‑focused smartphones on a proprietary OS, sold exclusively through one carrier with a mandatory, full‑price data plan and no app store, leveraging two years of sunk development to capture the teenage social‑network market.

**Skeptic attacks**  
- **App ecosystem**: Teens choose phones for app variety; rivals already offer thousands of apps. Without an app store, the devices lack core value, likely driving users to competitors.  
- **Price sensitivity**: A data plan equal to a full‑price smartphone plan is unaffordable for most teenagers, reducing adoption dramatically.  
- **Carrier lock‑in**: Exclusive carrier limits market reach; if the carrier’s teen segment is small, sales volumes will be insufficient.  
- **New OS risk**: Building a fresh OS doubles development cost and time, while the company’s own mobile platform is untested, increasing technical risk and delaying updates.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
1. **Use an existing OS (e.g., Android)** and tap its app store, cutting development risk and instantly providing a rich app catalog.  
2. **Partner with multiple carriers** and offer tiered, teen‑friendly data plans, expanding reach and price accessibility.  
3. **Delay launch** to add an app store and subsidized data, converting the project into a low‑risk pilot rather than a full market entry.

**Kill‑criteria** (one‑way door)  
- If pre‑orders in the first 6 months fall below 50 k units, abort the program.  
- If average revenue per user (ARPU) is ≤ $5 /month after launch, cease production.  

**Conclusion**: The proposal’s core risks—no app store, high data‑plan cost, carrier exclusivity, and a brand‑new OS—are fatal for a price‑sensitive teen market. Viable, lower‑risk paths exist that preserve the sunk investment while mitigating market failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
