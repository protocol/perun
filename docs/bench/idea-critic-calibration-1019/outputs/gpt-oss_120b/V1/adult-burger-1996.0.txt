**Steelman** – Introduce a premium adult‑focused burger, priced above the core menu, backed by an unprecedented advertising spend to reposition the brand from child‑centric fast food to a venue that also satisfies adult cravings.

**Alternatives**  
1. Do nothing – retain current child‑focused positioning.  
2. Pilot the premium burger in a limited market before national roll‑out, using modest promotional spend.  
3. Introduce a “gourmet” line of existing items (e.g., upgraded chicken sandwich) with a smaller price premium and test response.

**Fit** – The chain’s long‑term vision includes broader demographic reach, but the core brand equity is tied to families and kids. A full‑scale premium launch risks diluting that equity without proven adult demand.

**Pre‑mortem / Door** – If the premium burger fails, the brand may be perceived as inconsistent, alienating both families (core revenue) and adults (price‑sensitive). This is a **two‑way door**: a failed pilot can be withdrawn, but a national launch consumes large budget and may be hard to reverse without brand damage.

**Evidence (given)** – Risks identified: adults may not associate the chain with premium quality; higher price could deter repeat customers; massive spend precedes demand validation.

**Kill‑criteria** – Proceed only if, within the first 12 weeks of a limited pilot, the premium burger achieves:  
- ≥ 8 % of total store sales, **and**  
- ≥ 70 % positive adult‑customer sentiment in surveys.  
If either metric is missed, the rollout must be halted and the product re‑evaluated.

**Recommendation** – Because the proposal skips market validation and commits extensive spend before demand is proven, the safest path is to test the concept on a small scale first.

DECISION: NO-GO
