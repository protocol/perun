**Steelman:** Build a four‑engine, ultra‑large wide‑body jet with a raised cockpit to enable future freighter conversion, using a new high‑bypass engine and a dedicated factory, to satisfy the airline’s request for dramatically lower cost‑per‑seat as demand surges.

**Skeptic checks**
- **Capital risk:** The factory and development outlay exceed any prior program; cash‑flow impact could jeopardize other product lines.
- **Engine maturity:** The high‑bypass engine has no flight history; certification delays or performance shortfalls would cascade to the whole program.
- **Market timing:** Forecasts anticipate supersonic airliners supplanting sub‑sonic jets; a massive sub‑sonic platform may become obsolete before recouping investment.
- **Customer concentration:** Only one airline has placed an order, creating a single‑point‑of‑failure if that airline’s strategy changes.

**Better‑way alternatives**
1. **Scale‑up existing model:** Stretch the current wide‑body design (e.g., longer fuselage, higher‑capacity variant) using proven engines and existing assembly lines—lower risk, modest CAPEX, and quicker entry.
2. **Joint‑venture with another carrier:** Secure additional launch customers to share development costs and reduce reliance on a single airline.
3. **Delay until engine proven:** Partner with the engine supplier on a smaller aircraft first, validating technology before committing to an ultra‑large platform.

**Kill‑criteria (one‑way‑door)**
- **Cost‑overrun threshold:** Exceeding 30 % of projected development budget without secured additional orders.
- **Engine certification delay:** More than 24 months beyond schedule.
- **Market shift:** Confirmation that supersonic aircraft will capture >20 % of long‑haul traffic within ten years.

**Pre‑mortem scenario**
If the engine fails certification, the factory sits idle, the airline cancels, and the company bears a multi‑billion‑dollar loss while competitors field smaller, flexible models that capture the market. This is a classic one‑way‑door failure.

**Conclusion:** The proposal’s risks outweigh its potential benefits, especially given the lack of diversified demand and unproven engine technology. A phased, lower‑risk approach should be pursued first.

DECISION: NO-GO
