**Steelman** – The company will develop a new four‑engine wide‑body jet, 2.5× larger than its current model, with a raised cockpit for future freighter conversion. A new factory and a high‑bypass engine from a partner will be built. The sole launch customer (its biggest airline) expects lower cost‑per‑seat as passenger demand rises.

**Key Risks & Attacks**  

1. **Capital exposure** – The development and factory program dwarf any prior spend. With only one launch order, the firm bears most of the cost, jeopardizing cash flow and dividend expectations.  
2. **Engine uncertainty** – The high‑bypass engine has no flight heritage; any delay or performance shortfall cascades to the airframe schedule and certification.  
3. **Market shift** – Forecasts in 1966 already anticipate supersonic transports supplanting subsonic jets on premium routes, eroding the long‑term market for very large subsonic aircraft.  
4. **Customer concentration** – Relying on a single airline removes market validation and makes the program vulnerable to that airline’s strategic changes.  

**Better‑Way Alternatives**  

- **Incremental stretch** of the existing model, re‑using current factories and engines, reducing risk and capital outlay.  
- **Joint development** with other airlines to diversify the order book and share costs.  
- **Delay** the new‑large aircraft until the engine supplier proves its product on a smaller platform.

**Fit with Strategy** – The firm’s core competency is efficient sub‑sonic jet production, not ultra‑large aircraft or new‑engine integration. The proposal stretches beyond its proven domain and conflicts with a cautious growth strategy.

**Pre‑mortem / One‑Way Door** – If the engine fails or the airline cancels, the factory becomes a sunk cost with limited alternative use (high‑bypass engines may not suit other programs). The door is effectively two‑way; reversal would require massive re‑tooling.

**Kill‑Criteria** – Lack of at least two firm launch customers and proof‑of‑concept for the engine before committing >$2 billion in capital.

**Conclusion** – The proposal’s upside is outweighed by financial, technical, and market risks that are not mitigated by concrete kill criteria. The prudent course is to reject the current plan and pursue a lower‑risk, incremental development path.

DECISION: NO-GO
