**Steelman:** The developer should launch a mandatory online client for all new PC titles, handling updates, anti‑cheat, purchases, and later becoming a marketplace for other developers. This creates a unified distribution and security platform, potentially increasing revenue and control.

**Attack & Better‑Way:**  
- **Customer friction:** In 2003 broadband penetration was low (≈15‑20 % in major markets). Mandatory online accounts and large downloads would alienate a majority of players, driving them to competitors or piracy.  
- **Retail partner risk:** Retailers could drop boxed versions if they fear reduced foot‑traffic, harming an already valuable sales channel.  
- **Technical risk:** Early‑stage client bugs would be highly visible, damaging brand reputation.  
- **Alternative:** Deploy the client **optionally** for the first few releases, offering a traditional offline installer and a “online‑enhanced” mode. Use the optional rollout to stabilize the client, gather telemetry, and negotiate retailer agreements before mandating it.

**Fit with Strategy:** The goal is to improve update delivery and anti‑cheat while preserving revenue. An optional, phased approach aligns better with the current market constraints and reduces immediate disruption.

**Pre‑mortem / Door:** This is a **two‑way door** (reversible) if introduced as optional. If made mandatory from day one, it becomes a **one‑way door** with high rollback cost (brand damage, lost retail shelf space).

**Kill‑Criteria (quantitative):**  
1. Broadband coverage < 30 % of target market → abort mandatory rollout.  
2. Retailer pushback ≥ 40 % of key partners → delay mandatory requirement.  
3. Critical client bugs > 5 % of early adopters → pause enforcement.

**Evidence:** Historical data shows Steam’s optional launch in 2003 succeeded after iterative improvements; a mandatory model was only viable after broadband became widespread (≈2007 onward).

**Conclusion:** Approving the proposal as written carries unacceptable risk given 2003 infrastructure and partner dynamics. The plan should be revised to an optional, phased rollout with clear kill‑criteria before moving to a mandatory model.

DECISION: NO-GO
