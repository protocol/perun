The community’s loyalty stems from the voting‑driven surfacing of stories; that mechanic is the brand’s differentiator. Replacing it with a publisher‑biased algorithm threatens the very reason users stay, and the proposal does not include any way to preserve or test the existing system.

A full rewrite is a classic “big‑bang” engineering effort. It introduces unknown bugs, integration gaps, and schedule overruns, especially when starting from zero code. Without a staged migration path, any critical failure would affect the entire user base, eliminating the possibility of a quick rollback.

Launching the new version to *all* users at once compounds the technical risk with a product‑risk cascade: users who encounter a broken or disliked experience will leave immediately, accelerating traffic decline. The rival community site, already gaining momentum, can capture disaffected users in real time.

A safer approach would be to:

1. **Retain the current platform** as a live baseline.
2. Develop the new version in parallel and expose it to a small, opt‑in cohort for real‑world validation.
3. Use A/B testing to compare engagement metrics, especially the impact of the publisher‑favoring algorithm versus pure voting.
4. Incorporate community feedback loops before any full migration.
5. Plan a phased rollout with a clear rollback plan.

These steps mitigate engineering uncertainty, protect the core community experience, and provide data to justify a full transition only if the new model demonstrably improves traffic without eroding loyalty.

Given the high probability of user backlash, the technical uncertainty of a total rewrite, and the strategic disadvantage of a single, irreversible launch while a competitor is gaining ground, the current proposal is too risky to approve as written.

DECISION: NO-GO
