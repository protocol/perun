The handheld market in 2004 is split between a graphics‑driven device that targets core gamers and a more modest platform that still dominates sales through its extensive library and brand loyalty. The rival’s superior graphics and media capabilities appeal to enthusiasts, but its higher price and single‑screen design limit reach into the expanding casual segment.

A dual‑screen, touch‑enabled system offers a clear point of differentiation that does not require matching the rival’s raw power. By pricing the new device below the competitor, the company can attract price‑sensitive consumers and families who value novel interaction over high‑end graphics. Maintaining production of the existing handheld preserves the current user base and revenue stream while developers transition to the new platform.

Key risk mitigations:

* **Developer confusion:** Provide a robust, free SDK that abstracts the dual‑screen and touch input, and launch a developer outreach program with funding for early titles. Emphasize that existing games can be ported with minimal changes, reducing entry barriers.
* **Graphics gap:** Position the device as a “play‑anywhere” platform where fun, innovative gameplay outweighs visual fidelity. Target casual and social games that thrive on touch mechanics, a market segment the rival largely ignores.
* **Touch‑screen uncertainty:** Leverage the growing popularity of pen‑based devices (PDAs, smartphones) to demonstrate consumer familiarity. Seed the market with flagship titles that showcase intuitive touch controls.
* **Cost of two screens:** Use a low‑resolution, power‑efficient LCD for the secondary screen and a resistive touch layer that adds minimal material expense. Bulk purchasing and shared components with the existing handheld can further contain costs.
* **Gimmick perception:** Reinforce the dual‑screen concept as a functional design—one display for primary action, the other for maps, inventories, or secondary controls—mirroring successful approaches in other entertainment media.

Overall, the proposal addresses a clear market gap, leverages the company’s brand, and mitigates the primary risks through developer support, price strategy, and focused positioning. Approving the launch aligns with the goal of expanding the audience while protecting core revenue.
