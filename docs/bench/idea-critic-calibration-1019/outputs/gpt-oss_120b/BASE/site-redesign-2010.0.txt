The proposal attempts to reverse a traffic slowdown by rebuilding the entire platform and replacing the core voting‑driven discovery model with a publisher‑biased front page. While a fresh UI could attract new readers, the plan ignores three critical constraints that are already evident:

1. **Community loyalty to voting** – The existing audience “values the existing voting mechanics.” Replacing that with a publisher‑first algorithm risks alienating the core base that sustains engagement and content quality. Losing that base would likely accelerate the traffic decline rather than halt it.

2. **Engineering risk of a full rewrite** – Building the product “from scratch” is a large engineering effort with unknown bugs, performance gaps, and integration challenges. Without a proven incremental path, the team faces a high probability of missing launch dates or delivering an unstable service.

3. **All‑users launch with no rollback** – Moving “all users to it at once” eliminates any safety net. If the new surfacing algorithm or the new codebase fails, the site cannot revert to the proven version, exposing the business to a total service outage or a severe user‑experience regression.

Additionally, a “rival community site is growing,” meaning the window to experiment is narrow. A misstep could push undecided users to the competitor permanently.

A more prudent approach would be to:

* Preserve the voting surface for the existing community, perhaps as a toggle or separate view.
* Introduce the publisher‑focused layout as an optional experiment for a subset of traffic, gathering data on engagement and churn.
* Deploy the rewrite incrementally (micro‑services or feature flags) to limit exposure and retain a quick rollback path.
* Allocate a dedicated stability sprint before any full migration.

Given the magnitude of risk relative to the uncertain upside, the current “big‑bang” plan should not be approved in its present form.

DECISION: NO-GO
