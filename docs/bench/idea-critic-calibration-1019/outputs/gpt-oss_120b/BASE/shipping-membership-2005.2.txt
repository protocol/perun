The proposal targets the retailer’s most cited barrier to repeat purchases: per‑order shipping fees. By offering an annual $79 membership that removes the two‑day fee and lowers the one‑day rate, the program aims to lock customers into a higher‑frequency buying pattern and improve perceived value.

**Potential upside**
* Removes the most salient friction point, likely boosting order frequency and average order value among price‑sensitive shoppers.
* Creates a recurring revenue stream that can be forecasted and used to smooth cash flow.
* Aligns the retailer with a proven market model (e.g., subscription‑based free‑shipping programs) that has driven loyalty in comparable businesses.

**Key risks**
1. **Cost‑overrun exposure** – Heavy buyers could increase shipment volume such that total two‑day shipping costs exceed the $79 fee, eroding margins.
2. **Short‑term margin dip** – The first year will see a reduction in per‑order profit until membership fees and higher volume compensate.
3. **Irreversibility** – Once customers have paid for the membership, the retailer cannot easily unwind the benefit without harming brand trust.
4. **Unproven economics** – No internal data yet confirm that the incremental revenue from added orders will offset the added logistics cost.

**Mitigation considerations**
* Pilot the membership in a limited geography or customer segment to capture real cost and uptake data before a full rollout.
* Set clear eligibility criteria for “eligible orders” to exclude low‑margin items that would otherwise drive loss.
* Implement usage caps or tiered shipping options (e.g., free two‑day only on orders above a minimum spend) to protect against excessive low‑margin shipments.
* Monitor the “heavy buyer” segment closely and adjust pricing or shipping policy if cost‑to‑serve exceeds targets.

Given the substantial unknowns around cost versus revenue and the potential for margin erosion, proceeding with the full‑scale launch as written carries undue risk. A controlled test phase would provide the data needed to validate the model before committing to a company‑wide rollout.

**Decision:** The proposal should not be approved in its current form; it requires a pilot and risk‑mitigation refinements.

DECISION: NO-GO
