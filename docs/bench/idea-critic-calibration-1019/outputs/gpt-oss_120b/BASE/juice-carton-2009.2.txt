The proposal seeks a wholesale redesign of the nation‑wide carton for the leading chilled orange‑juice brand, swapping the long‑standing orange‑with‑straw image for a minimalist glass‑and‑cap visual and a vertically rotated brand name. The product recipe and price remain unchanged, and the launch is intended to be simultaneous across all stores with a large advertising push.

**Key strengths of the plan**

1. **Modern visual appeal** – A cleaner, contemporary look could refresh the brand’s image and potentially attract younger, design‑sensitive shoppers.  
2. **Unified rollout** – Introducing the new packaging everywhere at once avoids market fragmentation and simplifies supply‑chain planning.  
3. **Advertising support** – A heavy media campaign can quickly educate consumers about the new look, mitigating confusion.

**Principal concerns**

1. **Shelf‑cue disruption** – The current orange‑with‑straw graphic is explicitly identified as the brand’s main visual cue. Removing it risks immediate shelf‑recognition loss, especially for older, loyal buyers who rely on that cue to locate the product.  
2. **Cost impact** – Nationwide re‑tooling of packaging, printing, and distribution represents a substantial upfront expense. Without evidence of a commensurate sales lift, the ROI is uncertain.  
3. **Consumer loyalty** – The buyer base skews older and is described as loyal. Historical data in similar categories shows that abrupt visual changes can trigger a measurable dip in sales for such segments.  
4. **Risk of brand dilution** – A minimalist design may not convey the product’s freshness and natural orange identity as strongly as the existing illustration, potentially weakening brand equity.

**Mitigation options not addressed in the proposal**

- Conducting a controlled pilot in a limited geography or test market to gauge shopper response before a full roll‑out.  
- Running focus‑group or eye‑tracking studies to confirm that the new visual retains sufficient recognizability.  
- Phasing the change (e.g., co‑branding both old and new designs for a transition period) to ease consumer adaptation.  
- Including a “new look – same great taste” tagline prominently on the packaging and in advertising to reinforce continuity.

**Conclusion**

While a refreshed design could deliver long‑term brand benefits, the immediate risks to shelf visibility, customer loyalty, and cost outweigh the projected advantages without empirical validation. The prudent course is to postpone the full‑scale launch, gather consumer insights, and explore a phased or test‑market approach.

DECISION: NO-GO
