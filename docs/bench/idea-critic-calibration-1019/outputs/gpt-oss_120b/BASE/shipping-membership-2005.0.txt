The proposal targets the retailer’s primary source of repeat‑customer friction—per‑order shipping fees—by offering an annual $79 membership that provides free two‑day shipping and discounted one‑day rates. Removing that friction is likely to improve customer loyalty, increase order frequency, and raise average order value, all of which can boost long‑term revenue.

However, the only data supplied are the fee amount and the identified risks. The key risk is that “heavy buyers will order more often and shipping costs may exceed fee revenue,” which could erode margins. The retailer would also incur a “large short‑term margin hit” while the membership base grows, and the “offer is hard to reverse once customers hold memberships.” Finally, the “economics are unproven,” meaning there is no evidence that the $79 fee will cover the incremental shipping costs across the expected mix of customers.

Without any quantitative forecasts—such as projected membership uptake, average orders per member, or average shipping cost per order—there is insufficient basis to confirm that the program will be profitable before launch. The risks identified could be mitigated by a controlled pilot, tiered eligibility, or caps on free‑shipping usage, but those mitigations are not part of the current proposal.

Given the lack of supporting financial modelling and the potentially significant margin impact, the prudent course is to request further analysis or a limited‑scale test before committing to a full rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
