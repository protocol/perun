The chip maker’s market is currently opaque to end‑users; buyers cannot identify the processor inside their computers, and clone makers dilute part‑number recognition. The proposal seeks to create a visible consumer brand and to subsidise OEM advertising that carries the new logo, with the aim of prompting purchasers to request the chip by name.

**Potential upside**  
* A consumer‑facing logo can turn a hidden component into a differentiator, allowing the chip maker to command premium pricing and to protect market share from clone competition.  
* Sharing a portion of OEM advertising spend reduces the financial burden on the chip maker and signals partnership rather than unilateral promotion.  
* If successful, the campaign could generate a lasting demand pull‑through effect, similar to other component‑branding initiatives that have transformed buying criteria.

**Key concerns**  
* Consumer advertising for a component is untested; the market may not respond to a logo without a clear value proposition for the end‑user.  
* Allocating a large slice of the marketing budget to an unproven tactic could strain resources needed for product development and channel support.  
* OEMs might view the co‑op requirement as interference in their brand messaging, risking resentment or reduced cooperation.  
* The benefit timeline is uncertain; sales uplift may only appear after a prolonged awareness period, making short‑term ROI difficult to measure.

**Mitigation considerations**  
* Begin with a limited‑scope pilot involving a few strategic OEM partners who are enthusiastic about co‑branding, allowing measurement of lift in brand recall and sales impact before a full rollout.  
* Structure the co‑op contribution as a rebate tied to specific performance metrics (e.g., ad placement prominence, sales growth) to align incentives and reduce perceived pressure on OEMs.  
* Complement the logo with clear consumer‑facing messaging that explains the performance or reliability advantage of the chip, giving buyers a tangible reason to demand it.

Given the substantial budget commitment and the novelty of consumer‑level component advertising, proceeding without a phased test and stronger OEM alignment presents a high risk of wasted spend and partner friction. The proposal should be refined and piloted before full implementation.

DECISION: NO-GO
