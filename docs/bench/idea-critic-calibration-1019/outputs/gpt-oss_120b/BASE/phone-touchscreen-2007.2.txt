The company’s core strength is in personal computers and a best‑selling digital music player, but it lacks any experience in handset design, carrier negotiations, or the mobile operating‑system ecosystem. Entering the phone market now is driven by the imminent threat that existing phone manufacturers will embed music‑player functionality and cannibalise the company’s iPod sales. A mobile device that combines a touchscreen, web browser, email, and the company’s music player directly addresses that threat.

**Key advantages of the proposal**

* Differentiation: a full‑screen touchscreen without a physical keyboard is novel in a market dominated by keypad phones, and it showcases the company’s design ethos.  
* Integrated ecosystem: controlling hardware and software allows seamless integration of the music player, potentially creating a compelling user experience that rivals competitors’ bundled solutions.  
* Revenue share: a share of monthly service revenue provides a recurring income stream beyond one‑off hardware sales.

**Principal risks**

1. **Lack of phone‑industry expertise** – design, supply‑chain, regulatory compliance, and carrier relationships are new domains; early missteps could damage brand reputation.  
2. **Premium pricing without a keyboard** – consumers associate high price with physical keyboards (e.g., BlackBerry); a premium price may limit adoption, especially for first‑time buyers.  
3. **Carrier exclusivity** – restricting sales to a single carrier reduces market reach, slows brand penetration, and makes the product vulnerable to that carrier’s market performance.  
4. **No third‑party applications** – the emerging app ecosystem is a major driver of handset value; a closed platform could quickly become stale.

**Mitigation considerations (not in the current plan)**  

* Partner with a carrier that has a strong premium‑segment presence and can co‑market the device.  
* Commit to a rapid development roadmap that opens the platform to third‑party developers within 6–12 months.  
* Pilot a lower‑priced “entry” model to test consumer acceptance before scaling the premium version.  
* Establish an internal “phone‑team” with hires from experienced handset firms to accelerate learning.

Given the strategic necessity of entering the mobile space, the concept has merit, but the current proposal leaves critical execution gaps that could jeopardise both the product launch and the company’s brand. Approving the plan without addressing the lack of an app ecosystem, carrier‑reach limitations, and pricing concerns is premature.

**Recommendation:** Do not approve the proposal as written; require revisions that add a clear path to third‑party apps, consider broader carrier options, and include a pricing strategy aligned with market expectations.

DECISION: NO-GO
