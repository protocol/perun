The proposal leans heavily on hardware competence but underestimates the ecosystem advantage of the market‑leader. Pricing the new tablet “in line” with that leader places it directly against a device with a mature, high‑visibility brand and a vastly larger app store. The acquired OS has almost no market share and its app catalog is only a fraction of the leader’s, meaning users will encounter immediate functional gaps that cannot be solved by advertising alone.

Heavy spend on developer outreach and advertising is necessary but not sufficient. Building a compelling app base takes time, and early adopters will compare the new tablet to the leader’s offering on a day‑to‑day basis. If the device cannot match the breadth of available software, the price parity will look unjustified, leading to poor conversion and high return rates.

Leadership transition adds internal risk: strategic focus may shift, and execution of the aggressive outreach plan could falter. Consumer perception of the product as “unproven” will be amplified by the lack of a robust app ecosystem and the visibility of the leadership change.

A more viable path would be to:

1. Differentiate on price, form factor, or niche use‑cases rather than direct price competition.
2. Phase the launch, starting with a lower‑cost model while the app ecosystem grows.
3. Secure commitments from key developers before launch to ensure a baseline app set.
4. Align the rollout with a stable leadership team to guarantee consistent strategic execution.

Given the current risk profile—dominant competitor, insufficient app support, nascent OS market share, and leadership uncertainty—the proposal as written is not ready for approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
