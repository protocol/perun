The plan attempts to create a larger retail platform by pairing two struggling businesses. While size‑related economies (shared back‑office, combined purchasing, brand cross‑selling) are attractive in theory, the facts highlight several red flags that outweigh the potential upside.

*Both chains are in decline.* The discount‑store operator has just emerged from court‑supervised restructuring, indicating fragile cash flow and limited flexibility for additional investment. The target, a larger department‑store and appliance network, is also experiencing falling sales, meaning that any revenue boost must come from rapid turnaround rather than organic growth.

*Physical assets are weak.* Stores are described as “old and under‑invested,” implying that significant capital will be required to refurbish locations, align them with a combined format, or meet customer expectations. Without clear financing for these upgrades, the merger could exacerbate cash strain.

*Cost‑saving assumptions are uncertain.* Merging corporate functions can generate efficiencies, yet integration risk is high when cultures and systems differ. The proposal provides no concrete estimate of the magnitude or timeline of savings, making it difficult to rely on them to offset integration costs.

*Customer mismatch.* Discount shoppers and department‑store clientele have distinct preferences and price sensitivities. Converting stores to a hybrid format could alienate both groups if the value proposition is not carefully calibrated, leading to further sales erosion.

*Competitive pressure.* Big‑box rivals are expanding, offering modern stores, broader assortments, and stronger logistics. A combined entity that inherits dated facilities and a fragmented brand portfolio is unlikely to compete effectively without substantial reinvestment.

Given the absence of detailed financial modeling, a clear integration roadmap, and a realistic capital plan to modernize stores, the proposal carries a high risk of value destruction rather than creation. The executive team should request a revised business case that quantifies synergies, outlines required store investments, and demonstrates how the combined brand will differentiate against big‑box competitors before proceeding.

DECISION: NO-GO
