The proposal directly addresses the developer’s two biggest operational pain points—delivering timely updates and curbing cheating—by centralising both functions in a dedicated online client. By making the client mandatory for all new releases, the company gains immediate control over patch distribution and can enforce anti‑cheat measures that are impossible to implement reliably through boxed media alone. This creates a stronger, more secure product experience that can protect the brand’s reputation and retain its existing player base.

From a revenue perspective, the client also lays the groundwork for a digital storefront, opening a future channel for additional sales, micro‑transactions, and third‑party titles. Early adoption of a platform model mirrors the successful launch of similar services in the market (e.g., Valve’s Steam) and positions the company to capture a larger share of the emerging digital distribution ecosystem.

The risks cited are real but manageable:

* **Player resistance to mandatory accounts and large downloads** – Most of the developer’s popular titles already have an engaged, internet‑connected audience; clear communication of the benefits (instant updates, cheat‑free play) can mitigate push‑back. Offering a lightweight installer that pulls only needed assets reduces download concerns.

* **Retail partner backlash** – The client does not eliminate boxed sales; it simply augments them with a digital activation layer. Retailers can continue to stock physical copies while the client handles post‑sale updates, preserving their shelf presence.

* **Early‑stage bugs** – A phased internal beta, followed by a limited public rollout, will surface critical issues before full deployment. The company can allocate a modest QA sprint to harden the client without delaying the launch of the next title.

* **Broadband availability** – By 2003, broadband penetration in the primary markets for PC gaming (North America, Europe) exceeds 50 %, and the client can fall back to dial‑up‑compatible patches for critical fixes, ensuring baseline functionality.

* **Competitive imitation** – The first‑mover advantage of an integrated update/anti‑cheat platform combined with the developer’s existing brand equity creates a barrier that competitors will find costly to replicate quickly.

Overall, the strategic upside—enhanced security, streamlined updates, and a foundation for a digital marketplace—outweighs the manageable risks. The proposal should be approved as presented.
