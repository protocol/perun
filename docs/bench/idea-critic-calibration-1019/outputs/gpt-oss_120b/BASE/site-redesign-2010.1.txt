The proposal aims to reverse a traffic decline by rebuilding the platform and reshaping the content‑surfacing algorithm. While a fresh codebase can address technical debt, the plan introduces three high‑impact risks that outweigh the potential upside if executed as described.

1. **Community Core Value** – The loyal user base is attached to the existing voting system. Changing that mechanism without a transition period is likely to alienate the most engaged members, causing immediate churn. Losing the core community would remove the very asset that differentiates the site from competitors.

2. **Engineering Scope & Roll‑back** – A complete rewrite is a large, uncertain engineering effort. With no ability to revert to the current version, any critical defect—performance, security, or data migration—would affect every user instantly. The lack of a fallback dramatically amplifies delivery risk.

3. **Competitive Pressure** – A rival community is gaining momentum. A disruptive, all‑at‑once launch could give the competitor an opening to capture disaffected users, especially if the new experience fails to meet expectations.

A more prudent approach would be to **preserve the existing voting experience**, introduce the new front‑page algorithm as an optional layer, and roll it out incrementally (e.g., A/B test with a subset of users). This mitigates community backlash, provides real‑world validation of the new surfacing logic, and retains a safety net to revert if needed. It also allows the engineering team to iterate on the rewrite while keeping the live product stable.

Given the current proposal’s lack of phased deployment, rollback capability, and protection of core community values, the risk profile is too high for immediate full‑scale launch.

**DECISION: NO-GO**
