**Assessment**

The chip maker’s market position is strong with computer‑maker customers, but end‑user awareness of the component is effectively zero. Creating a consumer‑facing brand could transform a commodity into a differentiated product, driving demand‑pull that forces OEMs to specify the chip by name. This “pull” strategy is rare for a B2B component, but the early‑1990s have shown successful consumer branding of formerly invisible technologies (e.g., Intel’s “Intel Inside” campaign launched in 1991). Replicating that model could yield similar upside: higher market share, pricing power, and protection against clone competition that exploits similar part numbers.

**Key Risks**

1. **Unprecedented consumer advertising** – The company lacks experience in mass‑media messaging for a component. Creative execution must be crystal‑clear to avoid confusing buyers who do not understand chip functions.  
2. **Budget allocation** – The proposal earmarks a large share of the marketing spend for the co‑op program. If the ROI does not materialize quickly, the firm could impair other critical initiatives (R&D, channel support).  
3. **OEM relationship strain** – Paying part of OEM advertising only when they display the logo could be perceived as intrusive, potentially souring long‑standing partnerships. OEMs may resist or demand higher rebates, eroding the intended cost‑share benefit.  
4. **Uncertain sales impact** – Brand lift does not automatically translate into unit sales, especially when purchase decisions are still dominated by OEM specifications and price.

**Mitigations & Recommendations**

- **Pilot the program** in a limited geography or with a subset of willing OEMs to test consumer response and OEM acceptance before a full roll‑out.  
- **Scale the budget** to a modest test‑phase (e.g., 10‑15 % of total marketing spend) to preserve flexibility for other priorities.  
- **Co‑create the logo and messaging** with OEM partners, positioning the initiative as a joint market‑development effort rather than a unilateral demand.  
- **Set clear KPIs** (brand awareness, OEM ad usage, incremental sales) and a short‑term review (6‑9 months) to decide on expansion.

**Conclusion**

The strategic rationale is strong, but the current proposal lacks safeguards against the highlighted risks. A phased, lower‑budget pilot with OEM collaboration is required before committing full resources.

DECISION: NO-GO
