The proposal targets a clear‑cola positioned as a “purer” alternative at a time when the company’s overall soft‑drink growth is decelerating. Test‑market data show a positive response, indicating that the concept can attract attention and generate trial. However, the evidence is limited to a small geographic sample and does not address several critical uncertainties.

**Consumer perception:** Cola flavor is historically tied to a dark hue. Even if the taste is identical, many shoppers may reject a clear version because they equate color with authenticity, potentially limiting repeat purchase rates.

**Novelty decay:** Early adopters in test markets may be driven by curiosity rather than genuine preference. Without clear evidence of sustained buying, the product could become a short‑lived fad, wasting the large marketing spend.

**Brand cannibalization:** Introducing a clear variant under the same brand umbrella risks pulling sales away from the flagship cola, especially if price points are similar. The net effect on total volume could be neutral or negative.

**Test‑market over‑optimism:** Positive results in limited markets often inflate demand forecasts. Scaling to a national audience introduces diverse consumer habits, distribution realities, and competitive pressures that were not captured in the pilot.

**Competitive response:** A high‑visibility launch invites rivals to quickly replicate the format, eroding any first‑mover advantage and turning the clear cola into a commodified niche.

Given these risks, a nationwide rollout with a large marketing budget appears premature. A more prudent approach would be a staged expansion—adding additional test regions that represent broader demographics, gathering longitudinal sales data, and developing a clear differentiation strategy (e.g., distinct branding, pricing, or limited‑edition positioning) to protect the flagship. Preparing contingency plans for rapid competitor copycats would also strengthen the launch.

**Recommendation:** Hold the current proposal. Refine the rollout plan to mitigate perception, cannibalization, and market‑scale uncertainties before committing to a full‑nation launch and the associated marketing spend.
