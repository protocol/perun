**Evaluation**

1. **Customer Impact** – In 2003 many gamers still rely on dial‑up or limited‑speed broadband. Mandatory large downloads and a required online account will frustrate a sizable segment, risking churn and negative publicity. The brand’s reputation for “box‑ed” convenience could be damaged, especially among collectors and regions with poor connectivity.

2. **Retail Partner Relations** – Retailers stock physical copies because they offer instant, offline play. Introducing a compulsory online client threatens that value proposition; partners may drop the titles or demand concessions, eroding a proven revenue channel.

3. **Technical Risk** – Launching a new client that must handle updates, anti‑cheat, and purchases is complex. Early‑stage bugs could lock users out of games they have already paid for, amplifying the backlash. A broken client would also expose the company to legal complaints over failed delivery of purchased products.

4. **Strategic Opportunity** – A unified online platform could eventually become a distribution hub, generating recurring revenue from other developers and giving the company tighter control over updates and cheating. However, this long‑term upside only materializes after the platform proves reliable and is accepted by the market.

5. **Competitive Landscape** – The concept is novel but not protected; rivals could launch similar services quickly, especially if the company’s rollout is troubled. Early missteps would hand them a market advantage.

6. **Mitigation Paths** – A phased approach—starting with optional online updates for new releases, limited to markets with high broadband penetration, and preserving the boxed experience for others—would reduce immediate friction. Parallel investment in client stability and a robust customer‑support pipeline is essential before mandating the platform.

**Conclusion**  
Given the high probability of consumer resistance, retailer push‑back, and technical instability at launch, proceeding with a mandatory, universal online client in 2003 presents an unacceptable risk to current revenue and brand equity. The proposal should be revised to incorporate an optional, phased rollout and stronger safeguards before full commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
