The PC maker’s current OS is a strategic liability: it is aging, internal replacement attempts have failed twice, and the company is losing market share. An operating system is a platform‑defining asset; without a modern, extensible core the firm cannot retain developers, attract new applications, or differentiate its hardware.

**Acquisition upside**  
* Owning the source code gives full control over roadmap, licensing, and integration with hardware.  
* The target’s object‑oriented design matches the industry shift toward modular, scalable systems, reducing future re‑engineering costs.  
* The founder’s technical expertise can accelerate knowledge transfer and help avoid the pitfalls that doomed the internal rebuild attempts.  
* Even though the target has sold few units, its technology represents a “greenfield” platform that can be commercialized at scale once embedded in the maker’s hardware ecosystem.

**Key risks and mitigations**  
1. **Limited market traction** – The target’s low sales mean little revenue to offset the purchase price. Mitigation: view the deal as a technology acquisition, not a revenue play; the real value lies in the IP and talent, which can be leveraged across the maker’s product line.  
2. **Migration complexity** – Moving existing applications and developer tools to a new kernel is costly. Mitigation: allocate a dedicated integration team, establish a phased migration path with compatibility layers, and retain key engineers from the target to shepherd the transition.  
3. **Founder’s difficult history** – Potential cultural friction could undermine the integration. Mitigation: define the founder’s role strictly as an advisory, time‑boxed position with clear performance milestones and a governance structure that balances his influence with existing leadership.  
4. **Alternative licensing** – Licensing another vendor’s OS (e.g., Windows) would be cheaper short‑term but would cede strategic control and lock the maker into another company’s roadmap. The acquisition preserves independence and opens future licensing revenue streams.

**Financial perspective** – A few hundred million dollars is sizable relative to the target’s revenue, but comparable to historic platform acquisitions that later generated multi‑billion‑dollar ecosystems (e.g., Apple‑NeXT). The maker’s declining market share makes a bold, forward‑looking move necessary; incremental licensing would not reverse the trend.

**Conclusion** – The strategic imperative to replace the obsolete OS, combined with the acquisition’s long‑term control benefits, outweighs the manageable risks when appropriate mitigation plans are put in place. Approve the proposal as presented.

DECISION: GO
