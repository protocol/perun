The membership idea directly attacks the primary barrier to repeat purchases: per‑order shipping fees. Removing that friction is likely to raise order frequency, basket size, and overall customer lifetime value, especially among price‑sensitive shoppers who abandon carts over shipping costs.

However, the proposal assumes that the $79 fee will cover the incremental cost of two‑day delivery for all members. Heavy‑use customers could consume many shipments, pushing the cost of fulfillment above the collected fee and eroding margins. The “unlimited” nature of the benefit also creates a lock‑in that is hard to reverse if the model proves unprofitable, exposing the company to a sustained short‑term margin dip.

Mitigations that are not present in the current plan include:

* **Usage caps or tiered eligibility** – limiting free two‑day shipping to orders above a minimum value or to a set number of shipments per month reduces exposure to extreme usage while preserving the perceived value.
* **Pilot program** – launching the membership in a limited geographic region or for a subset of product categories would generate real cost‑to‑revenue data before a full rollout.
* **Dynamic pricing** – adjusting the annual fee based on observed shipping cost trends or offering a discounted rate for early adopters can align revenue with expense.
* **Cross‑selling incentives** – bundling the membership with exclusive product offers or early‑access events can increase the perceived value and drive additional sales that offset shipping costs.

Without any of these controls, the proposal relies on untested economics and places the company at risk of a margin squeeze and a potentially irreversible commitment to a costly benefit structure. The prudent course is to refine the offering and gather empirical data before a full launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
