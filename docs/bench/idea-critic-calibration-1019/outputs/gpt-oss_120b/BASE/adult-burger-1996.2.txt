The proposal seeks to reposition a child‑focused fast‑food brand by introducing a premium adult burger and backing it with unprecedented advertising spend. While the concept could attract a new segment, the facts highlight several critical mismatches:

1. **Brand perception** – The chain is known for family‑friendly, value‑oriented meals. Adults already associate it with quick, inexpensive food, not premium quality. Shifting that perception requires more than a single product; it demands a sustained, credible repositioning effort.

2. **Customer expectations** – Core patrons visit for kids’ meals and low‑price options. A higher‑priced burger risks alienating them, especially if it replaces or crowds out existing value items.

3. **Price elasticity** – The premium price is above the current menu average. Without evidence of demand at that level, the price may suppress trial and repeat purchases, eroding overall traffic.

4. **Financial exposure** – The plan calls for the largest advertising and promotion budget ever in the industry, yet demand has not been validated. Deploying such capital before confirming market appetite could strain cash flow and generate low ROI.

5. **Competitive landscape** – Established premium burger brands already command adult loyalty through quality, ambience, and price positioning. Competing directly without a differentiated proposition or proven brand shift is a steep uphill battle.

Given these risks and the lack of supporting data, proceeding as proposed would likely waste resources and damage the brand’s existing equity. A more prudent path would be to test a limited‑run premium offering, gather consumer response, and adjust pricing and marketing spend accordingly.

DECISION: NO-GO
