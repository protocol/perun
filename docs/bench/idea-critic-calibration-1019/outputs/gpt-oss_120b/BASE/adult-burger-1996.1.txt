The chain’s core strength is its family‑friendly, value‑oriented image. Introducing a premium burger that costs significantly more than existing items challenges that positioning on three fronts:

1. **Brand perception** – Adults already view the brand as a convenient, low‑price venue for kids. A single upscale product will not instantly shift that perception; it may instead create confusion or be dismissed as a gimmick.

2. **Price elasticity** – The target adult segment is price‑sensitive when it comes to fast‑food. A higher‑priced sandwich risks alienating regular patrons who are accustomed to low‑cost meals, and it may not attract diners who already have established premium fast‑casual alternatives.

3. **Capital risk** – Committing the industry’s largest advertising and promotion budget before any market validation places a heavy financial burden on the company. If demand does not materialize, the spend will erode margins without generating a sustainable revenue stream.

A more prudent approach would be to test the concept in a limited geography, gauge adult traffic, and refine pricing before scaling. Incremental marketing spend tied to measurable performance metrics would protect the brand’s existing profitability while allowing the chain to explore the adult market.

Given the current proposal’s reliance on a full‑scale launch and massive upfront spend without evidence of demand, the risk outweighs the potential upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
