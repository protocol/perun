The proposal aims to refresh a long‑standing visual cue that has anchored the brand on shelves for years. The current orange‑with‑straw image is the primary identifier for shoppers, especially for the brand’s core, older, loyal buyers. Removing it in a single nationwide rollout creates three immediate threats:

1. **Shelf‑recognition loss** – Without the familiar cue, many consumers may overlook the product or mistake it for a competitor, leading to an immediate sales dip.
2. **Cost exposure** – Re‑tooling packaging for all SKUs and distributing a large advertising blitz simultaneously will strain the budget, with no guarantee of offsetting revenue.
3. **Consumer resistance** – Older, repeat purchasers are less likely to embrace a drastic visual change, especially if the product’s taste and price remain unchanged.

Potential upside—modern, minimalist packaging—could attract younger shoppers and signal brand vitality, but this benefit is speculative without market testing. A safer path is to validate the new design in a controlled environment before committing to a full‑scale launch.

**Recommended approach**

* **Pilot phase** – Introduce the redesign in a limited geography or in a single retail channel (e.g., a regional supermarket chain). Track sales lift, brand‑recognition metrics, and consumer sentiment.
* **Dual‑SKU period** – Keep the legacy packaging alongside the new design for a transition window, allowing loyal shoppers to locate the product while new shoppers encounter the refreshed look.
* **Cost‑phased spend** – Spread the advertising budget over several quarters, focusing first on awareness in pilot markets, then expanding if results are positive.
* **Feedback loop** – Use in‑store surveys and digital listening to gauge acceptance among the older base and to refine messaging before a nationwide push.

These steps reduce risk, preserve existing revenue, and provide data to confirm whether the aesthetic shift truly broadens the audience. Approving the proposal as written, with an all‑at‑once national rollout, would expose the brand to unnecessary disruption and financial strain.

**Conclusion:** The concept has merit but requires validation and a staged implementation before full deployment.
