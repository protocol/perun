**Assessment**

1. **Market position** – In 2006 the incumbent digital‑music player controls the vast majority of hardware sales and has built a tightly integrated ecosystem (device + store + DRM). Entrants must either offer a compelling price/feature edge or a disruptive business model to win users and label support.

2. **Timing** – The proposal arrives well after the incumbent’s launch, when consumer expectations and developer support are already aligned with the incumbent’s standards. Late entry raises acquisition cost and compresses the window for market share growth.

3. **Technical incompatibility** – Dropping the copy‑protection standard that the company previously championed severs the existing partnership network. Labels and device makers that rely on that DRM may refuse licensing or impose restrictive terms, directly threatening the core revenue stream of the music store.

4. **Sharing feature risk** – Wireless song sharing is attractive but conflicts with label‑imposed rights management. Without a proven, label‑approved sharing protocol, the feature could be blocked, muted, or become a legal liability.

5. **Value proposition** – The proposed larger screen, desktop client and subscription model do not create a clear price or functional advantage over the incumbent’s offering. Marketing spend alone is unlikely to overcome entrenched loyalty and network effects.

6. **Financial exposure** – A “large marketing budget” without a differentiated product or secured label agreements inflates cost while the probable return on investment remains uncertain.

**Conclusion** – The combination of late market entry, loss of DRM alignment, uncertain label cooperation, and lack of a distinct price/feature advantage creates a high‑risk scenario. The proposal does not demonstrate sufficient mitigation of these risks to justify immediate commitment of resources.

DECISION: NO-GO
