The plan hinges on rapid scale through three high‑cost levers: a national TV push, a large acquisition, and expanded warehousing, while intentionally pricing many items below cost to win market share. Each lever alone consumes significant cash; together they create a burn rate that the company’s current cash flow cannot support.

**Financial sustainability** – Selling products below the purchase price generates negative gross margins on a material portion of the catalog. Shipping heavy, low‑margin goods adds a fixed cost per unit that cannot be offset by the low prices. Even with a surge in volume, the contribution margin will remain negative, forcing the business to rely on external financing to stay afloat.

**Customer‑acquisition economics** – The proposed TV campaign, especially a major sports broadcast, is an extremely expensive acquisition channel. With already high CAC, the additional spend will only deepen the cash drain unless the lifetime value of a customer can be dramatically increased – a prospect that is doubtful when many purchases lose money.

**Capital requirements and market risk** – The IPO is presented as the source of funding, yet the capital markets are volatile and could close at any time, especially for a company with an unproven profitability model. If the offering is delayed or priced down, the company will lack the cash needed for the ad buy, acquisition, and warehouse build‑out.

**Competitive dynamics** – Rivals are also investing heavily in advertising and logistics. Matching that spend without a clear path to positive unit economics risks a price‑war where all participants bleed cash.

**Strategic alternatives** – A more disciplined approach would be to (1) focus on a core set of profitable SKUs, (2) improve gross margins before scaling advertising, (3) pursue smaller, strategic acquisitions that add capabilities rather than sheer volume, and (4) test targeted digital marketing to reduce CAC. These steps could improve margin and cash flow, making an eventual IPO and larger spend far less risky.

Given the current proposal’s reliance on unsustainable pricing, high‑cost acquisition, and uncertain capital market timing, proceeding as outlined would likely erode shareholder value and jeopardize the company’s viability.

**Conclusion:** The proposal should not be approved in its present form.

DECISION: NO-GO
