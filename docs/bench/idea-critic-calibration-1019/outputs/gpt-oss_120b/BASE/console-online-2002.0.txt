The proposal targets a strategic gap: competitors offer fragmented, free‑or‑publisher‑controlled online play, while the company would introduce a unified, subscription‑based service. The envisioned benefits—single player identity, friends list, voice chat, and cross‑title matchmaking—could create a compelling ecosystem that encourages loyalty and recurring revenue.

**Market realities (2002)**  
* Broadband penetration remains low; most households still rely on dial‑up. A service that “requires broadband” will exclude a sizable portion of the console’s installed base, limiting early adoption and reducing the network effect that is critical for multiplayer services.  
* Consumer expectations for console online play are still forming. Existing rivals provide free or publisher‑controlled online access, so a mandatory subscription will be perceived as a barrier, potentially driving players to those alternatives or to rival consoles that later adopt free models.

**Operational considerations**  
* The company has never run large‑scale hosting or support operations. Building a reliable, low‑latency infrastructure, staffing 24/7 support, and managing billing and account services will demand significant capital and talent acquisition. The learning curve and risk of service outages could damage brand reputation at a critical launch window.  
* Ongoing costs (servers, bandwidth, customer service) will be high relative to the modest subscription fee that can be justified in a market where many users are not yet accustomed to paying for online connectivity.

**Financial outlook**  
* Even if a modest 5‑10 % of console owners subscribe, revenue may not cover the upfront infrastructure investment within a reasonable timeframe, especially given the limited broadband pool.  
* The annual subscription adds a price premium on top of the console cost, potentially reducing overall unit sales in a price‑sensitive market.

**Strategic alternatives**  
* Delay the service launch until broadband adoption reaches a critical mass (e.g., 30‑40 % of the user base).  
* Pilot a free‑to‑play model with optional premium features (e.g., enhanced voice chat, exclusive matchmaking) to gauge demand and build the necessary operational capabilities gradually.  
* Partner with an established online service provider to outsource hosting and support, reducing initial capital outlay and operational risk.

Given the current low broadband penetration, consumer expectations for free online play, and the company’s lack of experience in running a large‑scale online service, proceeding with the proposed paid, subscription‑only launch would likely limit adoption, strain resources, and expose the brand to reputational risk.

**Recommendation:** Hold the proposal, address market readiness and operational capability before committing to a full‑scale paid service.

DECISION: NO-GO
