The proposal calls for a transformational leap: a four‑engine, wide‑body jet that would be roughly 2½ times larger than the company’s current offering, built in a brand‑new factory and powered by a yet‑to‑prove high‑bypass engine. The airline that initiated the request is the company’s biggest customer, and the aircraft is designed with a raised cockpit to enable later conversion to a freighter, potentially expanding its market appeal.

**Key concerns**

1. **Capital intensity** – The development and factory outlay far exceeds any previous program. This creates a massive financial exposure, especially if the market does not materialize as expected.
2. **Engine risk** – The high‑bypass engine, while promising superior fuel efficiency, has no flight history. Any delay or performance shortfall would directly impact the aircraft’s schedule and cost structure.
3. **Market uncertainty** – Only a single airline has placed an order. Although the airline is the largest customer, the program’s economics rely on a broader order book that has not yet been secured.
4. **Technological disruption** – Industry speculation that supersonic airliners will soon dominate could erode demand for large subsonic jets, making the investment vulnerable to a shift in passenger preferences.

**Potential upside**

- If the aircraft achieves the promised seat‑cost reduction, it could set a new industry standard and attract a cascade of orders from other carriers.
- The freighter conversion capability adds a secondary market, extending the aircraft’s life cycle and revenue potential.

**Assessment**

Given the scale of financial commitment, the unproven engine, and the lack of a diversified order base, the risk profile is exceptionally high. The strategic rationale hinges on securing additional customers and mitigating engine development risk before committing full resources. Proceeding without these safeguards could jeopardize the company’s financial stability.

**Conclusion**

The proposal should not be approved in its current form. The company should seek additional airline commitments, explore risk‑sharing arrangements for the engine program, and reassess the market outlook for subsonic versus supersonic travel before moving forward.

DECISION: NO-GO
