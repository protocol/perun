**Evaluation Summary**

**Market Opportunity**  
- The post‑war baby‑boom cohort is now reaching prime car‑buying age. Their purchasing power is rising and they value style as much as price.  
- A “sporty” look on a compact platform directly addresses a gap: a low‑cost vehicle that feels youthful without the premium price tag of full‑size sports cars.

**Product Strategy**  
- Using the existing compact car’s mechanicals cuts engineering time and tooling costs, preserving margins.  
- A two‑door coupe and convertible with a long hood/short deck visual cue delivers the desired “sporty” image while keeping the proven drivetrain.  
- A long options list lets buyers personalize the vehicle, increasing average transaction value and catering to varied taste.

**Key Risks**  

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation Needed |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| Unproven volume demand for small sporty cars | Sales could fall far short of forecast, leaving excess capacity and inventory. | Conduct a limited‑region pilot (e.g., 5‑10% of national dealer network) to gauge true demand before full rollout. |
| Perception of cheapness due to shared parts with economy model | Brand dilution; buyers may reject the car as “just a dressed‑up economy car.” | Distinguish exterior trim, interior materials, and badging; add exclusive styling cues that cannot be retro‑fitted to the base model. |
| Large launch advertising spend before sales validation | Cash flow strain; ROI uncertain if demand is weak. | Phase advertising: start with targeted, lower‑cost media in test markets, scaling up only after early sales data confirm traction. |

**Financial Outlook**  
- Parts commonality promises a favorable cost structure, but the high upfront ad budget could erode early profitability if sales lag.  
- The options strategy can boost per‑unit revenue, but only if buyers perceive the base model as a credible platform for upgrades.

**Recommendation**  
Approve the concept **conditionally**: proceed with a controlled market introduction, refined branding to separate it from the economy car, and a staged advertising spend. This approach preserves the strategic intent while limiting exposure to the identified risks.

**Decision**  
DECISION: NO-GO
