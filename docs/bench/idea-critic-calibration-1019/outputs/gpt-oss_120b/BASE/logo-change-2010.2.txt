**Assessment**

The retailer’s logo is a long‑standing visual anchor: white capital letters inside a dark‑blue square. Decades of exposure mean the square alone is a primary cue for brand recognition, especially among loyal shoppers. Replacing it with a neutral‑typeface wordmark plus a smaller blue square removes the dominant visual element without any customer validation.

**Key Risks**

1. **Brand‑recognition loss** – The square is the most recognizable feature; removing it could dilute instant recall across all touchpoints.  
2. **Customer attachment** – Loyal patrons often associate the existing logo with trust and heritage; abrupt change may be perceived as a break with the brand’s identity.  
3. **Social‑media amplification** – Unannounced, wholesale rollout invites immediate, unfiltered feedback; negative sentiment can spread rapidly and affect sales.  
4. **No user testing** – Without quantitative or qualitative research, the new design’s appeal, readability, and emotional impact are unknown.

**Potential Upside**

A contemporary wordmark could modernize the visual language, aligning the brand with current design trends and potentially attracting a younger demographic. However, the upside is contingent on acceptance, which cannot be gauged without testing.

**Recommendation**

Proceed with a **phased, data‑driven approach**:

- Conduct controlled A/B testing with a representative sample of existing customers and target prospects.  
- Use a soft‑launch in limited markets or on digital channels, gathering real‑time sentiment and sales impact.  
- Develop a communication plan that explains the rationale, celebrates heritage, and introduces the new look gradually.  
- Retain the iconic blue square as a secondary mark or incorporate it subtly to preserve recognizability during transition.

Only after confirming positive reception and minimal brand‑equity erosion should a full rollout be executed.

**Decision:** The proposal as presented carries excessive risk and lacks validation.

DECISION: NO-GO
