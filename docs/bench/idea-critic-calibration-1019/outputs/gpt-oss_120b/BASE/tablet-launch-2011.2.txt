The proposal targets a market that is already dominated by a single, well‑established tablet. The leader’s strong brand, mature hardware, and deep app ecosystem create a high barrier for any newcomer. Launching a device that “prices in line with the market‑leading tablet” places the new offering directly against a product with far greater consumer awareness and proven sales velocity.

The operating system acquired a year ago has “almost no market share” and a “small app catalog.” Tablet users select devices largely based on the breadth and quality of available applications. Even with an aggressive developer‑outreach program, building a compelling app ecosystem takes months, if not years, to reach a critical mass. Early adopters will encounter a sparse selection, reinforcing the perception of an “unproven” product and driving them to the incumbent.

Leadership transition adds further uncertainty. New executives may shift priorities, and the organization could lack the focus needed to sustain the heavy advertising and developer‑investment required to overcome the entrenched market leader.

Investing heavily in advertising and outreach while simultaneously confronting a thin app catalog and low OS adoption creates a mismatch of resources. The likely outcome is a high‑cost launch that fails to achieve sufficient market traction, leading to inventory surplus and brand dilution.

Given these constraints—dominant competitor, insufficient app ecosystem, minimal OS penetration, and internal leadership volatility—the risk profile outweighs the potential upside of an on‑time launch. A more prudent approach would be to pause, evaluate alternative strategies (e.g., partnership, OS licensing, or a differentiated hardware proposition), and only proceed once the ecosystem and leadership stability are demonstrably stronger.

DECISION: NO-GO
