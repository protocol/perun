**Steelman** – The retailer will instantly replace its decades‑old logo with a neutral‑typeface wordmark plus a tiny blue square, deploying the change across all channels simultaneously and without any prior communication.

**Attack**  
1. **Customer attachment** – Loyal shoppers identify the dark‑blue square as the visual anchor of the brand. Removing it abruptly can erode brand equity and trigger negative sentiment, especially on social platforms where backlash spreads instantly.  
2. **Recognition loss** – The square is the primary cue for brand recall in crowded retail spaces. Eliminating it without a transition period risks a measurable dip in unaided brand awareness.  
3. **Untested design** – No customer research or A/B testing has been conducted. A neutral wordmark may not resonate with the target demographic, potentially lowering perceived relevance and purchase intent.  
4. **All‑channel roll‑out** – Simultaneous change removes the safety net of a staged pilot that could reveal unforeseen operational or legal issues (e.g., trademark registration, signage updates).

**Better‑way** – Conduct limited‑scope testing (focus groups, online surveys, pilot stores) and plan a phased rollout with an announcement campaign that frames the redesign as a refresh, preserving the blue square as a subtle brand element.

**Kill‑criteria** – If pilot testing shows >15 % negative sentiment or a >5 % drop in brand recall, abort the full launch.

Given the high risk of brand damage and the absence of validation, the proposal should not be approved as‑is.

DECISION: NO-GO
