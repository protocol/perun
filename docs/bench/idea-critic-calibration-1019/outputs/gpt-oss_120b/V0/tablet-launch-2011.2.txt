**Steelman claim** – Launch a tablet built on the newly‑acquired OS, priced competitively with the market‑leader, while rapidly expanding the app catalog through aggressive developer outreach and a heavy advertising push.

**Skeptic attacks**  
1. **Price parity without differentiation** – Matching the leader’s price gives no advantage; with a thin app ecosystem the product will be judged on price alone and likely lose to the incumbent.  
2. **App‑catalog gap** – A “small” catalog means many core apps are missing; early‑adopter reviews will highlight this deficiency, reducing sales regardless of marketing spend.  
3. **OS market‑share near zero** – Developers will be reluctant to invest unless a sizable user base is guaranteed; outreach budgets may not yield the needed ecosystem growth.  
4. **Leadership transition** – Unstable senior management can delay critical launch‑readiness decisions, increasing time‑to‑market risk.  
5. **Consumer perception of “unproven”** – Retailers and buyers typically avoid first‑generation tablets from unknown platforms, leading to weak initial demand.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Phase‑in strategy** – Release a low‑cost, education‑focused tablet first, leveraging bulk contracts and a limited app set, then expand to the consumer market once the ecosystem proves viable.  
- **Partner‑first approach** – License the OS to an established tablet OEM rather than building hardware, reducing capital risk while growing the app base.  
- **Differentiation via unique features** – Identify a niche (e.g., stylus‑heavy productivity, rugged enterprise use) and price accordingly, rather than competing head‑on with the market leader.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**  
- If, after six months, the app catalog remains <20 % of the leader’s count **or** unit sales are <5 % of the quarterly tablet market, halt the program.  
- If leadership transition is unresolved after three months, suspend launch activities.  
- If cumulative advertising + outreach spend exceeds $150 M without a clear path to break‑even within 24 months, terminate.

**Concrete failure scenario** – Launch at leader price, but with only 30 % of required apps; first‑quarter sales hit 8 000 units, generating a $45 M loss and eroding brand credibility, triggering a cash‑burn runway crisis.

**Conclusion** – The proposal proceeds with too many unresolved high‑risk assumptions and lacks a defensible mitigation path. It should be revised to adopt a phased, differentiated, or partnership model before committing resources.

DECISION: NO-GO
