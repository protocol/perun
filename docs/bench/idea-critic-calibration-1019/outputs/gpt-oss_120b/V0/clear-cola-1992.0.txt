**Steelman:** The company should launch a clear‑cola nationwide, leveraging positive test‑market feedback, with a major marketing push to capture growth in a stagnant soft‑drink market and establish a differentiated, premium brand.

**Attack & Alternatives**  
1. **Risk of low repeat purchase:** Novelty drinks often see a sharp drop after the first trial. Without proven repeat demand, the large marketing spend may not be recouped.  
2. **Cannibalization:** A clear variant may simply shift existing flagship customers, eroding overall margin.  
3. **Test‑market bias:** Limited markets can over‑state demand, especially when the product’s novelty is a focal point.  
4. **Competitive copycat:** Rivals can quickly introduce similar clear colas, neutralizing any first‑mover advantage.

*Alternatives*  
- **Do nothing** – preserve cash and avoid brand dilution.  
- **Phased regional rollout** – test repeat purchase and cannibalization metrics before national spend.  
- **Co‑brand or limited‑edition release** – lower marketing cost while gauging demand.

**Fit:** The proposal aligns with a growth‑oriented strategy but conflicts with risk‑averse financial stewardship given the high upfront spend.

**Pre‑mortem & Kill‑criteria:**  
- Abort if 3‑month repeat purchase < 15 % or flagship sales dip > 5 % in the same period.  
- Pull back if ROI on marketing falls below 1.5× within six months.

**Conclusion:** The uncertainties and high irreversible cost outweigh the tentative test‑market optimism. A controlled, metric‑driven rollout is required before committing to a nationwide launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
