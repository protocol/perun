**Steelman** – Launch a $89.95 handheld with a small monochrome screen, modest processor, long battery life, bundled puzzle game, supported by a $20 M marketing push, to capture price‑sensitive gamers and extend the brand into portable play.

**Attack** – The market already has a rival handheld offering a color screen and stronger hardware. A monochrome display will be perceived as inferior, limiting appeal to casual buyers. Modest specs risk poor game performance and limit future titles. The company’s legacy of single‑game devices gives no ecosystem advantage. At $89.95 the gross margin is thin; a $20 M marketing spend further pressures profitability.

**Better‑ways** –  
1. **Do nothing** – preserve capital and avoid margin erosion.  
2. **Upgrade to color** – raise price modestly (≈$119) to match rival hardware, leveraging existing R&D for a competitive product.  
3. **Partner/licence** – co‑publish on the rival’s platform, sharing costs and gaining instant market presence.

**Fit** – Expanding into handheld aligns with the goal of broader market reach, but the proposal does not advance the company’s north‑star of premium, differentiated experiences.

**Pre‑mortem** – Failure would stem from poor sales, negative press over “outdated” screen, and inability to recoup marketing spend, leading to a loss of $5‑10 M in the first year and brand dilution.

**Evidence** – All points derive from the proposal’s facts; no external data is available.

**Kill‑criteria** – Abort if first‑year unit sales < 200 k (≈$18 M revenue) or if gross margin falls below 15 % after marketing costs.

**Dissent ledger** – Risks of inferior hardware, thin margins, and lack of ecosystem outweigh the low‑price advantage.

**Remaining risk** – Market perception of a “budget” handheld could erode premium brand equity.

Given the competitive disadvantage and financial exposure, the proposal should be revised before approval.  

DECISION: NO-GO
