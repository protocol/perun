**Steelman** – Launch a free, invitation‑only webmail service offering 1 GB storage, fast full‑text search, and a responsive UI, funded by context‑matched text ads scanned from users’ messages.

**Critical attack**

1. **Cost & scalability** – In 2004, storage cost was roughly $10‑$15 per GB per month. Even a modest user base of 100 k would require $1‑1.5 M/month for raw storage alone, not counting redundancy, indexing, or bandwidth. The proposal provides no budget or break‑even analysis, making the financial risk unbounded.

2. **Privacy & legal exposure** – Scanning message content to serve ads raises immediate privacy objections and may violate emerging data‑protection regulations (e.g., EU Directive 2002/58). The plan offers no mitigation (opt‑out, on‑device processing) and no user‑trust strategy, exposing the company to backlash and potential litigation.

3. **Market dynamics** – By 2004 Google had announced Gmail with 1 GB free storage and ad‑supported model, already gaining rapid adoption. Established players (Yahoo, Hotmail) also dominate user habits. The proposal lacks differentiation beyond “invitation‑only” and does not address network‑effects or migration costs.

4. **Revenue timing** – No direct revenue at launch and reliance on ad inventory that depends on message scanning creates a chicken‑egg problem: without users, no ad revenue; without ad revenue, storage cannot be funded.

**Kill‑criteria** – If monthly storage cost per user exceeds $0.10, or if privacy compliance cannot be demonstrated within 3 months, the project must be halted.

**Conclusion** – Given the unquantified high operating cost, severe privacy risk, and strong incumbent competition, the proposal cannot be approved as‑is.

DECISION: NO-GO
