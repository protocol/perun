**Steelman** – The proposal asserts that launching two teen‑focused smartphones on a proprietary OS, sold through one carrier with a bundled data plan and no app store, will capture a lucrative social‑media‑savvy market despite rival ecosystems and higher‑than‑typical costs.

**Attack**  
1. **App ecosystem gap** – Teens choose phones for the breadth of apps. A launch without an app store forces reliance on pre‑installed services, which competitors already eclipse. Even a later store will be retro‑fitted, losing early adopters.  
2. **Price sensitivity** – A mandatory data plan priced like a full‑price smartphone plan exceeds typical teen budgets (often <$30/month). This creates a price barrier that rivals already undercut with subsidized plans.  
3. **OS risk** – A brand‑new OS lacks developer tools, security maturity, and OTA support. The company’s existing mobile platform would have reduced time‑to‑market and leveraged existing code‑bases.  
4. **Carrier lock‑in** – Tying the device to a single carrier eliminates price competition and limits distribution, reducing market reach in a fragmented teen market.

**Alternatives**  
- **Do nothing** – Preserve capital and wait for market consolidation.  
- **Partner with an existing OS (e.g., Android)** – Leverage a mature app catalog, reduce development risk, and allow carrier‑agnostic hardware.  
- **Launch a carrier‑agnostic device with a low‑cost, optional data plan and an app store at launch** – Aligns with teen expectations and competitive pricing.

**Fit** – The company’s stated aim to expand into youth social‑media hardware conflicts with the high‑cost, low‑flexibility model. The proposal does not advance the broader strategic goal of scalable, profitable mobile growth.

**Pre‑mortem** – Likely failure modes: <5 % adoption in the first 12 months, negative press over “locked‑in” pricing, developer abandonment, and high support costs for a niche OS.

**Kill criteria** – If early sales (first‑quarter) fall below 10 % of forecast or churn exceeds 30 % after six months, the program should be halted.

**Recommendation** – Given the steep ecosystem, pricing, and lock‑in disadvantages, the proposal should not proceed as written.

DECISION: NO-GO
