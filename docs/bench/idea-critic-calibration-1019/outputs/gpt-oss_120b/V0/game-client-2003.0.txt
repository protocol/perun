**Steelman claim** – Building a mandatory online client for all new titles will guarantee seamless updates, robust anti‑cheat enforcement, and a unified purchase system, later expanding into a marketplace for other developers.

**Skeptic attacks**  
1. **Player acceptance** – In 2003 many gamers value offline play; forcing an online account risks alienating a core segment, potentially cutting sales.  
2. **Retail fallout** – Retailers may drop boxed versions if they must bundle a required online client, shrinking a proven distribution channel.  
3. **Broadband limits** – Large initial downloads assume widespread high‑speed Internet, which was far from universal in 2003, creating a barrier for many customers.  
4. **Early‑stage bugs** – Launching a brand‑new client across all titles invites stability issues that could damage the studio’s reputation.  
5. **Copy risk** – Competitors could quickly replicate the model, eroding any first‑mover advantage.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Optional client**: Offer the client as a convenience rather than a mandate; players can continue using existing offline installers.  
- **Incremental rollout**: Start with a single flagship title, gather telemetry, and refine before scaling.  
- **Partner with emerging digital platforms** (e.g., early Steam‑like services) to handle updates and anti‑cheat without building from scratch.  
- **Server‑side anti‑cheat**: Deploy cheat detection on game servers, reducing client complexity.

**Kill‑criteria** (must be monitored)  
- Adoption < 70 % of the player base within 3 months.  
- Retail shelf‑space loss > 20 % of pre‑launch levels.  
- Crash/bug rate > 5 % in the client after release.  
- Broadband‑ready market share < 50 % in primary regions.

**Pre‑mortem** – The most probable failure mode is a backlash from both consumers (who reject mandatory online accounts) and retailers (who cease stocking boxed editions), leading to revenue loss and brand harm that outweighs the update/anti‑cheat benefits.

**Conclusion** – The proposal’s strategic intent is sound but the mandatory‑online requirement is too risky given the 2003 ecosystem. It should be revised to make the client optional, pilot it on a single title, and include clear stop‑loss metrics before a full rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
