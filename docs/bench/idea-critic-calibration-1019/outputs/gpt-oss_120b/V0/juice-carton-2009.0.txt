**Steelman claim:** Replacing the long‑used orange‑with‑straw carton with a minimalist glass‑and‑cap design, launched nationwide with a big ad push, will refresh the brand without harming sales.

**Skeptic checks**
- **Shelf cue:** The proposal assumes the current image is the primary shelf cue, yet no market‑research data are provided to confirm that shoppers rely on it over other attributes (price, placement, brand name).  
- **Recognition risk:** No baseline awareness metrics are given, so the magnitude of a potential drop in recognisability is unknown.  
- **Cost:** The redesign and nationwide rollout are described as “costly” but no budget or ROI estimate is supplied.

**Better‑way alternatives**
1. **Pilot rollout** in a representative set of regions (e.g., 10 % of stores) to measure sales impact, brand‑recognition scores and cost before a full launch.  
2. **Dual‑packaging**: keep the classic design on the primary SKU while introducing a limited‑edition minimalist pack for promotional use, allowing A/B testing without full commitment.  
3. **Do nothing** until a formal consumer‑insight study validates that the current visual cue is indeed a liability.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**
- If post‑launch sales fall ≥5 % in the first quarter relative to the same period last year, revert to the original packaging within 6 months.  
- If brand‑recognition surveys show a ≥10 % drop in unaided recall, halt further production of the new design.  
- If total redesign cost exceeds the allocated budget (not quantified here), the project must be paused.

**Risk profile**
The change is a two‑way door with high reversal cost (new packaging, distribution, advertising spend). Without data‑driven validation, the risk of alienating a loyal, older buyer base outweighs the speculative brand‑refresh benefit.

**Conclusion:** Proceed with a controlled pilot rather than an immediate nationwide launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
