**Steelman** – The plan seeks to create a recognizable consumer‑facing brand for the chip, using a logo and a co‑op advertising fund that reimburses computer makers when their ads display the logo, so end‑users will begin demanding the chip by name.

**Skeptic assumptions**  
- Consumers will care about an internal component and will ask for it.  
- Computer makers will willingly place the logo and accept the co‑op fund.  
- The extra share of the marketing budget will not starve other critical programs.  

**Concrete attack** – If even a single major computer maker refuses to run the logo, the program loses the broad reach needed to educate buyers, and the remaining spend yields negligible consumer awareness. This scenario is plausible given the unprecedented nature of consumer‑level component advertising.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
1. **Pure B2B promotion** – Continue focusing on technical advantages and volume discounts directly to OEMs; zero consumer‑ad spend, no risk of OEM pushback.  
2. **Certification badge** – Develop a “Certified Chip” mark that OEMs can display voluntarily; costs are limited to badge design and minimal co‑op funds, while still signaling quality to informed buyers.

Both alternatives achieve differentiation at lower cost and with fewer relationship risks.

**Kill‑criteria** (pre‑mortem)  
- If a six‑month post‑launch consumer survey shows <5 % unaided brand recall, halt the program and reallocate funds.  
- If any leading OEM withdraws from the co‑op fund, cease further spending immediately.  

**Remaining risk** – High uncertainty that consumer demand will translate into sales; potential strain on OEM relationships; large budget allocation with unproven ROI.

**Verdict** – The proposal carries disproportionate risk relative to its untested upside and ignores cheaper, lower‑risk paths that could achieve similar differentiation.

DECISION: NO-GO
