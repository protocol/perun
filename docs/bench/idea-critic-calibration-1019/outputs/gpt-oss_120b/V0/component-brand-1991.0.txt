**Steelman** – A consumer‑facing campaign with a distinctive logo and a co‑op advertising fund will raise brand awareness, prompting buyers to request the chip by name, thereby driving higher sales for the chip maker.

**Skeptic attacks**  
1. **Consumer relevance** – In 1991 most buyers cannot differentiate microprocessors; asking for a chip by name assumes a level of technical knowledge they likely lack.  
2. **Maker cooperation** – Computer manufacturers may view the program as interference, risking strained relationships or reduced marketing spend on the chip maker’s behalf.  
3. **Budget impact** – Allocating a “large share” of the marketing budget to a speculative consumer effort could starve other proven channels.  
4. **Unproven ROI** – No data on awareness lift, conversion rates, or incremental revenue are provided.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- Strengthen OEM branding on packaging and product literature (low cost, direct).  
- Run targeted B2B campaigns highlighting performance benchmarks to makers (leverages existing relationships).  
- Pilot the consumer logo in a limited market before a full rollout.

**Kill‑criteria** (to be set before launch)  
- If after 6 months consumer awareness of the logo is <20 % or sales lift <5 %, terminate the program.  
- If any major computer maker opts out of the co‑op fund, halt further funding.  
- If total spend exceeds 15 % of the annual marketing budget without measurable lift, stop.

**Conclusion** – The proposal rests on unverified assumptions and lacks a modest pilot or clear stop‑loss metrics. Approving it as‑is would commit significant resources to an untested consumer‑brand strategy.

DECISION: NO‑GO
