**Steelman:** Acquiring the OS startup gives immediate access to a modern, object‑oriented kernel, bypassing the PC maker’s two failed internal attempts and potentially restoring market relevance. The founder’s return could smooth technical integration and provide strategic guidance.

**Attack & Better‑Way:**  
1. **Cost vs Revenue:** “A few hundred million” for a firm that has sold few units yields a poor price‑to‑sales multiple. Licensing an established OS (or adopting a mature open‑source alternative) would cost a fraction and avoid paying for unproven market traction.  
2. **Migration Risk:** Moving existing applications and developers to a brand‑new core is historically high‑risk; many such transitions lose third‑party software support, eroding the very market share the PC maker hopes to regain.  
3. **Founder History:** A “difficult” founder raises governance and cultural integration concerns that can stall the project for months, nullifying any time‑to‑market advantage.  
4. **Alternative Paths:** Incremental modernization of the current OS (e.g., modular updates) or a licensing partnership could deliver a modern stack with far less integration overhead and financial exposure.

**Kill‑Criteria (must be met to proceed):**  
- Integration must be demonstrably complete within 12 months.  
- Projected incremental revenue over the next five years must exceed acquisition cost by at least 150 %.  
- Founder‑related governance issues must be resolved with a formal advisory charter before closing.

**Pre‑mortem:** If third‑party software refuses to port, the new OS will ship with a barren ecosystem, leading to further market decline. Delays beyond 12 months would waste the acquisition premium and damage brand credibility.

**Conclusion:** Given the high acquisition price, unproven product sales, significant migration risk, and a viable lower‑cost licensing alternative, the proposal does not satisfy the essential kill‑criteria. The executive team should pursue a licensing or incremental modernization route instead.

DECISION: NO-GO
