**Steelman:** Launch a new handheld featuring two screens—one touch‑sensitive—priced below the rival’s high‑end device, while continuing sales of the existing model. The goal is to expand the market beyond core gamers by offering a novel form factor that differentiates the brand without matching the rival’s graphics power.

**Attack:**  
- **Developer uncertainty:** A dual‑screen, touch interface diverges from the established SDK, likely raising learning curves and fragmenting tooling, which could deter third‑party titles.  
- **Cost pressure:** Adding a second display and touch layer raises bill‑of‑materials; achieving a lower price than the rival may compress margins or require aggressive cost cuts elsewhere.  
- **Graphics gap:** The rival’s superior GPU will still attract performance‑focused gamers, leaving the new device appealing only to a niche seeking novelty.  
- **Unproven touch market:** In 2004, touch‑screen gaming lacks a track record; consumer demand is uncertain, increasing the risk of low adoption.  
- **Gimmick perception:** Media may label the design a gimmick, harming brand prestige and making retailers hesitant.

**Better‑way alternatives:**  
1. Incrementally upgrade the current handheld’s processor and screen to improve graphics while keeping the proven form factor, preserving developer continuity.  
2. Release a modest accessory (e.g., detachable stylus) to test touch interaction without redesigning hardware.  
3. Delay the dual‑screen launch until touch‑screen adoption is demonstrated in other consumer segments, reducing market risk.

**Strategic fit:** Expanding beyond core gamers aligns with growth objectives, but the approach conflicts with the company’s reputation for polished, developer‑friendly platforms.

**Pre‑mortem:** If developer support stays below 20 % in six months, or if the unit cost exceeds the budget by >10 %, the product will likely be a commercial flop, eroding brand equity and incurring inventory loss.

**Kill criteria:** Abort if (a) fewer than 15 % of existing licensed developers commit to the new SDK within 3 months, (b) projected margin falls below 8 %, or (c) press coverage predominantly brands the device a gimmick.

Given the high development risk, unclear consumer demand, and potential margin squeeze, the proposal should not proceed without further validation.

**DECISION: NO-GO**
