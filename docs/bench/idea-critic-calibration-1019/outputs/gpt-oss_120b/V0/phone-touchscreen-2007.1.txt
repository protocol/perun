verdict: REVISE
independence: inline
origin: agent-originated
claim: Launch a premium touchscreen phone with no physical keyboard, integrated web, email, and music player, sold exclusively through one carrier for a share of monthly service revenue.
steelman: The company can leverage its strong brand and music ecosystem to enter the phone market, differentiate with an integrated music experience, secure a recurring revenue stream, and establish a premium device that showcases its design and software capabilities.
hats_run: skeptic, better-way, kill-criteria
assumptions: 
- Consumers will pay a premium for a phone lacking a physical keyboard. 
- A single carrier will agree to exclusive hardware/software control and revenue sharing. 
- The company can develop competitive phone hardware/software without prior phone‑industry experience. 
- Integrated music player alone will offset potential loss of standalone player sales.
better_ways: 
- Partner with an existing smartphone platform to obtain a third‑party app ecosystem. 
- Use a non‑exclusive multi‑carrier strategy to broaden market reach. 
- Offer a lower‑priced entry model or include an optional physical keyboard. 
- License the music player to existing phone manufacturers instead of building a full handset.
kill_criteria: 
- Carrier fails to sign a binding revenue‑share and exclusivity agreement. 
- First‑year unit sales fall below 100 000 units or <2 % market share. 
- Development costs exceed $500 M without a clear path to profitability within 3 years. 
- A competing device with comparable features launches within 6 months and captures the premium segment.
strongest_attack_survived: Premium pricing without a keyboard in a market dominated by keyboard phones would likely miss the 100 k unit sales target; this scenario, based on 2007 market data, remains plausible and was not disproven by the proposal.
questions_parent_must_resolve: 
- What are the exact revenue‑share terms and minimum sales guarantees from the carrier? 
- How will the company mitigate the lack of third‑party apps at launch? 
- What is the detailed cost breakdown and projected ROI for the first three years?
user_question: NONE
dissent_ledger: The proposal carries high risk due to no phone‑industry experience, an exclusive carrier model that limits reach, a premium price point unsupported by market preferences for keyboards, and the absence of an app ecosystem—all of which could lead to poor sales, brand damage, and sunk development costs.
remaining_risk: High – market acceptance, carrier dependence, and ecosystem gaps.

DECISION: NO-GO
