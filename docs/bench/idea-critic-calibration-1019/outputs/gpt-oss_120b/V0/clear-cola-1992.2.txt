**Steelman claim** – A nationwide launch of a clear‑cola “purer” variant, supported by a large marketing push, will capture new customers, revive growth, and become a major brand extension for the second‑largest cola maker.

**Skeptic attacks**  
1. **Consumer perception** – If shoppers equate “cola” with the traditional dark color, a clear product may be rejected as “not a real cola,” limiting repeat purchases.  
2. **Test‑market bias** – Positive test‑market response often inflates demand because of limited geographic exposure, novelty hype, and heightened media attention.  
3. **Cannibalization** – Existing flagship sales could be siphoned off, eroding overall margin if the clear cola merely replaces, not adds, volume.  
4. **Competitive response** – Rivals can launch similar “clear” variants within months, eroding early‑mover advantage.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Phased rollout**: Begin with a regional launch and modest spend, using real‑world sales data to validate demand before national scaling.  
- **Co‑brand or sub‑brand**: Position the clear cola under a distinct brand umbrella to avoid confusion with the flagship and reduce cannibalization risk.  
- **Limited‑edition trial**: Offer a time‑bound release to gauge repeat purchase intent without long‑term commitment.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**  
- First‑quarter sales < 60 % of forecasted volume.  
- Cannibalization > 15 % of flagship’s net sales.  
- A rival launches a comparable clear cola within 6 months, capturing > 5 % market share.

**Remaining risk** – High uncertainty around consumer acceptance and the magnitude of cannibalization; test‑market data may not translate nationally.

Given the lack of concrete, nationwide demand evidence and the sizable risk of brand dilution, the proposal should not be approved as‑is. A more incremental approach is required to validate assumptions before committing large marketing spend.

DECISION: NO-GO
