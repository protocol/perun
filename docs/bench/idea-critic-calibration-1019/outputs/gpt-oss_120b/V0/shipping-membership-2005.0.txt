**Steelman:** Offer an annual “Prime‑like” membership for $79 that removes per‑order shipping fees and adds discounted one‑day shipping, aiming to cut friction, boost repeat purchases, and lock in upfront revenue.

**Attack & Risks:**  
- **Cost overrun:** Heavy users could generate shipping expenses that exceed the $79 fee, eroding margin.  
- **Margin hit:** Immediate loss of per‑order shipping profit may strain cash flow.  
- **Irreversibility:** Once sold, memberships are hard to unwind without refunds or churn.  
- **Unproven economics:** No data on how many customers will adopt or how much order frequency will rise.

**Better‑Way Alternatives (including “do nothing”):**  
1. **Do nothing** – retain per‑order fees; no upfront risk but friction remains.  
2. **Lower‑price tier** – $49 membership with free two‑day shipping; reduces barrier and risk.  
3. **Spend‑threshold free shipping** – free two‑day shipping on orders > $50; only rewards larger baskets and avoids locking customers into a fee.  
All three cost less upfront and allow testing of price elasticity.

**Fit with Strategy:** The goal to increase repeat purchases aligns, but the $79 price point may be high for a 2005 market where comparable programs were rare. A pilot or lower price better matches a cautious growth strategy.

**Pre‑mortem (worst‑case):** Shipping cost per member exceeds $79 within 6 months, causing a net loss; customers feel trapped and churn after a year, damaging brand perception.

**Kill‑Criteria (to stop the program):**  
- If after 6 months total shipping cost for members > 1.5 × membership revenue, or  
- If net profit from members is negative for two consecutive quarters, terminate the program.

**Conclusion:** The proposal carries significant upside but also high, unquantified downside. Without pilot data or a lower‑risk entry point, approving the $79 membership as written is premature.

DECISION: NO-GO
