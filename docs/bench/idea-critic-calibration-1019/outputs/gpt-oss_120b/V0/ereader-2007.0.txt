**Steelman claim:** Design and sell a proprietary e‑reader with a paper‑like display and free cellular connectivity, enabling customers to buy and download books directly from the company’s catalog at a low per‑title price.

**Skeptic checks**
- **Hardware inexperience:** The firm has never built consumer hardware; development, supply‑chain, QA, and support costs are unknown and likely high.
- **Margin squeeze:** Low e‑book prices will reduce per‑title profit; without a proven pricing model, margins could fall below sustainable levels.
- **Publisher resistance:** Publishers already fear piracy; a low‑price, always‑online device may intensify pushback, risking loss of titles.
- **Cannibalization risk:** Easy digital access may accelerate decline of higher‑margin print sales, especially for a catalog‑heavy retailer.
- **Market precedent:** Earlier dedicated e‑readers sold poorly, indicating weak consumer demand for standalone devices.

**Better‑way alternatives**
1. **Partner with an existing e‑reader maker** (e.g., integrate store app on a proven device) – leverages established hardware, reduces R&D cost, and mitigates supply‑chain risk.
2. **Develop a mobile app** for smartphones/tablets – captures the growing smartphone market, avoids hardware investment, and offers the same low‑price model.
3. **Do nothing (focus on print & existing online sales)** – preserves current profit structure while monitoring market evolution.

All alternatives are cheaper, faster to market, and reversible; they also keep the company within its core competencies.

**Fit with strategy**
The proposal diverges from the company’s strength (catalog & retail) into hardware, a non‑core area, without clear strategic benefit beyond a modest differentiation claim.

**Pre‑mortem / kill‑criteria**
- **Cost overrun:** If hardware R&D exceeds $30 M (a plausible ceiling for a first‑generation device), abort.
- **Margin breach:** If projected gross margin on e‑books falls below 20 % after device cost amortization, cancel.
- **Publisher boycott:** If ≥ 2 major publishers refuse participation, halt launch.
- **Sales target miss:** If first‑year units sold < 50,000, discontinue.

**Evidence** (as given): prior e‑readers sold poorly; publishers fear piracy; company lacks hardware experience; risk of cannibalizing print sales.

**Decision:** Given high uncertainty, steep upfront cost, and viable lower‑risk alternatives, the proposal should not be approved as written.

DECISION: NO-GO
