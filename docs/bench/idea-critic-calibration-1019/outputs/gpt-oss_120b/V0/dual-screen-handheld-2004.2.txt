**Steelman claim**  
Launch a dual‑screen handheld (one touch‑screen) at a lower price than the rival, keep the existing device on sale, and broaden the audience beyond core gamers.

**Key assumptions**  
1. Development tools and SDKs can be produced quickly enough for third‑party studios to adopt the new form‑factor.  
2. Hardware cost can be kept low enough to under‑price the rival despite the extra screen and touch layer.  
3. Consumers will value a touch‑screen and dual display enough to buy a “gimmick” device.  
4. Maintaining the older handheld will not cannibalize sales of the new unit.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- Upgrade the current handheld’s graphics and add optional accessories (e.g., attachable touch strip).  
- Release a single‑screen, higher‑performance model that directly competes on graphics.  
- Focus on exclusive software titles for the existing platform to retain market share.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**  
- Developer support < 5 % of total game releases in the first 12 months → abort.  
- Manufacturing cost > 15 % above target price → abort.  
- First‑year sales < 70 % of forecast → abort.  
- Negative press labeling the device a “gimmick” leading to > 10 % market‑share loss vs. rival → abort.

**Concrete failure scenario**  
If SDK rollout is delayed 6 months, only 3 % of third‑party titles ship with dual‑screen/touch support, the library is thin, and the device sells 40 % below forecast, triggering the sales‑kill criterion. This scenario is plausible given no evidence that developers are ready for a new hardware paradigm.

**Remaining risk**  
Unverified cost structure and developer adoption; market appetite for touch‑screen gaming is unproven.

**Recommendation**  
Do not green‑light the launch as proposed. Conduct detailed cost modeling, prototype developer kits, and a limited market study before proceeding.

DECISION: NO-GO
