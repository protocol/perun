**Steelman claim**  
The company will design, manufacture, and sell a low‑cost e‑reader with a paper‑like display and free cellular connectivity, enabling customers to browse and download books directly from the company’s store at a single low price per title, thus expanding the digital market and increasing customer lock‑in.

**Attack on the steelman**  

1. **Hardware expertise gap** – The firm has never built consumer hardware; tooling, supply‑chain, quality control, and after‑sales support are all new, raising unit‑cost and time‑to‑market risk.  
2. **Margin compression** – Offering a “single low price per title” will likely force ebook prices below publisher‑desired levels, reducing gross margin and risking publisher pull‑back.  
3. **Market precedent** – Earlier dedicated e‑readers (e.g., early Kindle, Sony Reader) sold poorly in 2007; consumer adoption was limited, especially for a new entrant without brand recognition in hardware.  
4. **Cannibalisation** – A cheap e‑reader could accelerate the shift from print to digital, eroding the higher‑margin print business the company currently relies on.

**Better‑way alternatives**  

- **Partner with an established e‑reader maker** (e.g., Amazon, Sony) to bundle the store app, leveraging existing hardware expertise and distribution.  
- **License the e‑reading technology** to third‑party manufacturers, earning royalties without capital‑intensive production.  
- **Focus on web‑ and app‑based ebook sales** (mobile, desktop) where the company already has competence.

**Fit with strategy** – Core competency is bookselling and content curation, not hardware design. Diverting resources to a hardware program misaligns with the company’s strategic focus.

**Pre‑mortem & kill criteria**  

- If first‑year unit sales < 10 000, abort.  
- If average ebook margin falls below 5 % or > 20 % of titles are withdrawn by publishers, cease the program.  
- If print sales decline > 10 % attributable to the e‑reader, shut down.

**Dissent ledger** – High hardware risk, margin squeeze, publisher backlash, and potential print cannibalisation outweigh projected digital growth.

**Decision** – The proposal should not be approved in its current form.  

DECISION: NO-GO
