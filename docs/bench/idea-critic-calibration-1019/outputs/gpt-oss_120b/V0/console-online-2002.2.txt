**Steelman**: A console‑wide paid online service (single identity, friends list, voice chat, matchmaking) launched in 2002 would give the company a differentiated, revenue‑generating platform, positioning it ahead of rivals that leave online play free or fragmented.

**Skeptic attacks**

1. **Broadband availability** – In 2002 only ~5‑7 % of U.S. households had broadband; most users rely on dial‑up, which cannot support low‑latency voice or matchmaking.  
2. **Price sensitivity** – An annual subscription (≈$50) on top of a $299 console adds ~17 % cost, likely deterring price‑conscious early adopters.  
3. **Operational readiness** – The company has no experience running large‑scale hosting; building data‑centers, support staff, and SLAs would require substantial upfront CAPEX and time.  
4. **Player expectations** – Early‑Internet gamers expect free online play; a mandatory fee may push them to rival consoles or PC platforms.

**Better‑way alternatives**

- **Phase‑in a free tier** with optional premium features (voice, matchmaking) to test demand while avoiding a hard barrier.  
- **Partner with an existing ISP or online service** to leverage their infrastructure, reducing CAPEX.  
- **Delay launch** until broadband penetration reaches ≥15 % (≈2004), then introduce the service with a proven user base.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**  

- **Broadband penetration <10 %** in target markets at launch → abort.  
- **Projected subscription uptake <20 %** of console owners → abort.  
- **Initial operational cost >$30 M** without clear breakeven within 2 years → abort.

**Remaining risk**: Even if these thresholds are met, the company must rapidly acquire hosting expertise and support staff, a non‑trivial transformation that could strain resources and distract from core console business.

Given the low broadband penetration, high price sensitivity, and lack of operational capability, the proposal fails multiple kill‑criteria and has stronger, lower‑risk alternatives.

**Decision**: The service should not be launched as proposed now.

DECISION: NO-GO
