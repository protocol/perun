**Steelman** – The company would create a four‑engine, ultra‑large wide‑body with a raised cockpit for future freighter conversion, built in a new factory and powered by a high‑bypass engine from a partner. The airline expects lower cost‑per‑seat as demand surges, and the aircraft would lock the airline into a long‑term fleet.

**Attack & Risks**  
1. **Capital exposure** – The development and factory outlay dwarf any prior project; historical analogues (e.g., the 747) required massive financing and suffered severe cost overruns.  
2. **Engine uncertainty** – The high‑bypass engine has no flight history; failure would delay certification and inflate costs.  
3. **Market uncertainty** – Only one airline has committed. The era’s hype around supersonic transport threatens sub‑sonic demand, making a single‑customer program fragile.  
4. **Technology lock‑in** – A four‑engine design may become obsolete if twin‑engine efficiencies improve (as later happened).

**Better‑Way Alternatives**  
- **Do nothing** – Continue scaling existing models (stretch variants) with proven engines, preserving cash and capacity.  
- **Incremental upgrade** – Develop a twin‑engine wide‑body with modest stretch, leveraging existing factories and engine families, reducing risk and capital.  
- **Partner program** – Share development cost with multiple airlines or a competitor, securing a broader order book before committing.

**Fit with Strategy** – The company’s strength lies in incremental innovation and leveraging existing production lines. A single‑customer, high‑risk program diverges from the proven path and threatens financial stability.

**Pre‑mortem** – If the engine fails or the airline cancels, the factory becomes a sunk cost, the company faces debt, and competitors could capture the market with lower‑risk offerings. The project is a two‑way door only with a massive capital barrier; exit would be costly.

**Kill‑Criteria** – Abort if: (a) additional airline orders < 2 within 12 months; (b) engine certification slips beyond 24 months; (c) projected cost‑overrun exceeds 30 % of budget.

Given the single‑order base, unproven engine, and massive financial exposure, the proposal should not be approved until broader market commitment and engine validation are secured.  

DECISION: NO-GO
