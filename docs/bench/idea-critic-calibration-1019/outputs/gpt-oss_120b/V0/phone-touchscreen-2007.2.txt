**Steel‑man claim** – The company can break into the handset market by launching a premium, touchscreen‑only phone that integrates its best‑selling music player, a full web browser and email, sold exclusively through a carrier that lets the company control hardware/software and shares monthly service revenue.

**Skeptic checks**

1. **No phone‑industry experience** – Designing a reliable RF front‑end, antenna tuning, battery management and carrier certification is a deep engineering domain. Mistakes here can delay launch or cause costly recalls.
2. **Premium price with no physical keyboard** – In 2007 the market still values tactile keyboards for texting; early touchscreen phones (e.g., iPhone) were priced > $600 but required a strong brand and ecosystem. A premium price without a proven handset pedigree risks low uptake.
3. **Carrier‑only exclusivity** – Limiting sales to a single carrier caps early volume, reduces brand visibility, and makes the product vulnerable to the carrier’s own strategic shifts.
4. **Zero third‑party apps** – The nascent app ecosystem is a key differentiator; lack of apps reduces perceived value versus rivals that already support Java/OS‑level apps.
5. **Revenue‑share model** – The carrier must be convinced to give up a portion of its ARPU to a newcomer; without clear upside, negotiations may fail or yield an unfavorable split.

**Better‑way alternatives**

- **Partner with an established OEM** (e.g., use its chassis and RF expertise) while providing the music‑player UI as a software layer. This reduces hardware risk and leverages existing supply chains.
- **Launch a lower‑priced, carrier‑agnostic device** with a physical keyboard initially, then add a touchscreen variant once brand trust is earned.
- **Adopt an open app platform** (e.g., Java ME) from day 1 to attract developers and increase stickiness.

**Kill‑criteria / pre‑mortem**

- If carrier negotiations do not secure ≥ 15 % revenue share and exclusive rights for ≥ 2 years → abort.
- If prototype hardware fails carrier certification within 6 months → abort.
- If initial sales forecast (based on market data) is < 50 % of projected premium‑price volume → abort.

**Remaining risk**

Even if carrier terms are met, the product may suffer from poor app support and limited market reach, making it hard to achieve profitability before the exclusivity period ends.

**Conclusion** – The proposal’s core assumptions (experience, pricing, exclusivity, app ecosystem) are not sufficiently mitigated. A safer path is to partner with an experienced handset maker and launch a more modest, carrier‑agnostic device first.  

DECISION: NO-GO
