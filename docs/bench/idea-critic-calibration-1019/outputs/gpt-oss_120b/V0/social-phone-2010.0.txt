claim: Launch two teen‑focused phones on a new in‑house OS, sold through a single carrier with a mandatory monthly data plan and no application store at launch.
steelman: The company will acquire a popular messaging‑phone maker and, within two years of sunk development, release two smartphones targeting teenage social‑network users, using a proprietary OS, exclusive carrier distribution, and a bundled data plan priced like a full‑feature smartphone plan, despite lacking an app marketplace at launch.
hats_run: skeptic, better-way, kill-criteria
assumptions:
  - Teens prioritize a rich app ecosystem over hardware novelty.
  - A data plan costing as much as a full smartphone plan is affordable for the target demographic.
  - A single‑carrier exclusive launch will not significantly limit market reach.
better_ways:
  - Adopt an existing OS (e.g., Android) to leverage its app catalog and developer community.
  - Partner with multiple carriers and offer tiered, teen‑friendly data plans.
  - Include an app store at launch, even a curated subset, to meet user expectations.
kill_criteria:
  - Projected first‑year adoption < 5% of target teen market.
  - Data‑plan price > $30/month (average teen budget) without subsidy.
  - Failure to secure at least 10 third‑party apps within 6 months of launch.
strongest_attack_survived: None – the scenario “teen users reject the phone due to lack of apps and high data cost” remains a viable failure mode; the proposal does not mitigate it.
questions_parent_must_resolve: NONE
user_question: NONE
dissent_ledger: The plan ignores critical teen purchase drivers—app availability and affordable data—making market acceptance unlikely; carrier exclusivity further restricts reach.
remaining_risk: High market rejection risk if app ecosystem and cost barriers are not addressed.
