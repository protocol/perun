**Steelman:** The company will develop a four‑engine, ultra‑large, high‑bypass jet with a raised cockpit, building a new factory and securing a novel engine, to halve seat‑cost for its biggest airline customer and position itself as the sole supplier of a new market‑segment aircraft.

**Attack:**  
1. **Capital exposure:** The required factory and tooling investment exceeds any prior program by >200 %; with only one launch customer, breakeven relies on securing multiple follow‑on orders that are not guaranteed.  
2. **Engine risk:** The high‑bypass engine has not completed a full flight‑test cycle; past programs show a 30‑40 % chance of schedule slip and cost overrun for unproven powerplants.  
3. **Market shift:** Forecasts from 1966 already anticipate supersonic transports gaining market share; betting on a sub‑sonic behemoth may leave the product obsolete before recouping costs.  
4. **Single‑customer lock‑in:** Relying on one airline’s order creates a “one‑off” program; if the airline renegotiates or cancels, the sunk cost becomes unrecoverable.

**Better ways:**  
- **Do nothing** – continue scaling existing models (stretch‑versions, higher‑density cabins) with modest upgrades, preserving cash.  
- **Incremental growth** – develop a slightly larger twin‑engine wide‑body (e.g., 300‑seat) using existing factories and proven engines, allowing the airline to test cost‑per‑seat improvements before committing to a super‑jumbo.  
- **Joint venture** – partner with another OEM to share development risk and factory costs, reducing exposure.

**Fit with strategy:** The firm’s core advantage is reliable, high‑volume sub‑sonic jets; a leap to a super‑jumbo strains that advantage and conflicts with a risk‑averse, cash‑flow‑driven strategy.

**Pre‑mortem / kill criteria:** Abort if (a) total program cost exceeds $5 B, (b) engine certification slips beyond 24 months, or (c) no additional airline orders secured within 18 months of launch.

**Dissent ledger:** Capital, engine, market, and customer concentration risks are unmitigated; alternatives offer lower exposure and preserve strategic focus.

**Remaining risk:** Even with mitigations, the program could still overrun cost by >30 % and become a stranded asset if supersonic traffic dominates.

DECISION: NO-GO
