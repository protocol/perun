**Steelman** – Introduce a $79 annual membership granting free two‑day shipping on all eligible orders and discounted one‑day rates, aiming to eliminate the per‑order shipping fee that deters repeat purchases.

**Attack & Risks**  
1. **Cost overruns** – Heavy buyers could dramatically raise the retailer’s shipping expense, potentially exceeding the $79 fee.  
2. **Margin hit** – Free two‑day shipping eliminates a high‑margin revenue stream, creating a short‑term profit dip.  
3. **Irreversibility** – Once customers acquire memberships, the program is hard to unwind without damaging brand trust.  
4. **Unproven economics** – No internal data confirm that incremental spend from members will offset shipping costs.

**Better‑Way Alternatives**  
- Retain per‑order fees but offer a modest discount on shipping for repeat customers.  
- Launch a lower‑priced tier (e.g., $49) with free standard shipping, testing price elasticity.  
- Implement a free‑shipping threshold (e.g., orders > $50) to preserve revenue from low‑value shipments.

**Fit with Strategy** – The goal is to boost repeat purchase frequency; free fast shipping is a proven lever, but the proposal lacks a clear path to validate that lift outweighs cost.

**Pre‑mortem** – If heavy buyers double their order volume, shipping cost could rise > $120 per member, turning the program into a net loss and eroding overall margin. The inability to roll back would lock in this loss.

**Kill‑Criteria (proposed)**  
- Abort if average shipping cost per member exceeds $50 within the first 12 months.  
- Abort if member‑driven revenue uplift is < 20 % of the cost baseline after one year.  
- Monitor churn; if > 30 % of members cancel after 6 months, discontinue.

**Conclusion** – The concept has strategic merit but requires rigorous financial modeling, a pilot, and explicit kill‑criteria before full rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
