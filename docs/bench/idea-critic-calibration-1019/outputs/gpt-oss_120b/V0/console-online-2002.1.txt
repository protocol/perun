**Steelman:** Launch a universal, paid online service in 2002, offering a single player identity, friends list, voice chat and matchmaking for every game, requiring broadband and an annual subscription.

**Attack & Alternatives**  
1. **Do nothing / free per‑game online:** Keeps cost to consumers zero, leverages existing publisher models, avoids operational launch risk.  
2. **Hybrid model:** Free basic matchmaking, optional paid premium features (voice, friends list). Lowers barrier while testing demand.  
3. **Delay launch until broadband penetration rises (e.g., 2004).** Allows time to build hosting infrastructure and learn from competitors.

**Fit with Strategy** – The console needs a differentiator, but a mandatory paid service risks alienating early adopters and contradicts the “plug‑and‑play” ethos of a new hardware platform. A free or hybrid offering aligns better with building a user base first.

**Pre‑mortem** – If subscriber uptake is <5 % of console owners in the first year, the service will lose money, damage brand perception, and force a costly rollback. Operational inexperience could cause outages, leading to negative press.

**Kill‑criteria (proposed)** – Abort if:  
- <5 % of installed consoles subscribe within 12 months, **or**  
- Hosting costs exceed $2 M in the first year, **or**  
- Broadband‑capable households are <15 % of the target market (estimated from 2002 data).

**Evidence & Assumptions** – Assumes 2002 broadband penetration in the primary market is ~10 % (industry reports of the era). Players are accustomed to free online play on competing platforms. The company has no prior hosting experience, implying a steep learning curve and staffing costs.

**Conclusion** – The high subscription barrier and required broadband dramatically limit the addressable market. A phased or free approach would capture users first, then monetize later when infrastructure and broadband adoption improve. Launching the full paid service now fails the kill‑criteria risk test.

DECISION: NO-GO
