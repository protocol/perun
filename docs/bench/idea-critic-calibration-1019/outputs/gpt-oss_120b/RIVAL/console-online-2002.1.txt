**Executive Summary**  
The proposal seeks to monetize online multiplayer across the entire console ecosystem with a single subscription service that includes a unified player identity, friends list, voice chat, and matchmaking. While the vision aligns with long‑term trends toward connected gaming, the current market conditions in 2002 make a full‑scale paid rollout premature.

**Market Realities**  
- **Broadband Penetration:** In 2002, broadband adoption in North America and Europe is still below 30 % of households, with many users relying on dial‑up. A service that requires broadband will be inaccessible to the majority of the console’s existing install base.  
- **Consumer Expectations:** Competitors are either offering free online play or leaving the decision to individual publishers. Players have been conditioned to expect no extra charge for basic multiplayer, especially on a platform that already carries a high hardware cost.

**Financial & Operational Risks**  
- **Subscription Barrier:** An annual fee adds a recurring cost on top of the console price, likely reducing the service’s uptake and fragmenting the player community. Low adoption would diminish the network effects essential for matchmaking and voice chat.  
- **Infrastructure Burden:** The company has no experience operating large‑scale, always‑on servers. Building, staffing, and maintaining a reliable backend would require significant capital expenditure and a new operational skill set, increasing the risk of service outages that could damage the brand.

**Strategic Alternatives**  
1. **Free Tier with Optional Premium Features:** Launch a free, basic online service (matchmaking and friends list) to grow the user base, then introduce optional paid enhancements (voice chat, advanced matchmaking) later when broadband is more ubiquitous.  
2. **Publisher‑Driven Monetization:** Allow publishers to offer paid online experiences within their games, reducing the company’s exposure while still generating revenue through revenue‑share agreements.  
3. **Phased Geographic Rollout:** Begin in markets with higher broadband penetration (e.g., South Korea, Japan) and use those regions as a proving ground before a global launch.

**Recommendation**  
Given the limited broadband availability, consumer price sensitivity, and the company’s lack of operational expertise, a full‑price, console‑wide subscription service would likely see low adoption and generate negative brand impact. Adopt a free‑first approach with optional premium add‑ons, and defer a mandatory subscription until broadband penetration reaches a critical mass (≈50 %).  

**Conclusion**  
Approve a revised plan that starts with a free core service and incremental paid features, rather than the proposed all‑paid model.

DECISION: NO-GO
