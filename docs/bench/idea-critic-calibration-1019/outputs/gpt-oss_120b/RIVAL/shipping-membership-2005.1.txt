The proposal tackles a clear pain point—shipping fees that deter repeat purchases—by offering an annual membership that removes the most common barrier (two‑day free shipping) and adds a modest discount on faster service. The $79 price point is modest enough to be attractive to a broad base, yet high enough to offset some cost if uptake is limited.

However, the risks identified are material and unmitigated:

1. **Cost‑Revenue Mismatch** – Heavy shoppers could shift from occasional to frequent orders, dramatically increasing the retailer’s outbound shipping volume. Without clear data on average order frequency and the proportion of “heavy” buyers, the membership could become a loss‑leader, eroding margins rather than driving them.

2. **Short‑Term Margin Hit** – The upfront $79 fee is a one‑time cash inflow, but the ongoing shipping subsidy is continuous. The retailer will absorb a sizable margin reduction each month for members, which may strain cash flow, especially if the membership uptake is high early on.

3. **Irreversibility** – Once members are enrolled, they will expect the benefit for the full year. Cancelling or altering the program later would damage brand trust and could trigger churn or legal exposure.

4. **Unproven Economics** – No financial model, pilot data, or sensitivity analysis is supplied. The proposal assumes that the membership fee will cover the incremental shipping cost, but this is speculative.

Given these gaps, the prudent path is to **validate the economics before full rollout**. A limited pilot (e.g., a single market or a subset of customers) with rigorous tracking of order frequency, shipping cost per member, and churn would provide the missing evidence. Until such data exist, committing to a full‑scale launch carries a high risk of negative margin impact and brand complications.

**Recommendation:** Hold the decision pending a data‑driven pilot and a detailed cost‑benefit model.
