**Strategic fit** – The company already dominates the sub‑sonic jet market, but the proposal jumps to a dramatically larger, four‑engine wide‑body that would require a brand‑new factory and a high‑bypass engine that does not yet exist. This represents a shift from incremental improvement to a capital‑intensive, technology‑driven program.

**Financial exposure** – The development outlay and factory construction are “far larger than any the company has made.” With only a single airline order, the revenue base cannot amortize the multi‑billion‑dollar investment. Cash‑flow risk and potential debt burden are high, especially given the historic volatility of aerospace programs.

**Technical risk** – The new high‑bypass engine is unproven. Engine development historically incurs schedule slips and cost overruns; coupling it with a novel airframe multiplies integration risk. The raised cockpit for freighter conversion adds structural complexity without immediate payoff.

**Market risk** – Two opposing forces undermine the case:
1. **Supersonic expectations** – Industry forecasts anticipate supersonic airliners to replace sub‑sonic jets, which could erode demand for a larger, slower aircraft before it reaches service.
2. **Limited demand** – Only one airline has committed. A successful program typically needs multiple launch customers to justify the scale.

**Opportunity cost** – Resources diverted to this program would delay or crowd out incremental upgrades to existing models, which could deliver cost‑per‑seat reductions with far lower risk and capital.

**Recommendation** – Defer the full four‑engine, new‑factory program until:
- Additional launch customers are secured.
- The high‑bypass engine reaches a mature, certified state.
- Market analysis confirms sustained demand for large sub‑sonic airliners despite supersonic developments.

In the meantime, pursue a modest stretch‑version of the current jet or a twin‑engine wide‑body that leverages existing tooling and engines, preserving cash flow and technological continuity.

**Conclusion** – The proposal’s risk profile outweighs its potential benefits under present conditions.

DECISION: NO-GO
