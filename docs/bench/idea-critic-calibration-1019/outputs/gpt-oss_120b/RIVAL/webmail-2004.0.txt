**Proposal Summary**  
Launch a free web‑mail service (2004 context) offering 1 GB storage per user, fast full‑text search, and a responsive web UI. Funding would come from text ads matched to the content of users’ messages. The service would start invitation‑only.

**Key Benefits**  
* Differentiation: 1 GB dwarfs the few‑megabyte limits of existing free mail at the time, potentially attracting power users.  
* Search advantage: Fast indexing could be a compelling feature for heavy mail users.  
* Early‑adopter buzz: Invitation‑only rollout can generate exclusivity and word‑of‑mouth.

**Risks & Constraints**  

| Risk | Impact (2004) | Mitigation Needed |
|------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Storage & server cost** | 1 GB per user means ~1000× the storage of competitors; hardware, power, and data‑center costs would be massive, especially before cheap cloud storage. | Aggressive cost modeling, tiered rollout, or limiting active users. |
| **Privacy & ad‑matching** | Scanning personal mail to serve ads raises strong privacy objections and potential legal exposure (e.g., EU data‑protection norms, US wire‑tap statutes). | Transparent opt‑out, on‑device ad matching, or abandoning content‑based ads. |
| **Mature market** | Entrenched players (Gmail not yet launched, Hotmail, Yahoo! Mail) already have large user bases and network effects. | Clear go‑to‑market differentiation beyond storage, e.g., security or integration. |
| **No direct revenue at launch** | Reliance on ad impressions before any paying user base creates cash‑flow risk. | Early monetization pilots, premium add‑ons, or phased ad rollout. |

**Strategic Fit**  
The proposal aims for a high‑profile launch but hinges on two unproven pillars: massive free storage and content‑based advertising. Both create cost and reputational exposure that outweigh the modest competitive advantage of larger mailboxes. In 2004, storage prices were still high (≈ $1 per GB on commodity disks) and scaling to millions of users would require capital beyond typical startup budgets. Privacy concerns would likely deter adoption and could attract regulatory scrutiny.

**Recommendation**  
Delay approval. Refine the business model to either (a) reduce storage to a more sustainable tier (e.g., 200 MB) and/or (b) move ad matching off‑line or to non‑content‑based placements. Conduct a detailed cost‑per‑user analysis and a privacy‑impact assessment before proceeding.

**Decision**  
DECISION: NO-GO
