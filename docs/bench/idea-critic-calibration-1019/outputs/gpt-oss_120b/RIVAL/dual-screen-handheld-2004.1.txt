The proposal targets a clear market gap: the rival’s handheld out‑shines our graphics and adds media features, but its higher price and single‑screen design limit appeal to casual users. By introducing a dual‑screen device—one touch‑sensitive—we create a distinctive user experience that can be marketed as “play anywhere, play differently,” which should attract families, younger gamers, and non‑core players who value novelty and price over raw graphics.

Key strengths:
* **Differentiation:** Dual screens and touch input are not direct hardware copies, avoiding a head‑to‑head graphics race we cannot win.
* **Price positioning:** A lower‑cost device undercuts the rival, making the platform accessible to a broader audience while preserving revenue from the existing handheld.
* **Risk mitigation:** Keeping the older handheld on sale maintains a familiar entry point for developers and existing fans, reducing the shock of a sudden platform shift.

Key risks and mitigations:
* **Developer confusion:** Provide a unified SDK that abstracts the dual‑screen layout and touch input, offering sample code and clear guidelines. Early dev‑kits and a “touch‑first” contest can seed the ecosystem.
* **Weaker graphics:** Emphasize gameplay innovation over visual fidelity; focus on titles that leverage the dual‑screen mechanic (puzzles, RPG maps, interactive books) where graphics are secondary.
* **Touch‑screen unproven:** Run a limited beta with third‑party studios to validate demand and refine the UI paradigm before full launch.
* **Cost of extra screen:** Bulk‑order LCD panels and reuse existing chassis components to keep the bill of materials modest.

Overall, the plan balances bold differentiation with pragmatic safeguards, positioning the company to expand its audience without abandoning its core base.

DECISION: GO
