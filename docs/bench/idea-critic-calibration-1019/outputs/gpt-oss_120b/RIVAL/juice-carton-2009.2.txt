The proposal offers a bold visual refresh while preserving product taste and price, aiming for a nationwide launch backed by a large advertising push. The current orange‑with‑straw image functions as the primary shelf cue; shoppers have learned to locate the brand instantly by that picture. Removing it risks a drop in immediate recognizability, especially for the older, loyal customer segment that may rely on visual shortcuts rather than brand name recall. A sudden, wholesale rollout magnifies that risk because there is no gradual test market to gauge acceptance or to fine‑tune the new graphics.

Redesign costs are also non‑trivial. New artwork, updated packaging molds, and a coordinated national media spend add upfront expense without a guaranteed lift in sales. The brand’s existing equity rests heavily on the familiar iconography, so any loss of shelf impact could translate into lost volume that outweighs the aesthetic gain.

Mitigation would require a phased rollout or A/B testing in select regions, a clear communication strategy that links the new glass‑and‑cap motif to the legacy orange cue, and a cost‑benefit analysis of the redesign spend versus projected incremental revenue.

Given the high uncertainty around brand cue loss, the sizable upfront cost, and the lack of a controlled test, the safest course is to postpone the full‑scale launch until these risks are validated.

DECISION: NO-GO
