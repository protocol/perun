**Premise & Goal**  
The chain wants to broaden its market by adding a premium adult‑focused burger, shifting from a child‑centric, value‑oriented image to a higher‑margin offering.

**Fit with Current Brand**  
Customers associate the brand with affordable kids’ meals and quick service. Adult diners typically look for either fast‑casual quality or genuine premium restaurants. The brand gap creates a perception risk: adults may reject the product as “fast‑food‑pricey,” while core families may feel alienated by a pricier menu item.

**Price Sensitivity & Demand**  
A premium price above the core menu raises the barrier for repeat purchases. Without proven demand, the high‑budget advertising could burn cash before the market validates willingness to pay. Fast‑food price elasticity is steep; a modest price increase often yields a disproportionate drop in traffic.

**Financial Exposure**  
Committing the industry‑largest promotion spend before a test market proves traction creates a sizable upfront loss risk. A phased test (limited geography, modest spend) would de‑risk the hypothesis.

**Recommendation**  
Pause the full‑scale launch. Conduct a small‑scale pilot with a modest budget to gauge adult interest, price tolerance, and brand perception impact. Use the pilot data to decide on scaling and appropriate spend.

**Conclusion**  
Given the brand mismatch, price risk, and large untested spend, the proposal should not proceed as written.

DECISION: NO-GO
