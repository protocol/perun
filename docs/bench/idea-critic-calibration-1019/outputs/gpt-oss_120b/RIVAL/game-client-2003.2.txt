The proposal promises a unified online client that would solve two chronic problems—delivering updates and fighting cheating—while opening a new distribution channel for third‑party titles. In principle that could raise long‑term revenue and improve player experience, but the risks outweigh the immediate benefits.

1. **Player friction** – Mandatory online accounts and large downloads directly conflict with the expectations of a PC audience accustomed to offline, boxed games. In 2003 many households still relied on dial‑up or slow broadband; forcing a 200 MB+ client would alienate a sizable segment and drive churn.

2. **Retail partner backlash** – Retailers sell physical boxes precisely because they avoid dependence on an online service. Introducing a required client could be seen as a “digital‑only” lock‑out, prompting partners to drop the titles or demand higher margins, eroding the core sales channel the company already owns.

3. **Technical debt and launch risk** – Building a robust, cheat‑free client is non‑trivial. Early bugs would quickly surface in a high‑visibility launch, damaging brand trust and delivering a poor first impression of the new platform. The cost of fixing those bugs would be higher than the incremental revenue from the client’s early adopters.

4. **Infrastructure constraints** – Home broadband penetration in 2003 was far from universal. Players without reliable connectivity would be unable to play, effectively shrinking the market.

5. **Competitive exposure** – By opening the client as a store for other developers, the company hands a strategic platform to competitors who could replicate or improve the service, eroding any first‑mover advantage.

Given these factors, the proposal should be revisited. The team needs to explore a phased rollout—perhaps an optional client for updates only, with a clear opt‑in for the store—while gathering data on broadband adoption and player sentiment. Without mitigating these high‑impact risks, moving forward as‑is would likely damage both revenue and brand reputation.

**Verdict:** The plan is not ready for immediate approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
