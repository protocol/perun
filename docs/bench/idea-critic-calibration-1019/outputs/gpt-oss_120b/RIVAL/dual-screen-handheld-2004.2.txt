The rival’s new handheld threatens our market share with superior graphics and media capabilities. Matching its hardware would pit us in a costly feature race we cannot win on performance alone. The proposed dual‑screen, one‑touch device offers a clear differentiator that can attract casual and non‑gamer users, expanding the audience beyond our core base. Pricing it lower than the rival gives us a price‑value advantage while retaining the legacy handheld to serve existing fans.

Key risks:

* **Developer uncertainty** – a new form factor may initially deter studios. This can be mitigated with a strong SDK, sample code, and early developer outreach.
* **Graphics gap** – the rival’s visual edge remains, but our focus shifts from raw fidelity to novel interaction, reducing the importance of that gap for the target audience.
* **Touch‑screen viability** – untapped but not untested; early titles can showcase the mode and prove the concept.
* **Added cost from a second screen** – offset by a lower overall price point and economies of scale from re‑using existing components.
* **Gimmick perception** – the dual‑screen concept is distinctive; with compelling launch titles and clear marketing of the “dual experience,” it can be framed as innovation rather than a fad.

Strategically, the move aligns with a broader audience expansion while protecting our existing product line. The risks are manageable with focused developer support, a curated launch library, and disciplined cost control.

Given the competitive pressure and the opportunity to redefine handheld play, the proposal merits execution as presented.

DECISION: GO
