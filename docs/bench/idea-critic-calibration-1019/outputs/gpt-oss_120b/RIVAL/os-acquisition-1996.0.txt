**Executive Summary**  
The PC maker’s legacy OS is eroding market share and internal attempts to replace it have failed twice. The proposal is to acquire a small software firm that has built a modern, object‑oriented OS, paying a few hundred million dollars and reinstating its founder as an advisor.

**Key Risks**  

1. **Revenue vs. Price** – The target company has sold only a few units, offering minimal cash flow. Paying a “few hundred M” far exceeds its current revenue, creating a poor return‑on‑investment and a long pay‑back horizon.  
2. **Integration Complexity** – Migrating existing applications and developer tooling to a brand‑new OS core is technically risky. Legacy software compatibility, driver support, and developer re‑training could cause costly delays and product instability.  
3. **Founder Concerns** – The founder’s difficult history with the target raises governance and cultural integration risks, potentially undermining the advisory role and future collaboration.  
4. **Alternative Path** – Licensing an existing third‑party OS avoids the acquisition premium, eliminates integration of a nascent codebase, and provides a proven, supported platform with clear upgrade paths.

**Strategic Fit**  
While acquiring a modern OS aligns with the need for a fresh platform, the financial outlay, limited market traction of the target, and high integration risk outweigh the potential benefits at this stage.

**Recommendation**  
Reject the acquisition in its current form. Pursue a licensing agreement or a smaller‑scale partnership to evaluate the new OS technology before committing to a costly purchase.

DECISION: NO-GO
