The proposal tackles a real problem—slowing traffic—but the cure introduces several high‑impact risks that outweigh the potential upside if executed as described.

**Community core value:** The loyal user base is anchored by the voting mechanic that defines the site’s identity. Replacing it with a publisher‑biased surfacing algorithm threatens the very reason members stay, likely causing churn precisely when traffic is already declining.

**Engineering risk:** A full rewrite from scratch is a massive effort with unknowns in architecture, data migration, and performance. Historically, such rewrites have high failure rates, especially when the existing codebase, however legacy, is already stable for the core flow.

**Launch risk:** Moving *all* users to the new version in a single cut‑over eliminates any safety net. Without a staged rollout or rollback path, any critical bug or unexpected user‑experience regression could lock the product into a broken state, making recovery costly and time‑consuming.

**Competitive pressure:** A rival community site is gaining momentum. A disruptive launch that alienates current users could accelerate migration to the competitor, worsening the traffic decline instead of reversing it.

**Alternative path:** A safer, incremental approach would preserve the voting core, introduce the redesigned front page as an opt‑in feature, and pilot it with a subset of users. This allows real‑world validation, mitigates rollback concerns, and lets the team refine the algorithm before a full migration. Parallel investment in performance, SEO, and community engagement can address traffic issues without jeopardizing the product’s DNA.

Given the magnitude of the engineering effort, the lack of a rollback strategy, and the high probability of alienating the existing community, the proposal as‑is carries too much risk to be approved now.

DECISION: NO-GO
