The proposal’s ambition is strong, but the core risks outweigh the potential upside at this stage.

**Community fit** – The existing voting mechanic is the heart of the platform’s identity. Replacing it with a publisher‑driven surfacing algorithm threatens the loyal user base that sustains traffic. Disrupting that experience without a proven alternative could accelerate the current slowdown.

**Engineering risk** – A full rewrite from scratch is a large, un‑bounded effort. In 2010 the team likely lacks the mature codebase, tests, and tooling needed to guarantee parity and stability. History shows that “rewrite‑and‑replace” projects often run over budget and miss critical edge‑case handling.

**Launch safety** – Migrating all users in a single, irreversible cut‑over leaves no safety net. If the new front‑page algorithm misbehaves, the platform cannot fall back to the known‑good version, exposing the site to downtime and user churn.

**Competitive pressure** – A rival community is already gaining momentum. An abrupt, risky launch could hand that competitor additional users rather than recapturing them.

A more prudent path would be an incremental redesign: keep the voting core, introduce publisher‑boosted surfacing as an opt‑in feature, and roll it out to a small percentage of users with a clear rollback plan. This preserves community trust while validating the new mechanics.

Given the current risk profile, the proposal should not be approved as‑is.

DECISION: NO-GO
