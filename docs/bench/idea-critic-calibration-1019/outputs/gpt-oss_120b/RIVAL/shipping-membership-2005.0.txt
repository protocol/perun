The proposal targets the retailer’s biggest repeat‑customer friction—per‑order shipping fees—by offering an annual $79 membership that guarantees free two‑day shipping and discounted one‑day rates. If successful, the membership could lower checkout abandonment, increase order frequency, and build a loyal customer base, delivering long‑term revenue growth beyond the flat fee.

However, the risks outlined outweigh the potential upside given the limited data:

* **Cost exposure:** Heavy buyers may increase order volume, driving shipping expenses above the $79 fee, eroding margins. Without a clear break‑even analysis, the retailer could subsidize a large share of shipping for a subset of customers.
* **Short‑term margin hit:** The membership fee is modest relative to shipping costs; the retailer will incur a sizable margin reduction before any upside materializes.
* **Irreversibility:** Once members are onboarded, the program is difficult to unwind without alienating customers, creating a lock‑in that limits future pricing flexibility.
* **Unproven economics:** The model is based on a 2005 concept with no contemporary validation; market dynamics, carrier rates, and consumer expectations have shifted.

Given only these facts, the prudent path is to defer approval until a detailed financial model, pilot results, or comparable benchmarks can demonstrate that the membership will at least break even and likely generate net profit. Proceeding now carries a high risk of sustained margin loss.

DECISION: NO-GO
