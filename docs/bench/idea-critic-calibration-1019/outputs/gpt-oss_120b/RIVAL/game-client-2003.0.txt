The proposal aims to turn the developer’s distribution model from boxed retail to a mandatory online client that would centralize updates, anti‑cheat enforcement, purchases, and later act as a marketplace for other studios. This could solve the current pain points of fragmented patch delivery and cheating, and it would give the company a new revenue channel by hosting third‑party titles.

**Benefits**
- Guarantees that every player receives the latest patches and anti‑cheat measures instantly, reducing support load and improving game integrity.
- Creates a direct relationship with the player base, opening opportunities for cross‑selling, DLC, and analytics.
- Positions the company as a platform, potentially attracting developers who need an easy distribution and protection layer.

**Key Risks**
1. **Player backlash** – In 2003, mandatory online accounts and large downloads were unpopular; many gamers valued offline play and resented forced connectivity.
2. **Retail partner strain** – Retailers could view the client as a threat to physical sales and might drop the company’s boxed titles, harming existing revenue streams.
3. **Infrastructure gaps** – Home broadband penetration was still limited; large initial client downloads could exclude a sizable portion of the user base.
4. **Technical maturity** – Launching a new client introduces bugs and security vulnerabilities that could be exploited, especially in an anti‑cheat context.
5. **Competitive imitation** – Early movers risk having their platform concept copied by larger publishers with deeper resources, eroding any first‑mover advantage.

**Mitigations (if pursued)**
- Phase the rollout, starting with optional use for updates only, then expanding to mandatory features after proving stability.
- Offer a lightweight “offline mode” for users without broadband, preserving basic gameplay while limiting online services.
- Secure strong agreements with key retailers, possibly offering co‑branded bundles that bundle the client with physical copies.
- Invest heavily in QA and security audits before public launch, and build a rapid‑response patch pipeline.
- Protect the platform with patents or exclusive partnerships to deter immediate copying.

Given the significant market and technical constraints of the era, the risks outweigh the immediate benefits. A more prudent path would be to pilot the client with a single new title, collect data, and only expand if adoption and performance meet expectations.

DECISION: NO-GO
