**Opportunity**  
- The second‑largest cola maker can differentiate from the saturated traditional market by offering a “clear” cola that signals purity, a trend that resonated in test markets.  
- A successful national launch could capture consumers seeking a lighter‑looking alternative and reinforce the brand’s innovative image.

**Key Risks**  
1. **Flavor‑Color Association** – Many consumers link cola taste with its dark color; a clear product may be perceived as “not a real cola,” reducing repeat purchase.  
2. **Novelty Fade** – Test‑market enthusiasm often reflects curiosity rather than long‑term loyalty; novelty buyers may not become repeat customers.  
3. **Cannibalization** – The new line could siphon sales from the flagship brand, eroding overall profit margins.  
4. **Demand Overstatement** – Small‑scale tests can inflate demand projections, leading to excess inventory and costly roll‑out.  
5. **Competitive Replication** – Rivals could quickly introduce similar clear colas, eroding first‑mover advantage.

**Mitigations**  
- Position the clear cola as a *premium* “purity” offering, emphasizing unique taste notes and health‑perceived benefits in messaging.  
- Begin with a **phased regional rollout** (e.g., 3‑5 key markets) to validate repeat purchase rates before national spend.  
- Protect flagship sales by **cross‑promoting** the clear cola as a complementary, not substitute, product.  
- Secure distinctive packaging and trademarked branding to raise barriers for copycats.  
- Build a robust **post‑launch analytics** framework to monitor repeat rates, cannibalization, and inventory health.

**Recommendation**  
Given the high uncertainty around sustained demand and brand perception, a full‑scale national launch now carries excessive risk. Proceed with a controlled, multi‑region pilot and the outlined mitigations before committing to a large marketing spend.

DECISION: NO-GO
