The logo is a core brand touch‑point that customers instantly recognize; decades of exposure have built strong associative value around the dark‑blue square and its white lettering. Removing that visual identity in a single, unannounced launch eliminates the only cue that most shoppers use to identify the retailer, creating immediate confusion and a sense of loss among loyal patrons.

Social‑media dynamics amplify any negative reaction. A sudden, uncommunicated change will be perceived as a unilateral decision, likely sparking backlash, memes, and criticism that can spread faster than the retailer can respond. The cost of damage control—public statements, paid media, and possible discounts to appease upset customers—will quickly outweigh any aesthetic benefit.

Crucially, the new wordmark and reduced square have not been validated with the target audience. Without customer testing, there is no evidence that the neutral typeface conveys the intended contemporary feel or that the smaller square retains sufficient brand distinctiveness. The risk of a design that feels generic or even less recognizable is high.

A safer, higher‑impact approach would be to:

1. Conduct controlled research (surveys, focus groups, A/B testing) to gauge reactions to the new mark versus the existing one.
2. Develop a phased rollout plan that introduces the new visual alongside the old, allowing customers to adjust and providing the brand time to explain the rationale.
3. Communicate the change proactively through owned channels, highlighting the reasons for modernization and reinforcing continuity of product quality and service.

These steps mitigate brand‑recognition loss, give the retailer data‑driven confidence, and reduce the likelihood of a costly social‑media storm.

Given the lack of testing, the abrupt, unannounced global launch, and the strong attachment to the existing logo, the proposal carries excessive risk and should not be approved in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
