The proposal promises a generous 1 GB of storage per user, fast full‑mail search, and a web UI, funded solely by contextual text ads. In 2004, disk cost was on the order of $10 / GB for enterprise‑grade storage, making the per‑user expense prohibitive at scale. Even an invitation‑only rollout would quickly exceed a modest pilot budget once adoption spreads, and the infrastructure needed for low‑latency search across billions of messages would further inflate capital and operational outlays.

Scanning user mail to serve ads raises immediate privacy concerns. Users accustomed to “no‑tracking” policies for free email would likely resist a service that reads their content, risking reputational damage and potential legal challenges, especially as data‑privacy awareness was already growing.

The email market was already dominated by established players with deep network effects and brand trust. Gaining meaningful market share without a clear differentiation beyond storage size would be difficult, and the lack of any direct revenue at launch leaves the business model fragile.

Given the high cost, privacy risk, and competitive landscape, the plan should be revisited to moderate storage expectations, explore alternative monetization that does not require content scanning, and define a clearer path to sustainable revenue before proceeding.

DECISION: NO-GO
