The orange‑juice market is dominated by visual shelf cues; the current carton picture of an orange with a straw is the primary identifier shoppers use when scanning aisles. Removing that cue in a nationwide rollout risks immediate loss of recognizability, especially for the brand’s core, older, loyal buyers who rely on the familiar image to locate the product quickly.

A minimalist redesign—glass of juice, orange‑shaped cap, vertically rotated name—offers a modern aesthetic but does not guarantee that shoppers will connect the new look to the existing product. The cost of re‑tooling packaging, printing, and a large advertising push is significant, and the return on that investment is uncertain without evidence that the new design will attract enough new customers to offset the drop in sales from confused shoppers.

A safer path would be a phased test: limited‑market pilots, in‑store signage linking old and new designs, and measurement of sales lift versus loss. This would validate recognition, cost impact, and consumer response before committing to a full‑scale launch.

Given the high risk of brand‑cue loss, the substantial redesign expense, and the lack of data on consumer reaction, the proposal should not be approved for immediate nationwide rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
