The proposal targets a strategic shift from pure retail to hardware‑enabled content delivery. While the vision of a paper‑like, cellular‑connected e‑reader aligns with the long‑term trend toward digital books, the plan ignores several critical constraints.

1. **Core competency gap** – The company has never designed consumer hardware. Developing a reliable, mass‑produced device requires expertise in industrial design, supply‑chain management, firmware, and regulatory compliance. Without a partner or internal team, the risk of costly delays and quality failures is high.

2. **Market precedent** – Earlier dedicated e‑readers sold poorly, indicating limited consumer demand at the time. The proposal does not present new market research or a differentiated value proposition beyond “paper‑like screen” and free cellular service, which may not be sufficient to overcome past buyer reluctance.

3. **Publisher relations** – Low e‑book prices could erode margins and alienate publishers already wary of piracy. The plan lacks a concrete strategy to negotiate pricing, royalty structures, or DRM that satisfies both parties.

4. **Cannibalization risk** – Introducing a low‑price digital channel could undercut existing print sales, threatening a proven revenue stream without clear mitigation.

5. **Financial exposure** – Hardware development entails substantial upfront capital (R&D, tooling, inventory) with uncertain return. The proposal offers no phased rollout, pilot testing, or partnership to share risk.

Given these gaps, the initiative should be re‑examined with a more incremental approach: validate demand through a software‑only app, explore OEM partnerships for the device, and secure publisher agreements before committing to full hardware development.

DECISION: NO-GO
