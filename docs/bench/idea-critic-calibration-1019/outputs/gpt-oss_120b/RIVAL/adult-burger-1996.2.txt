The proposal targets a dramatic shift in brand perception: moving from a child‑focused fast‑food image to a premium adult offering. This creates three core strategic tensions.

1. **Brand‑fit mismatch** – The chain’s core equity is built on family‑friendly value and kids’ meals. Adults already associate the brand with quick, inexpensive fare, not with premium quality. Introducing a high‑priced burger risks diluting the existing perception and may alienate the loyal family segment.

2. **Price elasticity** – Premium pricing “above the core menu” will likely deter the current customer base, who are price‑sensitive and visit for value. Without clear evidence of adult willingness to pay a premium at this chain, the higher price could depress overall traffic and cannibalize existing sales.

3. **Capital intensity before market validation** – Allocating “the largest advertising and promotional budget the industry has seen” before any demand signal is captured creates a heavy upfront cost with uncertain return. If demand does not materialize, the chain will incur a significant financial loss and may have to cut back on other growth initiatives.

Mitigating actions (e.g., limited‑region test launch, price‑tier experiments, co‑branding with a recognized premium partner, or a phased marketing spend) are not outlined. The proposal therefore lacks a risk‑reduction pathway and a clear go‑to‑market validation plan.

Given the high brand‑fit risk, uncertain price elasticity, and the large, untested marketing outlay, the current proposal does not meet a prudent risk‑adjusted threshold for approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
