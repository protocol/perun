**Executive Summary**  
The proposal aims to create a paid, console‑wide online service in 2002, offering a unified player identity, friends list, voice chat, and matchmaking for all games. While the feature set would be a strong differentiator, the market and operational realities of the time make the plan high‑risk.

**Key Risks**  
1. **Broadband Penetration** – In 2002 only a small minority of households have broadband. Requiring it excludes a large portion of the existing console base.  
2. **Price Sensitivity** – Players are accustomed to free online play or publisher‑managed services. An additional annual subscription will likely deter adoption, especially when rivals provide free or optional online options.  
3. **Operational Inexperience** – The company has never run large‑scale hosting, billing, or support. Building this capability from scratch adds significant engineering, staffing, and capital costs, with an uncertain timeline.  
4. **Competitive Landscape** – Rivals already offer free or publisher‑specific online play, reducing the incentive for consumers to pay for a universal service.

**Potential Upsides**  
- A unified service could lock users into the ecosystem and generate recurring revenue.  
- Early mover advantage in integrated voice chat and matchmaking could become a compelling selling point if broadband adoption grows quickly.

**Mitigation Paths**  
- **Phase‑In Model**: Start with a free tier for basic matchmaking, adding premium features later once broadband adoption reaches a critical mass.  
- **Partnerships**: Leverage existing ISP or telecom networks to bundle service access, reducing the broadband barrier.  
- **Pilot Program**: Run a limited beta with a small subscription fee to validate demand and operational readiness before a full launch.

**Recommendation**  
Given the low broadband penetration, strong price sensitivity, and the company’s lack of hosting expertise, launching a full‑price, mandatory‑subscription service now poses a high probability of low adoption and operational strain. The prudent course is to pause the proposal, explore a phased or partnership approach, and re‑evaluate once market conditions improve.

DECISION: NO-GO
