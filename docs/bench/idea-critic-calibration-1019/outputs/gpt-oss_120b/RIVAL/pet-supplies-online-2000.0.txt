The plan calls for a public offering to fund a massive TV ad blitz, an acquisition of the biggest online rival, and rapid expansion of warehousing while deliberately pricing many items below cost to “win customers.”  

**Financial risk:** Selling below cost creates negative gross margins on a large portion of the catalog. Combined with heavy, low‑margin goods that are expensive to ship, the business would need an unsustainable cash burn to stay afloat.  

**Capital risk:** Going public requires market confidence; if capital markets tighten or the IPO underperforms, the company may not raise enough money to cover the ad spend, acquisition price, and warehouse build‑out. The proposal does not include contingencies for a failed offering.  

**Acquisition risk:** Buying the largest competitor adds integration complexity and debt. If the target’s margins are similarly weak, the acquisition could amplify losses rather than provide synergies.  

**Customer‑acquisition cost:** The plan already acknowledges high CAC. Adding a national television campaign—especially a major sports broadcast—will dramatically increase that cost, further eroding any thin margins.  

**Competitive dynamics:** Rivals are also investing heavily in marketing. Matching or exceeding their spend without a clear path to profitability is likely to trigger a price war, deepening margin pressure.  

**Operational risk:** Rapid warehouse expansion assumes demand growth that the current pricing strategy cannot sustain. Over‑building capacity could lock in fixed costs that outpace revenue, especially if the ad campaign fails to generate profitable sales.  

Given the combination of negative margins, high CAC, reliance on a successful IPO, and aggressive expansion without a clear profitability roadmap, the proposal exposes the company to significant financial and operational danger. A more prudent approach would be to first achieve sustainable positive margins, validate a lower‑cost acquisition strategy, and test targeted advertising before committing to a public offering and massive spend.

**Verdict:** The current plan is too risky to approve as written.

DECISION: NO-GO
