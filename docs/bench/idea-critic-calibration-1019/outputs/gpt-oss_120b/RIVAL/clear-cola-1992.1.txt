**Executive Summary**  
The second‑largest cola maker is confronting a plateau in traditional soft‑drink sales. A clear‑cola concept has generated a favorable reaction in limited test markets, suggesting a niche appetite for a “purer” product. The proposal calls for an immediate nationwide launch with a large marketing push, treating the clear cola as a major brand extension.

**Key Risks & Mitigations**

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation Needed |
|------|--------|-------------------|
| **Flavor‑color association** – consumers may reject a colorless cola because they equate brown hue with authentic cola taste. | Potential early churn, brand‑image damage. | Conduct sensory studies to isolate flavor perception from color, and develop messaging that educates consumers on the product’s taste equivalence. |
| **Novelty purchase fatigue** – trial buyers may not become repeat purchasers. | Low long‑term volume, wasted marketing spend. | Implement a phased rollout with repeat‑purchase incentives (e.g., loyalty coupons) and monitor repeat‑purchase rates before scaling. |
| **Cannibalization of flagship** – clear cola could siphon sales from the core brand without expanding the overall market. | Revenue shift rather than growth, margin erosion. | Model cannibalization scenarios; set a “cannibalization ceiling” (e.g., ≤5 % of flagship volume) and pause rollout if exceeded. |
| **Test‑market over‑estimation** – limited markets may not reflect national demand. | Over‑investment, inventory surplus. | Expand test markets to diverse demographics and regions; use a statistically robust sample before national commitment. |
| **Competitive copycat** – rivals could quickly launch similar clear colas, eroding differentiation. | Rapid loss of first‑mover advantage. | Secure distinctive branding (e.g., unique packaging, proprietary formula) and file appropriate trade‑dress/IP protections. |

**Strategic Recommendations**

1. **Staged Launch** – Begin with a regional rollout (3–5 markets) covering different consumer segments, allowing real‑world repeat‑purchase data to validate demand.
2. **Brand Positioning** – Position the clear cola as a “premium, pure‑taste” offering rather than a direct extension, reducing perceived threat to the flagship.
3. **Marketing Scope** – Allocate a modest, test‑phase budget focused on digital and experiential channels; reserve full‑scale TV spend for later phases pending performance metrics.
4. **Performance Triggers** – Define clear KPIs (repeat purchase rate >30 % after 90 days, cannibalization <5 %, market share growth ≥2 % in test regions) to gate progression to national launch.

**Conclusion**  
The concept shows promise, but the current plan lacks sufficient safeguards against cannibalization, repeat‑purchase risk, and over‑optimistic demand assumptions. A phased, data‑driven approach with predefined performance triggers is essential before committing to a costly nationwide rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
