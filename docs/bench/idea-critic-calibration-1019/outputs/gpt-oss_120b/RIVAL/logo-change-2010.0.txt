The logo is the most visible expression of a brand’s identity, especially for a mature retailer whose customers have associated the dark‑blue square with reliability and heritage for decades. Changing it to a neutral word‑mark with a tiny blue square removes the distinctive visual cue that differentiates the brand in a crowded market.

Key concerns:

1. **Customer attachment** – Loyal shoppers have built a mental shortcut around the existing mark. Removing it without warning can feel like a loss of familiarity, prompting negative sentiment.
2. **Speed of backlash** – Social media amplifies any perceived misstep instantly. A sudden, unannounced switch gives no time for the brand to shape the narrative or address questions, increasing the risk of viral criticism.
3. **Recognition loss** – The square is the primary recognizer of the brand across all touchpoints (stores, packaging, ads). A subtle redesign may actually make the brand less recognizable, hurting brand recall.
4. **Lack of validation** – No customer testing means there is no evidence that the new word‑mark resonates or that the tiny square conveys the intended modern feel. Without data, the change is speculative.

A more prudent approach would be to:

* Conduct controlled testing (focus groups, A/B digital tests) to gauge reactions.
* Develop a phased rollout plan—pilot the new logo in limited markets or channels first.
* Communicate the rationale and timeline to customers ahead of time, framing the change as an evolution rather than a sudden replacement.

These steps reduce risk, preserve brand equity, and provide measurable feedback before a full‑scale launch.

Given the high exposure of the change, the absence of testing, and the potential for immediate negative fallout, proceeding as‑is is too risky.

DECISION: NO-GO
