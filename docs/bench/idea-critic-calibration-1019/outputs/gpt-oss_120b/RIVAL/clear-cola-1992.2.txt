The test‑market results show a clear, positive consumer response to a “purer” cola, indicating that the concept can attract interest when introduced in a limited setting. This validates the core idea and suggests a viable market niche for a clear‑cola offering.

However, several substantial risks are evident from the proposal itself:

1. **Brand‑association risk** – Consumers may instinctively link cola flavor with its traditional dark color. A clear version could create confusion or reduce perceived authenticity, weakening the product’s appeal.
2. **Novelty‑decay risk** – Early adopters who are attracted by the novelty may not become repeat purchasers, limiting long‑term sales momentum.
3. **Cannibalization risk** – The new product could siphon sales from the flagship cola, eroding overall profit margins rather than expanding the total market.
4. **Demand‑over‑estimation risk** – Test‑market performance often inflates demand because of heightened local awareness and limited competition. Scaling to a national rollout may not sustain the same enthusiasm.
5. **Competitive‑response risk** – Rivals could quickly emulate a clear‑cola, eroding any first‑mover advantage and turning the launch into a costly arms race.

Given these concerns, the prudent course is to gather additional data before committing to a nationwide launch. Options include:

- **A) Conduct a larger, multi‑regional test** to assess repeat purchase rates and brand perception across diverse demographics.
- **B) Develop a phased rollout** starting with a limited number of key markets, paired with rigorous tracking of cannibalization metrics.
- **C) Hold the launch** until the above studies confirm sustainable demand and minimal impact on the core brand.

The current information does not sufficiently mitigate the identified risks, and a full‑scale launch could jeopardize the company’s flagship position without guaranteeing lasting growth.

DECISION: NO-GO
