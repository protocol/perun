**Issue**  
The company wants to launch a proprietary e‑reader with a paper‑like display and built‑in cellular connectivity, letting customers buy and download books directly from its online catalog. This would be the firm’s first foray into consumer hardware, a market where earlier dedicated e‑readers performed poorly. Publishers are already concerned about piracy, and the proposal calls for low e‑book pricing that could compress margins and strain publisher relationships. There is also a risk that the device could cannibalize existing print‑book sales.

**Key Risks & Trade‑offs**  

| Risk | Impact if Wrong | Why It Matters |
|------|----------------|----------------|
| **Hardware Inexperience** | Development delays, cost overruns, quality problems | The company has never designed consumer hardware; learning‑curve costs are high and could jeopardize the product launch. |
| **Low E‑book Prices** | Margin squeeze, publisher push‑back | Aggressive pricing may make the business model unsustainable and damage key supplier relationships. |
| **Market History** | Weak demand, inventory surplus | Prior e‑readers sold poorly, indicating limited consumer appetite for standalone devices. |
| **Cannibalization** | Reduced print revenue, brand dilution | An inexpensive e‑reader could shift customers away from higher‑margin printed books. |
| **Piracy Concerns** | Publisher disengagement, legal exposure | Free cellular connectivity could be perceived as facilitating unauthorized copying, worsening publisher mistrust. |

**Completeness**  
The proposal covers the core product concept but omits mitigation strategies for each risk (e.g., partnership with experienced hardware OEMs, tiered pricing, robust DRM, phased market testing). Coverage is roughly **5/10** – the high‑level idea is present, but detailed risk‑management and financial modeling are missing.

**Recommendation**  
**NO‑GO** – the initiative is premature given the company’s lack of hardware expertise, historical market failures, and significant publisher and margin risks. The proposal should be revised to:

1. Conduct a market validation study (surveys, prototype testing).  
2. Explore partnerships or licensing with an established e‑reader manufacturer to reduce hardware risk.  
3. Develop a clear pricing and royalty model that protects margins and satisfies publishers.  
4. Model the impact on print sales and define safeguards (e.g., bundled print‑e‑book offers).  

Only after these steps are addressed should the executive team reconsider moving forward.

DECISION: NO-GO
