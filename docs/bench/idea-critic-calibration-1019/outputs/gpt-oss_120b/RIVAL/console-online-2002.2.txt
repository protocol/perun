The proposal introduces a console‑wide paid online service in 2002, a time when broadband penetration is still limited. Most households rely on dial‑up, so requiring broadband will exclude a large portion of the existing console audience, reducing immediate adoption. Competitors are offering free or publisher‑controlled online play, setting a market expectation that online connectivity does not carry an additional cost. Introducing a mandatory subscription therefore risks alienating players who view online features as a standard, cost‑free part of the gaming experience, potentially shrinking the player base and discouraging third‑party publishers from supporting the platform.

Beyond market concerns, the company has no prior experience operating large‑scale hosting or support services. Building and maintaining the necessary infrastructure, staffing a support operation, and handling billing and customer service represent significant new cost centers and operational risk. Without proven expertise, the likelihood of service reliability issues, poor customer satisfaction, and cost overruns is high.

Given the mismatch between the required broadband adoption, consumer expectations for free online play, and the company’s lack of operational capability, the proposal’s risk profile outweighs its potential benefits at this stage. A more prudent path would be to defer a paid service until broadband becomes more ubiquitous, explore partnerships for hosting, or pilot a free tier to gauge demand.

DECISION: NO-GO
