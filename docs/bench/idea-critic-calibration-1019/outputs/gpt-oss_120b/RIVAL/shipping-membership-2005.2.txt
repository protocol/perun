The core friction—shipping fees—clearly hurts repeat‑purchase frequency, so a value‑added membership that removes that friction is a logical lever. A $79 annual price is modest relative to typical order values, and free two‑day shipping aligns with consumer expectations for speed.

However, the proposal rests on several unverified assumptions:

1. **Cost vs. revenue** – Without data on average orders per heavy buyer, the risk that shipping expenses outpace the $79 fee is real. If heavy users double their order count, the incremental shipping cost could exceed the membership margin.

2. **Margin impact** – The retailer will incur a short‑term hit to gross margin on all qualifying orders. The proposal does not outline a breakeven point (e.g., number of orders per member needed to cover shipping costs).

3. **Reversibility** – Once customers hold memberships, the program is hard to unwind without damaging brand trust. A clear exit strategy is missing.

4. **Economic validation** – No pilot results, market tests, or comparable benchmarks are provided to prove the model works for this retailer’s size and logistics network.

Given these gaps, the prudent path is to run a limited pilot (e.g., a single‑region, 3‑month trial) with clear metrics: membership uptake, average orders per member, shipping cost per order, and net contribution margin. The pilot would also allow the retailer to design an easy‑out clause for members.

Until that evidence is gathered, approving the full rollout carries significant financial and brand risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
