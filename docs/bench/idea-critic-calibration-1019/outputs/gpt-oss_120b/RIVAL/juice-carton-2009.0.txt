**Analysis**

The proposal aims to refresh a long‑standing visual cue – the orange with a straw – with a minimalist carton that shows a glass of juice, an orange‑shaped cap, and a vertically rotated brand name. The potential upside is a more contemporary look that could attract younger shoppers and differentiate the brand on shelves. However, the existing image is the primary shelf cue that decades of shoppers, especially the brand’s core older demographic, rely on to identify the product quickly. Removing that cue in a single nationwide rollout risks:

* **Recognition loss** – shoppers may overlook the product, leading to immediate sales dip.
* **Brand equity erosion** – the iconic image carries emotional value; its removal could alienate loyal customers.
* **High implementation cost** – redesigning packaging, retooling production lines, and launching a large advertising campaign require significant capital.
* **Market uncertainty** – no data on how the new design performs in real‑world retail environments.

A safer approach would be a staged test in select markets, gathering sales and perception data before a full rollout. This would allow the brand to validate the new design’s effectiveness, mitigate recognition risk, and refine the advertising spend.

**Recommendation**

Do not proceed with the full‑nation launch as described. Instead, run a controlled pilot (e.g., 5‑10 % of stores) to measure impact on sales, brand perception, and cost recovery. Use the findings to decide whether a broader rollout is justified or if a hybrid design (retaining the orange cue alongside the new elements) would better balance modernization with brand continuity.

**Conclusion**

Given the high risk of losing shelf recognition and the substantial upfront cost, the proposal should be revised to include a phased test before committing to a nationwide launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
