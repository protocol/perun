**Executive Summary**  
The proposal aims to enter a tablet market that, as of 2011, is dominated by a single incumbent with a mature hardware‑software ecosystem, extensive app catalog, and strong brand trust. The acquired mobile‑OS has negligible market share and an app ecosystem that is a fraction of the leader’s. Pricing the new tablet “in line with the market‑leader” will place it directly against a product that already enjoys economies of scale, deep distribution channels, and consumer confidence. Heavy spending on developer outreach and advertising can increase awareness, but without a critical mass of apps the device will struggle to deliver compelling user value, limiting adoption despite marketing spend.

**Key Risks**  
1. **Competitive dominance** – The incumbent’s market share creates a high barrier to entry; matching price erodes margins without guaranteeing differentiation.  
2. **App ecosystem deficit** – A small catalog reduces the tablet’s utility, making the platform unattractive to both consumers and developers.  
3. **OS market share** – Near‑zero penetration means the hardware offers little advantage over existing platforms.  
4. **Leadership transition** – Organizational instability can impede execution and strategic focus.  
5. **Consumer perception** – An unproven product will face skepticism, especially at premium pricing.

**Mitigations Needed**  
- Differentiate on hardware features or niche use‑cases rather than price parity.  
- Secure flagship apps or exclusive content before launch.  
- Phase the rollout, starting with a lower‑cost, limited‑release model to test market response.  
- Stabilize leadership and align internal priorities before major investment.

Given the current risk profile and lack of clear differentiators, proceeding as‑proposed would likely result in poor market traction and financial loss.

**Recommendation** – Revise the plan to address ecosystem and differentiation before committing resources.

DECISION: NO-GO
