The claim is that offering 1,000x more storage than competitors, funded by content-scanned ads, will disrupt a mature email market despite high infrastructure costs and privacy risks.

Steelman: By eliminating the need to delete emails and enabling instant retrieval of the entire history, the service creates a uniquely sticky product. The advertising model aligns revenue with usage depth, turning the cost of storage into an investment in ad inventory precision.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing: Cede the strategic data layer to incumbents; remain only a search engine.
2. Incremental increase (e.g., 100MB): Fails to change user behavior or generate headlines; easily matched by competitors.
3. Paid-only model: Loses the network effect and data volume required to train superior search/ad algorithms.

Fit: This moves the north star from "searching the web" to "organizing the world's information," including private communications. It leverages existing search infrastructure for the ad-matching mechanism.

Pre-mortem: The initiative fails if privacy backlash causes regulatory bans or mass user rejection before the network effect locks in. It also fails if storage costs per user exceed ad revenue per user permanently. However, this is a two-way door; the invitation-only launch limits blast radius, allowing a pivot or shutdown if unit economics or public sentiment turn negative. The cost of delay is high: if a competitor secures the "permanent inbox" mindset first, the search company loses its primary data advantage.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the assumption that ad targeting based on full-content scanning yields significantly higher CPMs than contextual page ads, offsetting the gigabyte storage cost. While privacy concerns are valid, the "invitation-only" rollout acts as a controlled experiment to gauge tolerance.

Strongest attack survived: The argument that storage costs would be prohibitive was tested against the trend of falling hardware prices and the potential for massive ad revenue scale. The calculation suggests that the lifetime value of a user with a permanent, searchable archive outweighs the marginal cost of a gigabyte of storage, provided the ad system functions as intended.

DECISION: GO
