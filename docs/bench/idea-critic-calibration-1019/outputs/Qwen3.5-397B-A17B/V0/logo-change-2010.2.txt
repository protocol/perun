The claim is that an untested, unannounced, immediate global logo replacement will successfully modernize a mature brand without triggering destructive backlash. The steelman: a radical, clean break is the only way to shed "dated" perceptions, and hesitation signals weakness.

However, this proposal fails the pre-mortem. Assuming the rollout has already occurred and failed: the cause was the removal of the primary recognition heuristic (the dark blue square) without customer validation, compounded by the "no advance announcement" strategy which transformed a brand update into a perceived erasure of heritage. Social media acts as an accelerant for this specific grievance, turning confusion into organized boycott before the company can explain its rationale.

The "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the "all at once" approach. A phased pilot (e.g., digital-only first) or a transitional period retaining the square alongside the new typeface would allow for sentiment measurement and adjustment. The current plan offers zero reversibility once physical stock and signage are replaced; the cost of delay (waiting for testing) is negligible compared to the cost of a forced re-rebrand after backlash.

The proposal lacks evidence that the "neutral typeface" resonates with the target demographic or that the loss of the square won't alienate the core loyalist base. It treats brand equity as a liability to be discarded rather than an asset to be evolved. The risk of instant, viral backlash is not just theoretical; it is the most probable outcome given the lack of testing and the abruptness of the change.

This is a one-way door decision with high irreversible costs and no safety net. The absence of customer testing and the deliberate choice to withhold announcement creates an unacceptable probability of value destruction. The proposal must be rejected in its current form to prevent a self-inflicted crisis.

DECISION: NO-GO
