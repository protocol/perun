The claim is that an untested, unannounced, simultaneous global logo replacement will modernize a mature brand without triggering destructive backlash. The steelman: a clean break signals contemporary intent faster than a phased transition, avoiding a "limbo" period that dilutes the message.

However, the proposal fails the pre-mortem test. Assuming the rollout has already caused a 20% sales dip and a social media firestorm, the cause is clear: the brand severed its primary visual anchor (the dark blue square) without validating customer attachment or preparing the market. The "no advance announcement" strategy removes the ability to frame the narrative, ceding control to confused loyalists and opportunistic critics. In the social media era, silence is interpreted as arrogance, not confidence.

The "better way" analysis reveals fatal flaws in the "all-at-once" approach compared to alternatives. A phased rollout (digital first, then physical) or a teaser campaign would allow for real-time sentiment adjustment. The "do nothing" option retains brand equity while exploring modernization through product lines rather than erasing core identity. The proposal ignores Chesterton's Fence: the old square exists for recognition; removing it entirely assumes recognition is a burden, not an asset.

Crucially, the proposal lacks any kill criteria or leading metrics. There is no defined threshold for aborting the rollout if initial reaction turns toxic, nor is there a plan to revert to the legacy mark if sales tank. The absence of customer testing on the new design is an unsupported assumption that "neutral typeface" equals "contemporary" to this specific demographic.

The strongest attack survived? None. The risk of immediate, irreversible brand alienation outweighs the speculative benefit of a "clean break." The lack of testing and the refusal to phase the rollout creates a single point of failure with no rollback mechanism. The cost of delay (waiting to test) is negligible compared to the cost of a botched global launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
