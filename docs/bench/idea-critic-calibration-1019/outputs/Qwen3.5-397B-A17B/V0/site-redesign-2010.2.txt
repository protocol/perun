The proposal to rewrite the entire platform from scratch and force an immediate migration of all users is fundamentally flawed due to excessive risk and misalignment with the core asset: the loyal community.

The primary failure mode is alienating the existing user base. The community values the current voting mechanics; altering these to favor publishers without a transitional period invites immediate backlash and potential mass exodus to the growing rival site. A "big bang" launch offers no rollback mechanism; if the new algorithm fails to engage users or breaks community norms, the damage is instantaneous and irreversible.

Furthermore, a full rewrite introduces massive engineering uncertainty. In 2010, rebuilding a complex social news architecture from zero carries a high probability of scope creep, bugs, and delayed delivery, during which traffic continues to decline. The proposal ignores cheaper, safer alternatives such as A/B testing the new surfacing logic on a segment of traffic, iterating on the existing codebase, or introducing publisher features incrementally.

The assumption that a complete overhaul is necessary to reverse slowing traffic is unproven. It is more likely that specific tweaks to the existing system could yield results without the catastrophic risk of a total replacement. The lack of a phased rollout or a kill criterion for the migration makes this a one-way door with unacceptable downside.

To proceed, the plan must be revised to include a phased rollout, preservation of core voting mechanics for legacy users, and a fallback strategy. As it stands, the proposal threatens the company's survival by gambling its entire user base on an unproven, unrollable rewrite.

DECISION: NO-GO
