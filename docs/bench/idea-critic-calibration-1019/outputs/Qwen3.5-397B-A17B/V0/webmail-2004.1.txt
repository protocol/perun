**Steelman:** Offering 1,000x more storage than incumbents, paired with superior search, will disrupt a mature market by solving the user pain point of deletion, monetized later via contextual ads.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is unit economics: if storage costs exceed ad revenue per user, the service bleeds cash indefinitely. In 2004, storage is expensive; a gigabyte per user is a massive liability compared to megabyte-scale competitors. The "invitation-only" gate mitigates initial blast radius but does not solve the fundamental cost/revenue mismatch if ad targeting fails or faces regulatory backlash.

Alternatives include: (1) **Do Nothing:** Avoid capital expenditure in a low-margin, saturated market. (2) **Paid Tier:** Charge for gigabyte storage immediately to validate willingness-to-pay before subsidizing with ads. (3) **Hybrid:** Launch with 500MB to halve risk while still dominating incumbents.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a strategy of leveraging core search competency to enter a new vertical. However, assuming users tolerate privacy invasion for storage is a dangerous bet; a scandal over "scanning mail" could destroy brand trust permanently. If ad fill rates are low or CPMs drop, the company is stuck with infinite liability (storage) and zero asset recovery. The cost of delay is low; email isn't disappearing next quarter. Waiting for storage costs to drop further reduces the burn rate significantly.

**Verdict:**
The proposal relies on unproven assumptions: that ad revenue will outpace heavy infrastructure costs immediately, and that privacy concerns won't trigger a backlash. The "free" model with such high per-user costs is a binary bet on ad technology maturing faster than user acquisition burns cash. Without a paid fallback or a clearer path to positive unit economics in year one, the risk of creating a massive, unusable legacy liability is too high. The invitation-only start is insufficient insurance against a flawed core economic model.

DECISION: NO-GO
