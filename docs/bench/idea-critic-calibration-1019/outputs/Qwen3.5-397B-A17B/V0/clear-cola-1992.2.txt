The claim assumes clear color signals "purity" enough to drive repeat purchases nationwide, overcoming deep-seated associations between cola flavor and dark color. The steelman: a clear cola can carve a durable niche as a pure alternative without eroding the flagship's core identity, leveraging test market enthusiasm into sustained national share.

However, the "purity" cue may be a novelty trigger rather than a value driver. If flavor perception remains tied to dark color, the product fails the taste test upon first sip, killing repeat rates. Test markets often inflate demand for novelties that fade quickly when the "newness" wears off. Cannibalization is a critical threat: if the clear version draws sales from the flagship without expanding the total category, the net revenue impact could be negative, especially given the high cost of a national launch campaign. Rivals can copy the clear aesthetic rapidly, eroding any first-mover advantage before profitability is reached.

The proposal lacks a specific kill criterion for repeat purchase rates or a cap on cannibalization. It treats test market positivity as a proxy for national durability without accounting for the "novelty decay" effect. A cheaper, lower-risk path exists: a staged regional rollout to measure repeat behavior and cannibalization metrics before committing to a full national campaign. This allows for pivot or stop if repeat rates fall below a sustainable threshold.

Without evidence that the "clear = pure" association drives long-term loyalty rather than one-time trials, and without a mechanism to limit cannibalization or a staged approach to verify national viability, the risk of a costly failure outweighs the potential gain. The proposal commits to a high-cost, low-reversibility national launch based on data that may not predict long-term behavior.

DECISION: NO-GO
