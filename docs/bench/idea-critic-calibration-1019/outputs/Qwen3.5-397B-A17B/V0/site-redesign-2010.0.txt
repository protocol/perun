**Steelman:** A complete rewrite with a publisher-focused algorithm and forced migration is the only way to reverse traffic decline and modernize the platform before a competitor captures the market.

**Attack:** This proposal fails on three critical fronts. First, it ignores the primary asset: the loyal community. Altering the core voting mechanics they value, while simultaneously favoring publishers over community submissions, creates a high probability of mass user exodus. Second, the "big bang" migration eliminates rollback capability; if the new algorithm fails to engage users or contains bugs, the site collapses instantly with no recovery path. Third, a full rewrite introduces massive engineering delay and risk, allowing the growing rival to solidify their lead while development occurs.

**Better Ways:**
1.  **Iterative Rollout:** A/B test the new surfacing logic on a small traffic segment while keeping the core engine intact.
2.  **Hybrid Approach:** Integrate publisher content as a separate section without altering the existing community voting mechanics.
3.  **Do Nothing (Short Term):** Optimize current infrastructure to improve speed while researching user sentiment on design changes.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** The proposal assumes users want publisher content over community curation, an unverified guess that contradicts the site's history. If launched as proposed, the most likely outcome is a revolt by power users, a broken migration script with no undo button, and a traffic cliff that benefits the rival. The cost of delay is real, but the cost of a failed total rewrite is existential.

**Kill Criteria:** The lack of a rollback plan and the alienation of the core user base are fatal flaws. The engineering risk of a scratch rewrite is unjustified without validating the new ranking logic first.

**Verdict:** The proposal demands excessive risk for unproven gains and threatens the community foundation. It must be rejected in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
