The claim is that launching two social-networking phones on a new, unproven operating system without an app store will succeed with teenagers in 2010. The steelman argues that leveraging the acquired messaging brand and exclusive carrier distribution creates a focused, differentiated ecosystem that bypasses the complexity of a full app market.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws. First, the target demographic (teenagers) is highly price-sensitive, yet the mandatory data plan equals the cost of a full smartphone plan, removing the primary adoption lever. Second, the "no application store" constraint is a critical failure point in 2010; rival devices already offer large catalogs, and social-networking users expect third-party extensibility. A phone without apps is functionally obsolete upon arrival. Third, building a new in-house OS ignores the company's existing mobile platform, doubling development risk and fragmenting support while competitors mature their ecosystems. The sunk cost of two years' development is a fallacy; continuing to pour resources into a product missing core market expectations (apps, affordable data) guarantees further loss.

The "better way" is to port the acquired messaging features to the company's existing, proven mobile platform and negotiate a subsidized, teen-specific data tier with the carrier. This reduces time-to-market, leverages existing app ecosystems, and addresses the price barrier. The current proposal fails the "do nothing" test because launching a premium-priced, app-less device on a new OS is statistically more likely to fail than maintaining the status quo or pivoting to the existing platform.

The pre-mortem is clear: the phones launch, teens reject the high monthly cost and lack of apps, the carrier demands subsidies the maker cannot afford, and the brand equity of the acquired messaging phone is destroyed by association with a failed hardware platform. There is no reversible "small step" here; the launch conditions (no store, new OS, high cost) are binary blockers.

DECISION: NO-GO
