**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee converts variable shipping friction into fixed revenue, locking in loyalty and increasing purchase frequency to offset margin dilution.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is adverse selection: heavy buyers exploit the cap, causing unit economics to collapse before volume gains materialize. The "hard to reverse" risk is real; refunding pro-rated fees upon cancellation creates immediate cash outflow.
*   *Do Nothing:* Retain per-order friction, preserving margin but capping frequency.
*   *Threshold Shipping:* Free shipping only above a high cart value (lower risk, weaker loyalty lock).
*   *Paid Per-Use:* Discounted rates without an upfront fee (no cash flow, no commitment).

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a growth-stage strategy prioritizing market share over immediate profitability. However, assuming 2005 logistics costs and unknown elasticity, a pre-mortem suggests that if average order value (AOV) drops or return rates spike, the $79 fee covers less than two shipments, guaranteeing a loss on every active member. The "unproven economics" is the critical blocker; launching broadly without a constrained test invites catastrophic margin erosion.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:**
No internal data exists to validate the "heavy buyer" volume projection. The proposal lacks a pilot mechanism.
*   *Kill Criterion:* If a limited beta shows member AOV is <15% lower than non-members or return rates exceed 8%, halt expansion.
*   *Leading Metric:* Contribution margin per member (fee + goods margin - fulfillment cost) tracked weekly.

**Verdict:**
The proposal fails the "evidence" check by asking for a full launch based on unproven unit economics. The risk of irreversible customer backlash and cash burn is too high without a validated model. While the strategic logic is sound, the execution plan ignores the necessity of a bounded experiment to prove the "heavy buyer" behavior actually generates net positive contribution. Approving this now is a gamble, not a decision. It must be revised to include a time-boxed, capped-enrollment pilot to gather real elasticity data before a general launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
