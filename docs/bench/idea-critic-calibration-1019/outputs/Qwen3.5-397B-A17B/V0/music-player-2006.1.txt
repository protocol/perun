Steelman: A well-funded entrant can displace a dominant music player by superior hardware (larger screen), seamless wireless sharing, and an integrated store, overcoming lateness and ecosystem lock-in through user experience and marketing.

**Skeptic Analysis**: The proposal relies on unverified assumptions: that labels will permit unrestricted wireless sharing despite previous copy-protection standards, and that marketing spend alone can break deep ecosystem loyalty. The "late to market" risk is severe; without a clear price advantage or exclusive content, the value proposition rests entirely on features competitors may quickly clone. Dropping copy-protection might alienate existing partner stores and device makers, isolating the new ecosystem before it gains traction.

**Better-Way Alternatives**: 
1. **Do Nothing**: Avoid the massive capital expenditure and brand risk of challenging an entrenched monopoly without a disruptive technological edge.
2. **Licensing Model**: License the software/interface to existing hardware manufacturers rather than building a proprietary device, reducing execution risk.
3. **Service-Only Launch**: Launch the store and subscription first on multiple platforms to build content relationships before committing to hardware.

**Kill Criteria & Pre-mortem**: If label negotiations fail to secure sharing rights within six months, the core differentiator vanishes. If market share does not reach 5% within two years despite the marketing blitz, the ecosystem flywheel has failed to ignite. The pre-mortem suggests failure occurs because content owners block the sharing feature, rendering the hardware merely a slightly larger version of the incumbent with no unique utility.

**Decision**: The proposal attempts to solve a content and ecosystem problem with hardware and marketing. Without guaranteed label support for the key differentiator (sharing) and facing a "late to market" disadvantage against a loyal user base, the risk of creating an orphaned ecosystem is too high. The abandonment of the industry copy-protection standard creates immediate friction with partners.

DECISION: NO-GO
