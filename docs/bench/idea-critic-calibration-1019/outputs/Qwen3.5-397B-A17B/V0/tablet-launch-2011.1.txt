**Steelman:** Leverage the acquired OS to enter the tablet market immediately, matching the leader's price while relying on aggressive marketing and developer outreach to overcome a tiny app ecosystem and brand skepticism during leadership transition.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on three verified fronts. First, pricing parity with a dominant leader while offering a fraction of the apps violates basic value perception; shoppers will not pay a premium for an unproven, incomplete ecosystem. Second, "heavy investment" in developer outreach is a lagging indicator; developers follow users, not marketing spend, creating a chicken-and-egg deadlock that cash alone cannot break quickly. Third, launching during leadership transition amplifies the "unproven" risk, signaling internal instability to the market.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "delay" option is superior. Wait for the OS to gain organic traction in mobile phones first, building a user base that naturally attracts tablet developers. Alternatively, undercut the market leader significantly to force trial, accepting lower margins to buy market share, rather than matching prices with an inferior product.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch fails in six months. The cause is clear: consumers bought the market leader because the app gap was too wide to ignore, and the high price made the risk unacceptable. The company burned cash on ads that drove traffic to a product users refused to buy, worsening the perception of failure.

**Fit:** This contradicts the north star of sustainable ecosystem growth. You cannot advertise your way out of a utility deficit. The small catalog is a fatal flaw, not a marketing challenge.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed unless the price is significantly below the leader to compensate for the app gap, or the app catalog reaches a critical threshold of parity. Neither condition is met. The current plan guarantees high costs for low adoption.

DECISION: NO-GO
