**Steelman:** A mandatory client centralizes update delivery and anti-cheat enforcement, transforming a fragmented retail distribution model into a persistent, defensible platform that secures long-term recurring revenue and ecosystem control.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The proposal risks immediate retail channel collapse and alienates users with poor connectivity in 2003. However, "do nothing" guarantees continued revenue leakage from piracy and unsustainable patching logistics. Physical-only distribution cannot support the frequency of updates required to neutralize modern cheats. A hybrid approach (optional client) fails because cheaters will simply bypass the optional layer, rendering anti-cheat useless. The "large download" friction is a one-time cost that vanishes as broadband adoption accelerates, whereas piracy is a permanent bleed.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the north star of shifting from one-time box sales to lifetime user value. If we assume the client launches with critical bugs, early adopters may churn. Yet, delaying launch to perfect the client allows competitors to entrench their own platforms or for piracy to erode the user base further. The cost of delay is the loss of first-mover advantage in owning the customer relationship.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:**
The premise relies on the trajectory of broadband adoption and the inability of physical media to support rapid iteration. The strongest attack—that retail partners will drop the games—is mitigated by the publisher's leverage: their titles are "very popular." Retailers need the hits more than the publisher needs the shelf space long-term.

**Decision:**
The strategic necessity of owning the distribution channel and securing the game integrity outweighs the transitional friction of mandatory online requirements. The risk of retail pushback is manageable given the strength of the IP, while the risk of inaction is existential. Proceed immediately to capture the platform advantage before competitors replicate the model.

DECISION: GO
