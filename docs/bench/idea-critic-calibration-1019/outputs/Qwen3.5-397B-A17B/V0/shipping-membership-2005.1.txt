**Steelman:** An upfront $79 fee secures customer loyalty and increases order frequency, offsetting per-order shipping friction and generating predictable recurring revenue despite higher fulfillment costs.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is adverse selection: heavy buyers extract more value than they pay, while light buyers subsidize them, causing a net margin collapse. The "unproven economics" risk is critical; without historical data on elasticity, the retailer risks scaling a loss-making unit. Reversibility is low; cancelling an active membership program triggers massive churn and brand damage.

Alternatives considered:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Retain current friction, preserving margins but limiting repeat purchase velocity.
2.  **Tiered Thresholds:** Free shipping only above a high cart value (e.g., $50), protecting margins on small orders.
3.  **Paid Per-Use:** Discounted shipping rates without an upfront fee, testing price sensitivity without commitment risk.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This assumes customers value speed over cost certainty. If the average member orders fewer than 8 times annually (assuming ~$10 avg shipping cost), the program loses money immediately. A pre-mortem reveals that if adoption is too high among existing heavy users before new behavior is generated, the short-term cash flow hit could cripple operations before long-term LTV gains materialize. The "hard to reverse" nature means a failed launch locks the company into a structural deficit.

**Kill Criteria & Evidence:**
No empirical evidence is provided regarding current order frequency distribution or shipping cost per order. The proposal relies entirely on the assumption that increased volume will outpace margin erosion. Without a pilot cohort or a capped rollout to validate the unit economics, proceeding exposes the entire firm to uncapped downside risk. The lack of a "stop" condition or a phased testing approach violates basic capital allocation hygiene for unproven models.

**Verdict:**
The proposal fails the evidence check. It commits to a structural change with high reversibility costs based on unverified assumptions about customer behavior and cost elasticity. The risk of immediate, scalable losses outweighs the potential for loyalty gains without a preliminary test.

DECISION: NO-GO
