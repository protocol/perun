The proposal addresses a critical vulnerability: the end-user is unaware of the product's identity, leaving the brand exposed to commoditization by clones with similar part numbers. In 1991, the computer market is expanding rapidly among general consumers who lack technical discernment. If buyers cannot distinguish quality, price becomes the only differentiator, eroding margins.

The strategy shifts power dynamics by creating direct consumer demand. By making the component a household name, computer makers are forced to include the chip to satisfy customer requests, effectively neutralizing the threat of generic alternatives. The risk of being "unprecedented" is not a deterrent but a first-mover advantage; if successful, it creates an insurmountable barrier to entry for competitors.

Financial concerns regarding the marketing budget share are valid but short-sighted. This expenditure is not merely advertising; it is a strategic subsidy that leverages the computer makers' existing media spend. By co-funding their ads, the company ensures its logo appears everywhere their partners advertise, multiplying reach without building a standalone media empire from scratch.

Potential resentment from computer makers is a manageable friction. While they may dislike external direction on advertising, they will prioritize sales volume over autonomy. If consumers begin asking for the branded chip by name, manufacturers who refuse the program risk losing shelf space to competitors who comply. The fear of unclear sales benefits ignores the long-term brand equity being built; without this campaign, the company remains an invisible supplier vulnerable to price wars.

The timing is optimal. As personal computing moves from business niches to living rooms, establishing brand recognition now defines the category for decades. Waiting for perfect certainty or consensus guarantees missed opportunity. The potential upside of owning the consumer mindshare in a booming market vastly outweighs the risks of budget allocation and partner friction. Execution must begin immediately to capture this window before clones solidify their foothold.

DECISION: GO
