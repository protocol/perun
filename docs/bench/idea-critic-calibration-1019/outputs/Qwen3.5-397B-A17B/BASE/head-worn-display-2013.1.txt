The proposal outlines launching a head-worn display with camera, voice control, and a screen to early adopters at $1,500, followed by a general consumer release. While the invitation-only approach attempts to manage initial rollout, the identified risks present critical barriers to immediate approval. Privacy objections regarding a wearable camera are substantial and likely to trigger public backlash and regulatory scrutiny before the product gains traction. A price point of $1,500 is prohibitively high for an "everyday consumer product," especially when the value proposition remains undefined for the average user. The lack of a clear everyday use case suggests the device solves no urgent problem for the mass market, making widespread adoption unlikely regardless of marketing efforts. Furthermore, social acceptance is a significant hurdle; wearing a camera in public invites hostility and discomfort from others, potentially stigmatizing users. Finally, short battery life undermines the utility of an all-day wearable device, rendering it impractical for continuous use. Proceeding now ignores these fundamental flaws in product-market fit, pricing strategy, and social viability. The combination of high cost, privacy concerns, undefined utility, and technical limitations creates a high probability of commercial failure and reputational damage. The risks outweigh the potential benefits of being first to market with an unrefined concept. Approval should be withheld until these specific issues are resolved through further development, price reduction, clear use-case definition, and privacy safeguards.

DECISION: NO-GO
