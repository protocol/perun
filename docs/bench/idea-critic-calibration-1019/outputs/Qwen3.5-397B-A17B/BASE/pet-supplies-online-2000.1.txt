The proposal presents an existential threat to the company's viability. The strategy relies on selling products below cost to acquire customers, which directly generates negative gross margins. When combined with the explicit risk that heavy, low-margin goods are costly to ship, every additional sale deepens the financial loss. This model assumes unlimited access to capital to subsidize operations indefinitely.

Seeking an Initial Public Offering in early 2000 to fund this burn rate is exceptionally dangerous given the stated risk that capital markets could close. If the window for fundraising shuts while the company is intentionally losing money on every transaction and facing high customer-acquisition costs, liquidity will vanish immediately. Furthermore, using scarce proceeds to buy a competitor and build warehouses expands the fixed-cost base precisely when the core unit economics are broken. Adding a national television campaign, including major sports broadcasts, will skyrocket expenses without guaranteeing a path to profitability, especially since competitors are already spending heavily.

Expanding operations while intentionally undermining revenue per unit creates a feedback loop of destruction. The plan requires continuous external funding to survive, yet it increases exposure to the very risk of market closure. Without a clear mechanism to achieve positive unit economics or reduce shipping burdens, this approach guarantees insolvency if investor sentiment shifts. The combination of negative margins, high logistical costs, and reliance on volatile public markets makes this specific course of action untenable. Approval would authorize the rapid depletion of remaining resources on a strategy designed to lose money at scale.

DECISION: NO-GO
