The proposal presents a high-risk entry into a market dominated by an entrenched competitor with a loyal ecosystem. Entering late without a clear price advantage significantly reduces the probability of gaining meaningful market share. The strategy relies heavily on a large marketing budget to overcome these structural disadvantages, which is an inefficient use of capital when the core value proposition is weak.

Critically, the decision to drop the established copy-protection standard creates immediate friction with existing partners, including record labels and device makers. This move alienates the very ecosystem required to secure content licensing. Given that labels are already likely to restrict the proposed wireless sharing feature due to piracy concerns, removing copy protection exacerbates this conflict rather than resolving it. Without label support, the music store cannot launch with a competitive catalog, rendering the hardware and subscription services unviable.

The combination of being late to market, lacking a price edge, and actively undermining partner relationships creates a scenario where failure is highly probable. The risks outlined are not merely operational hurdles but fundamental flaws in the business model that threaten the company's reputation and financial stability. Proceeding as proposed would likely result in significant losses with little chance of displacing the market leader. The strategic alignment with partner needs is absent, and the regulatory and licensing environment appears hostile to the proposed features.

Approval cannot be granted for a plan that simultaneously antagonizes content providers and offers no distinct economic benefit to consumers. The foundational assumptions regarding partner cooperation and consumer adoption are unsupported by the current market realities described.

DECISION: NO-GO
