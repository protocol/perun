The proposal introduces a paid, console-wide online service in 2002, requiring broadband and an annual subscription. This contrasts sharply with rivals offering free or publisher-managed online play. Significant risks are explicitly identified: broadband adoption is currently low, creating a small addressable market; consumers expect online functionality to be free, suggesting strong resistance to a mandatory fee; and the company lacks any existing infrastructure or experience in hosting and supporting such operations.

Launching a service that requires hardware (broadband) most customers do not possess, charges for a feature competitors give away, and relies on operational capabilities the company has never built presents an unacceptably high probability of failure. The combination of limited market readiness, negative consumer sentiment regarding pricing, and internal operational gaps means the venture is likely to incur heavy losses without gaining traction. Proceeding immediately ignores these fundamental barriers to entry and adoption. The risks outweigh the potential benefits of a unified identity and friends list at this specific time. Approval cannot be granted without first addressing the lack of broadband penetration, validating willingness to pay against free alternatives, and establishing the necessary technical operations. As the proposal stands, it demands resources for a service the market is not ready to accept and the company is not ready to deliver.

DECISION: NO-GO
