The proposal outlines a 2013 launch strategy for a head-worn display featuring a camera, voice control, and a small screen. The plan involves an initial invitation-only sale to early adopters at $1,500, followed by a general public release positioned as an everyday consumer product. However, the identified risks present critical barriers that cannot be ignored.

Privacy objections regarding the wearable camera are severe and likely to trigger immediate public backlash and regulatory scrutiny. A price point of $1,500 is prohibitively high for a mass-market everyday device, especially when the utility for the average person remains undefined. The proposal explicitly admits there is no clear everyday use case for most people, which directly contradicts the positioning strategy. Without a compelling reason for daily adoption, the product will fail to move beyond a niche gadget status.

Furthermore, social acceptance is a major hurdle; wearing a camera-equipped device in public invites hostility and discomfort from others, potentially making the user a social pariah. Finally, short battery life undermines the core promise of an "everyday" product, as users cannot rely on it for sustained use throughout the day.

Launching now with these unresolved issues guarantees market rejection. The high cost combined with low utility, significant privacy concerns, and poor social viability creates a perfect storm for failure. The invitation-only program may generate initial hype, but it will not validate the fundamental flaws preventing mass adoption. Proceeding as proposed ignores the reality that the product lacks a defined problem it solves for the general public. The risks outweigh any potential first-mover advantage. The strategy requires a complete overhaul to address privacy, reduce costs, define clear use cases, and improve hardware limitations before any public-facing sales can occur. Approving this now would be fiscally irresponsible and damaging to the company's reputation.

DECISION: NO-GO
