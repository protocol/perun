The proposal to launch a clear cola nationwide in 1992 carries unacceptable risks relative to the potential rewards. While test markets showed positive responses, historical data in this industry frequently demonstrates that such environments overstate actual demand when scaled nationally. The core concern is the fundamental disconnect between consumer expectations and the product offering: shoppers strongly associate traditional cola flavor with dark color. Removing the color may satisfy curiosity but fails to deliver the sensory experience required for repeat purchases.

The risk of attracting only novelty buyers is severe. If the product is perceived as a gimmick rather than a genuine alternative, initial sales spikes will collapse once the novelty wears off, leaving the company with excess inventory and damaged retailer relationships. Furthermore, launching a major brand extension with a large marketing campaign invites direct retaliation from competitors who can quickly release copies, diluting any first-mover advantage.

Most critically, the potential for cannibalization of the flagship product is high. Without clear evidence that this new product attracts a distinct demographic or usage occasion, it will likely merely shift volume from the existing high-margin portfolio to a new, unproven line. This internal competition erodes overall profitability without expanding the total market share. The slowing growth of the soft-drink category suggests the need for genuine innovation or diversification, not a cosmetic variation that confuses the core brand identity.

The current data does not support the massive investment required for a nationwide rollout. The identified risks regarding flavor perception, repeat purchase rates, and cannibalization are too significant to ignore based solely on limited test market results. A more prudent approach involves further refinement of the value proposition and additional testing to isolate repeat purchase behavior before committing to a full-scale launch. Approving this now exposes the company to substantial financial loss and brand dilution.

DECISION: NO-GO
