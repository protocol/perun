The proposal presents an unacceptable level of risk to the brand's market position. The primary function of the current packaging is immediate shelf recognition, driven by the iconic image of an orange with a straw. Removing this established visual cue threatens to sever the connection with the existing customer base, which is described as loyal and older. This demographic typically relies on familiar patterns for purchasing decisions and may struggle to identify the product under a minimalist design featuring a glass of juice and a rotated brand name.

The plan involves a simultaneous nationwide launch across all stores. This "big bang" approach eliminates the possibility of testing consumer reaction in controlled markets before committing full resources. If shoppers fail to recognize the carton, sales could collapse immediately on a national scale, with no opportunity for a gradual adjustment or localized correction. The financial exposure is compounded by the high cost of the redesign and the extensive advertising campaign required to explain the new look to consumers who already know the product.

While keeping the recipe and price constant maintains product value, it does not mitigate the danger of visual obscurity on the shelf. The risks identified—loss of recognition, alienation of the core older demographic, and high implementation costs—are direct consequences of the proposed strategy. Proceeding without a pilot phase or evidence that the new design effectively communicates the brand identity to the target audience is reckless. The potential loss of shelf presence outweighs the aesthetic benefits of the minimalist update. The proposal must be rejected in its current form to prevent probable significant revenue loss and brand erosion.

DECISION: NO-GO
