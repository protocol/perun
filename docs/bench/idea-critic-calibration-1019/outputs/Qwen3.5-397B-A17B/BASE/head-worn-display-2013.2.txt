The proposal outlines a 2013 launch strategy for a head-worn display featuring a camera, voice control, and a screen. The plan involves an initial invitation-only release at $1,500 targeting early adopters, followed by a general public launch positioned as an everyday consumer product. However, the identified risks present critical barriers to immediate approval.

Privacy objections regarding the wearable camera are significant and likely to trigger immediate public backlash and regulatory scrutiny. A price point of $1,500 is prohibitively high for a mass-market everyday device, limiting the addressable market to a tiny fraction of users. Furthermore, the absence of a clear everyday use case for the majority of consumers undermines the core positioning of the product. Without defined utility, adoption will stall after the initial novelty wears off.

Social acceptance poses another major hurdle; wearing a camera-enabled device in public may be perceived as intrusive or awkward, leading to social friction that discourages usage. Finally, short battery life severely restricts functionality, preventing the device from serving as a reliable all-day companion as intended.

Launching now with these unresolved issues risks brand damage, poor sales performance, and negative market perception. The combination of high cost, privacy concerns, unclear utility, social stigma, and technical limitations suggests the product is not ready for the proposed trajectory. An invitation-only program does not sufficiently mitigate these fundamental flaws if the end goal is a near-term general release as an everyday item. The risks outweigh the potential benefits in the current configuration.

DECISION: NO-GO
