The proposal to launch a clear cola nationwide in 1992 carries substantial, unmitigated risks that outweigh the positive test market results. While the concept of a "purer" alternative resonated in limited trials, the fundamental concern that consumers psychologically associate cola flavor with dark color remains unaddressed. If this sensory expectation is violated, initial curiosity will not translate into the repeat purchases necessary for long-term viability.

Furthermore, the potential for cannibalization of the flagship brand is severe. As the second-largest maker, protecting the core revenue stream is paramount. Diverting marketing budgets and shelf space to a novelty product could erode the market share of the primary driver of profits without guaranteeing net growth. The risk is compounded by the likelihood of rapid imitation by rivals; if the concept succeeds, larger competitors with deeper pockets could quickly flood the market with copies, diluting the first-mover advantage before a loyal customer base is established.

Test markets are notoriously prone to overstating demand for novel items because they attract early adopters who do not represent the broader population. Scaling immediately to a national launch with a large marketing campaign assumes these early signals will hold true across diverse demographics, a dangerous assumption given the product's reliance on breaking established consumer habits. Without a strategy to ensure retention beyond the initial trial or to protect the flagship brand's dominance, proceeding now exposes the company to significant financial loss and brand confusion. The uncertainties regarding consumer psychology, competitive response, and internal portfolio impact require further analysis and a more cautious rollout strategy before full commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
