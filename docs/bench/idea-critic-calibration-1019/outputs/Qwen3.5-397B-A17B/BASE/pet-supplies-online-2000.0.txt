The proposal presents an existential threat to the company's viability. The strategy relies on selling products below cost to acquire customers, which directly generates negative gross margins. When combined with the explicit risk that heavy, low-margin goods are costly to ship, every additional sale deepens the financial hole rather than building value. This unit economics failure cannot be solved by volume; it accelerates cash burn.

Funding this operation through an initial public offering in early 2000 is dangerously speculative. The proposal itself admits that capital markets could close. If the IPO window shuts due to market volatility or investor skepticism regarding the negative margins, the company will lack the liquidity to fund the planned national television campaign, the acquisition of the largest competitor, and the construction of new warehouses. Without these funds, operations would likely halt immediately.

Furthermore, the plan to buy the largest competitor while simultaneously engaging in a price war creates a redundant capital drain. Competitors are already spending heavily, suggesting a saturated market where customer acquisition costs are inflating for everyone. Pouring venture funding and potential IPO proceeds into a major sports broadcast campaign under these conditions ignores the diminishing returns of advertising in a hyper-competitive space.

The combination of negative gross margins, high shipping costs, and reliance on uncertain future capital creates a scenario where the company runs out of money before achieving profitability. Expanding warehouse capacity and acquiring rivals while losing money on every transaction is not a growth strategy; it is a path to insolvency. The risks identified in the proposal are not merely potential hurdles; they are fundamental flaws in the business model as described. Approving this plan would authorize the rapid depletion of remaining resources with a low probability of survival. The company must stabilize its unit economics and secure reliable funding sources before attempting such aggressive expansion.

DECISION: NO-GO
