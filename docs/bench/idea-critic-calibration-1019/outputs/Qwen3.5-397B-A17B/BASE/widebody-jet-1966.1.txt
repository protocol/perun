The proposal presents a transformative opportunity but carries existential risks that currently outweigh the potential rewards. The financial exposure is unprecedented, with development and factory costs exceeding any previous company outlay. Committing resources of this magnitude based on a single customer order violates fundamental principles of capital preservation and market validation. While the request comes from the largest airline, relying on one client for a project of this scale creates dangerous dependency; if that airline falters or cancels, the company faces insolvency.

Technological uncertainty further compounds the danger. The proposed engine is unproven, introducing significant performance and reliability variables that could derail the entire program. Additionally, the strategic landscape is shifting rapidly. The industry expectation that supersonic airliners will soon replace subsonic jets suggests this massive subsonic investment could become obsolete before reaching profitability. Building a specialized factory and supply chain for a technology potentially nearing its sunset is strategically unsound without clearer market signals.

The design feature allowing conversion to a freighter is forward-thinking, yet it does not mitigate the immediate threats of cost overruns, engine failure, or market displacement. The assumption that air travel growth will strictly favor larger subsonic jets ignores the disruptive potential of supersonic competitors. Without multiple launch customers to share the financial burden and validate the market need beyond a single entity, proceeding now is reckless. The company must secure additional orders, verify engine viability through testing, and obtain clearer data on the supersonic timeline before locking in such massive expenditures. The current facts support a pause to de-risk the venture rather than an immediate full-scale commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
