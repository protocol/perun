The proposal to replace a decades-old logo with an untested design, rolled out simultaneously across all channels without advance notice, presents an unacceptable level of risk. The brand's identity is currently anchored by the dark blue square, which serves as the primary recognition factor for loyal customers. Removing this established visual asset in favor of a plain neutral-typeface wordmark and a diminished blue square severs the immediate visual link consumers have with the brand.

The risks identified in the proposal are severe and currently unmitigated. Loyal customers are explicitly noted as being attached to the existing logo, creating a high probability of emotional backlash. In the modern landscape described, where social media enables instant negative reaction, launching such a drastic change without a buffer or communication strategy is reckless. The absence of customer testing for the new design means the company is proceeding blindly, assuming the new aesthetic will be received positively despite evidence suggesting strong attachment to the heritage look.

Furthermore, the strategy of "no advance announcement" eliminates the opportunity to prepare the market, explain the rationale, or manage the narrative. This approach treats a core brand asset as a simple switch rather than a significant cultural shift for the customer base. A mature retailer seeking to appear contemporary must evolve its image without alienating its foundation. Doing so requires validation, phased implementation, and strategic communication, none of which are present in this plan.

Proceeding immediately with this specific plan invites confusion, reputational damage, and potential revenue loss from a confused or offended customer base. The lack of testing and the abrupt nature of the rollout make this proposal fundamentally flawed in its current form. Approval cannot be granted for a strategy that ignores the very customers the brand relies upon and fails to validate its new direction before full-scale execution.

DECISION: NO-GO
