The proposal introduces a fundamental shift from transactional shipping fees to a recurring revenue model centered on customer loyalty. The primary friction identified is the per-order cost, which discourages repeat purchases. By charging $79 annually for unlimited two-day shipping, the retailer aims to eliminate this barrier. However, the risks outlined are substantial and immediate.

The economic model relies on the assumption that increased order frequency will generate enough marginal profit from goods sold to offset the loss of shipping fees and the cost of logistics. If heavy buyers significantly increase their volume, the variable cost of shipping could rapidly exceed the fixed $79 revenue per member, creating a loss on every active subscriber. The proposal explicitly admits the economics are unproven, meaning there is no data to validate whether the lift in gross merchandise value will cover the operational deficit.

Furthermore, the financial impact involves a guaranteed short-term margin hit. Without existing cash reserves or a proven buffer, absorbing these costs threatens overall liquidity. The difficulty of reversing the offer once customers have purchased memberships creates a long-term liability; if the model fails, the company cannot easily retract the promise without severe reputational damage and potential refund obligations.

Launching an unproven financial structure that risks immediate margin erosion and creates irreversible customer expectations violates prudent executive oversight. The potential for unlimited liability on shipping costs, combined with the lack of pilot data or validated unit economics, makes the current proposal too dangerous to execute. The strategic intent to boost retention is sound, but the specific mechanism lacks the necessary safeguards and proof points to proceed immediately. A pilot program or detailed financial modeling is required before a full-scale launch can be considered.

DECISION: NO-GO
