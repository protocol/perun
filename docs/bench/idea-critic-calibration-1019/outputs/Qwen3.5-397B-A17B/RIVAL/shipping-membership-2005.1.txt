This proposal targets the correct friction point: per-order shipping fees discourage repeat purchases. However, the 2005 context introduces specific constraints that make the current plan dangerous.

**The Economics Are Backward for 2005**
In 2005, two-day shipping infrastructure was not commoditized. Logistics costs were significantly higher relative to average order values than today. A flat $79 fee assumes a specific frequency of purchase that heavy buyers will almost certainly exceed. If a customer spends $500 annually and normally pays $150 in shipping, capturing them at $79 is a direct loss. Without dynamic modeling of "heavy buyer" elasticity, the retailer risks subsidizing their most expensive logistics at a loss.

**The Reversibility Trap**
The risk assessment correctly identifies that reversing this offer is politically and operationally difficult once customers hold memberships. In 2005, customer goodwill was harder to rebuild post-internet-bubble. A failed program that angering the top 10% of customers (who likely become the "heavy buyers") could cause disproportionate brand damage.

**Missing Validation**
The proposal admits economics are unproven. Launching a nationwide, annual commitment without a regional pilot or a capped beta is reckless. The margin hit described is not a risk; it is a certainty for the initial cohort until data proves otherwise.

**Required Changes Before Approval**
1.  **Pilot Program:** Restrict launch to a single geographic region or a specific customer segment for 90 days to validate the break-even frequency.
2.  **Dynamic Cap:** Implement a "fair use" policy or tiered structure rather than unlimited free shipping, protecting against extreme outliers.
3.  **Exit Strategy:** Define a clear grandfathering clause for existing members if the program must be terminated, reducing the reversal friction.

As it stands, the proposal asks the executive team to approve a structural margin reduction based on unproven assumptions in a high-cost logistics environment. The potential for catastrophic unit economics on heavy users outweighs the loyalty benefits without prior validation.

DECISION: NO-GO
