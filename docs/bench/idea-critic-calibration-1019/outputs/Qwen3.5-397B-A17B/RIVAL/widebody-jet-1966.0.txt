This proposal represents a classic "bet-the-company" moment. The customer is asking for a 2.5x capacity increase, which is not an incremental upgrade but a fundamental shift in aviation logistics. The wide-body, four-engine design with a raised cockpit for freighter conversion demonstrates forward-thinking architecture that addresses both immediate passenger density and long-term cargo utility.

However, the risks are existential. The development and factory outlay exceeds any previous commitment, creating massive financial leverage. Relying on an unproven high-bypass engine introduces technical uncertainty that could ground the entire fleet. Furthermore, the market timing is precarious: if supersonic airliners render subsonic jets obsolete as predicted, this massive investment becomes a stranded asset before it achieves ROI. Having only one airline customer for such a colossal program concentrates demand risk dangerously; if that single carrier falters or cancels, the program collapses with no backup buyers.

The "do nothing" cost is also severe. If air travel grows as projected and this capacity isn't built, the airline will turn to competitors, potentially ending the dominant market position. The raised cockpit design is a brilliant strategic hedge, ensuring the aircraft retains value as a freighter even if passenger dynamics shift.

The critical failure point is the single-customer dependency combined with the supersonic threat. Before committing, the company must secure additional launch customers to diversify risk and validate the subsonic market thesis against the supersonic prediction. The engine development also requires a parallel path or guarantee to prevent propulsion delays from sinking the airframe program.

As proposed, with only one airline ordered and the supersonic overhang unresolved, the risk profile is too skewed. The technical vision is sound, but the commercial foundation is insufficient for this magnitude of capital exposure. The company should negotiate for more launch commitments and engine guarantees before breaking ground on the factory.

DECISION: NO-GO
