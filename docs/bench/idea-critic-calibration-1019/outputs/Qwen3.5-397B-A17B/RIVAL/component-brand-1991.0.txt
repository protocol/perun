This proposal represents a fundamental shift from a component supplier to a consumer-facing brand, effectively bypassing the immediate customer (computer makers) to influence the end user. The core insight is sound: in a market where products are indistinguishable to the buyer, branding creates the only meaningful differentiation. By making the invisible visible, the company forces computer makers to compete on the quality of its component, turning a commodity into a premium feature.

The risks are substantial but manageable. Consumer advertising for a component is indeed unprecedented, which is precisely why it will work; first-mover advantage in brand mindshare is insurmountable once established. The concern regarding budget allocation is valid, yet traditional B2B marketing yields diminishing returns when the product is perceived as identical to competitors. Redirecting funds to pull-demand generation is a higher-leverage use of capital.

Computer maker resentment is a tactical hurdle, not a strategic blocker. The program is voluntary; manufacturers seeking a competitive edge will adopt it, while those who refuse will find themselves at a marketing disadvantage when rivals promote "Powered By" superiority. The leverage shifts because the end consumer begins demanding the branded component, making the logo a license to sell.

The lag in sales data is an expected characteristic of brand building, not a failure of the concept. Success metrics must shift from immediate unit lift to brand awareness studies and premium pricing power. If the campaign succeeds, the company secures pricing power and shelf space, effectively locking out clone makers who cannot match the brand equity.

The proposal correctly identifies that technical superiority alone is insufficient when the market cannot distinguish it. This strategy transforms the purchase decision from a spec-sheet comparison to a brand preference. The unprecedented nature of the risk is the source of its potential reward. Hesitation here cedes the narrative to competitors who may eventually copy the technical specs but cannot easily copy the brand position once claimed.

DECISION: GO
