This proposal represents a high-risk brand transformation executed with dangerous negligence. While the strategic goal—modernizing a mature retailer's image—is valid, the proposed execution method violates fundamental principles of brand equity management.

The plan to roll out changes "at once with no advance announcement" is the critical failure point. Brand recognition is built over decades; the dark blue square is not merely a logo but a visual asset carrying significant goodwill and immediate market identification. Removing it without transition treats loyal customers as obstacles rather than stakeholders. In 2010, social media was already a potent force for consumer organization; an unannounced, drastic change invites immediate, coordinated backlash that the company has no plan to mitigate.

Furthermore, the admission that the new design "was not tested with customers" is unacceptable for a change of this magnitude. Replacing a known, loved asset with an untested one is speculation, not strategy. The risk of alienating the core customer base—who may perceive the change as abandoning the brand's heritage—far outweighs the potential gain of appearing "contemporary" to new audiences. A plain neutral-typeface wordmark is also a commodity design choice that may fail to distinguish the brand in a crowded market.

The proposal lacks a transition strategy, customer validation, or crisis communication plan. It assumes compliance rather than engagement. A successful modernization would involve phased rollout, customer co-creation or testing, heritage integration (perhaps evolving the square rather than discarding it), and a narrative that honors the past while embracing the future.

As presented, this plan prioritizes internal desire for change over external brand reality. It gambles the company's established market position on an untested aesthetic preference.

DECISION: NO-GO
