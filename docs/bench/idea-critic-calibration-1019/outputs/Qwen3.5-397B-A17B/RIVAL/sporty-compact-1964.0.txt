This proposal targets a clear, emerging demographic shift: postwar baby boomers entering the market with a desire for style over pure utility. The strategy of leveraging existing compact car mechanics drastically reduces development risk and cost, allowing for an aggressive base price point that lowers the barrier to entry.

The "long hood, short deck" styling directly addresses the "dull" perception of the current lineup without requiring a ground-up engineering effort. Crucially, the long options list transforms the vehicle from a static product into a customizable platform, enabling high-margin upsells while maintaining a low advertised entry price. This captures both budget-conscious buyers and those willing to pay for prestige features.

The identified risks are manageable. The "unproven demand" is precisely why a massive launch campaign is necessary; it creates the market rather than waiting for it to materialize. The risk of appearing "cheap" due to parts sharing is mitigated by the sporty styling and the psychological effect of the low entry price combined with high-visibility advertising. If the campaign succeeds, volume will justify the spend; if it fails, the shared architecture limits downside exposure compared to a clean-sheet design.

The timing is critical. Waiting to validate demand with smaller tests cedes the segment to competitors. The baby boom wave is a transient, high-volume opportunity that requires a bold, immediate capture strategy. The proposal balances innovation (styling, marketing) with prudence (shared mechanics, modular options).

DECISION: GO
