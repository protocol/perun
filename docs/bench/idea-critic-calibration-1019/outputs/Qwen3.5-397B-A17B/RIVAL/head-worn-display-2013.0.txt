This proposal attempts to launch a disruptive hardware platform into a market that is not yet ready for its constraints. The identified risks are not merely hurdles; they are fundamental blockers to mass adoption in the proposed form.

First, the privacy objection is existential. A always-on wearable camera creates immediate social friction and legal ambiguity. Without a clear, indisputable indicator of recording status or a robust policy framework, public backlash is guaranteed, not speculative. Launching to the general public with this unresolved invites regulatory scrutiny and brand toxicity.

Second, the value proposition is undefined. "No clear everyday use" is a fatal flaw for a $1,500 consumer device. Early adopters tolerate bugs; they do not tolerate uselessness. Positioning this as an "everyday consumer product" without a killer app contradicts the internal assessment that no such use case exists. The price point exacerbates this, placing it in premium territory without premium utility.

Third, the go-to-market strategy ignores the social acceptance barrier. An invitation-only program mitigates risk slightly, but the transition to "general public" assumes these hard problems will solve themselves over time. They will not. Battery life and social stigma are engineering and cultural challenges, not marketing ones.

The proposal asks to proceed with a full consumer launch trajectory despite admitting the product lacks a core reason to exist for the target demographic. It bets on hope rather than product-market fit. The risks are too high, the use case too vague, and the social headwinds too strong to approve a general release strategy.

The company should pause the general public rollout. Resources must be redirected to enterprise or vertical-specific use cases where the value proposition is clear (e.g., field service, medical, logistics) and privacy concerns are manageable. Do not launch to consumers until a definitive "everyday use" is proven and the privacy/social framework is bulletproof.

DECISION: NO-GO
