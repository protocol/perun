The proposal addresses critical failures in update delivery and cheating but introduces severe adoption friction in 2003. While the long-term vision of a unified store is strategically sound, the immediate execution carries unacceptable risks for the company's current revenue base.

**Critical Flaws in Current Proposal:**
1.  **Retail Alienation:** Forcing a mandatory online client for boxed games in 2003 will likely trigger immediate retaliation from retail partners who drive the majority of sales. They may delist products or reduce shelf space, causing a revenue collapse before the digital model matures.
2.  **Infrastructure Mismatch:** Home broadband penetration is not universal. A mandatory large download creates a hard barrier to entry for a significant portion of the existing customer base, directly reducing sales volume.
3.  **Trust Deficit:** Players already dislike mandatory accounts. Coupling this with anti-cheat surveillance and purchase requirements in a buggy v1 client creates a high probability of public backlash and review bombing.
4.  **Competitive Exposure:** Announcing a store platform early signals intent to competitors without having the network effect to defend it. They can copy the feature set while leveraging their own established user bases.

**Required Revisions Before Approval:**
*   **Decouple Updates from Activation:** The client should be optional for single-player experiences or offered as a "silent" background updater for boxed goods to preserve retail relationships.
*   **Phased Rollout:** Launch the client as a voluntary beta for a single title to stress-test infrastructure and refine the user experience before making it mandatory across the portfolio.
*   **Retail Partnership Program:** Develop a revenue-sharing model for digital codes sold through retail boxes to align incentives rather than disrupting the channel.
*   **Broadband-Lite Mode:** Ensure the core game remains playable offline, with the client handling only periodic checks rather than constant connectivity.

The strategic direction is correct, but the "mandatory now" approach threatens the company's immediate financial stability. The execution must prioritize channel harmony and user tolerance over speed of platform dominance.

DECISION: NO-GO
