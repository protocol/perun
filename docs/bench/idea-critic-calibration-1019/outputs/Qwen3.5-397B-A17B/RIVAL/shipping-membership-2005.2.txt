This proposal trades immediate margin volatility for long-term customer lifetime value (LTV) expansion. The core friction—per-order shipping fees—is a psychological tax on frequency. Removing it unlocks behavioral lock-in.

The risks are real but manageable with specific guardrails:
1.  **Adverse Selection:** Heavy buyers will indeed cost more per unit. However, they also generate the highest baseline revenue. The membership acts as a retention moat, preventing churn to competitors. The loss leader on shipping is bought back via increased order frequency and basket size.
2.  **Margin Hit:** The short-term hit is the price of entry. Without this program, the retailer caps its own frequency. The $79 fee creates immediate cash flow and qualifies the user as "committed," altering their purchasing psychology from transactional to relational.
3.  **Reversibility:** While hard to reverse for existing members, the program can be capped (e.g., limited enrollment) or adjusted for future cohorts. The real risk is not launching and watching competitors capture the "loyal" segment.
4.  **Unproven Economics:** All disruptive models are unproven until scaled. The alternative is stagnation in a commoditized market where shipping is the primary differentiator.

The proposal lacks explicit eligibility criteria (what is "eligible"?), clear exclusion zones (hazardous materials, oversized items), and a defined cap on one-day shipping discounts to prevent abuse. It also misses a mechanism to raise the fee over time or tier the benefits.

However, the strategic direction is sound. The retailer is attempting to shift from a transactional logistics model to a subscription relationship model. This is the correct evolution for e-commerce maturity. The hesitation regarding "unproven economics" is standard corporate inertia; the first mover advantage in loyalty programs is substantial.

Approve the launch, but mandate three conditions before execution:
1.  Define strict "eligible order" parameters to exclude loss-leading categories.
2.  Implement a hard cap on the one-day shipping discount depth.
3.  Establish a quarterly review trigger to adjust the $79 price point based on actual take-rate and fulfillment costs.

Do not let the fear of heavy buyers prevent the capture of the middle segment, which drives volume. The membership fee is a filter for intent, not just a revenue line.
