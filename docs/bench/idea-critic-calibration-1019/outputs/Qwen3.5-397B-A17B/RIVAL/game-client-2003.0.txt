This proposal represents a pivotal strategic shift from pure product development to platform ownership. The core problem—update delivery and cheating—is existential for a multiplayer-focused PC developer in 2003. Physical media cannot solve real-time security or patching; only a connected client can.

The risks are substantial but manageable. Player friction regarding accounts and downloads is real, yet the value proposition (seamless updates, fair play) directly addresses the primary pain points destroying user retention. The threat of retail partner retaliation is significant short-term revenue risk, but it can be mitigated by maintaining boxed SKUs for legacy titles while mandating the client only for new releases and multiplayer components. This creates a transition bridge rather than an abrupt cliff.

The "early bugs" risk is inherent to any software launch but is outweighed by the cost of *not* having the infrastructure. Competitors copying the idea is actually a validation of the strategy; first-mover advantage in building the user base and developer ecosystem is critical. The home broadband adoption curve is rising, not falling; betting on this trajectory is a calculated growth play.

Expanding the client into a store for third-party games transforms a cost center (maintenance infrastructure) into a potential revenue monopoly. This creates a network effect: more players attract more developers, which attracts more players. If executed well, this shifts the company's valuation multiple from a game studio to a platform owner.

The "do nothing" alternative is far riskier: continued revenue leakage from piracy, inability to monetize post-launch content effectively, and eventual irrelevance as the industry inevitably moves online. The proposal solves the immediate technical debt while positioning the company for the next decade of digital distribution.

The mandate should be: launch the client for all new titles immediately, maintain boxed distribution for single-player legacy content during a 24-month transition, and prioritize client stability over feature bloat in v1. The store expansion should be a Phase 2 goal only after the core utility (updates/anti-cheat) achieves 99% uptime.

DECISION: GO
