The proposal to launch a clear cola nationwide in 1992 carries significant strategic risk that outweighs the positive test market data. While the "purer alternative" positioning is innovative, the fundamental assumption that consumers will decouple cola flavor from dark color is unproven at scale. History suggests color is a primary heuristic for flavor expectation; violating this creates cognitive dissonance that often kills repeat purchases.

The reliance on test markets is dangerous here. Test environments often inflate novelty demand, masking the critical metric: long-term retention. If the product is perceived as a gimmick rather than a genuine improvement, the initial surge will collapse, leaving expensive shelf space and marketing spend wasted.

Cannibalization is the most immediate financial threat. Without a distinct functional benefit beyond clarity, the clear cola will likely steal share from the flagship brand rather than expanding the total user base. This dilutes marketing efficiency and confuses the brand identity. Furthermore, the low barrier to entry means rivals can quickly launch copycats, turning a first-mover advantage into a crowded, low-margin segment.

The risks identified—novelty fade, cannibalization, and competitive response—are not minor hurdles; they are existential threats to the brand's core equity. A nationwide launch with a "large marketing campaign" locks the company into a high-cost trajectory before validating the repeat purchase rate. The proposal lacks a phased approach to mitigate these specific failures. It assumes the test market success translates directly to national loyalty, ignoring the difference between trial and habit.

Before committing national resources, the company must validate that the clear cola survives the novelty curve and does not erode the flagship's dominance. The current plan moves too fast, betting the brand's clarity on an unproven consumer behavior shift. The potential downside of confusing the core brand and wasting capital on a fleeting trend exceeds the upside of a successful extension at this stage.

DECISION: NO-GO
