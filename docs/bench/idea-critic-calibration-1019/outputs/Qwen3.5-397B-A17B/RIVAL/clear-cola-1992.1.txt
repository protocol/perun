The proposal to launch a clear cola nationwide in 1992 carries significant strategic risk despite positive test market data. The core assumption—that "purity" translates to a desirable cola experience—conflicts with established consumer psychology where dark color signals rich, authentic cola flavor. Removing color may inadvertently signal "diet" or "weak" to the mainstream base, alienating loyalists without guaranteeing new adoption.

Test markets are notoriously unreliable for novelty products. Early adopters drawn to the gimmick of "clear" often fail to convert into repeat buyers once the novelty fades. If the product lacks a distinct taste advantage beyond its appearance, retention will collapse post-launch. Furthermore, a national rollout with a "large marketing campaign" locks the company into high fixed costs before proving unit economics or repeat purchase rates.

Cannibalization is the most immediate financial threat. Without a clear differentiation in taste profile, the clear cola will likely steal share from the flagship brand rather than expanding the total user base. This dilutes marketing efficiency and confuses the brand portfolio. Additionally, the barrier to entry for competitors is low; if the concept gains traction, rivals can quickly deploy copycats, turning a first-mover advantage into a shared commodity battle with compressed margins.

The risks outlined—flavor-color association, low repeat rates, cannibalization, inflated test data, and competitive response—form a cohesive argument against a full national launch at this stage. The positive test market response is insufficient evidence to override these structural vulnerabilities. A "go" decision now ignores the high probability of a costly failure that could damage the flagship brand's equity.

A prudent path forward requires de-risking the novelty factor and validating repeat purchases in a broader, longer-duration pilot before committing to national distribution and massive ad spend. The current proposal skips these critical validation steps.

DECISION: NO-GO
