This proposal represents a classic brand-extension trap: attempting to stretch a value-oriented, family-focused brand into a premium adult segment without addressing the fundamental brand equity mismatch. The strategy relies entirely on spending power to override deeply ingrained consumer perceptions, which is historically inefficient and often catastrophic for quick-service restaurants (QSR).

**Critical Flaws:**

1.  **Brand Dissonance:** The chain's core identity is "fun for kids" and "value." Adults do not visit this ecosystem seeking premium culinary experiences; they visit for nostalgia or convenience while managing children. A premium burger creates cognitive dissonance. You cannot advertise your way out of a brand position that says "cheap and fun" to suddenly mean "expensive and sophisticated."
2.  **Price Elasticity Mismatch:** The core customer base is price-sensitive. Introducing a high-price item alienates the loyal base without guaranteeing acquisition of the target adult demographic, who have established preferences for competitors already positioned as premium or casual dining.
3.  **Financial Asymmetry:** The proposal bets the farm on the "largest advertising budget" before validating product-market fit. This is a "build it and they will come" strategy applied to a saturated market. If the product fails to resonate (likely, given the brand mismatch), the sunk cost in marketing will be unrecoverable, damaging margins for years.
4.  **Operational Distraction:** Launching a premium line often complicates kitchen operations, slowing down the core high-volume service that drives QSR profitability.

**The Verdict:**
The risk profile is unacceptable. The plan assumes that perception is malleable through sheer volume of spend, ignoring that brand trust is built on consistency, not just awareness. A better approach would be incremental: test a limited-time adult offering with modest support to validate demand before committing to a permanent menu change and massive media buy. Proceeding as proposed invites a high-visibility failure that could tarnish the core brand's reputation for value.

DECISION: NO-GO
