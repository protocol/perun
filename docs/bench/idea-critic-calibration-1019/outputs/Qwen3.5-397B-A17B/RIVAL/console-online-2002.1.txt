The proposal demands a fundamental shift from selling hardware to operating a persistent service platform. In 2002, broadband penetration is critically low, meaning the addressable market for this mandatory service is a fraction of the console's installed base. Charging for online access when competitors offer it free or decentralized creates immediate friction. Players accustomed to free LAN parties or publisher-hosted servers will resist a double-pay model (console + subscription).

The operational risk is severe. The company has no history of running large-scale hosting or 24/7 support operations. Building this infrastructure from scratch while simultaneously launching hardware divides focus and capital. If the service suffers outages or latency issues at launch, it damages the core hardware brand, not just a software title.

However, the strategic upside justifies the pain. A unified identity and friends list create a "walled garden" that locks users into the ecosystem. Once a player's social graph and reputation are built on this network, switching consoles becomes socially costly. This transforms the console from a commodity device into a sticky platform. Competitors offering fragmented, free experiences cannot match the convenience of a seamless, console-wide layer.

The "few homes have broadband" risk is actually a first-mover opportunity. By forcing the issue now, the company positions itself as the premium, future-proof choice. As broadband adoption inevitably grows, this service becomes the default standard, leaving rivals playing catch-up. The subscription model also creates recurring revenue, smoothing out the volatile boom-bust cycle of hardware sales.

The execution risk is real but manageable if treated as an existential priority. The company must hire operational talent immediately and perhaps partner with established ISPs or hosting providers rather than building everything in-house. The "mandatory" nature is the key differentiator; optional services fail to create network effects.

Despite the high risk of early adoption friction and operational inexperience, the long-term moat created by a unified, paid network is too valuable to ignore. This is a bet on the future of connectivity, not just current market conditions.

DECISION: GO
