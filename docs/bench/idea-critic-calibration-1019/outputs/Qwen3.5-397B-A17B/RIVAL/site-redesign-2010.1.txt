This proposal fails on three critical dimensions: community trust, engineering safety, and competitive timing.

First, the community is the asset. They value the existing voting mechanics. Changing how stories surface and explicitly favoring publisher content over community curation directly contradicts the core value proposition that built the loyal user base. In 2010, users are already sensitive to "sellout" behavior. Favoring publishers looks like monetization at the expense of user voice. This risks an immediate exodus to the growing rival, effectively handing them your market share overnight.

Second, the "big bang" rewrite strategy is archaic and reckless. A full rewrite from scratch introduces massive unknown engineering risks. Moving all users at once eliminates the ability to test, learn, or rollback. If the new algorithm depresses engagement or the new codebase crumbles under load, there is no fallback. You lose everything in a single moment.

Third, the timing is wrong. With traffic already slowing and a rival growing, you need quick wins and validated learning, not a months-long black box development cycle. A rewrite delays feedback until it is too late.

The proposal solves the wrong problem. Slowing traffic might be due to content quality, site performance, or lack of new features, not necessarily the ranking algorithm itself. Favoring publishers might solve a revenue problem but creates a user retention problem.

A viable path would involve:
1.  **Incremental Iteration:** A/B test ranking changes on a small traffic segment to measure impact before full rollout.
2.  **Community Involvement:** Engage power users in the design process to ensure changes respect community norms.
3.  **Modular Updates:** Improve the front end and backend incrementally rather than risking a total rewrite.
4.  **Data-Driven Decisions:** Validate the "publisher content" hypothesis with small experiments before betting the company on it.

As proposed, this plan bets the entire company on an unproven algorithm, alienates the core user base, and removes all safety nets. It is a high-risk gamble with a high probability of catastrophic failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
