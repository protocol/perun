**Steelman:** A simultaneous, unannounced rebrand shocks the market into perceiving the retailer as modern, avoiding the "slow death" of a transitional phase.

**Attack:** This plan ignores fundamental brand equity mechanics. The proposal admits loyal customers are attached to the old logo and that recognition relies on the specific dark blue square. Removing the primary visual anchor overnight, without testing or announcement, guarantees confusion rather than contemporary appeal. In 2010, social media amplifies consumer outrage instantly; a "surprise" deletion of a heritage symbol will be framed as corporate arrogance, not innovation.

**Better Way:** A phased approach allows for customer feedback and adjustment. Test the new wordmark in select markets or digital channels first. Announce the evolution as a respect for heritage while embracing the future, inviting customers on the journey rather than alienating them.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch failed. Cause: Core customers felt betrayed by the erasure of a symbol they identified with, leading to a boycott and negative viral campaigns highlighting the "sterile" new look. The cost of delay (waiting to test) is negligible compared to the cost of a botched global rollout requiring a revert.

**Fit:** The goal is to look "contemporary," not to erase history. Contemporary brands often leverage heritage; they do not blindly discard it. The proposal conflates "new" with "modern."

**Evidence & Calibration:** The risks raised in the prompt (attachment, recognition, lack of testing) are factual premises provided in the scenario. The proposal offers no mitigation for these specific, admitted vulnerabilities. There is no kill criteria or pilot plan. The "all at once" strategy creates a one-way door with high reversal costs if backlash occurs.

**Conclusion:** The plan relies on a gamble that shock equals value. Given the explicit risks of alienating the core base and the absence of any validation step, the probability of damaging brand equity is unacceptably high. The proposal fails to beat the "do nothing" or "test first" alternatives.
