The claim is that acquiring a small, modern OS vendor is the viable path to rescue a failing PC maker with a dated platform. The steelman: this is the only available asset that provides a modern, object-oriented foundation quickly enough to halt market share erosion, and the founder's return is essential for technical continuity despite past friction.

Alternatives were weighed. "Do nothing" guarantees continued decline given two prior internal failures. Licensing another vendor's system surrenders control of the core differentiator and margins, potentially accelerating commoditization. Building from scratch again has already failed twice; a third attempt risks total collapse before completion. The acquisition cost, while high relative to current revenue, is a buy-price on future capability, not current cash flow.

The pre-mortem assumes the integration fails because of cultural clash between the acquirer and the difficult founder, or because the developer ecosystem refuses to migrate. However, the alternative (death by stagnation) is a certainty, whereas this path offers a non-zero probability of revival. The risk of the founder's history is mitigated by limiting their role to "advisor" rather than immediate operational command. The low unit sales of the target are irrelevant if the technology architecture is superior and portable, which is the specific asset being purchased.

The strongest attack is that the price is unjustifiable for a company with negligible revenue. This fails because the valuation is based on strategic necessity and the cost of delay (continued market share loss), not traditional multiples. There is no cheaper, safer path that solves the "modern core" requirement within the needed timeframe. The one-way door nature of this decision demands action; waiting for perfect data ensures the window closes entirely.

Kill criteria are implicit in the urgency: if the technology cannot be integrated within 12 months or the founder refuses to engage meaningfully, the capital is written off, but the alternative was already zero. The leading metric is the successful porting of key applications to the new core within the first year.

DECISION: GO
