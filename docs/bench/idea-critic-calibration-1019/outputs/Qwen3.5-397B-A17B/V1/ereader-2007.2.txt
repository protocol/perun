The claim is that a non-hardware bookseller should launch a proprietary e-reader with free cellular connectivity to dominate the emerging digital market. Steelman: Only a seamless, device-agnostic ecosystem can overcome piracy fears and previous adoption failures by removing friction entirely.

However, critical flaws exist. First, the company lacks hardware DNA; designing reliable consumer electronics while managing a global supply chain is a distinct competency gap that historically destroys retailers who attempt it. Second, the economic model is dangerous: subsidizing both the device hardware and perpetual cellular data while charging low prices per title creates a negative margin loop unless volume is instantaneous and massive. Third, publishers, already fearful of piracy, may resist a platform where the retailer controls the entire pipe, potentially withholding key titles.

Alternatives include licensing existing hardware or partnering with device makers to sell content only, avoiding capital expenditure and operational risk. The "do nothing" option preserves current print margins while waiting for the hardware market to mature.

Pre-mortem: The device launches with buggy hardware, cellular costs balloon beyond revenue, publishers restrict catalogs due to pricing pressure, and the unit economics collapse before reaching critical mass. This is a one-way door involving significant sunk costs in tooling and contracts. The cost of delay is low; the market is nascent, and being second allows learning from early adopters' mistakes without bearing the R&D burden.

The strongest attack is the competency mismatch combined with the unproven unit economics. While the vision is compelling, the execution risk for a company with zero hardware experience is fatal without a pilot or partnership strategy. The proposal assumes the company can instantly master industrial design, manufacturing, and telecom negotiations while simultaneously restructuring publisher relationships. This is not a minor gap; it is a fundamental strategic overreach.

DECISION: NO-GO
