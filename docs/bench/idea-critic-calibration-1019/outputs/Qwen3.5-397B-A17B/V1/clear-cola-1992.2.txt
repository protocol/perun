**Steelman:** A clear cola leverages positive test data to capture health-conscious demand and extend the brand before competitors act.

**Attack:** The core failure mode is category confusion. Cola flavor is psychologically anchored to dark color; a clear liquid signals "citrus" or "water," creating a cognitive dissonance that kills repeat purchases after the initial novelty fades. Test markets notoriously overstate demand for novel visuals while understating the importance of habit. The risk of cannibalizing the flagship—a high-margin, stable cash cow—is severe if the new product fails to retain users. Furthermore, a "purer" claim invites regulatory scrutiny and competitor counter-attacks on ingredients. The proposal lacks a specific kill criterion for repeat purchase rates, relying instead on broad "positive response" metrics that likely measure curiosity, not loyalty.

**Better Way:** Instead of a costly nationwide launch, execute a staged regional rollout with a strict "repeat purchase" gate. If 30-day repurchase rates do not match the flagship within six months, terminate the campaign immediately to protect brand equity. Alternatively, license the formula to a smaller player to test the waters without risking the parent brand's shelf space.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Assuming the goal is growth, this bet ignores the "do nothing" option of optimizing the core portfolio. If launched nationwide and failed, the brand suffers a dual loss: wasted marketing capital and diluted identity. The "one-way door" nature of a massive launch makes reversal expensive and reputationally damaging.

**Decision:** The proposal treats a novelty spike as sustainable demand and ignores the psychological barrier of color-flavor association. Without a proven repeat-purchase mechanism and a capped, reversible trial, the risk of cannibalization and brand dilution outweighs the speculative upside. The lack of a defined stop-condition for repurchase failure is fatal.

DECISION: NO-GO
