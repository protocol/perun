**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to validate demand, refine the product based on early adopter feedback, and establish a premium brand before a general release, mitigating initial production risks.

**Attack:** The proposal fails because it treats severe, structural flaws as mere "risks" to be managed rather than fatal blockers for a mass-market consumer launch.
1.  **Privacy & Social Acceptance:** A always-on camera in a social device creates immediate, visceral hostility ("Glassholes"), leading to bans in venues and social ostracization. This is not a tweakable feature; it is a fundamental rejection of the form factor by the general public.
2.  **Value Proposition:** The proposal admits "no clear everyday use for most people." Selling a $1,500 solution without a defined problem is a guaranteed market failure. Early adopters tolerate bugs, not uselessness.
3.  **Battery Life:** Short battery life in an "everyday" device renders it non-functional for its intended purpose, causing immediate churn.
4.  **Pricing:** $1,500 is a luxury price point for an unproven category with no software ecosystem, limiting the "early adopter" pool to tech speculators rather than genuine users.

**Better Way:** Do not position this as an "everyday consumer product." Pivot immediately to a strictly enterprise/industrial B2B model (logistics, manufacturing, medical) where the camera adds verifiable value, privacy concerns are contractually managed, and the high price is justified by productivity gains. Alternatively, halt the consumer launch entirely until battery technology and use cases mature.

**Kill Criteria:** If social backlash results in venue bans within 3 months, or if daily active usage drops below 2 hours due to battery/utility issues, the consumer program must be terminated. The current plan lacks these tripwires and assumes acceptance that history proves will not happen.

The evidence provided (no clear use, privacy objections, social friction) confirms this specific plan—selling to the general public as an everyday item—will result in reputational damage and financial loss. The "invitation-only" tactic is insufficient to overcome the fundamental lack of product-market fit described.

DECISION: NO-GO
