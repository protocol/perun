**Steelman:** Aggressive expansion via IPO will secure market dominance through scale, brand visibility, and competitor elimination, accepting short-term losses for long-term monopoly power.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on capital markets remaining open despite negative unit economics. Selling products below cost while shipping heavy, low-margin items creates a "burn multiplier": every additional sale increases total losses. A national TV campaign and warehouse build-out lock in massive fixed costs precisely when variable margins are negative. If capital markets tighten (a stated risk), the company cannot fund operations or debt service, leading to immediate insolvency. Buying a competitor adds their losses to an already unsustainable model. This is not a path to dominance; it is a mechanism to accelerate bankruptcy by scaling inefficiency.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing/Status Quo:** Continue organic growth; preserve cash; fix unit economics before scaling.
2.  **Pivot to Profitability:** Raise prices or optimize logistics to achieve positive gross margins before seeking IPO.
3.  **Targeted Acquisition:** Buy the competitor only after stabilizing own margins, using stock rather than cash if possible.

**Pre-mortem:** The IPO fails due to investor rejection of negative margins. The company runs out of venture funding while committed to expensive TV contracts and warehouse leases. Forced to sell assets at fire-sale prices or liquidate.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   **Metric:** Positive gross margin per unit (including shipping).
*   **Threshold:** Must be achieved *before* IPO filing.
*   **Stop Action:** If margins remain negative by Q3 2000, cancel IPO and halt expansion.

**Verdict:** The plan violates basic financial sustainability. Scaling a business with negative gross margins and high fixed-cost expansion is fatal if funding pauses. The risks outweigh any theoretical first-mover advantage.

**Dissent Ledger:** Proponents argue speed is essential to win the category. However, speed without a viable economic model ensures the category is won by a survivor, not this specific entity.

**Remaining Risk:** Competitors may achieve profitability first and capture the market while this company burns cash.

DECISION: NO-GO
