The claim is that a non-hardware bookseller should launch a subsidized e-reader with free cellular data to dominate the emerging digital market. The steelman: only a seamless, device-plus-content bundle removes the friction that killed earlier e-readers, and owning the hardware is the only way to guarantee the user experience required to convince publishers to authorize digital titles despite piracy fears.

Alternatives include licensing software to existing device makers (lower risk, but no control over experience or pricing) or waiting for the market to mature (low cost, but cedes the ecosystem to competitors). Doing nothing preserves margins but risks irrelevance if digital shifts consumer behavior.

This is a one-way door with high capital exposure. The pre-mortem reveals failure modes: hardware supply chain disasters, unsustainable cellular subsidies, and publisher revolts over pricing. However, the "cannibalization" fear is a fallacy; if the company does not cannibalize its print sales with its own digital ecosystem, a competitor will. The margin squeeze is real but acts as a moat; the goal is lifetime customer value, not per-unit hardware profit. The lack of hardware experience is a critical gap, but the proposal implicitly requires partnering with manufacturers rather than building factories, mitigating this risk. The free cellular connection is the key differentiator that solves the "friction" problem identified in previous failures.

The strongest attack is the financial bleed from subsidizing both the device and the data. This fails only if the attach rate of book purchases per device is low. Given the company's massive catalog and customer base, the probability of low adoption is mitigated by the convenience factor. The risk of publisher pushback is neutralized by the promise of DRM and a unified low-price point that expands the total market.

The decision hinges on whether the company can execute the hardware partnership. Assuming they can outsource manufacturing, the strategic necessity of owning the customer interface outweighs the operational risks.

DECISION: GO
