The claim is that a mandatory online client will solve update/cheating issues and create a new store revenue stream. Steelman: Centralizing delivery and verification is the only scalable path to protect game integrity and capture platform value, outweighing short-term friction.

**Skeptic:** The premise assumes universal broadband in 2003, which is false; many target users have dial-up or no internet, making mandatory online checks a hard block for sales. Retail partners, currently the primary distribution channel, may retaliate by delisting boxed games if the client undermines their role, causing immediate revenue collapse before the store matures.

**Better-way:** The "do nothing" alternative maintains retail relationships and avoids alienating offline users. A superior middle path is an optional client: offer incentives (bonus content, faster patches) for adoption without mandating it, allowing broadband penetration to grow naturally while preserving boxed sales.

**Kill-criteria:** If retail partners representing >30% of current revenue threaten to drop the titles, or if beta testing shows >15% of users cannot complete the mandatory download within 2 hours, the initiative fails. The one-way door is burning retail bridges before the store has inventory; the cost of delay is losing the first-mover advantage in digital distribution to competitors.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because the company overestimates 2003 infrastructure readiness and underestimates retail leverage. The mandatory nature triggers a consumer backlash and retailer boycott, starving the company of cash flow before the store can monetize third-party titles. The "anti-cheat" benefit is nullified if the player base shrinks due to access barriers.

**Decision:** The proposal ignores critical 2003 constraints (bandwidth, retail dependence) and mandates a high-friction solution where an optional, incentive-driven approach would de-risk adoption. The fatal flaw is the "mandatory" requirement in an era of limited connectivity, which threatens the core boxed-games revenue stream without a guaranteed store payoff.

DECISION: NO-GO
