**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 validates demand for a head-worn camera while mitigating initial privacy backlash through exclusivity, aiming to normalize the device as an everyday consumer product.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The proposal fails the "everyday consumer" fit test. In 2013, a $1,500 price point excludes the mass market required to establish social norms, yet the "everyday" positioning invites immediate hostility from the general public who cannot afford it but must tolerate its surveillance. The "invitation-only" gate contradicts the goal of broad social acceptance; it creates an elite class of recorders, intensifying resentment rather than normalizing usage.

*Do Nothing:* Avoids brand damage from a failed mass-market promise and privacy scandals.
*Alternative (Enterprise First):* Target specific industrial or medical workflows where the camera adds provable value and privacy is controlled. This justifies the high cost and isolates social friction.
*Alternative (Accessory First):* Release the camera module for smartphones to test utility without the social friction of a face-mounted screen.

**Pre-mortem:**
Assume failure: The device launches as "consumer," but battery life forces it to be a "occasional novelty." Early adopters wear it in sensitive spaces (gyms, restrooms), triggering immediate bans and media backlash labeled "Glassholes." The high price prevents the volume needed to lower costs or improve battery tech. The company is forced to retreat to enterprise, having already poisoned the well for consumer wearables.

**Fit & Evidence:**
The proposal assumes social acceptance can be bought with exclusivity. History suggests the opposite: visible inequality in surveillance capability accelerates rejection. The risks (privacy, price, utility, social acceptance, battery) are not merely "raised"; they are fatal to the specific strategy of positioning this as an *everyday consumer product* immediately. The kill criterion is social normalization; the proposed path actively prevents it by creating a two-tier system of watchers and watched.

**Decision:**
The plan attempts to skip the necessary "tool/niche" phase and jump straight to "lifestyle essential" without solving the core utility or power constraints. The privacy and social risks are unmanaged by an invitation list; they are exacerbated by it.

DECISION: NO-GO
