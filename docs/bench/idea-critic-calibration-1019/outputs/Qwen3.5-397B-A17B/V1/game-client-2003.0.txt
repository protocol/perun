The claim is that a mandatory online client will solve update/cheating issues and create a new store revenue stream. Steelman: A proprietary client centralizes distribution, enabling rapid patching, robust anti-cheat enforcement, and a scalable digital storefront, securing long-term recurring revenue despite short-term friction.

**Skeptic:** The premise assumes universal broadband in 2003, which is false; many target users have dial-up or no internet, making large mandatory downloads a hard barrier to entry. Retail partners, currently the primary sales channel, may retaliate by delisting boxed goods if the client undermines their value, causing an immediate revenue cliff before digital sales mature.

**Better-Way:** Instead of a mandatory gate, offer the client as an optional "premium" installer with auto-updates and anti-cheat, while maintaining boxed sales with manual patching (via web or mail) for offline users. This captures digital benefits without alienating the non-broadband majority or provoking retail partners immediately. Alternatively, license an existing anti-cheat solution rather than building a whole new platform.

**Kill-Criteria:** If retail partners representing >30% of current revenue threaten to drop the title within 3 months of announcement, or if beta testing shows >15% of users cannot complete the initial download due to connection failures, the mandatory requirement must be scrapped.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because the client launches with game-breaking bugs, driving piracy (cracked offline versions) and causing a PR backlash over privacy/connectivity, leading to a sales collapse that the new store cannot offset. This is a one-way door for brand reputation; once trust is lost, it is hard to regain. The cost of delay is low; waiting 12-18 months for broadband penetration to increase reduces the addressable market risk significantly.

**Verdict:** The proposal ignores the 2003 infrastructure reality and channel conflict. The "mandatory" aspect is fatal given the market conditions. The strategic goal (digital distribution) is sound, but the execution timing and compulsion are flawed.

DECISION: NO-GO
