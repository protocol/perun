**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can forcibly reposition a family brand as premium, overcoming adult skepticism through sheer visibility and a superior product.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests. First, brand perception is a deep structural constraint, not a messaging gap; adults visiting a "child-centered" chain for value are unlikely to accept a price point exceeding core menu items regardless of ad volume. Second, the strategy violates basic unit economics by committing to the "largest advertising budget" before validating demand. This creates a one-way door: if the premium positioning fails, the sunk cost in brand confusion and media spend is unrecoverable, while the core value proposition is diluted.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "staged pilot" alternative dominates. Test the premium burger in a limited adult-demographic market with modest support. If adults buy it without the massive spend, the product has merit; if they don't, the chain saves millions. Chesterton's Fence applies: the current focus on kids/value exists because that is the profitable core. Abandoning it for an unproven, expensive adult pivot ignores the established revenue engine.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. The chain spends millions, adults perceive the high price as inconsistent with the "fast/kids" brand identity, and regular value customers feel alienated by the shift in focus and pricing structure. The result is a confused brand that satisfies neither segment.

**Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks a stop condition. There is no metric defined to halt the spending if initial uptake is low. Committing to a fixed, record-breaking budget regardless of early performance data is financial negligence.

**Conclusion:** The plan relies on brute force (spending) to solve a strategic mismatch (brand fit). It ignores the cheaper, safer path of validation. The risk of brand dilution and capital loss outweighs the speculative gain.

DECISION: NO-GO
