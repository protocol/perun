The claim is that a mandatory online client will solve update/cheating issues and create a new store revenue stream, despite significant friction.

**Steelman:** Centralizing distribution via a proprietary client is the only viable long-term defense against piracy and cheating while securing a direct customer relationship, outweighing short-term retail friction.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Continue boxed sales. *Result:* Cheating persists, updates fail, revenue leaks to used game markets. High risk of product degradation.
2.  **Optional Patcher:** Release updates via website. *Result:* Fails to stop offline cheating; fragmented user base; no store leverage.
3.  **Mandatory Client (Proposal):** *Risks:* Retail revolt, broadband gaps (2003 context), user backlash. *Mitigation:* The "mandatory" aspect is the feature, not a bug; it forces the ecosystem shift required for anti-cheat efficacy. Retail partners dropping boxes is a painful but necessary transition to digital margins. Broadband adoption is accelerating; early adopters (the core gaming demographic) are already connected.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if the client launches with critical bugs that prevent playing purchased games, causing immediate mass refund demands and reputational collapse. However, the proposal acknowledges "early bugs" as a risk, implying mitigation planning. Competitors copying the idea validates the market opportunity rather than negating it; first-mover advantage in ecosystem lock-in is decisive.

**Fit:** This moves the north star from "selling boxes" to "owning the platform." It transforms a vulnerability (distribution/cheating) into a moat. The cost of delay is high: every month without anti-cheat erodes the multiplayer community, the primary asset.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:** The premise relies on the shift to broadband (observable trend) and the failure of current boxed models (stated fact). No fatal flaw exists where the risks cannot be managed as execution challenges rather than strategic dead-ends. The "retail drop" risk is actually a strategic acceleration toward higher-margin direct sales.

**Decision:** The proposal beats "do nothing" and "optional" approaches because only a mandatory gate solves the cheating problem fundamentally. The risks are executional, not existential. Proceed with a capped, time-boxed launch for new titles only to limit blast radius while validating the model.

DECISION: GO
