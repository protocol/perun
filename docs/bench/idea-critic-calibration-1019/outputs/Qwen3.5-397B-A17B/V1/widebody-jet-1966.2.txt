The claim is that committing to a massive, unproven wide-body jet will secure market dominance despite existential financial and technological risks. The steelman: this specific design (raised cockpit, high-bypass engines) creates a dual-use asset (passenger/freight) that locks in the largest customer and preempts competitors, justifying the unprecedented capital outlay.

However, the failure scenarios are catastrophic. The development and factory costs exceed all prior precedents, threatening bankruptcy if the single current order cancels or if the unproven engine fails. The market assumption—that subsonic travel will grow despite the anticipated shift to supersonic airliners—is a binary gamble; if supersonic transport becomes the standard, this massive investment becomes obsolete scrap. Relying on one airline for a project of this scale violates basic portfolio risk management. The "freighter conversion" feature is a valuable hedge, but it does not mitigate the immediate cash flow crisis of development or the risk that the engine supplier cannot deliver.

The "do nothing" alternative preserves capital while the supersonic question resolves. A "better way" would be to demand multiple launch customers before breaking ground on the factory or to co-develop the engine with government backing to share the technical risk. The proposal as stated requires betting the entire company on a single customer's forecast and an unproven supply chain in a shifting regulatory and technological landscape.

The strongest attack is the financial exposure: if the engine program slips or the sole customer defaults, the company faces insolvency before the first plane flies. The proposal acknowledges these risks but offers no concrete kill criteria, such as securing three additional launch orders or achieving a specific engine thrust milestone before full factory construction begins. Without these tripwires, the commitment is a blind leap rather than a managed strategy. The risk of building a "white elephant" factory for a technology that may be superseded by supersonic flight is too high to approve without immediate risk-sharing mechanisms.

DECISION: NO-GO
