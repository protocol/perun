**Steelman:** Leverage existing compact mechanics to rapidly deliver a stylish, customizable coupe/convertible at a low entry price, targeting young buyers with massive advertising to create a new market segment.

**Critique:**
1.  **Assumptions:** Assumes "dull" reliability is a sufficient base for "sporty" perception. If the shared chassis feels too economy-grade despite styling, the brand risks cheapening its image without gaining performance credibility.
2.  **Better Ways:** A "do nothing" approach preserves capital but cedes the emerging youth demographic to competitors. A smaller pilot launch reduces financial exposure but sacrifices the cultural impact required to define a new category. No cheaper alternative exists to achieve the necessary market penetration speed.
3.  **Fit:** Directly targets the identified demographic shift (postwar boomers). The long hood/short deck styling signals sportiness without expensive re-engineering.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the "cheap" parts perception overrides the styling, or if the advertising spend fails to convert interest into volume sales quickly enough to cover high fixed marketing costs. The one-way door is the massive ad spend; the product itself is reversible via production adjustments.
5.  **Evidence:** The proposal relies on demographic data (young buyers entering market) and existing mechanical assets. The risk of unproven demand is mitigated by the low base price and high customization, allowing price-sensitive buyers to enter while enthusiasts add value.
6.  **Kill Criteria:** If initial pre-orders or first-month sales do not cover 50% of the launch ad spend, halt further advertising expansion. The leading metric is units sold per advertising dollar in the first quarter.

**Verdict:** The strategy effectively balances risk (using proven mechanics) with reward (capturing a new demographic via styling and marketing). The "cheap" risk is offset by the low entry price point, which aligns with the target audience's budget. The heavy ad spend is a calculated bet on category creation, which is necessary given the unproven nature of the market. The potential reward of defining a segment outweighs the risk of moderate failure, provided the kill criteria are monitored.

DECISION: GO
