**Steelman:** Differentiate via novel interaction and price rather than raw power to expand the market beyond core gamers while maintaining legacy support.

**Skeptic:** The dual-screen form factor risks developer confusion and increased manufacturing costs. If the touch interface is seen as a gimmick without compelling software, the hardware advantage of the rival (graphics/media) will dominate consumer choice. The assumption that "unproven" touch games will succeed is a significant leap without guaranteed first-party titles.

**Better-Way:** The alternative is matching the rival's specs, but this leads to a price war against a well-funded competitor with superior tech, likely resulting in margin erosion and loss of core identity. "Do nothing" cedes the high-end market. The proposed path is the only one offering distinct value, provided the software library supports the hardware uniqueness immediately.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This fits a strategy of market expansion (blue ocean) rather than direct competition. Failure mode: Developers ignore the second screen, making the device a costly, underpowered single-screen console. The "gimmick" perception becomes reality if launch titles do not demonstrate unique gameplay impossible on the rival's machine.

**Evidence & Calibration:** The proposal relies on the unproven premise that touch/dual-screen appeals to a broader audience. However, the risk of competing directly on graphics against a "well-funded" rival with "stronger graphics" is a near-certain loss. The proposed mitigation (keeping the older handheld) protects the core base while testing the new hypothesis.

**Kill Criteria:** The fatal flaw would be launching without confirmed software that *requires* two screens. However, the proposal itself identifies the hardware strategy, not the software execution. As a strategic direction, it avoids the trap of direct hardware competition. The risks (developer confusion, gimmick perception) are manageable through strong first-party curation, which is within the company's control. There is no evidence provided that the cost increase makes the lower price point impossible.

**Decision:** The proposal correctly identifies that winning requires changing the rules of engagement, not playing the rival's game better. The risks are inherent to innovation but preferable to the high probability of failure in a direct spec-for-spec battle.

DECISION: GO
