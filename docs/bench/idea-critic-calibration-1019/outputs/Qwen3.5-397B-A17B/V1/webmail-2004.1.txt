The claim is that offering 1,000x more storage than incumbents, funded by content-matched ads, will disrupt the mature email market despite high infrastructure costs and privacy risks.

**Steelman:** Only a massive leap in utility (1GB vs. MB) combined with perfect search can dislodge entrenched users, and ad-relevance is the only viable monetization path for a free tier at this scale.

**Skeptic:** The premise assumes storage costs will drop fast enough to sustain 1GB/user before revenue scales. If hardware costs remain static, the burn rate will be catastrophic. Privacy scanning may trigger regulatory backlash or user exodus before network effects kick in. The "invitation-only" gate slows adoption, potentially starving the ad algorithm of data needed for relevance.

**Better Way:** Instead of 1GB immediately, launch with 500MB to halve initial capex while still dominating competitors. Alternatively, license the search tech to existing providers rather than building a full service. However, "do nothing" guarantees ceding the data moat to rivals; a smaller tier risks being perceived as "just another webmail."

**Kill Criteria:** If cost-per-user exceeds ad-yield by 200% after six months, or if privacy complaints exceed 1% of active users, the model fails. The one-way door is building custom data centers; the two-way door is the ad-algorithm tuning.

**Pre-mortem:** The service fails because advertisers boycott "scanning" emails, or because the cost of spinning up servers outpaces the viral invite rate. The "mature market" defense holds only if the product is merely incremental; at 1GB, it is a category shift.

**Decision:** The risk of being late to the data-game outweighs the infrastructure bet. Storage costs historically follow Moore's Law; waiting guarantees obsolescence. The privacy risk is real but manageable via transparency. The "invitation" strategy creates artificial scarcity to drive demand, offsetting the slow start. No fatal flaw exists that cannot be mitigated by the sheer magnitude of the value prop.

DECISION: GO
