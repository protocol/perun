The claim is that an untested, surprise logo replacement will successfully modernize a mature brand without triggering destructive backlash. The steelman is that a decisive, clean break signals contemporary intent faster than iterative change, betting that design quality overrides attachment.

However, the proposal fails the pre-mortem. Assuming the rollout has already occurred: loyal customers, confused by the sudden disappearance of their recognized symbol (the blue square), flood social media. Without prior testing, the company cannot distinguish between "resistance to change" and "genuine loss of brand equity." The "no advance announcement" strategy removes the ability to manage the narrative or prepare customer support, turning a branding update into a crisis. The risk is not just aesthetic dislike; it is the immediate erosion of trust in a mature retailer where consistency is a primary value.

Alternatives exist that dominate this approach on risk and reversibility. A phased rollout (online first) allows for real-time sentiment analysis. A "soft launch" with community engagement tests the "contemporary" hypothesis without burning the bridge to the past. The "do nothing" option retains current equity while a proper testing regimen is conducted. The proposed "big bang" approach is a one-way door with no safety net; once the old logos are replaced everywhere, the cost of reversal is massive and admits failure publicly.

The proposal lacks any kill criteria or leading metrics. It assumes the redesign works without evidence. In a mature market, alienating the core base to chase a vague "contemporary" label without validation is speculative gambling, not strategy. The absence of customer testing is a fatal flaw for a brand whose primary asset is recognition. The plan prioritizes the executive desire for a "fresh start" over the economic reality of customer retention.

DECISION: NO-GO
