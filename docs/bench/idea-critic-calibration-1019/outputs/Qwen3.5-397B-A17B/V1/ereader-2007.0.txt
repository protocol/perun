The claim is that a non-hardware bookseller should launch a proprietary e-reader with free cellular connectivity to dominate the emerging digital market. The steelman: owning the device and distribution pipe creates a defensible moat, removing friction for customers and locking them into the store, which outweighs hardware risks.

**Skeptic:** The company lacks hardware DNA; execution failure could bankrupt the venture. Publishers, fearing piracy and margin erosion, may withhold content, rendering the device useless. Free cellular data creates a massive, uncapped operating expense that could destroy unit economics if adoption scales.

**Better-way:** License existing hardware or partner with an established manufacturer to reduce capex and risk. Wait for the market to mature rather than pioneering it. However, "do nothing" cedes the digital future to competitors. Partnering dilutes the integrated experience that drives store loyalty. The proposed vertical integration, while risky, is the only path to controlling the entire customer journey.

**Kill-criteria:** If publisher buy-in is not secured for at least 100,000 titles before mass production, halt. If the cost per device exceeds the projected lifetime customer value from book sales, pivot. The one-way door is heavy manufacturing investment; the two-way door is the cellular plan terms.

**Pre-mortem:** The device fails because the screen is too slow, the battery dies quickly, or the store lacks bestsellers. The risk of cannibalizing print is real but irrelevant if the alternative is losing the customer entirely to a digital-native competitor. The "low price" strategy is essential to drive volume and subsidize the ecosystem.

**Decision:** The risks are severe but manageable through phased rollout and strict vendor management. The strategic necessity of owning the digital shelf space dominates the fear of hardware execution. The free cellular connection is the killer feature that removes the biggest barrier to entry: friction. Without it, adoption stalls. The potential to define the market justifies the bet.

DECISION: GO
