**Steelman:** The brand must modernize its visual identity to signal freshness and premium quality, betting that a bold, minimalist redesign will attract new buyers without alienating the core base, provided the recipe and price remain unchanged.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the primary function of the current packaging: instant shelf recognition. The "orange with a straw" is a deeply ingrained heuristic for the product itself, especially for the stated older, loyal demographic. Removing this cue creates a high probability of "shelf blindness," where existing customers simply walk past the product, mistaking it for a new or different brand. The risk is not just confusion; it is immediate sales collapse before advertising can retrain consumer behavior. A nationwide, "big bang" launch eliminates the ability to test this hypothesis or mitigate losses regionally. The cost of re-educating the entire market simultaneously outweighs the aesthetic benefits of minimalism.

**Better Way:** A phased approach is superior. Test the new design in a limited geographic market or specific retail chain while maintaining the old design elsewhere. Measure sell-through rates and customer feedback specifically regarding recognition. If recognition drops, iterate on the design (e.g., reintroduce the orange element) before a national rollout. Alternatively, evolve the current design gradually rather than executing a hard break.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot test shows a >5% drop in repeat purchase rate among existing customers within the first 4 weeks, or if shelf-dwell time increases (indicating confusion) without a conversion lift, the redesign must be halted or reverted.

**Verdict:** The proposal fails the "do no harm" test for a mature brand with high loyalty. The risk of severing the visual link to the product for the core demographic is too high to justify a simultaneous nationwide launch without prior validation. The lack of a pilot phase makes this an unacceptable gamble with the brand's primary revenue stream.

DECISION: NO-GO
