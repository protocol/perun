The claim is that a mandatory, paid, broadband-only online service will succeed despite market norms of free, publisher-run multiplayer and low broadband adoption in 2002.

Steelman: A unified, high-quality service creates a premium ecosystem that differentiates the console, ensures security, and generates recurring revenue, justifying the friction if the user experience is vastly superior to fragmented alternatives.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current proposal's rigidity. First, the "broadband required" constraint ignores the reality of 2002, where dial-up is dominant; excluding the majority of the installed base at launch creates an immediate network effect failure. Second, a "mandatory subscription" for a feature competitors offer free is a severe value proposition gap without a proven library of "system-seller" games that strictly require this infrastructure. Third, the company lacks operational history in hosting; scaling this globally without prior stress testing risks catastrophic outages that would brand the console as broken.

The "do nothing" or "hybrid" alternative is stronger: offer the service as an optional premium layer while allowing free, publisher-managed play over dial-up to build the user base. This preserves the vision of a unified identity without the fatal adoption barrier.

The proposal fails the "fit" test against the current market stage; it is a one-way door that locks out the majority of potential users before the network effect can start. The cost of delay (waiting for broadband penetration) is lower than the cost of a failed launch due to an empty matchmaking pool. The risks of operational inexperience combined with a restrictive access model create a concrete failure scenario: high churn and negative press within six months due to low player counts and connection issues.

To proceed, the plan must be revised to include a dial-up fallback and a free tier to seed the network. As proposed, with mandatory broadband and fees, the risk of market rejection is too high.

DECISION: NO-GO
