**Steelman:** Differentiate via novel interaction (dual-screen/touch) and price to expand the market beyond core gamers, rather than engaging in a losing specs war.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is developer abandonment due to architectural novelty and perceived gimmickry. However, matching the rival's graphics directly is a "no-win" scenario given their head start and funding; a specs war cedes the initiative. The "do nothing" option guarantees slow erosion as the rival captures the mainstream.

The proposal mitigates cost risk by retaining the older hardware line, serving price-sensitive core gamers while the new device targets expansion. The "unproven" nature of touch is precisely the opportunity to create a blue ocean; waiting for proof allows the rival to define the category. The dual-screen cost is justified if it enables software experiences the rival cannot replicate, shifting competition from raw power to unique gameplay.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because first-party software did not demonstrate the touch interface's value immediately, causing third-party developers to flee. The kill criterion is clear: if launch titles do not showcase mandatory touch mechanics that drive hardware sales within the first quarter, the strategy collapses.

**Fit & Evidence:** The goal is audience broadening. A cheaper, distinct device aligns with this; a costly, me-too device does not. The risk of confusing developers is real but manageable through strong first-party support and clear documentation, a standard industry practice. The "gimmick" label is a marketing challenge, not a product flaw, solvable by demonstrating utility in flagship titles.

**Decision:**
The proposal correctly identifies that competing on identical terrain against a stronger incumbent is fatal. It forces a shift in the basis of competition. While developer friction is a valid concern, it is a solvable execution problem, whereas a specs war is a structural deficit. The retention of the legacy line provides a safety net. The potential reward of creating a new mass market outweighs the risk of initial confusion.

DECISION: GO
