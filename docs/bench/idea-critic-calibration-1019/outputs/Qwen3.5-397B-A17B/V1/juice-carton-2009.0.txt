**Steelman:** A modernized, minimalist design will signal premium quality and attract new, younger demographics, outweighing the temporary confusion for existing loyalists if supported by a massive advertising blitz.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on the assumption that advertising can instantly retrain deep-seated visual recognition. For a "leading" brand with an "older, loyal" base, the primary purchase driver is often habitual shelf scanning, not active evaluation. The "orange with a straw" is not just an image; it is the primary cognitive shortcut for identification. Removing it creates a high probability of "shelf blindness," where loyalists bypass the product entirely, assuming it is out of stock or a different brand.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" option preserves current revenue. A superior alternative is a phased transition or a hybrid design that retains the iconic straw graphic while introducing modern elements. This captures freshness without severing the visual tether to the brand's heritage. A "big bang" nationwide launch removes the ability to test shelf recognition before committing the entire revenue stream.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch fails. The cause is not the quality of the juice or the ad spend, but the immediate drop in repeat purchases because the target demographic literally did not see the product on the shelf. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a failed national rollout that alienates the core base.

**Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks a pilot. It commits to a one-way door (printed cartons are irreversible) without verifying the core hypothesis: that the new design is recognizable. Without a measurable threshold for shelf-recognition retention in a test market, the risk is unmanaged.

**Verdict:** The risk of alienating the core, older demographic by removing their primary visual cue is fatal to the proposal as stated. The "large advertising campaign" is a lagging indicator that cannot compensate for immediate shelf invisibility. The plan must be revised to include a regional pilot and a design that preserves the iconic straw element during the transition.

DECISION: NO-GO
