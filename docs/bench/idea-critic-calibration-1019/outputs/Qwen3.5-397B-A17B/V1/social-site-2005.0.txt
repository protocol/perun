**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform for $580M secures a dominant, customizable social network for a young demographic, allowing the conglomerate to monetize via cross-channel advertising before rivals solidify.

**Attack:** The valuation is indefensible based on current revenue; the price assumes massive future ad yield from a demographic historically difficult to monetize. Brand-safety risks from user-generated pages are existential for a global media conglomerate, potentially contaminating existing premium channels rather than enhancing them. The "separate unit" structure may fail to integrate ad sales effectively while exposing the parent to reputation damage. The technology debt suggests high integration costs and instability. A college-focused rival indicates the market is still fluid; buying now locks in a specific, unproven model while a competitor might capture the more valuable university segment. The "fast-growing" metric may be a bubble driven by novelty rather than stickiness.

**Better Way:** Do nothing. Wait for the college rival to mature or for the target's revenue to prove sustainability. Alternatively, negotiate a significantly lower price reflecting the modest revenue and high risk, or pursue a partnership rather than a full acquisition to test ad integration without assuming total liability for brand safety and tech debt.

**Fit:** This contradicts a prudent capital allocation strategy given the lack of proven monetization. The risk of brand contamination outweighs the speculative ad revenue.

**Pre-mortem:** In two years, the site is banned by advertisers due to unsafe content, the tech stack collapses under scale, and the $580M is written off while the college rival dominates the high-value student market.

**Kill Criteria:** The deal requires a hard cap on liability for brand-safety incidents and a verified path to profitability within 18 months. Neither is present. The price is purely speculative.

**Verdict:** The proposal relies on optimistic assumptions about monetizing youth and integrating ad sales while ignoring severe brand-safety and technology risks. The price is too high for the current evidence.

DECISION: NO-GO
