**Steelman:** A well-funded entrant can disrupt a saturated market by leveraging superior hardware (screen), a seamless ecosystem (wireless sharing), and a unified store/subscription model, overcoming lateness through brand power and user experience rather than price.

**Attack:** The proposal faces catastrophic structural headwinds. First, abandoning the established copy-protection standard alienates existing partners (labels, device makers) whose content is essential, risking a library too thin to attract users. Second, "wireless sharing" directly conflicts with label restrictions likely to be imposed on a new, unproven platform, creating a legal or functional deadlock at launch. Third, entering late against a dominant, loyal ecosystem without a clear price advantage requires the marketing budget to burn indefinitely to change habits, a notoriously low-probability bet. The "larger screen" is a marginal feature, not a category killer.

**Better Way:** Do not launch a full hardware/store/subscription triad simultaneously. License the software to existing device makers to build user base without hardware risk, or acquire a smaller player with existing label deals to bypass the "late mover" content barrier.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches with a limited catalog because labels, angered by the dropped DRM and wary of sharing features, withhold key albums. The marketing budget burns while users stick to the incumbent for content availability. The company exits the market within two years, having lost billions.

**Fit & Evidence:** The proposal assumes labels will accept a new DRM-free or different-DRM standard from a newcomer despite rejecting it from others; no evidence supports this. It assumes marketing can override content scarcity; history suggests otherwise. The "wireless sharing" feature is likely non-compliant with pending label demands.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a one-way door with massive capital exposure. The failure scenario (no content, no adoption) is highly plausible given the specific risks of alienating partners and entering late without a content moat. The proposal lacks a pilot, a phased rollout, or a verified path to securing label buy-in for the new sharing model.

DECISION: NO-GO
