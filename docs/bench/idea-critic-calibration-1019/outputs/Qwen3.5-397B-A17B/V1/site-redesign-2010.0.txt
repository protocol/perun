The claim is that a full rewrite with altered voting mechanics and a forced migration will reverse slowing traffic. The steelman: a modernized interface favoring publishers will attract new volume sufficient to offset community churn.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the "all at once" strategy and the modification of core mechanics. The community's loyalty is tied to the existing voting system; changing this directly attacks the product's value proposition for its most engaged users. A rival site is already growing, suggesting market vulnerability. A "big bang" launch eliminates the ability to measure the impact of the new voting algorithm against the old one. If the new mechanics drive users away, there is no rollback path, resulting in total platform failure rather than a tunable decline.

The "do nothing" option preserves the loyal base while allowing for incremental UI tweaks. A better alternative is a staged rollout: launch the new design to a small percentage of traffic or new users only, keeping the voting mechanics identical initially to isolate design impact. Then, A/B test voting changes separately. This approach reduces engineering risk and provides an escape hatch.

The proposal fails the pre-mortem: if the rewrite launches to everyone and the community revolts due to changed voting rules, traffic collapses with no way to revert. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed launch; waiting to implement a phased approach prevents catastrophic loss. The evidence provided highlights that the core community values the *existing* mechanics, making the proposed change a direct conflict with user retention drivers.

Without a phased rollout, specific kill criteria for the new voting logic, or a rollback plan, this proposal bets the entire company on an unproven hypothesis with zero safety nets. The risk of alienating the loyal base while failing to guarantee new growth is unacceptable.

DECISION: NO-GO
