**Steelman:** Acquiring a modern, object-oriented OS core from a small vendor is the only viable path to rapidly replace a failing internal platform, arrest market share decline, and leverage external innovation where internal attempts have twice failed.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Continued reliance on a dated OS guarantees further market share erosion and eventual irrelevance. Internal development has already failed twice; a third attempt without new assets is likely to fail again.
2.  **License Another Vendor:** This surrenders control of the core product roadmap and differentiation to a competitor or third party, reducing the hardware maker to a commodity assembler.
3.  **Financials & Risk:** While the acquisition price is high relative to the target's current revenue, the value lies in the technology IP and engineering talent, not current sales. The "few units sold" metric is irrelevant if the architecture is superior. Migration risk is inherent in any OS change but is lower than building from scratch. The founder's "difficult history" is mitigated by limiting their role to "advisor" rather than operational control.

**Pre-mortem:** The initiative fails if the cultural integration rejects the external technology or if the migration timeline slips, burning cash before launch. However, the cost of delay (continued market collapse) outweighs the execution risk. The "one-way door" nature of this strategic pivot requires decisive action; hesitation is more costly than a flawed execution that can be iterated upon.

**Fit:** This directly addresses the North Star of restoring competitiveness through a modern technical foundation. The alternative (licensing) fails the strategic fit of owning the user experience.

**Conclusion:** The proposal survives the strongest attack: that the target is financially small. The strategic necessity of owning a modern core outweighs the premium price and integration risks. The specific mitigation of the founder's role addresses the personnel risk. No fatal flaw exists that justifies halting a strategic rescue when internal options are exhausted.

DECISION: GO
