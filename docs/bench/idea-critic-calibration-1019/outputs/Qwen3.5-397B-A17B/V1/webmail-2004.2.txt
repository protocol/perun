**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is unit economics: if storage costs do not drop faster than adoption, the service bleeds capital indefinitely. However, disk prices were already trending down exponentially in 2004; betting on this curve is a calculated risk, not a guess. The "do nothing" alternative cedes the high-value user segment to incumbents who cannot pivot their legacy infrastructure quickly. A "paid tier" alternative fails because the network effect and data volume required for ad targeting require mass adoption, which only "free" guarantees.

This aligns with a strategy of organizing the world's information. If we assume the ad-matching algorithm works, the lifetime value of a user exceeds the marginal storage cost. A pre-mortem suggests failure occurs if privacy backlash becomes regulatory or if users refuse to trust a search engine with private correspondence. Yet, users already delete mail due to space constraints; removing that pain point is a powerful incentive to overcome privacy hesitation. The invitation-only launch creates artificial scarcity, driving demand while limiting initial infrastructure load.

The risk of "no direct revenue" is mitigated by the long-term data asset accumulation. The "mature market" argument is weak; maturity implies stagnation, which this proposal disrupts via a step-change in utility (1GB vs. MBs).
