**Steelman:** An upfront $79 fee secures customer loyalty and increases order frequency, offsetting per-order shipping friction and generating predictable recurring revenue despite short-term margin compression.

**Skeptic:** The core assumption is that increased order volume from "heavy buyers" will not outpace the fixed revenue cap of $79. If the average marginal shipping cost per order exceeds the break-even point (approximately 8–10 orders/year depending on actual logistics costs), every additional order deepens the loss. The proposal admits economics are "unproven," yet seeks full launch without a pilot.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" option preserves current margins while testing alternative friction reducers like free shipping thresholds ($50+ orders). A superior alternative is a capped pilot: limit the membership to a specific geographic zone or customer segment for 90 days to validate unit economics before a global rollout. This reduces reversibility risk; a full annual launch creates immediate contractual obligations that are legally and reputationally difficult to reverse mid-year.

**Kill-Criteria & Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 12 months: customer acquisition was high, but average orders per member hit 15, causing shipping costs to consume 25% of revenue versus the projected 15%. The fatal flaw is launching nationwide without a verified break-even model. The risk of "hard to reverse" is compounded by the lack of a kill switch; once sold, the promise cannot be unmade without brand damage.

**Verdict:** The proposal fails the evidence check. Launching an unproven economic model with high fixed costs and low reversibility violates basic capital allocation hygiene. The "short-term margin hit" is not a risk; it is a certainty if the volume assumptions are wrong. You cannot approve a bet where the downside is unlimited liability per customer and the upside is theoretical.

**Required Action:** Do not launch. Run a 90-day regional pilot with strict monitoring of orders-per-member. Establish a kill criterion: if average orders exceed 10 per member in the pilot, halt expansion.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The argument that "competitors might do it first" was considered but rejected; being first to lose money on logistics is not a winning strategy.

**Dissent Ledger:** High risk of negative unit economics; irreversible customer contracts; unverified demand elasticity.

DECISION: NO-GO
