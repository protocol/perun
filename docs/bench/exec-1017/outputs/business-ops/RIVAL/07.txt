**Recommendation:** Proceed with the price increase to $15/user, but **do not** apply it universally on Day 1. Instead, execute a staggered rollout: apply the new price immediately to all *new* customers and the bottom 20% of existing accounts (lowest usage/support burden), while grandfathering the top 20% for 6 months. This captures immediate upside while creating a control group to measure true elasticity before exposing your highest-risk revenue. You must also explicitly budget the $0.50/user support cost; your net gain per user is $2.50, not $3.00.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Elasticity Miscalculation:** If actual elasticity is -1.0 (highly sensitive) rather than -0.3, a 25% price hike could trigger >25% volume loss (seat reduction or churn), causing total revenue to drop despite the higher unit price.
2.  **Support Margin Erosion:** If the price increase triggers complex billing disputes or configuration changes, the $0.50 estimated support cost could spike to $1.50+, wiping out 60% of your marginal gain.
3.  **Competitive Anchor:** Competitors positioned at $12–$13 may aggressively target your churning mid-market segment with "switching credits," accelerating defection.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
 Halt the broader rollout immediately if **monthly churn exceeds 8.5%** (a ~40% increase over baseline) for two consecutive months among the pilot group, or if **net seat count** (total active users across all clients) drops by more than 10% in Q3.

*Note: Ensure your billing system can handle prorated changes mid-cycle to minimize friction during the staggered rollout.*
*Prepare a "value add" announcement (e.g., a minor feature release) to accompany the price change, framing it as an upgrade rather than a pure cost hike.*
