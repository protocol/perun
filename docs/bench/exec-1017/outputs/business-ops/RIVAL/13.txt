**Decision Memo: MetroLine Pulse Premium Price Increase**

**Recommendation:** Proceed immediately with raising the premium price from $1.99 to $2.49.

At current scale (118k MAU), this move increases monthly subscription revenue from ~$7,360 to ~$11,230 (assuming the 38% opt-in target is met), a ~52% uplift. Even if opt-in rates stagnate at the current 31%, the price hike alone generates ~$9,150/month (+24%). This incremental revenue ($38k–$45k annually) comfortably absorbs the maximum potential compliance fee ($48k/year) while significantly improving unit economics. The current $1.99 price point risks signaling low value; $2.49 remains within typical consumer psychological barriers for utility apps while capturing more willingness to pay.

**Primary Failure Modes & Arithmetic Oversights:**

1.  **Churn Sensitivity Underestimation:** The plan assumes churn holds steady at 4% despite a 25% price increase. In consumer apps, even small price hikes often trigger disproportionate churn. If monthly churn spikes to 7-8% among existing subscribers due to the hike, the net revenue gain evaporates within 6 months as the subscriber base erodes faster than new opt-ins replace them.
2.  **Opt-In Conversion Fallacy:** The projection assumes raising prices will coincide with increasing opt-in rates from 31% to 38%. Historically, price increases suppress conversion unless paired with significant value additions. If the price rises but opt-in rates drop to 25% (price resistance), total subscription revenue could actually decline to ~$7,300, negating the strategy entirely.
3.  **Variable Cost Scaling:** The model cites $0.03/user for storage. If "data sharing expansion" implies heavier data payloads or retention requirements for out-of-state partners, this variable cost could double. At 118k users growing 15% MoM, a jump to $0.06/user adds ~$3,500/month in costs by Q4 end, eating 15% of the projected price-hike gains.
4.  **Compliance Fee Timing:** The fee is quarterly ($8k–$12k), but revenue is monthly. A cash flow gap may occur in Q1 2025 if the fee hits before the full year's accrued revenue is available, especially if growth slows.

**Single Pivot Metric:**

**Net Subscriber Growth Rate.** If the combined effect of the price hike causes the *net* number of premium subscribers to decline for two consecutive months (i.e., churn + downgrades > new opt-ins), halt the price increase immediately. This indicates the price elasticity is worse than modeled, and the strategy is destroying lifetime value rather than capturing it.

*Note: Ensure the compliance fee contract includes a cap or volume-based scaling to prevent uncapped liability as MAU grows.*
