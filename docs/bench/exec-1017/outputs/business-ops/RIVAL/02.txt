**Recommendation: Modify Before Launch**

Proceed with the tiered structure, but **pause the October 15 launch** to correct critical financial modeling errors. Your current projection overstates revenue by ignoring payment processing fees and attrition, while understating the risk of donor downgrading. Specifically, the assumption that 30% of donors will immediately upgrade to $5,000 without a defined cultivation strategy is optimistic; historically, introducing a lower entry tier ($1,500) often causes existing donors to downgrade rather than upgrade. Recalculate your net revenue assuming a 15% downgrade rate to the entry tier and apply the 2.5% processing fee to gross receipts before presenting to the board.

**Three Biggest Risks of Failure**

1.  **Revenue Cannibalization:** Current $2,500 donors may self-select into the new $1,500 tier, instantly reducing annual revenue by $200,000 (if 50% downgrade) before any upgrades occur.
2.  **Margin Erosion:** With a fixed acquisition cost of $12,000 per donor, your cost-to-serve exceeds the first-year contribution of any single donor in the $1,500 or $2,500 tiers. You are losing money on every new donor acquired unless they stay for multiple years, which contradicts your "six-month upgrade" hope.
3.  **Operational Blind Spots:** Ignoring the 2.5% processing fee (~$16,250 on projected revenue) and 10% attrition creates a false surplus. If attrition hits early, the high acquisition cost becomes unrecoverable, turning the program into a net loss within year one.

**Stop-Loss Trigger**

Re-evaluate or terminate the program immediately if **net revenue (after processing fees and acquisition costs) falls below $380,000** in the first 12 months. This threshold accounts for the high upfront acquisition spend and ensures the program covers its own existence; anything less indicates the tier structure is diluting value rather than capturing it.
