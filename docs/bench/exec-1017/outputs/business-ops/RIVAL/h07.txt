**Recommendation:** Do not launch the full 5,000-bike fleet this quarter. Your current model projects a monthly net loss of approximately $345,000, making a 12-month break-even impossible. The $6M capital outlay is at severe risk because the revenue forecast ($900k/month) fails to account for $225k in insurance costs, up to $20k in congestion fees, and the high churn rate which drastically reduces the active billing base within months. Instead, execute a phased pilot of 500 bikes to validate ride frequency and congestion costs before committing the remaining 90% of capital.

**Three Biggest Failure Points:**
1.  **Catastrophic Unit Economics:** You omitted insurance ($0.15/ride/bike), which alone consumes 25% of projected revenue ($225k/month). When combined with the new congestion fees and an 8% monthly churn (losing over half your paying users in six months), the contribution margin turns deeply negative immediately.
2.  **Channel Cannibalization & Churn:** Assuming 70% adoption from existing ride-share users is dangerous; if these users simply swap higher-margin car rides for lower-margin bike rides without net new revenue, you dilute overall profitability while facing an unsustainable 8% monthly churn that requires constant, expensive reacquisition.
3.  **Regulatory Cost Volatility:** The congestion pricing fee is an unknown variable ($10k–$20k/month) that scales with usage. If usage exceeds expectations, your costs rise linearly with your "success," potentially turning your busiest months into your most unprofitable ones.

**Pull-the-Plug Metric:** Sustained monthly net loss exceeding $150,000 for two consecutive months after the pilot phase, or an actual daily ride frequency averaging below 1.2 rides per bike.

*Note: The current math shows a loss, not a profit; the $900k revenue is insufficient to cover the identified operational costs.*
