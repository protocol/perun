**To:** Hana Patel, CFO
**From:** Pricing Strategy Lead
**Subject:** Recommendation on Invoice Financing Discount Rate Adjustment

**Recommendation**
Proceed with an immediate increase of the discount rate from 2.5% to **2.9% per month**.

While the market may absorb a full 0.5-point increase to 3.0%, we must retain a 30-basis point buffer below the potential regulatory cap of 3.2%. Pricing at 2.9% captures 80% of the available upside while mitigating the risk of immediate regulatory non-compliance or forced rollback. At the target volume of 200 invoices/month (assuming the corrected average invoice value of $150k based on your Q2 trajectory, or $180k if volume quality improves), this increase projects an additional $540k–$648k in annual revenue, significantly widening the margin above the $250k operating cost baseline even if default rates tick upward slightly.

**Primary Failure Modes**
1.  **Volume Contraction:** A rate hike of this magnitude could drive price-sensitive SMB clients to competitors or alternative financing, causing monthly invoice processing to stagnate below the 150-unit threshold required to offset fixed costs.
2.  **Adverse Selection (Higher Defaults):** Increasing the cost of capital may attract riskier borrowers who cannot secure cheaper financing elsewhere, potentially pushing the default rate above the modeled 3% ceiling and eroding net yield.
3.  **Regulatory Friction:** Even at 2.9%, operating near the suspected 3.2% cap may trigger heightened scrutiny or an emergency regulatory notice if the jurisdiction decides to lower the cap to protect borrowers during economic downturns.

**Stop-Loss Triggers & Adjustment Metrics**
We will halt the rate increase or revert to 2.5% immediately upon observing either of the following:
*   **Volume Decline:** Two consecutive months of negative net new invoice volume growth, or a total drop in processed invoices exceeding 15% from the pre-hike baseline.
*   **Asset Quality Deterioration:** The rolling 3-month default rate exceeds 4.5% (a 50% increase over the 3% baseline), indicating the higher rate is degrading loan book quality.
*   **Regulatory Action:** Receipt of any formal inquiry, notice, or guidance from the primary jurisdiction's regulator regarding discount rate limits.

*Note: Ensure the Q3 2025 volume model uses the corrected $18M Q2 baseline for accurate yield forecasting. Legal counsel should confirm the 3.2% cap assumption before public announcement.*
