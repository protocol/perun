**Recommendation: Do not raise the price to $95 on July 1, 2024.**

Hold the price at $85 until after the FDA guidance is released in September. While a $10 increase appears attractive on paper—potentially adding $15M in annual revenue if volume holds—the current 18% gross margin is dangerously thin for a physical product facing regulatory uncertainty. At $85, your cost of goods sold (COGS) is approximately $69.70. A price hike to $95 improves margins, but if the FDA mandates packaging changes that cost even $4–$5 per unit, your net gain evaporates instantly.

Furthermore, with 1.5 million units sold annually, a mere 3.5% drop in conversion (well within normal elasticity for a 12% price hike) would erase the entire revenue gain from the increase. Implementing a price change two months before a potential regulatory cost shock creates a "double whammy" risk: you alienate customers with a hike, then potentially have to raise prices again or absorb costs in Q4. Wait for the FDA data, recalculate your true margin floor, and then price accordingly.

**Main Failure Modes:**
*   **Regulatory Margin Erosion:** FDA packaging requirements increase COGS by >$5/unit, turning the price hike's profit buffer into a loss leader.
*   **Volume Sensitivity:** Conversion drops by >4% due to the price increase, reducing total gross profit dollars despite the higher per-unit price.
*   **Channel Conflict:** The new $500k Instagram campaign drives traffic that converts poorly at the new price point, spiking blended CAC above $35.

**Pivot/Stop Trigger:**
If monthly repeat orders drop by **5%** within 45 days of implementation, or if the FDA guidance indicates compliance costs will exceed **$3.50 per unit**, immediately revert to $85 or pause the hike.
