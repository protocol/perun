**Recommendation: Do not proceed with the blanket price increase to $15 or the launch of the $8 Enterprise tier as currently modeled.**

Your current plan relies on two dangerous assumptions: that churn will remain flat at 5% despite a 25% price hike in a market where a key competitor (SignFlow) just lowered prices to $11, and that an "Enterprise" tier priced lower than your core product ($8 vs. $12) is a viable upsell.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Churn Spiral:** Raising prices to $15 while SignFlow drops to $11 creates a $4/month value gap. If churn rises from 5% to just 8%, your LTV collapses from $240 to $150. With a $30 CAC, your LTV:CAC ratio drops from 8:1 to 5:1, and if churn hits 10%, it falls to 3:1, endangering your Series A narrative of efficient growth.
2.  **Pricing Inversion:** Launching an $8 "Enterprise" tier undermines your $12 base plan. Enterprise customers expect to pay a premium for security and admin features, not a discount. This signals confusion in your packaging strategy to investors.
3.  **Runway Illusion:** The projected $45k monthly gain assumes zero attrition. If you lose 6% of your base (750 users) immediately due to the hike, you lose $9k in base revenue, netting only $36k. If churn accelerates in month two, the revenue gain evaporates entirely.

**Specific Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
Implement the price change only for **new customers** first. If existing customer churn exceeds **6.5% in the first 30 days** of any broader rollout, or if new customer conversion rates drop by more than **15%** compared to the August baseline, immediately halt the price increase and revert to $12.

*Note: To fix the "Enterprise" concept, repackage it as a premium tier at $29+ with SSO and team management, rather than a discount tier. Test the $15 price point on a small cohort (5% of users) before a full launch.*
