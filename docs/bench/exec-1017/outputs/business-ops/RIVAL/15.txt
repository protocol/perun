While your model assumes a 20% hike is safe, a 15% increase provides a critical buffer against the unknown compliance costs ($150k–$300k) and reduces the risk of triggering a formal procurement review. At 15%, you capture ~$270k in new annual revenue from the base (assuming full adoption), which largely covers the worst-case compliance scenario without relying on the projected 18,000 new seats. **How This Could Fail:**
1.  **Contractual Lockout:** The District 7 contract may have a cap on annual price increases (e.g., tied to CPI); if the 15–20% hike exceeds this clause, they can reject the renewal entirely, causing immediate 100% churn for that district.
2.  **The "Compliance Tax" Backfire:** If compliance costs hit $300k and the price hike triggers even 5% churn across the broader base (not just District 7), the lost revenue (~$1M+) will dwarf the price gain, destroying the 58% gross margin.
3.  **Procurement Friction:** A double-digit increase often forces school boards to put the contract out for competitive bid, inviting competitors to undercut you during a vulnerable transition period.

**Stop/Reconsider Triggers:**
*   **Churn > 4%:** If pilot discussions or early renewal talks indicate a loss of more than 4% of total seats (6,000 students), abort the increase; the revenue loss exceeds the compliance buffer.
*   **Compliance Quote > $250k:** If the firm quote for certification exceeds $250k, pause the price hike and instead present the cost as a mandatory line-item surcharge to the district, passing the specific regulatory cost directly rather than bundling it into the base rate.
*   **Legal Cap Breach:** If legal review confirms the proposed % increase violates the current contract's escalation clause, do not attempt to negotiate it; offer the maximum allowed increase plus a fee for new features.
