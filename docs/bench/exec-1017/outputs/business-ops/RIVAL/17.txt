**Recommendation: Pause the June 1 rollout.**

Do not proceed with the price increase to $140 on June 1. Your current monthly churn is 8%, which already indicates significant friction or product-market fit issues. At an 8% monthly churn rate, your average customer lifespan is only ~12 months, but this is highly unstable; increasing price on a base that is already leaving at this rate will almost certainly spike churn beyond recovery levels. With a CAC of $150 and a current LTV of $600, your LTV:CAC ratio is 4:1, which looks healthy on paper but is fragile given the high churn. If the price hike pushes monthly churn to just 10%, the average lifespan drops to 10 months, slashing LTV to $500 (assuming no behavioral change) or lower if downgrades occur. Furthermore, your assumption that acquisition speed will remain constant despite a 17% price increase is untested and risky in a competitive market where rivals may discount in July. Stabilize the existing base first; aim to reduce organic churn to <5% before testing price elasticity.

**Three Main Ways This Plan Could Fail:**
1. **Churn Spiral:** The price hike triggers existing members to leave faster than new ones join, causing net negative growth and collapsing LTV below CAC.
2. **Acquisition Stall:** The higher $140 entry point increases friction for new signups, causing the 500/month acquisition target to miss while CAC rises due to lower conversion rates.
3. **Competitive undercutting:** If competitors launch discounts in July as feared, your new higher price makes you an immediate outlier, accelerating defections among both new and existing users.

**Stop/Change Course Metric:**
If monthly churn exceeds **10%** within 45 days of implementation (or if net new member growth turns negative for two consecutive months), immediately revert to the $120 price point or introduce a grandfathered legacy plan.
