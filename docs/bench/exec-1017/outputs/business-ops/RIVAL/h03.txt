**Recommendation: Test Before Full Rollout**

Do not implement a blanket price increase to $18 on October 1, 2026. Instead, initiate a controlled A/B test on new signups only for 60 days, keeping existing customers at $15 temporarily.

**Rationale:** Your current data contains a critical blind spot. While a projected 3% drop in conversion seems manageable, applying an unverified price hike to your entire base (12,000 users) risks accelerating your already high quarterly churn (5%). If the price sensitivity is actually 6–8% rather than 3%, you could trigger a net revenue decline despite the higher unit price. Furthermore, your current ARR calculation ignores churn; a realistic model shows you are likely losing ~$810k annually to attrition alone ($2.7M × ~30% annualized churn). Adding unknown compliance costs ($24k–$36k) and support costs ($72k/year for current base) tightens margins further. You need empirical data on the $18 price point's impact on *net* retention before locking it in.

**Main Ways This Could Fail:**
1.  **Churn Acceleration:** The price increase triggers existing landlords to re-evaluate the tool, spiking quarterly churn from 5% to 8%+, erasing revenue gains from the higher price.
2.  **Acquisition Stall:** The 3% conversion drop is underestimated; if CAC rises from $30 to $45 due to lower close rates, the LTV:CAC ratio breaks, halting growth.
3.  **Margin Compression:** Compliance fees and support costs exceed estimates while volume growth stalls, leaving you with higher operational overhead and flat revenue.

**Stop/Change Course Metric:**
Halt the rollout immediately if **Net New ARR** (New Sales ARR minus Churned ARR) falls below **$150,000** in any single month during the test period, or if **quarterly churn exceeds 6.5%** among any cohort exposed to the new price.
