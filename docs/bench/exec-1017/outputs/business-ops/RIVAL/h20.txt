**Recommendation for CFO Maya Patel:**
Do not implement a blanket price increase to $7 on October 15. Instead, execute a segmented rollout: raise the price to $7 only for **new** sellers and those upgrading to premium starting October 15, while grandfathering existing premium users for 90 days.

The finance team's projection of $151,200 is flawed because it assumes zero volume loss. With an elasticity of –0.8, a 40% price hike ($5 to $7) predicts a ~32% drop in premium listing volume. If volume falls by 32%, revenue actually *decreases* from $131,400 to roughly $125,000, excluding the $5,000 support cost increase. Furthermore, your current unit economics are dangerous: with an ARPU of $9.50 and churn of 5%, your LTV is $190. Against a CAC of $22, this looks healthy, but if the price hike accelerates churn even slightly (e.g., to 6%), LTV drops to $158, eroding your margin for the allocated $30,000 retention budget. By testing the price on new users first, you validate the true elasticity without triggering immediate mass churn or support overload from legacy users. Use the $30,000 marketing budget to offer "lock-in" annual plans to current users before the change is announced, securing cash flow and reducing churn risk.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Elasticity Underestimation:** If true elasticity is closer to –1.5 (common in non-essential marketplace features), volume could collapse by >50%, causing net revenue to fall below $90,000.
2.  **Support Overload:** The predicted $5,000 support cost increase may be conservative; if confused users flood channels during a blanket hike, costs could spike to $15,000+, negating any marginal revenue gain.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
 Halt the rollout immediately if **net revenue growth is under 5% after 45 days** or if **monthly churn exceeds 7%** among affected user segments.
