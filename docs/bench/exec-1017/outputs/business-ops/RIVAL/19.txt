**Recommendation: Do not implement the blanket price increase on November 1.**

Your current unit economics are fragile, and the proposed hike exposes Tidewater Fresh to immediate negative cash flow risk. Your LTV calculation is incorrect; with a 5% monthly churn rate, the average customer lifespan is 20 months ($1/0.05$), but you must apply this to your *actual* contribution margin, not top-line revenue.

Currently, your margin per order is $18 ($45 revenue - $25 variable - $2 packaging). With 2 orders/month, your monthly contribution per user is $36. Over a 20-month lifespan, the true LTV is **$720**, not $1,800. Since your CAC is $120, your LTV:CAC ratio is 6:1, which looks healthy, but the absolute dollar buffer is thin.

If churn rises by 1.5 points to 6.5% as feared, the average lifespan drops to ~15.4 months. Even with the extra $5 revenue (assuming no volume drop), the new margin is $46/month. The new LTV becomes ~$708. **You would generate less lifetime profit per customer while simultaneously accelerating subscriber loss.** The projected $570k revenue gain ignores the compounding erosion of the subscriber base. Furthermore, the potential $0.15 regulatory cost per order ($3.60/user/month) would wipe out 20% of your current margin before the price hike even begins.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Churn Sensitivity Error:** A mere 1.5% increase in churn negates the lifetime value gains of the price hike, leaving you with fewer customers and lower total profit within 14 months.
2.  **Regulatory Margin Compression:** If the single-use bag fee passes, your variable costs rise by $3.60/user/month. Without a price increase, your LTV drops to $588; with the price increase and higher churn, you risk dipping below a 4:1 LTV:CAC ratio, making growth unsustainable.
3.  **Order Frequency Elasticity:** You assumed order volume (2/month) remains static. If the price hike causes users to order less frequently (e.g., 1.8 orders), your contribution margin collapses faster than subscriber count.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
Monitor **Net Revenue Retention (NRR)** weekly for the first 60 days. If NRR drops below 95% (indicating that churn and downgrades are outweighing the price uplift), immediately pause the rollout for new cohorts and revert existing customers to the legacy rate to prevent a mass-exit event.
