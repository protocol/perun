**Critical Flaws in the Current Plan**

1.  **Revenue Miscalculation:** The projection assumes offices consume 20 lb/month but only charges for 15 lb/month.
    *   *Current Math:* 100 offices × 15 lb × $12 = $18,000/month ($216,000/year).
    *   *The Risk:* If offices actually consume 20 lb, you are either under-supplying (causing immediate churn) or absorbing 5 lb of COGS ($27/office/month) for free. If you bill for the actual 20 lb usage, annual revenue is $288,000, not $180,000. The break-even timeline changes drastically based on which scenario occurs.
2.  **The "Free" Logistics Trap:** Assuming $0 incremental logistics cost for multi-stop office delivery is dangerous. Wholesale distribution (pallets to one dock) differs vastly from office delivery (small parcels to multiple floors/buildings). Even a $1.50/lb delivery cost wipes out 28% of your gross margin, pushing break-even beyond month 12.
3.  **Runway Exposure:** With only $250k left, a six-month ramp to 100 accounts requires significant upfront working capital for beans and delivery infrastructure before the "month 8" break-even point. One delay in sales velocity creates a cash crisis.

*   **Margin Erosion:** Hidden last-mile delivery costs exceed $1.00 per pound, turning the 55% gross margin into a loss.
*   **Supply Leakage:** Offices consume 20 lb but are capped at 15 lb, leading to service failures and reputation damage, or you inadvertently subsidize the extra 5 lb.

*   **Contract Rigidity:** The 12-month lock-in may deter prospects if the price per pound ($12) is significantly higher than their current commoditized supplier, slowing adoption.
