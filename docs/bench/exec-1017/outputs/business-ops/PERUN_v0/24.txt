**Recommendation:** Do not launch the subscription commercially until EU regulatory clearance is secured; instead, initiate a limited "pilot" with existing customers under explicit terms that telemetry features are provisional pending approval. Launching a paid subscription dependent on unapproved firmware creates a high risk of breach of contract and regulatory penalties if the 18-month scenario materializes.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Regulatory Blockade:** If EU clearance takes 18 months, you cannot legally enable the telemetry feature in that market. With a 3-year minimum commitment, you would be selling a product unable to deliver its core value proposition, leading to immediate churn, refund demands, and potential legal action once the delay exceeds a reasonable grace period.
2.  **Unit Economics Collapse:** Your current model assumes an 8% churn rate based on "industry SaaS average." Hardware-enabled subscriptions often face different churn drivers (device failure, site closure) than pure software. If actual churn is 12% instead of 8%, your Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) drops from ~$1,875 to ~$1,360 (using $500 ARPU). Against a $2,000 CAC, you would lose money on every customer acquired, making the $25M ARR goal mathematically impossible without drastically increasing prices or slashing sales costs.
3.  **Sales Capacity Mismatch:** To sell 10,000 units in Year 1 with 8 reps, each rep must close ~1,040 units/year (roughly 4 units per working day). At a $500/year price point with a 3-year commit ($1,500 total contract value), the commission per deal may be too low to motivate an enterprise sales team accustomed to larger upfront device sales, causing the pipeline to stall despite the aggressive headcount.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
 Halt the full commercial rollout immediately if **regulatory approval is not granted within 9 months** of the pilot start date. This midpoint check prevents locking in multi-year contracts that you cannot fulfill within the first third of their term. Additionally, pivot the pricing model if the **pilot cohort's voluntary churn exceeds 10% in the first 6 months**, indicating the product-market fit for the subscription tier is weaker than the hardware-only baseline.

*Note: The $3M net profit target by Q4 2025 relies heavily on the 6-month regulatory scenario; stress-test this cash flow against the 18-month delay before presenting to the board.*
*Ensure legal counsel reviews the "provisional" language in pilot contracts to mitigate liability if firmware approval is denied entirely.*
