**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **limited pilot** of the "Reserve" tier before the March 1 full rollout. While the projected $24M ARR uplift is compelling, the unit economics are highly sensitive to the unknown impact on churn and the increased Cost of Goods Sold (COGS). A full launch exposes the company to a potential margin collapse if the premium kit's higher cost ($65) combines with higher-than-expected churn. Use the pilot to validate the real CAC and churn rates for the $200 price point before committing the full inventory and marketing spend.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Margin Erosion:** If the premium COGS rises to $65 while the price is $200, the gross margin drops to 67.5% (from the current 62.5% on the base tier, though the absolute dollar margin increases from $75 to $135). However, if the "premium" perception fails to justify the price, volume may not offset the fixed cost of holding expensive imported inventory.
2.  **Churn Amplification:** The model assumes a flat 6% churn. If the price hike causes existing customers to cancel rather than upgrade, or if the new premium users churn faster than 6%, the Lifetime Value (LTV) will plummet, making the $150 CAC unsustainable.
3.  **Cash Flow Trap:** With only $3.2M cash and a 9-month runway, a $2M raise is critical. If the rollout burns cash faster than anticipated due to slow adoption or high return rates on the expensive kits, the company could run out of capital before the raise closes.

**Stop/Change Triggers:**
*   **Churn > 8%:** If monthly churn for the Reserve tier exceeds 8% during the pilot, the LTV:CAC ratio likely falls below the healthy standard of 3:1, threatening long-term viability.
*   **CAC > $200:** If the actual cost to acquire a premium customer exceeds $200, the payback period will extend beyond acceptable limits given the current burn rate.
*   **Upgrade Rate < 15%:** If fewer than 15% of existing customers upgrade (half the projected 30%), the revenue thesis fails, and the inventory risk becomes too high.

**Note:** The $24M ARR projection assumes 30% of 12,300 users (3,690 users) pay an *additional* $960/year ($80/mo increase), which equals ~$3.5M, not $24M; please verify if the $24M figure includes the base revenue of those users or implies a much larger total addressable market. Ensure the $2M raise terms do not trigger securities compliance issues before marketing it.
