**Recommendation:** **Pause the 10% price increase** until the unknown licensing fee for premium titles is disclosed. Raising the price while a major cost variable is undefined risks breaking the unit economics before the contract renewal on July 15, 2024.

**Three Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Margin Erosion:** If the new licensing fee exceeds ~$2.50 per active user/month, the contribution margin turns negative even at the higher $10.99 price point.
2.  **Conversion Collapse:** The model assumes an 80% conversion from print to digital; if actual conversion is <50%, the revenue base shrinks by $150k annually, making the service unsustainable regardless of price.
3.  **Retention Overestimation:** The plan relies on 70% of users staying after six months. If churn spikes to >40% (retention <60%) due to the price hike or content gaps, the Lifetime Value (LTV) drops below the acquisition/support cost.

**Stop/Rethink Triggers:**
*   **Churn > 15%** within the first 90 days of any price change.
*   **Blended Cost Per Download > $3.50** (once licensing fees are known), which would reduce gross margin below 65%.
*   **Active Members < 1,200** by Month 4 of the renewed contract.

**Arithmetic & Assumption Issues:**
*   **Revenue Calculation Error:** Your estimated annual revenue of $299,700 assumes 100% retention for 12 months ($9.99 × 2,500 × 12 = $299,700). However, your own assumption states only 70% stay after six months. A realistic projection accounting for this churn would be significantly lower (~$240k–$250k range depending on *when* churn occurs), not $299,700.
*   **Cost Ambiguity:** The "$0.75 per-download" cost is undefined against a "$9.99 per-month" subscription. If heavy users download 20 books/month, your cost is $15.00/user, creating an immediate loss on every heavy user. You must define "average downloads per subscriber" to validate the $0.75 figure.
*   **Timeline Conflict:** The pilot started Jan 3, 2024. By the July 15 renewal, the pilot will only be ~6.5 months old. You will not have full 12-month retention data to validate the "70% stay after six months" assumption before signing the new contract.
