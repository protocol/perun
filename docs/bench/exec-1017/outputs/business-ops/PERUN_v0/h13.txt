**Recommendation: Do not proceed with the pilot in its current structure.**

Your unit economics are fundamentally broken before you even account for the unknown insurance adjustments or lab fees. With a $35 monthly fee and an estimated 1.1 visits per month, your revenue per pet is $35. Even ignoring the $12 base claim cost and the missing $8 lab fee, your Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) of $500 is unsustainable. At $35/month, it takes **14.3 months** just to recover the CAC, assuming zero service costs and zero churn. However, with a 5% monthly churn rate, the average customer lifespan is only ~20 months, and your calculated 12-month LTV of $756 is overly optimistic given the high churn and undefined variable costs.

When you include the known variable costs ($12 claim + $8.80 lab fee = $20.80), your contribution margin drops to ~$14.20 per month. At this margin, it takes **35 months** to recover the $500 CAC. Since the average customer stays only ~20 months (due to 5% churn), you lose money on every single subscriber acquired. You are paying $500 to acquire a customer who generates roughly $284 in *gross* profit over their lifetime, resulting in a net loss of ~$216 per subscriber before fixed overhead.

**Main Ways the Model Could Fail:**
1.  **CAC/LTV Inversion:** As shown above, the cost to acquire a customer vastly exceeds the profit they generate. The LTV:CAC ratio is approximately 0.57:1 (healthy businesses aim for >3:1).
2.  **Churn Sensitivity:** A 5% monthly churn is extremely high for a subscription model. If churn drops to 2%, LTV improves significantly, but at 5%, the model collapses regardless of pricing.
3.  **Variable Cost Underestimation:** You noted the per-claim cost after insurance is unknown. If the actual cost exceeds your $12 estimate, the contribution margin shrinks further, accelerating losses.
4.  **Adoption Overestimation:** If adoption falls below 30% (fewer than 360 subscribers), the $12,000 fixed overhead becomes an even larger burden per unit, deepening the monthly burn.

**Stop/Re-tool Metric:**
**Pause acquisition immediately if the blended Contribution Margin per subscriber remains below $25 after the first 50 signups.** Given your current inputs, you are likely negative from day one. Alternatively, if **actual monthly churn exceeds 3%** in the first 90 days, the unit economics will not support the $500 CAC, requiring an immediate pivot to either reduce acquisition spend or increase pricing/retention.

*Note: You must validate the "unknown" insurance-adjusted claim cost before any financial commitment, as this variable could further negate any path to profitability.*
