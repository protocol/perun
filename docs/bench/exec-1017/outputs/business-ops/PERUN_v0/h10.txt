**Recommendation: HOLD.**
Proceeding without quantifying the GDPR compliance cost creates an unacceptable financial blind spot. While the arithmetic suggests a runway extension to ~$1.2M, this figure is invalid until the "unknown" legal cost is subtracted. If GDPR implementation exceeds $200k, your net cash position post-rollout drops to $1.0M or less, fundamentally altering the risk profile of a price hike that could accelerate churn.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Churn Sensitivity:** Your model assumes a steady conversion rate and only 5% quarterly churn. A price increase of 20% ($99 to $119) often triggers disproportionate churn in price-sensitive segments. If actual churn rises to 8% quarterly, the lifetime value (LTV) collapses, and the projected $2.3M revenue vanishes.
2.  **Unquantified Regulatory Liability:** GDPR compliance for EU user data is not optional. If the legal team determines you need new infrastructure, Data Protection Officer (DPO) retention, or complex consent mechanisms, costs could easily surpass the $200k marketing budget, turning the projected surplus into a deficit.
3.  **CAC Underestimation:** You estimated CAC at $30. If the price hike reduces organic conversion, forcing heavier reliance on paid channels to maintain growth, CAC could spike. At a higher CAC, the payback period extends, consuming the cash reserve intended for runway extension.

**Specific Stop/Revise Triggers:**
*   **Churn > 6.5% Quarterly:** If early data shows churn exceeding this threshold within the first 60 days, the LTV:CAC ratio likely falls below the healthy 3:1 benchmark, warranting an immediate price rollback.
*   **Compliance Cost > $175k:** If legal quantifies the GDPR obligation above this amount, the net benefit of the price hike is too marginal to justify the operational risk; revise the plan to phase the increase or exclude EU users initially.
*   **Net Runway < $800k:** If the combination of marketing spend and compliance costs leaves less than $800k in the bank, the buffer is insufficient to absorb further market volatility; hold the increase until capital efficiency improves.
