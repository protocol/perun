**Recommendation: Test before full implementation.**
Do not roll out the $18 price to all 12,000 landlords on 1 Oct 2026. Instead, apply the $18 price only to the 300 pilot users converting in Q4 and a small, randomized segment (e.g., 5%) of new signups. Keep existing customers at $15 for now. This isolates the "3% conversion drop" variable you flagged as unverified data. If the test group shows a churn spike >3% or CAC rising above $30, you have contained the damage. If metrics hold, expand the price hike in Q1 2027.

**How this could fail:**
1.  **Churn Sensitivity Underestimation:** Your model assumes a static 5% quarterly churn. If the price hike triggers even a 2% incremental churn (to 7%), the loss of recurring revenue from the base 12,000 users could outweigh the margin gain from the $3 increase, especially given your high fixed user count.
2.  **Margin Erosion from Compliance:** You estimated state filing fees at $8k–$12k each for three states ($24k–$36k total). If these are recurring annual fees rather than one-time costs, or if additional states require similar filings, this directly reduces the net contribution margin intended to fund growth.
3.  **Support Cost Scaling:** The $0.50/user support cost is treated as fixed. If the price increase generates a higher volume of support tickets (cancellation requests, billing disputes), this variable cost may rise, shrinking the unit economics further.

**Stop/Change Metric:**
 Halt the price increase rollout immediately if **Net Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) from the test cohort declines for two consecutive months** after the price change, or if the **blended churn rate across the entire base exceeds 6% per quarter** within 90 days of implementation.
