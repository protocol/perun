**Recommendation:** Do not launch the full 5,000-unit fleet this quarter; the current unit economics show a projected monthly net loss of approximately $340,000–$350,000, making the 12-month break-even target mathematically impossible without drastic changes to pricing, utilization, or cost structure. A phased pilot of 500 units is recommended to validate utilization and the congestion-fee impact before committing the full $8M budget.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Utilization Overestimation:** The model assumes 2 rides/bike/day immediately. If actual utilization is only 1 ride/day, revenue halves to $450k while fixed costs remain, doubling the monthly burn and extending break-even beyond the company's runway.
2.  **Unmodeled Regulatory & Insurance Costs:** Excluding insurance ($22.5k/month) and treating the congestion fee as a minor variable ignores that these are direct marginal costs. If the fee hits the high end ($20k) and insurance claims exceed estimates, the contribution margin per ride could turn negative.
3.  **Cannibalization vs. Incremental Revenue:** Assuming 70% of rides come from existing users risks cannibalizing higher-margin ride-share revenue. If these users switch from a $15 ride-share trip to a $3 bike ride without generating new volume, overall company revenue may decline despite high bike utilization.

**Pull-the-Plug Metric:** Sustain a **Monthly Net Loss greater than $250,000 for two consecutive months** post-launch, or a **utilization rate below 0.8 rides per bike per day** after the first 60 days of operation.
