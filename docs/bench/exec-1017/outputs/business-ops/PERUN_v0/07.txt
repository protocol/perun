**Recommendation for Maya Patel:** Do not roll out the price increase to all customers in Q3 2024 yet. Instead, execute a **controlled cohort test** on 10–15% of new signups or low-risk existing accounts immediately. Your current model ignores the $0.50/user support cost (reducing your net price lift from $3.00 to $2.50) and assumes zero elasticity despite having no price-sensitivity data. A blanket rollout risks triggering churn >8%, which would mathematically erase the revenue gain and damage LTV:CAC ratios before you can validate the –0.3 elasticity assumption.

**How This Could Fail:**
1.  **Churn Sensitivity:** If actual elasticity is –1.0 instead of –0.3, losing just 15% of customers negates the entire $3M projected lift.
2.  **Margin Compression:** The unmodeled $0.50/user ops cost reduces contribution margin; if volume drops, fixed support costs per remaining user rise further.
3.  **Competitive Response:** Mid-size manufacturers may switch to competitors if Peoplegrid becomes the price leader without added value perception.

**Stop/Pivot Threshold:** Halt the expansion if **monthly churn exceeds 8%** in the test cohort for two consecutive months, or if **net revenue retention (NRR) drops below 95%**.

*Note: Use the test data to run a Van Westendorp price-sensitivity survey before full implementation. Bring the cohort's actual CAC and churn figures to the CFO meeting to replace assumptions with observed data.*
