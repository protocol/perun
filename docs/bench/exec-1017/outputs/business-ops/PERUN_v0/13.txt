**Recommendation:** Proceed with the price increase to $2.49 only if you can validate the 38% opt-in assumption via a limited A/B test before the board meeting. The arithmetic supports the move *if* conversion holds, but the reliance on a 7-point jump in opt-in rate (from 31% to 38%) while raising prices is a high-risk assumption that contradicts typical demand elasticity. Do not commit to the full rollout based solely on linear projections.

**How This Could Fail:**
1.  **Elasticity Error:** Raising prices typically reduces conversion. Assuming opt-ins rise from 31% to 38% *while* increasing price by 25% is mathematically optimistic; if opt-ins stay flat or drop, revenue gains vanish.
2.  **Compliance Floor:** You modeled a range ($8k–$12k/quarter) but did not stress-test the P&L against the maximum ($12k). If user growth stalls and costs hit the upper bound, contribution margin could turn negative.
3.  **Linear Growth Fallacy:** Projecting 15% month-over-month growth linearly through Q4 ignores market saturation; if MAU growth slows, the fixed $5,000 server cost becomes a larger percentage of revenue, eroding margins.
4.  **Churn Underestimation:** A 4% monthly churn on a higher price point often increases; a rise to 6% churn would negate the lifetime value (LTV) gains from the price hike.

**Pivot Metric:** **Net New Premium Subscribers.** If the net addition of premium subscribers (new opt-ins minus churn) drops below **1,200 users per month** after the price change, stop the rollout. This threshold ensures you are covering the variable data costs ($0.03/user) and contributing sufficiently to the fixed server and potential compliance costs.

*Note: The compliance fee is a regulatory determination; confirm the exact liability with counsel before finalizing Q4 budgets. The "38% opt-in" figure must be treated as an unverified hypothesis until tested.*
