Your unit economics support the move: raising the price from $20 to $25 increases your LTV significantly without increasing CAC, improving your LTV:CAC ratio from ~6.4x to ~8.0x (assuming churn holds). The add-on introduces high-margin revenue ($450 contribution per 1% conversion) that directly offsets potential hosting cost increases. **How this plan could fail:**
1.  **Churn Sensitivity:** Your model assumes churn remains at 7%. If the price increase causes churn to spike to just 9%, you lose ~108 customers/month instead of 84. At $25/month, that extra churn burns ~$600 in MRR monthly, negating nearly half the gain from the price hike alone within six months.
2.  **Regulatory Cost Shock:** If the EU data-residency compliance requires dedicated infrastructure rather than a simple configuration change, hosting costs could rise disproportionately. If these costs exceed the $720 MRR generated by the add-on, the "Premium Events" tier effectively subsidizes compliance rather than driving profit.
3.  **Conversion Friction:** If the 4% upgrade conversion does not materialize because the $15 add-on is perceived as essential rather than premium (forcing a de facto price hike to $40 for many), effective churn may rise as users cancel rather than pay the combined rate.
