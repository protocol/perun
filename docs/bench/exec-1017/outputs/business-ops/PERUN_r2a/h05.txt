**The Arithmetic (Lane A):**
*   **Current State ($85):** Gross Profit per Unit = $85 × 18% = **$15.30**.
*   **Proposed State ($95):** Assuming variable costs remain fixed at $69.70 ($85 - $15.30), the new margin is $25.30 per unit (26.6%).
*   **The Break-Even Sensitivity:** To maintain the same *total* gross profit dollars, you can only afford a **39.6% drop in unit volume**.
    *   *Formula:* Required Volume = (Old Margin × Old Volume) ÷ New Margin.
    *   *Calculation:* ($15.30 × 1.5M) ÷ $25.30 ≈ 907,000 units.
*   **The Hidden Risk:** Your CAC is $28. At the current $15.30 contribution margin, you are losing **$12.70 on every new customer acquired** before accounting for LTV realization. Raising the price improves unit margin but does not fix the negative immediate contribution unless LTV realization is immediate and guaranteed. If the price hike causes conversion to drop from 2% to 1.5% (a standard elasticity reaction), you lose 25% of volume, reducing total gross profit despite the higher price per unit.

**Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Regulatory Surprise (Lane R Trigger):** The September 2024 FDA guidance on packaging materials is a legal/compliance variable. If this mandates new packaging, your Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) will rise. If you raise prices now based on old COGS, and COGS spikes in September, your margin expansion vanishes. *Action:* Route FDA impact assessment to legal/compliance counsel immediately; do not finalize pricing until the cost impact is modeled.
2.  **Volume Elasticity:** A $10 increase (11.7%) on a commodity-adjacent cleaning kit often triggers disproportionate churn. If repeat order frequency drops by >15%, the LTV of $210 collapses, invalidating the CAC payback model.
3.  **Negative Contribution Amplification:** With a current negative unit contribution ($15.30 margin - $28 CAC), increasing marketing spend ($500k Instagram) accelerates cash burn unless the price hike immediately fixes the conversion-to-margin ratio.
