**Applied Unit Economics (Lane A)**
*Current State (Annualized Estimate):*
- **Gross Revenue:** 12,000 users × $15 × 12 months = $2.16M (Note: Your $2.7M figure ignored churn; this uses steady-state logic).
- **Churn Impact:** At 5% quarterly churn (~1.7% monthly), steady-state revenue is significantly lower than gross booked. A simple approximation: Effective Users ≈ 12,000 × (1 - 0.05) ≈ 11,400 stable base.
- **Support Cost:** 12,000 × $0.50 × 12 = $72,000.
- **Net Contribution:** Revenue - Support - CAC amortization.

*Proposed State ($18/mo):*
- **Upside:** $3/user/mo × 12,000 users = +$432,000 potential gross uplift.
- **Break-even Churn Threshold:** To maintain current revenue, you can afford to lose up to 16.7% of your user base immediately ($15/$18 = 0.833 retention required). If the "3% drop" refers to *conversion rate* on new leads, not existing churn, the risk is slower growth, not immediate revenue collapse.
- **Compliance Drag:** 3 states × ~$10k avg = ~$30k one-time. Negligible against $432k upside *if* retention holds.

**Main Failure Modes**
1.  **Churn Underestimation:** The "3% drop" assumption is thin data. If price elasticity causes >5% monthly churn in existing users, the $3 uplift is erased within 4–6 months.
2.  **Pilot Contamination:** Applying the hike before the 300 pilot users convert in Q4 may cause them to abandon before becoming paying customers, wasting the acquisition spend ($30 × 300 = $9k).
3.  **Compliance Timing:** Filing fees are known, but if the price change triggers a "material change" notification requirement in those three states before filing is complete, you risk regulatory friction.

**Halt Trigger**
Stop the rollout or revert to $15 if **Net ARR (after churn and support costs) falls below $2.4M** within 90 days of implementation, or if **monthly churn exceeds 2.5%** (approx. 30% annualized) among the priced-up cohort.
