**Recommendation:** Proceed with the price increase to $119 **only if** you validate the $12.5M ARR uplift assumption against a sensitivity analysis where churn exceeds 4%. Your current model assumes zero elasticity (churn stays at 4% despite a 20% price hike), which is statistically improbable. The math supports the move *if* your retention holds, but the risk lies entirely in that single input.

**Applied Unit Economics (Lane A):**
*   **Current LTV:** $99/mo × 36 months = $3,564 (Revenue only). Your stated $4,320 likely includes the $5 surcharge or gross margin adjustments not fully detailed.
*   **New LTV (at $119, 4% churn):** $119/mo × 36 months = $4,284.
*   **LTV:CAC Ratio:** $4,284 / $1,200 = **3.57x**. This remains healthy (>3.0x), suggesting the unit economics work *if* churn holds.
*   **Break-even Churn Sensitivity:** To maintain the same *total monthly revenue* from the existing base (5,200 practices), you can afford to lose roughly 16.8% of your customers immediately (one-time drop) or sustain a higher ongoing churn rate.
    *   Current Monthly Revenue: 5,200 × $99 = $514,800.
    *   Required Customers at $119: $514,800 / $119 ≈ 4,326 practices.
    *   Allowable One-Time Drop: ~874 practices (16.8%).
    *   *However*, if ongoing monthly churn spikes from 4% to **6%**, the average customer lifetime drops from 36 months to ~16.7 months.
    *   **Revised LTV at 6% churn:** $119 × 16.7 = $1,987.
    *   **Revised LTV:CAC:** $1,987 / $1,200 = **1.65x**. This is below the sustainable threshold for SaaS growth, destroying unit economics.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Churn Elasticity:** The assumption that churn stays at 4% is the critical vulnerability. A rise to 6% collapses LTV below CAC.
2.  **Competitor Reaction:** If ClearDental undercuts by $10 ($109 vs your $119), price-sensitive segments may switch, accelerating churn beyond the 6% trigger.
3.  **Support Cost Variance:** If the $5 surcharge does not fully cover the marginal support cost of retained users, gross margin dilution occurs.
