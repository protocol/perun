**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with a blanket price increase to $15 yet; instead, run a targeted Van Westendorp price-sensitivity survey on 20 key accounts immediately to validate the –0.3 elasticity assumption before the June 12 meeting.

**The Arithmetic (Lane A)**
Your projected $3 M lift assumes zero volume loss, but your unit economics reveal a fragile break-even point once support costs are included.
*   **Current Contribution Margin:** $12.00 (price) – $0.50 (new support cost) = $11.50 per user/month.
*   **Proposed Contribution Margin:** $15.00 – $0.50 = $14.50 per user/month.
*   **Break-Even Retention Rate:** To match current total contribution ($11.50 × 10,000 users = $115k/mo), you need only $115k / $14.50 ≈ 7,931 users. This implies you can lose **20.7% of your user base** and still match current cash flow.
*   **The Risk:** Your assumed elasticity (–0.3) suggests a 25% price hike yields only a 7.5% volume drop. However, without data, if actual elasticity is –1.0 (unit elastic), a 25% hike causes a 25% drop, wiping out the gain and leaving you below current contribution.

**Decision Memo for CFO**
*   **Call:** Pause blanket implementation; validate willingness-to-pay (WTP) first.
*   **Key Number:** The plan flips from profit-positive to negative if churn exceeds **20.7%** total (not just monthly) or if monthly churn spikes above **8%** sustained for two quarters.
*   **Sensitivity:** At 8% monthly churn (vs. 6%), Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) drops from ~$192 to ~$145 per user. With a $2,500 CAC, your LTV:CAC ratio falls from 3.1:1 to 2.3:1, dangerously close to the 3:1 danger zone for SaaS sustainability.
*   **Halt Trigger:** If the pilot survey indicates >15% of respondents label $15 "too expensive," abort the broad rollout.
*   **Fallback Trigger:** If post-launch monthly churn hits 7.5% in Q3, revert to $12 for renewals only.
*   **Next Step (This Week):** Draft the four Van Westendorp questions (Too Cheap, Cheap, Expensive, Too Expensive) and send to 20 diverse customers by Friday.

**How This Could Fail**
1.  **Elasticity Underestimation:** If elasticity is –0.8 instead of –0.3, you lose 20% of users, negating the revenue lift while increasing support strain.
2.  **Support Cost Creep:** If the $0.50 estimate is low and rises to $1.00 due to increased ticket volume from price complaints, margins compress further.
3.  **Competitive Response:** Competitors may anchor their pricing against your old $12 rate, positioning themselves as the "stable" alternative during your transition.
