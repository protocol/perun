**Recommendation:** Do not launch next quarter. The arithmetic shows a likely reduction in net revenue per loan without a confirmed legal pathway for the reduced spread, and the risk-reserve provision is currently unpriced. **Top Three Failure Modes:**
1.  **Regulatory Block:** The state regulator determines the reduced spread violates usury laws or licensing requirements for a "streamlined" product, forcing an immediate halt or retroactive repricing.
2.  **Margin Erosion:** The combination of lower interest income (6.5% vs 7.5%), eliminated origination fees (1.2%), and higher defaults (2.0% vs 1.5%) turns the product negative on a contribution basis once the correct risk reserve is applied.
3.  **Cannibalization without Growth:** The 20% capture rate consists entirely of existing customers refinancing from the higher-yield legacy product, shrinking the overall portfolio yield while increasing the fixed technology cost burden.

**Stop/Pivot Triggers:**
*   **Regulatory Objection:** Any formal inquiry or informal indication from the state regulator that the rate/fee structure is non-compliant.
*   **Default Rate > 1.8%:** If early vintages exceed 1.8% (approaching the 2.0% model assumption) within the first two quarters, pause origination to recalibrate the risk model and reserve.
*   **Negative Unit Economics:** If the calculated contribution margin (Interest + Fees − Cost of Funds − Operating Cost − Expected Loss − Risk Reserve) is negative on a per-loan basis using the actual Q3 data.

**Note:** The current model omits the dollar value of the increased risk reserve; this must be calculated before a final go/no-go decision. The "20% capture" of existing customers requires careful tracking to ensure net portfolio growth.
