**Recommendation:** Proceed with a targeted price increase test rather than a blanket 20% hike immediately. Your current math shows a unit contribution of $7 ($12 price − $5 cost). A 20% hike raises price to $14.40, increasing contribution to $9.40 per seat. However, your stated revenue figure contains an error: $12 × 150,000 students × 12 months equals $21.6 million, not $18 million. Correcting this baseline is critical before modeling growth.

**How this could fail:**
1.  **Churn Sensitivity:** Your model assumes churn stays ≤3% despite a 20% price jump. If actual churn exceeds 4%, the lost recurring revenue from departing districts outweighs the margin gain from the price hike, reducing total annual profit.
2.  **Compliance Cost Overrun:** The undefined state data-privacy certification is a Lane R (regulatory) variable. If the final cost hits the high end ($300k) or exceeds it due to scope creep, it directly erodes the incremental profit generated by the price increase.
3.  **Contractual Friction:** District 7's March 15 renewal is a hard deadline. Attempting to impose a 20% hike during renewal negotiations without prior value demonstration could trigger a "walk-away" scenario, losing 1/3 of your base instantly.

**Stop/Reconsider Triggers:**
*   **Churn > 4%:** If pilot data or historical elasticity suggests churn will exceed 4%, halt the broad rollout.
*   **Certification Cost > $250k:** If firm quotes for compliance exceed $250k, the net benefit of the price hike diminishes significantly relative to the risk; reconsider the pricing strategy or seek cost-sharing with districts.
*   **District 7 Rejection:** If District 7 explicitly rejects the increase during renewal talks, pause the strategy for the other two districts to avoid a coordinated pushback.

**Decision Aid:**
*   *Break-even Churn Check:* You can afford to lose ~14% of your volume if you capture the full 20% price increase on the remainder, assuming fixed costs don't rise. Your 3-4% assumption is safe *if* accurate, but unverified.
*   *Next Step:* Before June 30, instrument a "willingness-to-pay" signal (e.g., a Van Westendorp survey item in the next administrator NPS) to validate the 20% tolerance. Do not rely on the "assumption" alone.
