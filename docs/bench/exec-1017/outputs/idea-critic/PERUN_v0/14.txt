**Recommendation: Proceed only under a "Go-If" condition.**
Do not launch the full rollout on August 15, 2025. Instead, approve a **4-week limited pilot** in a single genre, conditional on verifying that content moderation costs do not exceed 40% of the projected incremental revenue. The current plan assumes a uniform 12% CPM lift and ignores the marginal cost of higher ad volume, creating a high risk of negative unit economics despite top-line growth.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Margin Erosion via Moderation:** The proposal explicitly excludes content moderation costs. If higher ad volume requires linear increases in human review or advanced AI filtering, the $150k/month hosting hike plus unmodeled moderation expenses could wipe out the projected $300k six-month revenue gain, resulting in a net loss.
2.  **Churn Outpacing Conversion:** You aim to lift free-to-premium conversion from 4% to 8%, yet admit zero data on how increased ad load affects churn. If aggressive ad insertion drives free-user churn up by even 2%, the loss of lifetime value from departing users will likely exceed the gains from the small fraction converting to premium.
3.  **Genre-Specific CPM Collapse:** Assuming a flat 12% CPM increase across all genres is statistically fragile. High-frequency ad insertion often degrades listener engagement in narrative or long-form genres, potentially causing CPMs to drop below the $20 baseline in those segments while only lifting performance in short-form content, skewing the overall average downward.

**Pre-Agreed Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
Halt the rollout immediately if, at the end of Month 2 of the pilot, the **Net Profit per Listener** (defined as: *Incremental Ad Revenue + Incremental Subscription Revenue – Hosting Costs – Estimated Moderation Costs*) falls below **$0.04**. This metric accounts for the hidden moderation variable and ensures you are not buying revenue at a loss.

*Note: The current $300k revenue projection appears overstated if it relies on a 12% lift across the entire 2.3M base without accounting for the ramp-up period or potential inventory fill-rate issues.*
