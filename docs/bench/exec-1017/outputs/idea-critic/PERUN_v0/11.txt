**Recommendation:** Proceed with the centralized hub and triage nurses, but **delay the $250,000 cloud software purchase** until a 30-day pilot proves the nurses alone can reduce no-shows.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Shortfall:** The projected $9,000/month savings relies entirely on a 20% reduction in no-shows; if triage nurses only achieve a 5% reduction, the new salaries will exceed savings, creating an immediate net loss.
2.  **Integration Downtime:** The assumption of "zero integration downtime" is historically improbable; even a 48-hour system blackout during migration could cancel hundreds of appointments, eroding trust and revenue faster than the efficiency gains can rebuild them.
3.  **Patient Satisfaction Collapse:** Centralizing three local desks into one hub often increases hold times and reduces personalized care; if patient satisfaction scores drop by more than 10%, referral rates may decline, offsetting all operational gains.

**Kill Criterion:** Terminate the project at the end of Q1 if the **net monthly savings (recovered no-show revenue minus new nurse salaries) is less than $4,000** or if **patient complaint volume regarding scheduling increases by >15%**.

*Note: This approach secures the operational benefit of triage nurses while treating the expensive software as a variable cost dependent on proven results. The deadline of 15 Oct 2024 allows time to structure this phased pilot.*
