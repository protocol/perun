**Recommendation:** Do not launch the "Performance Pack" on 1 March 2025 as proposed. Instead, run a 60-day pilot with 500 existing high-value customers to validate the upgrade conversion rate and true logistics cost before committing the $650,000 in upfront capital.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Math Error:** Your projection assumes 8,000 *subscribers* generating $2M, but $25 × 8,000 × 12 months equals $2.4M. If you meant $2M revenue, you only need ~6,667 subscribers. More critically, this ignores the 5% monthly churn; sustaining 8,000 active subscribers requires acquiring ~530 new customers *every month* just to replace those leaving, drastically inflating the required marketing spend beyond the $500k budget.
2.  **Margin Collapse:** The 20% gross margin ($5.00/box) is fragile. You excluded the $0.08/mile fuel surcharge. If average delivery is 20 miles, that's $1.60/box, slashing margin to 13.6%. Any food cost variance or packaging error pushes this toward breakeven or loss.
3.  **Unverified Demand:** You have zero data on how many of the 22,000 existing customers will upgrade. If the conversion rate is below 2.5% (needing 550 upgrades), the entire subscriber target relies entirely on new customer acquisition, which is significantly more expensive and risky.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Pivot Triggers):**
*   **Churn > 8% per month** during the pilot (indicates the product/price fit is insufficient for elite athletes).
*   **Net Margin < 12%** after accounting for fuel surcharges and actual packaging waste (indicates the unit economics cannot support the logistics model).
*   **Upgrade Conversion < 1.5%** from the existing base after 30 days (indicates the core assumption about current customer demand is false).

*Note: The 15-minute delivery improvement must be quantified in dollars to justify the fuel cost; do not assume it boosts retention without evidence. Recalculate the $2M revenue target using a cohort model that includes churn, not a simple multiplication of end-state subscribers.*
