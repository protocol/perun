**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" conditional launch.**
Launch the bundle only if a binding pilot conversion rate of >45% is secured from the existing 500 customers before committing the full $150k budget. The unit economics are attractive ($80 incremental revenue vs. negligible marginal cost), but the 80% upgrade assumption is dangerously optimistic compared to the 12-restaurant pilot sample.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Adoption Cliff:** The 80% upgrade projection assumes near-universal appeal based on a tiny, likely biased pilot (12 users). If real-world conversion lands at a more typical 20–30%, you will fail to recover the $120k development cost within the first year, turning the project into a cash drain rather than a growth engine.
2.  **Support & Complexity Hidden Costs:** Bundling creates a single point of failure. If the "lightweight" inventory tracker bugs out, it threatens the core payroll relationship. The $120k budget likely covers build, not the sustained support burden of troubleshooting inventory discrepancies for 500+ restaurants, which could erode margins and brand trust.
3.  **Competitor Pre-emption:** If FoodFlow launches a similar bundle before January 2026, your "first-mover" advantage vanishes. Without a locked-in user base, you may be forced into a price war, compressing the $179 price point and destroying the projected ROI.

**Kill Criteria:**
Abort or pivot the project immediately if the pre-launch binding pilot (offering the bundle to 50 random existing customers) yields a conversion rate below **35%** or if customer support tickets related to inventory data accuracy exceed **5% of active bundle users** in the first 30 days post-launch.
