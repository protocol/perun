**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" Pilot, Not a Full Rollout.**

Do not commit to the full $220,000 budget or the 12,000-student target yet. Instead, launch a limited pilot with the three midsize districts starting 1 May 2025, but condition the full expansion on verifying two critical assumptions before 15 June 2025: (1) that at least two of the three pilot districts have confirmed budget allocation exceeding $0.10 per student specifically for this tool, and (2) that the state compliance deadline of 31 July 2025 legally mandates the specific type of simulation you are building. If these are not confirmed, pivot to a lower-cost consulting model or halt development.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Budget Misalignment:** Schools may have cybersecurity budgets, but they are likely already allocated to mandatory compliance tools rather than optional gamified simulations; your $0.10 assumption is unverified and may be zero.
2.  **Unvalidated Competitive Gap:** If SecureLearn's 40% higher engagement claim is true and price-insensitive, your $8 price point may signal lower quality rather than better value, causing districts to stick with the incumbent despite the cost.
3.  **Integration Debt:** You explicitly excluded integration licensing fees; if districts require LMS (Learning Management System) integration to adopt, these unbudgeted costs could erase your margin before the first invoice is paid.

**Kill/Pivot Trigger:**
If, by 15 June 2025, fewer than two of the three pilot districts sign a letter of intent confirming they will pay the full $8/student rate *and* cover any necessary integration fees, **kill the product build** and pivot to selling the simulation as a add-on module through an existing platform partner instead of a standalone service.

*Note: Your Q1 revenue math assumes immediate full adoption; realistically, sales cycles in K-12 often exceed the quarter, so cash flow will likely lag this projection.*
*Ensure the "compliance deadline" is a hard legal mandate for simulations, not just general training, or the urgency disappears.*
