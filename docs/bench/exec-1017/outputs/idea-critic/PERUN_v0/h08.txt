**Recommendation: Proceed with a conditional pilot ("Go-If"), not a full build.**

Do not commit the full $250k development budget yet. Instead, launch a 30-day manual concierge pilot with 100 landlords to validate the willingness to pay the $5 surcharge before writing code. The current data (50 mixed survey responses) is statistically insufficient to justify a $280k total investment for a projected $35k/month return. If the pilot confirms a >70% acceptance rate, proceed to full development immediately.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **False Revenue Assumption:** The survey sample (50 landlords) is too small and mixed to represent the 1,200-landlord base. If actual acceptance is only 40% rather than the projected 70%, monthly revenue drops to ~$20k, extending the payback period from ~8 months to over 14 months and potentially failing to cover the increased support load you assumed would remain static.
2.  **Hidden Support Costs:** The assumption that support load will not increase is risky. Automated renewals often fail due to edge cases (e.g., rent control changes, partial payments, or disputed lease terms). If even 10% of automated renewals require human intervention, your support costs could rise by $15k–$20k annually, eroding the net margin of the new module.
3.  **Churn Contagion:** Introducing a mandatory or opt-in surcharge may trigger higher-than-historic churn. If the 5% annual churn rate spikes to 8% among landlords who refuse the fee but leave the platform entirely, the loss of existing revenue ($1,250/unit) could outweigh the gains from the new surcharge.

**Kill Criteria:**

Terminate the project if the manual pilot reveals a **willingness-to-pay conversion rate below 60%** after 30 days. This threshold provides a safety margin below your 70% target; falling below it indicates the unit economics cannot support the $250k development cost within an acceptable timeframe.

*Note: The "concierge" approach (manual execution) validates the market risk for near-zero engineering cost. Ensure your pilot tracks support ticket volume per renewal to validate the "no extra load" assumption before scaling.*
