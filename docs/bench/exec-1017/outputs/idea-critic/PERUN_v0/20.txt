**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **conditional pilot (Go-If)** rather than full development. Commit the $120k only if a 4-week prototype with 5 enterprise customers demonstrates at least **15% adoption intent** and confirms the **30% build time reduction** without exceeding **$40k in prototype costs**. Do not build the full distributed agent yet; simulate the performance gain on a subset of infrastructure to validate the core assumption that speed drives renewal before locking in the architecture.

**How This Could Fail:**
1.  **Value Mismatch:** The 11-minute savings (35m to 24m) may not justify a $9/seat add-on if competitors offer unlimited builds for $12, making your pricing appear poor value despite the speed gain.
2.  **Adoption Illusion:** The assumed 30% uptake could be far lower if the friction of opting into an add-on outweighs the perceived benefit, leaving you with high fixed costs and low revenue.
3.  **Churn Misdiagnosis:** If the 7% churn is driven by features, support, or pricing rather than build speed, the investment will miss the 4% target entirely.

**Kill Criteria:**
Terminate or fundamentally rethink the project if:
*   **Adoption:** Pilot sign-ups are below **10%** of the invited enterprise cohort.
*   **Performance:** Actual build time reduction is less than **20%** (failing to hit the majority of the promised 30% gain).
*   **Cost:** Prototype development exceeds **$40k** or full project costs breach **$150k**.
*   **Revenue:** After 3 months of general availability, the add-on generates less than **$15k MRR** (implying <550 seats sold, well below the 30% uptake model).
