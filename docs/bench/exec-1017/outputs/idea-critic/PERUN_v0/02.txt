**Recommendation: MODIFY (Proceed with a Phased Pilot, Not Full Rollout)**

Do not authorize the full $1.2M outlay or the hiring of 12 officers immediately. Instead, approve a **90-day pilot** opening only **two** branches and hiring **four** officers, with a hard capital cap of $500k. This approach tests the "instant-approve" competitor threat and your internal underwriting capacity assumptions before committing to the full 30% staff overhead increase. The current plan's revenue projection is flawed: a simple 15% volume increase does not equal 15% revenue growth if net interest margins compress due to competitive pressure or if the cost of funds rises. Furthermore, assuming flat interest rates and zero additional compliance staffing for a 15% book expansion creates significant regulatory risk; loan volume growth typically triggers disproportionate compliance workload that existing staff cannot absorb without overtime burnout or errors.

1.  **Margin Compression & Revenue Miss:** Competitor "instant-approve" products force rate cuts to win business, meaning the $2M revenue gain (based on static rates) fails to materialize despite hitting volume targets.
2.  **Operational Bottleneck:** The assumption that current underwriting capacity handles 15% more volume without new compliance staff is false; this leads to processing delays, regulatory breaches, or staff turnover just as new branches open.
3.  **Fixed-Cost Trap:** Signing leases and hiring 12 officers based on optimistic adoption rates creates a high fixed-cost base that cannot be reduced if the fintech competitor captures more than 20% of the target market share.
