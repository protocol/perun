**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with the pitch only if you reframe the commitment as a **6-month pilot** capped at $1.2M, contingent on verifying API latency (<200ms) and securing the data-sharing agreement before full development funding is released. Do not commit to the full $3M or the 12-month rollout yet.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **Latency-Induced Obsolescence:** The "under 500ms" estimate is likely an average, not a p99 guarantee. If real-world latency spikes above 2 seconds during peak congestion (when the data is most valuable), the feature becomes misleading rather than helpful, eroding user trust instead of driving the projected 8% ridership lift.
2.  **Regulatory & Privacy Bottleneck:** Assuming the data-sharing agreement will be signed by Q2 is a single point of failure. Municipal data deals often stall on anonymization standards; if the city requires stricter aggregation that reduces granularity (e.g., station-level vs. car-level), the core value proposition collapses while costs remain fixed.
3.  **Revenue Attribution Error:** The model assumes a direct $0.78 per-ride bump from crowding data. However, if the 8% ridership lift comes from existing users shifting travel times rather than new net riders, or if the feature drives users to less crowded but lower-revenue routes, the projected $2.5M annual revenue gain will not materialize, extending the payback period beyond the budgeted two years.

**Kill Criterion**
Terminate the project if, after six months of the pilot, the **net new ridership lift is below 3%** (less than half the projected 8%) OR if the **p99 API latency exceeds 1.5 seconds** for more than 5% of peak-hour requests.

*Note: The current plan treats unverified assumptions (latency, legal signing) as facts; the pilot structure forces validation before full spend. Ensure the "anonymized" claim withstands legal scrutiny regarding Bluetooth beacon re-identification risks.*
