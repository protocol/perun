**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with the 90-day pilot only if you secure a written commitment from the three clinics to share daily operational data and if the total development burn rate is capped at $150,000/month. Do not commit to the full $650,000 investment or the 18-month revenue projection until the pilot validates the 30% no-show reduction.

**Main Failure Modes**
1.  **Adoption Math Error:** Your projection assumes 84 customers (70% of 120) adopt in six months. At a 30% no-show drop yielding ~$66k/month/clinic, you need roughly 18 months just to recoup the $650k initial investment *if* adoption is instant. If adoption ramps slowly (typical for B2B), your 18-month "$1.2M incremental revenue" target is mathematically impossible unless new market pull is massive, which you admitted is unknown.
2.  **Pilot Selection Bias:** Testing only "top clinics" with high volumes (~1,200 appointments) creates a best-case scenario. These clinics likely have better staff compliance and patient demographics than your average customer. A 30% drop here may translate to only 10–15% in typical clinics, destroying the unit economics for the broader rollout.
3.  **Implementation Drag:** The $450k development budget implies a ~7-month runway at standard rates. If the July 1 start date slips, the 90-day pilot pushes into Q4, a historically slow period for elective dental procedures, skewing results downward.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Trigger**
Terminate the project immediately if, by Day 45 of the pilot, the aggregate no-show rate reduction across the three clinics is less than **15%** (half your target) OR if the cost to acquire one additional new practice via the marketing spend exceeds **$4,000**.

**Hidden Risks**
*   **Revenue Cannibalization:** If the "SmartSchedule" fills slots faster but shifts patients to lower-margin time slots or discount tiers to ensure attendance, the net revenue lift could be negligible despite higher utilization.
*   **Staff Resistance:** Front-desk staff often manage no-shows manually; automating this may be perceived as a threat or create extra work if the system requires manual override, leading to sabotage or non-compliance that skews pilot data.
*   **Contractual Liability:** If the module fails and causes revenue loss for the pilot clinics, your current contracts may not limit liability for "software performance," exposing you to churn risk among your top 3 accounts.

*Note: Your $66k/month lift calculation assumes 100% of recovered appointments are successfully filled and paid; verify your actual fill-rate capacity before presenting the $1.2M figure.*
*The "unknown" market pull is the largest variable; treat the $200k marketing spend as experimental R&D, not guaranteed growth capital.*
