Proceed with the pilot launch only if you immediately revise the revenue model to account for a realistic ramp-up period and secure a minimum of 10 signed letters of intent (LOIs) before committing the full $200k marketing and lease budget. Your current math assumes 100% instant saturation of 500 locations in Q3, which is operationally impossible for a new B2B channel; the "flat consumption" assumption also contradicts your own concern about work-from-home trends.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **The "Instant Scale" Fallacy:** Signing 500 corporate clients in one quarter requires an enterprise sales cycle (3–6 months) that clashes with your Q3 launch date. If you spend the $200k budget trying to force this speed, you will burn cash on unqualified leads or unsustainable discounts, leaving you with high churn once the 12-month contracts expire.
2.  **Margin Erosion via Hidden Logistics:** Your $2 shipping cost per bag likely fails at scale for fragmented office deliveries. If offices consume less than the projected 100 bags/month due to lower headcount, your per-unit shipping and servicing costs will spike, turning the projected 52% gross margin into a loss on every under-performing account.
3.  **Structural Demand Decline:** Banking on "flat" office consumption ignores the structural shift to hybrid work. If average daily attendance drops 20%, consumption could drop 40% (as remote days eliminate office coffee entirely), leaving offices with stale inventory and cancelling contracts early, invalidating the 12-month revenue certainty.

**Kill/Pivot Trigger**
**Trigger:** If fewer than **15 locations** have signed binding 12-month contracts and are actively receiving shipments by the end of Month 2 of the launch quarter.
**Action:** Immediately halt the $120k marketing spend and pivot to a "concierge" model targeting only 50 high-density campuses rather than 500 distributed offices.
