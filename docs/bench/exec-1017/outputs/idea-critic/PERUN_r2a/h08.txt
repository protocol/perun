**Recommendation: GO, conditional on a 45-day pilot validating willingness-to-pay.**
The potential $420k annual net profit (Year 2) justifies the $280k total initial outlay, provided the core assumption about surcharge acceptance holds. **Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Survey Sampling Error:** The 50-landlord survey is statistically insufficient (4% sample size) to predict behavior for 1,200 landlords. If the broader population reacts to the "mixed" signals rather than the positive ones, actual adoption could drop below 40%, rendering the $35k revenue target unachievable and extending payback beyond 3 years.
2.  **Hidden Support Costs:** The assumption that support load will remain static is high-risk. Introducing a fee often triggers dispute spikes, billing inquiries, and cancellation requests. If just 10% of adopting landlords require manual intervention, the cost of additional support staff could erase 20–30% of the projected margin.
3.  **Churn Contagion:** The 5% churn rate is historic for a free/standard service. Monetizing a previously free renewal process may increase overall churn beyond 5% if landlords perceive the value proposition as degraded, causing a loss of base rental volume that outweighs the new surcharge revenue.

**Kill Trigger:**
Terminate the project immediately if the pilot conversion rate (landlords actively opting into the $5 surcharge) is **below 55%** after 45 days. This threshold provides a safety buffer below the 70% target; falling short of it indicates the $35k revenue model is mathematically impossible to sustain at scale.

*Note: The "mixed" survey results suggest segmentation is required; target high-volume landlords first where $5/unit is a smaller friction point. Ensure the pilot measures support ticket volume per 100 adopters, not just conversion.*
