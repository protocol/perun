**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Margin Compression & Negative Unit Economics:** If interest rates rise or the competitor forces a rate war to defend their "instant-approve" share, your net margin could drop below the break-even point required to cover the 30% staff increase. The plan assumes flat rates and ignores the cost of funds; a 1% rate hike could erase profitability on the new volume.
2.  **Regulatory & Compliance Bottleneck:** The assumption that "current underwriting capacity can handle extra volume" is the highest risk. A 15% book growth usually triggers heightened regulatory scrutiny (e.g., CRA exams, concentration limits). Without additional compliance staff, you risk examination findings, fines, or forced lending halts that stall the very growth you are paying to accelerate.
3.  **Adoption Miscalculation (The "Fintech" Risk):** If small businesses prefer the competitor's digital experience over new physical branches, the $200k/branch technology spend becomes stranded capital. You are betting on brick-and-mortar expansion while the market signal (the competitor's product) suggests a shift to digital speed. If adoption of your new branches is <50% of forecast, the fixed costs of 12 new officers will drain capital reserves.
