**Recommendation:** Proceed with the 3-month pilot starting 1 Oct 2024, but **reject the 70% tutor adoption assumption** as the basis for financial planning. Instead, model the pilot success on a conservative 15% adoption rate (60 tutors), targeting a net revenue contribution of $48,000/month rather than $320,000. This shifts the goal from "maximizing immediate upside" to "validating unit economics and student demand" before scaling marketing spend.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Cannibalization of High-Margin Revenue:** Students may switch from existing one-on-one sessions (likely priced higher than $25/hr) to the cheaper group option, reducing overall revenue per tutor even if group classes fill up. The proposal assumes incremental growth without proving these are new customers or additional hours.
2.  **Tutor Capacity & Quality Dilution:** Managing 8 students simultaneously requires different skills than 1-on-1 tutoring. If tutors struggle to engage all participants, satisfaction scores could drop, churn increasing for both students and tutors. The 90-minute format may also lead to tutor burnout if not managed carefully.
3.  **False Positive from Early Adopters:** The 70% adoption assumption relies on enthusiastic early adopters. If the remaining 85% of tutors cannot fill classes due to lack of student interest or scheduling friction, the projected $320k/month revenue will not materialize, leaving fixed development costs uncovered.

**Halt Trigger:**
Terminate the pilot immediately if, by the end of Month 2, the **net revenue contribution per active group class tutor falls below $400/month** (after accounting for marketing amortization and support costs). This indicates the unit economics do not support scaling, regardless of total sign-up numbers.

**Hidden Risk Note:**
The proposal omits the cost of customer support and technical moderation for live multi-user video sessions, which scales non-linearly with group size and could erode the thin $25/hr margin if technical issues arise frequently.
