**Recommendation: NO-GO in current form; proceed only as a "Go-If" pilot.**

Do not launch the full package based on the current $675k ARR projection. The math relies on a 30% conversion rate from free to a $15k/year product within six months, which is exceptionally aggressive for solo practitioners without verified willingness-to-pay data. Instead, adopt a **Go-If** strategy: Proceed to full build only if a paid pilot of 10 lawyers achieves a 40% retention rate at the $15k price point over 90 days. If this threshold is not met, halt development and revert to a lower-tier offering.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Conversion Delusion:** The 30% conversion assumption ignores price sensitivity. Solo lawyers often resist high fixed costs; if actual conversion is only 5% (7.5 users), ARR drops to ~$112k, failing to cover the $195k upfront investment (licensing, UI, marketing) in year one.
2.  **Support Capacity Collapse:** The assumption that current staff can handle a 50% ticket increase with no new hires is a single point of failure. Premium users expect white-glove service; if response times slip due to overload, churn will spike immediately, invalidating the retention model.
3.  **Usage Mismatch:** The budget assumes 3 hours of usage per month. If the tool is too complex or solves a problem that only arises quarterly, perceived value will plummet, leading to non-renewal after the first year given the unknown churn risk.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Condition:**

Terminate the project immediately if **Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) does not reach $37.5k (equivalent to 30 paid subscribers) by 31 March 2025**. This date allows for the six-month ramp-up from a hypothetical Q4 launch. Falling short of 20% of the total addressable market (30 out of 150) by this deadline indicates the 30% conversion goal is unattainable.

*Note: The $675k figure assumes 100% of the 150 targeted lawyers are currently on the free tier; verify this denominator before finalizing any budget.*
