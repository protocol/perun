**How This Plan Could Fail:**
1.  **Revenue Overestimation:** The projection assumes 100% retention of the 8,000 subscribers throughout the year. With a 5% monthly churn, you will lose approximately 4,800 subscribers annually, requiring constant, expensive reacquisition to hit the $2M revenue target. The actual year-one revenue is likely closer to $1.2M–$1.4M.
2.  **Margin Erosion:** The 20% gross margin ($5.00/box) is fragile. The unpriced $0.08/mile fuel surcharge for refrigerated trucks could easily consume $1.00–$1.50 per box depending on delivery density, slashing margins by 20–30%. Additionally, influencer CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) is not amortized in your margin calculation; if CAC exceeds $40 per acquired subscriber, the unit economics turn negative immediately.
3.  **Cannibalization & Logistics:** Shifting existing customers to this premium tier may not be net-new revenue but a margin-neutral upgrade. Furthermore, the promised 15-minute delivery reduction may not correlate linearly with retention if the core value proposition is meal quality rather than speed, rendering the refrigerated truck investment inefficient.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Pivot Triggers):**
*   **Churn Trigger:** Halt expansion if monthly churn exceeds **7%** in the first 90 days (indicating the product does not retain elite athletes as hypothesized).
*   **Margin Trigger:** Pivot or stop if the verified net margin per box drops below **$3.50** (14%) after full logistics and fuel costs are applied.
*   **Conversion Trigger:** Do not scale beyond the pilot if fewer than **8%** of the targeted existing customer base upgrades within the first 60 days.

**Note:** Your current revenue math ignores churn decay; recalculate Year 1 revenue using a cohort decay model before presenting to the board. The $0.08/mile fuel cost must be converted to a per-box metric based on average drop density to validate the $4.50 net margin threshold.
