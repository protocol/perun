**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed only if a binding Service Level Agreement (SLA) guarantees API latency under 200ms for 99% of requests and the city data-sharing agreement is fully executed before development begins. Without these, the core value proposition collapses.

**Top Three Failure Modes**
1.  **Latency-Induced Obsolescence:** The "under 500ms" estimate is likely an average, not a percentile guarantee. If real-world latency spikes to 400–500ms during peak congestion (when users need data most), the crowd density display will lag behind reality, rendering it useless or misleading. This destroys user trust immediately.
2.  **Revenue Math Mismatch:** The projection assumes an 8% ridership lift (256k extra rides) purely from this feature. This conflates *feature adoption* with *net new ridership*. It is far more likely that existing riders will use the tool without increasing their total ride count, resulting in high engagement but zero incremental revenue to cover the $3M Year-1 cost.
3.  **Integration Underestimation:** Assuming backend integration takes only 4 weeks ignores the complexity of normalizing heterogeneous Bluetooth beacon data from a municipal network. Data cleaning, privacy scrubbing, and real-time synchronization often take months, jeopardizing the 12-month rollout and inflating the $2.5M development budget.

**Kill Criterion**
Terminate the project if, after a 6-month pilot, the feature fails to generate a **net increase of 1.5% in total daily ridership** (approx. 48k new rides/day) or if the average API latency exceeds **250ms** during peak hours for two consecutive weeks.

*Note: The $0.78 per-ride revenue bump must be rigorously defined as incremental fare income, not shifted usage. Ensure the legal team validates the "anonymized" status of the beacon data to prevent privacy litigation risks.*
