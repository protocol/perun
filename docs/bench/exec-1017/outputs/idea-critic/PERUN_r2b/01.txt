**Recommendation:** Proceed with the 3-month pilot starting 1 Oct 2024, but **revise the financial model** to base projections on a conservative 30% tutor adoption rate (not 70%) and explicitly cap group seats to prevent cannibalization of higher-margin 1:1 sessions. The potential $320k/month upside justifies the $200k initial outlay, provided the pilot validates student demand before full scaling.

**Top Three Failure Modes:**
1.  **Cannibalization:** Students switch from $40/hr private lessons to $25/hr groups, reducing revenue per tutor even if total student hours rise.
2.  **Adoption Friction:** Tutors fail to fill the 4 weekly groups due to scheduling complexity or lack of student interest, leaving the $800/week projection unmet.
3.  **Quality Dilution:** Managing 8 students in 90 minutes degrades the learning experience compared to 1:1, leading to churn in the core business.

**Halt Trigger:** Stop the rollout immediately if, by the end of Month 2 of the pilot, the **net revenue contribution** (group revenue minus lost 1:1 revenue and direct costs) is less than **$5,000 per participating tutor** for that month.

**Hidden Risks:**
*   **Support Overhead:** Group dynamics create complex scheduling conflicts and refund disputes that 1:1 does not, potentially spiking customer support costs.
*   **Technical Load:** Simultaneous video streams for 8 students × 400 tutors could strain infrastructure if not load-tested, risking platform stability for all users.

*Note: The 70% adoption assumption within one month is highly aggressive; the revised 30% baseline protects against over-hiring or over-marketing before product-market fit is proven.*
*Ensure the pilot tracks "net new revenue" specifically, distinguishing between students who are new to the platform versus those migrating from private lessons.*
