**Recommendation: Proceed with a Conditional Pilot, Not a Full Migration**

Do not commit to a full platform migration on January 15, 2025. Instead, authorize a 90-day "Strangler Fig" pilot targeting a single, non-critical module (e.g., attendance reporting) for one of the three interested districts. A "big bang" cutover of a student-information system serving 12,000 schools carries existential risk; if the vendor cannot quantify downtime costs, they have not performed the necessary failure modeling. Secure the $141k operational savings and validate the $1.2M revenue hypothesis on a small scale before exposing the entire installed base to migration failure.

**Primary Failure Modes**

*   **Technical (Data Integrity & Downtime):** The highest probability of catastrophic failure lies in data synchronization during the cutover. Student records, grades, and attendance data must remain perfectly consistent. If the legacy monolith and new micro-services drift even slightly during the transition, you face regulatory compliance violations and immediate loss of trust. The unquantified downtime cost likely hides the reality that a rollback could take days, not hours, leaving schools without access to critical data during peak academic periods.
*   **Contractual (Vendor Lock-in & SLA Gaps):** Moving to a custom cloud architecture often shifts liability. If the vendor's Service Level Agreement (SLA) does not explicitly cover data migration errors or define financial penalties for extended downtime exceeding 4 hours, Maple Learning Systems absorbs all risk. Furthermore, retiring the legacy license before the new system is proven removes your leverage to demand fixes or rollbacks.
*   **Adoption (The 80% Assumption):** Assuming 80% adoption (9,600 schools) within 12 months is dangerously optimistic for enterprise ed-tech. School districts operate on rigid fiscal and academic calendars; if the pilot reveals even minor workflow disruptions, procurement cycles will delay adoption by 12–18 months, destroying the projected Year 1 revenue.

**The Kill Switch Criteria**

Establish a hard "Stop/Pivot" trigger effective at the 4-month mark of the pilot. You must halt the broader migration and revert to the legacy roadmap if **either** of the following occurs:

1.  **Revenue Shortfall:** The pilot fails to secure a signed Letter of Intent for full deployment from at least **two** of the three pilot districts, indicating the realized value is insufficient to drive procurement.
2.  **Cost/Performance Overrun:** The pilot incurs unplanned engineering costs exceeding **$150,000** OR records any data inconsistency event requiring manual remediation.

If either threshold is breached, the micro-services architecture is not yet viable for your scale. Pause the initiative, renegotiate the vendor contract based on pilot data, and extend the legacy support term.
