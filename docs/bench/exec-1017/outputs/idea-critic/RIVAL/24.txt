**Recommendation: Proceed with a Phased Pilot, Not Full Launch**

Approve the $120,000 budget only for a limited Q3 pilot targeting 300 new donors (10% of the goal) rather than the full 600. The unit economics break even too narrowly ($45 cost vs. $45 first-year gift) to justify full-scale spending without validating the unknown churn rate. Use the pilot to measure actual 90-day retention and refine the acquisition cost before committing the remaining funds in Q4.

**Main Failure Modes**
1.  **Negative Lifetime Value (LTV):** If new donor churn exceeds 75% in year one, the campaign loses money despite hitting the headcount target, as the $45 acquisition cost is never recovered.
2.  **Channel Saturation:** The email list or community venues may not yield enough high-quality leads at the modeled $45 cost, forcing spending to rise to $60+ per donor to hit volume targets.
3.  **Operational Drag:** Hiring two coordinators creates fixed overhead; if recruitment lags or ramp-up takes >6 weeks, Q3 momentum dies while burn rate accelerates.

**Pre-Agreed Kill/Pivot Trigger**
Stop the initiative immediately if, by December 31, 2025, the **Net New Revenue from the cohort is below $15,000** (indicating an effective cost-per-acquisition >$50 or retention <50%). At that point, pivot funds from hiring to retention experiments or pause entirely.
