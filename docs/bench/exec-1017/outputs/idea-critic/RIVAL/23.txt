**Recommendation: Do not launch the "Unlimited Flex" tier as currently proposed.**

The financial model contains a critical arithmetic error and underestimates the operational risk. Your projection assumes 480 upgrades, but you failed to account for the revenue lost when those same members cancel their standard $85 subscriptions. You are counting the *incremental* $35 gain but ignoring the *cannibalization* of the base price.

**Corrected Month 1 Financials:**
*   **Gross Uplift:** 480 members × $35 = +$16,800
*   **Added Churn Cost:** You estimated a 1% total base churn increase (32 members). If these are lost from the *standard* pool, that is 32 × $85 = -$2,720 lost recurring revenue.
*   **Operating Cost:** -$4,500 (instructors)
*   **Net Monthly Gain:** $16,800 - $2,720 - $4,500 = **$9,580**
*   **Break-even Timeline:** $22,000 tech cost / $9,580 monthly profit = **2.3 months.**

While a 2.3-month break-even seems attractive, the model is fragile because it relies on the "15% uptake" happening instantly without accelerating churn beyond your conservative 1% estimate. In fitness studios, introducing a high-value unlimited tier often causes "self-selection," where your most frequent (and previously profitable) users upgrade, while casual users feel squeezed out or perceive the standard tier as poor value, leading to higher overall churn.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Cannibalization Acceleration:** If more than 15% of your *best* customers upgrade, your average revenue per user (ARPU) might actually drop if the $35 upcharge doesn't cover the marginal cost of their increased attendance.
2.  **Capacity Crunch:** "Unlimited" + "Priority Booking" creates a zero-sum game. If 480 power users monopolize prime slots, standard members cannot book classes, driving *their* churn up significantly more than the modeled 1%.
3.  **Competitor Response:** If PulseFit drops prices or launches a similar tier in June, your 15% uptake assumption evaporates, extending the break-even period indefinitely while you carry the $4,500 monthly overhead.

**Kill/Pivot Trigger:**
Launch a 4-week pilot to 10% of the base (320 members). **Kill the plan immediately if:**
*   Upgrade uptake is <8% (indicating weak demand).
*   OR overall studio churn rises >2% during the pilot (indicating capacity friction or value perception issues).
*   OR instructor overtime costs exceed $5,500/month (indicating the unit economics are broken).

If uptake is strong but capacity is the bottleneck, **pivot** to a capped "Unlimited" (e.g., 15 classes/month) rather than true unlimited access.
