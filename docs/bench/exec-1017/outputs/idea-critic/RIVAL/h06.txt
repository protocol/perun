**Recommendation: Proceed with a conditional pilot, but pivot the pricing model immediately.**

Do not launch with a flat $8/student fee. Your assumption that schools have $0.10/student in discretionary cybersecurity budget is your single point of failure; K-12 budgets are categorical, and "cybersecurity" funds are often locked into infrastructure, not training. If you cannot secure a signed letter of intent from at least one of the three pilot districts confirming they have identified the specific budget line item to cover the $8 fee by April 1, 2025, you will reach launch day with zero revenue. Instead, propose a "compliance-grant" pricing model where the district pays only if the state mandate is formally confirmed for their specific region, or bundle the cost into a broader professional development package where funds already exist.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Budget Category Mismatch:** Districts agree with the *need* but lack the legal authority to move funds into a new vendor line item before the fiscal year closes, causing the pilot to stall despite verbal interest.
2.  **Unvalidated Engagement Claims:** If SecureLearn's 40% engagement claim is accurate and your gamification feels "corporate" rather than "student-native," teachers will disable the simulations due to classroom disruption, killing renewal chances regardless of compliance needs.
3.  **Integration Debt:** You have budgeted $0 for licensing fees. If the pilot districts require Single Sign-On (SSO) or SIS (Student Information System) integration to deploy at scale, custom development costs will instantly consume your $150k development budget before the first student logs in.

**Trigger Date:** April 15, 2025.
**Condition:** If you have fewer than **two** fully executed contracts with *confirmed funding sources* (not just memorandums of understanding).
**Action:** If triggered, **kill** the B2C-style direct billing approach immediately. **Pivot** to a B2G (Business-to-Government) model, lobbying the state education department to include your tool in a statewide bulk-licensing deal, thereby bypassing individual district budget approval cycles entirely.
