Your current proposal relies on unverified assumptions about network capacity and inventory handling that could erase the projected $2M savings. Specifically, you have not modeled how diverting 30% of volume to pickup affects last-mile density for remaining deliveries. If delivery routes become too sparse, per-unit costs for home delivery will spike, potentially offsetting the savings from the pickup hubs. Additionally, assuming existing warehouse space can absorb extra handling without fees is a critical risk; if third-party logistics providers charge surge fees for non-standard pick-up staging, your margin expansion drops from 5% to near zero.

**Principal Failure Modes:**
1.  **Network Fragmentation:** Diverting volume makes remaining delivery routes inefficient, increasing home-delivery costs by >20% and negating total network savings.
2.  **Hidden Handling Costs:** Existing warehouse partners impose surcharges for pickup staging, consuming $400k+ of the projected budget.
3.  **Cannibalization without Margin Gain:** Customers who would have paid delivery fees switch to free pickup, reducing revenue while fixed hub costs remain.

*   **Cost Overrun:** If the Cedar Creek pilot shows setup or operational costs exceeding **$150k** (25% over pro-rated budget) in the first 90 days.
