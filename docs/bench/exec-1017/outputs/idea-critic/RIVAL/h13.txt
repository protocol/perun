**Recommendation: Proceed with a Phased Pilot, Not Full Rollout**

Do not commit the full $75,000 upfront yet. Instead, propose a $15,000 "Phase 0" prototype to validate the 75% adoption assumption before November 2026. The math only works if adoption hits ~62%; your 75% target is aggressive given typical freelancer tool fatigue. A pilot reduces your downside exposure to $19,000 (dev + 6 months hosting) while proving the unit economics to Maya.

**Primary Failure Modes & Hidden Risks**

1.  **Adoption Shortfall (The Silent Killer):** If only 50% of freelancers use the feature, you generate ~$108k in incremental annual value against a $99k annualized cost (amortized dev + hosting). You break even technically, but once you factor in engineering maintenance and support, you are burning cash. Freelancers often resist new workflows unless the value is instant and undeniable.
2.  **The 2026 Compliance Cliff:** The November 2026 data-privacy regulations are a material risk. If the AI module processes personal data to generate suggestions, you may face a costly architectural refactor or data-sovereignty issues mid-stream. This could turn a $2k/month hosting bill into a $50k+ emergency migration project.
3.  **Churn Amplification:** If the AI suggestions are generic or incorrect, they may frustrate users, potentially *increasing* churn rather than decreasing it. An 8% quarterly churn is already high; annoying your top performers could accelerate revenue loss faster than the conversion lift replaces it.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Condition**

Stop or pivot the project immediately if:
**"Active usage by freelancers does not reach 40% within 90 days of launch, OR if the legal team flags the data processing architecture as non-compliant with the draft 2026 regulations."**
