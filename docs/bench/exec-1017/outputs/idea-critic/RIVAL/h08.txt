Do not commit the full $250k yet. The math works only if your 70% adoption assumption holds, but your survey data (n=50, mixed results) is statistically too weak to validate a $420k/year revenue stream. Launch a 30-day concierge pilot with 50 landlords manually processing renewals for the $5 fee. If you cannot secure 60% paid commitment in this low-friction test, the engineering build is unjustified.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Adoption Overestimation:** The "mixed" survey results likely indicate price sensitivity. If actual adoption lands at 40% instead of 70%, annual revenue drops to ~$240k, extending your payback period from ~10 months to over 2 years, destroying the unit economics.
2.  **Hidden Support Costs:** You assumed zero impact on support, but automated renewals often trigger complex edge cases (partial payments, lease term disputes, failed auto-charges). If this generates just 0.5 extra tickets per landlord monthly, you could burn $40k/year in support labor, eroding 10% of your projected margin.
3.  **Churn Contagion:** The 5% historic churn is your baseline risk. If the new module introduces friction or perceived "nickel-and-diming," causing churn to rise to just 7%, you lose ~$180k in existing lifetime value, negating the new revenue gains entirely.

**Kill Trigger:**

Terminate the project immediately if the pilot conversion rate falls below **55%** of invited landlords signing the paid addendum within 14 days. This threshold provides a safety buffer below your 70% target while still proving viable unit economics; anything lower suggests the market does not value the automation enough to pay the surcharge.
