**Recommendation: Proceed, but pivot the rollout strategy.**
Do not launch a blanket upgrade campaign targeting 80% conversion. Instead, launch a **targeted beta** to the top 20% of your most engaged restaurant clients first. The assumption that 400 out of 500 clients will upgrade within six months is dangerously optimistic for a B2B add-on; typical SaaS attach rates for legacy bases hover closer to 15–25%. By forcing a mass-market launch, you risk high churn if the inventory tool isn't perfectly polished for every edge case, damaging the core payroll brand. Validate the 15% admin-time reduction with a larger, diverse group before committing the full $30k marketing budget.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **The "80% Adoption" Delusion:** If only 20% upgrade (100 restaurants) instead of 80% (400 restaurants), your projected revenue drops from ~$576k/year to ~$144k/year. At the lower adoption rate, you will not recover the $150k initial investment (dev + marketing) within the first 18 months, turning this into a cash-flow negative feature that distracts engineering from core payroll stability.
2.  **Support Cost Explosion:** Inventory management is significantly more complex than payroll. Payroll is rule-based and periodic; inventory is real-time and prone to user error (miscounts, unit mismatches, spoilage logic). A "lightweight" tool often generates "heavy" support tickets. If the inventory module increases support ticket volume by even 20%, the cost of hiring/training support staff could erase the margin gained from the $80 price uplift.
3.  **Competitor Pre-emption & Price War:** If FoodFlow launches a similar bundle before January 2026, they may price aggressively to block your upsell. Since your inventory tool was "built last year," it may lack modern UX polish compared to a net-new competitor launch. If FoodFlow offers a similar bundle for $149, your value proposition collapses, and you may be forced into a discount war that devalues your core payroll product.

**Kill/Pivot Trigger Metric:**

**The "30-Day Active Usage" Rate.**
After the targeted beta launch, track the percentage of upgraded restaurants that log inventory data **at least 3 times per week** after 30 days.
*   **Kill/Pivot Threshold:** If **<40%** of upgrading restaurants hit this usage frequency, **kill the mass rollout**.
*   **Reasoning:** High signup but low usage indicates the feature is a "nice to have" that doesn't actually solve a painful daily problem, meaning the promised "15% admin time reduction" is not materializing in practice. Without habitual usage, churn will spike once the novelty wears off, and the unit economics will fail. Do not spend the remaining marketing budget until this engagement metric is proven.
