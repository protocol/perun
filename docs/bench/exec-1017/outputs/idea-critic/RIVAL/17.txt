**Recommendation: Proceed with a Conditional Pilot, Not Full Adoption**

Approve the $57,000 initial investment only as a six-week pilot program restricted to two active projects, not the full portfolio. The modeled $150,000 annual saving is plausible but hinges entirely on achieving the 5% variance target, which is unproven in your specific environment. The financial upside justifies the experiment, but the unknown data-migration effort and lack of support SLA make a full rollout premature. Treat the first three months as a paid proof-of-concept where full subscription renewal is contingent on hitting the variance reduction target.

**Primary Failure Modes**
1.  **Integration & Migration Drag:** The three-week ERP integration estimate is optimistic; if data migration takes double the expected time, labor costs will erase the first year's savings.
2.  **Functional Gaps:** The tool's limited multi-phase project handling may force estimators to maintain parallel spreadsheets, creating data drift rather than reducing variance.
3.  **Hidden Recurring Costs:** The potential $8,000/year hidden subscription fees and lack of support SLA could degrade ROI if response times slow down critical bid preparations.

**Kill Condition**
Terminate the contract immediately if, after three months of live usage, the actual estimate variance reduction is less than 2 percentage points (i.e., variance remains above 6%). This partial success threshold accounts for implementation learning curves while ensuring the tool delivers material value.

**Strongest Objection**
"The tool creates a false sense of precision." Critics will argue that an 8% variance stems from volatile material costs and scope ambiguity, not software limitations. If Cornerstone merely automates bad inputs without improving underlying data quality, the variance will persist despite the new interface, rendering the $57,000 expense a sunk cost with no operational improvement.

**Better Alternative**
Before purchasing, execute a "Process-First Audit." Engage a senior estimator to manually analyze the root causes of the last ten variances. If the errors stem from inconsistent takeoff methods or missing scope items, a standardized internal checklist and targeted training may achieve a 3–4% variance reduction at near-zero cost. Only if the audit confirms the gap is due to calculation complexity or data silos should you proceed with the Cornerstone pilot. This de-risks the decision by ensuring you are buying a solution to a verified problem rather than hoping software fixes a process issue.
