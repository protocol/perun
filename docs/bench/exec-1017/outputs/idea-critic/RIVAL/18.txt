**Recommendation: Proceed with a phased pilot, not a full launch.**
The math works only if acquisition costs stay below $24 per member and churn remains under 3.5%. Your current projection ignores the $2 shipping subsidy, which actually improves your unit economics, but it dangerously assumes zero operational friction. Launch to 1,000 users first to validate the "seasonal churn" variable before committing the full $150k budget.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Churn Spike from Shipping Delays:** If seasonal delays cause churn to hit 5% in the first two months, the lifetime value (LTV) of a "Reserve Club" member drops below the $120k campaign cost, turning the initiative into a net loss.
2.  **Margin Erosion via Support Costs:** The model assumes a clean 30% margin, but premium members demand white-glove support. If customer service tickets per user exceed 1.5x the standard tier, your effective margin collapses to ~18%.
3.  **Cannibalization of Existing Tiers:** If more than 15% of the 5,000 new sign-ups are existing users upgrading solely for the packaging (rather than net new revenue), you are subsidizing your own base without growing the top line.

**Specific Kill/Pivot Triggers:**
*   **KILL:** If Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) exceeds **$28** after the first 500 sign-ups, or if churn exceeds **4.5%** in any single month during the pilot.
*   **PIVOT:** If shipping delays affect more than **10%** of orders, immediately switch the value prop from "exclusive physical delivery" to "virtual cellar credits + guaranteed future allocation" to bypass logistics bottlenecks.

*Note: Your current profit projection of $405k is likely overstated by ~12% because it doesn't account for the variable cost of the packaging redesign amortization.*
