**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased pilot** limited to one department before full consolidation. While the financial logic holds, the operational risk of merging three desks simultaneously without baseline patient satisfaction data is too high. Validate the 25% speed improvement and nurse triage efficacy in a controlled environment first.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Integration Downtime Reality:** The plan assumes zero downtime, but legacy-to-cloud migrations historically incur 2–5 days of reduced booking capacity. If this occurs during peak flu season, the backlog could negate the $9,000 monthly savings within the first quarter.
2.  **Triage Bottleneck:** Adding nurses assumes they can process patients faster than the current desk staff. If the new triage protocol adds even 3 minutes per call, the "25% faster booking" target will fail, increasing hold times and potentially driving the no-show rate up due to frustration rather than down.
3.  **Patient Satisfaction Drop:** Consolidating three local desks into one central hub removes personalized community rapport. If patients feel "processed" rather than "cared for," no-show rates may remain static or increase despite nurse intervention, destroying the project's core ROI premise.

**Kill Criterion:**
Terminate the project if, after Q1, the **net no-show reduction is less than 10%** (half the projected target) OR if **patient complaint volume regarding scheduling increases by more than 15%** compared to the prior quarter baseline.

*Note: Ensure the pilot includes a mechanism to capture patient satisfaction scores immediately, as this is currently the largest blind spot in the proposal.*
*The $250k upfront cost requires a break-even analysis that accounts for the potential 3-month delay in realizing full savings.*
