**Recommendation: Proceed, but restructure as a "Performance Tier" upgrade rather than a seat-based add-on, and validate willingness-to-pay before writing code.**

Do not build the $120k solution yet. Your math relies on a 30% adoption rate among enterprise clients to justify the investment, yet you have zero survey data and a competitor offering a superior value proposition (unlimited builds) for only $3 more. Selling a "partial" improvement (faster builds but still metered) against a competitor's "complete" solution is a high-risk strategy.

Instead, package this as a distinct "Enterprise Performance Tier." Rather than charging $9/seat, bundle the distributed agent into a higher-priced tier that includes the speed boost *and* matches the competitor's "unlimited builds" feature. This neutralizes the competitor's primary advantage and aligns the revenue model with the value delivered (speed/reliability) rather than penalizing large teams with per-seat fees. Before committing the full $120k, spend $5k–$10k on a concierge test: manually offer the speed upgrade to 5 friendly enterprise accounts at the proposed new price point. If you cannot close 3 out of 5, the assumed 15% renewal uplift is fantasy, and the project should be killed.

**How This Could Fail:**
1.  **Adoption Collapse:** Enterprise customers refuse to pay extra for speed when the competitor offers speed + unlimited builds for a marginal difference. If adoption lands below **12%** (less than half your optimistic estimate), the unit economics break, and you burn $120k for negligible revenue impact.
2.  **Churn Misdiagnosis:** You assume slow builds are the primary churn driver. If the 7% churn is actually driven by missing features, poor support, or pricing friction unrelated to build time, cutting latency from 35 to 24 minutes will not move the needle on retention, leaving you with a costly feature nobody renewals because of.
3.  **Technical Debt & Cost Overrun:** Distributed build agents are notoriously difficult to maintain. If complexity spikes and the project drags past 4 months or costs exceed **$150k**, the ROI timeline extends beyond your 6-month churn target window, rendering the investment useless for your immediate goal.

**Kill/Rethink Triggers:**
*   **Validation Failure:** Less than **40% of surveyed enterprise accounts** express strong intent to purchase the new tier during the pre-build concierge test.
*   **Adoption Failure:** Post-launch uptake is below **15%** of the eligible enterprise base within 90 days.
*   **Budget/Time Breach:** Development costs exceed **$150k** or the timeline slips beyond **4 months**.
*   **Churn Stagnation:** Overall enterprise churn does not drop by at least **1 percentage point** (e.g., from 7% to 6%) within 3 months of rollout.
