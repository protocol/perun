**Recommendation: Do Not Launch Next Quarter**

The pilot is financially unviable under current assumptions and carries unacceptable regulatory risk. The projected revenue ($33k/month) fails to cover the combined operating costs ($12k engine + amortized dev costs) and expected credit losses ($210k/month), creating an immediate monthly shortfall of approximately $190k before accounting for capital costs or operations. Additionally, launching without a regulatory timeline exposes Fernhill to potential enforcement actions or forced shutdowns after incurring sunk integration costs.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**

1.  **Catastrophic Unit Economics:** The 0.8% fee covers only ~19% of the expected 5% default cost. Unless the default rate drops below 0.8% (highly unlikely for rapid unsecured credit) or fees increase 6x, every financed dollar loses money.
2.  **Regulatory Stop-Order:** Launching prior to explicit approval risks a cease-and-desist order, resulting in total loss of the $78k development investment and reputational damage with suppliers.
3.  **Adverse Selection Spiral:** The "24-hour" speed constraint likely bypasses deep fraud checks, attracting bad actors and pushing the actual default rate significantly above the historical 5% baseline, accelerating losses.

**Abort Trigger**

**Immediate Abort** if regulatory written approval is not received by **T-minus 2 weeks** from the planned launch date, or if the **cumulative net loss** (defaults minus fees) exceeds **$150,000** in the first 60 days of operation.

**Hidden Risks in Numbers**

*   **Revenue Miscalculation:** The $33k revenue figure appears to assume the fee applies to the total $4.2M invoice volume. If the fee applies only to *financed* invoices (and not all invoices uploaded), and only a fraction of suppliers utilize the line, revenue will be drastically lower than projected, widening the loss margin.
*   **Static Default Assumption:** Applying last year's average default rate to a new, faster, likely riskier product segment is dangerous. "Rapid" credit typically correlates with higher fraud and default; the 5% assumption is likely a floor, not a ceiling.
*   **Supplier Churn:** Onboarding 35 new suppliers simultaneously while changing payment terms may strain operational support, leading to integration errors that delay funding beyond the promised 24 hours, negating the product's core value proposition.
