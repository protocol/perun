**Recommendation: Proceed with a Conditional Pilot, Not Full Rollout.**
Do not commit the full $3M budget yet. The "under 500ms" latency estimate is a critical single point of failure; if the real-world API latency exceeds 200ms, the user experience will feel laggy, destroying the feature's utility. Allocate $150k immediately for a 6-week technical proof-of-concept (PoC) to validate actual latency and data reliability on one line. If the PoC confirms sub-200ms latency and >99% uptime, greenlight the full build. If not, kill it before spending the development budget.

**Top Three Failure Modes:**
1.  **Latency-Induced Obsolescence:** If the sensor API averages >300ms, the "real-time" data will arrive after passengers have already boarded. Users will lose trust in the app instantly, resulting in zero ridership lift while incurring full licensing costs.
2.  **Privacy & Regulatory Blockade:** Anonymized Bluetooth data often faces re-identification risks. If legal or city councils block the data-sharing agreement in Q2 (a common delay in municipal tech), the $500k licensing budget burns with no product to show, and the 12-month timeline collapses.
3.  **Integration Underestimation:** The assumption that backend integration takes "no more than 4 weeks" is dangerously optimistic. Ingesting high-frequency streaming telemetry into a legacy transit backend typically requires 3–4 months of engineering to handle load spikes without crashing the core ticketing system, potentially delaying launch by two quarters.

**Kill Metric:**
Terminate the project if, after six months of live operation, the **net new ridership lift is below 3%** (less than 96k extra rides/day).
*Reasoning:* Your business case relies on an 8% lift to cover costs in two years. If you achieve less than half that target (3%) after the initial novelty wears off, the revenue bump ($0.29/ride vs. the projected $0.78) will never recover the $3M investment, making the project a permanent drain on operational margins.

**Note:** Ensure the data-sharing agreement includes a "termination for latency" clause to avoid paying the $500k license for unusable slow data.
