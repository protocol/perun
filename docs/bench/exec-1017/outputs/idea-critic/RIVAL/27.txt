**Recommendation: Proceed with a Conditional Pilot, Not a Full Rollout**

Move forward immediately with a limited technical pilot restricted to internal staff and a small, controlled patient group (under 500 families), but **halt all broad marketing and general availability plans** until regulatory clearance is formally secured. The current timeline assumes a best-case regulatory scenario (3 months) while budgeting for a median one; launching broadly before sign-off exposes Northwind Kids Health to unacceptable legal liability and reputational risk given the pediatric data sensitivity. The $500,000 capital budget appears sufficient for development, but you must explicitly model a recurring licensing cost of approximately $60,000–$90,000 annually (estimated at 12–18% of dev costs for enterprise AI maintenance and API usage), which is currently missing from your financial model.

**Primary Failure Modes**
1.  **Regulatory Stall:** Clearance takes 9+ months, burning cash on a stranded asset while competitors gain first-mover advantage.
2.  **Adoption Illusion:** The 25% opt-in rate relies on a small survey (n=200) that likely suffers from selection bias; real-world parental anxiety regarding AI diagnosing children may drive actual adoption below 5%, rendering the ROI negative.
3.  **Data Privacy Breach:** A single incident involving leaked pediatric data would destroy trust permanently, outweighing any efficiency gains.

**Pre-Agreed Kill/Pivot Triggers**
*   **Regulatory Trigger:** If full regulatory sign-off is not received by **Month 6**, pivot immediately to a "Human-in-the-Loop" only mode (bot drafts, doctor sends) or terminate the project.
*   **Adoption Trigger:** If active usage is **below 10%** of the target cohort after **six months** of post-clearance operation, terminate the project.
*   **Safety Trigger:** Any confirmed instance of the bot providing medically unsafe advice results in immediate termination.
