To grow from 3,200 to 5,000 donors, you need 1,800 net new donors.
*   **Gross Acquisition Needed:** Assuming 30% churn on the *new* cohort alone, you need to acquire ~2,570 donors to net 1,800. At $22/acquisition, this costs **$56,540**.
*   **Remaining Budget:** $120k total - $40k (portal) - $56.5k (acquisition) = **$23,500** for events, contingencies, and creative.
*   **Revenue Projection:** 5,000 donors × $120 × 70% retention = $420,000 annual recurring.
*   **Gap:** Your current budget covers the math *only if* the $22 acquisition cost holds and the corporate match is secured for operational leverage, not counted as direct donor acquisition budget.

**Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Conversion Black Box:** You assume a 25% open rate but have no data for open-to-donor conversion. If the portal UX is friction-heavy, your $22 estimated cost could spike to $40+, blowing the budget before reaching 4,000 donors.
2.  **Timing Mismatch:** If the portal testing slips by 3 weeks, you miss the optimal giving window before Q3 end, forcing a rush spend that lowers quality and increases cost per donor.
3.  **Participation Dependency:** Relying on the corporate match ($150k) as a psychological lever for the email campaign before the deal is signed creates reputational risk if the negotiation stalls.

**Kill Tripwire (Stop/Change Condition):**
**Stop the current acquisition strategy if:** After spending **$15,000** of the acquisition budget (approx. 680 new donors), the **actual cost per acquired donor exceeds $28** OR the **30-day retention rate of new donors falls below 55%**.
