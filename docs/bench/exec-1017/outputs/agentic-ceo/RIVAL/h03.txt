**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Controlled Expansion" rather than a full rollout.**

Do not expand beyond the current 5 clinics yet. The financial model is viable only if enrollment targets are met, but the undefined operational costs for in-home sample collection represent a critical risk that could erase margins. Dr. Patel should authorize the completion of the 90-day pilot to gather hard data on the $5/pet collection cost and actual churn. If the pilot confirms enrollment >250 pets and the collection cost stays under $4/pet, proceed to regional expansion. If not, pivot to an in-clinic-only model before spending further on marketing.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Margin Erosion from Hidden Logistics:** The model assumes a $120 revenue cap but leaves the $5/pet/month in-home collection cost unverified. If this cost rises to $7–$8 due to travel time or failed visits, the gross margin per pet collapses, making the subscription less profitable than standard fee-for-service visits.
2.  **Enrollment Shortfall:** The plan requires 360 enrollments (30% of 1,200) to hit revenue targets. If conversion stalls below 200 pets (approx. 17%), annual revenue drops to ~$240k. At that volume, the fixed $75k pilot budget and software integration costs cannot be amortized effectively, resulting in a net loss.
3.  **Churn Spike Post-Incentive:** The $10k incentive drives the first 100 sign-ups, but these are price-sensitive early adopters. If true churn exceeds 15% after six months (once the novelty wears off or price sensitivity returns), the lifetime value (LTV) of a subscriber will fail to cover the initial acquisition and integration costs.

**Stop/Change Course Metrics:**

Dr. Patel should halt expansion or pivot the model immediately if either of these thresholds is hit by day 60 of the pilot:
*   **Enrollment < 200 pets:** Indicates the market price point ($120) is too high or the value proposition is unclear.
*   **Operational Cost > $6 per pet per month:** Specifically for in-home collection. If this variable cost exceeds $6, the logistics model is unsustainable, and the plan must shift to "in-clinic drop-off only" to preserve margins.
