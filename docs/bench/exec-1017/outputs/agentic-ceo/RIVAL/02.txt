# Tip‑Sync Rollout Plan: Acme Payroll

**Recommended Order of Work & Deferrals**
1.  **Immediate Start:** Build Tip‑Capture API (Critical Path).
2.  **Parallel Track:** Secure legal sign‑off on Q4 labor‑law amendment *before* coding POS integrations. If the amendment passes, integration requirements may change.
3.  **Conditional Start:** Begin POS integrations only after legal clearance.
4.  **Defer:** Final pricing strategy until Beta Week 1 results are in. The 5% discount assumption is unvalidated; use beta data to model elasticity rather than guessing now.

**Three Most Likely Failure Modes**
1.  **Regulatory Whiplash:** The Q4 labor‑law amendment passes, requiring new reporting fields not in the current API spec, forcing a costly refactor post‑build.
2.  **Integration Fragmentation:** The "30 hours per integration" estimate assumes uniform POS APIs. In reality, legacy POS systems often require custom middleware, likely blowing the $15k budget and delaying the July 1 beta.
3.  **Adoption Delusion:** The 80% adoption target relies on voluntary uptake. If the feature isn't bundled or mandated, restaurant staff may ignore it, resulting in <40% adoption and negative unit economics.

**Stop/Change Triggers**
*   **Beta Adoption <60%:** If fewer than 72 of the 120 beta restaurants actively use the feature by July 14, pause the Q3 launch. This indicates a product‑market fit or UX issue, not a sales problem.
*   **Legal Ambiguity:** If the labor‑law amendment status remains unresolved by August 1, delay the September 15 launch to avoid compliance liability.

**Hidden Costs & Risks in Your Numbers**
*   **Budget Math Error:** You allocated $150k total. Your costs are $120k (API) + $15k (Integrations) = $135k. This leaves only $15k for QA, project management, and beta support, which is insufficient for a payroll product requiring 100% accuracy. You are likely under‑budgeted by ~$40k for a compliant launch.
*   **Revenue Double‑Counting:** You noted a $12k revenue reduction from the 5% discount. However, if adoption is only 80% (240 restaurants) rather than 100%, your total revenue base is already 20% lower than the "target 300" model implies. The financial impact of the discount is actually smaller, but the total revenue projection is overly optimistic.
*   **Support Load:** A two‑week beta with 120 customers will generate significant support tickets. No budget or headcount is allocated for this, risking churn if early experiences are buggy.

**Note:** The regulatory risk is the single biggest threat; treat legal clearance as a gate before integration spend.
**Note:** Re‑allocate $20k from the "contingency" or marketing budget to QA immediately to cover the math gap.
