### Executive Plan: Redoak Secure AI Phishing Rollout

**Recommendation: Order of Work & Deferrals**
Execute in this strict sequence to validate the $450k ARR hypothesis before full capital deployment:

1.  **Weeks 1–3: Integration Discovery & Metric Baseline ($15k allocated).** Before building the engine, run technical spikes on the top 3 client SIEM environments. Simultaneously, audit historical data to verify if the 45% adoption rate of the 2023 module actually correlates to the assumed 15% breach reduction. *Do not write core engine code yet.*
2.  **Weeks 4–14: Core Engine Development ($80k).** Proceed only if integration variance is <15%. Build the simulation engine with a "SIEM-agnostic" middleware layer to absorb integration complexity.
3.  **Weeks 15–16: Security Peer Review ($12k).** Conduct the audit on the completed build.
4.  **Weeks 17–18: Pricing Validation & Comms ($5k + deferred).** Finalize the $15/seat increase logic based on early pilot feedback, not assumptions. Prepare the deck.
5.  **Deferred:** Full-scale marketing rollout and non-essential UI polish. Wait for pilot adoption data before committing to the 2.5× lift assumption.

**Budget Status:** $112k committed/estimated. **$8k Contingency** remains for integration overruns.

---

**Top 3 Failure Modes**
1.  **Integration Cost Explosion:** The "unknown" SIEM effort exceeds the $8k contingency, forcing a scope cut on the engine or eating into the review budget.
2.  **Adoption/Value Mismatch:** Clients adopt the tool (>60%) but it fails to reduce breaches by 15% because human behavior, not detection speed, is the bottleneck. This invalidates the $15 price hike.
3.  **Timeline Slip:** The 2-week security review discovers architectural flaws requiring rework, pushing the July 15 launch into Q3 and missing the fiscal window.

**Stop/Pivot Triggers**
*   **Technical:** Integration spikes reveal >20% variance in effort across client environments. *Action: Pivot to a managed-service model rather than self-integrated software.*
*   **Market:** Pilot adoption is <30% in the first 30 days post-launch. *Action: Halt pricing increase; investigate usability friction.*
*   **Outcome:** Breach reduction is <5% after 60 days despite high adoption. *Action: Stop sales narrative on "risk reduction"; pivot messaging to "compliance training."*

---

**Critical Assumption & Arithmetic Flags**
*   **Causal Fallacy:** You assume a 45% adoption rate *mirrors* a 15% breach reduction. Adoption does not guarantee efficacy. If the simulations are too easy or ignored, adoption can be 100% while breach reduction stays 0%. You are selling outcomes, not usage.
*   **Revenue Math:** A $15/seat increase capturing $450k ARR implies you need **30,000 seats** ($450,000 / $15). Does your current install base support this, or does this require net-new sales? If net-new, the 2.5× lift assumption is highly aggressive for a 90-day window.
*   **Budget Fragility:** The $80k engine estimate leaves only 10% of the total budget for the highest-risk item (integration). This is statistically likely to overrun.
