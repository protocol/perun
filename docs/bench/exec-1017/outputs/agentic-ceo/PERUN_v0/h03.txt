**Recommendation: Proceed with a staged rollout, but pause the full regional launch until unit economics are verified.**

Do not commit the full $75k budget or target the full 360 pets (30% of 1,200) immediately. Your current projection shows a potential annual revenue of $432k, but your cost model has a critical gap. You are missing confirmed costs for tele-medicine ($8/visit), lab fees ($20/pet/quarter), and potentially in-home collection ($5/pet/month). If these costs materialize as expected, they could consume $156–$216 per pet annually, drastically reducing your margin.

Execute a **4-week "Data Sprint"** with only 50 enrolled pets across two clinics. Use this window to measure the *actual* operational cost per pet. If the verified margin remains positive after deducting all variable costs, authorize the full rollout. If the margin disappears, pivot the pricing model or service scope before spending the remaining budget.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Negative Unit Economics:** The unverified costs (tele-medicine, labs, collection) exceed the $120 monthly price point. If the true cost to serve is $100+ per month, the plan generates revenue but loses money on every enrollment, scaling losses as you grow.
2.  **Operational Bottleneck:** The "in-home sample collection" requirement overwhelms staff. If logistics take more time than anticipated, clinic throughput drops, causing regular appointment delays and damaging the core business reputation while trying to launch the new product.
3.  **Churn Spike Post-Incentive:** The $10k incentive drives the first 100 sign-ups, but once the novelty wears off or the first bill hits without perceived high value, churn exceeds the 10% assumption. If early adopters leave quickly, the Customer Acquisition Cost (marketing spend) cannot be recovered.

**Stop/Change Course Metrics:**

*   **Enrollment < 40 pets** after the 4-week Data Sprint (indicates market fit is weaker than the 30% hypothesis).
*   **Verified Variable Cost > $90 per pet/month** (leaves insufficient margin for overhead and profit at the $120 price point).
*   **Churn > 5%** within the first 60 days of paid service (suggests the 10% annual churn assumption is dangerously optimistic).
