**Board Recommendation: Adopt a "Riverside-First" Staggered Rollout**

Do not open all three locations simultaneously. Instead, treat the Riverside location (Jan 15) as the primary pilot for the "Pulse" series and new hardware, pushing Downtown to Feb 1 and Hillcrest to March 15. This six-week buffer allows us to validate the heart-rate monitoring licensing costs and the 20% conversion assumption in a live environment before committing the full $120k marketing budget and remaining instructor hires across the chain.

**Proposed Order of Work**
1.  **Renovation & Permitting (Immediate):** Prioritize resolving the $300k Riverside permitting uncertainty before breaking ground elsewhere.
2.  **Hardware Licensing (Weeks 1–3):** Secure final pricing for heart-rate monitors; do not install until costs are fixed.
3.  **Hiring (Weeks 4–8):** Hire the core lead instructors for Riverside first; delay the full cohort of six until the pilot scope is confirmed.
4.  **Marketing (Weeks 9–12):** Launch trial campaigns sequentially, starting with Riverside, to manage lead flow against actual studio readiness.

**Three Most Likely Failure Points**
*   **Regulatory Blockade:** The suspected $300k permitting fee in Riverside is real and unbudgeted, causing a total budget overrun of 12.5% and delaying the critical pilot launch.
*   **Conversion Collapse:** Actual trial-to-membership conversion falls below 10% (half the projection), reducing incremental revenue to $600k and failing to cover the $450k in new annual instructor salaries plus marketing spend.
*   **Hardware Integration Delay:** Licensing negotiations or technical integration for the heart-rate monitors slip past the Jan 15 date, forcing a launch without the key "Pulse" differentiator and diluting the marketing message.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger**
 Halt the expansion to Downtown and Hillcrest immediately if the Riverside pilot yields a **trial-to-membership conversion rate of <12%** after the first 30 days of operation.

*Note: The current $2.4M budget excludes the potential $300k permitting fee and the undefined hardware licensing costs; these must be quantified before final board approval. The revenue projection assumes the full $1.2M is realized evenly, but cash flow will be back-loaded based on the staggered opening dates.*
