**Rollout Plan: Bluebird Roasters Wholesale Office Program**

**1. Recommended Order of Work**
*   **Weeks 1–2 (Validation & Packaging):** Immediately commission the packaging redesign ($8k estimated) while simultaneously conducting manual outreach to TechCo, GreenSpace, and Apex to validate interest *before* finalizing pricing. Do not set final pricing until competitor data is gathered via direct inquiry during these sales conversations.
*   **Weeks 3–6 (Pilot Execution):** Finalize contracts with the three pilots based on validated willingness-to-pay. Begin limited production runs using existing capacity to test the new bags. Implement the measurement framework from day one of delivery.
*   **Weeks 7–12 (Optimization & Scale Prep):** Analyze pilot margin and logistics friction. If margins hold >8% and delivery is stable, authorize overtime to boost capacity to 1,560 lb/week in preparation for Q4.
*   **Q4 Start:** Full operational launch if pilot KPIs are met; otherwise, pivot pricing or logistics model.

**2. Three Biggest Failure Modes**
*   **Margin Erosion:** The 10% gross margin assumption fails because competitor pricing forces a discount, or the "biodegradable" bag costs exceed the $8k estimate, rendering the unit economics negative.
*   **Capacity Bottleneck:** The assumed 30% capacity boost via overtime proves unsustainable due to labor shortages or machine heat limits, causing missed deliveries and contract breaches during the pilot.
*   **Churn Before Scale:** Pilots treat the service as temporary; if two of the three clients cancel before Q4 due to coffee quality inconsistency or delivery friction, the revenue base collapses, leaving fixed costs uncovered.

**3. Stop/Pivot Trigger**
*   **Hard Stop Condition:** If cumulative pilot revenue is **< $5,000** after two months of active delivery OR if the realized gross margin drops below **5%** after accounting for actual packaging and labor costs. This indicates the unit economics cannot support the $150k budget recovery goal.

**Notes**
*   Competitor pricing must be treated as a critical unknown; do not lock in long-term rates until sales calls reveal market tolerance.
*   The 30% capacity increase is a risk assumption; verify machine tolerances with your head roaster before promising Q4 volume.
