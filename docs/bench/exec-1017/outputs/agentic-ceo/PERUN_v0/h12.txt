**Q4 'PowerPack' Rollout Plan & Recommendation**

**1. Recommended Execution Order**
Prioritize streams based on critical path to the November 15 launch and cash flow preservation:
*   **Week 1–2 (Immediate): Supplier Negotiations & Legal Review.** Start supplier talks immediately to lock in the 5% COGS increase and define contract terms. Concurrently, initiate the $12k legal review. These are gating items for final pricing and margin validation.
*   **Week 1–4 (Parallel): Recipe Finalization & Certification.** Begin the 4-week, $120k certification process immediately. This is your longest lead-time item; any delay here slips the launch date.
*   **Week 3–5: Packaging Redesign.** Once the recipe is stable, execute the $30k packaging redesign to ensure label accuracy before print.
*   **Week 4–6: E-commerce Build.** Start the 3-week, $85k build once pricing and packaging specs are finalized to avoid rework.
*   **Week 7–8: Marketing Launch.** Deploy remaining funds (~$53k after all costs) only after the above are locked.

**2. Financial Reality Check**
Your available marketing budget is **$53,000**, not $85,000.
*   *Expenses:* $120k (Cert) + $85k (Tech) + $30k (Packaging) + $12k (Legal) = **$247,000**.
*   *Remaining:* $300k - $247k = **$53,000**.
*   *Revenue Goal:* $240,000 (5,000 units @ $48).
*   *Risk:* The assumed 10% conversion boost from prep time reduction is unverified. With a tighter marketing budget, customer acquisition cost (CAC) must be strictly monitored.

**3. Primary Failure Modes**
*   **Certification Delay:** If lab certification exceeds 4 weeks, the November 15 launch is impossible regardless of tech readiness.
*   **Margin Erosion:** The 5% COGS increase combined with a higher price point may suppress volume more than the prep-time benefit increases it, missing the $240k revenue target.
*   **Cash Flow Mismatch:** Spending $247k upfront requires confidence in immediate recoupment; a slow start leaves no budget for corrective marketing.

**4. Halt / Re-prioritization Metric**
**Stop or Pivot if:** Week 1 sales are **< 1,250 kits** (25% of month-one goal).
*   *Rationale:* Selling only 2,000 units by week 4 implies a run-rate of 2,000/month, missing the 5,000 target by 60%. Waiting until the end of the month to react is too late given the fixed Q4 timeline. A <25% start in Week 1 indicates a fundamental product-market fit or pricing issue that additional marketing spend cannot fix.

**Note:** Secure the supplier contract before finalizing the subscription engine logic to ensure pricing rules match the negotiated COGS. Do not commit to the full marketing spend until Week 1 conversion data validates the 10% uplift assumption.
