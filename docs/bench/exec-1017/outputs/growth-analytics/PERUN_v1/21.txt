**Arithmetic & Assumption Flags:**
1.  **Sign-up Lift:** The variant increased sign-ups from 24.0% (1,200/5,000) to 34.6% (1,800/5,200). This is a **44% relative increase**, not 5%. Your math on the headline metric is correct, but the assumption linking it to long-term value is dangerous.
2.  **The "5% Translation" Error:** You assumed a 5% click-through increase (20 percentage points) equals a 5% engagement increase. This is **UNVERIFIED**. Clicking a banner is not retaining a patron. Without churn data, you cannot claim long-term value. You are optimizing for activation, not retention.
3.  **Missing Costs:** You have the license cost ($45k) but **zero** data on maintenance or implementation labor. A rollout could fail simply because the ongoing operational cost exceeds the budget, regardless of user adoption.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
*   **Retention Leak:** High sign-up rates mask immediate churn. If users sign up but never return after week 2, the library spends $45k on empty activity.
*   **Budget Overrun:** Unknown maintenance costs could spiral, forcing a cutback mid-year.
*   **Technical Debt:** Scaling from 5,200 users to a county-wide audience may expose latency or stability issues not seen in the test.

**The Stop/Pivot Tripwire:**
Do not use an arbitrary "3% growth" target. Use a **retention-based guardrail** derived from your own baseline.
*   **Action:** Define the "Core Action" (e.g., borrowing an item or logging in during Week 2).
*   **Tripwire:** If the **Week-2 retention rate** of the new portal's sign-ups falls below the **control group's historical Week-2 retention** by more than 10%, **halt the rollout** immediately.
*   **Date:** Review this metric on **31 Jan 2027** (end of Q1). If the tripwire is hit, pivot to fixing retention before expanding further.
