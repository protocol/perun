**Recommendation: Do not roll out yet; declare the result UNVERIFIED.**

Your arithmetic on the relative lift (12.5%) is correct, but the causal claim is invalid. You launched a parallel email campaign on March 3, nine days before the test ended. Because you cannot isolate the widget's effect from the email's effect, attributing the 9.0% conversion solely to the UI violates experiment rigor. Furthermore, if you monitored these numbers daily and stopped because they looked "good," you have introduced peeking bias, inflating the false-positive rate. A win read mid-test without a pre-committed stopping rule is noise, not data.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Confounded Attribution:** The lift is driven entirely by the email campaign, meaning the widget adds zero value while increasing interface complexity.
2.  **Retention Leakage:** The widget boosts one-time bookings but frustrates users, causing 30-day repeat booking rates (your true North Star) to drop below the control group's baseline.
3.  **Tutor Supply Shock:** Increased student bookings overwhelm tutor availability, driving down tutor satisfaction scores and causing supply-side churn that cancels revenue gains.

**Stop/Change Condition:**
 Halt the rollout immediately if the **30-day repeat booking rate** for Group B falls more than 2% below Group A's historical baseline, or if **tutor satisfaction scores** drop by more than 0.5 points on your internal scale. Do not use CPA thresholds until the attribution error is resolved.
