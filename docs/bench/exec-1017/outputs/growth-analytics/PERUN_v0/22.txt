**Recommendation:** Do not roll out to all customers yet. Execute a **guarded expansion** to 20% of the user base for 30 days, contingent on pre-committing a sequential testing design with a cost guardrail.

**Rationale:** While Variant B shows a raw conversion lift (10.8% vs 8.5%), the claim of a "30% uplift" against the prior pilot is statistically invalid due to the pilot's tiny sample size (n=500) and lack of controlled comparison. More critically, the unit economics are unverified. At $15 per completion, the incremental cost for the extra 220 completions in the test was $3,300. Without a confirmed incremental revenue per upsell, you cannot verify if the 5% upsell lift covers this cost. Rolling out globally risks scaling a feature where the marginal cost exceeds marginal revenue. The arithmetic error in summing completions further indicates data hygiene issues that must be resolved before a full launch.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Negative Unit Economics:** The cost of licensing/support ($15/completion) exceeds the incremental revenue generated by the 5% upsell lift, eroding Q4 margins.
2.  **Support Overload:** A global rollout increases completion volume by ~2,200 users/month immediately; if support ticket volume scales non-linearly with completions, operational costs will spike beyond the $15 estimate.
3.  **False Positive Retention:** The observed conversion lift is noise from "peeking" or seasonal variance, not a true causal effect, leading to a permanent cost increase without revenue gain.

**Stop/Change Triggers:**
*   **Conversion Rate:** If the guarded expansion cohort drops below **9.0%** (losing the buffer over control).
*   **Cost Efficiency:** If the fully loaded cost per completion exceeds **$20** (accounting for hidden support overhead).
*   **Upsell Realization:** If the actual upsell rate among completers falls below **3%** (missing the conservative threshold of the 5% assumption).

*Note: The $1.2M Q4 revenue and $350 CAC are context only; the decision hinges entirely on the marginal unit economics of the feature itself.*
*Ensure the 30-day guardrail period uses a pre-defined sample size to avoid "peeking" bias before making the final go/no-go decision.*
