**Recommendation:** Do not roll out agency-wide yet. The test lacks a valid causal claim for ridership (unknown data) and suffers from confounding variables (the Sept 10 ad push) that invalidate the DAU lift. Furthermore, while click-through increased, revenue per click dropped 11%, creating a net revenue risk that outweighs the unproven satisfaction benefit.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1. **Revenue Erosion:** Scaling a feature that lowers average revenue per click could reduce total yield even if volume rises, specifically if the "Live-Crowd" alerts discourage high-value trips or promote low-fare off-peak travel.
2. **Confounded Growth:** Attributing the DAU increase to the feature rather than the city-wide ad campaign leads to over-investment in a product that may not drive retention once ad spend stops.
3. **Notification Fatigue:** If the 22% click lift stems from novelty rather than value, long-term engagement may crash as users mute or uninstall due to irrelevant alerts.

**Stop/Change Triggers:**
*   **Immediate Halt:** If post-rollout monitoring shows average revenue per click drops below **$0.38** (compounding the test trend) or if the net revenue impact turns negative for two consecutive weeks.
*   **Feature Reversion:** If click-through rates fall below **1.5%** after the initial novelty period (4 weeks), indicating the content does not sustain user interest.
*   **Guardrail Breach:** If uninstall rates or push-notification opt-out rates increase by more than **5%** relative to the baseline, signaling active user dissatisfaction.

**Note:** The assumption that click-through equals satisfaction is unverified; instrument a specific "trip taken after alert" event before scaling. Re-run the analysis excluding the Sept 10–28 cohort to isolate the feature's true effect from the ad campaign.
