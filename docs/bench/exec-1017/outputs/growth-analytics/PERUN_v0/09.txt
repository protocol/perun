**Recommendation:** Do **not** approve a district-wide launch based on these results. The treatment group (Live-Assist) demonstrated a statistically significant **negative impact** on conversion, with uptake dropping from 12.0% in the control group to 9.0% in the treatment group. Launching a feature that reduces paid pilot adoption by 25% relative to baseline contradicts growth objectives. Furthermore, the preliminary ROI is inflated; including the $10k implementation support cost per district will drastically lower margins, potentially rendering the $120/teacher price point unviable at current uptake rates.

**Two Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Dilution:** Scaling a feature that actively suppresses conversion will reduce total contract value across the 12 existing districts and jeopardize new acquisitions, compounding losses as implementation costs scale linearly with district count.
2.  **Churn Amplification:** The assumption that baseline churn (15%) remains static is unverified. If the "Live-Assist" feature adds complexity without perceived value, it may increase friction, causing actual churn to exceed 15% and accelerating revenue loss beyond the initial conversion dip.

**Halting/Pivot Trigger:**
Halt the rollout immediately if the **Week-4 retention rate** of the teachers who *did* convert in Group B is lower than Group A. If the feature not only lowers initial uptake but also fails to retain the fewer users who adopt it, the product strategy must pivot from "feature addition" to "usability remediation" or deprecation.
