**Recommendation:** Do not approve a full fleet-wide rollout yet. Instead, authorize a targeted expansion to 300 vehicles only after implementing mandatory charging infrastructure upgrades at the four identified bottleneck locations. While the unit economics are strong ($9,500 monthly savings vs. $15,000 upfront premium yields a payback period of under two months), the 15% failure rate to meet uptime targets due to charging bottlenecks represents a critical operational risk. Scaling immediately without resolving the infrastructure constraint will replicate these downtime incidents across the entire fleet, potentially eroding the calculated fuel and maintenance savings through lost revenue and service level agreement (SLA) penalties.

**Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Infrastructure Saturation:** Expanding the fleet without upgrading charging capacity will linearly increase downtime, turning a localized pilot issue into a systemic operational failure.
2.  **Battery Degradation Variance:** If real-world battery degradation exceeds current projections after the 12-month mark, the long-term total cost of ownership (TCO) could surpass diesel equivalents, negating the upfront maintenance benefits.
3.  **Revenue Erosion:** The current analysis assumes static revenue; however, if downtime exceeds 12 hours frequently, lost delivery revenue may exceed the $5,500 monthly operational savings, making the electric vans net-negative.

**Stop/Change Course Triggers:**
*   **Uptime Threshold:** Halt expansion immediately if post-upgrade uptime for any cohort drops below **88%** for two consecutive months.
*   **Cost Variance:** Pause the program if verified battery degradation costs or unplanned repair expenses reduce the net monthly savings per vehicle to below **$4,000**.
*   **Downtime Frequency:** Revert to diesel procurement for affected routes if more than **5%** of vehicles experience >12 hours of downtime in a single month despite infrastructure upgrades.

*Note: The driver satisfaction increase (65 to 78) is a positive leading indicator for retention but should not outweigh the hard financial triggers listed above.*
