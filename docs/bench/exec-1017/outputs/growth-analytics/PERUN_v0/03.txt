**Financial Reality Check & Arithmetic Corrections**
Your revenue calculation contains a critical assumption error. You estimated a 15% increase in *average procedure revenue per visit* ($120). However, the A/B test only proved an increase in *visit volume* (show-up rate), not spend per visit. Unless the SMS explicitly upsells higher-margin procedures, you should model revenue based on **volume uplift only**.
*   **Corrected Uplift:** The variant generated 968 visits vs. a projected 836 for control (38% of 2,200). That is **132 extra visits**.
*   **Conservative Revenue:** 132 visits × $120 = **$15,840** (not the higher figure implied by increasing the per-visit average).
*   **Test Costs:** Messages ($2,600) + Development ($12,000) = **$14,600**.
*   **Net Test Profit:** ~$1,240. This is a razor-thin margin (~8%) that leaves no room for churn or integration bugs. The "15% revenue per visit" assumption must be validated before scaling.

**Primary Failure Modes**
1.  **Churn & Annoyance:** If the 6% uplift comes from patients who feel harassed and cancel future appointments (negative LTV), the system destroys value.
2.  **Integration Fragility:** If the 80-hour dev estimate was optimistic and maintenance requires ongoing engineering hours, the $0.052/message cost will spike.
3.  **False Positive Volume:** If the "extra" visits are simply cannibalizing slots from other high-value procedures without adding net capacity, revenue will not rise.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger**
**Halt expansion immediately if:** Net profit (Revenue from extra visits minus SMS and maintenance costs) is **negative for two consecutive months** post-rollout, OR if patient opt-out/complaint rates exceed **0.5%** of messaged users. Do not wait three months; the margin is too thin to absorb a bad month.
