While the test showed a raw lift in sign-ups and projected revenue, the decision to roll out nationwide is currently **UNVERIFIED** due to three critical gaps that violate experiment rigor:

1.  **Confounded Results (The "Construction Boom"):** The test period coincided with an 8% regional activity surge. Without adjusting for this external variable, we cannot isolate whether the 17.9% lift was caused by the "Forecast" tier or simply the market tide. Attributing organic market growth to product changes is a classic vanity metric error.
2.  **Peeking at Intermediate Metrics:** The decision relies entirely on *sign-ups* (Acquisition) while ignoring *Retention*. In a bottom-up funnel analysis, acquisition gains are wasted if the new tier fails to retain users. With zero data on 90-day churn, assuming sign-ups translate linearly to quarterly revenue is a dangerous assumption. If Forecast users churn 2x faster than Standard users due to complexity or pricing mismatch, the higher initial revenue will evaporate.
3.  **Unverified Cost Assumptions:** The support cost estimate ($0.10/user) is a guess, not an instrumented metric. If the new tier generates complex tickets requiring senior engineer time, the margin could flip from positive to negative immediately upon scale.

**Principal Failure Modes:**
*   **The "Leaky Bucket" Failure:** We scale acquisition into a product with poor retention. The construction boom masks early churn signals, leading us to double down on a tier that users abandon after month two.
*   **Margin Erosion:** The "Forecast" tier attracts high-maintenance users. If actual support costs hit $0.50/user instead of $0.10, the $1,200 LTV assumption collapses, and the tier becomes profitable only on paper.
*   **Cannibalization:** Existing "Standard" users who would have renewed naturally might downgrade or churn if forced into the new pricing structure during a full rollout, netting a loss despite the new sign-up spike.

**Stop/Pivot Triggers (The Guardrails):**
*   **Churn:** If 90-day retention for "Forecast" is **>15% worse** than the "Standard" baseline, halt expansion and revert to pricing experiments.

*   **Net Revenue:** If the *net* revenue (Revenue minus Support Costs) per cohort does not show a statistically significant lift after adjusting for the 8% market baseline within 45 days, pivot to a feature-gated add-on rather than a tier replacement.
