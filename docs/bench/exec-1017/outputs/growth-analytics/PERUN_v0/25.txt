While the variant shows a 4.5% lift in trial sign-ups, this result is **unverified** because the critical retention data for the new cohort is missing. **Decision Protocol:**
1.  **Hold full rollout.** Enable the variant only for new traffic while instrumenting the 90-day retention cohort immediately.
2.  **Re-evaluate in 90 days.** Compare the retention curve of the March variant cohort against the March control cohort.
3.  **Launch condition:** Proceed only if the variant's 90-day retention is statistically equal to or greater than the control's, ensuring the sign-up lift translates to actual user value.

**How This Decision Could Fail:**
*   **False Positive (Peeking Risk):** If you launch based solely on the sign-up lift, you may scale a wizard that attracts low-intent users who churn before paying, increasing support costs ($12k/mo) while diluting lifetime value.
*   **Instrumentation Gap:** If you fail to tag the March cohort with a stable, pseudonymous user ID now, you will be unable to calculate their 90-day retention later, forcing another 3-month delay.
*   **Support Overload:** The variant increases volume; if the "completed onboarding" lift includes confused users requiring high-touch support, the $12k/month estimate may be underestimated.

**Stop/Reconsider Triggers:**
*   **Primary Trigger:** If the variant cohort's **90-day retention rate drops more than 2% absolute** below the control cohort's rate (e.g., Control is 80%, Variant is <78%). This indicates the wizard sacrifices quality for quantity.
*   **Secondary Trigger:** If the **cost-per-active-user** (support cost divided by retained users) for the variant exceeds the control by >10% after 60 days.
*   **Immediate Stop:** If qualitative feedback indicates the new wizard causes critical compliance errors or data integrity issues in patient records.
