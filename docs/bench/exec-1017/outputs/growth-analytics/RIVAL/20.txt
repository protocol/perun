**Recommendation: Proceed with a Phased Rollout (50% Traffic) Pending Default Rate Validation**

Approve the new digital loan application flow for immediate expansion to 50% of total traffic, but withhold a 100% bank-wide mandate until default data matures. The observed 1.4-point conversion lift (8.5% to 9.9%) is statistically significant given the sample size (1,200 per variant) and generates a compelling gross revenue upside (~$150k/month). However, because the net profitability depends entirely on the quality of these additional borrowers, a full rollout exposes the bank to unacceptable tail risk before the default curve is established. The $2,000 incremental underwriting cost per loan is already factored into your net revenue estimate and does not justify delay, but the unknown default rate does. Use the 50% phase to gather sufficient default data (minimum 3–6 months of performance) while capturing the majority of the upside.

**Primary Failure Modes & Risks**
1.  **Adverse Selection (The "Lemon" Risk):** The new flow may be removing friction specifically for high-risk borrowers who would have abandoned the old form, leading to a spike in defaults that erases the $150k revenue gain. If the default rate on the variant exceeds the control by >1.5 percentage points, the net present value (NPV) of the cohort turns negative.
2.  **Operational Bottleneck:** The projection assumes underwriting capacity scales linearly. If the 16% volume increase (driven by higher conversion) creates a backlog that delays funding, conversion rates may decay, and the $150k revenue target will not be realized.
3.  **Regression to Mean:** The March test window may have captured a specific customer segment (e.g., tax-season filers) that responds uniquely to the new UI. As traffic sources normalize, the lift could compress to <0.5 points, making the $2,000 incremental cost harder to justify.

**Stop/Pivot Triggers**
*   **Default Rate:** Halt expansion immediately if the variant's 90-day delinquency rate exceeds the control group by **2.0 percentage points** (e.g., if control is 4.0%, stop if variant hits 6.0%). This threshold protects the bank's capital adequacy ratios.
*   **Revenue Shortfall:** Pivot back to the old form if actual net revenue (after underwriting costs and charge-offs) trails the projection by **>25% for two consecutive months**.
*   **Cost Overrun:** If the incremental underwriting cost rises above **$3,500 per loan** due to manual interventions required by the new flow's data structure.

*Note: Ensure your data team isolates the "funded" cohort specifically; do not let "application started" metrics dilute the default rate calculation.*
*Final approval for 100% rollout requires a signed risk memo from the Chief Risk Officer confirming the default delta is within tolerance.*
