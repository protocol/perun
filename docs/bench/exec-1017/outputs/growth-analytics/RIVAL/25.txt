**Recommendation: Do Not Fully Launch Yet – Proceed to Limited Rollout with Guardrails**

The observed 4.5% lift in trial sign-ups (322 vs. 308) is not statistically significant at the 95% confidence level. With ~7,500 users per variant and a baseline conversion rate of ~4%, the minimum detectable effect (MDE) for significance is approximately 18-20%. Your observed 4.5% lift falls well within the range of random noise (p-value ≈ 0.35). The 2.1% lift in onboarding steps is even less significant.

Launching fully based on these results risks spending $57,000 (initial + first month) on a change that provides no proven benefit. However, the variant showed no negative signals, suggesting it is safe to test further.

**Decision:** Approve a **limited rollout to 20% of traffic** for 60 days. This allows you to:
1.  Gather sufficient sample size to detect a true 4-5% lift (requires ~40k users per variant).
2.  Measure the critical unknown: 90-day churn/retention for the new cohort.
3.  Validate support costs before full commitment.

**Main Ways This Decision Could Fail:**
*   **False Negative Risk:** The wizard actually drives a meaningful 5%+ lift, but we delay full revenue capture by 2 months while gathering more data.
*   **Hidden Churn Risk:** The wizard attracts lower-quality sign-ups who convert initially but churn at 2x the baseline rate within 90 days, destroying LTV despite higher top-of-funnel volume.
*   **Support Cost Underestimation:** The $12k/month support estimate is based on early data; if complexity scales non-linearly, unit economics could turn negative at scale.

**Stop/Reconsider Triggers:**
During the 60-day limited rollout, halt expansion immediately if:
*   **90-day retention** for the variant cohort drops below **78%** of the control group's retention (e.g., if control is 80%, stop if variant < 62.4%).
*   **Support ticket volume** per user exceeds **1.5x** the control group's rate.
*   **Statistical analysis** after 40k users shows the variant is **significantly worse** (p < 0.05) on trial-to-paid conversion.

Maya should authorize the limited rollout only if Engineering confirms the 20% toggle is reversible within 1 hour and Customer Success agrees to weekly churn monitoring.

*Note: The initial test was underpowered; treating the 4.5% lift as factual is a statistical error. The limited rollout is a risk-mitigation step, not an endorsement of the current results.*
