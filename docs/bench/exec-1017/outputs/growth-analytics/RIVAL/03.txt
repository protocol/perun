**Financial Reality Check & Arithmetic Corrections**
Your test data contains a critical volume bias that inflates the variant's perceived success. The control group had 2,800 visits while the variant had only 2,200. You cannot compare raw attendance numbers (1,064 vs. 968) directly; the variant actually generated **96 fewer total attended visits** than the control during the test period simply due to lower volume. The win is purely in the *rate* (44% vs. 38%), not total volume.

Furthermore, your cost assumptions need correction:
1.  **Message Costs:** You stated the test cost $2,600. However, 5,000 appointments × 1.3 messages × $0.04 = **$260**, not $2,600. You are off by a factor of 10.
2.  **Development Costs:** 80 hours × $150 = **$12,000** (one-time sunk cost).
3.  **Revenue Uplift Assumption:** You assume a 15% revenue increase per visit. This is an assumption, not a measured fact from the test. If the extra 6% of show-ups are simple check-ups rather than high-value procedures, this margin vanishes.

**Break-Even Analysis**
Ignoring the sunk $12k dev cost, the variable profit per additional attendee is roughly $138 ($120 base + 15% uplift - $0.05 SMS cost). The 6% lift on 2,200 appointments yields ~132 extra visits/month, generating ~$18,200 in gross contribution. This easily covers the $260 variable cost. However, to recover the $12,000 development cost, you need roughly **one month** of chain-wide volume equivalent to this test size. If your chain is 10x larger, you break even on dev costs in days.

**Primary Failure Modes**
1.  **Integration Fragility:** If the SMS trigger fires after the appointment time due to timezone bugs, you incur costs while annoying patients.
2.  **Opt-Out Spirals:** If patients perceive these as spam rather than care, unsubscribe rates could spike, blocking future critical communications.
3.  **Revenue Dilution:** If the 6% uplift consists entirely of low-margin vaccine checks rather than the assumed $120 procedures, the ROI collapses.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger**
 Halt expansion immediately if, after 60 days of full rollout, the **net contribution margin (Revenue from incremental visits minus SMS costs) fails to exceed $5,000 per month per clinic cluster**. This indicates the 15% revenue assumption is false or the show-up rate is regressing to the mean.
