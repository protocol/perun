**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **staged rollout** (25% of traffic) immediately, but **do not** commit to a full platform-wide launch until 90-day churn and support volume data are verified.

While the variant delivered a statistically significant 33% lift in sign-ups (9% vs. 12%), the assumption that this translates linearly to revenue is unproven without retention data. A staged rollout limits exposure to potential negative unit economics while allowing you to gather the missing 90-day cohort data.

**Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Negative Unit Economics:** The $120,000 cloud credit cost may exceed the Lifetime Value (LTV) of the incremental 150 users if their churn is higher than the control group (e.g., users attracted by "zero-config" expectations churn when they hit complexity limits).
2.  **Support Scalability:** If the "zero-config" promise creates a gap between expectation and reality, support ticket volume could spike, eroding margins and damaging brand trust.
3.  **Infrastructure Strain:** A sudden influx of free-tier users utilizing cloud credits could strain infrastructure or trigger unexpected overage costs not modeled in the test.

**Stop/Pivot Triggers:**
*   **Churn:** Stop full rollout if 90-day churn for the variant cohort exceeds **45%** (assuming control is ~30%; a >50% relative increase indicates poor fit).
*   **Support Volume:** Pivot if support tickets per active user increase by **>20%** compared to the control baseline.
*   **LTV:CAC:** Halt if the calculated LTV of variant users (factoring in the $800 incremental cost per new user: $120k/150 users) falls below **3x** the acquisition cost.

**Hidden Assumptions & Arithmetic Issues:**
*   **Linear Revenue Fallacy:** You assumed sign-up growth equals revenue growth. This ignores that free-tier users often have near-zero marginal revenue but high marginal costs (cloud credits). If these 150 extra users never convert to paid plans, the test generated a **net loss of $120,000** for the month, not profit.
*   **Cost Attribution:** The $120,000 was attributed to the *entire* test period, but the cost likely scales with usage, not just sign-ups. If the variant users are heavier users of the cache feature, the cost per *retained* user could be significantly higher than the average calculated here.
*   **Sample Representativeness:** The test ran on a "random 5,000-user segment." Ensure this segment's behavior mirrors the broader population; if the test group was unusually active or prone to trying new features, the 12% conversion rate may not hold at scale.
*   **Timeframe Mismatch:** You are making a long-term rollout decision based on 30-day acquisition data while explicitly admitting a lack of 90-day retention data. This is the critical gap; high churn could render the acquisition lift meaningless.
