**Recommendation for Library Director Maya Patel:**
Proceed with a **phased county-wide rollout** of the "Digital Explorer" portal, contingent on securing a maintenance budget cap before full deployment. The A/B test demonstrates a statistically decisive win: sign-ups increased by 50% (from 1,200 to 1,800) and banner engagement rose by 83% (from 24% to 44%). These gains vastly outweigh the known $45,000 annual license cost, provided the unknown maintenance costs do not exceed the value of the ~600 additional patrons acquired in a single month.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Budget Overruns:** Rolling out without defining the "real unknown" maintenance costs could turn a win into a fiscal liability.
2.  **Technical Scalability:** The test ran on a controlled sample; county-wide traffic may expose latency or stability glitches not seen in June.
3.  **Engagement Illusion:** High click-throughs may reflect novelty rather than utility if users do not return after the initial sign-up.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
Halt expansion or revert to the legacy portal if **net new sign-up growth falls below 15%** (not 3%) in the first quarter post-rollout. Given the test showed a 50% lift, a drop to 3% indicates the test environment did not reflect real-world conditions or the effect has completely decayed. Additionally, pivot immediately if confirmed maintenance costs exceed $15,000/quarter.

**Critical Arithmetic & Assumption Flags:**
*   **Faulty Engagement Assumption:** You stated, "a 5% increase in click-through directly translates to a 5% increase in long-term patron engagement." This is mathematically and logically flawed. First, your click-through increase was **83%** (20 percentage points on a 24% base), not 5%. Second, a click is a micro-conversion; assuming it maps 1:1 to *long-term* engagement is a dangerous oversimplification that ignores churn. You have zero data on retention.
*   **Sample Size Sufficiency:** With 5,000+ visitors per variant and a massive lift in conversions (1,200 vs. 1,800), the result is statistically significant (p < 0.001). The risk here is not statistical error, but **external validity**—whether June's traffic represents the whole county.
*   **Missing Denominator for ROI:** You cannot calculate true ROI without the maintenance cost. Do not present a "profit" figure to the board; present a "break-even maintenance cost" threshold instead.
