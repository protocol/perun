**Recommendation: Do not roll out Variant B system-wide yet; continue with Variant A while running a properly concurrent A/B test.**

The current data does not support switching to the personalized voice call workflow. The observed difference in confirmation rates (27.6% vs. 28.5%) is statistically insignificant given the sample sizes, meaning the 0.9% lift is likely random noise rather than a true effect. More critically, the test design is flawed: because Variant A and B ran in different weeks (Mar 3–10 vs. Mar 11–18), external factors like weather, flu trends, or day-of-week effects could explain the difference. Additionally, Variant B costs 4.4× more per contact ($0.22 vs. $0.05). To justify this cost, you would need a massive reduction in no-shows that this tiny, unreliable confirmation lift cannot guarantee. Without tracking post-confirmation cancellations or actual no-show rates, you are optimizing for a vanity metric (confirmation) rather than the business outcome (attended appointments).

**How This Decision Could Fail:**
1.  **Hidden Labor Costs:** If the $0.22 voice call cost excludes manual dialing or follow-up labor, the true cost per patient could be double, destroying ROI.
2.  **Patient Preference Shifts:** Patients may find unsolicited voice calls intrusive compared to SMS, potentially increasing opt-outs or dissatisfaction long-term, even if short-term confirmations rise slightly.
3.  **False Confidence:** Rolling out based on non-significant data may lead to spending 4× the budget for zero actual improvement in attendance.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
 Halt any expansion of the voice call workflow immediately if a properly randomized, concurrent A/B test shows a **sustained no-show rate above 12%** for Variant B, or if the **cost per attended appointment** for Variant B exceeds Variant A by more than 15% after accounting for all labor and telephony fees.
