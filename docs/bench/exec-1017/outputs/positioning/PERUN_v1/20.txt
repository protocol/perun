**Positioning Recommendation: The "Event-Activated Donor" Hypothesis**

**Target Segment:** Alumni who attended at least one university event in the last 24 months (the "Event-Activated" segment), rather than the entire alumni base.
**Value Proposition:** Transform sporadic event attendance into consistent giving by automating the post-event engagement window when donor intent is highest.
**Category:** Alumni Engagement & Conversion Platform.
**Differentiation:** Unlike LegacyConnect and DonorBridge, which focus on broad database management, Kestrel's platform specifically targets the "event-to-gift" conversion gap, leveraging your internal data that event attendees are 2x more likely to give.
**Headline Message:** "Turn Event Attendance into Enduring Support: The only platform engineered to capture the 2x giving potential of your engaged alumni."

**Primary Failure Modes & Kill Metric**
1.  **Attribution Error:** The 12% lift and $1.2M pledges were driven by a specific major gift or external factor, not the platform. If a controlled A/B test against a holdout group shows <5% incremental lift, the ROI case collapses.
2.  **Hidden Cost Overrun:** The total cost of ownership is currently underestimated. If integration complexities push costs beyond $15k (including maintenance), the payback period exceeds acceptable limits for a pilot tool.
3.  **Churn Cliff:** If Year 1 churn exceeds 20% (a reasonable benchmark for SaaS without sticky features), the lifetime value (LTV) drops below the acquisition cost, making the model unsustainable.

**Kill Metric for Dr. Graves:** Stop or change course if the pilot's net incremental revenue (after subtracting the $13k total cost) is less than $50,000 by Q2, or if churn exceeds 15% within the first 6 months of full deployment.

**Hidden Risks & Arithmetic Slips**
*   **Sample Bias:** You interviewed 78 donors (45% response rate), but these are likely your *most engaged* donors. This skews the perceived value proposition; non-donors or lapsed alumni may not respond similarly.
*   **Cost Blind Spot:** You noted the $3k integration cost but missed ongoing maintenance, training, and potential CRM API usage fees. The $13k figure is likely a floor, not a ceiling.
*   **Correlation vs. Causation:** The "2x likely to give" stat is an observation, not proof the platform causes it. Without a control group, you cannot claim the platform *creates* this behavior, only that it targets those already prone to it.
*   **Competitor Comparison:** Comparing against LegacyConnect ($8k) and DonorBridge ($10k) is valid only if feature sets are identical. If they include modules you lack, the price comparison is misleading.

*Note: This positioning is a hypothesis based on your pilot data; it requires validation with a broader, less engaged alumni segment before full-scale rollout.*
*Ensure Dr. Graves sees the "event-activated" focus as a strategic narrowing, not a limitation, to maximize early wins.*
