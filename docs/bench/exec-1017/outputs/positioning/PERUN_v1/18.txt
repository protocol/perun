**Positioning Recommendation: SolarFlex Texas Launch**

**Target Segment:** Texas homeowners facing immediate rate hikes (trigger) who are rejected or discouraged by traditional bank loans due to credit rigidity or "financing fatigue."

**Value Proposition:** SolarFlex is a residential solar package that guarantees a 14% bill reduction within six months with zero upfront capital, bypassing traditional loan hurdles through a flexible lease-to-own model.

**Differentiation:**
*   **Vs. BrightVolt:** While BrightVolt competes on a 10-year hardware warranty, SolarFlex competes on *financial accessibility*. We remove the barrier of upfront cost and credit rigidity that stops the sale before the warranty matters.
*   **Vs. HelioHome:** While HelioHome bundles a battery lease (adding complexity and cost), SolarFlex focuses purely on immediate bill reduction with a simpler, lower-commitment entry point for cost-sensitive buyers.

**Headline Tagline:** "SolarFlex: Cut Your Bill 14% Today. Pay Only When You Save."

**Top Three Failure Modes:**
1.  **Margin Erosion:** The unaccounted $1,200 Texas permitting fee destroys the unit economics of the "zero upfront" model, making the lease unprofitable at the current price point.
2.  **Misidentified Driver:** The assumption that "upfront cost" is the primary driver is wrong; if customers actually fear long-term contract lock-in more than initial cash outlay, the lease model will underperform against cash-purchase competitors.
3.  **Competitor Response:** BrightVolt or HelioHome matches the financing terms, neutralizing our primary differentiator while retaining their brand trust or battery upsell advantages.

**Abort/Pivot Metric:**
If the **net conversion rate** on qualified leads drops below **15%** (adjusted for the new permitting cost reality) by the end of Q2 2024, or if the **customer acquisition cost (CAC)** exceeds **$2,400** (reflecting the permitting fee impact), abort the current pricing model and pivot to a hybrid upfront/lease structure.

***

**Note:** The $1,200 permitting fee must be immediately modeled into the unit economics before any marketing spend; the current cost model is incomplete. The "financing fatigue" driver remains an assumption tagged `[ASSUMED]` and requires validation in the next round of buyer interviews.
