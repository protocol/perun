**Positioning Recommendation: "The Private Ledger"**

**Hypothesis to Validate:** For **millennial savers who have stopped using banking apps due to data-sharing fears** [ASSUMED trigger], Pennywise is the **private budgeting ledger** that **guarantees your financial data never leaves your device**; unlike **ad-supported free apps or cloud-synced competitors**, it **monetizes via subscription, not your data**.

**Why this position:** Your data shows 55% of power users cite privacy as a deal-breaker, and churn reduction (12% → 8%) suggests retention is tied to trust. While 42% want social features, prioritizing "social saving" contradicts the core privacy constraint of your most loyal segment. This position leverages your existing $2.5M ARR stability to own the "anti-surveillance" niche before competitors like BudgetBuddy potentially obscure their data practices with new AI features.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **The "Good Enough" Alternative:** Users claim to care about privacy but ultimately choose free, ad-supported rivals (SaveSmart) because the *perceived* risk of data mining is abstract, while the $4.99 cost is immediate.
2.  **Feature Gap Friction:** The "privacy-first" architecture technically prevents the "automatic expense categorisation" (valued by 68%) if that feature relies on cloud processing, forcing a trade-off that breaks the value proposition.
3.  **Acquisition Cost Mismatch:** The $45k monthly marketing spend fails to reach the specific "privacy-paranoid" subset efficiently, resulting in a CAC that exceeds the LTV of the $4.99 tier despite the lower churn.

**Kill Metric (Pivot Trigger):**
Stop or pivot this positioning if, after 60 days of the new campaign, the **conversion rate from landing page view to paid trial for the "privacy" headline is below 2.5%** (approx. 1/40th of your current upgrade velocity relative to traffic), indicating the market will not pay a premium for privacy alone without the social or automation hooks.
