**Positioning Recommendation**

**Hypothesis:** For independent restaurant owners (10–30 seats) who lose critical hours to manual payroll administration, Acme Payroll is a cash-flow operations tool that reduces payroll processing time by 50%; unlike generic HR platforms or the status quo of spreadsheets, it delivers "Profit-First" certainty by guaranteeing same-day fund availability and eliminating compliance penalties.

**Rationale:** Your data indicates "time spent" (68%) is the dominant pain point, significantly outranking "accuracy" (42%). Therefore, the "Profit-First" tagline must be framed as *time reclaimed equals profit protected*, rather than just financial accounting. The differentiator targets the specific constraint of small operators who cannot absorb the lag time or hidden costs of larger competitors like BistroPay.

**Three Ways This Could Fail**
1.  **Misaligned Value Prop:** Owners with <10 seats may prioritize absolute lowest cost over speed, rendering the "Profit-First" premium message irrelevant if the $2,500 integration estimate creates a barrier they cannot cross.
2.  **Unproven Differentiator:** If "same-day fund availability" is not legally or technically guaranteed, the core promise collapses, and the tagline becomes perceived as marketing fluff rather than a operational relief.
3.  **Competitor Response:** If BistroPay lowers its barrier to entry for small seats, our specific advantage (agility vs. scale) evaporates without a defensible proof point beyond speed.

**Kill Metric / Pivot Signal**
Maya should stop or pivot if, in a controlled landing page test with 500 targeted owners, the click-through rate on the "Profit-First" headline is <2% *or* if fewer than 15% of demo requests cite "time savings" as their primary driver during qualification. This validates whether the market actually connects your speed claim to their profitability before scaling the message.
