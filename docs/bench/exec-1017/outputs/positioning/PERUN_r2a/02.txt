**Headline Positioning Statement (Hypothesis to Validate)**
"For FinTech, HealthTech, and SaaS engineering leaders struggling with pipeline latency, Quickbuild is a CI accelerator that guarantees 30% faster build times; unlike TurboCI and BuildFast, it delivers this speed without requiring a 15-minute daily maintenance window, though the $45,000/seat price point and long-term retention remain unproven assumptions."

**Three Primary Failure Modes**
1.  **Net-Speed Illusion:** The "30% faster" claim collapses when prospects calculate the 15-minute daily "auto-scale" maintenance overhead, making the net time savings negligible compared to competitors.
2.  **Price-to-Value Mismatch:** At $45,000/seat, the cost exceeds the perceived value of speed alone if the maintenance burden shifts labor costs back to the customer, causing the projected 70% adoption rate to fail.
3.  **Retention Cliff:** The 42% "willingness to switch" was based on raw speed; if churn exceeds expectations due to operational friction, the unit economics break before reaching the $4.7M ARR target.

**Pivot/Halt Triggers**
*   **Churn Trigger:** Confirmed churn rate >12% within the first six months of paid usage.
*   **Market Loss Trigger:** Loss of >5% of the 150 target enterprise accounts to BuildFast specifically citing "operational overhead" or "total cost of ownership" as the deciding factor.

**Falsification Test (Run Now)**
Conduct a "Net-Speed" sales simulation with 10 target buyers from the interview pool: Present the full value proposition *including* the 15-minute daily maintenance requirement and the $45k price. Measure the conversion rate to a pilot agreement. If fewer than 3 out of 10 agree to a pilot when the maintenance cost is explicit (vs. the 42% interest when it was hidden), the positioning hypothesis is falsified, and the launch must be halted to re-engineer the auto-scale feature or repricing model.

*Note: The $4.7M ARR figure and 70% adoption rate are internal projections based on unverified churn assumptions; do not present these as market facts to the board. The competitor speed comparisons must be validated by third-party benchmarks before public claims are made.*
