**Three Main Ways This Could Fail**

1.  **The "Accuracy" Blind Spot:** While only 42% of interviews cited accuracy as a top pain, payroll errors are catastrophic for small businesses. If the "Profit-First" messaging implies speed over precision, and a single early adopter suffers a tax penalty or underpaid staff due to a glitch, trust evaporates instantly. Speed is a nice-to-have; accuracy is a license to operate.
2.  **The Integration Cost Trap:** You estimated integration support at $2,500/location but haven't priced it. If the "Profit-First" promise requires deep POS integration to work, and the real cost to onboard a 10-seat restaurant exceeds their lifetime value (LTV), the unit economics will collapse before you reach scale. The value prop fails if the cure costs more than the disease.
3.  **Misidentified Buyer Priority:** The tagline assumes owners care most about "cash flow forecasting." However, if the primary trigger for switching is actually "fear of compliance audits" (a latent pain not captured in the "top pain" statistic), a message focused on profit optimization will feel tone-deaf compared to a competitor promising "100% Compliance Guarantee."

**Kill Metric / Pivot Signal**

**Metric:** Conversion rate on the landing page or sales demo where the primary headline is "Profit-First Payroll" vs. a control headline focused on "Zero-Error Payroll."

**Threshold for Maya to Stop/Pivot:** If, after testing with 50 qualified leads (owners of <30 seat restaurants), the "Profit-First" variant yields a demo request rate **lower than 15%** (or statistically significantly lower than the control), stop the launch. This signals that "time saved" or "profit visibility" is not the dominant trigger for purchase, and the core hypothesis regarding the buyer's primary motivation is false. Do not proceed to full GTM until the message resonates with at least 1 in 5 prospects.
