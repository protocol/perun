**Positioning Recommendation for Alana Torres**

**Target Segment:** Daily commuters in Metro Line's coverage zone who currently rely on inconsistent official schedules or generic maps and have missed a connection in the last month.
**Value Proposition:** The only rider app guaranteed to predict arrival times within 30 seconds by ingesting raw city telemetry, eliminating the anxiety of missed connections.
**Differentiation:** Unlike TransitPro (broad coverage, generalized estimates) or CityPulse (fare-focused), we trade feature breadth and discounts for superior temporal precision.
**Headline:** "Don't just watch the bus. Know exactly when it arrives."

**Primary Failure Modes**
1.  **Data Dependency:** The city's open data API proves too unstable or costly to integrate, breaking the core accuracy promise before launch.
2.  **Market Misread:** While users *say* they value accuracy in interviews, they may not switch apps if the perceived gain (saving 2 minutes) doesn't outweigh the friction of downloading a new tool.
3.  **Competitor Response:** TransitPro or CityPulse rapidly improves their own prediction algorithms, neutralizing our sole differentiator.

**Kill Metric & Pivot Trigger**
Alana should stop or pivot if, after a $50k spend (one-third of the budget), the **Day-7 Retention Rate** for new users falls below **25%**.
*Reasoning:* If the product truly delivers superior accuracy, users dependent on timing will return daily. Low retention indicates either the accuracy isn't perceptibly better than competitors, or the "accuracy" job-to-be-done isn't strong enough to drive habit formation on its own.

**Note:** This position assumes the API integration is technically feasible; immediate validation of integration costs is required before full budget commitment. Competitor market share claims (e.g., TransitPro's 30%) are unverified external assertions and should not be cited as fact in public messaging without independent audit.
