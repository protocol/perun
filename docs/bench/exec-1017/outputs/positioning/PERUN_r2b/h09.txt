**Positioning Recommendation for Maya Patel**

*   **Target Segment:** Commuters in pilot zones prioritizing schedule reliability over lowest fare (based on 78% "convenience" priority).
*   **Value Proposition:** Guaranteed on-demand transit that eliminates wait-time uncertainty for a predictable $8 trip.
*   **Differentiation:** Unlike SkyShuttle ($7, lower reliability) or MetroLink ($9, fixed routes), Harbor Transit offers the optimal balance of cost and immediate availability, validated by a 1.5:1 pilot win ratio.
*   **Headline:** "Reliability on Demand: The $8 Ride That Shows Up When You Do."

**Three Biggest Risks to Failure**
1.  **Margin Erosion:** The current $8 price point assumes stable labor costs; if unaccounted driver overtime adds $1.2M annually, the unit economics collapse, forcing a price hike that contradicts the "affordable reliability" promise.
2.  **Regulatory Shock:** An upcoming surcharge (estimated up to $0.50/ride) could push the fare to $8.50+, narrowing the value gap against MetroLink ($9) and triggering price-sensitive churn if convenience isn't perceived as worth the premium over SkyShuttle.
3.  **Misaligned Driver:** Positioning around "convenience" while internally assuming "price sensitivity" creates conflicting go-to-market tactics; if the 12% price-focused segment is actually the volume driver, this messaging will fail to convert the majority.

**Kill Metric / Pivot Trigger**
Stop or pivot this positioning immediately if the **net win/loss ratio drops below 1:1** in any consecutive 30-day period *after* factoring in the regulatory surcharge and overtime-adjusted pricing. This indicates the market no longer perceives the convenience premium as valuable enough to offset the increased cost relative to competitors.
