**Positioning Recommendation for Atlas Arts Pass**

**Target Segment:** Frequent local art donors and families (based on your 20/42 interviewees) who currently buy individual tickets or ad-hoc memberships.
**Value Proposition:** A unified annual pass that eliminates per-visit friction and unlocks exclusive donor-level access for a flat fee.
**Differentiation:** Unlike CulturePass ($79) and ArtAccess ($85), Atlas Arts Pass includes zero-fee integration for partner museums and prioritizes "donor-tier" engagement over simple entry, justifying the $99 price point despite the $20 implementation fee.
**Headline:** "One Pass, Unlimited Access, Exclusive Impact: The Only Membership That Turns Visitors Into Patrons."

**Three Main Failure Modes:**
1. **Price Sensitivity Miscalculation:** The $99 price point (plus hidden $20 implementation fee confusion) proves too high compared to competitors if the "donor" benefit isn't immediately tangible to families.
2. **Corporate Bulk Adoption Gap:** The revenue model relies on unknown corporate bulk purchases; if this segment does not materialize, the gap between the finance team's $1.5M projection and the calculated ~$1.2M (based on 12k passes) cannot be closed.
3. **Integration Friction:** The $20 implementation fee deters smaller museums from joining the network, reducing the total inventory of available tickets and weakening the "unlimited" value promise.

**Kill Metric:**
Stop or pivot if the **trial-to-paid conversion rate drops below 3.5%** after clearly disclosing the $20 implementation fee during the signup flow. This threshold accounts for your current 5% churn and ensures the unit economics remain viable against the $150k marketing cap.
