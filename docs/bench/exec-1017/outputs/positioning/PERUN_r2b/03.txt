**Positioning Recommendation for ClassPulse**

**Target Segment:** Mid-to-large K‑12 districts (1,000+ teachers) currently struggling with low teacher adoption of existing LMS tools, specifically those where IT directors prioritize daily engagement over feature breadth.

**Value Proposition:** ClassPulse is the engagement-first LMS module that transforms passive platforms into daily teaching habits, ensuring technology investments actually reach the classroom.

**Differentiation:** Unlike EduTrack, BrightPath, or ScholarSphere, which compete on price, AI features, or implementation costs, ClassPulse focuses exclusively on maximizing daily active usage. While competitors measure success by contract signing or setup speed, we measure it by teacher login frequency and workflow integration.

**Headline Message:** "Stop buying LMS features your teachers ignore. ClassPulse guarantees daily adoption, turning your LMS budget into actual classroom impact."

**Key Risks & Assumptions:**
1.  **Adoption Gap Assumption:** We assume the 0.3 daily login deficit against EduTrack (1.8 vs. 2.1) is a critical pain point for buyers rather than a neutral metric; if districts view 1.8 logins as "sufficient," our differentiation collapses.
2.  **Hidden Cost Risk:** Our "engagement-first" approach may require higher touch customer success resources to maintain login rates, potentially eroding margins if not priced into the $150k budget assumption.
3.  **Feature Perception:** We risk being perceived as "limited" compared to BrightPath's AI or ScholarSphere's pricing model if we cannot prove that higher adoption directly correlates to better student outcomes.

**Kill Metric:**
If ClassPulse fails to achieve **>2.0 daily active logins per teacher** within the first 3 months of the Riverbend pilot (surpassing the current EduTrack benchmark), we must pause general rollout. This threshold indicates we have not solved the core engagement problem better than the incumbent, invalidating our primary differentiator.

***

*Note: The stated "70% win rate" relies on a small sample size (42 interviews/quarter) and should be presented to the CEO as early traction rather than statistical proof of market fit. Budget variance data must be sourced from actual district RFPs before finalizing pricing strategy.*
