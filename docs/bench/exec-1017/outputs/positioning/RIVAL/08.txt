**Positioning Recommendation:**
Position Acme Payroll as the **"Time-Recovery Engine for Independent Restaurants,"** explicitly targeting owners with fewer than 30 seats who lose 10+ hours monthly on manual payroll processing. Do **not** launch with the "Profit-First" tagline. The data shows 68% of your interviewed owners prioritize time savings over financial optimization (only 42% cite accuracy/cash flow as top concerns). Your competitive advantage lies in serving the "long tail" of smaller venues (avg. 22 seats) that BistroPay overlooks in favor of larger chains (avg. 45 seats). Messaging should focus on reclaiming operator hours rather than abstract profit concepts, directly addressing the primary pain point validated by your Q1-Q2 research.

**Three Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Misaligned Value Proposition:** Launching with "Profit-First" ignores the dominant customer need (time), causing immediate message-market mismatch and low conversion rates among time-starved owners.
2.  **Unit Economics Collapse:** The estimated $2,500 integration support cost per location may exceed the lifetime value of small accounts (<10 seats), especially if price sensitivity is high and pricing cannot cover service delivery.
3.  **Competitive Displacement:** BistroPay could lower barriers to entry for smaller venues, leveraging their brand dominance to crush your niche before you establish a defensible foothold.

**Pivot Trigger:**
Maya should stop or pivot if **pilot conversion rates fall below 25%** among targeted independent restaurants or if the **customer acquisition cost (CAC) exceeds 3x the estimated first-year revenue** due to unabsorbable integration support costs.

*Note: Validate the $2,500 support cost assumption immediately; if it cannot be reduced via automation, the small-restaurant segment may be unprofitable regardless of positioning.*
