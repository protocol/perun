**To:** Ravi Kaur
**Subject:** Positioning Recommendation for "Ethereal Echo"

**Recommendation:**
Position "Ethereal Echo" explicitly as the **"Narrative-First Alternative to Skyward Quest."** Leverage our data showing 34 of 87 interviewed prospects come from narrative-focused communities to target players who feel alienated by BrightStar Studios' graphics-heavy approach. The core message must be: "Deep, branching storytelling where every choice echoes, without the bloat of cutting-edge graphics." By repurposing 60% of the "Moth Lantern" art pipeline, we can redirect resources toward writing and branching logic, directly addressing the 45% churn seen in our current title (likely caused by a lack of long-term narrative engagement). This positions us not as a cheaper clone, but as the specialized choice for the underserved "story-over-spectacle" segment that BrightStar is ignoring.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Misreading the Competitor:** If "Skyward Quest" players actually prioritize graphics over story, our "narrative-first" pitch will fail to convert them, leaving us with a niche too small to sustain revenue.
2.  **Underestimating Engine Costs:** If the licensing fee for the required physics engine exceeds our budget, the core mechanic (the "echo" system) may be degraded, rendering the unique value proposition void.
3.  **Over-relying on Churn Data:** Assuming the 45% churn in "Moth Lantern" is solely due to narrative lack is risky; if the churn is actually due to poor onboarding or bug stability, a new story-focused game will suffer the same fate.

**Pivot/Scrap Triggers:**
*   **Scrap:** If beta sign-ups remain under **2,500** after the June 2025 test, indicating the "narrative-first" hook does not resonate with the broader market.
*   **Pivot:** If the physics engine licensing fee forces a >20% increase in the projected burn rate, requiring us to shift positioning from "premium narrative experience" to a lower-cost, episodic release model to recover costs.

*Note: Validate the physics engine cost immediately before committing art resources. Ensure the "narrative" claim is backed by actual gameplay depth, not just marketing copy.*
