**Positioning Recommendation: The "Event-Activated Donor" Strategy**

*   **Target Segment:** Alumni who have attended at least one university event in the last 24 months (your "activated" segment), specifically excluding cold donors who have never engaged physically.
*   **Value Proposition:** Converts sporadic event attendance into consistent giving habits by automating personalized follow-ups within 48 hours of engagement, proven to lift donation frequency by 12% and increase pledged volume by 20% ($1.2M vs $1M baseline).
*   **Differentiation:** Unlike LegacyConnect and DonorBridge, which function as generic donor databases, the Kestrel Platform is an "engagement-to-revenue" engine that specifically targets the 2x higher conversion probability of event attendees, justifying the true fully-loaded cost of ~$13k/year (software + integration) through higher ROI per active user.
*   **Headline Message:** "Stop managing lists and start mobilizing attendees: The only platform engineered to turn event participation into predictable revenue."

**Primary Failure Modes**
1.  **Segmentation Saturation:** If the pool of "event-attended" alumni is too small to sustain growth, the platform hits a revenue ceiling quickly.
2.  **Integration Friction:** The hidden $3k integration cost is a one-time estimate; if ongoing CRM maintenance or data syncing requires manual labor, the operational cost will erode the 20% revenue gain.
3.  **Attribution Error:** The 12% lift may be due to the novelty of the pilot or specific high-value participants rather than the platform itself; if the effect doesn't scale to the full population, the ROI collapses.

**Stop/Change Course Trigger**
Dr. Graves should halt or pivot if the **Year 1 churn rate exceeds 15%** or if the **cost-per-acquired-donor (including the $13k total cost) exceeds the average gift size** of the targeted segment. Specifically, if the net new revenue (after subtracting the $13k cost) is less than $50k in the first full year, the business case fails.

**Hidden Risks & Arithmetic Flags**
*   **The "Total Cost" Blind Spot:** You noted the $3k integration cost but treated the $8k–$10k competitor pricing as apples-to-apples. Competitors may also have hidden integration fees; however, your *internal* math must reflect the $13k fully-loaded Year 1 cost, not the $10k sticker price, when calculating break-even.
*   **Sample Bias:** You interviewed 78 donors (45% response rate). This is a strong sample, but these are likely your *most engaged* alumni. The pilot results (12% lift) may not replicate across the broader, less engaged alumni base.
*   **The Churn Unknown:** You explicitly lack churn data. If the 312 pilot participants leave after year one because the "event follow-up" novelty wears off, your Lifetime Value (LTV) calculations are currently inflated. Assume a worst-case churn of 20% for your presentation to be conservative.
