**Positioning Brief: Greenfurrow Leasing**
**To:** Maya Patel, COO | **Date:** 15 Nov 2025 | **Context:** Board Meeting

**1. Concise Positioning Recommendation**
*   **Target Segment:** Mid-scale row-crop farmers (500–2,000 acres) in the Midwest facing seasonal cash-flow volatility and equipment obsolescence risks.
*   **Value Proposition:** "Preserve operating capital by accessing premium equipment through flexible, short-term leases (avg. 4.3 months) that align costs strictly with harvest revenue cycles, avoiding the debt burden of ownership."
*   **Differentiation:** Unlike **CropCycle Lease** (rigid 12-month terms) and **AgriFlex** (complex credit checks), Greenfurrow offers rapid, harvest-aligned leasing with a 68% win rate against the former due to superior term flexibility.
*   **Headline:** "Greenfurrow: Equipment Flexibility That Matches Your Harvest Cycle, Not Your Debt Schedule."

**2. Three Main Ways This Could Fail**
1.  **Regulatory Obsolescence:** The proposed Iowa cap limiting leases to 3 months (effective Jan 1, 2026) directly contradicts our core product metric (4.3-month average), rendering our current unit economics and value proposition illegal in a key market.
2.  **Misaligned Customer Priority:** If farmers prioritize long-term asset equity over short-term cash flow (contrary to our assumption), they will reject leasing in favor of traditional loans once interest rates stabilize, viewing our model as "renting money" without building equity.
3.  **Competitor Mimicry:** CropCycle Lease or AgriFlex could rapidly introduce "harvest-season" riders to match our 4.3-month flexibility, neutralizing our primary differentiation and forcing a price war we cannot sustain with only $7.5M ARR.

**3. Stop or Pivot Signal**
**Metric/Signal:** If >20% of active contracts in Iowa require restructuring to meet the new 3-month cap by Q1 2026, **STOP** current expansion and **PIVOT** immediately to a "micro-lease" operational model or shift focus to states without term caps. Alternatively, if win rates against CropCycle drop below 50% in two consecutive quarters despite unchanged pricing, the market no longer values flexibility over brand trust, requiring a strategic pivot to reliability or cost-leadership.
