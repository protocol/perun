**Positioning Recommendation (Hypothesis to Validate)**

*   **Target Segment:** Small landlords (1–20 units) currently managing leases via spreadsheets or manual routines, specifically those triggered by scaling past 10 units where ad-hoc methods break down.
*   **Value Proposition:** "Rowan Rentals automates the administrative burden of small-scale property management, reclaiming 5+ hours monthly so you can focus on portfolio growth rather than paperwork."
*   **Differentiation:** Unlike LandLordPro (built for large property managers) or generic spreadsheets, Rowan offers a flat-fee model ($14.99/mo) with no per-unit transaction fees, aligning costs with the specific cash-flow reality of the 1–20 unit owner.
*   **Headline:** "The flat-fee operating system for landlords outgrowing spreadsheets."

**Main Ways This Could Fail**
1.  **Price Sensitivity Mismatch:** The assumption that landlords prioritize time savings over subscription cost may be false; if the $14.99 flat fee exceeds their perceived value compared to free spreadsheets, adoption will stall regardless of time saved.
2.  **Regulatory Whiplash:** The pending July 2025 state law could invert our value prop. If it mandates digital receipts, it might boost demand, but if it imposes heavy compliance costs we haven't built for, we become a liability rather than a solution.
3.  **Engagement Gap:** A DAU of 2.4 suggests low stickiness. If landlords only open the app for monthly rent collection and ignore other features, they will churn once the initial novelty fades, proving the "time saved" claim is overstated.

**Stop or Pivot Signal**
Pivot or halt if the **pilot conversion rate drops below 15%** (currently ~72% in the small sample, but needs scale validation) OR if **DAU/MAU ratio remains below 0.15** after three months, indicating the product is a utility used once a month rather than an operating system. Specifically, if >40% of churned users cite "price" as the primary reason, the flat-fee hypothesis is invalid.
