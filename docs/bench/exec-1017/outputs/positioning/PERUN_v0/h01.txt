**Positioning Recommendation for CEO Maya Patel**

*   **Target Segment:** Existing Juniper members who have downloaded the app but not yet booked a class (the 38% inactive cohort) and high-frequency attendees seeking optimization.
*   **Value Proposition:** A hyper-personalized fitness companion that curates class recommendations based on individual performance history, prioritizing result optimization over generic scheduling.
*   **Differentiation:** Unlike PulseFit and CoreWave, which compete on broad content libraries and lower monthly fees, the Juniper App leverages exclusive in-studio data to deliver a bundled, high-touch experience that increases member retention rather than just digitizing access.
*   **Headline Message:** "Don't just book a class. Optimize your journey with the only app powered by your personal Juniper history."

**Three Biggest Risks to This Positioning**

1.  **Price Sensitivity Mismatch:** If the core market actually prioritizes a lower monthly fee over personalization (contradicting our primary assumption), positioning on "optimization" will fail to convert price-sensitive churners lost to PulseFit.
2.  **Integration Friction:** If the $12k–$18k integration cost per wearable platform delays or limits data syncing, the "hyper-personalized" claim becomes unverifiable, reducing the app to a basic scheduler where we lack differentiation.
3.  **Engagement Ceiling:** With 38% of pilot users never opening the app post-download, the value prop may be invisible to a significant portion of the base; if we cannot activate these users, the "personalization" engine lacks the data density to function correctly.

**Pivot/Stop Signal for Launch**

Maya should halt or pivot the launch if, within 60 days of full rollout, the **app-to-class conversion rate for previously inactive users remains below 15%** (matching the pilot's active booking baseline) OR if **churn rates among bundled users exceed those of the standalone in-studio package**, indicating the app adds friction rather than value.
