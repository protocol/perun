**Positioning Hypothesis for Maya Patel**

*For engineering leaders in FinTech, HealthTech, and SaaS who lose developer velocity to pipeline latency, Quickbuild is a CI accelerator that guarantees 30% faster build times; unlike TurboCI and BuildFast, it delivers this speed through auto-scaling, provided teams accept a 15-minute daily maintenance window.*

**Top Three Failure Modes**
1.  **Maintenance Overhead:** The required 15-minute daily window negates the time saved by faster builds, causing net-zero or negative ROI for the buyer.
2.  **Unverified Speed Claim:** The "30% faster" benchmark fails in real-world environments once the auto-scale maintenance is factored into total cycle time, invalidating the core value proposition.
3.  **Price-to-Value Mismatch:** At $45,000/seat, the cost exceeds the perceived value of latency reduction if churn exceeds 12% in the first six months, indicating the pain point was not urgent enough to justify the price.

**Pivot/Halt Triggers**
*   **Churn > 12%** within the first six months of deployment.
*   **Loss of > 5%** of the 150 target enterprises to BuildFast due to maintenance friction.
*   **Real-world benchmarks** showing < 15% net time savings after maintenance overhead.

**Immediate Falsification Test**
Run a "Maintenance Stress Test" with 10 friendly beta customers: deploy Quickbuild in their production environment for 14 days, explicitly measuring *net* developer time saved (build speed gain minus 15-min daily maintenance). If the net time saved is < 10% or if > 2 customers disable the auto-scale feature due to disruption, the positioning hypothesis is false and the launch should halt.
