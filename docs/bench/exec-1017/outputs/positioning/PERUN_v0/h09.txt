**Positioning Recommendation for Harbor Transit On-Demand Pilot**

*   **Target Segment:** Time-sensitive commuters in low-density zones who currently rely on infrequent fixed-route buses or expensive ride-hail services for "last-mile" connections.
*   **Value Proposition:** Reliable, door-to-door transit that guarantees arrival within 15 minutes at a predictable cost, eliminating the uncertainty of waiting for scheduled buses.
*   **Differentiation:** Unlike SkyShuttle (which competes on lowest price but lacks route integration) or MetroLink (which offers coverage but poor frequency), Harbor Transit provides seamless integration with the existing regional rail network, allowing single-ticket transfers.
*   **Headline:** "Your Connection, On Demand: Bridging the Last Mile Without the Wait or the Surge."

**Three Biggest Risks of Failure**
1.  **Unit Economics Collapse:** If the unaccounted driver overtime ($1.2M annually) and potential regulatory surcharges ($0.50/ride) push the effective cost per ride above $9.50, the service becomes more expensive than MetroLink without offering proportionally higher value, destroying the margin needed for scale.
2.  **Misidentified Purchase Driver:** Your assumption that price is the primary driver contradicts your own data (78% of interviews cited "convenience"). Positioning primarily on price against a cheaper competitor (SkyShuttle at $7) ignores your actual strength and invites a race to the bottom you cannot win given your cost structure.
3.  **Regulatory Shock:** If the regional surcharge is implemented at the high end of estimates, it erodes the price advantage over MetroLink entirely, making the "predictable cost" promise difficult to keep without subsidizing riders heavily.

**Stop or Pivot Triggers**
*   **Stop/Pivot Metric:** If the blended cost per ride (including overtime and surcharges) exceeds $9.00 for two consecutive months, immediately pivot from a "value/convenience" pitch to a "premium express" tier or halt expansion until subsidies are secured.
*   **Pivot Event:** If win/loss ratios drop below 1:1 when competing directly against SkyShuttle on price-sensitive routes, abandon price-based messaging entirely and reposition strictly as a premium reliability layer for rail commuters.

**Note:** Your current assumption that price is the primary driver conflicts with your interview data showing 78% prioritize convenience; leaning into price may weaken your position against SkyShuttle. The $1.2M overtime cost must be modeled into the per-ride unit economics before finalizing any pricing strategy.
