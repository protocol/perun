### Legal Distinctions: Data Hosting vs. Professional Valuation

In both the UK and EU (including France), the legal distinction between "data hosting" and "professional valuation" generally hinges on **who creates the professional opinion** and **who holds themselves out as the expert**.

1.  **Nature of the Service (Hosting vs. Advice):**
    *   **Data Hosting:** If RelicCloud merely provides the digital infrastructure (servers, database, image storage) where dealers upload *their own* appraisals, you are typically classified as a "data processor" or "hosting provider." Under the UK Contract Act and EU E-Commerce Directive (and subsequent Digital Services Act), hosts are generally not liable for the content stored by users unless they have actual knowledge of illegal content and fail to act. You are selling the *container*, not the *content*.
    *   **Professional Valuation:** Fiduciary duties or professional negligence claims usually arise when a party provides a professional *opinion* or *advice* upon which another relies for financial decision-making. If your software does not generate the valuation figures itself (via AI or algorithm) and explicitly states that the dealer is the sole author of the appraisal, you are likely not acting as a valuer.

2.  **Fiduciary Duty Threshold:**
    *   A fiduciary duty requires a relationship of extreme trust where one party acts solely in the interest of the other. Mere software provision rarely creates this. It usually requires a contractual assumption of responsibility or a specific regulatory license (common in financial services, less so for inventory logs).
    *   **Jurisdiction Nuance:** In France, the concept of *obligation de sécurité* (security obligation) in cloud contracts is strict regarding data availability, but it does not automatically extend to the *accuracy* of user-generated content unless the provider interferes with or validates that content.

3.  **The Role of Terms of Service (ToS):**
    *   Your exposure is heavily mitigated by clear ToS stating that RelicCloud is a storage platform, not a valuation authority. Clauses must explicitly disclaim liability for business interruption (missed sales) and data accuracy, limiting liability to service credits or refund of fees, rather than consequential damages (the value of the antique).
