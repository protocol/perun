### Structural Risks in Your Proposed Model

Your current plan to pay physicians a flat $2,500/month for "remote oversight" while retaining control over scheduling, pricing, and patient acquisition creates a high risk of violating CPOM doctrines, particularly in New York. Regulators and state boards of medicine look beyond contract language to the **economic substance** of the relationship. If the corporation controls the "business" aspects to the degree that it effectively dictates medical practice, the contract will be deemed a sham employment arrangement.

**1. The "Control" Trigger (New York & Florida)**
The primary trigger for regulatory scrutiny is not the payment itself, but who controls the **patient relationship** and **clinical workflow**.
*   **Scheduling & Pricing:** If HydrateGo sets the $150 price point and owns the scheduling platform, regulators may argue the physician is merely a figurehead. In strict CPOM jurisdictions, the professional entity (the doctor) must typically retain the right to set fees for medical services and accept or reject patients based on medical necessity, not corporate quotas.
*   **The Flat Fee Risk:** A fixed monthly fee for "oversight" regardless of patient volume can be interpreted as a **kickback** or a fee-splitting violation if it correlates implicitly with the volume of referrals generated by your marketing. It suggests the doctor is being paid to lend their license rather than to provide distinct, billable professional services.

**2. Specific Contractual Clauses That Trigger Scrutiny**
Avoid clauses that grant the franchise corporate control over:
*   **Medical Protocols:** The corporation cannot dictate specific IV formulations or dosages; this must remain under the physician's independent medical judgment.
*   **Hiring/Firing of Clinical Staff:** If the corporation hires the nurses who administer the IVs, the doctor's "oversight" becomes legally tenuous. In many CPOM states, the professional entity (the doctor) must employ or directly contract with the clinical staff administering treatment.
*   **Exclusivity without Autonomy:** Contracts that bind a doctor to your brand while stripping them of the authority to modify treatment plans for specific patients are red flags.

**3. Operational Separations Required**
To mitigate risk, the operational model often requires a **Management Services Organization (MSO)** structure:
*   **Two-Entity Structure:** The physician forms a separate Professional Entity (PC/PLLC) that employs/contracts the medical staff and owns the medical records. HydrateGo forms an MSO that provides *non-clinical* services (marketing, rent, admin software) to the PC for a fair-market-value management fee.
*   **Firewalls:** The MSO cannot direct clinical care. The physician must have final sign-off on all medical policies.
