**Critical Warning: Do Not Implement the Proposed Rebate Structure**

Your proposed model—charging $50/patient/month and offering a $5,000 quarterly cash rebate contingent on reducing readmissions by 15%—creates an extreme risk of violating the **Federal Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS)**.

Under the AKS, it is a criminal offense to knowingly offer, pay, solicit, or receive any remuneration (including cash rebates) to induce referrals of items or services reimbursable by federal healthcare programs (Medicare/Medicaid). Since remote patient monitoring for chronic diseases often involves Medicare/Medicaid patients, your rebate could be interpreted as paying hospitals to refer patients to your platform or to continue using it, rather than paying for the software's intrinsic value.

**Why Your Specific Model is Dangerous:**
1.  **Cash Remuneration:** Direct cash rebates tied to performance metrics are rarely protected. The government may view the $5,000 not as a discount, but as a "kickback" for achieving a specific outcome that benefits the hospital financially (via reduced penalties for readmissions).
2.  **Lack of Fair Market Value (FMV) Alignment:** If the rebate effectively lowers your price below FMV only when a specific referral-generating condition is met, it looks like an inducement.
3.  **No Applicable Safe Harbor:** There is **no specific safe harbor** for "performance-based cash rebates" for technology vendors. The "Discount Safe Harbor" generally requires discounts to be fixed and disclosed at the time of sale, not variable based on clinical outcomes. The "Warranty Safe Harbor" covers product replacement/repair, not clinical performance bonuses.

**Recommended Structural Alternatives:**
To incentivize hospitals without violating federal law, consider these compliant approaches instead of cash rebates:
*   **Fixed Volume Discounts:** Offer a pre-negotiated, fixed percentage discount based on total patient volume committed upfront (not clinical outcomes). This must be set in the contract before any referrals occur.
*   **Value-Based Pricing (Fee Adjustment):** Structure the base fee differently. Instead of a flat $50 + rebate, charge a lower base fee (e.g., $40) with a potential *fee increase* only if specific high-value milestones are met (though this also carries risk and requires strict FMV analysis).
*   **Service Credits, Not Cash:** If you must link to performance, consider offering non-cash service credits (e.g., additional training, extended support hours) rather than cash, though legal review is still mandatory.
*   **Outcome-Based Contracting via Legal Counsel:** Some advanced arrangements exist under waiver programs (like the CMS Innovation Center models), but these require specific federal waivers your hospital partners may not possess.

Before signing any agreement, you must engage a specialized healthcare attorney. Present them with this checklist:

1.  **AKS Risk Assessment:** "Does our proposed outcome-based rebate constitute 'remuneration' intended to induce referrals under the Anti-Kickback Statute given our patient mix (Medicare/Medicaid %)?"
2.  **Safe Harbor Analysis:** "Which specific AKS safe harbor (if any) can we structure this deal under? Does the 'Discount Safe Harbor' apply to variable rebates tied to clinical metrics?"
3.  **Fair Market Value (FMV):** "Can you perform or review an independent FMV analysis to prove our $50 price point (and any adjusted effective price) reflects the true market value of the software, independent of the rebate?"
4.  **Stark Law Implications:** "If the hospital physicians have any ownership interest or compensation relationship with us, does this trigger Stark Law violations regarding designated health services?"
5.  **State Law Variations:** "Florida has its own anti-kickback statutes. Do these state laws impose stricter prohibitions on our proposed model than federal law?"
6.  **Contract Language:** "How must we rewrite the contract to ensure the payment structure is viewed as a bona fide service fee adjustment rather than an inducement?"
7.  **Documentation Requirements:** "What specific contemporaneous documentation do we need to maintain to prove the commercial reasonableness of this arrangement if audited?"
