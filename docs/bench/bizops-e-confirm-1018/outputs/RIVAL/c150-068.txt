**Executive Summary: Your current model is likely compliant, but your positioning is risky.**

Charging a flat fee for software does not inherently violate legal ethical rules, provided the fee is clearly distinguished as a **technology cost** rather than a **legal fee**. Ethical billing rules (such as ABA Model Rules) govern how *lawyers* charge *clients* for legal services. They generally do not regulate how lawyers pay their vendors for tools, provided the total cost passed to the client is reasonable and properly disclosed.

**1. Technology Charge vs. Legal Fee**
Your $2,500 fee is a **technology charge**. You are selling a license to use case management software, not performing legal work.
*   **The Distinction:** Ethical rules require that *legal fees* be reasonable relative to the work performed. However, *vendor costs* (like LexisNexis subscriptions, court filing fees, or your software) are typically passed through as flat expenses or overhead.
*   **The Risk:** The bar association inquiry likely stems from confusion over whether your fee is being marketed as a "case processing fee" (implying you are doing the work) or a "software license fee" (implying you are providing the tool). If law firms bill their clients "$2,500 for VisaFlow to handle this case," it implies the software replaces lawyer hours, which triggers scrutiny on reasonableness. If they bill "$2,500 for Case Management Software License," it is a standard vendor expense.

**2. Do You Need Tiered Pricing Based on Complexity?**
**No.** Introducing tiered pricing based on "case difficulty" is dangerous and counterproductive for a SaaS vendor.
*   **Liability Trap:** If you charge more for "complex" cases, you are implicitly admitting that your software requires more effort or carries higher risk for those cases. This blurs the line between tool provider and service provider.
*   **Misaligned Value Metric:** Your value is not the *hours saved*; it is the *outcome enabled* (filing the case correctly). A simple case that fails due to poor software is worthless; a complex case that succeeds is invaluable. Pricing based on the lawyer's manual review time anchors your value to their inefficiency, not your solution.
*   **Compliance Solution:** You do not need to change your pricing structure to be compliant. You need to change your **contractual language and invoicing description**. Ensure your agreements explicitly state that fees are for "software access and licensing" regardless of case volume or complexity, and that VisaFlow performs no legal services.

**3. Strategic Recommendation**
Do not switch to hourly billing. It destroys SaaS economics and signals low confidence in your product. Instead:
*   **Clarify Positioning:** Rebrand the $2,500 strictly as an "Enterprise Case License."
*   **Educate Your Customers:** Provide your law firm clients with a "Billing Guidance Memo" explaining how to ethically pass this cost to their clients as a necessary technology expense, distinct from legal fees.
*   **Ignore the "Hours" Argument:** The variance in lawyer hours (2 vs. 40) is irrelevant to your license fee. Just as Microsoft charges the same for Word whether you write a 2-page letter or a 400-page brief, you charge for the capability, not the consumption of time.

**Conclusion:** Your flat fee is compliant as a technology charge. The bar association's concern is likely a misunderstanding of your product's nature. Fix the narrative, not the price.
