**Why This Cannot Be Self-Resolved**

1.  **Nexus is Physical, Not Product-Based:** Sales tax nexus is generally triggered by the physical presence of your employees (the installation crew) within a state, regardless of whether the county is "dry" or if the end product (alcohol) is restricted there. By sending crews to Kansas, Texas, and Utah, you have likely already established nexus in those states. The legality of the brewery's operation in a specific county does not automatically negate the state's right to tax the sale of tangible personal property (the tanks) or the installation labor delivered within its borders.
2.  **The "Dry County" Misconception:** State tax authorities and local dry laws operate on different legal tracks. A county banning alcohol production does not inherently grant a sales tax exemption on equipment manufacturing. In many jurisdictions, the equipment itself is taxable upon sale or installation, even if the buyer's intended use is later deemed non-compliant by local zoning. Relying on the "dry" status as a tax shield is a dangerous legal conflation.
3.  **Manufacturing Exemptions are Specific:** While many states offer exemptions for manufacturing equipment, these are rarely automatic. They typically require the *buyer* (the brewery) to provide you with a valid, state-specific resale or exemption certificate *before* the sale. If you did not collect these certificates, the state likely views the transaction as a taxable retail sale. Furthermore, installation labor is taxable in some states and exempt in others; this varies strictly by statute, not by general industry practice.
4.  **Audit Exposure:** With 30 installations across multiple states and plans to expand to ten more, you are flying under the radar only until a random audit or a competitor tip-off occurs. If audited, you will be liable for:
    *   Back taxes for all uncollected amounts.
    *   Penalties and interest (which can exceed the tax itself).
    *   The risk that the state demands you pay the tax out of your own margins since you failed to collect it from the customer.

**Next Steps**

Do not attempt to apply "standard exemptions" without legal counsel. Your immediate workflow should be:
1.  **Stop and Assess:** Do not start new installations in new states until a nexus study is complete.
2.  **Hire Expertise:** Retain a firm with specific experience in "brewery equipment taxation" and "multi-state nexus." General accountants often miss the nuances of installation labor vs. equipment sales in regulated industries.
3.  **Voluntary Disclosure:** If your advisor determines you have existing liability, discuss entering a Voluntary Disclosure Agreement (VDA) with the affected states. This can often waive penalties and limit the look-back period, protecting your cash reserves.

Your expansion plans depend on fixing this foundation. The cost of a professional assessment is negligible compared to the potential six-figure liability of an audit.
