### Unit Economics & Cash Gap Analysis

**Current Unit Economics (Per Device)**
*   **Revenue:** €8,000 (Fixed reimbursement)
*   **Variable Costs:** €1,800 (Current polymer) + Estimated €200 (other consumables) = €2,000
*   **Contribution Margin:** €6,000 per unit
*   **Monthly Contribution:** 50 units × €6,000 = €300,000
*   **Fixed Costs:** €60,000 (Staff)
*   **Net Monthly Cash Flow:** €240,000 (Positive)

**The Lag Impact Calculation**
Your crisis is not profitability, but **working capital timing**. You are paying today's inflated costs (€1,800) while waiting six months to be reimbursed at a rate based on older costs (likely ~€1,200).
*   **Cost Gap per Unit:** €1,800 (current) - €1,200 (reimbursement basis) = €600 loss *per unit* during the lag period.
*   **Monthly Cash Drain from Lag:** 50 units × €600 = **€30,000/month**.
*   **Runway:** With €350,000 reserves and a €30k monthly drag (assuming base operations break even otherwise), you have approximately **11–12 months** before reserves are depleted if costs stabilize now. If costs rise further, this accelerates.

**Strategic Recommendations**

1.  **Do Not Pause Production:** Halting production stops revenue entirely and risks patient outcomes, while fixed staff costs (€60k) continue. Your contribution margin (€6k/unit) is still strong enough to cover fixed costs and the lag penalty. Stopping would accelerate cash burn.

2.  **Immediate Action: Hybrid Pricing Strategy**
    Since insurance rates are fixed, you must isolate the variable cost risk.
    *   **Self-Pay Surcharge:** Introduce a "Material Volatility Fee" for self-pay patients or those with flexible policies. Calculate this as the difference between current material cost and the reimbursed baseline.
    *   **Insurance Negotiation:** Do not ask for a general price increase yet. Request an **emergency interim rate adjustment** specifically citing the >50% raw material spike. Provide invoices proving the €1,200 → €1,800 jump. Many insurers have clauses for "extraordinary cost events."

3.  **Financial Modeling Adjustment**
    Restructure your model to track **Cash Flow** separate from **P&L**.
    *   Create a "Lag Reserve" line item. Every month, calculate `(Current Material Cost - Reimbursed Material Cost) × Volume`. Treat this number as an immediate cash liability, even if the P&L shows profit.
    *   Model three scenarios: Costs stabilize at €1,800, rise to €2,200, or drop. Determine the break-even volume for each if reimbursement does not change.

4.  **Hedging vs. Inventory**
    Financial hedging (futures) is likely too complex for polymer volumes of this size. Instead, use **strategic inventory**: If you have cash reserves, buy 3–6 months of polymer *now* at €1,800 if you fear it rising to €2,500. This locks your cost basis. Do not do this if you believe prices will peak and drop.

**Conclusion**
Your business is fundamentally profitable but liquidity-constrained by the reimbursement lag. Prioritize negotiating an interim rate with insurers and implementing a surcharge for non-fixed contracts. Use your €350k reserve strictly to bridge the timing gap, not to subsidize permanent cost increases.

*Note: Consult a German healthcare legal expert regarding the legality of surcharging insured patients before implementation.*
*Monitor material markets weekly; if prices drop, adjust your inventory strategy immediately.*
