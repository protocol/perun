Instead, flag the specific file and notify the uploading user that the document contains sensitive PII (Personally Identifiable Information) requiring attention before abstraction can proceed.

**2. Legal Compliance Strategy**
You are likely subject to the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA), New York SHIELD Act, and potentially CCPA/CPRA depending on tenant residency. As a processor of financial data, you have a duty of care.
*   **Automatic Redaction is Mandatory:** You must implement automatic redaction of SSNs immediately upon ingestion, before any human review or long-term storage. Relying on explicit consent from *tenants* is operationally impossible; your contractual relationship is with the property management firms, not the tenants. Your clients (the firms) are responsible for obtaining tenant consent for data processing; your responsibility is securing the data they provide.
*   **Reject vs. Redact:** Do not reject files outright. This breaks your clients' workflows. Instead, ingest the file, instantly redact the SSN pattern (e.g., `XXX-XX-XXXX`), process the remaining lease terms, and return the abstracted data with a security log noting that PII was redacted. This maintains your value proposition (speed/accuracy) while mitigating liability.

**3. Contractual & Policy Updates**
*   **Update Terms of Service:** Explicitly state that LeaseLens automatically redacts SSNs and that clients warrant they have the legal right to upload the documents. Shift the liability for unauthorized uploads to the client firm.
*   **Data Processing Agreement (DPA):** Ensure your B2B contracts include a robust DPA compliant with NY SHIELD Act requirements, specifying encryption standards and breach notification timelines.

**4. Technical Implementation**
*   Deploy a regex-based or NLP-based pre-scan layer in your ingestion pipeline to detect SSN patterns.
*   Ensure that even redacted data is encrypted at rest and in transit.
*   Log all redaction events for audit trails without storing the original unredacted SSN.

Proactively inform your 50 clients about this new security layer. Frame it as a "Compliance Upgrade" that protects *them* from liability, rather than a restriction. This turns a potential friction point into a trust-building feature, reinforcing your platform's enterprise readiness.
