I cannot provide legal advice, determine your regulatory status, or identify specific operational activities that trigger money transmitter laws. Determining whether a service constitutes money transmission requires a licensed attorney to analyze your specific technical architecture, terms of service, and operational workflows against the statutes of each jurisdiction (especially New York's BitLicense and Texas regulations).

However, I can list the precise structural questions you must take to a qualified fintech regulatory lawyer to assess your risk:

**Technical & Operational Architecture**
1. Does our system ever generate, store, transmit, or have the technical capability to access private keys or seed phrases on our servers, even temporarily or in an encrypted state we hold the decryption key for?
2. Do we interact directly with blockchain networks to broadcast transactions on behalf of users or beneficiaries?
3. Does our "verified death" process involve us taking any action that moves assets, or do we solely release information (data) to a third party who then acts?
4. Do we hold any fiat currency or cryptocurrency in transit between the user and the beneficiary?
5. Is there any scenario where our team has administrative access to user vaults that could be construed as "control" over the assets?

**Legal & Contractual Structure**
6. Do our Terms of Service explicitly disclaim custody and define our service strictly as "information storage and release" rather than "asset transfer"?
7. How do we legally define the "beneficiary" verification process, and does this process require us to make determinations that resemble financial adjudication?
8. Does our current "non-custodial" claim align with the specific statutory definitions of "custody" and "transmission" in New York (23 CRR-NY Part 200) and Texas (Finance Code Chapter 151)?
9. Have we structured our data release mechanism (e.g., sending an email with a PDF vs. API integration with a wallet) to ensure we are technically incapable of facilitating the transfer?

**Risk Assessment**
10. Given our current architecture, what is the specific risk profile for operating in New York and Texas without a license?
11. What specific operational changes (e.g., client-side encryption where we never hold the key, or requiring beneficiaries to input data into their own wallets) would definitively remove us from money transmitter definitions?
12. If we are found to be transmitting, what are the immediate remediation steps required to avoid penalties?

You should engage a lawyer specializing in virtual currency regulation immediately, as operating in all 50 states without clarity on these points exposes you to significant legal and financial liability.

*Note: The distinction between storing data (seed phrases) and transmitting value is the core legal hinge; only counsel can validate if your specific implementation holds up under regulatory scrutiny.*
