**Current Reimbursement Reality for Standalone VR Therapy**

Based on current Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) guidelines and major commercial payer policies in Texas, **you cannot currently bill standard psychotherapy codes (such as CPT 90832–90838) for sessions where no licensed human therapist is physically or virtually co-located with the patient in real-time.**

Standard reimbursement codes for exposure therapy and psychotherapy are predicated on "therapeutic interaction" between a qualified provider and a patient. Your current model—where software runs standalone with only asynchronous or passive remote monitoring—does not meet the definition of "face-to-face" or "interactive" services required by codes like 90834 (45-minute psychotherapy) or 90837 (60-minute psychotherapy). Claims submitted under these codes without documented, real-time therapeutic intervention by a licensed provider will almost certainly be denied as "not medically necessary" or "service not rendered."

**The Critical Distinction: Tool vs. Provider**
Payers view VR currently as a *tool* used by a therapist, not a *provider* itself. Even in telehealth, reimbursement requires the licensed clinician to be actively engaged via audio/video during the session. Passive monitoring (watching a feed without intervening) generally does not qualify as billable time under current CPT definitions.

**Operational Adjustments Required for Reimbursement**
To access the tripled revenue potential via insurance, you must alter your operational model to ensure a licensed provider is billably involved. You have three viable paths:

1.  **Synchronous Telehealth Model (Most Viable):** The licensed supervisor must transition from "monitoring" to active, real-time telehealth participation. They must be on a live audio/video call with the patient throughout the VR session, guiding the exposure, processing trauma responses, and intervening if distress becomes unmanageable. This allows you to bill standard psychotherapy codes with a telehealth modifier (typically **Modifier 95**). The VR session becomes the "medium" of therapy, but the billable service is the therapist's time and clinical decision-making.
2.  **Incident-To Billing (Clinic Setting):** If you establish physical clinics where a licensed therapist is physically present in the office suite (though not necessarily in the VR room), you may be able to bill under the supervising physician's NPI using "incident-to" rules. However, this requires strict adherence to supervision ratios and immediate availability, which may negate your scalability advantages.
3.  **Remote Therapeutic Monitoring (RTM) Codes:** Investigate CPT codes 98975–98977. These cover non-physician monitoring of patient data. However, these codes typically require "interactive communication" and are often capped at specific monthly amounts, likely yielding significantly less revenue per session than standard psychotherapy codes. They are also subject to evolving interpretations regarding what constitutes valid "monitoring" data.

**Specific Code/Modifier Guidance**
There is **no specific modifier or code set** currently approved by CMS or major Texas commercial payers (such as Blue Cross Blue Shield of Texas or UnitedHealthcare) that reimburses for "software-only" or "AI-driven" therapy sessions without real-time human clinical involvement. Attempts to use unlisted codes (e.g., 90899) for this purpose face high administrative burdens and frequent denials without a established precedent.

**Immediate Recommendation**
Do not submit claims for your current standalone model under standard therapy codes; this creates compliance risk and potential audit liabilities for fraud. To proceed with insurance billing:
*   Restructure sessions to include mandatory, real-time audio/video connection with a licensed therapist.
*   Document the therapist's active clinical interventions, not just the patient's VR usage.
*   Bill standard psychotherapy codes (90832–90838) with Modifier 95 (Telehealth).

Your software remains the delivery mechanism, but the *billable entity* must remain the licensed human provider delivering care through that medium.
