GreenMow's insurance cost divergence stems from how underwriters classify risk exposure based on location. When an incident occurs on **private property**, it is typically covered under General Liability (GL) policies. Underwriters price this based on premises liability factors: the frequency of human presence in the yard, the robot's speed limits within fenced areas, and emergency stop reliability. The risk is contained, predictable, and limited to the customer's domain.

Conversely, accidents on **public sidewalks** shift the classification toward Commercial Auto or specialized mobility policies. Underwriters price this significantly higher due to three specific factors:
1.  **Uncontrolled Environment:** Sidewalks introduce unpredictable variables (pedestrians, cyclists, pets, debris) that do not exist in private yards.
2.  **Severity Potential:** Collisions on hard concrete surfaces at even low speeds carry higher injury liability than grass collisions.
3.  **Regulatory Precedent:** Public right-of-way incidents often trigger stricter regulatory scrutiny and higher legal defense costs.

To calculate the specific cost impact, underwriters likely apply a "exposure multiplier" to your premium based on the percentage of route distance traveled on public sidewalks. If 20% of your robot's travel time is on sidewalks, they may apply a 3x–5x cost multiplier to that portion of the risk compared to private property time.

Regarding your routing software: **Yes, you should alter your routing to avoid public rights-of-way entirely if technically feasible.**

Eliminating sidewalk travel provides immediate financial and operational benefits:
*   **Premium Reduction:** It allows you to argue for a pure General Liability classification, potentially dropping the $8,000/month commercial auto surcharge.
*   **Underwriting Leverage:** You can present data proving 0% public exposure, forcing the carrier to re-rate your policy based solely on contained premises risk.
*   **Liability Cap:** It removes the "uncontrolled environment" variable, making your risk profile resemble a static appliance rather than a vehicle.

If avoiding sidewalks is impossible due to lot topology, you must implement "high-caution zones" in your software that reduce speed to near-zero on public strips and log these events separately. This data can be used to negotiate a lower multiplier by proving enhanced safety protocols, though it will not eliminate the commercial auto classification entirely.
