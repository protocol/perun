**Critical Warning: Do not roll out the 10% success-based fee model without immediate legal consultation.**

Charging a contingency fee (a percentage of the grant award) for grant writing services is widely considered a violation of ethical standards and frequently breaches foundation guidelines. While your math shows higher potential revenue ($5,000 vs. $500), this model poses severe risks to your non-profit clients.

**Ethical Standards and Professional Codes**
The Association of Fundraising Professionals (AFP) and the Grant Professionals Association (GPA) explicitly prohibit compensation based on a percentage of charitable contributions. The AFP Code of Ethical Principles states that members shall not accept compensation based on a percentage of charitable contributions. If your clients adhere to these codes (which most reputable non-profits do), hiring you under this model could force them to terminate their relationship with you or face censure from their own boards and peers.

**Foundation Guidelines and Administrative Costs**
You correctly identified the 15% administrative cap. However, the issue is deeper than just hitting a ceiling:
1.  **Prohibited Use of Funds:** Most private foundations and federal grant programs explicitly forbid using grant funds to pay for fundraising activities, including grant writing fees, especially on a contingency basis. They view this as "private inurement" or using charitable assets to generate private profit based on uncertain outcomes.
2.  **Disqualification Risk:** If a non-profit pays your 10% fee directly from the grant award, they may be flagged for audit, forced to return the funds used for your payment, or blacklisted from future funding from that foundation. Many applications require a budget narrative that discloses consultant fees; a line item described as "10% of award" is an immediate red flag that often leads to automatic rejection.
3.  **Misaligned Incentives:** Foundations dislike contingency fees because they encourage applicants to seek larger grants regardless of fit or need, rather than focusing on programmatic impact.

**The "Direct Payment" Misconception**
Paying you directly from the grant award does not solve the problem; it confirms it. Whether the money comes from the general fund or the grant check, if the expense is categorized as a fundraising cost tied to a percentage of the award, it violates the spirit and often the letter of donor restrictions.

**Recommendation**
1.  **Halt the Rollout:** Do not offer this tier to your 40 active users. The risk of them losing future funding outweighs the potential upside for GrantAI.
2.  **Consult a Specialist:** You must consult a non-profit law specialist before proceeding. They can review specific foundation guidelines in your target sectors, but expect them to advise against percentage-based models entirely.
3.  **Alternative Models:** Consider a hybrid model if you need to align with success, such as a lower base retainer plus a fixed bonus upon award notification (not a percentage). However, even fixed bonuses must be carefully vetted against specific foundation rules regarding fundraising costs.
