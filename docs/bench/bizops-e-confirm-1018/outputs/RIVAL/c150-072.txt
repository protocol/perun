**Typical Vendor Liability Standards in Tax Software**
In the tax software industry, vendors generally structure agreements to limit liability significantly. Standard practices often include:
*   **Disclaimer of Warranty:** Explicitly stating the software is provided "as is" and that the vendor does not guarantee absolute accuracy, especially regarding rapidly changing legislation.
*   **Limitation of Liability:** Capping damages at a specific amount, often limited to the fees paid by the customer in the 12 months preceding the claim, or a fixed dollar amount.
*   **Exclusion of Consequential Damages:** Explicitly excluding liability for indirect losses, such as penalties, interest, lost profits, or business interruption resulting from software errors.
*   **Customer Responsibility Clause:** Reinforcing that the user (the tax preparer) is the licensed professional responsible for the final review and submission of all returns, regardless of software suggestions.

**Contract Structure and Sales Representations**
While your Terms of Service (ToS) may contain protective clauses, their enforceability can be challenged if:
*   **Contradictory Representations:** Your sales team's emphasis on "accuracy" during demos could be argued as an express warranty that overrides general disclaimers in the ToS, particularly if specific promises were made about handling legislative updates.
*   **Negligence Claims:** If the delay in updating the algorithm is deemed grossly negligent rather than a simple error, some liability caps may not hold up in court, depending on state law.
*   **Conspicuousness:** Liability waivers must often be conspicuous and clearly agreed to; buried clauses may be scrutinized.
