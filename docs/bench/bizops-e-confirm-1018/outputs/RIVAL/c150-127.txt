There is no single "standard" liability cap universally applied to autonomous vehicle (AV) pilots with municipal airport authorities, as these agreements are highly bespoke and depend on the specific risk profile of the technology, the jurisdiction, and the negotiating leverage of each party. However, based on typical structures in emerging technology pilots and transportation contracts, here is the strategic landscape and recommended approach for your negotiation with Denver International Airport (DEN).

**Typical Financial Exposure Ranges**
In AV pilot programs, it is rare for a startup to accept uncapped liability. Most municipal authorities understand that requiring a startup to assume unlimited risk is a deal-breaker that stifles innovation. Typical liability caps in these scenarios often align with the operator's available insurance coverage plus a reasonable self-insured retention.
*   **Common Cap Range:** Contracts frequently cap liability at the amount of the operator's primary insurance policy (in your case, $10M) or slightly above, often ranging between **$10 million and $20 million**.
*   **The "Insurance-Backed" Standard:** A very common compromise is to cap liability at "the amount of insurance proceeds actually recoverable." This protects the airport by ensuring you carry robust insurance, while protecting you from bankruptcy due to claims exceeding your coverage limits.
*   **Catastrophic Carve-outs:** Authorities may push for higher caps or uncapped liability only for specific "gross negligence," "willful misconduct," or failures to maintain the vehicle according to agreed standards. They rarely succeed in making these uncapped for general operational accidents in a pilot phase.

**Structuring the Indemnity for Deal Closure**
Given your financial constraints ($400k reserves, $150k cost for excess insurance) and the strategic importance of this pilot (20% of projected revenue), you should propose a structured compromise rather than a flat rejection of their terms.

1.  **Propose an "Insurance-Limited" Cap:** Draft language stating that ShuttleGo's total aggregate liability for any incident shall not exceed the limits of its commercial general liability and auto liability insurance policies then in force (currently $10M). This directly ties your risk to your insurable capacity.
2.  **Offer a Tiered Approach:** Propose a cap of **$10 million** for general incidents, with a mutual agreement to review and potentially increase this cap to **$15–20 million** once specific performance milestones are met (e.g., 1,000 successful trips without incident). This shows good faith without immediate financial exposure.
3.  **Define "Catastrophic Failure" Narrowly:** If DEN insists on higher exposure for catastrophic events, negotiate a strict definition. Limit this to scenarios involving proven gross negligence or failure to adhere to specific, documented safety protocols, rather than any accident involving a full shuttle.
4.  **Mutual Indemnity:** Ensure the indemnity is mutual. The airport should indemnify ShuttleGo for liabilities arising from airport infrastructure failures, control tower errors, or negligence by airport staff, which balances the risk profile.
5.  **Alternative Security:** Instead of uncapped liability, offer to name the airport as an "additional insured" on your policy and provide a certificate of insurance. This gives them direct recourse against your insurer up to the policy limit, which is often their primary concern.

**Recommendation for Immediate Action**
Do not agree to uncapped liability; it poses an existential threat to your company. Present the $10M insurance-backed cap as the industry-standard starting point for pilots. Emphasize that forcing a cap beyond your insurable limits requires purchasing excess insurance that makes the pilot economically unviable, potentially causing the project to stall. Frame the $10M cap not as a limitation of responsibility, but as a reflection of the responsible, insured operational envelope of a pilot program. If they remain rigid, propose a joint working session between your legal teams to explore a "step-up" clause tied to future funding rounds or proven safety metrics, kicking the can on higher exposure down the road until you have the revenue to support it.
