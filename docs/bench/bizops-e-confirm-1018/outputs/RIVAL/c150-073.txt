### Fee Structure Recommendation for DepoTech

**Immediate Compliance Assessment**
You are likely operating in a regulatory gray area that poses a significant risk. In New York, if the regulation mandates a maximum rate of $4.25 per page for "certified transcripts," regulators may interpret this as the total price the client pays for that specific deliverable. If your platform adds a $25 connectivity fee and a 15% commission on top of the reporter's $4.25 charge, the law firm pays more than the statutory cap for the transcript. Even if you argue the fee is for "connectivity" and not the transcript itself, a regulator could view this as an end-run around the rate cap, violating the spirit and potentially the letter of the law. In Florida, while no cap exists, market expectations ($3.50) mean adding significant fees could make your platform uncompetitive.

**Recommended Fee Structure: The "Reporter-Net" Model**
To ensure compliance and maintain margins, you must shift from a **fee-on-top** model to a **commission-included** model.

1.  **New York Strategy (Compliance-First):**
    *   **Mechanism:** The $4.25 cap becomes the *total* billable rate to the law firm for the transcript.
    *   **Calculation:** Your 15% commission must be deducted from the $4.25, not added to it.
    *   **Reporter Earnings:** $4.25 × (1 - 0.15) = **$3.61 per page**.
    *   **Connectivity Fee:** Eliminate the separate $25 fee for New York transactions or bundle it into a volume discount that keeps the effective per-page cost under $4.25. A separate line item fee risks being viewed as part of the transcript cost.
    *   **Result:** The law firm pays exactly $4.25. You remain compliant. The reporter earns less than the cap, which is legally permissible (caps are ceilings, not floors), but you must ensure $3.61 is acceptable to your freelance network.

2.  **Florida Strategy (Market-First):**
    *   **Mechanism:** Align with the $3.50 market expectation as the total price.
    *   **Calculation:** Deduct your 15% commission from the $3.50 market rate.
    *   **Reporter Earnings:** $3.50 × (1 - 0.15) = **$2.98 per page**.
    *   **Connectivity Fee:** You may retain the $25 fee in Florida *only if* it is clearly positioned as a platform technology fee unrelated to the per-page transcript cost. However, to remain competitive, absorbing this or waiving it for high-volume firms is advisable.

3.  **Implementation Plan:**
    *   **Dynamic Pricing Engine:** Configure your platform to automatically detect the jurisdiction.
    *   **NY Mode:** Hard cap total client price at $4.25. Commission is internal. No extra line items for transcript delivery.
    *   **FL Mode:** Default total client price to $3.50. Optional connectivity fee allowed if disclosed separately as a tech service, not a transcript fee.
    *   **Reporter Communication:** Be transparent that in regulated markets, their "gross" rate is the cap, and their "net" rate is post-commission.

**Required Legal Counsel**
Before your next funding round, you must engage counsel to validate this structure. Specifically, you need:
1.  **New York Administrative Law Specialist:** Someone with specific experience in NYS Court Reporter licensing and the interpretation of "maximum rates" under NY Judiciary Law or relevant court rules. They must provide a written opinion on whether "total cost to client" includes platform fees.
2.  **Regulatory Compliance Attorney (Multi-State):** To review your Terms of Service and fee disclosures, ensuring your "connectivity fee" cannot be legally recharacterized as a transcript fee in either jurisdiction.
3.  **Due Diligence Prep:** Investors will flag the regulatory risk. You need a legal memo stating your model is compliant to clear this hurdle.

Do not proceed with the current "fee-on-top" model in New York without this validation; the risk of fines or de-platforming is high.
