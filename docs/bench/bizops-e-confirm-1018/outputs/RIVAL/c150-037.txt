**Critical Warning: Do Not Proceed with In-House Financing Yet**

Offering direct financing to consumers across state lines without legal clearance poses an existential risk to TinyMod. If you originate loans in states where you are not licensed, you face severe penalties, including cease-and-desist orders, fines, criminal charges, and the potential voiding of all loan contracts (forcing immediate full refunds to borrowers). With only $300,000 in operating cash, a multi-state enforcement action could bankrupt the company immediately.

**Regulatory Reality Check**
In the United States, originating consumer loans generally triggers a requirement to obtain a state-specific consumer lending license in **every state** where the borrower resides. There is no single "national" license for private consumer lending.
*   **The Threshold:** The trigger is typically the act of *offering* or *originating* the loan, not the volume of loans. Even one unlicensed loan in a strict state can trigger enforcement.
*   **The "Manufacturer Exemption" Myth:** While some states have narrow exemptions for sellers financing their own goods (often called "vendor financing" or "retail installment seller" licenses), these rules vary wildly.
    *   Some states exempt you only if the loan term is short (e.g., under 12 months), which conflicts with your 10-year plan.
    *   Some states exempt you only if the interest rate is below a specific usury limit, which may conflict with your 6% target depending on the state's definition of usury for unlicensed lenders.
    *   Many states explicitly **exclude** mobile homes, RVs, or structures on wheels from retail seller exemptions, categorizing them strictly as real estate or personal property loans requiring full lending licensure.
*   **The Cost:** Your estimate of $50,000 per state is realistic when factoring in application fees, surety bonds (often required per state), and legal setup. Doing this in 12 states would likely exceed $600,000 upfront, far beyond your $300,000 cash reserve, excluding the ongoing compliance costs.

**Required Legal Experts**
You must engage the following specific legal counsel before accepting a single financed payment:
1.  **A National Consumer Finance Regulatory Attorney:** Specifically one who practices in "multi-state lending compliance." Do not use a general corporate lawyer. You need a firm that maintains a matrix of state-by-state licensing requirements for retail installment sales and consumer loans.
2.  **A Surety Bond Broker Specializing in Financial Services:** If licensure is required, every state will mandate a surety bond (often $25k–$100k per state) to protect consumers. You need to know if you can even qualify for these bonds given your current balance sheet.

**Strategic Recommendation**
Given your cash constraints and the regulatory minefield, **do not build an in-house lending arm.** Instead, pursue one of these two paths:
*   **Partner with an Existing Lender:** Integrate with a lender already licensed nationwide that specializes in manufactured housing or RVs (e.g., LightStream, GoodSam, or specialized credit unions). They take the regulatory burden; you get a "financing available" badge on your site. You may pay a referral fee or slightly lower margin, but you avoid millions in liability.
*   **Third-Party Loan Origination Platform:** Use a fintech platform (like Sheffield Financial or similar manufactured-housing specialists) that handles the compliance and underwriting as a service.
