**Immediate Risk Assessment & Action Plan**

Your current controls (encryption at rest and limited access) are **necessary but insufficient** for processing Social Security Numbers (SSNs) in an AI workflow. The critical vulnerability is not storage, but **processing**. If your AI model ingests raw documents containing SSNs, those numbers likely exist in memory, logs, or vector embeddings in unmasked formats during computation. This creates a high-liability exposure.

**1. Specific Data Privacy Obligations**
While there is no single "federal real estate privacy law," you are subject to a patchwork of obligations triggered by the data type (SSN) and location (NYC):
*   **NY SHIELD Act (Stop Hacks and Improve Electronic Data Security):** Requires "reasonable safeguards" for private information (including SSNs) of NY residents. Courts often interpret "reasonable" to include data minimization and masking. If you can mask the data before AI processing and don't, you may be found negligent.
*   **GLBA (Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act):** If your commercial leases involve financial institutions as tenants or lenders, GLBA safeguards rules may apply to how you handle non-public personal information.
*   **Contractual Liability:** Your enterprise contract likely has indemnification clauses. A breach involving SSNs usually triggers mandatory notification laws (NY General Business Law § 899-aa), requiring you to notify all affected individuals and the state attorney general within specific timeframes. Failure to notify carries statutory fines.

**2. Potential Liabilities in a Breach**
If a breach occurs involving unmasked SSNs:
*   **Regulatory Fines:** NY Attorney General penalties can reach $20,000 per instance of violation under SHIELD if deemed unreasonable.
*   **Class Action Litigation:** SSN exposure is a primary driver for class-action lawsuits. Plaintiffs need only prove the failure to implement reasonable security (e.g., lack of masking), not actual identity theft, to secure settlements.
*   **Contractual Damages:** You face immediate termination, clawback of fees, and liability for your client's costs (forensic audits, credit monitoring for tenants, legal fees).
*   **Reputational Collapse:** In the NYC commercial real estate market, trust is the primary currency. A single publicized SSN leak involving tenant data will likely make you uninsurable and unhireable by major property managers.

**3. Critical Regulatory Questions for Your Privacy Attorney**
Do not renew contracts until you have written counsel on these specific points:
*   **Data Minimization Scope:** Does "reasonable safeguard" under NY SHIELD legally mandate redaction/masking of SSNs *before* ingestion by a third-party AI model or LLM, even if the model is self-hosted?
*   **Retention & Logging:** What are the mandatory retention periods for audit logs containing processed lease data, and do our current vector database retention policies violate data minimization principles?
*   **Sub-processor Liability:** If we use any underlying model infrastructure (even open weights hosted on cloud GPUs), does that constitute a "third-party disclosure" requiring explicit tenant consent under our clients' leases?
*   **Breach Notification Triggers:** Does the extraction of an SSN into a vector embedding constitute "acquisition" of data under NY law if the raw file is never exfiltrated but the embedding is compromised?
*   **Contractual Indemnity Caps:** Can we legally cap liability for data breaches involving statutory violations (like SHIELD), or are such caps unenforceable in NY commercial contracts?

**Immediate Technical Recommendation:** Implement a pre-processing step to detect and mask/redact SSNs (using regex or NER) *before* the document is passed to the AI abstraction engine.
