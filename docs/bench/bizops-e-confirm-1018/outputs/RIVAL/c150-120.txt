The landscape you are navigating involves two primary US regulatory frameworks: the International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) and the Export Administration Regulations (EAR).

**ITAR (Department of State)** controls defense articles and services listed on the United States Munitions List (USML). Nuclear defense articles, including certain data related to nuclear weapons, special nuclear material, or specific defense-related nuclear facilities, often fall under Category XVI of the USML. If your digital twin software or the simulation data it generates is specifically designed, modified, or configured for a defense application, or if it reveals classified information, it is likely subject to ITAR. ITAR generally prohibits the export of technical data to foreign persons (including foreign-owned entities within the US) without a license or exemption. "Export" under ITAR includes not just shipping physical items abroad, but also transmitting data electronically to foreign nationals or allowing foreign nationals to access it, even if the servers remain in the US.

**EAR (Department of Commerce)** controls dual-use items (commercial items with potential military applications) listed on the Commerce Control List (CCL). Nuclear-related items often fall under Category 0 (Nuclear Materials, Facilities, and Equipment). Even if your software is commercial, the specific precision of your neutron flux and cooling system stress models could trigger controls if they exceed certain performance thresholds or are destined for specific end-uses or end-users. The EAR applies to re-exports and transfers within the US to foreign nationals ("deemed exports").
