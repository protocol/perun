### Strategic Framework for AKS Compliance

To structure your B2B contract safely while maintaining competitiveness, you must align with established **Safe Harbors** under the Anti-Kickback Statute. The hospital's suggestion of a "Fair Market Value (FMV) lease" is a valid path, but so is a properly structured service agreement.

#### 1. The Personal Services and Management Contracts Safe Harbor
This is the most common safe harbor for SaaS and monitoring services. To qualify, your contract must meet these strict criteria:
*   **Written Agreement:** A signed contract specifying the services covered.
*   **Term:** The agreement must last for at least **one year**.
*   **Aggregate Compensation:** The total compensation must be set in advance, consistent with **Fair Market Value (FMV)**, and **not determined in a manner that takes into account the volume or value of any referrals** or other business generated between the parties.
*   **Commercial Reasonableness:** The services must be necessary and commercially reasonable, even if no referrals were made.

**Actionable Strategy:** Instead of a "volume discount" (which looks like paying for referrals), structure your pricing as a **tiered FMV schedule** based on objective operational metrics (e.g., data storage volume, number of API calls, or patient count bands) that are documented by a third-party FMV valuation. The price per unit may decrease at higher tiers due to economies of scale, but this must be justified by reduced cost-to-serve, not the promise of referrals.

#### 2. The Equipment Rental Safe Harbor (If pursuing the Lease Model)
If you proceed with the hospital's suggestion to structure this as a lease:
*   **Written Lease:** Must specify the premises or equipment covered.
*   **Term:** At least **one year**.
*   **Rent:** Must be set in advance, consistent with FMV, and **not determined based on the volume or value of referrals**.
*   **Exclusive Use:** The equipment must be for the exclusive use of the lessee during the lease term.

**Critical Warning:** A pure "lease" model may be difficult for a data analytics dashboard. If you are providing ongoing software updates, data analysis, and monitoring services, regulators may view this as a service contract regardless of how it is labeled. Mislabeling a service as a lease to avoid scrutiny can be seen as bad faith.

#### 3. Prohibited Structures to Avoid
*   **Per-Referral Fees:** Never tie compensation to the number of patients referred or the value of services billed by the hospital.
*   **Percentage of Revenue:** Do not charge a percentage of the hospital's reimbursement from Medicare/Medicaid.
*   **Free Implementation as "Inducement":** While you can absorb costs, explicitly offering "free" services in exchange for a contract can be scrutinized. Instead, **bundle** implementation into the total annual fee. The total contract value must still reflect FMV for the *entire* package.

#### Recommended Contractual Language Approach
1.  **FMV Certification:** Include a clause stating that compensation has been determined to be consistent with FMV, ideally referencing an independent valuation report.
2.  **No Referral Clause:** Explicitly state that the agreement is not contingent on, nor does it consider, the volume or value of referrals.
3.  **Commercial Reasonableness Statement:** Document why the hospital needs these specific services regardless of referral relationships.
4.  **Bundled Pricing:** Define the "Service Fee" as inclusive of implementation, training, and software access, avoiding line items that look like free gifts.
