### Monthly Cash Flow & Runway Scenarios for Northwind Cutlery

**Current Baseline (Pre-Increase):**
*   **Revenue:** $6,000 (50,000 units × $0.12)
*   **Cost:** $5,400 (50,000 units × $0.108 adjusted cost*)
*   **Gross Profit:** $600
*   **Net Cash Flow:** -$24,400 ($600 profit - $25,000 burn)
*   **Runway:** ~4.9 months

*\*Note: Your prompt states a 20% cost increase on the $0.09 base ($0.09 × 1.20 = $0.108). Your current margin is already negative based on these figures ($0.12 price - $0.108 cost = $0.012 gross profit/unit). The calculations below assume the new cost basis of $0.108/unit.*

**Proposed Price:** $0.138 per unit ($0.12 + 15%)
**New Gross Margin per Unit:** $0.03 ($0.138 price - $0.108 cost)

#### Scenario A: 10% Volume Decline
*   **Volume:** 45,000 units
*   **Revenue:** $6,210 (45,000 × $0.138)
*   **Total Cost:** $4,860 (45,000 × $0.108)
*   **Gross Profit:** $1,350
*   **Net Cash Flow:** -$23,650 ($1,350 - $25,000)
*   **Runway:** 5.1 months
*   **Verdict:** Marginal improvement. The price hike barely offsets the volume loss. You remain deeply unprofitable.

#### Scenario B: 20% Volume Decline (Ravi's Warning)
*   **Volume:** 40,000 units
*   **Revenue:** $5,520 (40,000 × $0.138)
*   **Total Cost:** $4,320 (40,000 × $0.108)
*   **Gross Profit:** $1,200
*   **Net Cash Flow:** -$23,800 ($1,200 - $25,000)
*   **Runway:** 5.0 months
*   **Verdict:** Negative leverage. Because your fixed burn rate is so high relative to your gross profit, losing volume destroys the slight gain from the higher price. Total revenue actually drops below the baseline.

#### Scenario C: 30% Volume Decline
*   **Volume:** 35,000 units
*   **Revenue:** $4,830 (35,000 × $0.138)
*   **Total Cost:** $3,780 (35,000 × $0.108)
*   **Gross Profit:** $1,050
*   **Net Cash Flow:** -$23,950 ($1,050 - $25,000)
*   **Runway:** 5.0 months
*   **Verdict:** Dangerous. Revenue collapses to $4,830, making it impossible to cover operating expenses.

### Critical Analysis
Your current business model is mathematically unsustainable regardless of the price increase. Even at full volume (50,000 units) with the new price, your maximum possible monthly gross profit is only $1,500 (50,000 × $0.03). Against a $25,000 monthly burn rate, you are covering only 6% of your operating costs with gross profit.

A 15% price increase is insufficient to save the company because the unit economics are broken. You are losing $0.078 on every unit sold when factoring in the allocation of your burn rate ($25,000 / 50,000 units = $0.50 overhead per unit + $0.108 COGS - $0.138 Price).

**Recommendation:** Do not rely on this price hike as a survival strategy. The math shows you will run out of cash in approximately 5 months under any scenario. You must seek alternative funding immediately or drastically reduce your $25,000 monthly burn rate, as no realistic volume retention will bridge a $23,000+ monthly deficit with these unit margins.

*The calculations above assume your $25,000 burn rate is fixed and does not decrease if production volume drops.*
*If your raw material cost increase has not yet hit your inventory, your current runway may be slightly longer, but the structural deficit remains.*
