### Recommended Pricing Framework for SkyCrop

To address the volatility of FAA waiver costs while maintaining customer trust and profitability, you should shift from a pure **$12/acre flat rate** to a **Base Rate + Variable Regulatory Surcharge** model. This separates your operational value (dusting) from external regulatory costs (waivers), preventing margin erosion without surprising farmers.

#### 1. The Contract Structure: "Base + Pass-Through"
Instead of bundling the waiver cost into the per-acre price, unbundle it in your contracts.

*   **Base Service Fee:** Set this at **$11.50 – $11.75 per acre**. This covers your drone operations, pilot labor, and a healthy margin, assuming a *standard* waiver cost.
*   **Regulatory Surcharge Clause:** Include a contract line item stating: *"FAA Waiver Surcharge: Actual cost passed through at $0.0X per acre based on daily FAA fee schedule."*
    *   **Mechanism:** If a flight covers 500 acres and the waiver is $50, the surcharge is $0.10/acre. If the waiver spikes to $400, the surcharge becomes $0.80/acre.
    *   **Transparency:** Provide farmers with a simple monthly ledger showing the date, flight acres, actual FAA fee paid, and the calculated surcharge. This builds trust that you are not profiting from the fee.

#### 2. Alternative: The "Congestion Tier" Model
If farmers strongly resist variable line items, offer two distinct pricing tiers based on flight timing, effectively averaging the risk.

*   **Standard Tier ($12.50/acre):** Flights scheduled during peak congestion times (higher probability of $300–$400 waivers). The higher base rate creates a buffer to absorb average high costs.
*   **Flex Tier ($11.00/acre):** Flights scheduled during off-peak windows (early morning/late evening) where waivers are historically lower ($50–$100). The farmer gets a discount in exchange for allowing you to optimize flight times for lower regulatory costs.

#### 3. Addressing FAA Negotiations
Regarding fixed-rate waivers: The FAA generally does not offer "volume discounts" on Part 107 waiver fees for commercial operators in the same way airlines negotiate landing slots. Fees are typically statutory or based on specific airspace complexity.
*   **Strategy:** Instead of seeking a fixed fee, seek a **blanket waiver** for recurring operations in specific low-congestion zones if your flight paths are consistent. This can sometimes reduce the administrative burden and cost per flight, though it depends heavily on the specific Flight Standards District Office (FSDO) managing your region. Consult with an aviation attorney specializing in Part 107 to see if your repetitive flight patterns qualify for a streamlined renewal process that lowers effective costs.

#### 4. Implementation Steps
1.  **Audit Historical Data:** Calculate the average waiver cost per acre over the last season. Use this to set your new Base Rate so that 80% of flights remain profitable without triggering the surcharge explanation.
2.  **Update Contracts:** Add the "Regulatory Surcharge" clause with a cap (e.g., "Surcharge will not exceed $1.00/acre without prior written approval") to prevent sticker shock.
3.  **Customer Communication:** Frame this as "Protecting your rates from government volatility." Explain that the $12 flat rate forced you to overcharge during cheap days to subsidize expensive days. The new model ensures they only pay the actual government cost when it spikes.

This approach stabilizes your core margin on the service you control while fairly allocating external regulatory risks.

*Note: Ensure your legal counsel reviews the surcharge language to comply with local agricultural contracting laws.*
*Consider offering an annual "true-up" discount if average waiver costs come in lower than projected to share the upside.*
