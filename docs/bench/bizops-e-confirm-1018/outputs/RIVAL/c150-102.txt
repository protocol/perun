### Unit Economics Model: FinalDignity

Your current model conflates **cash flow timing** with **revenue recognition**. For your SaaS unit economics, the 24-month hold on *your customer's* funds (the funeral home's pre-need capital) does not delay *your* revenue recognition, provided you invoice your $299 subscription monthly. You are selling software access, not holding the insurance funds yourself.

However, the regulation critically impacts your **customer's ability to pay** and their **churn risk**, which indirectly dictates your LTV. Here is the corrected model based on your data:

**1. The Core Metrics (Per Location)**
*   **Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR):** $299
*   **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):** $1,200
*   **Payback Period:** 4 months ($1,200 / $299). This is healthy.
*   **Gross Margin:** Assume ~80-90% (standard for SaaS), unless you have heavy implementation costs.

**2. The "Hold" Impact on LTV**
The 24-month hold prevents the funeral home from recognizing revenue or accessing capital from the $3,500 contracts they sign.
*   **Customer Cash Flow Reality:** They sign 8 contracts/month ($28,000 value), but cannot touch that cash for 2 years. They must operate on existing capital or debt.
*   **Churn Risk Driver:** If a funeral home lacks operating capital due to this hold, they may churn despite liking your software. Your LTV is not limited by *your* pricing, but by *their* survival rate during the 24-month cash gap.
*   **Effective LTV Calculation:**
    *   Standard Formula: $LTV = (ARPA \times Gross Margin) / Churn Rate$
    *   Your Variable: **Churn Rate** is now the critical unknown.
    *   *Scenario A (Stable Client):* If they survive the hold, and stay 5 years (60 months) with 2% monthly churn: LTV ≈ $9,000.
    *   *Scenario B (Cash Crunch):* If the hold causes them to fail or cancel software within 18 months: LTV drops to ≈ $5,400.

**3. Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)**
*   **Your CCC:** Likely negative or very short. You pay your servers/staff monthly; customers pay you monthly. The 24-month regulatory hold **does not enter your CCC** because you never hold the pre-need funds.
*   **The Misconception:** You are modeling the funeral home's CCC, not your own. Your CCC is only impacted if you offer *them* net-60 or net-90 payment terms because they are cash-strapped. If you require monthly auto-pay, your CCC remains efficient.

**4. Strategic Adjustment**
Do not lower your price to $299 to accommodate their cash flow; it masks the problem. Instead, consider:
*   **Annual Pre-pay Discount:** Offer 2 months free if they pay $2,990 upfront. This tests their liquidity immediately. If they can't pay annually, they are a high churn risk.
*   **Value Metric Pivot:** Can you charge a small % of contracts *managed* rather than flat fee? No, this complicates things. Stick to flat fee but tighten credit checks.

***

**CPA vs. Internal Handling**

*   **Handle Internally:** Your SaaS revenue recognition ($299/mo as earned), CAC amortization, and standard churn modeling. The regulatory hold on *client* funds is a customer success/risk issue, not an accounting entry for you.
*   **Take to a Funeral-Specialized CPA:**
    1.  **Nexus & Regulatory Exposure:** Does hosting data for FL/OH pre-need contracts trigger any "fiduciary technology" licensing or bonding requirements in those states?
    2.  **Trust Accounting Integration:** If your software touches the *recording* of these held funds, does your error & omissions insurance need specific riders for "misappropriation of trust funds" even if you don't hold the cash?
    3.  **Audit Trails:** Verify if your current data retention policies meet the specific statutory audit requirements for pre-need contracts in FL/OH (often 10+ years), which impacts your storage costs and liability.

*Note: Your unit economics are sound on paper; the risk is entirely concentrated in your customers' liquidity during the 24-month window.*
*Focus your modeling on predicting which clients will survive that cash gap, not on adjusting your own revenue recognition.*
