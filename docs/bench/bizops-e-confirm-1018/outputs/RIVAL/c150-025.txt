Your current pricing model presents a **high-risk compliance violation** that likely requires an immediate pivot.

Most foundations that cap administrative overhead at 10% define "administrative costs" broadly to include **any** fees paid to third parties for grant writing, consulting, or fundraising services. When you charge a 10% success fee on top of a non-profit's internal overhead, you are almost certainly pushing their total administrative burden over the allowable limit.

Here is the breakdown of the risk:
1.  **The Math:** If a non-profit has even 1% in internal administrative costs related to the grant (staff time, overhead allocation), adding your 10% success fee brings the total to 11%, exceeding the typical 10% cap.
2.  **The Definition:** Foundations generally view success fees as "fundraising costs," which are frequently categorized under administrative overhead or explicitly prohibited as a direct charge against the grant award.
3.  **The Consequence:** If audited, the foundation may disallow the fee, forcing the non-profit to repay it from unrestricted funds, or worse, deem the entire grant application non-compliant, damaging the client's reputation and your own.

**There are no safe, universal exceptions you can leverage without legal counsel.** While some private donors or corporate sponsors may be flexible, institutional foundations (especially government or large family foundations) strictly enforce these caps. Relying on "exceptions" is a gamble your 10-month runway cannot afford.

**Recommended Immediate Actions:**
*   **Stop selling the 10% success fee model** to clients targeting foundations with strict overhead caps.
*   **Pivot to a flat-fee or retainer model.** You mentioned charging $2,000 upfront. Consider increasing this flat fee to capture more value (e.g., $5,000–$7,000 flat) while removing the percentage-based success fee entirely. This classifies your service as a pre-award consulting expense, which many foundations allow as a direct cost or permit to be paid from unrestricted funds, rather than a contingent fee that looks like a kickback.
*   **Reframe the value proposition.** Market your service as "compliance-safe grant writing" where the fee is fixed and transparent, ensuring clients never risk their grant eligibility due to vendor fees.

Your average revenue per client is $7,000. To maintain this without the success fee, you would need to charge a flat fee of approximately $7,000 per grant. Given you have helped 50 organizations, you have the case studies to justify a higher upfront premium for a "risk-free" (for the client's compliance) flat-fee structure.

Do not wait for a legal opinion to pause the success-fee sales pitch; the warning from the partner foundation is a sufficient market signal that your current model is a barrier to your clients' success.
