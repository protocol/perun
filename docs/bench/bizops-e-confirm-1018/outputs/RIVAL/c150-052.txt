### Financial Modeling Strategy for EternalRest

To accurately model your unit economics and present a clear picture to investors, you must decouple **Cash Flow** (liquidity) from **Revenue Recognition** (profitability). Your current 4% monthly churn is a critical risk factor that exacerbates the cash trap created by the 24-month statutory hold.

#### 1. The Dual-Track Model Structure
Build your model with two distinct tabs that feed into a unified dashboard:

**Track A: Cash Flow & Liquidity (The Survival View)**
This tracks actual money in the bank.
*   **Inflow:** 100% of pre-need payments received.
*   **Outflow:** 70% immediately transferred to Trust (non-operational); 30% retained for operations.
*   **Net Operational Cash:** Only the 30% retention is available to cover your $40k burn.
*   **Current Reality Check:** With $45 clients × $15k = $675k monthly sales volume, you retain only $202.5k (30%). After $40k burn, you generate ~$162.5k in monthly operational surplus. *However*, if churn spikes, this surplus evaporates instantly because you cannot dip into the trust.

**Track B: GAAP Revenue & Profitability (The Investor View)**
This tracks earned income for valuation and CAC calculations.
*   **Revenue Recognition:** $0 recognized in Months 1–24 for new contracts.
*   **Deferred Revenue Liability:** Record the 30% retained portion as a "Deferred Revenue" liability on the balance sheet, not income.
*   **Recognition Event:** Only after 24 months (and assuming the contract hasn't churned) do you move funds from Deferred Revenue to Recognized Revenue.

#### 2. Solving the CAC Payback Distortion
Standard CAC payback (CAC ÷ Monthly Gross Margin) fails here because your denominator is zero for two years. Using it would show an infinite payback period, scaring investors. Instead, use **Modified CAC Payback**:

**Formula:**
$$ \text{Modified Payback} = \frac{\text{CAC}}{(\text{Monthly Cash Retention} \times \text{Retention Probability}_{24mo})} $$

*   **Numerator:** Fully loaded Customer Acquisition Cost.
*   **Denominator:** Use the **30% cash retention** as your proxy for value capture, adjusted heavily for churn.
*   **Churn Adjustment:** With 4% monthly churn, only ~38% of customers survive to the 24-month recognition window ($0.96^{24}$). Your model must show that you are effectively acquiring 2.6 customers to get one "real" long-term payer.

**Investor Narrative:** Present CAC payback based on *cash retention* immediately available to service debt or reinvest, while explicitly modeling the "J-Curve" of profitability. Show that while GAAP profit is delayed, the *asset value* (the book of business) grows monthly.

#### 3. Critical Liquidity Warning
Your 4% monthly churn is dangerous. In a pre-need model, churn before month 24 often requires returning the retained 30% (depending on specific contract cancellation terms and state penalties). If you must refund the retained portion upon cancellation, your effective cash flow per customer is near zero until the 24-month cliff.

**Immediate Action:**
1.  **Refine Churn Logic:** Model three scenarios: Churn stays at 4%, drops to 2%, or increases to 5%. At 4%, your long-term unit economics may be negative once acquisition costs are factored against the low survival rate.
2.  **Cash Runway:** Do not count the trust funds as assets available for operations. Your $300k cash / $40k burn = 7.5 months runway *only if* new sales stop completely. If sales continue, your runway extends, but only via the 30% retention flow.

**Summary for Investors:**
Present a "Sources and Uses" chart showing that while GAAP revenue lags, the **Book Value** of the trust grows predictably. Frame the business not as a SaaS recurring revenue play, but as an **asset accumulation play** with a delayed monetization trigger. The key metric to highlight is **Net Retention Rate of Cash**, not just MRR.

*Note: Consult a CPA specializing in Texas pre-need laws to confirm if the 30% retention must also be refunded upon early cancellation; this drastically alters the cash flow risk.*
*Your 4% churn is the single biggest lever to pull; reducing it to 2% doubles the volume of contracts reaching the 24-month revenue recognition event.*
