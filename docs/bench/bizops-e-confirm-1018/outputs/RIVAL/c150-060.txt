### Liability Analysis: Algorithmic Suggestions vs. Final Submission

**1. Direct Liability Exposure**
You likely face **direct liability** regardless of your Terms of Service (ToS). In healthcare, regulators (such as the OIG and DOJ) and insurers often view "upcoding" as a strict liability issue regarding the claim submitted. If your algorithm systematically suggests higher-level codes that are denied or flagged as fraudulent during an audit, you could be implicated in a "knowing submission" scheme, even if the clinic pressed the final button.
*   **The ToS Limitation:** A ToS clause stating "Clinic is solely liable" protects you in a *contract dispute* with the clinic, but it **does not shield you from federal fraud investigations, False Claims Act lawsuits, or insurer recoupment actions.** Regulators can pierce corporate veils and ToS disclaimers if the tool is deemed the primary driver of fraudulent billing patterns.
*   **Joint Liability Risk:** If audits reveal that clinics are blindly accepting your suggestions without review, both the clinic and CodeAssist could be named in joint liability suits. The argument will be that your tool was marketed as an "assistant" but functioned as an unregulated "decision-maker."

**2. The Necessity of Errors & Omissions (E&O) Insurance**
**Yes, you absolutely need specific E&O insurance.**
*   **Standard Policies May Exclude This:** General tech E&O policies often exclude "professional services" related to healthcare billing or coding. You require a policy specifically endorsed for **Healthcare IT** and **Medical Coding Assistance**.
*   **Coverage Scope:** Ensure the policy covers legal defense costs for regulatory audits, not just civil lawsuits. Given your $180k cash reserve, a single audit defense could bankrupt the company without insurance.
*   **Carrier Requirements:** Many insurers will require proof of your "human-in-the-loop" workflow (see below) before underwriting you.

**3. Structuring Your Disclaimer and Workflow**
To mitigate joint liability, you must operationally and contractually enforce the "Human-in-the-Loop" standard. Your disclaimer must be active, not passive.

*   **Contractual Language (ToS/Master Services Agreement):**
    *   Explicitly state: "CodeAssist provides *suggestions only*. It does not make billing determinations."
    *   Mandate Review: "Client agrees that a certified medical coder or qualified provider must review, verify, and approve every code suggestion prior to claim submission."
    *   Audit Trail Clause: Require the clinic's software to log *who* reviewed and approved the code. If you can prove a human reviewed it, your liability drops significantly.

*   **Product/UI Implementation (Critical for Defense):**
    *   **Forced Interaction:** Do not allow "auto-submit." The user must click a specific "I have reviewed this suggestion" button for every claim or batch.
    *   **Visual Warnings:** Display a persistent, non-dismissible banner on the coding screen: "WARNING: These are AI suggestions. Federal law requires a certified professional to verify all codes before submission."
    *   **Confidence Scores:** If the AI is unsure, display a low confidence score and force a higher level of human verification (e.g., requiring a senior coder's login).
