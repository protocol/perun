**SafeCloud Subpoena Response Protocol**

**1. Immediate Technical Assessment (The "Can We?" Check)**
Before any legal filing, your engineering lead must verify the specific architecture for the targeted user.
*   **If True End-to-End Encryption (E2EE) exists:** If the decryption keys are solely on the user's device and never transmitted to SafeCloud servers, you **cannot** technically comply with a request for decrypted logs. You physically do not possess the data in a readable format.
*   **If Keys are Escrowed or Server-Side:** If SafeCloud holds keys or can decrypt data server-side, you **must** comply with a valid court order to avoid contempt of court. Technical inability is the only absolute defense; policy promises do not override lawful subpoenas if you possess the capability to decrypt.

**2. Legal Triage & Validation**
Do not ignore the subpoena. With a $30k legal reserve and a five-person team, you cannot afford a contempt ruling.
*   **Engage Counsel Immediately:** Use your reserve to retain a lawyer specializing in California digital privacy and Second Amendment issues. Do not attempt to draft responses internally.
*   **Validate the Order:** Ensure the subpoena is legally valid, properly served, and specific. Challenge overbroad requests (e.g., "fishing expeditions") that violate user rights or exceed the scope of the investigation.
*   **Determine Notification Rights:** Ask counsel if you are legally permitted to notify the user. Often, subpoenas come with gag orders. If no gag order exists, transparency is vital for trust.

**3. The Response Strategy**
*   **Scenario A (Technically Impossible):** Your legal counsel should file a declaration stating that due to your E2EE architecture, SafeCloud does not possess the decryption keys and therefore cannot produce the requested content. Provide the encrypted blob if demanded, but explicitly state it is unintelligible without the user's key. This honors your privacy policy and complies with the law by proving inability.
*   **Scenario B (Technically Possible):** If you hold the keys, you must decrypt and provide the data upon a valid court order. Your privacy policy likely contains standard legal exceptions for lawful government requests. Honoring the policy means protecting data from *unauthorized* access, not defying valid court orders.

**4. User Communication Protocol**
*   **If Permitted to Notify:** Send a transparent, factual notice: "SafeCloud received a legal request for your data. We challenged the scope where possible. Due to our encryption design, we [could not/could] provide readable data. We do not voluntarily share data; we only comply when legally compelled and technically able."
*   **If Gag-Ordered:** Prepare a generic transparency report for your website (aggregated data only) to maintain brand trust without violating the specific order.
*   **Brand Messaging:** Reiterate that your system is designed so that *even you* cannot access user data unless the architecture specifically allows it. Frame the inability to comply (in Scenario A) as a feature, not a bug.

**5. Operational Safeguards**
*   **Document Everything:** Log the receipt of the subpoena, technical verification steps, legal advice received, and the final response.
*   **Review Architecture:** If you intend to guarantee non-compliance with future subpoenas, you must architecturally ensure key escrow is impossible. If you currently hold keys, you must accept that valid subpoenas will result in data disclosure.

**Note:** This protocol assumes US/California jurisdiction; laws vary by state and federal district.
**Note:** Do not destroy data upon receiving a subpoena; this constitutes spoliation of evidence and carries severe criminal penalties.
