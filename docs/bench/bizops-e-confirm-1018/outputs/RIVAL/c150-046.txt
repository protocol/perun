### Compliance Assessment: Ireland vs. Germany/France

**1. The Core Legal Framework**
Your current architecture (storing data in Ireland while serving customers in Germany and France) operates entirely within the European Economic Area (EEA). Under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), Ireland, Germany, and France are all subject to the same unified data protection rules.
*   **No Data Residency Violation:** There is **no** requirement under current EU law or specific German/French national laws that mandates security access logs for short-term rentals be stored physically within the country where the guest stays.
*   **Intra-EU Transfers:** Moving data from Germany/France to Ireland is considered a domestic transfer within the EU single market. It does not trigger "cross-border data transfer" restrictions that apply to transfers outside the EEA (e.g., to the US or Asia). Therefore, complex mechanisms like Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) for international transfer are not required for this specific flow.

**2. Potential Legal Pitfalls**
While the server location is compliant, your specific data type (guest access logs) introduces other risks you must address for the hotel chain contract:
*   **Lawful Basis for Processing:** You must clearly define *why* you store entry times and durations. For short-term rentals, this is often justified by "legitimate interest" (security) or "contractual necessity," but you cannot retain this data indefinitely. Excessive retention violates the GDPR principle of storage limitation.
*   **Data Subject Rights:** Guests (data subjects) have the right to access, rectify, or delete their logs. Your system must technically support these requests efficiently. If the hotel chain cannot fulfill a guest's deletion request because your system makes it difficult, they will be liable, and you will be in breach of your Data Processing Agreement (DPA).
*   **Security Measures:** Access logs are sensitive security data. While the location is fine, the *security* of that Irish server must meet high standards (encryption at rest and in transit, strict access controls). A breach here would trigger mandatory reporting to authorities in Germany/France within 72 hours.

**3. Contractual Requirements vs. Legal Requirements**
The hotel chain may demand "data sovereignty" in their contract that exceeds legal minimums.
*   **Negotiation Point:** They may mistakenly believe they need servers in Germany. You can legally assure them that Ireland is fully GDPR-compliant and offers equivalent protection.
*   **Data Processing Agreement (DPA):** You will need a robust DPA defining you as the "Processor" and the hotel as the "Controller." This document, not server migration, is the primary compliance artifact they will audit.
