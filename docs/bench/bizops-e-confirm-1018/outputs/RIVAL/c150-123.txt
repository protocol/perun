### Recommended Fee Structure: The "Platform Service Fee" Model

To ensure compliance with the California Judicial Council's $3.75 per page cap on certified transcripts while maintaining your 20% margin, you must immediately **unbundle** your fees. The statutory cap applies strictly to the reporter's charge for the *transcript service*, not to ancillary administrative or platform fees charged by a third-party marketplace.

**1. The Pricing Mechanics**
You must shift from a "Gross Bundle" to a "Net Plus Fee" model. The law firm pays two distinct line items:
*   **Line Item A (Reporter Fee):** The cost of the deposition and transcript, calculated strictly at or below the $3.75/page cap (plus any other allowable statutory appearance fees). This money goes 100% to the reporter (or through you as a pass-through).
*   **Line Item B (DepoStream Platform Fee):** A separate administrative fee for matching, scheduling, and technology. This is your 20% margin, calculated as a percentage of the total transaction or a fixed markup, but legally distinct from the transcript rate.

*   **DepoStream Fee:** $120.00 (Listed as "Platform & Administrative Service Fee").
*   **Total Law Firm Cost:** $600.00 (Unchanged for the customer).

**2. Invoice Presentation for Compliance & Transparency**
Your invoices and checkout flow must clearly separate these costs to avoid the appearance of the reporter inflating their rate to cover your commission.

*   **Description for Line Item A:** "Certified Court Reporting Services & Transcript (Rate: $3.75/page max, CA Gov. Code compliant)."
*   **Description for Line Item B:** "DepoStream Technology & Scheduling Fee."
*   **Visual Layout:** Do not show a single "Total Price" until the breakdown is visible. Use a sub-total structure:
    > Services Rendered by Reporter: $480.00
    > Platform Service Fee: $120.00
    > **Total Due: $600.00**

**3. Mitigating Conversion Friction**
Law firms are accustomed to line-item billing in legal services. Friction will be minimal if you frame the platform fee as a value-add rather than a hidden tax.
*   **Communication Script:** "To ensure full compliance with new CA Judicial Council rate caps, we are updating our invoicing to separately list court reporter fees and our platform administration fee. Your total cost remains the same; this change ensures our freelance network remains fully licensed and compliant."
*   **UX Implementation:** On the booking screen, show the "Estimated Reporter Fee (based on avg. pages)" and "DepoStream Fee" side-by-side before checkout. Transparency builds trust with legal counsel who must audit these bills.

**4. Immediate Action Plan**
1.  **Update Terms of Service:** Explicitly state that DepoStream charges a separate administrative fee independent of the reporter's statutory rates.
2.  **Modify Invoice Templates:** Implement the two-line item structure immediately for all new bookings.
3.  **Reporter Onboarding:** Instruct freelancers to quote only the statutory rate for their services. They must never quote a "all-in" price that includes your commission.

By separating the fee, the reporter technically charges $3.75/page (compliant), and you charge a separate market-rate fee for software logistics (unregulated). This protects your freelancers from disciplinary action and secures your revenue model.

*Note: While this structure aligns with standard marketplace compliance strategies, you should have a local attorney review the specific wording of your invoice line items against the latest CA Judicial Council advisory.*
