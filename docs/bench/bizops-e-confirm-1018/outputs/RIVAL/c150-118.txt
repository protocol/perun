**Important Clarification:** Your concern stems from a misunderstanding of who is billing whom. Ethical billing rules (ABA Model Rules and state bar associations) apply to **law firms billing their clients**, not to **software vendors billing law firms**.

**1. Ethical Guidelines for Your Pricing Model**
You are selling a B2B SaaS tool to law firms. Your $2,000 flat fee is a **vendor expense** for the law firm, similar to paying for Westlaw, Clio, or office rent.
*   **No Ethical Violation:** There is no ethical requirement for software vendors to charge hourly or usage-based fees. Law firms routinely pay flat fees for software regardless of how much they use it.
*   **Responsibility:** The ethical obligation lies entirely with the **law firm**. When the firm bills *their* client, they must ensure their total fee (including your software cost) is "reasonable" under Rule 1.5 of the ABA Model Rules. How they allocate your $2,000 cost across their internal margins is their business problem, not yours.
*   **Flat Fees are Permissible:** In fact, flat fees for legal services are widely encouraged for immigration matters to provide cost certainty to clients. The fact that case complexity varies is a risk the law firm accepts when setting their own flat fee with their client; it does not invalidate your flat fee to the firm.

**No.** Switching to usage-based pricing (e.g., per hour or per document) will not solve an ethical problem that does not exist, and it may actually hurt your business model:
*   **Misaligned Incentives:** Your value proposition is efficiency. If you charge per usage, you penalize firms for using your tool to work faster.
*   **Complexity:** Usage-based pricing introduces friction in procurement and budgeting for law firms.
*   **Ethical Neutrality:** Changing your model does not shift ethical liability. If a firm charges a client $5,000 for a simple renewal because they passed through your high usage fees, the firm is still responsible for that "unreasonable" total fee.
