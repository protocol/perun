### 1. Determining the Applicable Tax Rate (Sourcing)
For vehicle rentals in California, the tax rate is generally determined by the **location where the vehicle is delivered to the renter** (the "pick-up" location), not the owner's residence.
*   **Rule:** Since the renter picks up the car in Alameda County, the combined state and local tax rate for **Alameda County** (including any specific city or Tourism Improvement District rates where the handover occurs) applies to the transaction.
*   **Action:** Do not apply San Francisco tax rates to these specific trips. You must maintain a geocoded database of tax rates for every potential pick-up zip code in your operating area.

### 2. Determining the Tax Base (What is Taxed)
This is the most critical area for potential overpayment or underpayment.
*   **General Rule:** In most California jurisdictions, sales/use tax and rental car taxes are calculated on the **gross rental charge paid by the customer**, not just the marketplace's commission.
*   **Implication:** If a renter pays $350/day, the tax is calculated on the full $350, even though LuxShare only keeps $52.50 (15%). The owner receives the remainder net of your commission, but the tax liability usually attaches to the total transaction value.
*   **Exception Check:** Some specific Tourism Improvement District (TID) assessments may have different bases. You must verify if any local ordinances allow taxing only the "facilitator fee" (your commission), but assume the **full rental price** is taxable until proven otherwise by counsel.

### 3. Registration and Filing Obligations
*   **State Level:** You likely need to register with the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration (CDTFA) as a "Marketplace Facilitator." As a facilitator, you are generally responsible for collecting and remitting taxes on behalf of your owners.
*   **Local Level:**
    *   **Single Registration vs. Multiple:** California often allows marketplace facilitators to file a single return with the CDTFA, which then distributes local taxes to the appropriate counties/cities. You typically do **not** need to register separately in every county (e.g., separately in SF and Alameda) if you are filing correctly as a facilitator with the state.
    *   **Verification:** Confirm with the CDTFA that your facilitator license covers all local jurisdictions where your cars are picked up.

### 4. Required Data Points for Compliance
To remain compliant and audit-ready, your system must track and store the following for **every single trip**:
1.  **Pick-up Location:** Exact address and zip code (determines the tax rate).
2.  **Drop-off Location:** Exact address (relevant if one-way fees or different drop-off taxes apply).
3.  **Transaction Date & Time:** Tax rates can change; the rate in effect at the time of pick-up usually applies.
4.  **Gross Rental Amount:** The total price paid by the renter before fees.
5.  **Facilitator Fee:** Your 15% commission (broken out separately on the receipt).
6.  **Tax Amount Collected:** The specific dollar amount charged to the renter.
7.  **Owner Location:** Address of the vehicle owner (for 1099 reporting, though less relevant for sales tax sourcing).
8.  **Renter Exemption Status:** Flag if the renter is tax-exempt (e.g., certain government or diplomatic entities).
