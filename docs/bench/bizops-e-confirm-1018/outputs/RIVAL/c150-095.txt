### Executive Recommendation: Do Not Pivot to Pay-Per-Completion

**The Verdict:** Your low completion rate (40%) is a critical product and adoption failure, **not** a pricing failure. Switching to a "pay-per-completion" model immediately would be fatal. It would slash your revenue by ~60% overnight (dropping ARR from $2M to ~$800k), accelerating your cash burn and likely triggering a down-round or insolvency within three months.

**The Analysis:**
Your current unit economics are strong on paper ($2M ARR vs. $1.8M annual OpEx), yielding a positive contribution margin. The threat is not the *price*, but the *value realization*. If customers aren't seeing employees finish courses, they will churn at renewal regardless of the pricing model. A usage-based model in this context signals that your product only has value when finished, admitting that the "seat" itself is worthless. This devalues your entire platform.

**Strategic Actions:**

1.  **Maintain Per-Seat Pricing (with a Twist):** Keep the $200/seat anchor. It aligns with corporate budgeting (L&D budgets are allocated per headcount). Do not move to transactional pricing which creates revenue volatility and caps your upside.
2.  **Introduce "Engagement-Backed" Guarantees:** Instead of discounting upfront, offer a performance guarantee. "If completion rates don't reach 60% by month 6, we provide free content customization or extend the contract by 3 months." This shifts the conversation from "price" to "partnership" without eroding the headline rate.
3.  **Reframe the Metric for the Board:** Stop presenting "40% completion" as a standalone failure. Present it as "40% organic completion with zero intervention." The investment thesis is not invalidated if the path to 70%+ involves better customer success (onboarding, manager nudges, integration with HRIS), which is cheaper than building new modules.
4.  **Immediate Cash Flow Fix:** Push for annual prepayments with a 15% discount if you aren't already. If you are, offer a "multi-year lock-in" at current rates to secure 12-18 months of cash runway immediately.

**Presentation to Stakeholders:**
Frame the narrative around **"Realizing Latent Value."**
*   **Problem:** We are selling seats, but customers aren't driving adoption.
*   **Solution:** Shift resources from *new module development* (75k/module) to *Customer Success & Implementation*.
*   **Ask:** Pause 50% of new content creation. Reallocate those funds to an implementation team that forces higher completion rates.
*   **Outcome:** Increasing completion from 40% to 60% increases perceived value by 50% without changing the price, securing renewals and justifying the $200/seat.

**Conclusion:**
Do not pivot pricing. Pivot your **go-to-market motion** from "sell and hope" to "implement and ensure." The low completion rate is a warning sign to fix the product adoption loop, not a signal to dismantle your revenue model.

*Note: With only 6 months of cash, freezing new module development is critical to extend runway while fixing engagement.*
*Focus all immediate energy on increasing completion rates in top 20 accounts to secure renewals.*
