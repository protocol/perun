**Immediate Action Plan for AuditChain**

Your current General Liability policy provides **no coverage** for this incident. The exclusion for "professional errors in code" explicitly bars claims arising from your core service: auditing smart contracts. You are currently self-insuring against professional negligence, which poses an existential threat given your $250,000 cash reserve versus a potential $200,000+ claim.

**1. Coverage Type: Tech E&O vs. Cyber Liability**
You need **Technology Errors and Omissions (Tech E&O)** insurance, not primarily Cyber Liability.
*   **Tech E&O:** Covers financial losses clients suffer due to your professional negligence, errors, or omissions in performing your service (the audit). This is the specific policy that responds when a bug you missed causes a client's fund loss.
*   **Cyber Liability:** Primarily covers data breaches, ransomware, and network security failures affecting *your* systems or client data privacy. It rarely covers the functional failure of code you audited unless specifically endorsed.

**2. Typical Exclusions & Critical Coverage Language**
Standard Tech E&O policies often contain traps for Web3 auditors. You must demand the following specific language and endorsements:
*   **Remove "Code/Software" Exclusions:** Many standard policies exclude liability related to software development or code review. You need a policy that explicitly **includes** smart contract auditing as a covered profession.
*   **Post-Deployment Coverage:** Ensure the policy covers vulnerabilities discovered **after** the report is delivered but within a reasonable "discovery period." Insurers may try to argue the "error" occurred only when the code was deployed by the client, not during your audit. The policy must define the "wrongful act" as the failure to identify the bug during the engagement, regardless of when the exploit occurs.
*   **Cryptocurrency/Digital Asset Definition:** Verify that "money," "funds," or "property" in the policy definition explicitly includes **cryptocurrencies and digital tokens**. Traditional policies often limit coverage to fiat currency or tangible property.
*   **Prior Acts Coverage:** Since you have completed 25 audits this year, ensure your new policy includes "prior acts" coverage to protect against claims arising from work done before the policy start date.

**3. Strategic Recommendations**
*   **Engage a Specialized Broker Immediately:** Do not use a general commercial broker. You require a broker specializing in **Web3, blockchain, and technology E&O**. The market for crypto-native insurance is niche, and standard carriers often decline these risks without specialized underwriting. A specialist broker knows which carriers (e.g., Lloyd's of London syndicates, specialized tech carriers) write smart contract audit coverage.
*   **Address the Current Claim:** Since you likely have no applicable insurance for this specific incident, you must handle the client negotiation directly. Consult with legal counsel specializing in crypto liability to determine your contractual liability limits. Your engagement letter likely contains a liability cap (often limited to the fee paid, i.e., $15,000); enforce this if legally viable in your jurisdiction.
*   **Risk Mitigation:** Until a policy is bound, consider pausing new engagements or requiring clients to sign updated waivers acknowledging the lack of insured coverage for post-audit exploits.

**Note:** This incident highlights a critical gap between your revenue model ($15k flat fee) and your risk exposure ($200k+ losses). Future pricing strategies should factor in the cost of adequate E&O coverage, which will be significant for smart contract auditing.
