### Current Financial Baseline
*   **Monthly Revenue:** $60,000
*   **Current Monthly Operating Cost:** $40,000 (10 vans × $4,000)
*   **Current Monthly Profit:** $20,000
*   **Current Cash Reserve:** $120,000

### Stress Test: 40% Fuel Cost Increase
Your current fuel cost is $1,500 per van ($15,000 total). A 40% increase adds $600 per van.
*   **New Fuel Cost per Van:** $2,100
*   **New Total Monthly Operating Cost:** $46,000 ($40,000 base + $6,000 increase)
*   **New Monthly Profit:** $14,000 ($60,000 revenue - $46,000 cost)

### Runway Projection
Under this specific scenario where costs rise but prices and staff remain static:
*   **You remain profitable.** You are generating $14,000 in positive cash flow every month.
*   **Runway:** Infinite. You will not deplete your $120,000 reserve; instead, you will continue to add to it.
*   **Emergency Funding:** Not required based on these numbers. Your current margins absorb the 40% fuel hike without dipping into savings.

### Strategic Recommendations
Since you remain profitable, the decision to hedge or surcharge depends on risk tolerance, not immediate survival.

1.  **Fuel Surcharge Model:** Implement a variable "fuel adjustment fee" tied to the local diesel price index. This passes future volatility directly to the customer, protecting your margin if prices rise another 20-30%. It is administratively easier than hedging for a fleet of this size.
2.  **Hedging:** Generally not recommended for a $15k/month fuel spend unless you have access to low-cost futures contracts. The capital required to margin a hedge might be better kept in your cash reserve.
3.  **Break-Even Analysis:** Your break-even point shifts from $40,000/month to $46,000/month. As long as revenue stays above $46k, you are safe.

### Variables to Adjust in Your Model
To refine this estimate, adjust these inputs:
*   **Revenue Elasticity:** Will adding a surcharge reduce demand? Model a 5-10% drop in volume if you raise prices.
*   **Efficiency Gains:** Can route optimization reduce miles driven by 10%? This directly offsets the price-per-gallon increase.
*   **Non-Fuel Inflation:** Are labor or supply costs also rising? If total operating costs creep up another 5%, your profit drops to ~$11,500, still positive but tighter.

**Conclusion:** Do not seek emergency funding. Your reserves are sufficient, and your operations remain cash-flow positive even with the projected spike. Focus on implementing a fuel surcharge to protect future margins rather than surviving the immediate shock.

*Note: This analysis assumes revenue remains constant at $60k. If the broader economy slows and demand drops simultaneously with fuel rising, recalculate using a reduced revenue figure.*
