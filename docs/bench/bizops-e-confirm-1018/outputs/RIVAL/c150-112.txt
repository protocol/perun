**Regulatory Risk Assessment and Immediate Action Plan for GlobalNotary**

**1. Primary Regulatory Risks**
Your current setup presents a high risk of GDPR non-compliance. The core issue is not merely storing data in the US, but the legal mechanism governing that transfer.
*   **Invalid Transfer Mechanism:** Following the *Schrems II* ruling, standard US privacy laws are deemed insufficient to protect EU citizen data from US government surveillance. Relying solely on legacy "Safe Harbor" concepts or un-supplemented Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) is likely insufficient. You must implement SCCs (2021 version) coupled with a "Transfer Impact Assessment" (TIA) to prove that US law does not impinge on the specific protections promised in the SCCs.
*   **Data Sovereignty & Localization:** While GDPR does not explicitly mandate data localization (keeping data inside the EU), it mandates that the *level of protection* travels with the data. If a TIA concludes that US surveillance laws (like FISA 702) compromise the data, you are legally required to implement "supplementary measures" (e.g., strict encryption where the provider holds no keys) or cease the transfer.
*   **Conflicting Retention Laws:** This is your most acute operational risk. US state notary laws typically mandate retaining journals and recordings for 5 to 10 years. Conversely, GDPR's "storage limitation" principle requires data be kept only as long as necessary (often argued as 3–7 years for legal defense, but indefinite retention is prohibited). Storing data for 10 years to satisfy US law may violate GDPR unless a specific legal exemption is documented.

**2. Infrastructure Recommendations: To Move or Not to Move?**
You do not necessarily need to move your physical hosting to Europe, but you must alter how data is secured and managed.

*   **Option A: Remain in Virginia (With Supplementary Measures)**
    *   **Requirement:** Implement End-to-End Encryption (E2EE) where encryption keys are generated and held solely by your EU entity or a trusted third party, not by the US cloud provider (AWS/Azure/Google). This ensures that even if US authorities request data, the provider can only hand over unreadable ciphertext.
    *   **Action:** Execute a Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA) documenting why E2EE mitigates US surveillance risks. Update your Data Processing Agreements (DPAs) with the 2021 SCCs.

*   **Option B: Hybrid/Regional Hosting (Recommended for Risk Reduction)**
    *   **Requirement:** Configure your cloud infrastructure to create a "data residency" boundary. Store all video recordings and PII for EU clients on servers physically located in an EU region (e.g., AWS Frankfurt or Dublin), while keeping US client data in Virginia.
    *   **Benefit:** This eliminates the "cross-border transfer" risk for the most sensitive assets (video recordings), simplifying compliance significantly. Only metadata necessary for billing might cross borders, which is easier to justify legally.

**3. Immediate Next Steps**
1.  **Conduct a Data Mapping Audit:** Explicitly document what data flows from EU to US, who accesses it, and under what legal basis.
2.  **Execute a Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA):** This is now a mandatory document for any EU-US data flow. Do not skip this; it is your primary defense in an audit.
3.  **Resolve Retention Conflicts:** Consult legal counsel to define a retention schedule that satisfies the *longest* mandatory US state requirement while adhering to GDPR's "necessity" principle. You may need to archive (encrypt and lock) data rather than keep it actively accessible after the GDPR limitation period expires.
4.  **Update DPAs:** Ensure all vendor contracts include the new Standard Contractual Clauses.

*Note: This assessment identifies structural risks based on current frameworks; it does not constitute legal advice. You must engage qualified EU/US privacy counsel to finalize your Transfer Impact Assessment and retention policies.*
