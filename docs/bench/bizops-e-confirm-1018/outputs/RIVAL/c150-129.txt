### Updated Runway & Survival Scenarios for MediDispense

**Current Reality Check**
*   **Cash on Hand:** $600,000
*   **Monthly Burn:** $45,000
*   **Organic Runway:** 13.3 months
*   **Delay Duration:** 18 months
*   **Projected Deficit:** To survive the 18-month delay without revenue, you need $810,000 total ($45k × 18). You are currently short **$210,000**. If you include a buffer for launch costs, your stated need of $1.4M implies additional post-clearance scaling costs, but the immediate survival gap is $210k.

Below are three modeled scenarios to bridge this gap and present to your board.

#### Scenario A: Aggressive Cost Restructuring (Internal Only)
*Strategy: Reduce burn immediately to extend organic runway without external capital.*
*   **Action:** Implement a 20% reduction in monthly burn ($9,000/mo) via temporary executive salary deferrals, renegotiating vendor contracts, and pausing non-essential prototyping iterations. New burn: $36,000/mo.
*   **Math:** $600,000 / $36,000 = **16.6 months runway**.
*   **Outcome:** This covers 92% of the 18-month delay. You would still face a **$54,000 shortfall** in the final month.
*   **Risk:** High. Slowing prototyping may jeopardize the quality of the additional clinical data required by the FDA, potentially causing further delays.

#### Scenario B: Hybrid "Service-First" Pivot (Revenue Generation)
*Strategy: Monetize non-regulated software features immediately while hardware awaits clearance.*
*   **Action:** Launch a "MediDispense Connect" SaaS layer for facilities using *competitor* dispensers or manual processes. Offer medication adherence tracking, inventory analytics, and staff scheduling for $299/facility/month.
*   **Assumption:** Conservative sales of 15 facilities by Month 3, growing to 40 by Month 12. Average revenue ramp: $15,000/mo by Month 6.
*   **Math:**
    *   Months 1–6: Net burn remains ~$45k (Total cost: $270k).
    *   Months 7–18: Net burn reduces to ~$30k (Revenue offsets $15k). Total cost: $360k.
    *   **Total 18-month cost:** $630,000.
*   **Outcome:** You exceed your cash on hand by **$30,000**. This is nearly breakeven and validates the software value proposition for investors.
*   **Risk:** Moderate. Requires sales effort from an engineering-focused team; does not require FDA clearance.

#### Scenario C: Non-Dilutive Grant Bridge (External Capital)
*Strategy: Secure specific SBIR/STTR grants or healthcare innovation funds.*
*   **Action:** Apply for NIH SBIR Phase I/II grants targeting elderly care technology and medication safety. These often award $50k–$250k with no equity loss.
*   **Assumption:** Secure one $250,000 grant within 4 months.
*   **Math:**
    *   Starting Cash: $600,000
    *   Burn (Months 1–4): $180,000
    *   Remaining Cash: $420,000
    *   + Grant Injection: $250,000
    *   New Total: $670,000
    *   Remaining Duration (14 months): $670,000 / $45,000 = **14.9 months**.
*   **Outcome:** Combined with the initial 4 months, this covers the full **18.9 months**.
*   **Risk:** Low financial risk, high execution risk. Grant writing diverts engineering resources; approval is not guaranteed.

### Recommended Board Presentation Strategy
Present **Scenario B (Hybrid Pivot)** as the primary plan, supported by **Scenario C (Grants)** as an accelerator.
1.  **Immediate Action:** Announce the "Connect" SaaS pilot to generate early cash flow ($15k/mo potential).
2.  **Contingency:** Allocate one engineer to lead grant applications to cover the remaining $30k gap.
3.  **Avoid:** Pure cost-cutting (Scenario A) as it threatens the FDA response quality.

This approach demonstrates proactive revenue generation rather than passive survival, making the company more attractive for future fundraising once clearance is achieved.

*Note: These models assume no unexpected legal fees or major manufacturing cost spikes. Monitor cash weekly.*
*Ensure your "service-only" offering explicitly avoids medical claims that would trigger FDA scrutiny before clearance.*
