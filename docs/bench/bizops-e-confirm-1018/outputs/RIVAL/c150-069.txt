**Recommendation: Adopt a "Base Rate + Dynamic Airspace Surcharge" Model**

Do not build a buffer into a higher flat rate. Given your thin 15% margin and the extreme volatility of FAA waiver fees (300% spikes), a flat rate forces you to either overprice during normal conditions (losing bids to tractor services) or underprice during peak harvest (erasing profits). You must decouple your operational efficiency from regulatory volatility.

**Proposed Invoice Structure**

Structure your pricing to separate the value you deliver (crop coverage) from the variable regulatory cost (airspace access). This mirrors how logistics companies handle fuel surcharges.

1.  **Base Service Rate ($36–$38 per acre):**
    Set this slightly below your current $40 rate to remain competitive against traditional tractor spraying. This covers your drone depreciation, pilot labor, battery costs, and a standard profit margin under *normal* airspace conditions. This is the number you lead with in sales conversations to win the deal.

2.  **Dynamic Airspace Regulatory Fee (Variable per acre):**
    This is a line-item surcharge triggered only when FAA waiver costs exceed a defined baseline.
    *   **Baseline:** Define a "standard" waiver cost (e.g., the average cost during non-peak hours).
    *   **Trigger:** If the actual waiver cost for a specific flight window exceeds the baseline by more than 15%, the surcharge activates.
    *   **Calculation:** Pass 100% of the excess waiver cost directly to the customer, calculated per acre for that specific job.

**Why This Works for SkySpray**

*   **Protects Margins:** A single high-cost flight no longer wipes out three profitable ones. The customer paying for the peak harvest slot absorbs the specific regulatory cost of that timing.
*   **Maintains Competitiveness:** During 80% of the season (normal waiver costs), your effective price is lower than competitors because you aren't baking a massive safety buffer into every quote. You win more volume during standard windows.
*   **Fairness:** Farmers understand paying more for "rush" or "peak" services. If a farmer insists on spraying during peak congestion, they pay the premium. If they have flexibility to spray at night or off-peak, they enjoy the lower base rate.

**Implementation Strategy**

To prevent "sticker shock," transparency is critical. Do not hide the surcharge; frame it as a regulatory pass-through.

*   **Quote Format:** Provide quotes with two clear lines: "Base Application Fee" and "Estimated Regulatory Surcharge (subject to final FAA assessment)."
*   **Contract Language:** Include a clause stating that the final invoice will reflect the actual FAA waiver costs incurred at the time of operation, with a cap or estimate provided upfront if possible.
*   **Sales Script:** "Our base rate is lower than tractor services because we don't make you pay for peak-hour airspace fees unless you choose to fly during those times. You only pay the surge fee if you need us during high-congestion windows."

This approach shifts the risk of regulatory volatility from your balance sheet to the specific jobs that create the cost, aligning price directly with the conditions of service delivery.

*Note: Consider offering a "Peak Season Subscription" for large farms needing guaranteed coverage during harvest, where they pre-pay a block of acres at a blended rate to smooth their own cash flow.*
