**Regime and Routing:**
The relevant regimes are **contract law** (specifically the grant agreement between the foundation and the non-profit), **non-profit compliance regulations** (state and federal rules governing charitable solicitation and fund usage), and **professional ethical standards** for grant writers.

Many private foundations and government grant programs explicitly prohibit "contingency fees" (compensation based on a percentage of the grant award). The rationale is that grant funds are restricted for charitable purposes, and paying a vendor a percentage of the award can be viewed as an impermissible private benefit or an excessive administrative cost that violates the donor's intent. If a foundation's guidelines cap administrative costs at 15%, a 10% success fee paid directly from the grant award consumes a significant portion of that allowable limit, potentially causing the non-profit to breach the agreement if other administrative costs exist. Furthermore, major professional bodies, such as the Association of Fundraising Professionals (AFP), have codes of ethics that strongly discourage or forbid percentage-based compensation for grant writing, considering it a conflict of interest.
