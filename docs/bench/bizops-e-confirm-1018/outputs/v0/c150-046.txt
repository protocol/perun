**Regime Identification**
The relevant regimes are the **EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)**, which applies directly across the EU, and potentially specific national implementations or sectoral laws in **Germany** (e.g., BDSG) and **France** (e.g., CNIL guidelines) regarding security data or telecommunications.

**Key Legal Concepts to Discuss with Counsel**
You must engage a data privacy specialist to analyze the following before signing the hotel chain contract:

1.  **Intra-EU Data Transfers:** Since Ireland, Germany, and France are all within the European Economic Area (EEA), the GDPR generally permits the free flow of personal data between these member states without additional "cross-border transfer agreements" (like Standard Contractual Clauses used for non-EU transfers). However, you must verify if "access logs" containing entry times and durations constitute "personal data" under the specific interpretation of local supervisory authorities (they likely do, as they can be linked to identifiable guests).
2.  **Data Residency vs. Data Sovereignty:** While the GDPR harmonizes rules, some member states have specific "data localization" requirements for certain categories of sensitive data or public sector contracts. A professional must determine if guest access logs fall into any such restricted category in Germany or France that would mandate physical server location within those specific borders.
3.  **Controller vs. Processor Obligations:** Your contract with the hotel chain will likely define roles. If you are a processor, the contract must meet Article 28 GDPR requirements. If you are a joint controller, Article 26 applies. The "strict compliance assurance" requested by the hotel chain will require a formal legal opinion on your architecture.
4.  **Security of Processing:** Under Article 32 GDPR, you must implement appropriate technical measures. Whether server location in Ireland satisfies "appropriate" measures for German/French security data is a legal risk assessment, not an arithmetic one.
