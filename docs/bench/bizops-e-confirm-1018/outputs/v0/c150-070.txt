**Key Concepts to Discuss with Counsel:**
1.  **"Deemed Exports":** Under U.S. law, releasing controlled technology or source code to a foreign national *within* the United States is "deemed" an export to that person's home country. The critical legal determination your counsel must make is whether your utility partner's engineers who will access the data are considered "foreign nationals" under the regulations.
2.  **Ownership vs. Nationality:** You asked if the 40% foreign *ownership* triggers a review. Counsel must clarify that export controls generally focus on the citizenship or permanent residency status of the *individuals* accessing the technology, not solely the corporate ownership structure. However, foreign ownership can trigger other screening requirements (such as CFIUS) or influence the "end-user" assessment. Do not assume domestic incorporation exempts you if foreign nationals will access the data.
3.  **Classification of Data:** Your counsel must perform a formal classification of your "reactor thermal dynamics simulation data."
    *   **EAR:** Is the data classified under an Export Control Classification Number (ECCN), particularly those related to nuclear reactors (e.g., Category 0) or general high-performance computing/simulation?
    *   **Atomic Energy Act:** Data classified as "Restricted Data" (RD) or "Formerly Restricted Data" (FRD) regarding nuclear weapons or atomic energy is subject to strict prohibitions that differ significantly from standard commercial export controls. This is a high-risk area for nuclear digital twins.
4.  **Exemptions and Licenses:** If the data is controlled, your attorney must determine if an exemption applies (e.g., if the data is publicly available or fundamental research, though this is unlikely for proprietary maintenance simulations) or if a specific license is required before transfer.
