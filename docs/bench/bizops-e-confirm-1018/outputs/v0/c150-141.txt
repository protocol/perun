### Lane A: Arithmetic & Pricing Structure (Apply)

Your current unit economics show a tension between margin and retention. Below is the math applied to your inputs to compare the status quo against the proposed engineering fix, followed by a framework to evaluate your pricing model choice.

**1. Current State vs. Proposed Fix (Per-Check Model)**
*   **Current Revenue:** 45,000 checks × £2.50 = £112,500.
*   **Current Churn Impact:** 12% monthly churn implies losing ~5,400 checks worth of volume monthly if not replaced.
*   **Proposed Scenario (4% False Positive Rate):**
    *   **New Variable Cost:** Existing cost + £0.60 increase. (Note: Your base infrastructure cost per check is unknown; let's call it $C_{base}$).
    *   **New Contribution Margin per Check:** £2.50 - ($C_{base}$ + £0.60).
    *   **Break-Even Analysis on Churn Reduction:** To justify the £0.60 cost increase purely on volume retention, you must calculate the "Churn Cost." If reducing false positives from 8% to 4% lowers churn from 12% to a target rate (e.g., 6%), the retained volume revenue must exceed the added infrastructure cost.
    *   *Formula:* $\text{Required Volume Retention} = \frac{\text{Total Added Cost}}{\text{Price per Check}}$.
    *   *Application:* Total Added Cost = 45,000 × £0.60 = £27,000. You need to retain enough volume to generate >£27,000 in gross revenue to break even on the engineering spend alone, ignoring the lifetime value (LTV) benefit of lower churn.

**2. Pricing Model Evaluation (Apply, Don't Direct)**
You are deciding between **Per-Check** (transactional) and **Tiered Subscription** (volume-based).
*   **Per-Check with Penalty Clause:**
    *   *Math:* Revenue = (Volume × Price) - (Penalty Payouts).
    *   *Trade-off:* This aligns your cost with the operator's risk. If the penalty equals the operator's regulatory fine or administrative cost of manual review, your net revenue becomes highly sensitive to your accuracy rate.
    *   *Sensitivity:* If penalties exceed £2.50 per error, your unit economics turn negative on false positives.
*   **Tiered Subscription:**
    *   *Math:* Revenue = Fixed Fee + (Overage × Discounted Rate).
    *   *Trade-off:* Predictable revenue aids runway planning. However, if false positives cause churn, the fixed fee provides no buffer against cancellation.
    *   *Decision Aid:* If your primary value prop is "compliance safety," a tiered model including a guaranteed accuracy SLA (Service Level Agreement) often commands higher willingness-to-pay than raw transaction fees.

**Decision Thresholds & Next Step:**
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the cost of the engineering fix (£0.60/check) is less than the LTV lost per churned customer divided by the number of checks that customer generates, the fix is mathematically justified.
*   **This Week:** Instrument the exact cost of churn. Calculate the average number of checks a churned operator would have processed over 12 months. If (Checks × £2.50) > (£0.60 × Total Checks / Churn Reduction %), proceed with the engineering fix.
*   **Pricing Method:** Consider running a **Van Westendorp** survey with 5–10 key operator accounts asking: "At what price would a verification service with a 4% error rate be too expensive?" vs "too cheap?" to find the acceptable price band before locking a tiered structure.

*   **Action Required:** You must route this to a **UK-qualified attorney specializing in gambling regulation and commercial contracts**.
