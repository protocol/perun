### Lane A: Unit Economics & Cash Flow Application

Your core challenge is distinguishing between **Revenue Recognition** (accounting profit) and **Cash Flow** (liquidity). The 24-month hold on *client funds* (the $3,500 contract value) typically restricts the funeral home's access to that capital, not necessarily your SaaS subscription revenue, unless your contract terms explicitly tie your fees to the funeral home's realization of those funds.

**Assumption for this model:** Your $299/month subscription is billed and collectible immediately upon service delivery, independent of the funeral home's 24-month hold on their client's principal. If your fees are contingent on their fund release, your cash conversion cycle equals their hold period (24+ months), which fundamentally breaks standard SaaS unit economics.

**1. Standard SaaS Unit Economics (Ignoring the Hold)**
*   **Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR) per location:** $299
*   **Annual Contract Value (ACV):** $299 × 12 = $3,588
*   **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):** $1,200
*   **Payback Period:** $1,200 / $299 ≈ **4.0 months**
*   **LTV (Simple, assuming 3-year lifespan):** $3,588 × 3 = $10,764
*   **LTV:CAC Ratio:** $10,764 / $1,200 ≈ **9.0x**

**2. Impact of the 24-Month Hold on *Your* Operations**
The regulation holds the *funeral home's* client funds. It does not mathematically alter your LTV or CAC unless:
*   **Churn Risk Increases:** If funeral homes cannot access operating capital due to the hold, they may fail before paying you for 24 months.
*   **Billing Terms Change:** If you agree to defer billing until they access funds.

**Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) Adjustment:**
If you must wait for their liquidity event to get paid:
*   **CCC = Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) + Hold Period.**
*   If DSO is 30 days + 730 days (24 months) = **760 days**.
*   **Result:** You fund 2 years of service delivery before seeing cash. Your "Effective CAC" becomes the present value of $1,200 spent today against revenue received in Year 3.
*   **Formula for Effective LTV (Discounted):** $\sum \frac{Revenue_t}{(1+r)^t}$ where $t$ starts at month 25. This drastically reduces real value compared to the nominal $10,764.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Read:** If your contract allows immediate billing, the 24-month hold is a *customer risk factor* (churn), not a direct math modifier to your LTV formula. If your billing is deferred, your business model requires 24 months of runway per customer before positive cash flow.
*   **Flip Input:** The critical variable is **Billing Trigger**. If "Billing Trigger = Contract Signed," use standard SaaS math. If "Billing Trigger = Fund Release," your payback period is >24 months.
*   **Threshold:** If payback > 12 months, standard VC growth models fail; you need debt financing or upfront annual prepayments.
*   **Next Step:** Audit your Master Services Agreement. If billing is immediate, model churn sensitivity assuming higher failure rates for clients under capital constraints.

### Lane R: Regulatory & Reporting Routing

**Regime Identified:** State-specific Funeral Service Regulations (FL & OH) and US GAAP/ASC 606 (Revenue Recognition).

**Route to Professional (CPA specializing in Funeral Service):**
You must engage a CPA to determine:
1.  **Revenue Recognition Timing:** Does ASC 606 require *you* to defer recognizing your own revenue if the underlying transaction (the funeral contract) is regulated? While unlikely for a pure SaaS vendor, if you are deemed a "participant" in the trust arrangement, rules change.
2.  **Trust Accounting Compliance:** Verify that your software's handling of these funds (even if just tracking) does not inadvertently make you a fiduciary or co-trustee under FL/OH law, which would trigger severe licensing and bonding requirements.
3.  **Sales Tax Nexus:** Pre-need contracts often have specific sales tax exemptions or deferrals in FL and OH. Your invoicing logic must align with these state-specific exemptions to prevent your clients from facing audits.

**Do not conclude** internally whether your software configuration satisfies "trust accounting" standards; this is a legal determination. Bring your data schema and billing logic to counsel before finalizing the architecture.

*Note: The math above assumes your $299 fee is not legally restricted by the same hold as the client's principal. Confirm this separation immediately.*
*Verification of specific FL/OH statutory hold periods should be confirmed with local counsel before hard-coding logic.*
