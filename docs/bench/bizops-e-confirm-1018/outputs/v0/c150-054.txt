*   **Your Inputs:** You have the "Turnover" variable ($30k/mo EU), but you lack the legal variables (Nature, Intent, Mitigation).
*   **Result:** Any percentage you apply to revenue (e.g., "reserve 10% of EU sales") is a fabricated input, not a derived figure.

### Lane R: Regulation & Insurance (Route to Professional)
You asked about GDPR fine calculation methods, specific insurance products for regulatory fines, and whether to use a fixed percentage reserve.

**Regime Named:** General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) (EU) 2016/679.
**Routing Required:**
1.  **Fine Calculation:** The 4% figure is a **maximum statutory cap**, not an expected cost or a probability-weighted liability. Regulators determine fines based on specific infringements (e.g., lack of consent vs. poor security). **Do not** create a financial reserve based on a percentage of revenue; this is not an accepted accounting standard for contingent liabilities without a specific legal threat. A qualified EU privacy counsel must assess your specific data flows (genetic data is "special category" data under Art. 9, carrying higher risk) to estimate exposure.
2.  **Insurance Coverage:** Whether insurance policies cover regulatory fines is a matter of contract law and local jurisdiction. Many D&O or Cyber Liability policies **explicitly exclude** regulatory fines because insuring against penalties violates public policy in some jurisdictions. Only a licensed insurance broker specializing in EU cyber risk can confirm if a product exists that covers legal defense vs. the fine itself.
3.  **Reserve Methodology:** Accounting standards (US GAAP/IFRS) generally require reserves for "probable and estimable" losses. Since GDPR fines are punitive and not "probable" if you are compliant, auditors may reject a blanket "fine reserve." A CPA and legal counsel must define the correct accounting treatment.

**Action:** Engage EU privacy counsel before collecting any EU genetic data. Genetic data triggers heightened obligations. Do not launch until the "design-time triage" confirms your engineering controls meet the regime's requirements.
