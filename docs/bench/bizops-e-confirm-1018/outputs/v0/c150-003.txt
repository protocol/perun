### Lane A: Unit Economics Application

Your current arithmetic confirms your concern: with the inputs provided, the unit economics are negative.

**1. Current State Calculation**
*   **Revenue per User (Current LTV):** $49/month × 3 months = **$147**
*   **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC):** **$65**
*   **Gross Margin per User:** $147 - $65 = **$82**
*   **Variable Costs:** You noted server/API costs are $4,000/month for 1,200 users (~$3.33/user/month). Over 3 months, this is ~$10.
*   **Contribution Margin per User:** $82 (gross) - $10 (variable) = **$72**.

*Analysis:* While the contribution margin per user is positive ($72), this must cover your fixed costs (team salaries, overhead).
*   **Total Monthly Contribution:** 400 new users needed to replace churn (1,200 users / 3 months) × $72 = **$28,800**.
*   **Total Fixed Burn:** $18,000 (total burn) - $4,000 (server costs) = **$14,000** (approximate fixed labor/ops).
*   **Net Result:** $28,800 (inflow) - $14,000 (fixed) = **+$14,800 surplus**.

*Correction on your premise:* Based strictly on the numbers you provided, you are **not** losing money on every acquisition if you maintain 1,200 active users. The issue is likely cash flow timing or the assumption that *all* 1,200 users are new every month. If your 1,200 users are a steady state, your inflow covers your burn. However, if you are trying to *grow*, the LTV:CAC ratio is the constraint.

**2. Modeling the "Re-subscription" Question**
There is no standard accounting method to automatically count a customer who *might* return in two years as active LTV today.
*   **Conservative Approach:** Treat them as one-time customers (LTV = $147). Any future re-subscription is treated as a new acquisition with a new CAC.
*   **Probabilistic Approach:** If you have data showing X% of users return, you can model: $LTV = (Initial Cycle Value) + (Return Rate \% × Discounted Future Value)$. Without historical data on returns, this input is **UNVERIFIED**. Do not fabricate a return rate.

**3. Sensitivity Analysis (The Decision Framework)**
To improve unit economics, apply these formulas to your specific levers:

*   **Levers to Pull:**
    *   **Increase Price (P):** New LTV = $(P + \Delta) \times 3$.
    *   **Extend Duration (M):** New LTV = $49 \times (3 + \Delta)$.
    *   **Lower CAC:** Directly increases contribution margin.

*   **Break-Even Thresholds:**
    *   If your goal is an LTV:CAC ratio of 3:1 (standard SaaS benchmark), you need an LTV of $195 ($65 × 3).
    *   **Option A (Price):** You would need to charge **$65/month** ($195 / 3 months).
    *   **Option B (Duration):** You would need to retain users for **4 months** ($195 / $49).
    *   **Option C (CAC):** You would need to lower CAC to **$49** to hit a 3:1 ratio on current LTV.
