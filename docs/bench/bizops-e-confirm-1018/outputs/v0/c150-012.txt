### Unit Economics & Break-Even Analysis

**1. Current Cost Structure (Baseline)**
*   **Current Selling Price:** $1,200
*   **Current Gross Margin:** 40%
*   **Current Cost of Goods Sold (COGS):** $1,200 × (1 - 0.40) = $720 per carat.
*   **Current Energy Cost:** 4,500 kWh × $0.12 = $540 per carat.
*   **Implied Non-Energy Costs:** $720 (Total COGS) - $540 (Energy) = $180 per carat.

**2. Summer Scenario Costs (Mixed Shifts)**
You specified a constraint where only 60% of production can shift to off-peak (night) rates, while 40% must occur during peak (day) rates.
*   **Off-Peak Energy Cost (60% volume):** $0.12 × 4,500 kWh = $540 per carat.
*   **Peak Energy Cost (40% volume):** $0.24 × 4,500 kWh = $1,080 per carat.
*   **Weighted Average Energy Cost:** (0.60 × $540) + (0.40 × $1,080) = $324 + $432 = **$756 per carat**.
*   **New Total COGS:** $756 (Weighted Energy) + $180 (Non-Energy) = **$936 per carat**.

**3. Break-Even Price for Target Margin**
To maintain a **25% gross margin** with the new COGS:
*   **Formula:** `Break-Even Price = COGS ÷ (1 - Target Margin)`
*   **Calculation:** $936 ÷ (1 - 0.25) = $936 ÷ 0.75 = **$1,248 per carat**.

**Result Read:** Your required retail price increases from $1,200 to **$1,248** to maintain a 25% margin under the 60/40 split scenario. This is a 4% price increase. If you cannot pass this increase to customers, your margin will drop below 25%.

**Sensitivity Check (The Flip Input):**
The variable that most drastically changes this outcome is the **percentage of off-peak production**.
*   If you achieve 100% off-peak: Energy = $540; Total COGS = $720; Price for 25% margin = $960.
*   If you are forced to 0% off-peak: Energy = $1,080; Total COGS = $1,260; You cannot achieve a 25% margin at the current $1,200 price point (you would operate at a loss).

**Decision Aid & Next Step:**
*   **Threshold Rule:** If market research indicates customers are unwilling to pay >$1,248, you must prioritize increasing off-peak capacity beyond 60% or reduce non-energy costs.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, instrument a test listing at $1,248 for a small batch of summer inventory to measure conversion rate elasticity before updating the full catalog.
