### Applied Pricing Analysis for SkySpray

Your current flat-rate model ($40/acre) fails because it averages a volatile cost (FAA waivers) that spikes 300% during peak demand. With a 15% average margin, a single peak-cost flight erodes the profit of three normal flights. The arithmetic dictates you must decouple the volatile regulatory cost from your base service fee.

**Option A: Dynamic Base + Congestion Surcharge (Recommended Structure)**
This method applies cost-plus logic only to the volatile component while keeping the base rate competitive against tractor services.

*   **Formula:** `Invoice = (Base Rate × Acres) + (Actual Waiver Cost × Acres)`
*   **Application:**
    1.  **Set Base Rate:** Calculate your average non-waiver costs (drone depreciation, pilot labor, battery, standard maintenance) plus your target margin. If your total average cost is $34/acre and the *average* waiver is $4, your non-waiver cost is $30. To achieve a 15% margin on the service excluding the pass-through waiver: `Base Rate = $30 / (1 - 0.15) ≈ $35.29/acre`.
    2.  **Pass-Through Waiver:** Invoice the actual FAA waiver fee incurred for that specific flight time/zone as a separate line item.
*   **Result:** Farmers see a stable base rate (~$35) comparable to competitors. The "surcharge" varies but is transparently tied to a third-party regulatory fee, not your markup.

**Option B: Higher Flat Rate with Buffer**
This method attempts to average the peak costs into a single number.

*   **Formula:** `Flat Rate = (Avg Cost + Peak Variance Buffer) / (1 - Target Margin)`
*   **Risk:** To cover a 300% spike without losing money on peak flights, you must inflate the rate significantly. If peak waivers turn a profitable flight into a loss, the buffer required might push your flat rate to $55+/acre.
*   **Result:** This likely prices you out of the market during off-peak times when farmers can choose cheaper tractor options, as you are overcharging them to subsidize peak volatility.

**Decision Aid & Sensitivity**
*   **The Flip Input:** The decision hinges on **price elasticity**. If farmers reject line-item variability, Option A fails regardless of the math. However, B2B agricultural clients often accept "fuel surcharges" or "regulatory fees" if framed correctly.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If the waiver cost exceeds 20% of the total invoice price on any given day, a flat rate model mathematically requires a base price increase that likely exceeds competitor benchmarks.
*   **Read:** Option A preserves your 15% margin on every flight without inflating the base price. Option B sacrifices volume during low-congestion periods to protect against rare high-cost events.
