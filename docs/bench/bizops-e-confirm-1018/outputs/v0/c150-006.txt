### Lane A: Unit Economics & Cash Gap Analysis

You can model the "true" contribution margin and the cash gap by separating **accrual economics** (profitability per unit) from **cash flow timing** (liquidity).

**1. Calculate Current Contribution Margin (Accrual Basis)**
This tells you if the business model is profitable ignoring timing.
*   **Revenue per unit:** €8,000
*   **Variable Cost (New):** €1,800 (Polymer) + *Other variable costs (unknown)*.
*   **Contribution Margin (CM) per unit:** €8,000 - €1,800 = **€6,200** (assuming no other variable costs).
*   **Total Monthly Contribution:** 50 units × €6,200 = **€310,000**.
*   **Fixed Costs:** €60,000 (Staff) + *Overhead (unknown)*.
*   **Estimated Monthly Net Burn/Profit:** €310,000 - €60,000 = **+€250,000** (Profitable on paper).

**2. Model the Cash Gap (The 6-Month Lag)**
The risk is not profitability, but liquidity. You are paying €1,800 today but getting reimbursed based on the old €1,200 cost structure (or a fixed rate) for 6 months.
*   **Extra Cost Incurred Monthly:** (€1,800 - €1,200) × 50 units = **€30,000/month**.
*   **Cumulative Cash Drain over 6 Months:** €30,000 × 6 = **€180,000**.
*   **Runway Impact:** Your €350,000 reserve covers this €180,000 gap with €170,000 remaining, *provided* no other cash outflows exceed inflows during this period.

**Formula for Cash Runway with Lag:**
`Adjusted Runway = Cash Reserve ÷ (Normal Monthly Burn + (Cost Increase per Unit × Volume))`
*Note: This assumes the reimbursement amount stays fixed at the lower rate for the lag period.*

**3. Decision Aid & Sensitivity**
*   **The Flip Input:** The break-even point flips if the cumulative cash drain exceeds your reserve before reimbursement adjusts. If the lag extends beyond ~11 months (€350k ÷ €30k), you deplete cash.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If your cash reserve covers less than 1.5x the expected lag duration, liquidity is critical. You currently cover ~11.6 months of *only* the inflation gap.
*   **Next Step:** Instrument a weekly cash flow forecast. Input the exact reimbursement date vs. material payment date. If the gap widens, consider **hedging** (locking in material prices) rather than pausing production, as your unit economics (€6,200 CM) remain strongly positive. Pausing production stops revenue entirely while fixed costs (staff) likely continue, accelerating cash burn.
