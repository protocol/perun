**Regime Identification:**
The relevant regulatory body is the **US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)** under the **Investment Advisers Act of 1940**. The core legal determination hinges on whether your algorithm's activity constitutes providing "investment advice" for compensation and whether the automation implies you are exercising "discretion" over client assets.

**Key Legal Determinations Required (Do Not Conclude Here):**
1.  **Definition of Investment Advice:** The SEC has issued guidance on whether automated platforms (robo-advisors) constitute investment advisors. Generally, if the software provides specific investment recommendations or manages assets based on a client's objectives, it may trigger registration, regardless of whether a human or an algorithm makes the trade.
2.  **Discretion vs. Execution:** A critical legal test is whether your platform exercises discretion. Even if users deposit funds generally, if the algorithm decides *which* protocols to shift funds into and *when* (every six hours) without specific per-trade user consent, regulators may view this as discretionary management.
3.  **The "DeFi" Exemption Misconception:** There is currently **no explicit statutory exemption** in the Investment Advisers Act solely for protocols that interact only with decentralized smart contracts. The SEC has increasingly taken the position that the underlying technology (blockchain/smart contracts) does not automatically exempt an entity from securities laws if the economic function matches that of a regulated intermediary.
4.  **Assets Under Management (AUM) Thresholds:** While there are federal registration thresholds (often $100 million AUM for full SEC registration, with state registration required below that), the *obligation to register* (either federally or at the state level) begins once you meet the definition of an investment advisor, regardless of the $12 million figure. The $12 million figure likely subjects you to **state-level** registration requirements if deemed an advisor, rather than exempting you entirely.
