**Lane R: Regulatory Classification (Route Immediately)**
Your question regarding whether the translation feature triggers new FDA clearance or falls under "general wellness" is a **Lane R** matter. It depends entirely on legal and regulatory determinations by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA).
*   **The Regime:** U.S. Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act (FD&C Act) and FDA guidance on "Software as a Medical Device" (SaMD) vs. General Wellness products.
*   **The Risk:** If the FDA determines your translation feature modifies the intended use of the cleared hardware (e.g., implying it treats a condition beyond hearing loss or creates a new diagnostic capability), you may be marketing an unapproved device. This carries risks of warning letters, seizures, or injunctions.
*   **The Route:** You must consult a regulatory attorney specializing in medical devices before making a public claim about the feature's classification. Do not conclude internally that this is "general wellness." The distinction often hinges on specific claims made in your marketing and labeling, not just the code's function.
*   **Action:** Do not pause sales automatically based on this chat, but **do not launch the new feature publicly** until counsel reviews your specific intended use statements and labeling against current FDA guidance.

    Formula: $1 \div \text{Churn Rate}$
    Calculation: $1 \div 0.04 = 25 \text{ months}$
*   **Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) - Revenue Only:**
    Formula: $\text{ARPU} \times \text{Lifespan}$
