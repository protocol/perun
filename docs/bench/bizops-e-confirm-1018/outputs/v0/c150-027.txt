Your question regarding whether your simulation data falls under the International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) or the Export Administration Regulations (EAR) is a regulation-dependent determination that cannot be resolved through arithmetic or general guidelines. You must engage a qualified export compliance attorney before enabling data sharing with partners in Japan and the UK.

**Why this requires legal counsel (The Regime):**
The distinction between ITAR (22 C.F.R. Parts 120–130) and EAR (15 C.F.R. Parts 730–774) hinges on specific legal classifications that only counsel can verify against your software's functionality and data outputs:

1.  **The "Defense Article" Test:** ITAR controls technical data related to items on the United States Munitions List (USML). Even if your data (layout maps, throughput speeds) appears non-classified, if it reveals specific performance parameters or design details of defense articles used by your defense contractor clients, it may be deemed a "defense service" or technical data under ITAR. The involvement of defense contractors does not *automatically* flag all data, but it significantly increases the risk that seemingly commercial logistics data could be interpreted as relating to defense articles.
2.  **Dual-Use Complications:** You mentioned warehouses store "dual-use goods." Under the EAR, items and data can be controlled for reasons including national security, even if not on the USML. Determining the correct Export Control Classification Number (ECCN) for your software and the specific data exports requires a formal commodity jurisdiction (CJ) request or a formal legal opinion.
3.  **Deemed Exports:** Sharing data with foreign nationals (even within the US) or transmitting data abroad to partners in Japan and the UK constitutes an "export." Penalties for inadvertent ITAR violations are severe, including massive fines and debarment, which aligns with your concern about bankruptcy.

**Actionable Next Steps (Before the Call):**
Do not attempt a self-assessment for ITAR/EAR status; the cost of error is too high. Instead, prepare the following for your attorney to expedite the engagement:
*   **Data Samples:** Anonymized examples of the exact simulation outputs (maps, speed metrics, inventory logs) you intend to share.
*   **Client Contracts:** Redacted copies of agreements with your defense clients to review any flow-down clauses regarding data handling or export compliance.
*   **Software Architecture:** A brief description of how the software generates these simulations (e.g., does it ingest classified inputs? Does it model specific military capabilities?).
*   **Destination Details:** The specific entities in Japan and the UK receiving the data.

There is no safe "self-assessment path" for ITAR applicability that protects you from liability if you guess incorrectly. The presence of defense contractors and dual-use goods creates a high-risk profile where a formal legal opinion is the only acceptable mitigation strategy.
