### Lane R: Regulation-Dependent Accounting & Financing (Route Immediately)

Your questions regarding inventory valuation, capitalization vs. expensing, and debt financing eligibility are **Lane R** matters. They depend entirely on specific accounting standards (US GAAP ASC 330, ASC 730, ASC 450) and regulatory interpretations that a non-licensed assistant cannot apply to your specific facts without risking material misstatement.

**1. Inventory Valuation & Capitalization Policy**
*   **The Regime:** US GAAP (specifically ASC 330 for Inventory and ASC 730 for Research & Development).
*   **The Issue:** Generally, R&D costs are expensed as incurred. However, materials and equipment with alternative future uses may be capitalized. For custom enzymes awaiting FDA approval, the critical determination is whether the inventory has "probable future economic benefit" and if the delay indicates "obsolescence" or "impairment."
*   **The Risk:** If the FDA delay suggests the product may not be sellable or will require significant rework, GAAP may require you to write down the inventory to its net realizable value (NRV) immediately. Continuing to capitalize costs on a stalled regulatory path could be viewed as non-compliant.
*   **Action:** Do not decide this internally. Present the FDA guidance timeline and the specific nature of the "custom" enzymes to your auditors. Ask them to define the "trigger point" where capitalization must cease and impairment testing must begin under current biotech precedents.

**2. Debt Financing Against Inventory**
*   **The Regime:** Commercial lending standards and UCC (Uniform Commercial Code) Article 9 regarding collateral eligibility.
*   **The Issue:** Lenders typically advance funds against inventory based on a "borrowing base" calculation, often excluding R&D work-in-progress that lacks a ready market or clear liquidation value. An FDA delay exceeding 12 months significantly increases the perceived risk of obsolescence, likely reducing the advance rate to zero or classifying the inventory as ineligible collateral.
*   **Action:** Route this to a specialized biotech lender or your CFO's financial advisor. Do not assume the $320,000 book value translates to borrowing power.

**3. Investor Communication Strategy**
*   **The Principle:** Transparency regarding regulatory headwinds is preferred over optimism that conflicts with GAAP constraints.
*   **Routing:** Have your legal counsel and auditors review any investor deck language describing the inventory. Framing the delay as a temporary hold while maintaining full asset value without an auditor's sign-off on the impairment status could signal non-compliance or distress if discovered later.
