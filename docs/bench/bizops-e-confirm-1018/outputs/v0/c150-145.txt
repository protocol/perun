### Lane A: Unit Economics Analysis (Apply, Do Not Direct)

To decide between lowering price or investing in gamification, we must model the impact on **Contribution Margin** and **Lifetime Value (LTV)** using your provided numbers.

**1. The Baseline (Current State)**
*   **Revenue per Seat (Year 1):** €3,200 (bundle) + €800 (license) = €4,000.
*   **Effective Cost per Completed Training:** Since only 40% complete the course, the effective cost per *successful* outcome is double the intended cost.
*   **At-Risk Revenue:** 800 seats × €800/year = €640,000 Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR).

**2. Scenario A: Lowering Price to Reflect Completion**
If you lower the price to match the "half-value" perception caused by 40% completion:
*   **Hypothetical Price Adjustment:** Reduce license fee by 50% (€400) or bundle discount.
*   **Formula:** `New Margin = (New Price - Variable Costs) × Volume`.
*   **Impact:** You immediately lock in a 50% reduction in LTV per user. Unless this price cut increases volume significantly beyond the existing 800 seats (which are already contracted/negotiating), you are voluntarily reducing revenue without fixing the root cause (engagement).
*   **Sensitivity:** If churn increases because the product is perceived as "cheap/low quality," LTV drops further.

**3. Scenario B: Investing in Gamification (Fixing Engagement)**
*   **Goal:** Move completion from 40% → 70% (industry standard).
*   **Value Unlock:** At 70% completion, the "cost per completed hour" for the client drops, justifying the current €800 fee.
*   **Break-Even Calculation for Development:**
    *   `Max Dev Spend = (Retained ARR × Expected Lifetime) - (Current Risked ARR × Expected Lifetime)`.
    *   If retaining the 800 seats (€640k ARR) for just **one year** depends on this fix, you can theoretically spend up to €640k to build the feature (though practically, you want payback <12 months).
    *   **Decision Threshold:** If the gamification build costs less than the net present value of the retained €640k ARR, the arithmetic favors building.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Read:** Lowering price treats the symptom and reduces LTV permanently; fixing engagement restores the value metric.
*   **Flip Input:** The specific cost of the gamification development. If `Dev Cost < €640,000` (one year of at-risk revenue), the math supports building.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Do not discount a B2B product for a usage problem unless the marginal cost of serving the user exceeds the marginal revenue. Here, the issue is efficacy, not marginal cost.
*   **Next Step:** Estimate the gamification build cost. If it is under €50k–€100k, consider proposing a pilot milestone: "We will build X feature; if completion rises to 60% in Q3, the full rollout proceeds at current pricing."
