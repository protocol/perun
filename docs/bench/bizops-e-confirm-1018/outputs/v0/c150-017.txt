### Customer Payback Analysis (Lane A: Arithmetic)

To determine the payback period, we must compare the customer's upfront cost against their annual savings. Since you provided the reduction in *output* (60%) but not the *monetary value* of that energy, the calculation below uses a variable for the ideal annual energy value ($V_{ideal}$).

**1. The Formula**
$$ \text{Payback Years} = \frac{\text{Unit Price}}{\text{Annual Energy Value} \times \text{Operating Factor}} $$

*   **Unit Price:** €14,000
*   **Operating Factor (Ideal):** 1.0 (24-hour operation)
*   **Operating Factor (Restricted):** 0.4 (A 60% reduction leaves 40% of original output)

**2. Applied Scenarios**
Let $V_{ideal}$ be the annual value of energy produced if the turbine ran 24/7.

*   **Ideal Scenario (24-hour):**
    $$ \text{Years} = \frac{14,000}{V_{ideal}} $$
*   **Restricted Scenario (10-hour/day):**
    $$ \text{Years} = \frac{14,000}{V_{ideal} \times 0.4} = \frac{35,000}{V_{ideal}} $$

**The Impact:** The operating restriction increases the payback period by **2.5 times** (the inverse of 0.4). If the ideal payback was 4 years, the restricted payback becomes 10 years.

**3. Decision Aid & Thresholds**
*   **Read:** The viability of the product hinges entirely on $V_{ideal}$. If German residential electricity prices are roughly €0.40/kWh, you must calculate the specific kWh yield of your turbine to fill this slot.
*   **Flip Input:** The break-even threshold for mass adoption is often considered <7 years for residential hardware. If $\frac{35,000}{V_{ideal}} > 7$, then $V_{ideal}$ must exceed €5,000/year for the product to remain viable under restrictions.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the restricted payback exceeds the product's expected lifespan (e.g., 15–20 years), the unit economics are likely unworkable for mass adoption without subsidies or price reduction.

### Unknown Variables & Sales Material Risks

You correctly identified seasonal daylight variations as a critical unknown. In Northern Europe (Germany), daylight hours fluctuate drastically:
*   **Winter:** ~8 hours of daylight (further reducing the already capped 10-hour window if wind patterns correlate with night hours).
*   **Summer:** ~16+ hours of daylight (allowing full utilization of the 10-hour allowed window, but potentially lower wind speeds).

**Risk:** If your sales materials assume an average of 10 hours year-round, you may be overestimating winter output. A conservative estimate should weight winter months heavily, as heating demand (and thus energy value) is highest when daylight (and allowed operating time) is lowest.

### Company Break-Even Note
Your company's fixed costs (€40,000/month) and manufacturing costs (€8,000/unit) determine *your* profitability, not the customer's payback.
*   **Contribution Margin:** €14,000 - €8,000 = €6,000 per unit.
*   **Company Monthly Break-Even:** €40,000 / €6,000 = **~7 units per month**.
You need to sell 7 units monthly to cover fixed costs, regardless of the customer's operational restrictions. However, if the customer payback period becomes unattractive (due to the restrictions), sales volume will likely drop below this 7-unit threshold, threatening company viability.
