**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent Analysis (Route to Counsel)**

Your question regarding whether a flat software fee violates ethical billing rules is a **Lane R** matter. It depends entirely on specific state bar rules, the interpretation of "fee sharing" between lawyers and non-lawyers, and the distinction between a technology vendor and a legal service provider. I cannot conclude whether your current model is compliant or if you must switch to tiered pricing.

**Regime Identification:**
The relevant regimes are:
1.  **State Rules of Professional Conduct:** Specifically rules governing lawyer fees (reasonableness), fee sharing with non-lawyers (often prohibited), and the supervision of non-lawyer assistants.
2.  **Unauthorized Practice of Law (UPL) Statutes:** Determining if your pricing or service structure implies you are practicing law or setting legal fees.
3.  **Vendor Contract Law:** How your Master Services Agreement (MSA) characterizes the transaction (software license vs. service fee).

**Actionable Preparation for Counsel:**
To get a definitive answer quickly, prepare the following for an attorney specializing in legal ethics or professional responsibility:
*   **The Distinction Argument:** Draft a clear statement distinguishing your $2,500 charge as a **technology license fee** paid by the firm for software access, explicitly *not* a pass-through cost to the client nor a share of the legal fee. The core legal question is whether bars view a flat-rate tool that reduces lawyer hours as "reasonable" or if it triggers fee-sharing concerns because the lawyer's cost basis varies.
*   **Current Contract Language:** Provide your current MSA. Counsel needs to verify if the language explicitly states the fee is for "software usage" regardless of outcome, and that the law firm retains sole discretion over their billing to their end-clients.
*   **The "Reasonableness" Inquiry Details:** Share the exact wording of the bar association's inquiry. Was it a formal ethics opinion request or an informal comment? The jurisdiction matters critically here (e.g., California vs. New York rules differ).
*   **Time-Sensitive Item:** If the bar inquiry is active, determine the response deadline immediately. Do not change your pricing model until counsel confirms the risk, as switching to hourly billing prematurely could admit liability or alter your commercial terms unnecessarily.
