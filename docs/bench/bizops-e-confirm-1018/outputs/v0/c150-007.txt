### Lane R: Regulation-Dependent Liability & Counsel Routing

Your situation involves a complex intersection of **maritime law**, **conflict of laws** (jurisdictional disputes between Florida, the Bahamas, and your entity's location), and **platform liability**. **1. The Legal Regimes Involved**
You are facing a multi-jurisdictional conflict where three distinct legal bodies may claim authority:
*   **Florida State & U.S. Federal Law:** As the location of the boat owner and potentially your platform's incorporation, U.S. laws regarding marketplace liability and consumer protection apply.
*   **Bahamian Maritime Law:** As the *lex loci delicti* (place where the tort occurred), Bahamian law likely governs the substantive rules of the accident, navigation rights, and damage assessment.
*   **Admiralty/Maritime Jurisdiction:** Because the incident occurred on navigable waters, this may fall under federal admiralty jurisdiction, which has unique procedural rules and liability caps distinct from standard land-based torts.

The core legal question is whether your platform is classified as a mere **payment processor/intermediary** (generally shielded from liability if terms are robust) or an **operator/broker** (potentially liable for negligence in vetting or insurance requirements). The fact that you process payments does not automatically create liability, but it does establish a nexus that plaintiffs will use to drag you into the suit.

You must retain legal representation immediately. Do not rely on general business counsel. You need a law firm or consortium with specific expertise in:
*   **Maritime/Admiralty Litigation:** Specifically attorneys licensed to practice in **Bahamian courts** and familiar with the Bahamas Merchant Shipping Act and local tort precedents.

*   **Insurance Coverage Counsel:** To interpret your current general liability and errors & omissions (E&O) policies regarding cross-border maritime incidents and "insured vs. insured" exclusions.
