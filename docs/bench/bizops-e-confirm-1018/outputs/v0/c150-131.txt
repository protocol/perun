### The Regulatory Boundary (Route to Counsel)
You must engage an attorney specializing in occupational safety and regulatory compliance before marketing your certification as "OSHA-compliant."
*   **The Regime:** Federal OSHA (29 CFR) sets performance standards for training but generally does not "certify" specific vendors or digital platforms. However, claiming your course meets these standards is a legal assertion. If a worker trained by you fails an inspection or is injured, OSHA or a plaintiff's attorney will scrutinize whether your curriculum, assessment, and VR methodology legally satisfied the specific CFR requirement (e.g., HAZWOPER 29 CFR 1910.120).
*   **The Risk:** If you assert federal recognition without explicit legal validation, you expose HazardSim VR to liability for misrepresentation. If a trained worker fails an official inspection, your internal records may be deemed insufficient if the training method (VR) is not explicitly accepted by the local OSHA Area Director or if the course content misses a mandatory element.
*   **Action:** Do not market the output as a "federally recognized certification" until counsel reviews your curriculum against the specific CFR codes and advises on the "competency" vs. "certification" distinction in Texas.

### Internal vs. External Steps (What You Can Do Now)
