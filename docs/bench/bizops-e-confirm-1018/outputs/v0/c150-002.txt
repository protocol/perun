### Scenario Modeling: Price Increase vs. Volume Decline

**Current Baseline (Pre-Increase)**
*   **Revenue:** 50,000 units × $0.12 = $6,000
*   **COGS:** 50,000 units × $0.09 = $4,500
*   **Gross Profit:** $1,500
*   **Net Cash Flow:** $1,500 (Gross) - $25,000 (Burn) = **-$23,500**
*   **Runway:** $120,000 / $23,500 ≈ **5.1 months**

**New Cost Structure**
Your raw material costs rose 20%. Assuming materials are the primary component of your $0.09 cost, we must adjust the unit cost. If we assume the entire $0.09 is variable material cost (worst-case for margin):
*   **New Unit Cost:** $0.09 × 1.20 = **$0.108**
*   **New Price:** $0.12 × 1.15 = **$0.138**
*   **New Unit Margin:** $0.138 - $0.108 = **$0.03** (Previously $0.03)

*Note: Your unit margin remains flat at $0.03 only if the entire $0.09 cost was variable material. If some costs were fixed labor/overhead included in that $0.09, your margin might improve slightly. We will proceed using the $0.03 margin assumption to be conservative.*

**Scenario Analysis (Monthly Cash Flow & Runway)**
*Formula: Cash Flow = (Units × $0.03 Margin) - $25,000 Fixed Burn*
*Formula: Runway = $120,000 / |Net Cash Flow|*

**Analysis of Results**
1.  **One-line read:** Under the assumption that your entire unit cost is variable material, the price increase fails to offset the cost hike on a per-unit basis, leaving your margin unchanged; therefore, any volume loss strictly accelerates your cash burn and shortens runway.
2.  **The Flip Input:** The variable that flips this answer is the **composition of your $0.09 cost**. If less than 100% of that cost is raw material (e.g., if $0.02 is labor/overhead that doesn't rise with corn starch prices), your new unit cost would be lower than $0.108, creating actual margin expansion.
    *   *Break-even Volume Calculation:* To maintain the current -$23,500 burn rate with the new $0.03 margin, you need $1,500 gross profit. $1,500 / $0.03 = 50,000 units. You cannot afford *any* volume loss if the margin is truly flat.
3.  **Decision Thresholds:**
    *   If **LTV:CAC** logic applies here, you are currently burning cash to serve customers. A rule of thumb is that if a price increase causes volume to drop such that total gross profit dollars decrease, the move is mathematically destructive unless it stops a bleeding cost trend.
    *   Here, because the unit margin did not expand, **any** volume drop reduces total gross profit dollars, worsening the burn.
4.  **Next Step:** Before implementing the hike, isolate the non-material portion of your $0.09 cost. Recalculate the unit margin with the precise new cost. If the new margin is not significantly higher than $0.03, a price hike that risks a 10-30% volume drop will shorten your runway below 5 months. Consider testing the price on a small subset of customers to gauge elasticity before a full rollout, or prioritize identifying alternative funding immediately given the <6 month runway in all scenarios.

*Note: This model assumes fixed operating expenses ($25k burn) remain constant. If volume drops significantly, verify if any variable operating costs (shipping, sales commissions) also decrease, which would slightly improve the cash flow figures above.*
