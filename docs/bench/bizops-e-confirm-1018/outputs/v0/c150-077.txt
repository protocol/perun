**3. Shared-Risk Model Structure (Applied Math)**
Instead of a hard cap increase, you can model a "layered" approach where the airport absorbs risk above your current coverage limit until a certain threshold.
*   **Formula:** `Airport Exposure = (Total Claim − Your Policy Limit) × Shared Risk %`
*   **Proposal Example:** You retain liability up to $2M (covered by insurance). For claims between $2M and $5M, the airport agrees to indemnify you for 50% of the excess, or accepts a lower cap of $3M where you self-insure the gap.
*   **Sensitivity:** If the probability of a >$2M claim is low (e.g., <1%), the expected value of self-insuring the gap may be lower than the $115,000 premium hike. You must estimate your specific loss probability to validate this.

### Lane R: Legal & Regulatory Routing (Regulation-Dependent)

The following questions depend on UAE (Dubai) and Singaporean law, specifically regarding autonomous vehicle liability, indemnification enforceability, and insurance mandates. **You must route these to qualified legal counsel in both jurisdictions before signing.**

**Regime:** Commercial Contract Law, Autonomous Vehicle Regulations (Dubai RTA / Singapore LTA), and Insurance Law.

**Questions to Ask Counsel:**
1.  **Enforceability of Indemnification:** Under local laws in Dubai and Singapore, are broad indemnification clauses requiring a startup to assume unlimited or excessive liability for autonomous system failures enforceable, or are they void as against public policy?
2.  **Statutory Insurance Minimums:** Do the specific autonomous shuttle regulations in Dubai (RTA) or Singapore (LTA) mandate a minimum liability coverage that supersedes contract negotiations? Is $2M legally insufficient regardless of the contract cap?
3.  **Definition of "Incident":** How do local courts interpret "per incident" vs. "aggregate" limits in the context of software glitches causing multiple sequential collisions? Does the contract language expose you to multiple caps for a single systemic failure?
4.  **Cross-Border Enforcement:** If an incident occurs in Dubai but the entity is structured elsewhere, which jurisdiction's liability caps apply, and does the airport authority have assets in your home jurisdiction to enforce a judgment?
5.  **Shared-Risk Validity:** Is a "shared-risk" or "layered liability" model legally permissible in government contracts with airport authorities in these specific emirates/states, or do procurement rules require a fixed, fully insured cap?

**Action:** Retain local transportation counsel in Dubai and Singapore to review the draft contract's indemnity and insurance sections. Do not rely on general business advice for these clauses.
