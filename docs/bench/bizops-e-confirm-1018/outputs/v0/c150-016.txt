### Lane A: Applied Analysis of Operational Thresholds
While the legal conclusion is for counsel, we can apply the **DOL's six-factor economic reality test** to your described operations to identify where your AI tools might create risk. The core question is: *Does the AI reduce the stylist's opportunity for profit or loss based on their own initiative?*

**1. The "Control" Factor & AI Impact**
Regulators examine the nature and degree of control. If your AI moves from "recommending" to "dictating," it shifts the needle toward employee status.
*   **Current State:** Stylists earn 20% commission on $150k GMV. Average earnings are $400/month.
*   **Risk Threshold:** If the AI tool **mandates** which items a stylist must show, sets the final price, or prohibits the stylist from sourcing outside the AI's list, you are exercising behavioral control typical of an employer.
*   **Applied Math:** If a stylist cannot deviate from the AI to find higher-margin items or negotiate better deals (thereby increasing their 20% cut), their "opportunity for profit or loss" is neutralized. They become wage earners, not business owners.

**2. Behavioral Controls to Avoid (To Preserve Contractor Arguments)**
To maintain an argument for independent contractor status, your operational design should avoid these specific controls:
*   **Mandatory Acceptance:** Do not require stylists to accept every AI-generated client match or curated list. They must be free to reject work without penalty.
*   **Prescriptive Scripts/Methods:** Do not use the AI to enforce specific scripts, interaction times, or mandatory steps in the styling process. The *method* of styling must remain the stylist's choice.
*   **Exclusivity:** Do not prevent stylists from working with other platforms or selling items outside your app. The $400/month average suggests this is likely supplemental income; restricting other income sources strongly signals employment.
*   **Discipline based on AI metrics:** Do not terminate or penalize stylists solely for failing to meet AI-suggested conversion rates. Performance management of this type is an employer trait.

**3. The Critical Unknown Variable: State-Level Preemption**
You requested one unknown variable regarding state laws that could override federal guidelines.
*   **The Variable:** Whether your operating states have adopted the **"ABC Test"** (used in California via *Dynamex*/AB5, Massachusetts, New Jersey, and others).
*   **Why it matters:** Under the federal DOL test, you balance multiple factors. Under the strict **ABC Test**, a worker is presumed an employee unless you prove **Prong B**: *"The worker performs work that is outside the usual course of the hiring entity's business."*
*   **The Risk:** If regulators argue that "styling" is the *core* of StyleMatch's business (rather than just providing a marketplace), you fail Prong B immediately, regardless of how much freedom your AI allows. Federal guidelines do not preempt stricter state tests in these jurisdictions. This requires immediate legal review to map your stylist locations against state statutes.
