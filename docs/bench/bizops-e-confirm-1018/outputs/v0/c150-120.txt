**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent Routing**

Your question regarding whether neutron flux and cooling system stress data can be sold to a foreign-owned utility in France falls strictly under **export control regulations**, specifically the intersection of the **International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR)** and the **Export Administration Regulations (EAR)**. This is a regulatory determination, not an arithmetic one. You cannot self-determine compliance based on general descriptions; a licensed attorney specializing in trade compliance must classify your specific technical data.

**The Regulatory Landscape**

1.  **ITAR (22 C.F.R. §§ 120–130):** Administered by the State Department, ITAR controls defense articles and services on the United States Munitions List (USML). Category XVI of the USML explicitly covers nuclear weapons, design, testing, production, and **nuclear reactors**. If your "high precision" simulation data relates to the design, operation, or maintenance of nuclear reactors in a manner specified by the USML, it is likely an ITAR-controlled technical dataset.
    *   **Implication:** If ITAR applies, sharing this data with a foreign person (including a foreign-owned entity, even if the data remains on US servers) constitutes an "export." You would generally need a license or exemption from the State Department. There is no "de minimis" rule for ITAR; if the data is controlled, it is controlled regardless of the dollar value ($500,000) or your R&D spend ($1.8M).

2.  **EAR (15 C.F.R. §§ 730–774):** Administered by the Commerce Department, the EAR controls "dual-use" items (commercial items with potential military applications) on the Commerce Control List (CCL). Nuclear-related technology often falls under Category 0 (Nuclear Materials, Facilities, and Equipment).
    *   **Implication:** If your software is not on the USML, it likely falls under the EAR. You must determine the Export Control Classification Number (ECCN). Even if the data stays on US servers, providing access to a foreign national (or a foreign-owned entity controlled by foreign nationals) can be deemed a "deemed export," requiring a license depending on the ECCN and the destination country (France is generally a favorable destination, but the end-user and end-use matter critically).

**Foreign Ownership and "Deemed Exports"**

Your concern about foreign ownership is valid. Under both regimes, releasing controlled technical data to a foreign national within the United States (or to a foreign entity) is an export. The fact that the buyer is a utility in France, or a French-owned entity operating in the US, triggers "deemed export" rules if any foreign nationals access the data. The location of the server does not automatically negate the export if access is granted to prohibited persons.

**Required Next Steps (Do Not Proceed Yet)**

1.  **Commodity Jurisdiction (CJ) Request:** You likely need to submit a formal request to the State Department to determine if your data is ITAR-controlled (USML) or EAR-controlled (CCL). Do not guess; an incorrect self-classification is a violation.
2.  **Screening:** Your counsel must screen the specific foreign utility partner against denied parties lists.
3.  **License Determination:** If the data is controlled, your counsel must determine if a license is required for France and if any exemptions (such as specific government-to-government agreements) apply.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the data describes nuclear reactor core physics (neutron flux) with high precision, assume it is controlled until a lawyer classifies it otherwise.
*   **Flip Input:** The classification (ITAR vs. EAR vs. EAR99) is the single input that flips the answer from "illegal without license" to "potentially permissible."
*   **Immediate Action:** Halt the data transfer. Gather your technical specifications and submit them to export counsel for a formal classification opinion. Do not redact data points yourself; the threshold for control is legal, not mathematical.

**Note:** The $500,000 contract value and $1.8M R&D spend are irrelevant to the legal threshold for control; only the technical characteristics and destination matter.
**Note:** France is a NATO ally, which may simplify licensing if required, but does not eliminate the need for classification.
