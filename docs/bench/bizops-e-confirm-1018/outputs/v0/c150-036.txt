**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent Analysis (Route to Counsel)**

Your question concerns liability for patient harm, the definition of a "Business Associate" under HIPAA, mandatory safeguards, and the specific audits required to sign contracts. These are legal determinations dependent on federal statutes (HIPAA/HITECH), state medical malpractice laws, and the specific terms of your Business Associate Agreements (BAAs). I cannot conclude whether you are liable for malpractice or definitively list the "mandatory" safeguards that will protect you from litigation; only an attorney can interpret how these laws apply to your specific algorithmic logic and data flows.

**Regime Identification:**
1.  **HIPAA Privacy and Security Rules:** Governs your status as a Business Associate handling Protected Health Information (PHI) from Covered Entities (hospitals).
2.  **State Medical Malpractice/Tort Law:** Governs liability for patient harm resulting from algorithmic recommendations.
3.  **FDA Regulations (Potential):** If your AI is deemed a "Software as a Medical Device" (SaMD), additional federal regulatory regimes may apply beyond HIPAA.

**Required Professional Routes:**
*   **Healthcare Counsel:** You need an attorney specializing in health law to draft and negotiate BAAs with each hospital network. They must determine if your data aggregation model satisfies the "minimum necessary" standard and how liability is allocated when data from multiple sources conflicts.
*   **Malpractice/ Liability Counsel:** An attorney must analyze whether your tool constitutes the "practice of medicine" in your operating states, which dictates your exposure to malpractice suits versus contract disputes.
*   **Third-Party Auditor:** Before signing major contracts, hospitals will likely require a third-party audit (e.g., SOC 2 Type II or HITRUST certification). You need a compliance consultant to prepare for these specific frameworks.

**Design-Time Triage & Engineering Leads:**
While the legal conclusion must come from counsel, your engineering team should prepare for the following obligations typically associated with these regimes:
*   **Data Integrity Controls:** Mechanisms to detect and flag mismatched or incomplete data before it reaches the recommendation engine (addressing your "bulletproof" pipeline concern).
*   **Audit Trails:** Immutable logs of every data ingestion, transformation, and recommendation generated, as required by the HIPAA Security Rule.
*   **Encryption:** End-to-end encryption for data in transit between the three EHR systems and your platform, and at rest.
*   **Access Controls:** Strict role-based access controls (RBAC) ensuring only authorized personnel can view PHI.
