### Regulatory Regimes and Structural Risks
The core legal regime governing your expansion is the **Corporate Practice of Medicine (CPOM)** doctrine, which varies significantly by state.
*   **New York:** Has one of the strictest CPOM prohibitions. Non-physician entities generally cannot employ physicians or control medical decision-making. The risk here is that your proposed "remote oversight" model may be deemed a sham if the corporation controls pricing, scheduling, or patient flow, effectively directing the practice of medicine.
*   **Florida:** Also prohibits the corporate practice of medicine, though it allows for specific management services organization (MSO) structures if strictly separated from clinical control.

**Typical Triggers for Regulatory Scrutiny:**
Regulators and plaintiffs' attorneys typically look for the following clauses or operational realities to argue that a non-physician entity is illegally controlling a medical practice:
1.  **Fee Splitting:** Contracts where the physician's compensation is directly tied to revenue volume or number of patients seen (e.g., a percentage of the $150 session fee) rather than a fair-market-value flat fee.
2.  **Control of Medical Decisions:** Any clause giving the franchise the right to approve/disapprove medical protocols, select specific IV fluids based on cost rather than medical necessity, or dictate patient eligibility criteria.
3.  **Scheduling and Staffing Control:** If the corporation hires the nurses/technicians who work under the physician, or if the corporation controls the appointment calendar in a way that pressures physicians to see more patients than medically advisable.
4.  **Branding and Representation:** Marketing materials that imply the *franchise* provides the medical care rather than the professional entity owned by the physician.

In strict jurisdictions, the standard remediation is often a **Friendly Professional Corporation (PC)** structure: a physician-owned entity employs the medical staff and holds the medical license, while your franchise operates as an MSO providing non-clinical admin services for a fixed fee. Your current plan of contracting directly with individual doctors for "oversight" while the franchise retains operational control is a high-risk profile in New York.
