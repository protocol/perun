### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Structure Application

Based on the figures you provided, here is the arithmetic applied to your current hypothesis:

*   **Revenue per unit:** $3,500
*   **Variable Cost per unit:** $1,200
*   **Gross Margin per unit:** $2,300 (65.7%)
*   **Pilot Volume:** 15 clinics
*   **Total Pilot Gross Profit:** $34,500 ($2,300 × 15)

**Risk Exposure Analysis:**
You noted a potential penalty of >$50,000 per breach incident.
*   **Break-even on risk:** One single breach incident ($50,000) exceeds the total gross profit of the entire 15-clinic pilot ($34,500).
*   **Implication:** The current flat-fee model treats the risk cost as zero until an event occurs. In unit economics, if the probability of failure ($P$) multiplied by the cost of failure ($C$) exceeds the margin, the model is unsustainable without risk transfer.

**Pricing Model Options (Applied to your inputs):**

1.  **Tiered Fee Based on Record Count (Usage-Based):**
    *   *Structure:* Base fee + Variable rate per record.
    *   *Application:* If your $1,200 cost scales with complexity/volume, a flat fee subsidizes large clinics at the expense of small ones.
    *   *Formula:* $Price = Base_{fixed} + (Rate_{per\_record} \times Count)$.
    *   *Decision Aid:* If a clinic has 500 records vs. 5,000, does the compliance effort differ? If yes, a flat fee misprices the service.

2.  **Bundling into Annual Subscription:**
    *   *Structure:* Amortize the $3,500 fee over a 12–36 month contract.
    *   *Application:* Reduces upfront friction for clinics with $10k budgets.
    *   *Cash Flow Impact:* Delays recognition of the $2,300 margin. You must have the cash runway to cover the $1,200 upfront cost per client before receiving full payment.

3.  **Liability Coverage Requirement (Risk Transfer):**
    *   *Structure:* Client purchases specific cyber-liability insurance or signs an indemnity clause.
    *   *Application:* Shifts the $50,000+ risk exposure from your balance sheet to an insurer or the client.
    *   *Cost:* If insurance costs the client $X, your price sensitivity increases.

**Decision Thresholds:**
*   If **(Probability of Breach × $50,000) > $2,300**, the current margin does not cover expected loss.
*   If small practices cannot absorb a price increase >20%, bundling or tiering may be required to maintain volume.

**Next Step:** Calculate the exact cost scaling per record. If the cost to migrate 500 records is significantly lower than 5,000, switch to a tiered model to capture margin from smaller clinics while remaining competitive for larger ones.

### Lane R: Regulatory & Legal Routing

**Regime Identified:** HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act) and NY State health data regulations.

**Routing Required:**
*   **Liability & Indemnity:** Determining whether you can contractually shift breach liability to the client or require them to hold specific insurance policies is a legal determination. Counsel must draft the Business Associate Agreement (BAA) and service terms.
*   **"Strictly Adhering" Verification:** Only qualified legal counsel can validate if your proposed encryption tunnels and officer oversight meet the specific "reasonable and appropriate" safeguards required by HIPAA for data migration.
*   **Insurance Products:** Structuring a requirement for clients to purchase additional liability coverage involves insurance law and contract enforceability.

**Action:** Engage healthcare counsel to review your BAA and liability clauses before finalizing any pricing model that attempts to transfer risk.
