### Unit Economics Analysis (Lane A)

**1. Cost and Revenue Structure**
*   **Total Cost Per User Per Month:** $10 (app) + $40 (coaching) = **$50**.
*   **Current Reimbursement (Revenue):** **$60** per member per month.
*   **Contribution Margin:** $60 - $50 = **$10** per user per month.
*   **Margin Percentage:** ($10 / $60) ≈ **16.7%**.

**2. Break-Even and Runway Context**
*   **Break-Even Volume:** To cover fixed overhead (salaries for 8 staff, rent, etc.), you must calculate your total monthly fixed costs.
    *   *Formula:* `Fixed Costs ÷ $10 margin per user = Users needed to break even`.
    *   *Example:* If your 8-person team and overhead cost $80,000/month, you need 8,000 active users to break even.
*   **Runway:** With $100,000 cash, your runway depends entirely on your current net burn (Fixed Costs + Variable Costs - Revenue).
    *   *Formula:* `$100,000 ÷ (Total Monthly Cash Outflow - Total Monthly Cash Inflow)`.
    *   Without your specific fixed cost data, a precise month count cannot be calculated here.

**3. Decision Aid: Fee-for-Service vs. Value-Based**
*   **Current Model (Fee-for-Service):** You retain a $10/user/month surplus regardless of outcomes. The risk is low; the margin is thin.
*   **Proposed Model (Value-Based):** You mentioned a 30% quit rate (vs. industry average).
    *   *Trade-off:* Value-based contracts often tie a portion of reimbursement to the "quit" event. If the base rate drops below $50 to fund the bonus, you lose money on every non-quitter.
    *   *Sensitivity:* If a value-based deal lowers the base to $45 but pays a $200 bonus per quit:
        *   Loss per non-quitter: -$5.
        *   Gain per quitter: $155 ($200 bonus - $5 loss).
        *   At 30% success: Average revenue = (0.7 * $45) + (0.3 * $245) = $31.50 + $73.50 = $105.
        *   *Result:* Highly profitable *if* the 30% rate holds at scale. If the rate drops to 15% at scale, you may operate at a loss.

**Recommendation Structure:**
Do not lobby for higher rates yet; the current $60 rate yields a positive margin ($10). The constraint is likely **volume relative to fixed costs**, not unit loss.
*   **Next Step:** Calculate your exact monthly fixed costs. Divide that number by $10. If the resulting user count is achievable within your cash runway, the current model is viable.
*   **Contract Strategy:** Only pursue value-based contracting if you have high confidence the 30% quit rate is reproducible at scale. Use the pilot data to model the "break-even quit rate" for any proposed value-based term before signing.
