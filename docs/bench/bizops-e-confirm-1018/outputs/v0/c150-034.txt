**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent Analysis (Route to Counsel)**

Your question regarding secondary liability, retailer classification, and the adequacy of your Terms of Service (ToS) falls strictly under **product liability law**, **consumer protection statutes**, and **New York state-specific regulations** governing marketplaces and food safety. No arithmetic can determine whether a court will view FreshLoop as a "technology connector" or a "retailer"; this is a factual and legal determination made by judges based on the degree of control your platform exerts over the transaction.

**Potential Legal Exposures & Regime Identification**
In the US (and specifically New York), courts increasingly scrutinize digital marketplaces that facilitate physical goods sales. The core legal risk is that by handling payment processing and scheduling, you may be seen as having sufficient "control" over the transaction to be deemed a seller, potentially piercing the veil of your ToS.
*   **Strict Liability:** If classified as a retailer, you could be held strictly liable for defects (salmonella) regardless of fault.
*   **Negligence:** Claims may arise if you failed to vet restaurant partners or ignored known safety complaints.
*   **ToS Limitations:** While ToS can limit contractual liability, they generally cannot waive liability for personal injury or gross negligence in many jurisdictions. A clause stating "we are not liable" may not hold up if a jury decides you acted as a de facto retailer.

**Required Professional Routes**
You need to engage two specific types of licensed professionals immediately:
1.  **Product Liability Defense Attorney (NY Specialized):** Do not use a general corporate lawyer. You need counsel experienced in New York food safety law and marketplace liability. They must review your current ToS, operational flow (payment/scheduling), and vendor vetting process to opine on your "retailer" risk profile.
2.  **Commercial Insurance Broker:** You need a policy structure that specifically addresses "contingent product liability" and "errors and omissions" for marketplaces. General liability is likely insufficient.

**Actionable Preparation Before Expansion**
Before expanding to five new cities, gather the following for your counsel:
*   **Operational Control Map:** Document exactly where your control ends and the restaurant's begins (e.g., do you approve specific listings? Do you handle customer complaints about food quality?).
*   **Vendor Vetting Protocol:** Evidence of how you verify restaurant health grades and food safety certifications.
*   **Current ToS & Insurance Policies:** Bring your existing documents for a gap analysis.

**Insurance Types to Investigate (Discuss with Broker)**
*   **Contingent Product Liability Insurance:** Covers you if you are sued for products you didn't manufacture but facilitated.
*   **Cyber Liability Insurance:** Since you handle payments and user data.
*   **Directors & Officers (D&O) Liability:** Protects your leadership team given your VC backing.
