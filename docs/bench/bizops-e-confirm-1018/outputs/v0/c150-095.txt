### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Structure Analysis

**Current State Arithmetic**
*   **Revenue:** $2,000,000 ARR (200 clients × 50 seats × $200).
*   **Burn Rate:** $150,000/month × 12 = $1,800,000/year.
*   **Net Cash Flow:** +$200,000/year (pre-tax, pre-development capex).
*   **Runway:** 6 months cash / $150k burn = **6 months**.
*   **Development Cost Context:** With 12 creators, if fully loaded cost matches the $75k/module figure, your fixed content costs are high relative to the thin surplus.

**The "Fatal Flaw" Test**
Low completion (40%) does not mathematically invalidate the thesis *if* the 40% who complete generate enough value for the client to renew. However, it creates a **renewal risk**. If clients pay for 100 seats but only 40 are utilized, they may view the cost-per-active-user as $500 ($200 / 0.40) rather than $200.

**Pricing Model Comparison (Apply to Your Data)**
You asked about restructuring. Here is the arithmetic framework to decide:

1.  **Current Model (Per-Seat):**
    *   *Formula:* `Revenue = Seats × Price`
    *   *Risk:* Client pays for unused capacity. Value metric (seats) is decoupled from outcome (completion).
    *   *Break-even Price per Active User:* $200 / 0.40 = **$500**. Can your clients justify $500 per trained employee?

2.  **Proposed Model A (Pay-Per-Completion):**
    *   *Formula:* `Revenue = Seats × Completion Rate × Price_Per_Completion`
    *   *Application:* To maintain $200/seat revenue at 40% completion, you must charge **$500 per completion**.
    *   *Trade-off:* Aligns price with value, but transfers volume risk to you. If completion drops to 20%, revenue halves unless price doubles to $1,000.

3.  **Proposed Model B (Tiered/Engagement Thresholds):**
    *   *Structure:* Base fee (e.g., $100/seat) + Success Fee (e.g., $250 per completion).
    *   *Application:* Guarantees some cash flow while capping client risk.

**Decision Aid**
*   **Read:** Your current model yields positive cash flow, so the flaw is strategic (retention), not immediate solvency.
*   **Flip Input:** The critical variable is **Client Perceived Value**. If clients renew despite low completion, the model holds. If churn rises, the value metric is broken.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If renewal rates drop below 80% or Net Revenue Retention (NRR) falls below 100%, the per-seat model is failing regardless of current cash flow.
*   **Next Step:** Before piviting pricing (which requires contract renegotiation), run a "Van Westendorp" style check with 5 key clients: Ask specifically, "At what price per *completed* training would this be too expensive?" vs "too cheap?" Use their answers to model the Pay-Per-Completion price point. Do not switch models immediately without testing if the $500/completion price point is acceptable to the market.

### Lane R: Regulatory & Compliance Routing

**Securities & Fundraising Context**
You mentioned having "6 months of cash left." If your plan to "improve cash flow" involves raising capital (selling equity, SAFEs, or convertible notes) to extend runway while you restructure:
*   **Regime:** This constitutes a **securities offering** under US federal law (SEC) and state "Blue Sky" laws.
*   **Action Required:** You must route this to **legal counsel** specializing in securities. Do not attempt to structure a round, set valuation caps, or draft SAFEs without an attorney. Missteps here can lead to rescission rights (investors demanding money back) or regulatory penalties.

**Employment Classification**
*   **Regime:** You have a team of 12 content creators. If any are classified as contractors but function as employees (set hours, exclusive work), you face misclassification risks under IRS and Department of Labor tests.
*   **Action Required:** Route classification audits to **employment counsel**.

**Summary Recommendation for Stakeholders**
Present the data as a "Value Alignment Opportunity" rather than a flaw. Show the math: "We currently charge $200/seat; the market effectively pays $500/completion. We propose testing a hybrid model to secure renewals." Do not present a pivot as a finalized decision until client price-sensitivity testing (Lane A) and legal review of new contract terms (Lane R) are complete.
