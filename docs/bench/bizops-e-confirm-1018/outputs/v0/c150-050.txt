### Runway Projection & Sensitivity Analysis

**1. Current Baseline (No Fuel Increase)**
*   **Total Monthly Operating Cost:** 10 vans × $4,000 = $40,000
*   **Current Monthly Revenue:** $60,000
*   **Current Net Burn:** $40,000 - $60,000 = **-$20,000** (You are currently profitable by $20k/month).
*   **Current Runway:** Infinite (assuming revenue/costs hold), as you are generating positive cash flow.

**2. Stress Scenario (40% Fuel Cost Increase)**
*   **Current Fuel Cost:** 10 vans × $1,500 = $15,000
*   **Projected Increase:** $15,000 × 40% = **+$6,000** additional monthly cost.
*   **New Total Monthly Cost:** $40,000 + $6,000 = $46,000
*   **New Net Burn:** $46,000 (Cost) - $60,000 (Revenue) = **-$14,000** (Still profitable by $14k/month).
*   **Projected Runway:** Infinite. Under these specific numbers, a 40% fuel hike reduces profit but does not create a cash burn requiring reserves.

**3. Break-Even Analysis (When do you run out of cash?)**
To deplete your $120,000 reserve, your monthly costs must exceed revenue.
*   **Formula:** `Break-Even Cost = Current Revenue`
*   **Threshold:** Your total costs can rise to $60,000 before you start burning cash.
*   **Headroom:** You have $20,000 of monthly cost headroom ($60k revenue - $40k current cost).
*   **Fuel Price Required to Burn Cash:** Fuel costs would need to increase by **133%** (from $15k to $35k) to eliminate all profit and hit break-even. To actually burn through your $120k reserve in 18 months, you would need a monthly net burn of ~$6,667, meaning total costs would need to hit ~$66,667.

**Decision Aid & Next Steps**
*   **Read:** Based strictly on the figures provided, a 40% fuel increase does not threaten your runway; you remain cash-flow positive. Emergency funding is not mathematically indicated by this specific variable alone.
*   **Key Variable to Adjust:** The most sensitive input is **Revenue Elasticity**. If you pass costs to customers via a surcharge, will volume drop? Model: `New Profit = (Revenue - Churn%) - (Base Cost + Fuel Hike)`.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If fuel costs rise >133% OR revenue drops >23% (to ~$46k) simultaneously, you enter a burn scenario.
*   **Action:** Do not hedge or seek funding based on a 40% hike alone. Instead, instrument a "fuel surcharge" clause in your contracts now, triggered only if diesel exceeds a specific threshold (e.g., $5.50/gal), to protect the margin without immediate price hikes.

**Note:** This analysis assumes fixed revenue; if higher fuel costs correlate with reduced customer demand, recalculate using the "Revenue Elasticity" variable above.
