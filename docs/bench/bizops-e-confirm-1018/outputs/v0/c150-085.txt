**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent Routing**

Your question regarding whether holding client funds in interest-bearing accounts requires a banking charter or trust license, and the associated legal risks, falls strictly into **Lane R**. The correct answer depends entirely on specific state statutes (e.g., New York Banking Law, Delaware Trust Code) and federal interpretations of what constitutes "taking deposits" or acting as a "fiduciary."

**The Regime:**
You are operating at the intersection of **Money Transmission**, **Trust Law**, and potentially **Banking Regulations**.
1.  **Money Transmission:** Most states require a license to transmit money or hold it for third parties. While escrow is often an exemption, *earning and sharing interest* on those funds frequently voids that exemption, reclassifying the activity as taking a deposit or operating an unlicensed investment vehicle.
2.  **Trust Licensing:** If you are holding funds for the benefit of others and managing the yield, regulators may view you as acting as a trustee. Many states (including NY and DE) strictly regulate who can act as a trustee or require specific "limited purpose trust company" charters.
3.  **Banking Charter:** Accepting funds with an obligation to return principal plus yield is the hallmark definition of a bank deposit. Doing this without a charter is a severe regulatory violation.

**Immediate Legal Risks (General Framework):**
While I cannot conclude your specific liability, engaging in regulated financial activities without the proper license typically exposes a company to:
*   **Cease and Desist Orders:** Immediate shutdown of operations by state banking departments or the FDIC.
*   **Civil Monetary Penalties:** Fines often calculated per day of violation or per transaction, which can exceed the capital raised.
*   **Criminal Liability:** In many jurisdictions, knowingly operating an unlicensed money transmission or banking business is a felony for founders.
*   **Restitution:** Courts may order the disgorgement of all fees earned and require full repayment of client funds, potentially piercing the corporate veil to hold founders personally liable.
*   **Inability to Enforce Contracts:** Courts in some states refuse to enforce contracts (including your fee agreements) made by unlicensed entities in regulated fields.
