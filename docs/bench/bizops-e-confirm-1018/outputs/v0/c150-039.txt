### Lane A: Unit Economics & Cash Flow (Apply)

**1. Revised Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)**
*Formula:* `LTV = (Monthly Price × Lifespan in Months) − Hardware Cost`
*Assumption:* We calculate LTV per unit over its physical life, ignoring other operating expenses for this specific hardware-margin view.

*   **Old Projection (30 months):** ($40 × 30) − $300 = **$900**
*   **New Reality (18 months):** ($40 × 18) − $300 = **$420**
*   **Impact:** Your gross profit per unit has dropped by 53%.

**2. Revised Payback Period**
*Formula:* `Payback = Hardware Cost ÷ Monthly Price`
*   **Current:** $300 ÷ $40 = **7.5 months**
*   **Context:** You recover costs in 7.5 months, but the unit dies at 18 months. This leaves only 10.5 months of pure profit per unit before replacement cost hits.

**3. Runway Analysis**
*Formula:* `Runway = Cash ÷ Net Monthly Burn`
*   **Current:** $250,000 ÷ $40,000 = **6.25 months**
*   **Risk:** If replacement costs spike your burn rate above $40k, runway shortens immediately.

**4. Pricing Scenario Modeling (User Decision Required)**
You asked whether to raise prices to $55 or charge an upfront fee. Here is the math for those options:

*   **Option A: Raise to $55/mo**
    *   New LTV: ($55 × 18) − $300 = **$690** (Recovers most lost margin).
    *   New Payback: $300 ÷ $55 = **5.45 months**.
*   **Option B: Upfront Hardware Fee (e.g., $200) + Lower Sub ($30)**
    *   New LTV: $200 + ($30 × 18) − $300 = **$440**.
    *   New Payback: ($300 − $200) ÷ $30 = **3.3 months** (Faster cash recovery, lower long-term value).

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Read:** At $40/mo, the 18-month lifespan leaves a thin margin buffer for overhead.
*   **Flip Input:** The break-even price to maintain the original $900 LTV over 18 months is **$66.67/mo** (`($900 + $300) ÷ 18`).
*   **Threshold Rule:** If LTV drops below 3x your fully loaded CAC (not provided), the model breaks.
*   **Next Step:** If churn is low, consider testing the $55 price on *new* customers only this week to measure conversion elasticity before applying to existing users.

### Lane R: Legal & Compliance (Route)

**1. Consumer Protection & Durability Claims**
*Regime:* **Florida Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act (FDUTPA)** and potential **Federal Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act** issues.
*Issue:* Advertising a 30-month lifespan when the product fails at 18 months due to known corrosion may constitute a deceptive practice or a breach of express/implied warranties.
*Action:* **Route to Florida consumer protection counsel.** Do not announce price changes or new terms until counsel reviews your original marketing materials and warranty language.

**2. Contract Modifications for Existing Customers**
*Regime:* **Florida Contract Law**.
*Issue:* Unilaterally raising prices or changing terms for existing subscribers often requires specific notice periods or consent, depending on your Terms of Service.
*Action:* **Route to contract counsel.** You need a review of your subscription agreement's "modification clause" before notifying the 1,000 existing subscribers.

**3. Required Professional Advice**
Before any announcement, secure:
*   **Legal Opinion:** On warranty liability for the corrosion issue and the legality of mid-contract price hikes.
*   **Tax Advice:** On how upfront hardware fees vs. subscription revenue are taxed in Florida (sales tax applicability differs).

**Summary:** The math shows a severe margin compression requiring a price increase to ~$67 to match old targets. However, the legal risk of changing terms while potentially facing warranty claims is high. Engage counsel immediately.
