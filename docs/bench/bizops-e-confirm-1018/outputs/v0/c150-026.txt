**Lane R: Regulation-Dependent (Route Immediately)**

Your situation involves **state and local sales/use tax nexus**, **service taxability**, and **manufacturing equipment exemptions**. These are strictly regulation-dependent questions that vary by jurisdiction (Kansas, Texas, Utah, etc.) and even by county. There is no universal arithmetic formula to solve this; the answer depends entirely on specific statutes and administrative rulings in each location.

**Why this requires a professional tax assessment immediately:**
1.  **Nexus Triggers:** The physical presence of your installation crew almost certainly creates "physical presence nexus" in the states where they work. This typically obligates you to register, collect, and remit sales tax, regardless of whether the end product (alcohol) is restricted in a specific "dry" county. The tax is generally on the *equipment* or the *installation service*, not the legality of the brewery's operation.
2.  **Taxability of Equipment vs. Service:** States differ wildly on whether custom fermentation tanks are taxable tangible personal property or exempt "machinery used directly in manufacturing." Similarly, some states tax installation labor separately, while others bundle it with the equipment price.
3.  **Risk Exposure:** Collecting *no* sales tax while having a physical presence (crew) in multiple states creates significant liability for back taxes, penalties, and interest. The "dry county" status of the customer rarely exempts the *builder* from sales tax on the materials or labor provided.

**Action Plan:**
You must engage a **licensed CPA or tax attorney** specializing in multi-state sales tax for manufacturing and construction. Do not attempt to self-assess exemptions or nexus thresholds.

**Preparation for your professional:**
Before your engagement, gather the following to reduce billable hours and accelerate the assessment:
*   **Deployment Log:** A list of every job site address from the last 3–4 years (the typical look-back period for audits), including dates your crew was physically present.
*   **Contract Breakdown:** Samples of contracts showing how you currently bill (lump sum vs. separated equipment and labor).
*   **Resale/Exemption Certificates:** Any documentation your customers have provided claiming exemption.
*   **Current Filings:** Proof of any sales tax currently collected/remitted (if any).

**Immediate Mitigation (Non-Legal):**
Until you have counsel, consider halting new installations in states where you are not registered if the risk of immediate audit is high, or begin setting aside a reserve percentage (e.g., 8–10% of contract value) specifically for potential tax liabilities. Do not continue the current practice of collecting zero tax while expanding to ten new states; this compounds the liability daily.

**Note:** The "manufacturing exemption" is a common defense, but it is rarely automatic; it usually requires specific paperwork and state-by-state qualification.
**Note:** "Dry county" laws regulate the *sale/consumption* of alcohol, not necessarily the *sales tax* on the construction of the facility producing it.
