**Specific Policy Language Areas to Review with Counsel:**
While I cannot interpret your policy, your attorney will likely scrutinize the following common exclusion categories in professional liability and cyber policies:

*   **Known Prior Acts / Prior Knowledge Exclusions:** Clauses that deny coverage for claims arising from issues the insured knew about (or should have known) before the policy period or before the specific audit engagement began.
*   **Failure to Perform / Professional Negligence Definitions:** How the policy defines "negligence" versus "gross negligence" or "willful misconduct." Some policies exclude claims where the auditor failed to follow established industry standards (e.g., missing a critical reentrancy vector).
*   **Code Modification / Scope Creep Exclusions:** Clauses addressing whether coverage applies when the audited codebase is altered post-delivery without a re-audit. Insurers often argue the risk profile changed, voiding the original assessment.
*   **Cryptocurrency / Digital Asset Exclusions:** Many general professional liability policies have specific carve-outs excluding losses related to private key management, smart contract failures, or loss of digital assets unless a specialized cyber endorsement is attached.
*   **Third-Party Beneficiary / End-User Standing:** Language determining if the policy covers claims from non-clients (the end-users who lost funds) versus only the direct client (the project founders).
*   **Damages Caps and Sub-limits:** Specific limits on payouts for "cyber events" or "data breaches" which might be lower than the general liability limit.
