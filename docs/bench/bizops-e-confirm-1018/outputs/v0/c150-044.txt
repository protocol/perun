### Unit Economics Analysis (Lane A)

Your current baseline shows a contribution margin of $105 per unit ($140 price - $35 cost). However, the 20% material volatility ($28–$42) creates a margin range of $98 to $112 per unit if the price remains fixed.

To hit your **60% gross margin target** dynamically:
*   **Formula:** `Target Price = Material Cost ÷ (1 - Target Margin)`
*   **At $28 cost:** Price must be $70 (unlikely, as current base is $140).
*   **At $42 cost:** Price must be $105.
*   **Reality Check:** Your current $140 price at $35 cost yields a **75% margin** ($105/$140), not 60%. If costs rise to $42, your margin drops to 70% ($98/$140). You are currently well above your 60% target even at peak volatility.

**The Real Risk:** While you miss the *specific* 60% target fluctuation, the absolute dollar profit swings by $14 per unit. On 300 units, that is a **$4,200 monthly profit variance**. With $20,000 in reserves, a sustained period of higher costs combined with a sales dip could threaten runway if other fixed costs are high.

### Strategic Options & Application

**Option 1: Cost-Plus with Floating Surcharge**
*   **Mechanism:** Base Price ($140) covers average cost + fixed overhead/profit. Add a "Material Volatility Surcharge" triggered only when wood costs exceed a threshold (e.g., $38).
*   **Application:** If wood hits $42, surcharge = $4. Customer sees transparency: "Sustainable material adjustment."
*   **Pros:** Protects margins immediately.
*   **Cons:** Friction at checkout; requires real-time cost tracking.

**Option 2: Quarterly Price Lock (Absorb Risk)**
*   **Mechanism:** Set price for 90 days based on a weighted average of material costs.
*   **Application:** Calculate the 3-month average cost. If the average is $36, set price to maintain margin on $36.
*   **Pros:** Stable customer experience.
*   **Cons:** You absorb the downside if prices spike late in the quarter. Given your $20k reserve, absorbing a $4,200 variance is manageable short-term but risky long-term if trends worsen.

**Recommendation Structure:**
Do not adopt a pure floating surcharge if it complicates the buying experience for custom goods. Instead, **adjust the base price quarterly** based on a rolling 60-day material average. This smooths the 20% swing into a manageable 5% price adjustment, preserving margin without daily friction.

### Decision Aid
*   **Break-even Sensitivity:** At $42 material cost, your margin is still 70%. You do not *need* a surcharge to survive, only to stabilize profit variance.
*   **Threshold Rule:** If material costs sustain >$45 for two weeks, trigger a price review.
*   **Next Step:** Calculate your fixed monthly overhead. If `($140 - $42) * 300` covers overhead + desired profit, hold prices. If not, implement a 5% base price increase effective next quarter.
