**Lane Determination:** Your question asks whether hosting data creates a **fiduciary duty** and what legal liabilities exist in the **UK and EU**. Because the correct answer depends entirely on statutory law, case law precedents regarding bailment vs. fiduciary relationships, and specific jurisdictional regulations, this is a **Lane R (Regulation-Dependent)** matter. I cannot conclude whether you have a fiduciary duty or if you are legally exposed; only a qualified solicitor or barrister in the relevant jurisdiction can make that determination.

### Regime Identification & Routing
The regimes involved are **UK Contract/Tort Law** (specifically duties of care and bailment) and **EU/UK Data Protection & Professional Services Regulations**. The core legal distinction you are asking about—between a "data host" (custodian) and a "professional valuer" (fiduciary/advisor)—is a binding legal classification that dictates your insurance requirements.

**Action:** You must route this to a **UK/EU technology liability solicitor** and a **specialized insurance broker**. Do not rely on general liability counsel for this specific nuance.

### Questions for Your Insurance Broker & Legal Counsel
To determine if your risk profile mandates Errors & Omissions (E&O) coverage, present the following specific inquiries to your professionals. Do not ask "Am I liable?"; ask these structured questions to force a precise analysis:

1.  **Classification of Duty:** "Under UK and French law, does the act of storing third-party written appraisals and high-resolution images on our servers create a **fiduciary relationship** or a **duty of care** that exceeds standard contractual bailment, specifically when the stored data represents assets worth >£5M?"
2.  **Scope of 'Professional Services':** "Does our Terms of Service sufficiently disclaim any role in the *creation* or *verification* of the appraisal values to prevent a court from reclassifying us as a participant in the valuation process rather than a neutral host?"
3.  **Negligence vs. Contractual Cap:** "If data corruption alters an appraisal value causing a missed sale, would current UK/EU precedents allow a plaintiff to pierce our liability caps in the Terms of Service by claiming gross negligence or a breach of statutory duty?"
4.  **Insurance Gap Analysis:** "Given our specific data type (antique valuations), does our current General Liability policy explicitly exclude 'cyber data corruption' or 'loss of business opportunity' for third-party assets, thereby creating a mandatory gap that only E&O or Cyber Liability insurance can fill?"
5.  **Jurisdictional Variance:** "Are there specific French consumer protection or commercial codes that impose stricter custodial duties on cloud providers handling high-value asset records compared to UK law?"

### Immediate Preparation (Before the Call)
To make the professional engagement efficient, prepare these facts:
*   Your current Terms of Service (specifically the limitation of liability and disclaimer of warranty sections).
*   A sample of the data structure (showing clearly that *you* do not input the values; the dealer does).
*   Your uptime SLA and current backup/disaster recovery protocol documentation.

**Note:** Do not purchase the £15,000 policy based on a hypothesis; the cost-benefit analysis depends entirely on the legal classification of your duty.
