**Lane R: Route to Licensed Professionals**

Your question regarding whether offering in-house financing triggers consumer lending licensure requirements in multiple states, the availability of manufacturer exemptions, and the specific compliance burden is a **regulation-dependent legal determination**. It cannot be answered with arithmetic or general business logic.

**The Regime:**
This falls under **State Consumer Lending Laws** and **Usury Statutes**. Each of the twelve states where you sell homes has its own statutory framework defining what constitutes "engaging in the business of lending," the thresholds for licensure, and specific exemptions (sometimes called "seller financing" or "vendor financing" exemptions). There is no single federal rule that preempts these state-level licensing requirements for this type of transaction. Additionally, federal regulations such as the **Truth in Lending Act (TILA)** and the **Dodd-Frank Act** (specifically ability-to-repay rules) impose disclosure and underwriting obligations on creditors, regardless of state licensure status.

**Why This Cannot Be Solved Here:**
1.  **State Variability:** An exemption that exists in Oregon may not exist in California, Texas, or New York. Some states have strict "one loan triggers licensure" rules, while others have volume or interest-rate thresholds.
2.  **Exemption Nuance:** "Manufacturer exemptions" often depend on specific deal structures (e.g., whether the loan is secured by the home itself, if the seller retains title, or if the note is sold to a third party later). Misinterpreting these nuances can result in severe penalties, voided contracts, or criminal liability.
3.  **Cross-Border Complexity:** Offering financing across state lines from an Oregon base creates a "nexus" issue in every destination state. The cost estimate of $50,000 per state is a variable input that requires verification against current fee schedules and bonding requirements for each specific jurisdiction.

**Required Action:**
You must engage **qualified legal counsel** specializing in **Consumer Finance Law** and **Fintech Licensing** before originating a single loan. Do not proceed with the financing program based on general information.

**Specific Experts to Consult:**
1.  **Consumer Finance Attorney:** Look for a law firm or practitioner with specific experience in **multi-state consumer lending licensure** and **seller-financed real estate/personal property transactions**. They will perform a "licensure mapping" for your twelve target states.
2.  **Compliance Specialist (Fintech):** Once counsel determines licensure is required, you may need a compliance consultant to build the ongoing reporting, auditing, and TILA disclosure infrastructure.
3.  **Alternative Route:** Ask these same attorneys to evaluate the feasibility of **partnering with an existing licensed lender** or using a "loan origination" model where a third party holds the license and the risk, while you act as a referral agent (which has its own, often lighter, registration requirements).
