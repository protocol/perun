### Lane A: Applied Unit Economics & Liquidity Modeling

Your situation requires separating **Cash Flow** (liquidity) from **Recognized Revenue** (profitability). The 24-month hold creates a timing mismatch that distorts standard SaaS metrics if not modeled correctly.

#### 1. The Core Constraint: Recognized Revenue vs. Cash
Under your described Texas regulation, 70% of inflows are restricted.
*   **Monthly Pre-need Sales:** 45 clients × $15,000 = **$675,000** total monthly volume.
*   **Restricted Cash (70%):** $472,500 (Must sit in trust; cannot fund operations).
*   **Available Cash (30%):** $202,500 (This is your actual top-line revenue for operations).

**The Trap:** If you calculate CAC payback using the $675k figure, you will overspend on acquisition. You must use the **$202,500** available cash figure for all liquidity and payback calculations.

#### 2. Adjusted CAC Payback Formula
Standard SaaS payback is `CAC ÷ Monthly Gross Margin`. You must modify the denominator to reflect the *realizable* margin after the trust hold.

**Formula:**
`Adjusted Payback (Months) = CAC ÷ (Available Cash % × Gross Margin % × Avg Revenue Per Account)`

**Application to your numbers (illustrative slots):**
*   Let **CAC** = $X (You must insert your actual sales/marketing spend ÷ new clients).
*   **Available Cash %** = 30% (0.30).
*   **Gross Margin %** = (Available Cash - Cost of Goods Sold) ÷ Available Cash. *Note: If your software costs are low, this might be ~80%.*
*   **Avg Revenue Per Account** = $15,000 (monthly sales volume per client).

**Example Calculation (assuming $5,000 CAC and 80% GM):**
*   Realizable Monthly Profit per Client = $15,000 × 0.30 (available) × 0.80 (margin) = **$3,600**.
*   Payback = $5,000 ÷ $3,600 = **1.4 months**.
*   *Contrast:* If you ignored the trust rule, you might falsely calculate payback as 0.4 months, leading to dangerous overspending.

#### 3. Runway & Liquidity Check
Your runway depends entirely on the **Available Cash** flow, not the total volume.
*   **Current Operating Cash:** $300,000.
*   **Monthly Burn:** $40,000.
*   **Current Runway (without growth):** $300k ÷ $40k = **7.5 months**.

**Growth Impact:**
Each new client adds ~$4,500 to your available cash pool ($15k × 30%). If you add 10 clients/month, you generate +$45,000 in available cash, potentially turning your burn positive immediately. However, the **24-month delay** means you cannot count on the *profit* from the trust portion until much later.

#### 4. Investor Presentation Strategy
To avoid distorting health for investors:
1.  **Present Two P&Ls:** One GAAP-compliant (showing deferred revenue liability for the 70%) and one "Cash Available for Operations" view.
2.  **Metric Rename:** Do not report standard "ARR" (Annual Recurring Revenue) using the gross $675k figure. Report **"Operational ARR"** based on the 30% accessible portion to prevent valuation confusion.
3.  **Churn Sensitivity:** With 4% monthly churn, your client base halves roughly every 17 months. Since you cannot touch the trust funds for 24 months, high churn means you may never recognize revenue from a significant portion of your book.
    *   *Decision Threshold:* If churn > 2.5%, the 24-month hold makes unit economics negative for many cohorts. Prioritize retention over acquisition.
