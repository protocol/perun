### The Regulatory Boundary (Why this is Lane R)
Depreciation is not merely an arithmetic choice; it is a tax election governed by the Internal Revenue Code.
1.  **Method Selection:** Switching from straight-line to an accelerated method (like Double Declining Balance) or a usage-based method (Units of Production) requires determining if your assets qualify under current IRS rules (e.g., MACRS classes).
2.  **Audit Risk:** Changing a depreciation method often requires filing **Form 3115** (Application for Change in Accounting Method) with the IRS. Doing this internally without proper filing can trigger audit flags and penalties.
3.  **Taxable Income:** Only a CPA can determine how a change in depreciation affects your specific taxable income, considering potential limitations like Section 179 expensing or bonus depreciation which may already offer accelerated benefits you aren't utilizing.

*   **Formula Concept (for discussion with CPA):**
    $$ \text{Depreciation per Period} = \left( \frac{\text{Cost} - \text{Salvage Value}}{\text{Total Estimated Rental Days}} \right) \times \text{Actual Rental Days in Period} $$

**Impact Analysis Framework:**

*   **Book-Tax Difference:** Be aware that your internal books (management reporting) can differ from tax filings, but consistency is required for audits.
