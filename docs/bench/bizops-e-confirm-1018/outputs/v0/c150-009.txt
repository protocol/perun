### Lane A: Arithmetic and Scenario Modeling

We can model the cash flow impact of potential lawsuits using your provided numbers to illustrate the trade-offs, but the final reserve percentage is a strategic decision you must make based on your risk tolerance.

**The Math of Exposure**
*   **Monthly Net Burn/Profit:** Revenue ($40,000) − Costs ($25,000) = **+$15,000 monthly surplus**.
*   **Current Runway (without new lawsuits):** Cash ($200,000) ÷ Net Burn ($0, since you are profitable) is technically infinite, but if revenue stopped, Runway = $200,000 ÷ $25,000 = **8 months**.
*   **Single Lawsuit Impact:** Defense cost ($50,000) represents **1.25 months of total revenue** or **3.3 months of your current surplus**.
*   **Dual Lawsuit Impact (Simultaneous):** Cost ($100,000) represents **2.5 months of revenue** or **50% of your current cash hoard**.

**Modeling Reserve Options**
You asked for a specific percentage. Below are three scenarios applying different reserve rates to your $40,000 monthly revenue.

| Reserve Rate | Monthly Set-Aside | Months to Fund 1 Lawsuit ($50k) | Months to Fund 2 Lawsuits ($100k) | Impact on Reinvestable Surplus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- :--- |
| **10%** | $4,000 | 12.5 months | 25 months | Reduces surplus from $15k to $11k |
| **25%** | $10,000 | 5 months | 10 months | Reduces surplus from $15k to $5k |
| **50%** | $20,000 | 2.5 months | 5 months | Eliminates surplus; breaks even |

**Decision Aid**
*   **The Flip Input:** The critical variable is the *probability* of a suit. If you believe the chance of two simultaneous suits in the next 12 months is high, the 25–50% range is mathematically necessary to avoid dipping into operating capital. If the risk is low, 10% may suffice, relying on the existing $200k cushion.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Many volatile media entities target a "catastrophe reserve" equal to 6–12 months of *maximum expected legal exposure*. Here, that is $100k–$150k. You currently have $200k total, but it also covers operating expenses.
*   **Next Step:** If you choose a 25% reserve ($10k/month), you will fund a single lawsuit defense in 5 months without touching operating cash. Consider testing this by moving $10,000 next month into a restricted account and monitoring if the reduced $5,000 reinvestable surplus stalls key growth metrics.

### Lane R: Regulation and Legal Strategy (Route to Professional)

**The Regime:** This involves **Media Liability Law**, **Defamation Tort Law**, and potentially **Insurance Regulation**.

**The Route:** You must engage a specialized **media liability attorney** immediately. Do not wait for a lawsuit to trigger this hire.

**Why this is Lane R:**
1.  **Privilege Determination:** Whether your "anonymous tips" qualify for any journalistic privilege or defense against defamation varies strictly by jurisdiction and specific editorial practices. An accountant cannot determine this; only counsel can.
2.  **Insurance vs. Reserve:** A lawyer may advise that self-insuring (cash reserves) is inferior to purchasing **media liability insurance**. Policies often cover defense costs ($50k+) that could otherwise drain your reserves. Only a broker/attorney can assess your insurability given the "anonymous source" risk profile.
3.  **Trigger Definition:** You asked what factors should trigger hiring counsel. The correct answer is **now**. Operating a network based on unverified anonymous tips regarding unsolved cases presents an immediate, non-zero risk of actual malice claims. Waiting for a "trigger" (like a cease-and-desist letter) may be too late to establish the editorial safeguards necessary for a robust legal defense.

**Pre-Call Preparation:** Before meeting counsel, prepare a dossier containing: (1) your current editorial verification workflow for anonymous tips, (2) the exact language of your current terms of service and disclaimers, and (3) a log of any past threats or complaints, even informal ones. This allows the attorney to assess your exposure level immediately.
