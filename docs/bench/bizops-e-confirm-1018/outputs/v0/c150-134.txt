### Lane R: Regulatory Routing (Anti-Kickback Statute)
Your proposed rebate structure triggers the **Federal Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS)**, a criminal law prohibiting the exchange of anything of value to induce referrals for services payable by federal healthcare programs (Medicare/Medicaid). Because your customers are hospitals treating chronic diseases, a significant portion of their revenue likely comes from federal programs.

**The Risk:** A $5,000 quarterly rebate tied to "reducing readmission rates" could be interpreted as remuneration contingent on the volume or value of referrals or business generated. Even if your intent is performance improvement, the AKS is broad. If the rebate is seen as an inducement for the hospital to continue using your device (and thus generating billable events) rather than a bona fide discount for verified outcomes, it poses severe legal risk, including fines and exclusion from federal programs.

**Safe Harbors:** You asked if a safe harbor covers technology vendors offering performance-based discounts.
*   **Do not conclude applicability here.** Determining if you fit the **Discount Safe Harbor** or the **Warranty Safe Harbor** requires a factual analysis of how the rebate is calculated, documented, and reported.
*   The **Care Coordination Arrangements Safe Harbor** (part of the 21st Century Cures Act updates) may be relevant for technology that improves care coordination, but it has strict requirements regarding the nature of the technology and the financial arrangement.
*   **Action:** You must route this to a healthcare attorney to determine if your specific "outcome-based rebate" fits an existing safe harbor or if the structure needs modification (e.g., changing the rebate to a fixed fee reduction unrelated to referral volume, or ensuring it meets the strict documentation requirements of the Warranty Safe Harbor).

$$ \text{Effective Margin Impact} = \frac{\text{Rebate Amount}}{\text{Quarterly Revenue}} $$
