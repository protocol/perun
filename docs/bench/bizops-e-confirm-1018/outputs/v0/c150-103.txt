### Lane R: Regulatory Regime and Routing
Your question regarding whether "governance boost" tokens constitute unregistered securities falls strictly under **Lane R**. The determination depends on the application of the **Securities Act of 1933** and the **Securities Exchange Act of 1934**, specifically the **Howey Test** established by the U.S. Supreme Court. Because the correct answer depends on a legal determination of what is permitted and how an instrument must be classified by the SEC, I cannot evaluate your model or give a verdict. You must route this immediately to a securities lawyer specializing in digital assets.

**Why this is a high-priority Lane R issue:**
The sale of 1,200 tokens to developers for $500 each to "fund platform maintenance" presents classic indicators that trigger securities scrutiny. Under the Howey Test, an instrument is likely a security if there is (1) an investment of money, (2) in a common enterprise, (3) with a reasonable expectation of profits, (4) to be derived from the efforts of others. Your description suggests:
*   **Investment of Money:** Developers are paying $500.
*   **Common Enterprise:** Funds are pooled for platform maintenance.
*   **Efforts of Others:** The value or utility of the "boost" may depend on your team's development work rather than the holders' own governance actions.
*   **Expectation of Profit:** If developers buy these expecting the token value to rise or to gain preferential treatment that yields financial return, this prong is met.

The fact that you have already raised $600,000 without a registered offering or a confirmed exemption (such as Regulation D or Regulation S) significantly increases the risk profile. The SEC has frequently acted against projects where "utility" tokens are sold primarily to raise capital for the builder's operations.

### Action Plan: Questions for Securities Counsel
Do not proceed with your next token sale event until you have answers to the following from a licensed attorney. Bring your tokenomics whitepaper, sales contracts, and marketing materials to this meeting.

1.  **Howey Test Application:** Based on our specific marketing materials and the economic reality of the "governance boost," does the SEC likely view these tokens as investment contracts? Specifically, how does the "efforts of others" prong apply given our team controls the platform roadmap?
2.  **Exemption Analysis:** If these are securities, did our past sales of 1,200 tokens qualify for any exemption from registration (e.g., Reg D 506(b)/506(c), Reg A+, or Reg S)? If not, what are our immediate remediation options to avoid enforcement action?
3.  **Rescission Rights:** If we are found to have sold unregistered securities, what are our obligations regarding rescission offers (refunds) to the 1,200 existing holders, and what is the statute of limitations exposure?
4.  **Future Offering Structure:** What structural changes (e.g., changing the token utility, restricting transferability, altering the buyer profile) are required to potentially fit within an exemption for future sales?
5.   **State Blue Sky Laws:** Beyond federal SEC rules, do our sales to Arizona and Nevada residents trigger specific state-level securities registration requirements?

**Note:** Do not fabricate a defense based on the "utility" label alone; courts look at economic substance over form. Your immediate next step is to pause sales and retain counsel.
