**The Legal Regime and Current Precedents**
Under current US law, copyright protection requires human authorship. The USCO has issued guidance stating that works generated entirely by artificial intelligence without human creative input are not copyrightable and effectively fall into the public domain. Key precedents and determinations include:
*   **Thaler v. Perlmutter (2023):** A federal court affirmed that works created autonomously by AI cannot be registered because they lack a human author.
*   **USCO Determination on "Zarya of the Dawn":** The Office ruled that while the selection and arrangement of AI images in a comic book could be copyrighted (due to human curation), the individual AI-generated images themselves could not.
*   **Generic Prompts vs. Creative Input:** The USCO generally views simple prompts (e.g., "find patents for a better mouse trap") as insufficient to establish the user as the "author" of the resulting output. If the AI determines the expressive elements of the report, the user likely holds no copyright claim.

**Implications for Your Business Model**
The lack of clear ownership creates significant risk for your planned business model:
1.  **User Claims:** If the output is deemed public domain due to lack of human authorship, users may not be able to claim exclusive rights, but neither can you automatically claim them unless your Terms of Service explicitly assign any potential rights to you (though such assignment may be ineffective if no copyright exists).
2.  **Aggregated Insights:** Selling aggregated data to law firms relies on your ability to license or own the underlying content. If the reports are uncopyrightable, competitors could potentially scrape and resell the same data, and your "license" may offer little legal protection against third-party use.
3.  **Terms of Service:** You cannot simply "assert ownership" in an agreement if the law dictates the work is ineligible for copyright. Your counsel must draft language that addresses the *contractual* right to use the data, even if *statutory* copyright is absent.
