### Arithmetic Application: The Administrative Cap Constraint

You stated many foundations cap administrative overhead at 10% of the total grant award. We will calculate if your fee alone exceeds this, ignoring the non-profit's internal costs (which would make the situation stricter).

**The Formula:**
$$ \text{Fee Percentage of Grant} = \left( \frac{\text{Success Fee Amount}}{\text{Total Grant Award}} \right) \times 100 $$

**Your Inputs:**
*   Average Grant Award: $50,000
*   Your Success Fee Rate: 10% of the award
*   Calculated Success Fee Amount: $50,000 \times 0.10 = \$5,000$

**The Calculation:**
$$ \text{Fee Percentage} = \left( \frac{5,000}{50,000} \right) \times 100 = 10\% $$

**Sensitivity Analysis (The "Total Admin" Problem):**
The constraint is usually on *total* administrative costs, not just your fee.
$$ \text{Total Admin \%} = \text{Your Fee \%} + \text{Non-Profit Internal Admin \%} $$
$$ \text{Total Admin \%} = 10\% + \text{Internal Costs} $$

**Result:**
If a foundation strictly caps total administrative overhead at 10%, and your fee constitutes exactly 10% of the grant award, then **any** internal administrative cost incurred by the non-profit (even 1%) pushes the total to 11%, technically exceeding the cap.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Read:** On a $50k grant, your success fee alone consumes the entire typical 10% administrative allowance.
*   **Flip Input:** The variable that breaks compliance is the *foundation's specific definition* of "administrative costs." Some may exclude external professional services from the cap; others include them.
*   **Threshold Rule of Thumb:** If a cap is 10% and your fee is 10% of the award, you have zero margin for the client's internal costs under that specific cap.
