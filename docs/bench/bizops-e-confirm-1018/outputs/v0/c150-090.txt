**Lane R: Regulatory Determination (Route Immediately)**
Your primary question—whether success-based fees violate donor restrictions or foundation guidelines—is a **Lane R** matter. In the United States, the permissibility of contingency fees for grant writing is not a math problem; it is a legal and ethical determination governed by:
1.  **The Association of Fundraising Professionals (AFP) Code of Ethical Principles:** Specifically Standard #22, which states members shall not accept compensation based on a percentage of charitable contributions. While AFP cannot fine non-members, many foundations adopt this standard into their grant agreements.
2.  **Foundation Grant Agreements:** Private foundations often explicitly prohibit using grant funds for contingency fees or "administrative costs" defined to include success fees.
3.  **State Solicitation Laws:** Some states regulate fundraising counsel and may restrict contingency arrangements.

**Action:** You must route this specific compliance question to legal counsel specializing in non-profit law before marketing a success-based model. A single violation could disqualify your clients from future funding, creating significant liability for GrantGenie. Do not implement the 10% model until a professional confirms it does not breach the specific guidelines of your target foundations.

1.  **Tiered Value-Based Pricing (Good-Better-Best):**
    *   *Structure:* Keep the $79 base. Add a "Pro" tier at $299/month that includes advanced analytics, priority support, or multi-user access.
    *   *Upside Logic:* If a client wins a large grant, they are more likely to upgrade to the Pro tier to manage it. You capture upside via volume/feature adoption, not a direct cut of the grant.
    *   *Math:* If 20% of users upgrade to $299, revenue becomes $(160 \times 79) + (40 \times 299) = $12,640 + $11,960 = $24,600. This increases monthly surplus to $12,600.

2.  **Outcome-Linked Retainer (Not Contingency):**
    *   *Structure:* Charge a higher flat fee (e.g., $500/month) contractually defined as "preparation and strategy services," regardless of the win.
    *   *Differentiation:* The fee is for the *work performed* (the input), not the *result* (the output). This is generally permissible where contingency fees are not, provided the contract does not state payment is *conditional* on winning.

3.  **Usage-Based Scaling:**
    *   *Structure:* Base fee of $79 + $X per grant application submitted or per page generated.
    *   *Upside Logic:* Clients applying for larger/more grants use more of your tool. You scale with their activity, not their award amount.
