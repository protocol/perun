**Actionable Triage & Next Steps:**

1.  **Name the Regime:** The relevant body is the **Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA)** under the U.S. Department of Labor. Key concepts include "authorized training," "competent person" definitions, and record-keeping validity under 29 CFR.
2.  **The Boundary:** OSHA generally does not "certify" or "accredit" private training providers directly for most general industry standards, but it strictly defines *who* can train (e.g., a "competent person" vs. an "authorized trainer" for specific Outreach programs). Whether a VR simulation satisfies the *content* and *interaction* requirements for a specific standard is a legal interpretation, not a math problem.
3.  **Immediate Route:** Engage an attorney specializing in industrial safety regulation or contact an **OSHA Education Center** (often affiliated with universities) for a formal review of your curriculum against specific standards.
4.  **Preparation for Counsel:** Before your consultation, gather:
    *   The specific OSHA standards your course claims to satisfy (e.g., Fire Prevention Plans, Emergency Action Plans).
    *   Your current curriculum outline and assessment methods.
    *   Documentation of your instructors' qualifications.
    *   The specific client inquiry regarding "compliance credit."

**Do not** proceed with the $30,000 content investment assuming regulatory acceptance until you have a written legal opinion or confirmation from an authorized body. The risk of your client being penalized for using unrecognized training is the primary liability here.

**Note:** OSHA sometimes accepts alternative training methods if they are proven to be as effective as prescribed methods, but the burden of proof lies with the employer/provider.
