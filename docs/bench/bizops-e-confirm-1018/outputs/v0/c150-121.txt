### Lane A: Arithmetic & Cash Flow Structure

Your runway model must treat the DEA quota as a hard ceiling on revenue, not a variable cost. Because your constraint is regulatory (200g limit), your "Net Monthly Burn" calculation changes from standard SaaS logic to a capacity-constrained model.

**1. The Capacity-Constrained Runway Formula**
Standard runway (`Cash ÷ Net Burn`) fails here because your burn is artificially inflated by refunds. You must model two scenarios:

*   **Scenario A (Current State):** You accept orders exceeding capacity and refund 15%.
    *   *Potential Revenue:* 85 subscribers × $450 = $38,250.
    *   *Refunded Revenue (15%):* $5,737 (Cash outflow via refunds or lost inflow).
    *   *Realized Revenue:* $32,513.
    *   *COGS:* 85 units × $120 = $10,200 (Assuming you purchase for all, then refund product/cash, or only purchase for fulfilled. If you refund cash, the COGS on refunded units is saved, but the administrative churn cost remains).
    *   *Adjusted Net Burn:* Operating Expenses − ($32,513 − Saved COGS on refunds).

*   **Scenario B (Capacity-Capped Growth):** You cap new sales to match the 200g limit exactly.
    *   *Max Fulfillable Subscribers:* If 1 unit = 1 subscriber dose, determine grams per unit. If 85 subscribers consume <200g, you have unused quota. If 85 subscribers ≈ 200g, you are at capacity.
    *   *Assumption:* If 85 subscribers already strain the 200g limit, adding new customers guarantees refunds.
    *   *Revised Runway:* `Cash ÷ (Operating Expenses − (Max Capacity Revenue − Max Capacity COGS))`.

**Decision Aid:**
If your refund rate (15%) exceeds your Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) amortization, acquiring new users destroys value. The "flip input" is your **Refund Rate vs. Margin**. If `Refund % > Gross Margin %`, growth is mathematically destructive.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* If LTV:CAC drops below 3 due to refund churn, freeze acquisition.
*   *Next Step:* Calculate the exact gram usage per subscriber. If `85 × grams/unit` approaches 200g, immediately halt new sales until quota increases.

### Lane R: Regulatory Compliance (Route to Counsel)

Your question regarding "carrying over unused quota allowances" and "compliance-safe smoothing" falls strictly under **Lane R**.

*   **Regime:** The Controlled Substances Act (CSA) and DEA procurement quotas (21 CFR § 1303).
*   **The Boundary:** DEA quotas are typically annual allowances with specific ordering rules. Whether unused monthly portions can be "banked," if there is a "use-it-or-lose-it" monthly clause, or if altering order patterns to smooth financials triggers audit flags is a legal determination.
*   **Action Required:** Do not attempt to structure inventory carry-over based on financial modeling alone. You must consult a regulatory attorney specializing in controlled substances.
    *   *Documents to bring:* Your current DEA registration, the specific quota approval letter showing the 200g limit, and your monthly order logs.
    *   *Time-Sensitive Item:** Ask counsel specifically if "quota borrowing" from future periods or "carry-forward" of unused monthly amounts is permitted under your specific schedule classification before adjusting your purchasing strategy.

**Summary:** Use the Lane A formulas to freeze acquisition if refunds exceed margins. Do not attempt to manipulate quota usage timing without explicit confirmation from DEA counsel (Lane R).
