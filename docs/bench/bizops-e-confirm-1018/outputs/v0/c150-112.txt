### Regulatory Risk Assessment & Infrastructure Options

Your situation involves **Lane R (Regulation-Dependent)** questions regarding data sovereignty and retention. The correct answers depend on specific legal interpretations of the GDPR, the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, and conflicting US state notary laws. I cannot conclude whether your current setup violates the law or if Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) are sufficient; only counsel can make that determination.

However, I can outline the regimes involved and the structural options you must discuss with a lawyer:

**1. The Regimes Involved**
*   **GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation):** Applies because you process personal data of individuals in the EU. Video recordings of notarizations are highly sensitive.
*   **EU-US Data Transfers:** The core issue is transferring EU personal data to the US (Virginia). Following the *Schrems II* ruling, relying solely on SCCs may be insufficient without a "Transfer Impact Assessment" (TIA) to ensure US law (e.g., FISA 702) does not undermine EU protections.
*   **US State Notary Laws:** Each US state has specific statutes regarding how long notary journals and recordings must be retained (often 5–10 years), which may conflict with GDPR's "storage limitation" principle (keep only as long as necessary).

**2. Immediate Risks to Address with Counsel**
*   **Invalid Transfer Mechanism:** If you rely only on SCCs without supplementary measures (like encryption where the provider holds no keys), regulators may deem the transfer illegal.
*   **Retention Conflict:** Storing data indefinitely to satisfy the strictest US state law could violate GDPR if the purpose (notarization) is fulfilled earlier.
*   **Sensitive Data Classification:** Video biometrics may classify as "special category data," triggering higher compliance bars.

**3. Structural Options for Your Infrastructure**
You do not necessarily *have* to move all hosting to Europe, but you likely need to modify your architecture. Discuss these three models with counsel:

*   **Option A: Data Residency (EU Hosting).** Move video storage entirely to EU-based servers. This eliminates the cross-border transfer risk for the primary data store. US access would then be treated as a separate, limited transfer.
*   **Option B: Supplemental Technical Measures.** Keep US hosting but implement "bring your own key" (BYOK) encryption or tokenization so that US server administrators cannot access the clear-text video content, potentially satisfying transfer requirements under SCCs.
*   **Option C: Hybrid Segmentation.** Store EU client metadata in the EU and only transfer anonymized or strictly necessary data to the US, though this is difficult for video evidence.

**4. Required Next Steps (Pre-Legal Prep)**
Before your legal consultation, prepare the following to make the engagement efficient:
*   **Data Mapping:** Document exactly what data flows from Germany to Virginia (IP addresses, video files, metadata).
*   **Current Contracts:** Gather your current Data Processing Agreements (DPAs) with your cloud provider.
*   **Retention Schedule:** List the specific retention periods required by the US states where your 25 notaries are commissioned.

**Decision Aid:** Do not assume SCCs are sufficient. The "safe harbor" approach is dead; the current standard requires a case-by-case assessment of US surveillance laws versus your specific data sensitivity. Engage a privacy attorney specializing in EU-US transfers immediately before accepting new German clients.
