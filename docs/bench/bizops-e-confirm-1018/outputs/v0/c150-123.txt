### Lane R: Regulatory Boundary (Route Immediately)
Your core concern—whether bundling your 20% commission causes a reporter to technically exceed the $3.75/page statutory cap—is a **legal interpretation of California fee schedules and unauthorized practice of law statutes**.
*   **The Regime:** California Code of Regulations regarding certified transcript rates and potential rules against "splitting fees" with non-reporters.
*   **The Risk:** If the state views your platform fee as part of the "charge for the transcript," your reporters could be in violation of the cap, risking their licenses. If the state views your fee as a separate "administrative" or "technology" charge, it may be permissible, but only if invoiced correctly.
*   **Action Required:** Do not implement a new pricing model until a lawyer confirms that unbundling (charging the firm a "platform fee" separate from the "transcript fee") is compliant. You need a formal opinion on whether the "maximum mandated rate" applies to the *total cost to the consumer* or strictly the *reporter's compensation*.

**Option 1: Strict Unbundling (Fee Separation)**
Structure the invoice with two distinct line items.
*   **Line Item A (Transcript):** Billed at exactly the statutory max (e.g., $3.75 × Page Count). Paid 100% to the reporter.
*   **Line Item B (Platform/Technology Fee):** A fixed fee or percentage charged by DepoStream for scheduling, video hosting, and matching.
*   **The Math:** To maintain your $120 average revenue per booking:
    *   If average deposition is 160 pages: Transcript Cap = $600. (This leaves $0 for your fee if the cap applies to the total).
    *   If average deposition is 120 pages: Transcript Cap = $450.
    *   **Your Slot:** Platform Fee = Total Bill - (Pages × $3.75).
    *   **Constraint:** You must ensure the "Platform Fee" is not legally construed as a surcharge on the transcript itself.
