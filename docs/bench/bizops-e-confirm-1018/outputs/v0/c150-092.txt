Your question regarding the correct depreciation schedule (12 years vs. 3 years) and its impact on taxable income is a **Lane R** matter. Determining the useful life of an asset for tax and book purposes depends on specific regulations (such as IRS MACRS guidelines in the US) and accounting standards (GAAP). A professional must determine if the software updates constitute a separate asset, a leasehold improvement, or a maintenance expense, and whether the hardware and software must be bundled. **Do not file taxes or finalize financial statements based on a chosen schedule without a CPA's validation.**

However, I can apply **Lane A** arithmetic to show how the *choice* of schedule mechanically affects your financial outputs, assuming you have validated the inputs with a professional.

### The Arithmetic of Depreciation Choices

**Inputs Provided:**
*   Unit Cost: $120,000
*   Units Sold/Deployed: 5
*   Total Asset Base: $600,000 ($120k × 5)
*   Scenario A (Hardware Life): 12 years
*   Scenario B (Software Cycle): 3 years

**1. Annual Depreciation Expense (Straight-Line Method)**
*Formula:* `Annual Expense = Total Asset Base ÷ Useful Life`

*   **Scenario A (12-Year):** $600,000 ÷ 12 = **$50,000 per year**
*   **Scenario B (3-Year):** $600,000 ÷ 3 = **$200,000 per year**

**2. Impact on Book Value Over 5 Years**
*Formula:* `Ending Book Value = Cost Basis − (Annual Expense × Years Elapsed)`

| Year | Scenario A (12-Yr) Book Value | Scenario B (3-Yr) Book Value |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Start | $600,000 | $600,000 |
| Year 1 | $550,000 | $400,000 |
| Year 2 | $500,000 | $200,000 |
| Year 3 | $450,000 | $0 (Fully Depreciated) |
| Year 4 | $400,000 | $0 |
| Year 5 | $350,000 | $0 |

**3. Impact on Taxable Income (Conceptual)**
Depreciation is a non-cash expense that reduces reported profit.
*   **Scenario A:** Reduces taxable income by **$50k/year**. Higher reported profit, higher potential tax liability (if profitable), but shows stronger assets on the balance sheet.
*   **Scenario B:** Reduces taxable income by **$200k/year** for the first three years. Lower reported profit, lower potential tax liability, but assets vanish from the balance sheet quickly.

### Decision Aid for Investor Context

You mentioned investors focus on "asset longevity."
*   **If you choose Scenario A:** Your balance sheet retains $350k in asset value at Year 5. This may signal stability and long-term utility to investors.
*   **If you choose Scenario B:** Your balance sheet shows $0 asset value for these units after Year 3. Investors may question the longevity of your core product or assume frequent, costly replacements are needed.

**The Flip Input:** The decision hinges on the **regulatory determination of "useful life."** If the IRS or GAAP mandates the 3-year cycle due to software obsolescence, you cannot choose the 12-year schedule to look better for investors; doing so would be non-compliant. If the software update is deemed "maintenance" rather than a life-limiting factor, the 12-year schedule may be permissible.
