*   **Variable Cost/Inspection:** $95
*   **Contribution Margin:** $155 ($250 - $95)
*   **Margin %:** 62%
*   **Comparison:** This scenario yields a higher absolute dollar margin ($155) than your current model ($130), *if* volume remains constant.

**Break-Even Volume Sensitivity**
To maintain your current total monthly contribution profit of $260,000 (2,000 units × $130 margin):
*   **At $150 price:** You need 4,728 inspections/month (likely impossible if margins are tight).
*   **At $250 price:** You need 1,678 inspections/month.
*   **Formula:** `Required Volume = Target Total Margin ÷ New Unit Margin`
*   **Interpretation:** At $250, you can lose ~16% of your customer base (322 inspections) and still match your current total profit dollars.

**Decision Aid**
1.  **Read:** Moving to $250 improves per-unit profit but introduces volume risk. The "flip input" is **price elasticity**: if demand drops by more than 16%, total profit declines.
2.  **Threshold Rule of Thumb:** If your historical data shows customers are highly sensitive to price (elastic), a 66% price hike may reduce volume beyond the 16% buffer. If demand is inelastic (e.g., insurance-mandated inspections), the hike may hold.
3.  **Next Step:** Before changing prices, run a "Van Westendorp" price sensitivity survey with a sample of past leads or current customers to identify the acceptable price band for $250 vs. $150. Do not assume the market will accept the increase without data.
