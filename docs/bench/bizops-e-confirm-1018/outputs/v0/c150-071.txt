### Lane A: Arithmetic & Unit Economics (Apply)

Your current model shows strong unit economics but a hard capacity ceiling.
*   **Contribution Margin per Box:** $450 (Price) − $210 (Variable Cost) = **$240**.
*   **Current Monthly Contribution:** 800 units (capped) × $240 = **$192,000**.
*   **Lost Contribution Due to Cap:** You have 1,200 subscribers but can only fill 800. Assuming you prorate or refund the 400 unfilled orders, you are leaving **$96,000** in monthly contribution margin on the table, plus incurring reputational damage from refunds.

**Pricing & Allocation Modeling**
Since you cannot increase volume beyond 800 units due to DEA quotas, your leverage lies in **price** and **allocation**.
1.  **Price Sensitivity Test:** To maximize revenue within the 800-unit cap, you could test a higher price point.
    *   *Formula:* `New Monthly Contribution = 800 × (New Price − $210)`.
    *   *Application:* If you raise the price to $600, contribution becomes $312,000/month. You must determine if demand remains at 800+ subscribers at this price. Use a **Van Westendorp** survey with your waitlist to find the acceptable price band before implementing.
2.  **Waitlist as Cash Reserve:** Instead of refunding the 400 overflow customers, place them on a paid waitlist (or hold a deposit).
    *   *Cash Flow Impact:* Collecting a deposit (e.g., $50) from 400 waitlisted users generates **$20,000** in immediate non-dilutive cash to smooth supplier payments, without triggering a fulfillment obligation that violates the quota.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Read:** Your bottleneck is regulatory, not market demand. Increasing price is the only arithmetic lever to increase total contribution margin immediately.
*   **Flip Input:** The break-even price for your current margin is $210. Any price above this yields profit; the optimal price is the highest the market bears before churn exceeds your ability to fill the 800 slots.
*   **Threshold:** If raising prices causes active subscribers to churn such that total demand drops below 800, the strategy fails. Monitor churn closely; if it spikes above 4% post-hike, revert.
*   **Next Step:** This week, segment your 400 unfilled customers. Offer them a "priority access" deposit option rather than a refund to test willingness to pay and generate immediate working capital.

### Lane R: Regulation & Compliance (Route)

Your request touches on three distinct regulated regimes that require professional counsel. Do not act on these without specific advice.

1.  **DEA Quota Compliance (Controlled Substances Act):**
    *   **Regime:** U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) regulations regarding Schedule II-V substances.
    *   **Risk:** "Expanding supplier network internationally" to bypass U.S. quotas is a high-risk trigger. Importing controlled substances requires specific DEA import licenses and adherence to international treaties. Sourcing outside the authorized quota system can lead to criminal liability and loss of licensure.
    *   **Action:** Route to a **controlled substances regulatory attorney**. Ask specifically: "What are the legal mechanisms, if any, to petition for a quota increase based on unmet medical need, and what are the compliance requirements for international sourcing?"

2.  **Securities & Cash Reserves:**
    *   **Regime:** SEC Regulations (if raising capital) or State Money Transmitter laws (if holding customer deposits).
    *   **Risk:** If "holding cash reserves" involves taking prepayments for future goods you cannot guarantee delivery on due to quotas, this may be scrutinized as unfair business practices or, if structured as an investment in future inventory, a securities offering.
    *   **Action:** Route to **general corporate counsel**. Ask: "Does holding deposits for a quota-constrained waitlist create liability under consumer protection laws or escheatment rules?"

3.  **International Trade & Sanctions:**
    *   **Regime:** OFAC (Office of Foreign Assets Control) and FDA import regulations.
    *   **Risk:** Sourcing ingredients internationally introduces sanctions screening and FDA prior notice requirements.
    *   **Action:** Route to an **international trade lawyer**.

**Summary for Professional Engagement:** Bring your current DEA quota order form, a list of prospective international suppliers, and your proposed waitlist terms to counsel. The time-sensitive item is the international sourcing plan; do not contact foreign suppliers until cleared.
