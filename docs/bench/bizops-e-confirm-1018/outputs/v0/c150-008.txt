### Lane A: Arithmetic Application & Trade-off Analysis

You provided specific inputs; the following applies your numbers to two pricing models to show the impact on Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) and cash flow over 18 months.

**Inputs Provided:**
*   Hardware Cost: $2,000/unit
*   Pilot Customers: 50 units
*   Monthly Burn: $35,000
*   Cash on Hand: $500,000
*   Timeframe: 18 months

**Model 1: High Upfront ($5,000 HW + $50/mo SW)**
*   **Upfront Cash per Unit:** $5,000
*   **Monthly Recurring per Unit:** $50
*   **Total Upfront Cash (50 units):** $250,000
*   **Monthly ARR (50 units):** $2,500 ($30,000 annualized)
*   **Gross Margin per Unit (HW):** $5,000 - $2,000 = $3,000
*   **18-Month Cash Flow Projection:**
    *   Inflow: $250,000 (HW) + ($2,500 × 18 mo) = $295,000
    *   Outflow (Burn): $35,000 × 18 = $630,000
    *   *Net Cash Change:* -$335,000
    *   *Ending Cash:* $165,000

**Model 2: Low Upfront ($2,000 HW + $400/mo SW)**
*   **Upfront Cash per Unit:** $2,000 (Break-even on hardware cost)
*   **Monthly Recurring per Unit:** $400
*   **Total Upfront Cash (50 units):** $100,000
*   **Monthly ARR (50 units):** $20,000 ($240,000 annualized)
*   **Gross Margin per Unit (HW):** $0
*   **18-Month Cash Flow Projection:**
    *   Inflow: $100,000 (HW) + ($20,000 × 18 mo) = $460,000
    *   Outflow (Burn): $35,000 × 18 = $630,000
    *   *Net Cash Change:* -$170,000
    *   *Ending Cash:* $330,000

**Comparative Results:**
*   **ARR Impact:** Model 2 generates **8x higher ARR** ($240k vs $30k). Since investors often value SaaS revenue at higher multiples than hardware sales, Model 2 likely yields a higher pre-money valuation *if* the revenue is recognized as recurring.
*   **Cash Flow Impact:** Model 2 leaves you with **$165,000 more cash** at month 18 due to the higher cumulative subscription inflow, despite lower initial upfront collection.
*   **Runway Extension:** At a $35k burn, Model 1 provides ~4.7 months of runway from current cash + pilot inflows. Model 2 provides ~9.4 months.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **The Flip Input:** The critical variable is **churn**. If farmers cancel the $400/mo plan rapidly because they prefer CapEx, the ARR advantage evaporates.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If LTV:CAC < 3 or payback > 12 months, reconsider the model. Here, payback on customer acquisition cost (not provided) must be weighed against the $400 monthly hurdle.
*   **Next Step:** If you fear the $400 price point reduces conversion by more than 50% compared to the $50 model, run a Van Westendorp price sensitivity survey with your pilot list to find the "too expensive" threshold before locking the model.
