**Regime Identification:**
The relevant regime is the **California Public Utilities Code**, enforced by the **CPUC**. The determination hinges on whether your activity constitutes "serving the public" with a "utility service." Key legal tests often include:
*   Whether you hold yourself out as serving all who apply (or a broad segment of the public).
*   Whether the service provided is essential to the public interest.
*   The nature of the aggregation and resale of energy.

**Potential Compliance Burdens (If Classified as a Utility):**
If the CPUC deems you a public utility, typical obligations (which your counsel must verify) may include:
*   **Rate Regulation:** Prices and fees may need CPUC approval; you cannot unilaterally set rates.
*   **Reporting & Auditing:** Mandatory financial and operational reporting, often subject to audit.
*   **Service Obligations:** Requirements to serve all customers within a territory without discrimination.
*   **Safety & Reliability Standards:** Strict adherence to grid safety and reliability protocols.
*   **Certificate of Public Convenience and Necessity (CPCN):** You may need to obtain this certificate before operating, a lengthy and costly process.
