### Lane A: Unit Economics & Pricing Structure (Apply Your Numbers)
We can apply arithmetic to your current data to visualize the trade-offs between price, volume, and margin. The decision on the final price remains yours.

**Current Snapshot (Monthly):**
*   **Volume:** 45,000 checks
*   **Price:** £2.00/check
*   **Revenue:** £90,000
*   **Variable Cost:** £0.40/check (£18,000 total)
*   **Contribution Margin:** £1.60/check (£72,000 total)
*   **Fixed Costs (Implied):** With a £35k burn and £72k contribution, your current net cash flow is positive (~£37k/month), consistent with your 18-month runway description.

**The False Positive Friction:**
An 8% false positive rate is a product quality metric, not just a pricing one. If this rate drives churn (loss of 3 major clients), lowering the price may not solve retention if the core utility (accurate verification) is compromised.

**Pricing Model Options to Analyze:**

1.  **Cost-Plus with Accuracy Buffer:**
    *   *Formula:* `Price = (Variable Cost + Target Margin) / (1 - Error Cost Factor)`
    *   *Application:* If you allocate a portion of revenue to fund the 6 engineers improving the algorithm, you might increase the price.
    *   *Sensitivity:* If you raise prices to £2.50 to fund R&D, but volume drops 10% due to competitiveness, does the higher margin per unit offset the volume loss?
        *   Current Contribution: £72,000.
        *   Scenario (£2.50 price, 10% volume drop): 40,500 checks × (£2.50 - £0.40) = £85,050.
        *   *Result:* Mathematically, a price increase with moderate volume loss improves cash flow to fund accuracy improvements.

2.  **Tiered/Outcome-Based Pricing:**
    *   *Structure:* Instead of a flat £2.00, consider a base fee + a premium for "guaranteed manual review" on flagged items.
    *   *Application:* Charge £1.50 for automated checks and £5.00 for any check requiring human intervention (which you trigger internally for borderline cases to lower the client's visible false positive rate).
    *   *Effect:* This shifts your variable cost structure. You absorb the cost of manual reviews only when necessary, potentially lowering the *effective* error rate seen by the client without lowering the headline price.

**Decision Aid:**
*   **Break-Even Analysis:** Your fixed costs are roughly £35k. At £1.60 contribution per check, you need ~21,875 checks/month to break even. You are currently at 45,000. You have significant room to absorb volume shocks.
*   **The Flip Input:** The variable that flips your strategy is **Churn Rate vs. Price Elasticity**. If raising prices causes >25% volume loss, the strategy fails. If keeping prices low causes >2 more major clients to leave next quarter, the strategy fails.
*   **Next Step:** Before changing prices, instrument the cost of the 8% error. Calculate the exact revenue lost from the 3 departed clients. If the cost of those lost clients exceeds the potential revenue from a price increase, prioritize using existing cash flow to fix the algorithm immediately rather than adjusting pricing.
