**The Call**
Do not reject local storage based solely on the insurance hike; the $84/unit cost reduces your net margin from 8% to roughly 4.5%, which is tight but viable if local storage saves more than $84/unit in engineering and cloud infrastructure costs. If the cloud alternative costs less than $84 per unit (amortized) to build and operate, choose cloud to preserve margin; otherwise, absorb the insurance increase and mitigate physical theft risk through device-level hardening.

**The Numbers**
*Current Unit Economics (Base Case):*
*   **Sale Price:** $2,400
*   **COGS (Manufacturing):** $850
*   **Gross Profit:** $1,550 (64.6% margin)
*   **CAC:** $400
*   **Current Insurance Allocation:** $60/unit ($18,000 / 300 units)
*   **Net Profit (stated):** ~$192/unit (8% of $2,400)

*Scenario A: Local Storage (With Insurance Hike)*
*   **New Insurance Allocation:** $84/unit ($25,200 / 300 units)
*   **Incremental Cost:** +$24/unit vs base allocation
*   **Projected Net Profit:** $192 - $24 = **$168/unit** (~7% net margin)
    *   *Note: Your estimate of 61% gross margin appears to treat insurance as COGS. Insurance is typically an operating expense. Even if treated as COGS, the gross margin drops to ~61%, but the critical failure point is the net margin compression.*

*Scenario B: Cloud Storage (Break-Even Analysis)*
*   **Target Savings:** To beat Scenario A, the total cost of cloud engineering + ongoing cloud ops must be **<$84/unit** lower than the local storage engineering cost.
*   **ASSUMED (Low):** Cloud solution costs $20/unit to implement/maintain. **Result:** Choose Cloud (Saves $64/unit vs insurance hike).
*   **ASSUMED (High):** Cloud solution costs $150/unit to implement/maintain. **Result:** Choose Local (Insurance hike is cheaper).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The decision flips on the **amortized engineering and operational cost of the cloud solution**.
*   **Trigger Value:** If your cloud architecture (development + 5-year hosting/encryption management) exceeds **$25,200 total** (or $84 × 300 units), the insurance hike is the cheaper option.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In high-security hardware, recurring liability costs (insurance) are often preferable to large upfront engineering debt unless the liability risk threatens existential litigation (beyond premium hikes).
*   **Step this week:** Calculate the total 5-year cost of the cloud encryption architecture. If the figure is under $25,000, pivot to cloud immediately. If over, accept the premium hike and invest the difference in physical anti-tamper hardware.

**Routing Note**
The insurance carrier's 40% hike is an underwriting determination, not a legal mandate. Before accepting the hike or spending engineering budget, ask your broker: "Is the 40% increase binding if we implement specific physical anti-tamper seals or remote-wipe capabilities on locally stored devices?" This may lower the risk profile without moving to the cloud.
