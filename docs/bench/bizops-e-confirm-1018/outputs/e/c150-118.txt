**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **who sets the final price charged to the immigrant client**.
*   **Trigger:** If a law firm charges a client a flat fee that results in an effective hourly rate of less than $50 (due to high complexity) or more than $1,000 (due to low complexity) without disclosure, an ethics complaint becomes likely.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Flat fees are ethical if the lawyer performs a "reasonableness check" before quoting.
*   **Actionable Step:** If your contract implies your $2,000 *is* the legal fee, consider adding a "complexity surcharge" module or, more safely, reposition your product as a tool with a fixed cost that *informs* the lawyer's variable fee quote, rather than the fee itself.

**Ethical Guidelines and Safeguards (Tier 2 Explanation)**
In the US, ABA Model Rule 1.5 permits flat fees if they are reasonable. Factors include time, labor, novelty, difficulty, and custom. Immigration law frequently uses flat fees. The responsibility for ethical billing lies **solely with the law firm**, not your software company. However, if your tool forces a rigid price that prevents the lawyer from adjusting for complexity, you become a business risk.
*   **Safeguard 1:** Add a "Case Complexity Estimator" inside CaseMate that suggests a fee range (Low/Med/High) based on case type, allowing the lawyer to override it.
*   **Safeguard 2:** Include a disclaimer in your terms: "CaseMate fees are software costs; Attorney fees are determined separately by the firm."
*   **Safeguard 3:** If firms must pass your cost directly, offer a tiered SaaS plan (e.g., $500 for renewals, $2,000 for asylum) to better align with work variance.
