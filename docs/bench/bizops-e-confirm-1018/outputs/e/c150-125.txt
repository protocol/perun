**The Call**
Do not market "full copyright ownership" of the raw AI-generated draft as a guaranteed deliverable; under current US Copyright Office (USCO) guidance, works with dominant AI generation (85%) are likely uncopyrightable regardless of user edits unless the human contribution constitutes the "bulk" of expressive originality. Pivot your value proposition immediately to "patent preparation and drafting assistance" rather than "IP ownership transfer," and treat the copyrightability of the final document as a tier-3 legal determination that requires counsel before your next feature launch.

**The Numbers**
There is no mathematical formula or "safe harbor" percentage (e.g., "15% is enough") for copyright eligibility; the USCO applies a qualitative test of human authorship, not a quantitative word-count threshold.
*   **Current State:** 85% AI / 15% Human.
    *   *Assessment (Tier 2):* High risk of rejection for copyright registration. The USCO has consistently ruled that AI-generated text is not protectable, and minor human editing does not salvage the whole work.
*   **Required Shift:** To claim copyright, the human must author the *protectable elements* (the specific expression, arrangement, and creative choices), not just the technical facts.
    *   *Assumption (Labelled):* If your users only input technical specs and claims (facts/ideas) while the AI writes the specification narrative, the resulting text is likely public domain.
*   **Financial Impact:** If copyright fails, your $199 price point may face churn if "ownership" was the primary driver, though the utility for filing a provisional patent (which relies on disclosure, not copyright) may remain.
    *   *Runway Check:* With $60k cash and ~$24k/mo revenue (assuming stable volume), you have ~2.5 months of runway if growth stalls due to this uncertainty.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the outcome is the **nature of the human input**, not the volume. If users provide the creative structure, specific phrasing, and unique argumentation flow (making the AI a mere spellchecker or formatter), copyright becomes viable. If users provide only data points and the AI generates the prose, it does not.
*   **Trigger:** The moment you market "guaranteed copyright ownership" without a legal opinion confirming your specific workflow passes the USCO's "human authorship" test.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the AI writes the sentences, the human doesn't own the copyright. If the human writes the sentences and the AI suggests them, the human might.
*   **Step This Week:** If your current flow is "user inputs facts → AI writes draft," consider adding a mandatory "human rewrite" step or clearly labeling the output as "uncopyrightable draft for patent filing purposes only" in your UI.
