**The Call:** Treat the 10% success fee as a probable compliance violation for foundations with strict 10% administrative caps; you cannot safely leverage exceptions without a legal determination, so you must prepare a pivot to a flat-fee or capped-fee model immediately while validating specific foundation rules.

**The Numbers:**
The risk is calculated by summing your fee against the non-profit's internal administrative costs against the 10% cap.
*Formula:* `Total Admin % = (Your Fee + Non-Profit Internal Admin Costs) / Total Grant Award`
*User Figures:*
*   Total Grant Award: $50,000
*   Your Success Fee: $5,000 (10% of $50k)
*   Your Fee % of Grant: 10%
*   Foundation Cap: 10%

*Scenario Analysis (Labelled Assumptions):*
1.  **Base Case (ASSUMED):** Non-profit has *any* internal administrative allocation (even 1%).
    *   Calculation: 10% (Your Fee) + 1% (Internal) = 11%.
    *   Result: **Violation** (11% > 10% cap).
2.  **Best Case (ASSUMED):** Non-profit has $0 internal administrative costs allocated to this grant (highly unlikely for reporting/compliance).
    *   Calculation: 10% (Your Fee) + 0% = 10%.
    *   Result: **Borderline** (Touches the exact limit; often rejected due to rounding or audit risk).
3.  **Flat Fee Only:** Remove success fee, charge only $2,000.
    *   Calculation: $2,000 / $50,000 = 4%.
    *   Result: **Safe** (Leaves 6% room for internal costs).

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **Non-Profit's Internal Admin Rate**. If a client can document zero internal admin costs, you might pass, but this is operationally rare.
*Trigger:* If a target foundation's guidelines state "administrative costs including external consultants," your current model is a hard stop.
*Rule of Thumb:* If your fee alone equals the cap, you have no margin for the client's unavoidable overhead.
*This Week's Step:* If your top 3 revenue-generating foundations have strict caps, consider pausing the success-fee component for those specific applications and switching to a higher flat fee or a "capped success fee" (e.g., success fee stops accruing once total admin hits 8%).

**Routing Note:**
Determining whether your specific contract structure classifies as "administrative overhead" under a specific foundation's bylaws or state solicitation laws is a binding legal determination. You must ask counsel: "Does our success-fee structure violate [Specific Foundation]'s administrative cap policy, and does it trigger state fundraising solicitor registration requirements?" Do not rely on general interpretations for this binary compliance gate.
