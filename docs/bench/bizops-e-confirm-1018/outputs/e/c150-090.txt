**The Call:** Reject the 10% success-based fee model immediately; it violates standard foundation guidelines and IRS regulations for U.S. non-profits, creating existential compliance risk for your customers. Pivot to a "Good-Better-Best" tiered subscription with a fixed "performance bonus" capped at a flat dollar amount or a percentage of *your* fee savings, not the grant total.

**The Numbers:**
*   **Current State:** $15,800 MRR - $12,000 Burn = $3,800 Monthly Surplus.
*   **Runway:** $60,000 Cash / $12,000 Burn = **5.0 months** (assuming no growth/churn).
*   **Hypothetical Success Model (Illustrative Only):** If a client secures a $50,000 grant, your fee is $5,000.
    *   *Constraint:* Most federal grants (2 CFR 200) and private foundations cap administrative/indirect costs between 10–14%. A 10% *contingency* fee is often viewed as an unallowable "finder's fee" rather than a legitimate administrative cost, regardless of the cap.
    *   *Risk:* If the fee is disallowed, the non-profit must repay it from unrestricted funds, damaging their relationship with the funder.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The binding constraint is not your revenue potential, but the **IRS prohibition on private inurement** and **foundation guidelines against contingency fees** for grant writing.
*   **The Rule:** The IRS generally considers contingency fees for grant writing as impermissible private benefit because the writer shares in the public funds intended for the mission. Many foundation guidelines explicitly state: "Grant funds may not be used to pay fundraising consultants on a contingency basis."
*   **The Trigger:** If a single major donor or foundation audits your largest client and flags your fee, that client will churn immediately to protect their status, and your reputation in the tight-knit non-profit sector will collapse.
*   **Alternative Structure (Tier 1 Math):** Instead of 10% of the grant, charge a **Fixed Success Bonus**.
    *   *Formula:* Base Subscription ($79) + Bonus (e.g., $500 flat fee upon award notification).
    *   *Why:* This treats the bonus as a fixed administrative cost, which is easier for non-profits to justify within their 10–14% indirect cost rate than an open-ended percentage of the award.
    *   *Action:* If your average grant win is $20,000, a $500 bonus is effectively 2.5% to the client but avoids the "contingency" label.

**Routing Note:**
Consult a U.S. non-profit tax attorney before finalizing any "performance" language in your contracts. Specifically ask: "Does a fixed bonus tied to grant award notification constitute a prohibited contingency fee under IRS Rev. Rul. 69-528 or specific state charitable solicitation laws?"
