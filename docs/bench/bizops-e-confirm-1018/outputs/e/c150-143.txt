**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The trigger is not the *amount* of power sold, but the *nature of the contract*. If GridShare takes title to the energy, sets the price independently of the homeowner's specific instruction for that transaction, or dispatches the battery without real-time, specific consumer consent, you are likely acting as a utility.
*   **The Flip Input:** The degree of "control" vs. "facilitation." If you shift from "selling power on behalf of users" to "buying capacity and reselling it," you cross the line.
*   **Stop/Pivot Trigger:** If the CPUC issues a specific inquiry or if your next aggregation event involves dispatching power without explicit, transaction-level homeowner authorization, treat this as a regulatory emergency.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In California, entities that "control the dispatch" of distributed energy resources for compensation are increasingly viewed as Public Utilities under the *Public Utilities Code*.
