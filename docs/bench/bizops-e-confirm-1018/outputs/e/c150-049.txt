*   **Legal Rule (Tier 2):** Under current US Copyright Office guidance and federal court precedents (e.g., *Thaler v. A generic prompt like "find patents for a better mouse trap" lacks the "creative selection or arrangement" required to make the user the author. Consequently, the output likely falls into the public domain immediately upon generation, meaning neither you nor the user holds exclusive rights unless contracted otherwise.
*   **Assumption (Labelled ASSUMED):** We assume the AI generates the analysis autonomously without significant human editing or curation by your team before delivery. If humans curate the results, the *compilation* might be copyrightable, but the raw AI text remains unprotected.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
*   **The Flip:** The level of human intervention. If your engineers or editors significantly select, arrange, or modify the AI output before it becomes the "report," you may claim copyright in that specific human-authored compilation.
*   **The Trigger:** Before launching the aggregated insights product to law firms (your next revenue stream), you must finalize a ToS update.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the prompt is generic and the machine does the work, the law sees no author. Contract law, however, can assign rights where copyright law cannot.
