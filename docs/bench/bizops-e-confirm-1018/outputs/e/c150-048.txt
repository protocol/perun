**The Call**
Do not market your current certificate as "OSHA-recognized" or guaranteeing federal compliance; under current rules, your VR training counts only if it meets specific content and evaluation standards, regardless of the delivery medium. Proceed with the $30,000 investment only if you redesign the program to include mandatory instructor-led interaction and documented skills verification, or partner immediately with an OSHA Authorized Training Organization (ATO).

**The Numbers**
There is no fee or formula to "buy" OSHA recognition. The economic risk is binary based on your validation model:
*   **Current State:** 500 workers × $50 = $25,000 revenue. If a client is audited and your certificate is rejected, their penalty risk (often $15,000+ per violation) makes your product a liability, not an asset.
*   **Investment Decision:** The $30,000 content spend is safe *only* if allocated to building the required assessment infrastructure (live proctoring, skills demos). If spent solely on VR assets without compliance scaffolding, the sunk cost is total.
*   **ASSUMED (Low/High):** Customer churn is 0% if you solve compliance; churn approaches 100% for enterprise clients if you cannot provide a defensible audit trail.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the verdict is **Documented Skills Verification**. OSHA policy explicitly states that purely self-paced or automated online training (including VR) is insufficient for standards requiring "hands-on" demonstration unless supplemented by direct interaction with a qualified trainer.
*   **Trigger:** If your platform cannot facilitate a real-time, documented evaluation by a human trainer where the worker demonstrates physical skills (even if simulated), you cannot claim compliance.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** "No human evaluator, no compliance credit."
*   **Step this week:** If you lack a live proctoring module, consider pausing the $30k build to prototype a hybrid workflow where a remote instructor validates the VR session before the certificate issues.

**Routing Note**
OSHA does not "accredit" third-party vendors directly; they authorize Training Organizations (ATOs) to teach specific curricula (like the 10/30-hour cards). Determining whether your specific VR curriculum satisfies the *content* requirements of a specific standard (e.g., 29 CFR 1910 Subpart E) without an ATO partnership is a binding legal determination. You must consult an occupational safety attorney or apply to become an OSHA ATO to legally issue credentials that carry federal weight.
