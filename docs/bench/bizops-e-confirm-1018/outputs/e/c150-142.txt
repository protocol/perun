**The Call**
Do not choose between 4 or 15 years based on cash flow preference; for a hardware-software hybrid where the software is essential to the hardware's function and requires replacement every 4 years, the IRS and GAAP likely require you to separate the assets or depreciate the entire unit over the shorter software life (or 5 years under current bonus depreciation rules). Depreciating the whole unit over 15 years risks an audit adjustment for "incorrect recovery period," forcing you to pay back taxes and penalties later. The safe, standard approach is to bifurcate: depreciate the mechanical shell over 7–15 years and the software/platform over 1–5 years, or apply the predominant use rule if separation isn't feasible.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Annual Depreciation = Asset Cost / Recovery Period.
*User Figures:* Unit Cost = $85,000; Total Units = 12; Total Basis = $1,020,000.
*Assumptions (ASSUMED):*
1. **Bifurcation Ratio:** We assume 60% of cost is hardware ($51k/unit) and 40% is software/platform ($34k/unit). If you cannot separate them, the "predominant character" rule may force the whole unit into the shorter life.
2. **Tax Regime:** We assume US Federal Tax law allowing 100% Bonus Depreciation (phasing down) or MACRS GDS.
3. **Tax Rate:** We assume a 21% corporate tax rate for liability calculations.

*Scenario A: All 15-Year (Mechanical Life)*
- Annual Deduction: $1,020,000 / 15 = $68,000/year.
- Year 1 Tax Savings: $68,000 * 21% = $14,280.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding input is the **feasibility of cost segregation**. If your invoices or engineering bills of materials clearly separate the "robotic arm/chassis" from the "control system/license," you must depreciate them separately.
*Trigger:* If your CPA cannot substantiate a split cost basis on the invoice, the IRS "predominant use" test applies. Given the device is useless without the software, the entire unit may default to the shorter 5-year (computer/equipment) or 4-year life, not 15.
*Rule of Thumb:* If software replacement is mandatory for operation, the asset's useful life is capped by the software.
*Step:* Before filing, instruct your engineering team to generate a Bill of Materials (BOM) assigning distinct costs to mechanical vs. digital components.
