**The Liability Scenarios & Numbers:**
Liability turns on whether you are viewed as a passive tool or an active participant in the claim submission.
*   **Scenario A (Passive Tool):** You provide data; the physician exercises independent medical judgment. *Liability:* Low for CodeAssist; high for the clinic.
*   **Scenario B (Billing Agent/Co-conspirator):** You guarantee code accuracy, train staff to override physician judgment, or share in the increased reimbursement revenue. *Liability:* High for CodeAssist. Under the False Claims Act, penalties are **$13,946 to $27,894 per false claim** (2024 adjusted amounts) plus **3x damages**.
    *   *Illustrative Exposure:* If 1% of your 50,000 monthly claims are deemed fraudulent upcodes averaging $100 extra:
    *   Damages: 500 claims × $100 × 3 = $150,000.
    *   Penalties: 500 claims × ~$20,000 (avg) = $10,000,000.
    *   *Total Potential Monthly Exposure:* **$10.15M** (far exceeding your $150k cash reserve).

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The trigger is **revenue alignment and workflow control**. If your contract ties your fees to the *amount* reimbursed (e.g., "% of collection") rather than volume ($0.15/claim), or if your UI makes it difficult for doctors to reject your suggestion, you shift from Tool to Agent.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* If the doctor can reject your code in <2 clicks without penalty, you are likely a tool. If rejection requires justification or slows workflow significantly, you are influencing the bill.
*   *Stop/Pivot Trigger:* Before signing any hospital contract, if the deal structure moves away from flat per-claim pricing, halt negotiations immediately.
