**The Call**
Do not lower your price; it rewards low engagement and destroys unit economics. Immediately pivot to investing in gamification to fix the 40% completion rate, as recovering the industry standard (70%) justifies the current €800 license fee and secures the €640k expansion.

**The Numbers**
*Current State (Pilot):*
Revenue per seat = €800.
Effective Revenue per *Completed* User = €800 / 0.40 = **€2,000** (cost to client per successful training).
*Target State (Industry Standard):*
Effective Revenue per *Completed* User = €800 / 0.70 = **€1,143**.
*Impact of Price Cut vs. Engagement Fix:*
If you cut price by 30% (to €560) to match the "double cost" complaint without fixing engagement:
New Effective Revenue = €560 / 0.40 = **€1,400**. You lose €240/seat in top-line revenue and train a bad habit.
If you fix engagement to 70% at current price:
Client cost per success drops from €2,000 to €1,143 (a 43% improvement for them), while you retain full €800 ARR.
*Development Threshold (ASSUMED):*
Let $D$ be the one-time gamification development cost.
Break-even vs. lost expansion: If the fix saves the 800-user rollout, you gain €640,000 ARR.
Any development cost $D$ < €640,000 is immediately justified by saving the deal alone, ignoring future retention benefits.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is the **marginal cost of development** relative to the **probability of success**.
*Trigger:* If the engineering team estimates gamification will cost >€640k OR cannot lift completion rates above 55% within 90 days, reconsider the pricing structure or contract terms.
*Rule of Thumb:* Never discount for product-market fit issues (engagement); discount only for cash-flow emergencies.
