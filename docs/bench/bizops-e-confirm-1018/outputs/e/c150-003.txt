**The Call**
Treat the initial job hunt as a standalone unit with negative economics under current terms; do not bank on re-subscription cycles two years out to fix today's CAC. Your immediate priority must be lowering Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) below $40 or increasing the initial subscription duration to 5+ months, as price increases risk accelerating churn in a finite-use case.

**The Numbers**
We calculate LTV using the standard formula for finite subscriptions: $LTV = ARPU \times Gross Margin \times Average Lifespan$.
*   **ARPU (Average Revenue Per User):** $49/month.
*   **Gross Margin:** ~91% (Revenue $58,800 minus $4,000 variable costs / $58,800).
*   **Average Lifespan:** 3 months (User provided).
*   **CAC:** $65 (User provided).

*Base Case (One Cycle):*
$LTV = \$49 \times 0.91 \times 3 = \$133.77$.
**Unit Profit:** $\$133.77 - \$65 = \$68.77$ (Gross), but this ignores fixed costs.
**Contribution Margin:** $\$133.77 - \$65 = \$68.77$ per user.
**Monthly Burn Coverage:** You need ~262 new users/month just to cover the $18k burn if ignoring existing revenue, but your current 1,200 users generate $58,800. The issue is not total cash flow yet, but *marginal* efficiency. Every new user adds $68.77 to the pot but costs $65 to acquire, leaving only $3.77 to contribute to fixed overhead. This is unsustainable for scaling.

*Scenario A: Banking on Re-subscription (ASSUMED: 20% return rate after 24 months)*
If 20% of users return for another 3-month cycle in two years, we must discount that future revenue to present value (PV). Assuming a 10% annual discount rate:
Future LTV add-on = $(\$49 \times 0.91 \times 3) \times 0.20 \times PV\_factor(2 years) \approx \$133 \times 0.20 \times 0.82 = \$21.80$.
Adjusted LTV = $\$133.77 + \$21.80 = \$155.57$.
**Result:** Still barely covers CAC ($155 vs $65 leaves $90 contribution), which is insufficient to support a 4-person team and growth marketing.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **CAC**. At a 3-month lifespan, your break-even CAC is ~$133. To achieve a healthy 3:1 LTV:CAC ratio (industry standard for SaaS), you need an LTV of $195.
*   **Trigger:** If you cannot lower CAC to **$45** within 60 days, the current LinkedIn ad strategy is mathematically broken for this specific user lifecycle.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** For finite-use products, LTV:CAC must exceed 4:1 initially to survive the long dormant periods between cycles.
*   **Step:** If your CAC remains above $50 next month, consider pivoting from broad LinkedIn ads to high-intent partnerships (outplacement firms) where CAC is often transaction-based rather than impression-based.
