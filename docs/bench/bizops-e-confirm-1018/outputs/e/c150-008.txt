**The Call:** Choose the **$2,000 hardware + $400/month software** model. While the high upfront fee generates immediate cash, investors value recurring revenue (ARR) significantly higher for Series A, and the lower entry price reduces friction for traditional farmers wary of large capital expenditures.

**The Numbers:**
We compare the two models over 18 months per unit.
*Formula:* Total Cash = Upfront + (Monthly Fee × 18). ARR = Monthly Fee × 12.

*Assumptions:*
1.  **Sales Velocity:** Assumed equal for both models in this base case (marked **ASSUMED**; in reality, the lower upfront cost usually increases velocity).
2.  **Churn:** 0% over 18 months for simplicity (marked **ASSUMED**).
3.  **Hardware Cost:** Fixed at $2,000/unit.

| Metric | Model A (High Upfront) | Model B (High Recurring) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Upfront Price** | $5,000 | $2,000 |
| **Monthly Fee** | $50 | $400 |
| **Gross Margin (Hardware)** | $3,000 ($5k - $2k) | $0 ($2k - $2k) |
| **ARR per Unit** | $600 ($50 × 12) | $4,800 ($400 × 12) |
| **Total Cash (18 mo)** | $5,900 | $9,200 |
| **Investor Value Driver** | Low (One-time heavy) | High (Recurring heavy) |

**Result:** Model B generates **55% more total cash** per customer over 18 months and creates **8x the ARR**. With 50 pilot customers, Model B reports $240,000 in ARR versus only $30,000 for Model A.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The decision flips if **sales velocity** drops drastically under Model B because farmers refuse the $400/month commitment.
*Trigger:* If your conversion rate on the $2,000 upfront offer is **less than 40%** of the conversion rate on the $5,000 offer, reconsider Model A to preserve cash runway.
*Rule of Thumb:* If you cannot close at least 4 deals on Model B for every 3 deals on Model A, the ARR multiple gain is erased by volume loss.
*Step this week:* Run a "fake door" test or survey with your 50 pilot leads: "Would you prefer $2k down + $400/mo or $5k down + $50/mo?" If >60% choose the lower upfront, proceed with Model B.

Since you are preparing for a Series A, the specific structuring of these contracts (especially if offering discounts for annual prepayment to boost cash) may have revenue recognition implications (ASC 606). Consult your CPA to ensure your reported ARR matches investor due diligence expectations before finalizing the pricing page.
