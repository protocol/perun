**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The binding factor is not the compact itself, but the **insurance policy's definition of "practice location."**
*   **The Trigger:** If your broker cannot provide a written certificate of insurance stating coverage applies where the *patient* is located (not just where the *doctor* is licensed), you must halt operations in that state immediately.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Compacts (like PSYPACT for psychologists or IMLC for physicians) streamline *licensing*, but they do not automatically unify *insurance*. Many policies are written on a "state-specific" basis.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you have pending patients in non-compact states, pause scheduling and ask your broker: "Does our current policy deny claims if the doctor is licensed in State A but the patient is physically in State B, even if the doctor holds a compact privilege?"
