**The Call:** Continue selling hardware outright for now; do not switch to leasing before your Series B close. The $500k working capital requirement and immediate cash drag ($350 loss per unit upfront) threaten your 12.5-month runway more than the potential ARR boost helps your valuation. Investors in a hardware-heavy Series B will penalize cash inefficiency and inventory risk more than they will reward inflated recurring revenue derived from capital-intensive leases.

**The Numbers:**
*Current Runway:* $1,500,000 Cash / $120,000 Burn = **12.5 months**.
*Current Hardware Margin:* $900 Price - $400 Cost = **+$500 cash inflow per unit**.

*Leasing Scenario (per unit):*
*   Upfront Cash Flow: -$400 (manufacturing cost) + $0 (no sale price) = **-$400 immediate outflow**.
*   Monthly Recurring: +$50 (lease) + $200 (software) = $250 total MRR.
*   Payback Period on Hardware: $400 Cost / $50 Lease Payment = **8 months** just to recover hardware cost (ignoring time value of money and software margin).
*   18-Month Cash Impact (assuming 20 new units/month for growth):
    *   *Sell Model:* 20 units * $500 margin = **+$10,000/month cash contribution**.
    *   *Lease Model:* 20 units * -$400 cost = **-$8,000/month cash drain** (before lease payments accumulate).
    *   *Net Swing:* Switching to leasing creates a **$18,000/month negative variance** in cash flow initially, accelerating burn.
*   Working Capital Need: You estimated **$500,000**. Adding this to your current burn without immediate equity injection reduces effective runway to ~8 months if the round is delayed.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The decision flips only if you secure the $500k working capital *before* changing the model, or if a lead investor explicitly termsheet-conditions the Series B on a lease-only model with a valuation step-up that outweighs the dilution/cost of that extra capital.
*Trigger:* If you cannot sign a term sheet including the $500k bridge within 45 days, abandon the lease pilot.
*Rule of Thumb:* In hardware, never sacrifice positive unit cash flow for recurring revenue unless your cost of capital is near zero.

**Routing Note:**
Yes, you need a tax advisor and legal counsel before drafting any lease agreement. Leasing hardware creates specific sales tax nexus obligations in Texas (different from software SaaS), potential UCC filing requirements for security interests, and revenue recognition complexities (ASC 842) that differ significantly from outright sales. Do not execute a lease contract without defining the tax treatment of the "lease" vs. "service" components.
