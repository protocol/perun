**The Call:** Do not roll out the 10% success-based fee model to your existing user base yet; this structure carries a high risk of violating standard foundation guidelines and ethical codes, potentially disqualifying your clients from current or future funding. Stick to the $500 subscription model or a hybrid approach while you validate specific donor permissions.

**The Numbers & Risk Analysis:**
Under your proposed model, a $50,000 grant yields a $5,000 fee (10%).
*   **Admin Cap Constraint:** The foundation limits administrative costs to 15% ($7,500 on a $50k grant).
*   **The Conflict:** While $5,000 fits within the 15% math ($5,000 < $7,500), the issue is not just the *amount* but the *method*. Most private foundations and federal grant guidelines explicitly prohibit "contingency fees" (compensation based on a percentage of the award) regardless of whether they fit within the administrative cap.
*   **Ethical Standard:** The Association of Fundraising Professionals (AFP) and the Grant Professionals Association (GPA) strictly forbid contingency fees for grant writing, classifying them as unethical because they compromise the integrity of the application process and create a conflict of interest.
*   **Client Impact:** If a non-profit pays you via contingency from restricted funds, they may be forced to return the grant, face audits, or be blacklisted by the funder.

**What Flips It & The Trigger:**
The deciding factor is the specific written guideline of the funding source.
*   **Trigger:** If a foundation's guidelines state "no contingency fees" or "consultant fees must be fixed," your model fails immediately. This is the default stance for ~90% of institutional funders.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Assume contingency fees are prohibited unless the Request for Proposals (RFP) explicitly allows them (which is rare).
*   **Immediate Step:** This week, audit the guidelines of the top 10 foundations your 40 clients apply to. If even one prohibits percentage-based compensation, you cannot safely scale this model without legal restructuring.
