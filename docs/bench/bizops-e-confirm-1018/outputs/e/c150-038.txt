**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes your posture from "risky" to "blocked" is the **classification of voice prints as "biometric identifiers."**
*   **Trigger Value:** If your algorithm extracts unique voice prints (even for pronunciation), you are likely processing biometric data.
*   **The Flip:** In the US (specifically Illinois BIPA and Texas), collecting biometric data from minors without explicit, written parental consent creates **private rights of action**. This means parents can sue you directly for statutory damages (e.g., $1,000–$5,000 per violation), which could total millions across 2,000 users, far exceeding the 4% GDPR cap.
*   **Stop/Pivot Trigger:** If you cannot verify "verifiable parental consent" (VPC) for every existing user by your next board meeting, you must pivot to on-device processing only or delete the training dataset.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If you cannot prove consent for a specific child, assume the data is toxic.
*   **This Week's Step:** If you lack a centralized consent log proving VPC for every active user, consider implementing a hard gate that blocks voice recording features until consent is re-verified via a high-friction flow (e.g., credit card verification or signed form).
