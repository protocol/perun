**The Numbers & Legal Distinction:**
*   **Distinction:** A **warrant** (Fourth Amendment) requires a judge to find "probable cause" that a crime occurred and specifically authorizes a search/seizure. A **grand jury subpoena** compels testimony or document production based on "relevance" to an investigation; it is issued by the prosecutor/grand jury, not necessarily signed by a judge initially.
*   **Obligation:** In the US (Virginia jurisdiction), a validly served grand jury subpoena generally carries the force of law. Ignoring it is not a valid option. However, you may challenge it *before* compliance.
*   **Risk Assessment (Qualitative):**
    *   *Non-compliance (ignoring):* High risk of contempt of court, fines exceeding your $5k budget, and forced compliance anyway.
    *   *Compliance (immediate):* Risk of user lawsuits (breach of contract if policy says "court order" and you treat subpoena as insufficient) and trust erosion among 2,000 users ($30k MRR at stake).
    *   *Challenge (Motion to Quash):* Costs legal fees (part of your $5k) but preserves trust and clarifies the "court order" definition.
