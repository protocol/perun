**The Call**
Stick to per-check pricing but introduce a service-level agreement (SLA) credit for false positives exceeding 5%; do not switch to a tiered subscription yet. The math shows that absorbing the £0.60 cost increase to halve your error rate is profitable only if the engineering fix reduces churn from 12% to below 7%. If churn remains above 7% post-fix, the margin compression destroys unit economics regardless of pricing model.

*Break-Even Churn Analysis:*
To justify the £27,000 monthly cost increase, the reduction in churn must save more than £27,000 in retained Lifetime Value (LTV).
Formula: $\Delta \text{Churn} \times \text{Active Operators} \times \text{LTV} > £27,000$.
If your average operator LTV is £50,000, you need to save just 0.04 operators (negligible). If LTV is £5,000, you must prevent ~5 churns (reducing churn rate by ~1 percentage point).
**Assumption (LOW):** LTV is £10k → Need to reduce churn by ~6% (impossible, as current is 12%).
**Assumption (BASE):** LTV is £50k → Need to reduce churn by ~1.2%.
**Assumption (HIGH):** LTV is £100k → Need to reduce churn by ~0.6%.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that flips the decision is **Operator LTV**.
*Trigger:* If your calculated LTV per operator is under £25,000, the £0.60 cost increase is not justified by churn reduction alone; you must raise prices or accept lower margins. If LTV is over £25,000, proceed with the engineering fix immediately.
*Rule of Thumb:* In UK gambling, compliance failure usually implies infinite LTV loss due to license revocation risks for the operator, suggesting LTV is high.
*Step this week:* Calculate exact LTV. If >£25k, greenlight the engineering spend and draft an SLA offering credits for error rates >5% instead of changing the base price.
