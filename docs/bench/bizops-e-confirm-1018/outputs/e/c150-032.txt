**The Call**
Do not write the inventory down to zero yet; capitalize the $1.2M as work-in-progress at cost, but immediately prepare a "value-in-use" sensitivity analysis showing the break-even approval date to present to your auditors.

**The Numbers**
Under general accounting principles (IFRS/GAAP), inventory is valued at the **lower of cost or net realizable value (NRV)**.
*   **Cost:** $1,200,000 (your actual spend on materials/ink).
*   **NRV:** Estimated selling price minus costs to complete and sell.
*   **Assumption (Labelled):** Since you have no revenue yet, NRV relies on the probability of regulatory approval.
    *   *Scenario A (Approval in 18 months):* NRV > Cost (No write-down).
    *   *Scenario B (Approval delayed >24 months):* If shelf-life expires or market window closes, NRV may drop to $0.
*   **Runway Check:** $3,500,000 cash / $180,000 monthly burn = **~19.4 months** of runway.
    *   *Risk:* Your runway (19.4mo) barely covers the expected approval timeline (18mo). A 1-year delay creates a cash crisis before an inventory crisis.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that forces a write-down is **evidence that regulatory approval will not be obtained before the materials expire or become obsolete**.
*   **Trigger:** If the EMA requests a major clinical trial amendment that pushes approval beyond the biological shelf-life of your ink/materials, you must recognize an impairment loss immediately.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the probability of approval drops below "more likely than not" (>50%) by year-end, auditors will likely demand a partial or full write-down.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your regulatory consultant indicates any "major deficiency" in the current filing, consider commissioning an independent valuation expert to document the shelf-life vs. approval timeline gap.
