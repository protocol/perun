**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The determining factor is not the dollar amount ($10k vs $100k) but the **nature of the risk transfer**.
*   **Tier 2 Explanation (General Rule):** A *product warranty* generally covers the repair or replacement of the *product itself* (the $80 sensor) or a refund of the service fee. It guarantees the quality of the good. An *insurance contract* involves three elements: (1) a premium (your $30/mo), (2) a fortuitous event outside your control (the water leak), and (3) a promise to indemnify the insured for a *loss* resulting from that event (the building damage). By promising to pay for the building damage, you are accepting the risk of the leak, not just the risk of your hardware breaking.
*   **The Trigger:** If your marketing or contract language uses terms like "coverage," "indemnification," "damages," or "protection plan" for property loss, you trigger insurance statutes.
*   **Decision Threshold:** If the payout is for *your product's failure* (refund/replace), it is likely a warranty. If the payout is for *consequential damage to the customer's property*, it is likely insurance.
*   **Step this week:** If you cannot restructure this as a "service level agreement" (SLA) that strictly limits liability to service credits or hardware replacement, consider partnering with a licensed commercial property insurer to underwrite the risk while you market the "detection guarantee."
