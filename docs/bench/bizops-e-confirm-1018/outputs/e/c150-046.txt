**The Call:** Do not migrate your servers to Germany or France; your current Ireland-based architecture is likely compliant for EU-wide operations, provided you have robust intra-EU transfer mechanisms and a completed Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA). The immediate blocker for your hotel chain contract is not server location, but the lack of a formalized DPIA and specific contractual clauses addressing "security data" classification.

**The Numbers & Rules:**
Under the GDPR, there is no general "data residency" requirement forcing data to stay within the specific member state where the user resides (e.g., Germany). Instead, the rule is "data sovereignty" within the European Economic Area (EEA).
*   **The Rule:** Ireland, Germany, and France are all EEA members. Data flowing between them is considered a domestic transfer, not an international one requiring complex adequacy decisions.
*   **The Assumption (Labelled):** We assume your Irish entity is the designated "Data Controller" or that clear Controller-Processor agreements exist between your French/German customers and your Irish hosting entity.
*   **The Compliance Metric:** Compliance is determined by Article 32 (Security of Processing) and Article 35 (DPIA), not physical disk location. Since you process 10,000 logs daily involving entry times (potentially special category data if it reveals religious habits or health status via duration/patterns), a DPIA is mandatory, not optional.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The one input that changes this recommendation from "proceed" to "stop" is if your logs are combined with other datasets to infer **special category data** (e.g., tracking a guest's movement to deduce religious attendance or medical visits) without explicit consent.
*   **Trigger Value:** If your data schema allows profiling beyond "access verification," you must pause the hotel deal.
*   **Decision Threshold:** If the hotel chain's legal team classifies "entry duration" as behavioral profiling rather than security logging, standard contracts will fail.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your current logs include duration patterns longer than 48 hours, consider segregating this data or anonymizing it before the contract review.
