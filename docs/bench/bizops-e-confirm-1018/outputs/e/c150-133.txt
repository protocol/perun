**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the regulatory outcome is the **definition of "consideration" tied to a real estate transaction outcome**.
*   **The Trigger:** In California, the Department of Real Estate (DRE) broadly interprets acting as an agent for another in exchange for compensation *contingent on a real estate result* (like reduced vacancy/rent collected) as acting as a broker.
*   **The Rule of Thumb:** If your fee is a flat design fee or hourly rate, you are likely a consultant. If your fee is a percentage of the lease value or rent saved, you are likely a broker.
*   **Decision Threshold:** If your contract ties compensation to the *execution* or *financial outcome* of a lease, you are likely unlicensed activity.
*   **Step This Week:** If you have signed contracts with the 20% clause, consider pausing new signings and drafting a "flat fee" or "hourly" alternative to present to counsel as a pivot option.

**Specific Activities That Typically Trigger Licensing (General Context)**
While not a verdict, California law generally considers the following activities as requiring a license when done for compensation:
1.  Negotiating leases or rental terms.
2.  Collecting rent or security deposits.
3.  Soliciting tenants for a specific property.
4.  Acting as an agent in the leasing process.
*Your Risk:* Your model arguably makes you an agent in the *result* of the leasing process, which regulators may view as equivalent to participating in the transaction itself.
