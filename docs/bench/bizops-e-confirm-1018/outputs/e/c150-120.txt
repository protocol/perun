**The Call:** Do not proceed with the sale or transfer of the simulation data until a licensed export control attorney completes a formal commodity jurisdiction (CJ) determination; the high-precision modeling of neutron flux and cooling stress in a nuclear context creates a substantial risk that the data is controlled under the International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR) or the Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) rules, regardless of server location.

**The Numbers & Regulatory Landscape:**
Export controls are not calculated by revenue or R&D spend ($1.8M is irrelevant to the legal threshold) but by the technical parameters of the item and the destination.
*   **The Regime:** Nuclear reactor technology and specific "technical data" regarding design, operation, or maintenance often fall under:
    1.  **ITAR (22 CFR §121):** Specifically Category XVI (Nuclear Weapons, Design, Testing, Production Equipment) or Category VIII (Aircraft/Propulsion if related to naval nuclear). If the software is "specifically designed" for a defense article, the data is ITAR-controlled.
    2.  **EAR (15 CFR §730-774):** If not ITAR, it likely falls under the Commerce Control List (CCL), specifically ECCN 0A911 (Nuclear reactors and equipment) or 0E911 (Technology).
    3.  **NRC Regulations (10 CFR Part 810):** Specific assistance to foreign entities in nuclear activities requires authorization.
*   **The Trigger:** The "foreign-owned" status of the utility in France is critical. Even if the data stays on US servers, providing *access* to a foreign person (including employees of a foreign-owned entity) constitutes an "export" or "deemed export."
*   **Assumption (UNVERIFIED):** We assume the "high precision" modeling exceeds public domain thresholds for nuclear physics, pushing it into controlled technical data.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **Commodity Jurisdiction (CJ) determination** by the US Department of State (DDTC) or the Department of Commerce (BIS).
*   **Trigger Value:** If the CJ determines the software is "publicly available" or falls under a broad license exception (e.g., TSR for certain technology), you may proceed. If it is deemed ITAR-controlled, a DSP-5 license is required, which is difficult to obtain for nuclear technologies involving non-NATO allies or sensitive dual-use items.
*   **Stop Rule:** If the software models "specifically designed" components for naval reactors or weapons-grade material processing, the deal is effectively blocked without a specific exemption.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, freeze all data access for the French partner and engage counsel to file a CJ request or perform an internal classification audit against the US Munitions List (USML) and CCL. Do not rely on internal redaction without legal sign-off, as partial data can still be controlled.
