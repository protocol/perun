*   **Total Fully Diluted Valuation (FDV):** 1,000,000 tokens × $0.50 = **$500,000**.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The triggering input is the **expectation of profit derived from the efforts of others**. Your model fails the *Howey Test* (the primary SEC framework for determining securities) on two counts:
1.  **Investment of Money:** Buyers are paying $0.50 cash.
2.  **Expectation of Profit:** You stated holders expect "value appreciation based on our platform's adoption."
3.  **Efforts of Others:** The value depends entirely on your five developers building the platform, not on the voters themselves.

*Mitigation Hypothesis:* Altering the distribution to a "free airdrop" for active users (removing the investment of money) or ensuring the token has immediate, standalone utility unrelated to speculation *might* shift the analysis, but changing the schedule alone does not fix the fundamental "profit from effort" dynamic. The trigger to pivot is immediate: if the token is sold before a functional product exists where the token is *required* for use (not just held for gain), the security risk is maximal.
