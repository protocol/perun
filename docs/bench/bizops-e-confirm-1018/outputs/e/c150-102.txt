**The Call**
Your subscription model ($299/mo) remains viable because you do not hold the pre-need funds; however, your clients' inability to access that capital for 24 months creates a severe churn risk you must model. Do not attempt to recognize the pre-need contract value ($3,500) as revenue or cash flow for *your* SaaS unit economics; your LTV is driven entirely by the subscription duration, which is threatened by your clients' cash crunch.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* LTV = (Monthly Subscription Price × Gross Margin %) × Average Customer Lifespan (months).
*Inputs:* Price = $299. CAC = $1,200.
*Assumptions (Labelled ASSUMED):*
1.  **Gross Margin:** 80% (Standard for SaaS, ASSUMED).
2.  **Churn Rate:** The critical variable.
    *   *Base Case (5% annual churn):* Lifespan = 20 months. LTV = ($299 × 0.80) × 20 = **$4,784**. LTV:CAC = 4.0x.
    *   *Stress Case (20% annual churn due to client cash flow issues):* Lifespan = 5 months. LTV = ($299 × 0.80) × 5 = **$1,196**. LTV:CAC = 1.0x (Break-even failure).

*Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC):* Since you charge monthly subscription fees upfront or net-30, your CCC is negative (you get cash before you pay servers/staff), *unless* clients delay payment due to their own liquidity traps. The 24-month hold on *their* funds does not directly enter your CCC math, but it indirectly inflates your Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) if they cannot pay you.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that flips this from a healthy business to a failure is **client churn driven by liquidity**. If your funeral home clients cannot operate because their pre-need capital is locked for 24 months, they will cancel your software.
*Trigger:* If more than 2 clients out of 45 cancel within the first 12 months due to "cash flow constraints," your CAC payback period exceeds 12 months, threatening runway.
*Rule of Thumb:* SaaS LTV:CAC must be >3x. If your observed churn exceeds 1.5% monthly, the model breaks.
*Step this week:* If churn >1.5% monthly, consider pivoting pricing to a lower base fee plus a transaction fee on *non-regulated* add-ons to align with their cash availability.

**Routing Note**
While your SaaS revenue recognition (ASC 606) is standard, the **accounting treatment of the pre-need funds held by your clients** is a high-stakes, jurisdiction-specific tier-3 issue. You must ask a CPA specializing in funeral regulations: "Does our software's handling of contract data create any 'constructive receipt' or fiduciary liability for FinalDignity if a client mismanages the 24-month hold?" Do not answer this internally; one misstep could pierce your corporate veil.
