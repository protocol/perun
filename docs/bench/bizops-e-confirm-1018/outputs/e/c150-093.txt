**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The determining factor is not revenue size, but the **act of aggregation and resale**. In California, the CPUC looks at whether you are "selling electricity" or "providing energy services."
*   **The Trigger:** The moment you aggregate third-party capacity and sell it back to the grid (or manage the dispatch for financial gain), you likely fit the statutory definition of a Public Utility under the California Public Utilities Code.
*   **The Threshold:** There is no specific kWh or dollar "safe harbor" for aggregation models. The risk flips from "theoretical" to "enforcement priority" as you scale from 200 to 1,000 users, attracting regulatory attention.
*   **Decision Rule:** If you cannot structure the transaction so the *homeowner* sells directly to the grid while you charge a distinct "software management fee," you are likely a utility.
