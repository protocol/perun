**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The trigger is the **degree of control** your contract exerts over clinical decisions. In New York, if your agreement allows HydrateGo to set patient pricing, dictate scheduling algorithms that prioritize revenue over care, or control medical supply selection, regulators will deem the physician an employee regardless of the "contractor" label.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the business controls the "business" side only (rent, marketing, billing collection via MSO fee), it is usually safe. If the business controls the "medical" side (who gets treated when, what fluids are used, pricing of medical acts), it is illegal CPOM.
