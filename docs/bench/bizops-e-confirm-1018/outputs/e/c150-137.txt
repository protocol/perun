**The Call**
Do not launch the $500 standalone report feature yet; selling pre-formatted documents that interpret specific transaction data against regulations creates a high risk of being classified as the unauthorized practice of law (UPL) in both the UK and Singapore. You must consult a legal ethics specialist in each jurisdiction before defining the product's scope or pricing, as the distinction between a "data tool" and a "legal opinion" hinges on whether the output asserts a definitive compliance status for a specific entity.

**The Numbers**
There is no arithmetic formula to calculate UPL risk; the "input" is the degree of interpretation your software performs.
*   **Current Model:** $3,000/mo for a platform (tool) where the user interprets the data.
*   **Proposed Model:** $500/report for a document asserting regulatory standing (potential opinion).
*   **Assumption (Labelled):** If the report states "Transaction X is compliant" rather than "Transaction X matches Pattern Y," the liability shifts from a software vendor to a legal advisor.
*   **Result:** The potential revenue ($500 × volume) is likely outweighed by the cost of defending against UPL claims or losing your existing enterprise contracts due to compliance breaches.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **wording of the output**.
*   **Trigger:** If the report includes any definitive conclusion on legality (e.g., "This filing meets FCA/MAS requirements"), you are likely practicing law.
*   **Pivot Rule:** If the report strictly provides raw data mapping (e.g., "These 5 transactions triggered Rule 12.4") without concluding whether the rule was violated, it may remain a data tool.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, draft two versions of the report: one with a "compliance verdict" and one with only "data flags." Take both to a qualified attorney in the UK and Singapore to determine which version crosses the line. Do not rely on internal interpretation.
