**The Call**
Reject the current fee-for-service structure; at $60 revenue against $50 cost, your $10/user/month contribution margin is too thin to sustain an 8-person team or absorb risk, making a value-based contract (priced on your 30% quit rate) the necessary pivot for viability.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Contribution Margin = Reimbursement Rate − (App Cost + Coaching Cost).
*Inputs:* $60 (Revenue) − ($10 App + $40 Coaching) = **$10/user/month**.

*Runway Analysis (Labelled Assumptions):*
To cover your 8-person team, we must estimate burn.
*Assumption (Low):* Lean team, $5k avg cost/person/month → $40k total burn.
*Assumption (Base):* Standard tech team, $7.5k avg cost/person/month → $60k total burn.
*Assumption (High):* Senior team/benefits, $10k avg cost/person/month → $80k total burn.

*Break-even Volume (Users needed to cover team burn only, ignoring other overhead):*
*Low Burn:* 4,000 users ($40k / $10).
*Base Burn:* 6,000 users ($60k / $10).
*High Burn:* 8,000 users ($80k / $10).

*Current Status:* With 500 pilot users, you generate $5,000/month in contribution. Against a likely $60k+ monthly burn, you are losing ~$55k/month.
*Runway:* $100k reserves / $55k loss ≈ **1.8 months** before insolvency at current scale.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **pricing model**. Under fee-for-service, you need 6,000+ active Medicaid users immediately to break even on team costs alone, which is operationally unrealistic for a startup.
*Trigger:* If you cannot secure a committed volume of 2,000+ users within 30 days, you must pivot to a **Value-Based Contract**.
*Rule of Thumb:* In digital therapeutics, payers often pay $500–$1,500 per successful outcome (quit). If you charge $800 per quit:
Revenue per 100 users = 30 quits × $800 = $24,000.
Cost per 100 users = 100 × $50 = $5,000.
New Margin = $19,000 per 100 users (vs. $1,000 under fee-for-service).
*Step this week:* Draft a proposal to Ohio Medicaid managed care organizations offering a pilot at $750 per verified 6-month quit, capping total liability for the payer while capturing your 30% efficacy premium.
