**The Call**
Do not market a fixed "years to recoup" figure yet; the 60% output reduction likely pushes residential payback beyond 15–20 years, rendering the value proposition unworkable for mass adoption without subsidies or a shifted target market (e.g., off-grid commercial). The product remains viable only if the customer's electricity cost is exceptionally high or if the "ideal" 24-hour baseline was already marginal.

**The Numbers**
To calculate the customer's payback period, we use:
*Payback (Years) = Customer Purchase Price / (Annual Energy Output × Customer Electricity Rate)*

*   **Customer Purchase Price:** €14,000 (Given)
*   **Manufacturing Cost:** €8,000 (Irrelevant to customer payback, but defines your margin: €6,000/unit)
*   **Output Reduction:** 60% less than 24-hour operation (Given)

2.  **Customer Electricity Rate:** Unknown. *Assumption:* German residential average ~€0.45/kWh (high volatility).

*Illustrative Scenario (Base Case):*

    *   *Payback:* €14,000 / €3,600 = **3.9 years**.

    *   Annual Savings: 3,200 kWh × €0.45 = €1,440/year.
    *   *Payback:* €14,000 / €1,440 = **9.7 years**.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that flips the verdict is the **Customer Electricity Rate**. If rates drop below €0.30/kWh, the restricted payback exceeds 14 years, killing demand.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If the calculated payback exceeds 10 years (the typical homeowner horizon), consider pivoting sales to commercial agricultural clients with higher daytime loads and tax incentives, rather than suburban homes.
*   **This Week's Step:** If your ideal output assumption is >7,000 kWh/year, consider commissioning a third-party wind audit for a specific German suburb; if <5,000 kWh, halt residential marketing immediately.

**Unknown Variables & Seasonal Risks**
You must disclose these in sales materials to avoid misleading customers:
1.  **Winter Daylight:** In Northern Germany, daylight averages only 7–8 hours in December, worsening the restriction beyond the 10-hour average.
2.  **Wind Diurnal Patterns:** Wind speeds often peak at night; restricting operation to daylight may cut output by *more* than 60% if the local wind profile is nocturnal.
3.  **Seasonal Wind Lulls:** Summer often has lower wind speeds in Europe, coinciding with longer daylight, creating a mismatch between availability and generation potential.
