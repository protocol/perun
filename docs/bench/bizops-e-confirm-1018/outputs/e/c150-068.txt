**The Call**
Your $2,500 flat fee is likely compliant as a **technology charge**, not a legal fee, provided your contracts explicitly define the product as a software license independent of the law firm's billing to their end-clients. Do not switch to hourly billing or introduce complexity-based tiers yet; instead, reinforce the contractual firewall between your SaaS subscription and the firm's ethical obligation to charge reasonable legal fees. The risk lies not in your price point, but in whether your marketing or contracts imply that the fee covers "case processing" rather than "software access."

**The Numbers**
*   **Current Model:** 40 clients × $2,500/case (average) = $100,000–$180,000 monthly revenue (implies ~40–72 cases/month processed).
*   **Unit Economics Risk:** If forced to hourly billing at a hypothetical $150/hr software support rate:
    *   *Low Complexity (2 hrs):* $300/case (88% revenue drop per case).
    *   *High Complexity (40 hrs):* $6,000/case (140% revenue increase per case).
*   **Assumption (LABELLED ASSUMED):** Your current $180k MRR represents an average of 72 cases/month across 40 firms.
*   **Result:** Moving to hourly billing creates extreme revenue volatility and misaligns incentives, punishing firms with efficient workflows while rewarding messy data. The flat fee acts as a predictable operating expense for the firm, which is generally permissible for vendor services.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is **contractual language**. If your agreement states you are paid for "handling the case" or "processing the application" (implying labor), regulators may reclassify the fee as part of the legal cost, triggering ethical scrutiny on reasonableness. If the agreement states you are paid for "access to the VisaFlow AI platform" (a tool), the variance in time spent by the *lawyer* using the tool is irrelevant to your fee's ethics.
*   **Trigger:** If a bar association issues a formal advisory opinion stating that *vendor* flat fees for case management tools constitute "fee splitting" or "unreasonable costs" passed to clients, you must pivot.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Vendors charge for *access*; lawyers charge for *time/outcome*. As long as you sell access, the lawyer's internal efficiency (2 hrs vs 40 hrs) is their margin to keep or lose, not your ethical burden.
*   **Step this week:** If your current contract uses verbs like "process," "file," or "manage," consider revising them to "provide platform access for" or "enable user processing of" to clarify the vendor relationship.
