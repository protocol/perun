**The Call**
Do not choose between the 5-year lease or 15-year life as a binary option; for tax purposes, you likely must depreciate over the **shorter** of the asset's recovery period or the lease term unless you can legally substantiate that the asset will be removed and reused. Given your 10% removal cost and the specialized nature of "smart glass" installed in a specific tower, the default tax position is often to treat this as a leasehold improvement depreciated over the 5-year lease term (or the statutory recovery period if shorter), rather than the 15-year physical life. The ability to extend depreciation to 15 years hinges entirely on proving a bona fide intent and economic feasibility to remove and reuse the assets, which is a binding legal determination.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Annual Depreciation = Asset Basis / Recovery Period (Straight-Line assumed for simplicity).
*User Inputs:* Basis = $1,200,000; Lease = 5 years; Physical Life = 15 years; Removal Cost = 10% ($120,000).

*Scenario A: Depreciate over Lease Term (5 years)*
*Assumption (ASSUMED):* Tax authority treats this as a leasehold improvement with no guaranteed reuse.
*Result:* $1,200,000 / 5 = **$240,000/year**.
*Impact:* Reduces your $400k profit to $160k taxable income annually for 5 years.

*Scenario B: Depreciate over Useful Life (15 years)*
*Assumption (ASSUMED):* You successfully prove the asset will be removed and reused, qualifying it as personal property rather than a leasehold improvement.
*Result:* $1,200,000 / 15 = **$80,000/year**.
*Impact:* Reduces your $400k profit to $320k taxable income annually for 15 years.

*The Reuse Threshold:*
To justify Scenario B, the Net Realizable Value of the removed glass must exceed the removal cost plus the tax benefit lost by slowing depreciation.
*Trigger:* If the reused glass cannot be sold or reinstalled for more than **$120,000** (the removal cost) plus the present value of the tax difference, the IRS may challenge the 15-year schedule as lacking economic substance.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that flips the call is **documented intent and feasibility of reuse**.
*Trigger Value:* A signed engineering or project management plan dated before installation detailing the removal process, storage, and specific target location for reuse.
*Stop/Pivot Rule:* If you cannot produce a written plan showing where the glass goes after year 5, default to the 5-year schedule.
*Rule of Thumb:* Tax authorities generally presume assets affixed to real property follow the lease term unless the taxpayer proves otherwise.
*Step this week:* If you have a specific future building lined up for these units, draft a "Asset Recovery Plan" now; if not, consider the 5-year write-off as the baseline for cash flow planning.
