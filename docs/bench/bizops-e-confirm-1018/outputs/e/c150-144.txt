**The Call:** Your current encryption and access controls are necessary but likely insufficient for processing Social Security Numbers (SSNs) at scale; you must immediately implement data masking (redaction) before AI ingestion and engage privacy counsel to validate your compliance posture before renewing the contract.

**The Numbers & Risk Exposure:**
While specific fines vary by statute and outcome, the financial exposure for mishandling SSNs is severe and often statutory (per record), not just based on actual damages.
*   **New York SHIELD Act:** Allows penalties up to **$20 per instance** for negligent safeguards, scaling to **$5,000 per instance** for intentional/reckless violations. With "thousands of documents," a single breach could theoretically expose you to millions in statutory penalties, excluding legal fees and forensic costs.
*   **Contractual Liability:** Your $40,000 annual contract likely contains indemnification clauses. A breach could trigger damages far exceeding the contract value, potentially threatening company solvency.
*   **Assumption:** You are processing ~2,000 documents/month containing SSNs. If 10% contain an SSN and a breach exposes one year of data (2,400 records), statutory penalties alone could range from **$48,000** (negligent) to **$12,000,000** (reckless).

**What Flips It, and The Trigger:**
The deciding factor is **data minimization via pre-processing**.
*   **Trigger:** If your AI model reads raw text containing SSNs, you are likely failing the "reasonable security" standard under NY law because the data exists in a usable form longer than necessary.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If the AI does not need the SSN to perform lease abstraction (e.g., extracting rent amounts or dates), the SSN must be masked *before* the file enters your processing pipeline.
*   **Action This Week:** If your current architecture allows the AI to see unmasked SSNs, consider halting ingestion of any new documents containing PII until a redaction layer is inserted upstream.
