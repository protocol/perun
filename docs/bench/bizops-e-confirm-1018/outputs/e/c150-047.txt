**The Call**
Bundle mandatory security and safety updates into the base lease to prevent negligence claims, but explicitly exclude "feature obsolescence" from the definition of breach. Do not sell updates as a separate paid service for core functionality, as withholding safety patches creates unacceptable liability exposure.

**The Numbers**
There is no direct revenue formula here, but the risk calculation relies on the cost of litigation versus the cost of development.
*   **Current Exposure:** 15 units × 3 years = 45 unit-years of potential liability.
*   **Growth Exposure:** Adding 10 units next quarter increases exposure by ~30 unit-years annually.
*   **Assumption (Labelled ASSUMED):** The cost of a single negligence lawsuit (legal fees + settlement) exceeds $50,000, while the marginal cost of pushing a software update is near zero.
*   **Result:** The economic decision is binary. If you unbundle safety updates, you risk a $50k+ loss per incident to save minimal dev costs. If you bundle them, your cost is fixed internal labor.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The deciding input is the **definition of "Functional"** in your contract.
*   **Trigger:** If a machine fails due to an unpatched security vulnerability or safety bug, you are likely liable for negligence regardless of contract language, because you maintain control over the software.
*   **Pivot Rule:** If the failure is due to "outdated features" (e.g., the robot cannot make a new trendy drink type), you are generally not liable *if* the contract states the hardware is leased "as-is" regarding feature evolution.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, draft a clause distinguishing "Critical Maintenance" (security/safety, mandatory/bundled) from "Feature Enhancements" (optional/not guaranteed).

**Standard Practices & Contract Language**
In Hardware-as-a-Service (HaaS) models, standard practice dictates that the provider retains responsibility for the integrity of the embedded software.
1.  **Negligence vs. Contract:** Even if a contract says "no updates," a court may still find you negligent if a known safety bug was not fixed and caused injury. You cannot contract away gross negligence or willful misconduct in most jurisdictions, including Illinois.
2.  **Bundling Strategy:** Bundle "Critical Updates" (security, safety, regulatory compliance) into the $800/month fee. This assures clients of safety and limits your liability by showing due diligence.
3.  **Exclusion Strategy:** Explicitly state that the lease does not guarantee the hardware will remain competitive with future market innovations or capable of new beverage recipes not supported at the time of manufacture.

**Suggested Contract Concepts (Not Legal Advice):**
*   *Maintenance Scope:* "Lessor shall provide software updates reasonably necessary to maintain safety standards and data security ('Critical Updates'). Lessor makes no representation that the Equipment will support future beverage trends or third-party integrations."
*   *Limitation of Liability:* "Liability is limited to direct damages arising from Lessor's failure to deploy Critical Updates within 30 days of release. Lessor is not liable for consequential damages related to business interruption or perceived obsolescence."
