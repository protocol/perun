**The Call**
Proceed with the $50,000 upfront payment strictly as a **non-exclusive or limited exclusive data licensing agreement** with no future revenue share; do not offer any structure tied to future profits or asset appreciation. The moment you offer a "revenue share based on future data sales," you cross from selling a product (Tier 1/2) into selling an investment contract (Tier 3), which triggers immediate securities registration requirements.

**The Numbers**
Your current unit economics are strong enough to sustain operations without desperate financing, reducing the pressure to risky structures.
*   **Monthly Recurring Revenue (MRR):** 400 herds × $15 = $6,000.
*   **One-time Hardware Revenue:** 400 units × $250 = $100,000 (historical).
*   **Monthly Burn:** $8,000.
*   **Current Monthly Deficit:** $8,000 (cost) - $6,000 (data fees) = -$2,000 (excluding hardware sales).
*   **Impact of Deal:** The $50,000 upfront covers 25 months of your current operating deficit ($50,000 / $2,000).

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes this from a safe commercial contract to a regulated security is the **promise of future financial return derived from the efforts of others**.
*   **Safe Zone (Data Vendor):** Fixed fee ($50k) or fixed per-record fee for access to historical Q3 data. The buyer pays for a product delivered.
*   **Danger Zone (Security Issuer):** Any clause stating the buyer receives X% of *future* data sales, or that the value of their purchase depends on Acme Livestock's success in gathering more data.
*   **Trigger Rule:** If the buyer's profit depends on your managerial effort to expand the herd or improve the data quality *after* the sale, the SEC likely views this as an investment contract under the *Howey* test.
*   **Actionable Step:** If the buyer insists on a "revenue share" or "royalty" model, stop negotiations immediately and engage counsel; do not sign a term sheet containing profit-participation language.

**Routing Note (Critical)**
You asked for specific thresholds to avoid registration: **There are no safe-harbor thresholds for structuring an unregistered security.** If the instrument is deemed a security, selling it without registration is a federal violation regardless of the dollar amount ($50k is not "small enough").
*   **Exact Question for Counsel:** "Does a data licensing agreement granting exclusive rights to Q3 datasets with a royalty clause tied to future aggregate sales constitute an 'investment contract' under the *Howey* test, and if so, can we utilize Regulation D (Rule 506(b)) for this single institutional buyer?"
*   **Time Sensitivity:** Do not accept funds or sign the agreement until a securities attorney reviews the "revenue share" language. The "exclusive access" clause also carries antitrust and data privacy risks specific to commodity markets that require legal review.
