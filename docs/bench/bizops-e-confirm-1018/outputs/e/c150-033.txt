**The Call**
Do not raise prices to $250 yet; the immediate risk is not market rejection but regulatory viability. Your current "fully autonomous, no pilot on-site" model likely violates existing FAA Part 107 rules (which already require a Remote Pilot in Command to maintain visual line of sight or use a qualified observer), meaning the "proposed" rule may simply be an enforcement crackdown on your current operations. Proceed with hiring pilots only after legal confirmation that your specific technology stack cannot qualify for a waiver, as the unit economics at $95 cost are marginal even at $250 price.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Monthly Profit = (Price − Variable Cost) × Volume − Fixed Costs.
*Current State (Verified):* Revenue $30k / $150 price = 200 inspections/month. Margin = $130/unit. Monthly Contribution = $26,000.
*New Scenario (Assumed):* Cost rises to $95/unit.
*Assumption (ASSUMED):* Fixed costs are derived from your burn. With $150k runway and 10 months life, monthly burn is $15,000. Assuming this is entirely fixed overhead (since variable costs are separated):
1.  **Status Quo Price ($150):** Margin = $55/unit. Contribution = $11,000. Net = -$4,000/month (Burn increases, runway drops to ~8 months).
2.  **Proposed Price ($250):** Margin = $155/unit. Contribution = $31,000. Net = +$16,000/month.
3.  **Break-Even Volume at $250:** Fixed Costs ($15k) / Margin ($155) = 97 inspections/month (48% drop in volume tolerated).
4.  **Break-Even Volume at $150:** Fixed Costs ($15k) / Margin ($55) = 273 inspections/month (36% increase required, impossible under current constraints).

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that flips the call is **Price Elasticity of Demand**. If raising prices to $250 causes volume to drop below 97 inspections/month, the business becomes unprofitable even with the price hike.
*Trigger:* If a blind survey of 50 past customers indicates less than 60% willingness to pay $250, do not raise prices; instead, pivot to a hybrid model or seek enterprise contracts (insurance companies) rather than homeowners.
*Rule of Thumb:* In home services, a 66% price increase ($150 to $250) typically reduces volume by 30–50%.
*Step this week:* If elasticity is unknown, consider running a "pre-order" test at $249 to 100 leads before hiring pilots.

**Routing Note**
**Critical:** The premise that a "new proposed rule" will *first* require a pilot on-site is legally suspect; FAA Part 107 already mandates a Remote Pilot in Command (RPC) who must maintain VLOS unless a specific waiver (e.g., for beyond visual line of sight) is held. You must ask an aviation attorney: "Does our current operation without an on-site RPC violate existing Part 107.31/35 regulations, and is our technology eligible for a BVLOS waiver under Part 107.205?" Do not hire based on "proposed" rules until you confirm you aren't already non-compliant.
