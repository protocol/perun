**The Call**
Carry the $650,000 work-in-progress (WIP) at cost less any estimated costs to complete and sell, but do not write it down yet unless you have specific evidence that a contract will fail; instead, formalize a policy stating that impairment is recognized only when "objective evidence" of rejection or cost overrun exists, and present this "cost-less-impairment" model to your auditors as the standard approach for long-cycle custom manufacturing.

**The Numbers**
*Formula:* Carrying Value = Accumulated Direct Costs (Materials + Labor + Overhead) − Impairment Charge.
*Your Figures:* Accumulated Costs = $650,000.
*Assumptions (Labelled ASSUMED):*
1.  **Probability of Success:** Base case 90% (given "major contracts" usually imply committed off-take); Low case 50% (high regulatory risk); High case 100% (guaranteed).
2.  **Cost to Complete:** ASSUMED $200,000 per contract remaining to reach approval.
3.  **Contract Value:** ASSUMED $1.5M+ per contract (ensuring positive margin if approved).

*Results:*
*   **Base Case (No specific failure risk):** Impairment = $0. Carrying Value = **$650,000**.
*   **Low Case (One contract deemed likely to fail):** If one of the three ($216k cost) is at risk, Impairment = $216,000. Carrying Value = **$434,000**.
*   **Trigger for Write-down:** Only if a regulatory body issues a negative preliminary ruling or the client terminates the agreement.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **Objective Evidence of Impairment**. Under general accounting principles (US GAAP/IFRS), you do not write down inventory simply because the cycle is long (14–18 months); you write it down only when the net realizable value (expected sale price minus costs to finish) drops below the current cost basis.
*   **Trigger Value:** A formal notice of deficiency from a regulator or a client cancellation letter.
*   **Stop/Pivot Date:** If no such notice is received by your Series A closing date next month, maintain the $650,000 valuation.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Time alone is not an impairment trigger in custom manufacturing; technical failure is.
*   **Step this week:** Draft a one-page "Inventory Valuation Policy" memo stating: "EnzymeWorks carries biological WIP at accumulated cost. Impairment is recorded only upon objective evidence of contract termination or regulatory rejection, defined as [specific milestone failure]." Send this to your auditors for pre-approval.

**Routing Note**
While the general rule is "cost less impairment," the specific definition of "objective evidence" for synthetic biology assets and the disclosure requirements for your Series A data room are jurisdiction-specific and high-stakes; bring your drafted policy to your CPA or external auditor immediately to confirm it satisfies the specific auditing standards (ASC 330 or IAS 2) applicable to your incorporation state and investor expectations.
