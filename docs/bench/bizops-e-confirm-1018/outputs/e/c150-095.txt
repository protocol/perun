**The Call**
Do not pivot to a pay-per-completion model immediately; it will collapse your revenue predictability and worsen your six-month runway crisis. Instead, retain the per-seat subscription but introduce "engagement-based rebates" or "success tiers" to align price with value without sacrificing upfront cash flow. The 40% completion rate is a product retention risk, not a fatal unit economics flaw, provided you can demonstrate that the 40% who complete the training deliver measurable ROI to the client.

**The Numbers**
*Current State:*
- **ARR:** $2,000,000 (200 clients × 50 seats × $200).
- **Annual OpEx:** $1,800,000 ($150k/month × 12).
- **Current Runway:** ~6.7 months (Cash / Monthly Burn). *Note: Your stated "6 months" aligns closely with this.*
- **Content Cost:** $75k/module. With 12 creators, assume a capacity of ~1 module/month (conservative), implying $900k/year in direct content labor alone, leaving $900k for overhead, sales, and G&A. This margin is tight.

*Scenario A: Pay-Per-Completion (Hypothetical)*
- **Assumption (ASSUMED):** To maintain $2M revenue with only 40% completion, the price per completion must rise to $500 ($200 / 0.40).
- **Result:** Clients face unpredictable costs and a 150% price hike per active user. Sales cycles will lengthen, and churn will likely spike as clients cap usage to control costs. Revenue becomes volatile, endangering immediate cash flow.

*Scenario B: Engagement-Tiered Pricing (Recommended)*
- **Structure:** Base fee of $150/seat (guaranteed cash flow) + $100/seat "success fee" triggered if completion > 50%.
- **Result at 40% completion:** Revenue drops to $150 × 10,000 seats = $1.5M.
- **Gap Analysis:** This creates a $500k shortfall against current OpEx.
- **Fix:** You must reduce OpEx or increase seat count. However, this model preserves the contract and allows you to upsell "implementation services" to drive completion rates above 50%, unlocking the full $200 value.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is **Client Churn Rate**. If churn exceeds 2% monthly due to low perceived value (low completion), the subscription model fails regardless of structure.
- **Trigger:** If next quarter's renewal rate falls below 90%, you must pivot to a service-heavy model (charging for implementation/training) rather than pure software licensing.
- **Rule of Thumb:** SaaS HR tools typically require >60% engagement to justify renewal without heavy hand-holding.
- **Actionable Step:** If completion remains <45% in 30 days, consider offering free "coaching add-ons" to top 20% of clients to artificially boost completion metrics and secure renewals.

**Routing Note**
Changing from a fixed subscription to a usage-based or success-fee model may alter revenue recognition rules (ASC 606) and require updated terms of service regarding data tracking for "completion." Consult your CPA on revenue recognition timing and counsel on contract language before presenting the new tiered structure to the board.
