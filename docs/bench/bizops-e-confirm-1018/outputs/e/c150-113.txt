**The Call:** Immediately cease capitalizing new costs for this specific batch and begin expensing them as incurred; the >12-month regulatory delay creates a high probability of impairment that likely requires writing down the existing $320,000 inventory value to its net realizable value.

**The Numbers & Accounting Principles:**
Under US GAAP (ASC 330 and ASC 205), inventory must be carried at the lower of cost or net realizable value (NRV). NRV is the estimated selling price minus costs to complete and sell.
*   **Current Status:** $320,000 (Book Value).
*   **The Trigger:** Regulatory approval >12 months. In biotech, delays often correlate with technical obsolescence or increased completion costs.
*   **The Test:** If the expected sale price minus remaining regulatory/completion costs is < $320,000, you must recognize an impairment loss immediately.
*   **Future Costs:** R&D costs incurred after technical feasibility is uncertain or regulatory paths are blocked must be expensed (ASC 730), not capitalized. Capitalizing them now inflates assets and violates the matching principle.

**What Flips It:**
The single factor that allows you to maintain the $320,000 asset value is a **binding off-take agreement** or **regulatory confirmation** proving the delay is purely administrative with no impact on efficacy or market price.
*   *Trigger:* If you cannot produce a signed letter from the FDA confirming the path forward within 30 days, or a buyer committed to the original price point, the write-down is mandatory.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* If the timeline exceeds the product's typical shelf-life or funding runway, the asset is impaired.
*   *This Week's Step:* If the FDA has not provided a written timeline extension approval by Friday, consider engaging a valuation specialist to calculate the specific impairment amount before your next board meeting.

**Debt Financing Impact:**
Lenders typically advance 20–50% against *verified* inventory. If the inventory is impaired (written down), your borrowing base shrinks immediately. Furthermore, lenders view "regulatory limbo" inventory as high-risk collateral; many will exclude it entirely from the borrowing base until approval is secured. Presenting inflated inventory values to secure debt without disclosing the impairment risk constitutes fraud.

**Presentation to Investors:**
Do not hide the write-down. Frame it as a prudent, non-cash adjustment aligning with conservative GAAP practices.
*   *Narrative:* "We are proactively impairing $Xk to reflect current regulatory timelines, ensuring our balance sheet accurately reflects realizable value. This is a paper loss that protects future equity holders from overvaluation."
*   *Signal:* This signals strong governance, not distress. Hiding it signals potential compliance issues.
