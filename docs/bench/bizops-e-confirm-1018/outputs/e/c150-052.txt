**The Call**
Model two parallel tracks: a "Cash Liquidity" view for runway survival and a "Recognized Revenue" view for unit economics. Do not blend them; the 24-month hold creates a structural working-capital gap that distorts standard CAC payback if you use cash inflows instead of recognized revenue. Your immediate constraint is the 7.5-month cash runway, not profitability.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes the verdict is the **actual cost of customer acquisition (CAC)**. If your CAC exceeds the total *recognized* gross profit you can extract from a client over their lifetime (post-24-month hold), the business model fails regardless of cash flow.
*   *Trigger:* If CAC > (Average Contract Value × 30% × Gross Margin), stop scaling immediately.
*   *Rule of Thumb:* In deferred-revenue models, CAC must be recoverable from the *unlocked* portion of the contract.
*   *Step this week:* Build a "Source of Funds" waterfall showing the $472.5k/mo accumulation vs. the $202.5k/mo availability. If X (CAC) > $4,500, consider Y (reducing sales spend or renegotiating trust terms if legally possible).
