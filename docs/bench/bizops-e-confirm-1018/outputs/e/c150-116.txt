**The Call**
Depreciate the $1.2 million installation over the **7-year lease term**, not the 20-year useful life, unless ClearView Glass holds a renewal option reasonably certain to be exercised or owns the underlying real estate. Treating this as a 20-year asset would likely violate accounting standards for leasehold improvements, artificially inflating your current net income and creating a future impairment risk.

**The Numbers**
The governing formula for annual expense is:
$$ \text{Annual Depreciation} = \frac{\text{Capitalized Cost}}{\min(\text{Useful Life}, \text{Lease Term})} $$

*   **Capitalized Cost:** $1,200,000 (Given)
*   **Useful Life:** 20 years (Given)
*   **Lease Term:** 7 years (Given)
*   **Renewal Probability:** 0% (ASSUMED: No renewal option mentioned or deemed certain)

**Computed Results:**
*   **Scenario A (Correct Approach - Lease Term):** $1,200,000 / 7 years = **$171,429 per year**.
*   **Scenario B (Incorrect Approach - Useful Life):** $1,200,000 / 20 years = **$60,000 per year**.

**Impact:** Using the 20-year life understates expenses by **$111,429 annually**. On $4 million revenue, this distorts net income by approximately 2.8% each year for seven years, followed by a potential total write-off of the remaining book value ($780,000) in year 8 if the lease ends and the asset is abandoned.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The single input that changes this call is a **lease renewal option** that ClearView is "reasonably certain" to exercise.
*   **Trigger Value:** If the lease includes a renewal clause and economic penalties or operational necessities make renewal virtually guaranteed, the term extends to include the renewal period (e.g., 7 + 5 years).
*   **Stop/Pivot Date:** Review the lease agreement immediately before finalizing the current year's financial statements. If no renewal is signed or certain by the filing deadline, stick to the 7-year schedule.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Always amortize leasehold improvements over the shorter of the asset's life or the lease term.
*   **This Week's Step:** If the lease lacks a renewal clause, consider negotiating a "restoration" or "purchase" clause with the building owner to clarify asset ownership at lease end, which could alter the accounting treatment.

**Routing Note**
While the "shorter of life or lease" rule is standard under US GAAP (ASC 842) and IFRS (IAS 16), the specific determination of "reasonably certain" regarding renewals and the tax-specific depreciation class life (which may differ from book depreciation, e.g., MACRS schedules) requires a licensed CPA. Bring your specific lease agreement and the question "Does our renewal option meet the 'reasonably certain' threshold for amortization extension?" to your accountant before filing.
