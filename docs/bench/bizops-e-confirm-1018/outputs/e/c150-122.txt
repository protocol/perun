**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes your risk profile is the **marketing promise vs. contract definition mismatch**.
*   **Trigger:** If a client sues based on the marketing phrase "up-to-date compliance" rather than the contract's "software tool" definition, a court may view the cap as unconscionable or the failure as gross negligence, voiding the $2,500 limit.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Liability caps generally hold for ordinary negligence in B2B software but often fail when the vendor explicitly warrants a specific outcome (tax savings/compliance) that proves false.
*   **Step this week:** If your marketing claims "compliance," consider issuing an immediate addendum to all clients redefining the service as "automation assistance subject to professional review" and capping liability strictly to fees paid, removing any outcome-based warranties.

**Industry Practice & Negotiation Clauses**
Similar B2B regulatory software vendors typically handle this via three specific mechanisms:
1.  **"Timely Update" Standard:** Replace "guaranteed compliance" with a covenant to update the system within $X$ days of a law's enactment. Liability is limited to failures to meet this timeline, not the existence of the law itself.
2.  **Professional Review Disclaimer:** Explicitly state that the software is a decision-support tool and that the licensed tax preparer (the client) retains final responsibility for reviewing and filing returns. This shifts the duty of care back to the professional.
3.  **Consequential Damages Waiver:** Ensure your contract explicitly waives liability for "indirect, special, or consequential damages," which includes the client's penalties, interest, or lost business reputation resulting from the error.
