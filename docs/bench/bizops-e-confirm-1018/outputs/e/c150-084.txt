**The Call**
Do not structure the deal as a "lease" to disguise a service subscription; instead, anchor your pricing and any discounts strictly to a documented Fair Market Value (FMV) appraisal and ensure the arrangement fits the "Personal Services and Management Contracts" safe harbor, not the equipment lease safe harbor.

**The Numbers**
Your current unit economics show a hardware cost of $300 (one-time) and a recurring margin of $25/patient/month.
*   **Current MRR:** $10,000 (400 patients).
*   **Hardware Exposure:** If the new contract adds 1,000 patients, upfront kit cost is $300,000.
*   **Runway Math:** To hit an 18-month runway with $85,000 cash:
    *   Formula: `Runway = Cash / (Monthly Burn - Monthly Contribution Margin)`
    *   **ASSUMED (Base):** Your current non-COGS operating burn is $20,000/month (derived from needing ~4.25 months of current MRR to cover 18 months if growth is zero, which is unlikely; this is a thin data point).
    *   **Result:** At $25 contribution per patient, you need ~840 total patients to break even on a $20k burn. The new contract must commit to enough volume to cover the hardware upfront or require prepayment.
    *   **Trigger:** If the hospital refuses prepayment for kits, your cash runway drops below 6 months immediately upon deployment.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The single input that changes the call is the **FMV Documentation**. Without an independent, written FMV opinion validating that your $25 fee (and any volume discount) reflects general market rates and not the volume of referrals, the arrangement creates Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS) liability.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Discounts are only safe if they are fixed in advance, not tied to the *value* or *volume* of referrals generated, and consistent with FMV.
*   **Stop Trigger:** If the hospital's legal team insists on a "lease" structure for software/data services, stop negotiations. Leasing safe harbors apply to tangible equipment, not data analytics or implementation services. Mischaracterizing services as a lease is a primary audit target.
*   **Step this week:** Engage a healthcare valuation firm to produce an FMV report for your specific SaaS + implementation bundle before signing term sheets.

**Routing Note**
This is a Tier 3 (Route) issue. The Anti-Kickback Statute is a federal criminal law; "safe harbors" are affirmative defenses that require strict adherence to every element. You must retain healthcare regulatory counsel to: (1) draft the contract under the "Personal Services and Management Contracts" safe harbor (42 C.F.R. § 1001.952(d)), ensuring the aggregate compensation is set in advance; and (2) review any "Electronic Health Records Items and Services" safe harbor applicability if you are donating interoperability software, though this rarely covers full monitoring dashboards. Do not rely on general business advice for this structure.
