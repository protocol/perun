**The Numbers & Regulatory Thresholds:**
Unlike unit economics, your "thresholds" are legal definitions, not just revenue targets. In California, the determination hinges on whether you are facilitating a private sharing arrangement or acting as a reseller/aggregator of electricity.
*   **Current State:** 200 hosts, 5,000 kWh total, ~$8,000 gross transaction value (estimated at $1.60/kWh avg), $1,200 commission revenue.
*   **The Trigger:** The CPUC generally views the *sale* of electricity to the public as a public utility activity. While "peer-to-peer" models exist, the moment you aggregate demand, set prices, or take title to the energy (even briefly) before reselling, you risk classification as an Electric Service Provider (ESP) or a Demand Response Aggregator.
*   **Assumption (Labelled ASSUMED):** You are currently operating under the assumption that individual homeowners retain full control and liability, treating the platform as a neutral marketplace. If your terms of service or payment flow suggest VoltShare is the merchant of record for the electricity itself, this assumption fails.

The risk is binary: either the activity is exempt (e.g., purely private sharing without aggregation) or it requires certification. **What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **legal structure of the transaction flow**. If VoltShare collects payment from the driver and then pays the host (taking a cut), you are likely acting as a reseller. If the driver pays the host directly and only pays a separate fee to VoltShare for software access, the regulatory burden may shift.
