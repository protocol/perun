**What Flips It, and the Trigger:**
The single input that changes the call is the **legal classification of "selling electricity" vs. "leasing equipment with an energy charge."**
*   **Trigger:** If the Colorado Public Utilities Commission (PUC) or relevant statute defines any entity selling kWh to an end-user as a "public utility" regardless of scale, your current model is illegal without a Certificate of Public Convenience and Necessity (CPCN).
*   **Rule of Thumb:** In many jurisdictions, third-party ownership (TPO) models are legal only if structured strictly as a lease of the *equipment* where the energy charge is incidental, or if specific statutory exemptions for "small generators" exist.
*   **Step this week:** If you cannot find a specific Colorado statute exempting <50kW residential solar PPAs from utility classification, consider Y: pausing the PPA rollout and negotiating a "lease-with-option-to-purchase" structure where the homeowner pays a fixed equipment lease fee, rather than a variable per-kWh rate, until counsel approves.
