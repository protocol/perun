**The Call**
Do not choose one schedule for both purposes; instead, apply **component depreciation** for your financial reporting (book) to satisfy investors, while electing the most aggressive **tax depreciation** allowed by current US law (likely Section 179 or Bonus Depreciation) to minimize immediate tax liability. Treating the software updates as a separate, shorter-lived asset component aligns your book value with the economic reality of the product's obsolescence, making your balance sheet more credible to Series A investors who scrutinize asset longevity.

**The Numbers**
*Unit Cost:* $120,000. *Units Sold:* 5. *Total Hardware Cost Basis:* $600,000.
*Assumption (ASSUMED):* We split the $120k unit cost into $90k Hardware (12-year life) and $30k Software/Integration (3-year life) based on your update cycle description.
*Assumption (ASSUMED):* Straight-line depreciation for book purposes; no salvage value.

*\*Component Math: Hardware ($90k/12 = $7.5k) + Software ($30k/3 = $10k, replaced every 3 yrs). Year 1-3 expense is $17.5k; Year 4 resets software basis.*

**Taxable Income Impact:**
*   **Aggressive Tax (Section 179/Bonus):** You could potentially expense nearly the entire $600k in Year 1, drastically reducing taxable income now but creating low book value later.
*   **Book (Component):** Higher annual expense ($17.5k vs $10k) lowers reported net income on paper, but accurately reflects the recurring $30k reinvestment need every three years.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The deciding factor is **investor perception of recurring CapEx**. If you depreciate over 12 years, investors may assume the asset generates cash for a decade without major reinvestment. When you inevitably spend $150k (5 units × $30k) in Year 3 on updates, it will look like an unexpected hit to margins if not reserved for.
*   **Trigger:** If your Series A term sheet includes covenants based on **EBITDA** or **Net Asset Value**, the component method prevents a future surprise.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** If reinvestment exceeds 15% of the original asset cost within the first 25% of its life, separate the components.
*   **Step:** Before closing your books this quarter, draft a fixed-asset policy explicitly defining "Software Module" as a distinct asset class with a 3-year useful life.
