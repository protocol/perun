**The Call**
Switch from straight-line to an accelerated method (likely MACRS or Units-of-Production) immediately to match the 18-month actual useful life; this will lower current taxable income and improve cash flow, but the formal election and audit defense require a CPA.

**The Numbers**
*Current Model (Straight-Line):*
Formula: `Cost Basis / 60 months`
Assumption: $100,000 total gear cost (illustrative based on revenue scale).
Result: ~$1,667/month expense.

*Revised Model (Accelerated/Actual Life):*
Formula: `Cost Basis / 18 months`
Result: ~$5,556/month expense.
*Impact:* Increasing monthly depreciation by ~$3,889 reduces your reported profit by that amount. On $150k revenue, this could eliminate taxable income entirely for the current year, deferring tax liability.

*Runway Check:*
Revenue: $150,000/yr ($12,500/mo).
Burn: $10,000/mo.
Net Cash Flow: +$2,500/mo.
Runway: Positive, but thin. Reducing tax outflow via higher depreciation preserves cash.

**What Flips It, and the Trigger**
The binding constraint is **IRS compliance**, not math. The "flip" occurs if you file with the old schedule: you risk an audit adjustment, penalties, and back taxes because your books materially misstate asset life.
*Trigger:* File Form 4562 for the current tax year. If your fiscal year ends soon, the decision must be made before filing.
*Rule of Thumb:* If actual life < 50% of book life, the straight-line method is indefensible in an audit.
*This Week:* If your gear logs show 18-month failure rates, gather purchase dates and costs for all assets and email a CPA with the subject line: "Request for Form 3115 (Change in Accounting Method) and MACRS analysis."

**Routing Note**
**Tier 3 (Route):** You cannot internally "adjust" the depreciation schedule for tax purposes once a method is established without filing **IRS Form 3115** (Application for Change in Accounting Method). Choosing between MACRS (statutory accelerated) vs. Units-of-Production (usage-based) depends on your specific asset class and election history. A CPA must prepare this filing to avoid automatic IRS rejection or audit flags. Do not attempt this adjustment in your bookkeeping software without professional sign-off.
