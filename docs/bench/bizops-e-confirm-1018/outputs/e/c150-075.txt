**The Numbers & Legal Reality**
There is no fixed mathematical "threshold" (e.g., "20% human input") that guarantees copyright.
*   **US Regime:** The US Copyright Office and recent case law (e.g., *Thaler v. Perlmutter*, *Anderson v. Stability AI*) maintain that works generated primarily by AI without substantial human creative control are not copyrightable. The "80% AI / 20% human" split you described is highly risky; if the human contribution is limited to prompts or minor edits, the USCO will likely refuse registration.
*   **Canadian Regime:** Canada requires a human author for copyright to subsist. While slightly less litigated than the US, the principle remains that purely machine-generated works without human intellectual creation do not attract copyright protection.
*   **Assumption:** I am assuming your "20%" consists of prompts and light edits rather than substantial restructuring or creative rewriting. If the user merely guides the AI, the output is likely unprotectable.
*   **Result:** Without copyright, competitors could theoretically copy the text. It does not necessarily become "public domain" (which implies a voluntary dedication or expired term), but it enters a state where no one owns exclusive rights, meaning your customers cannot stop others from using their drafts.

**What Flips It, and The Trigger**
The deciding factor is the **degree of creative control and modification** exerted by the human user, not the percentage of words typed.
*   **Trigger:** If a user cannot demonstrate they made specific, creative choices in arranging, selecting, or modifying the AI output beyond simple prompting, copyright fails.
*   **Actionable Step:** This week, audit 10 random drafts. If the user's edits are purely corrective (grammar, formatting) rather than creative (restructuring arguments, adding unique technical insights), consider your product's output as uncopyrightable by default. Update your ToS to state: "User acknowledges that AI-generated content may not be eligible for copyright protection; PatentEase makes no warranty regarding copyrightability or patent office acceptance."
