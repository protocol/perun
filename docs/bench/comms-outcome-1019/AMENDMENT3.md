# Amendment A3 (2026-10-19), written after the first tune run (V0, NONE, RIVAL on 40 tune scenarios) was scored

Finding: while reading failed answers, 12 of the 40 tune scenarios held a gold `action` that is the drafting task itself ("Draft and send a message to Dana asking her to decide") rather than a step for the reader. The question asks the reader what the sender wants done next, so no message can match that gold, and it also duplicates the decision question. The check uses the gold text only, not any arm output or score. Tune accuracies had been seen when this was decided (V0 0.642, NONE 0.663, RIVAL 0.641); tune never yields a verdict, but the scenario set change is disclosed here.

- fixed2.py adds the rule (gold action matches `draft`, `message to`, `email to`, or `send ... message|email`) to the audit. The 12 flagged tune scenarios are dropped and replaced by scenarios written with `fixed2.py tune --fill 40` (same writer brief, fresh seed, unused kind and domain pairs, all audit rules). Kind balance after the audit is pr 12, incident 11, decision 8, exec 7, status 2 (status notes mostly ended in a drafting action). Outputs, reads and grades of the dropped ids stay in outputs/, reads/ and grades/ for transparency and are not scored.
- The held-out scenarios go through the same audit (drop, fill to n=30) before they are hashed.
- The grader's strictness is unchanged: it also marks some reasonable paraphrases false (for example "Reply with Path A or Path B" against "approve which path"). This lowers every arm's action score alike and is kept as a known limit rather than changed after a look at the scores.
- scenarios_tune.json was rewritten in place by the drop and fill, so its line in SHA256-frozen.txt (frozen by A1) is replaced by the new hash; the ledger event for A3 records the old and the new hash.
