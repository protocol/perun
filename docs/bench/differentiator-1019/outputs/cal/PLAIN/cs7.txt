There is no single, publicly available statistic for the "typical" trial-to-paid conversion rate specifically for fleet-tracking software sold to taxi cooperatives in Lagos. This data is considered proprietary by SaaS vendors and varies significantly based on the specific vendor, the structure of the cooperative, and the economic conditions at the time of sale.

However, we can estimate a realistic range based on general B2B SaaS benchmarks in emerging markets and the specific dynamics of the Lagos transportation sector.

In the global B2B SaaS sector, trial-to-paid conversion rates typically hover between **15% and 25%**. In emerging markets like Nigeria, where price sensitivity is higher and trust in digital tools must be earned through demonstrated ROI, this rate often skews lower, potentially falling between **10% and 20%** for self-service models.

For the specific niche of Lagos taxi cooperatives, several unique factors influence this metric:

1.  **Decision-Making Structure**: Taxi cooperatives in Lagos are often member-owned entities with collective decision-making processes. A "trial" usually involves a pilot with a small subset of vehicles. Conversion depends less on individual driver adoption and more on the cooperative leadership's confidence in the data provided (e.g., reduced fuel theft, improved route efficiency). If the pilot clearly demonstrates cost savings that outweigh the subscription fee, conversion rates can exceed **30%**.
2.  **Infrastructure Challenges**: Intermittent internet connectivity and power supply issues in Lagos can hinder the real-time functionality of tracking software during a trial. If the software fails to perform reliably during the pilot due to infrastructure gaps rather than software flaws, conversion rates will drop sharply.
3.  **Payment Models**: Many successful vendors in this region adapt their models to local cash flows. Instead of standard monthly subscriptions, they may offer weekly or daily payment structures aligned with drivers' earnings. Vendors that offer flexible payment terms typically see higher conversion rates than those insisting on rigid annual or monthly contracts.
4.  **Trust and Localization**: Solutions that offer localized support (e.g., Yoruba or Pidgin English interfaces, local customer success teams) tend to convert better than generic international solutions.

**Estimated Range**:
Considering these factors, a well-executed sales cycle involving a structured pilot program with a Lagos taxi cooperative likely yields a conversion rate between **20% and 35%**. Lower figures (under 15%) usually indicate a mismatch in pricing, poor trial support, or infrastructure failures during the pilot phase.

To get precise data for a specific business case, one would need to conduct primary research by interviewing local fleet management providers (such as local branches of international firms or indigenous startups like Monitran or similar logistics tech firms operating in West Africa) or analyzing internal sales data from companies currently serving the Lagos transport union (NURTW) affiliates.
