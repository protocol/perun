There is no single, publicly available dollar figure for the average Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) specifically for **B2B fintech startups selling to credit unions in Ohio**.

CAC is a private operational metric that varies wildly based on a startup's specific sales motion, product complexity, and efficiency. Furthermore, granular data segmented by both industry vertical (credit unions) and geography (Ohio) is not aggregated in public reports. Providing a specific number would be a fabrication and misleading for your financial model.

However, to help you build a realistic assumption for your model, here are the relevant benchmarks and logic you should use:

**1. The Sales Motion Dictates CAC**
Selling to credit unions is rarely a low-touch, self-serve motion. It is typically a **sales-led enterprise motion** involving long procurement cycles, security reviews, and committee approvals.
*   **Self-Serve SaaS:** CAC is often < $1,000.
*   **SMB Sales-Led:** CAC ranges from $5,000 to $15,000.
*   **Mid-Market/Enterprise (Credit Unions):** CAC frequently ranges from **$20,000 to $50,000+**.

**2. The "Ohio" Factor**
Geography alone does not significantly lower CAC in this context. While Ohio has a high density of credit unions, the decision-makers are cautious. The cost is driven by the *time* your sales team spends navigating bureaucracy, not the zip code. If anything, selling to smaller, local credit unions might require *more* hand-holding than selling to a national bank, potentially keeping CAC high relative to deal size.

**3. Recommended Modeling Approach**
Instead of guessing a flat fee, model CAC based on your **Sales Cycle** and **Fully Loaded Sales Cost**:
*   **Formula:** `(Total Sales & Marketing Expense) / (Number of New Customers Acquired)`
*   **Estimation Strategy:** If your Average Contract Value (ACV) is $25,000, a healthy B2B fintech LTV:CAC ratio is 3:1. This implies a target CAC of roughly **$8,300**. However, in early stages with inefficient processes, CAC often equals or exceeds the first year's ACV.

**Actionable Estimate for Your Model:**
If you must input a placeholder to test sensitivity:
*   **Conservative (High Efficiency):** $12,000
*   **Realistic (Standard Enterprise Sales):** $28,000
*   **Pessimistic (Long Cycle, Low Close Rate):** $45,000

**Recommendation:** Do not hard-code one number. Build a sensitivity table in your model varying CAC from **$15k to $40k** to see how it impacts your cash flow runway. Relying on a single "average" found online (which doesn't exist for this niche) is a critical modeling error.
