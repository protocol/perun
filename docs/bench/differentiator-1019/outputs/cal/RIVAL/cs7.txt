There is no publicly available benchmark for the specific trial-to-paid conversion rate of fleet-tracking software sold to taxi cooperatives in Lagos. This is a highly niche segment where data is rarely published.

However, we can derive a realistic target range by analyzing the intersection of three factors: **market maturity**, **buyer persona**, and **sales motion**.

### 1. The Context: Emerging Market + Cooperative Model
*   **Market Maturity:** In emerging markets like Nigeria, SaaS adoption for operational tools is growing but still faces friction regarding digital payments and trust. Conversion rates here are often lower than in mature markets (US/EU) during the early education phase.
*   **Buyer Persona (Cooperatives):** Taxi cooperatives in Lagos are not typical SMBs. They are community-based organizations with collective decision-making structures. The "trial" user (perhaps a tech-savvy dispatcher) is rarely the sole decision-maker. The final "yes" often requires consensus from union leaders or a board, extending the sales cycle and depressing immediate trial-to-paid metrics.
*   **Value Urgency:** If your software directly prevents theft or recovers stolen vehicles (a high pain point in Lagos), willingness to pay is high. If it is merely for "optimization," conversion will be low.

### 2. Estimated Conversion Benchmarks
Given the high-touch, trust-based nature of selling to cooperatives:

*   **Self-Serve Trial (Low Touch):** If you expect them to sign up online and convert without human interaction, expect **<2%**. This model rarely works for this specific demographic without significant local adaptation (e.g., WhatsApp-based onboarding).
*   **Sales-Assisted Trial (High Touch):** If the "trial" includes a demo, a physical meeting, and a pilot with a few cars managed by your team, a healthy conversion rate is **15% – 25%**.
    *   *Why so high?* Because the qualification bar is higher. You aren't getting thousands of signups; you are likely piloting with 10 specific co-ops. If the pilot proves value (e.g., "we recovered 2 cars in week 1"), the conversion should be very strong due to the acute pain of vehicle theft.

### 3. Critical Variables Influencing Your Rate
To move toward the higher end of that range, consider:
*   **Payment Friction:** Can they pay easily? If you require international credit cards, conversion is zero. You must support local bank transfers, USSD, or direct debit.
*   **Hardware Dependency:** Fleet tracking usually requires GPS hardware installation. If the trial doesn't include installed hardware, it's not a real trial. The conversion rate is tied to the speed of your installation team.
*   **Trust Signals:** Cooperatives rely on peer validation. A trial converts faster if you can show another Lagos cooperative already using the system successfully.

### Recommendation
Do not benchmark against global SaaS averages (typically 5-10% for self-serve). Instead, treat this as a **pilot-to-contract** motion.

Set an internal target of **20%** for qualified pilots where you have met the decision-makers physically. If you are below 10%, the issue is likely not the price, but a lack of trust in the hardware reliability or the payment mechanism. Focus your energy on on-ground relationships and localized payment options rather than optimizing a signup form.
