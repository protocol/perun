There is no single, publicly available statistic representing the average discount percentage that Estonian SaaS vendors offer specifically to public universities on three-year contracts.

This specific data point does not exist in any centralized database or public report for several reasons. First, B2B and B2G (business-to-government) pricing in the SaaS industry is almost universally confidential. Discount rates are negotiated individually based on the vendor's margin structure, the specific software module, the number of seats (users), and the strategic value of the university as a reference client. Second, while Estonia has a vibrant SaaS sector (including companies like Bolt, Wise, and Pipedrive), there is no industry body that aggregates or publishes granular pricing data segmented by customer type (public universities) and contract length (three years).

Furthermore, public procurement rules in Estonia and the EU often require transparency in the final contract value, but they do not mandate the disclosure of the "list price" versus the "discounted price" in a way that allows for easy calculation of a percentage discount across the entire market. A university might receive a 15% discount from one vendor for a niche tool and a 40% discount from another for a core platform, making an "average" statistically meaningless without a massive, private dataset of actual signed contracts.
