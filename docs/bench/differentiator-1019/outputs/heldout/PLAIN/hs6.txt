Software pricing in Chile, as in most global markets, is highly fragmented and depends on several variable factors that prevent a single "exact" number from existing:

1.  **Vendor Diversity**: The market includes global giants (like Clio, LexisNexis, or Thomson Reuters) and local Chilean providers (such as legal-tech startups or traditional ERP adaptors). Global companies often price in USD with purchasing power parity adjustments, while local firms price in CLP (Chilean Pesos) based on local economic conditions.
2.  **Tiered Pricing Models**: Most SaaS platforms offer multiple tiers (e.g., "Starter," "Professional," "Enterprise"). A solo lawyer might choose a bare-bones document storage plan for $15/month or a full-suite litigation management tool for $80/month. The "average" shifts drastically based on the feature mix selected by the user base.
3.  **Discounts and Contracts**: ARPA is heavily influenced by annual vs. monthly billing cycles. Users committing to annual contracts often receive 15–20% discounts, which lowers the effective monthly revenue recognized by the vendor compared to the list price.
4.  **Lack of Public Reporting**: Private software companies do not publish unit economics like ARPA per specific country and persona (solo lawyer). Even public companies report regional revenue in broad strokes (e.g., "Latin America") without breaking it down by firm size or specific product line.
