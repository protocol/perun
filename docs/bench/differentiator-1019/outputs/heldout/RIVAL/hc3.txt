The premise of your request contains three distinct factual errors:

1.  **No Specific Proof:** There is no academic paper or industry study that definitively "proves" per-seat pricing underperforms usage pricing as a universal rule. Pricing performance is highly contextual, depending on the product's value metric, customer persona, and market dynamics. While usage-based pricing (UBP) has gained significant traction in SaaS (popularized by companies like AWS, Snowflake, and Stripe), per-seat pricing remains the dominant and highly successful model for collaboration tools (e.g., Slack, Notion, Microsoft 365).
2.  **No Geographic Specificity:** There is no body of academic literature specifically analyzing "Finnish legal-tech startups" to the degree of isolating pricing model performance in a peer-reviewed proof. While Finland has a vibrant legal-tech scene (e.g., Smartia, Lacerte), no study has been published comparing pricing models exclusively within this narrow geographic and vertical segment.
3.  **Nature of Pricing Research:** Pricing strategy is rarely "proven" in the mathematical sense via a single paper. Instead, it is validated through market experimentation, Van Westendorp surveys, MaxDiff analysis, and A/B testing. Industry reports from firms like OpenView, Pacific Crest, or Price Intelligently may discuss trends, but they do not offer absolute proofs applicable to specific niches like Finnish legal-tech.

**Context on Pricing Models:**
The debate between per-seat and usage-based pricing is well-documented in general SaaS literature. Usage-based pricing aligns cost directly with value consumed, which can lower barriers to entry and scale with customer success. However, it introduces revenue unpredictability. Per-seat pricing offers predictable recurring revenue (ARR) but can create friction when adding users.
