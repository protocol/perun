No such randomized trial exists.

There is no academic study, industry report, or clinical trial that has established a "best tier ladder" specifically for **hobbyist beekeeping apps**. The market for beekeeping software is a micro-niche within the broader AgTech sector, characterized by a small number of specialized tools (e.g., HiveTracks, BeeKeepPal, Apiary Book) rather than a saturated market ripe for large-scale pricing experimentation published in major venues.

In the SaaS industry, pricing structures are rarely determined by external randomized trials published in academic journals. Instead, they are derived from:
1.  **Internal A/B Testing:** Proprietary data collected by the specific company on their own user base.
2.  **Van Westendorp Surveys:** Qualitative research on willingness to pay.
3.  **Competitive Benchmarking:** Observing what similar niche tools charge.
4.  **Value Metric Alignment:** Structuring tiers based on colony count, apiary locations, or data history rather than arbitrary feature gating.

If you are building a beekeeping app and need a pricing strategy, you should not look for a non-existent study to copy. Instead, apply standard SaaS pricing fundamentals:
*   **Value Metric:** Charge per **hive** or **apiary site**, as this scales directly with the user's operational complexity and value derived.
*   **Tier Structure:**
    *   **Free/Hobbyist:** 1–5 hives, basic logging, local storage.
    *   **Pro (Core):** 6–50 hives, cloud sync, treatment reminders, harvest tracking.
    *   **Sideline/Commercial:** 50+ hives, multi-user access, API integrations, advanced analytics.
*   **Price Points:** Given the hobbyist nature, price points typically range from **$5–$15/month** for Pro tiers, with annual discounts.

Attempting to find a specific "randomized trial" for this niche will yield no results because the sample size of beekeeping app users globally is likely too small to produce statistically significant results worthy of publication in a major venue like *Management Science* or *Journal of Marketing Research*. The "best" ladder is the one you validate with your own customers through iterative testing.
