There is **no single, publicly verified exact dollar amount** for the average revenue per account (ARPA) of solo-lawyer practice-management software specifically in Chile. SaaS companies rarely disclose granular ARPA data broken down by both profession (solo lawyers) and specific geography (Chile) in public reports.

However, to help you build your pricing page benchmark, we can derive a **highly informed estimate** based on Chilean market economics, legal tech purchasing power, and global SaaS benchmarks adjusted for purchasing power parity (PPP).

### The Estimated Benchmark Range

For a solo lawyer in Chile, the realistic ARPA range is **$25 – $45 USD per month**.

*   **Low End ($15–$25):** Basic tools, local startups, or early-stage products competing on price.
*   **Target Benchmark ($30–$40):** The "sweet spot" for a robust, value-based tool solving core pain points (billing, case tracking, client portal).
*   **High End ($50+):** Premium tools with advanced AI, integrations with Chilean government systems (e.g., Poder Judicial), or strong brand dominance.

### The Logic Behind the Number

1.  **Purchasing Power & Income:** A solo lawyer in Chile typically bills between $1,500 and $4,000 USD monthly depending on specialization. SaaS spend usually caps at 1–2% of gross revenue for non-mission-critical tools, but practice management is core. Therefore, a $30–$40 monthly fee represents ~1% of a modest income, which is psychologically acceptable for a business-critical tool.
2.  **Competitive Landscape:** Global giants (Clio, LexisNexis) often price too high for the average Chilean solo practitioner without localization. Local competitors often underprice due to feature gaps. The gap in the middle is where your benchmark sits.
3.  **Currency Friction:** Pricing in USD creates friction. If you charge in CLP, the psychological anchor changes. $35 USD is approx. 33,000 CLP. Pricing at **29,990 CLP** or **34,990 CLP** often converts better than a direct USD conversion.

### Strategic Recommendation for Your Pricing Page

Do not simply copy a competitor's number. Instead, use the **$10 / $100 / $1,000 rule** adapted for emerging markets:

*   **Avoid the "$9 Trap":** Pricing at $9 USD (approx. 8,500 CLP) signals "low quality" or "toy tool" to professionals. It attracts high-churn, high-support customers who cannot afford to grow with you.
*   **Anchor Higher:** Start your "Professional" tier at **$39 USD (approx. 37,000 CLP)**. This signals reliability.
*   **The "Learn From It" Approach:** As noted in pricing fundamentals, your first price is a bet. Pick **$35 USD**, launch, and measure conversion. If conversion is >5%, raise it. If <1%, investigate value communication before dropping price.

**Final Verdict:** Use **$35 USD/month** as your central benchmark for modeling, but plan to A/B test between **$29** and **$49** to find your specific market's willingness to pay.
