The proposal presents a high-risk entry into the cloud gaming market with a fundamentally flawed value proposition. The core offering requires users to pay a monthly subscription fee while also purchasing games at full price, despite already owning them on other platforms. This double-dipping model ignores established consumer expectations and creates an immediate barrier to adoption.

Furthermore, the technical limitations are significant. Streaming quality is entirely dependent on the user's internet connection, introducing input lag that undermines the experience for fast-paced, console-quality titles. In an era where low latency is critical for gaming, this technical hurdle cannot be ignored. The lack of exclusive content from major publishers further weakens the appeal, as there is no compelling library to drive subscriptions.

The company's reputation for discontinuing products adds another layer of risk. Consumers and publishers alike will be hesitant to invest time and money into a platform that may be shut down prematurely. This trust deficit makes securing exclusive deals even more difficult, creating a vicious cycle that stifles growth.

Launching proprietary game studios is a capital-intensive endeavor with long lead times and uncertain returns. Without an existing foothold in the gaming industry or a differentiated offering, this vertical integration strategy appears premature and financially imprudent. The combination of a poor pricing model, technical constraints, lack of content, and reputational risks suggests that the current proposal is not viable.

The market dynamics in 2019 favor established players with strong content libraries and proven streaming infrastructure. Entering this space without addressing the fundamental flaws in the business model would likely result in significant financial losses and damage to the brand. A complete strategic overhaul is necessary before any resources are committed.

DECISION: NO-GO
