The proposal presents a high-risk venture with multiple unresolved threats that outweigh the potential first-mover advantage. The core product relies on an ingredient that regulators explicitly flag with a warning label regarding digestive effects. In the fast-moving consumer goods sector, placing a health warning directly on packaging for a snack food is a significant barrier to adoption, likely validating the concern that buyers will be scared away.

Furthermore, the market environment appears hostile. Active opposition from consumer groups suggests an impending public relations battle that could damage the new brand before it gains traction. Launching with heavy advertising while under attack by advocacy groups would burn capital inefficiently. The economic model is also flawed; the chips cost more than regular alternatives. Asking price-sensitive snack buyers to pay a premium for a product with a digestive warning and controversial ingredients is a weak value proposition.

Finally, the strategic timing is questionable. The proposal acknowledges a potential shift in dieter focus away from fat reduction. If the primary consumer benefit is low fat, but the market trend is moving toward other metrics like carbs or sugar, the product may become obsolete shortly after launch. Investing heavily in a national rollout under these conditions ignores the convergence of regulatory stigma, activist opposition, price disadvantages, and shifting consumer trends. The risks are too concentrated and immediate to proceed without addressing these fundamental flaws. A pilot test or reformulation strategy would be prudent, but the current plan for a full national launch is unsound.

DECISION: NO-GO
