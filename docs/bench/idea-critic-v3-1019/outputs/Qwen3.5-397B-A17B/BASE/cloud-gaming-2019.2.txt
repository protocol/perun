The proposal presents a high-risk entry into the cloud gaming market with a fundamentally flawed value proposition. The core offering requires users to pay a monthly subscription fee while also purchasing games at full price, despite already owning many titles on other platforms. This double-cost model is uncompetitive compared to existing services that offer libraries included in the subscription or allow digital license porting.

Technical viability is severely compromised by the reliance on user internet connections. Streaming console-quality games introduces input lag, which directly degrades the experience for fast-paced genres essential to the medium. Without proprietary compression technology or a dedicated low-latency network backbone explicitly mentioned as ready, the service will likely underperform against local hardware.

Strategic alignment is weak. The company has no prior game business experience and a documented reputation for discontinuing products. This history erodes consumer trust, making users hesitant to invest in a new ecosystem where their purchased games and subscriptions could vanish. Furthermore, the lack of exclusive content from major publishers removes the primary driver for platform adoption. Building internal studios is a capital-intensive, long-term strategy that delays revenue and increases burn rate without guaranteeing hit titles.

The combination of a hostile pricing model, technical limitations regarding latency, a trust deficit with consumers, and a lack of content partners creates a scenario where failure is the most probable outcome. The risks outweigh the potential rewards, and the current plan lacks the necessary pivots to address these critical weaknesses. Approving this now would commit significant resources to a likely failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
