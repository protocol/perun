The proposal to shift from perpetual licenses to an exclusive monthly subscription model presents a transformative opportunity but carries immediate, severe risks that cannot be ignored without mitigation. The primary concern is the vocal objection from the existing customer base, who view the change as losing ownership of essential tools. This sentiment is not merely emotional; it directly threatens revenue stability. If users feel forced into a rental model they dislike, the predicted migration to cheaper rivals or pirated copies becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy, eroding the very market share the company seeks to secure.

Furthermore, the admission that revenue will dip during the transition is a critical red flag for an executive team responsible for fiscal health. A deliberate reduction in income, combined with the potential loss of customers to competitors, creates a dangerous financial valley. Without a clear strategy to bridge this gap or evidence that the long-term recurring revenue will outweigh the immediate churn and brand damage, proceeding immediately is reckless. The risk of users losing access entirely upon payment cessation adds a layer of friction that could accelerate defection rates beyond recovery.

While the industry trend may favor subscriptions, executing this as an abrupt "stop and switch" mandate ignores the specific friction points raised. The loud objections indicate a lack of market readiness for such a hard cutoff. A successful transition requires a phased approach, perhaps offering both models temporarily or creating compelling incentives that make the subscription undeniable rather than mandatory. Approving this proposal as written, with its guarantee of revenue dips and customer alienation, fails the basic test of risk management. The potential for permanent brand damage and irreversible customer loss outweighs the theoretical benefits of continuous updates at this specific moment without further planning. The risks are too high, and the mitigation strategy is non-existent in the current text.

DECISION: NO-GO
