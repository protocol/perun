The proposal suggests acquiring the third-most-visited website for $3.6 billion in stock to boost user numbers and page views. While the target offers massive traffic, the financial logic is deeply flawed. The acquisition price is enormous compared to the hosting service's current small revenue, creating an unjustifiable valuation gap. Paying with stock at record market highs exposes the company to severe downside if the bubble bursts, effectively overpaying with inflated currency.

Strategic risks further undermine the deal. Advertisers explicitly dislike placing brands next to amateur content, which constitutes the core inventory of the target site. This fundamental mismatch suggests the projected advertising revenue will not materialize as expected. Additionally, attempting to monetize by changing terms of service risks alienating the very user base being purchased, potentially causing a mass exodus of content creators and visitors.

The plan relies on optimistic assumptions about converting free users into profitable ad impressions without addressing the quality concerns of buyers. Buying traffic volume does not guarantee revenue quality. Given the excessive cost, the reliance on peak market valuations, the hostility of advertisers to the content environment, and the high probability of user backlash, the risks overwhelmingly outweigh the potential benefits. The proposal fails to demonstrate a viable path to recouping the $3.6 billion investment under realistic conditions. Proceeding now would likely destroy shareholder value.

DECISION: NO-GO
