The proposal presents a classic pivot scenario where existing assets are repurposed to address a failing core business. The game is unprofitable with insufficient players, yet significant venture capital remains. This liquidity provides a runway to attempt a strategic shift before funds are exhausted. The internal chat tool, already built with searchable history and integrations, represents a tangible asset with proven utility within the studio.

However, the risks are substantial. The market in 2013 for enterprise communication is crowded with established email and chat solutions. The team lacks specific experience in business software, having focused entirely on gaming. Furthermore, the human cost of laying off most staff could devastate the morale of the remaining engineers needed to rebuild the product for a commercial audience. Investors also face a misalignment, having funded a game rather than a B2B utility.

Despite these hurdles, the alternative is the gradual depletion of funds on a non-viable game. Continuing the current path guarantees failure once the money runs out. The proposed pivot leverages the remaining capital to test a new hypothesis using a tool that already functions. While the team lacks business software experience, they have successfully engineered a complex coordination tool for their own needs, suggesting technical competency. The market risk is real, but the opportunity to capture value from a working prototype outweighs the certainty of the current trajectory's failure. The layoffs, while painful, are a necessary restructuring to align the burn rate with the new strategic direction. The investor mismatch is a governance issue to be managed through transparent communication about preserving capital value, rather than a reason to abandon a potentially viable product.

Stalling to revise or holding the project ignores the urgency of the financial situation. The only path that offers a chance of return on the remaining investment is to execute the pivot immediately, accepting the risks as the cost of survival.

DECISION: GO
