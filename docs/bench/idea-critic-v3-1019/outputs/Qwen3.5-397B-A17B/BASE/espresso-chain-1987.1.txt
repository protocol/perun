The proposal presents a high-risk expansion strategy in a market where the core product remains a niche. The majority of consumers prefer cheap drip coffee, while the proposed espresso drinks cost several times more than standard diner options. This significant price disparity creates a substantial barrier to mass adoption. Furthermore, the plan requires expanding into distant cities, which demands heavy capital investment at a time when the buyer possesses no existing funds and must rely entirely on raising money from external investors.

Combining a small operator of a few bars with a six-store roaster does not provide the scale necessary to justify the financial exposure of a multi-city rollout. The reliance on investor capital for an unproven concept in unfamiliar markets increases the likelihood of failure. The risks identified—limited market demand, high price points, and the necessity of significant upfront funding without a safety net—outweigh the potential benefits of rebranding and expansion. Proceeding immediately would expose the investors and the operator to untenable financial danger without first validating the concept beyond its current local footprint or securing a more conservative growth path. The current economic environment for specialty coffee does not support such an aggressive, capital-intensive leap.

DECISION: NO-GO
