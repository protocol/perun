The proposal to launch a preview of this new communication tool to 100,000 users in 2009 carries unacceptable risks given the current state of the product. The core value proposition relies entirely on network effects, yet the mechanism for growth is fundamentally broken. If invitations are scarce and the tool is only useful when contacts also participate, the initial cohort will likely fail to derive any value, leading to immediate churn and negative word-of-mouth.

Furthermore, the product fails on basic functional grounds. Users are already confused about the tool's purpose, indicating a critical failure in positioning and user experience design. In a market dominated by established email protocols, launching a system that does not interoperate with existing email creates a massive barrier to entry rather than a seamless transition. Users cannot be expected to abandon a universal standard for a walled garden that offers no clear advantage.

Technical performance is equally concerning. Running slowly in current browsers suggests the underlying architecture is not ready for scale. A real-time collaboration tool requiring heavy browser resources will frustrate users accustomed to the snappiness of standard email clients. Releasing a slow, confusing, isolated product to a large preview group risks cementing a reputation of failure before the technology can mature.

The strategy of replacing email immediately is premature. The lack of interoperability means this cannot function as a primary communication channel for most users. Without fixing the speed, clarifying the use case, and enabling connection with the existing email ecosystem, expanding access will only amplify these defects. The scarcity of invitations combined with high friction ensures the pilot will not generate the organic growth needed to justify the expansion. Resources should be redirected to resolve these foundational issues before any public rollout is attempted. Approving this now would waste capital and damage the company's brand credibility.

DECISION: NO-GO
