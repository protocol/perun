The proposal to shift from perpetual licenses to a mandatory monthly subscription model presents significant immediate threats that outweigh the potential long-term benefits in the current context. The primary concern is the vocal and substantial objection from the existing customer base, who explicitly dislike the concept of renting essential tools. Forcing this transition risks alienating loyal users who rely on permanent access, potentially driving them toward cheaper competitors or encouraging the use of pirated copies, which directly erodes market share.

Furthermore, the admission that revenue will dip during the transition creates an unacceptable financial vulnerability. Without a safety net or detailed mitigation plan for this shortfall, the company risks destabilizing its operations before the recurring revenue stream can mature. The loss of access for non-paying users adds another layer of friction, removing the incentive for lapsed customers to return and potentially damaging the brand's reputation for reliability.

While continuous updates offer value, the proposal fails to address how to manage the cultural and financial shock of removing the perpetual option entirely. The risks of customer churn, revenue contraction, and increased piracy are too severe to proceed without a revised strategy that includes a hybrid approach or a more gradual migration path. Approving this plan as written invites unnecessary instability.

DECISION: NO-GO
