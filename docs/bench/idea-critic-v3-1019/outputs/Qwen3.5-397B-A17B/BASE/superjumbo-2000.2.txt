The proposal seeks to commit $10.7 billion to develop a 550-seat double-deck aircraft, relying on 50 firm orders to challenge a dominant rival. However, the strategic alignment with current market trends is critically flawed. The data explicitly states that airlines are shifting toward point-to-point routes using smaller twin-engine jets, while major hub airports face severe congestion. This new aircraft directly contradicts these shifts by requiring even larger capacity and imposing significant infrastructure burdens.

The risks identified are not merely theoretical; they are structural barriers to success. Requiring airports to modify gates and taxiways creates a massive adoption hurdle, limiting the number of viable destinations for this aircraft. If hubs are already congested, adding a plane that requires specialized, space-intensive handling exacerbates the problem rather than solving it. Furthermore, the assumption that the market for very large aircraft remains robust ignores the stated industry migration toward smaller, more flexible jets.

The competitive landscape adds another layer of uncertainty. The rival, holding the largest market share for three decades, possesses the flexibility to stretch an existing jumbo model rather than funding an entirely new development program. This allows them to meet niche demand for capacity at a fraction of the cost and risk. Meanwhile, this proposal locks the company into a $10.7 billion expenditure with a high probability of budget overruns, targeting a shrinking segment of the market.

With only 50 orders from six customers, the initial demand is insufficient to guarantee long-term viability against the backdrop of changing airline preferences. The combination of misaligned market trends, high infrastructure requirements for customers, and the potential for a lower-cost competitive response makes this investment untenable in its current form. The financial exposure is too great given the clear evidence that the industry is moving away from the very concept this aircraft represents.

DECISION: NO-GO
