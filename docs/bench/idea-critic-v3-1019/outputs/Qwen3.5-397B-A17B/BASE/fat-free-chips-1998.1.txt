The proposal presents a high-risk venture with multiple unresolved threats that outweigh the potential first-mover advantage. The product relies on an ingredient that regulators explicitly flag with a mandatory warning label regarding digestive side effects. In the fast-moving consumer goods sector, placing a health warning directly on packaging for a snack food is a significant barrier to adoption, likely validating the concern that buyers will be scared away.

Furthermore, active opposition from consumer groups creates an immediate reputational hazard. Launching a national campaign while under fire from advocacy organizations invites negative media cycles that heavy advertising spending may only amplify rather than mitigate. The economic model is also fragile; the product costs more than regular chips, yet offers a single benefit (fat reduction) that is already under scrutiny due to shifting consumer trends. If dieters are moving their focus away from fat content toward other nutritional concerns, the core value proposition is eroding before the launch even begins.

Proceeding with a national rollout under these conditions ignores the convergence of regulatory stigma, activist opposition, price disadvantage, and changing market preferences. The combination of a mandatory negative label and higher price point creates a scenario where the product is both less appealing and more expensive than existing alternatives. Heavy advertising cannot easily overcome a fundamental distrust of the ingredient or a warning printed on the bag. The risks are too systemic to be solved by marketing spend alone. Approval at this stage would expose the company to significant financial loss and brand damage without a clear path to profitability. The proposal requires substantial revision to address the ingredient controversy, pricing strategy, and shifting consumer demands before any launch can be considered.

DECISION: NO-GO
