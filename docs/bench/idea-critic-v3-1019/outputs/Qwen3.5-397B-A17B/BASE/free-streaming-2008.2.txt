The proposal presents a high-risk venture in a volatile market. While the concept of legal, instant streaming addresses the piracy crisis, the financial structure is precarious. The requirement for large upfront payments and equity grants to major labels creates an immediate, heavy cash burn before any revenue is generated. Given that ad revenue per listener is acknowledged as low, the free tier will likely operate at a significant loss, requiring massive scale to break even.

Furthermore, the assumption that users will convert to a paid ad-free tier is weak when high-quality piracy remains free and effortless. If most users never pay, as the risks suggest, the company will rely almost entirely on advertising income that may not cover the substantial licensing costs. The combination of high fixed costs (label advances), low marginal revenue (ads), and uncertain conversion rates creates a path to insolvency rather than profitability.

Entering several European countries simultaneously multiplies these risks, stretching limited resources across different regulatory and market environments without proven unit economics. The equity giveaway further dilutes future value for the founders and early investors, reducing the upside even if the company survives. Without a clear mechanism to force conversion or drastically lower licensing costs, the business model cannot sustain itself against the entrenched alternative of free piracy. The current terms favor the record labels disproportionately, transferring most of the financial risk to the startup while capping its potential reward.

Approval in the current form would likely result in rapid capital depletion with little chance of long-term viability. The fundamental economics do not support launch under these specific conditions.

DECISION: NO-GO
