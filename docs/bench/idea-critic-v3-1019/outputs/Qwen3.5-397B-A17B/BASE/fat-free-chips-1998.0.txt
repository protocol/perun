The proposal to launch a national line of fat-free potato chips in 1998 faces critical, unresolved threats that jeopardize immediate success. While regulatory approval exists for savory snacks, the mandatory label warning regarding digestive side effects creates a significant psychological barrier for consumers. In the food industry, explicit warnings on packaging often deter trial, regardless of advertising spend. This risk is compounded by active opposition from consumer groups, which can rapidly erode brand trust before the product gains traction.

Furthermore, the economic value proposition is weak. The chips cost more than regular alternatives, yet they offer a compromised experience due to potential digestive discomfort. Consumers are unlikely to pay a premium for a product that carries a health warning, especially when cheaper, traditional options remain available. The market landscape is also shifting; if dieters are moving their focus away from fat reduction toward other nutritional concerns, the core benefit of this fat substitute becomes less relevant. Launching a heavy advertising campaign under these conditions risks burning capital on a product fundamentally misaligned with current consumer sentiment and price sensitivity.

The combination of a stigmatizing label, activist opposition, higher price points, and evolving dietary trends suggests a high probability of market rejection. Proceeding now would expose the company to significant financial loss and reputational damage without a clear path to profitability. The risks outweigh the potential first-mover advantage in the fat-free category. Approval cannot be granted without a strategy to mitigate the label impact, address the price disparity, or validate the continuing demand for fat-focused dieting. As the proposal stands, it is too vulnerable to fail.

DECISION: NO-GO
