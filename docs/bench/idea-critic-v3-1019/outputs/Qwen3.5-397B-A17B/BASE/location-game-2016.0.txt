The proposal presents a high-reward opportunity tempered by significant operational and reputational risks. Partnering with a famous franchise offers immediate access to a massive audience, potentially transforming the studio's modest player base into a global phenomenon. However, the current infrastructure is critically insufficient. The existing servers, proven only with a small, devoted group, will likely collapse under the weight of a mainstream launch, leading to immediate user churn and brand damage.

Safety concerns regarding users walking while distracted are not merely hypothetical; they represent a tangible liability that could result in lawsuits and negative media coverage, especially given the high visibility of the partner franchise. Furthermore, the revenue model relies on a volatile conversion rate typical of free-to-play titles. If the majority of users do not spend money, the cost of scaling infrastructure to support them may outweigh the income generated from the few "whales."

Historically, location-based games have remained niche for valid reasons, including battery drain, data usage, and the friction of physical movement. While the partner brand might temporarily overcome this niche status, sustaining engagement requires more than just a famous name; it demands a flawless technical experience and robust safety protocols, neither of which are currently addressed in the proposal.

Approving this project "as proposed, now" ignores the glaring gap between current capabilities and projected demand. The risks of server failure, public safety incidents, and financial underperformance due to low conversion rates are too severe to proceed without mitigation. The studio must first demonstrate the ability to scale its technology, implement comprehensive safety features, and validate the economic model with a larger pilot group before committing to a full-scale launch with a major intellectual property. Proceeding immediately invites catastrophic failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
