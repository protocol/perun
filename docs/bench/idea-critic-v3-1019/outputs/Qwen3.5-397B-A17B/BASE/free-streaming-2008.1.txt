The proposal presents a high-risk venture in a volatile market. While the concept of legal, instant streaming addresses the piracy crisis, the financial structure is precarious. The requirement for large upfront payments and equity to major labels creates an immediate, heavy capital burden before any revenue is generated. Given that ad revenue per listener is acknowledged as low, the free tier will likely operate at a significant loss, requiring the paid tier to subsidize it heavily. However, the risk that most users will never convert to paid subscriptions, combined with the continued ease of free piracy, suggests the conversion rates will be insufficient to cover licensing costs and operational expenses.

The timing in 2008 adds complexity; digital infrastructure and consumer willingness to pay for streaming are unproven at the scale required to satisfy label demands. Without a guaranteed path to profitability or a mechanism to reduce the massive upfront licensing costs, the company faces a high probability of cash flow failure shortly after launch. The risks outlined by the team are not merely theoretical hurdles but fundamental threats to the business model's viability. Proceeding as proposed would expose the organization to substantial financial loss with a low probability of sustainable return. The current terms favor the record labels disproportionately, transferring almost all market risk to the startup. Until the cost structure is renegotiated or a more robust revenue projection is demonstrated, approval cannot be granted.

DECISION: NO-GO
