The proposal presents a high-reward opportunity tempered by significant operational and safety risks that cannot be ignored. Partnering with a famous franchise offers immediate access to a massive audience, potentially solving the "niche" limitation of previous location-based games. However, the current infrastructure is wholly inadequate for this scale. The studio's servers have only supported a modest player base; a sudden influx from a major franchise partnership will almost certainly cause catastrophic system failures, ruining the user experience and damaging the brand reputation of both parties immediately upon launch.

Furthermore, the safety concerns regarding players walking while distracted are not merely theoretical liabilities but potential public relations disasters waiting to happen. Without established protocols or features to mitigate these risks, the company exposes itself to legal action and severe public backlash. The revenue model relies on a small percentage of users making purchases, which is a standard but volatile strategy. If the server crashes or safety incidents occur early, user retention will plummet before any significant revenue can be generated.

The combination of unproven scalability, unaddressed safety liabilities, and the inherent volatility of free-to-play monetization creates a scenario where the likelihood of failure outweighs the potential for success in the current state. Proceeding without first reinforcing the technical backbone and establishing comprehensive safety measures is reckless. The gap between the studio's current capabilities and the demands of a global franchise launch is too wide to bridge instantly. Approval in the present form invites disaster. The project requires a fundamental restructuring of its technical roadmap and risk mitigation strategies before any green light can be considered.

DECISION: NO-GO
