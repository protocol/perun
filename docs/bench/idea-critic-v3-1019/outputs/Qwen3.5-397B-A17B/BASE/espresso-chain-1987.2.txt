The proposal presents a high-risk expansion strategy in a market where consumer habits strongly favor cheap drip coffee over expensive espresso drinks. The core assumption that a niche, premium product can rapidly scale to distant cities contradicts the stated reality of national preferences. Furthermore, the financial structure is precarious; the venture requires heavy capital for geographic expansion while simultaneously needing to raise all purchase funds from external investors. This creates a double burden of debt or equity dilution before a single new store opens.

The operator's experience is limited to a few small bars, yet the plan jumps immediately to a multi-city chain without a proven model for scaling operations or managing the complexities of a roaster-retailer hybrid. The price point, being several times higher than standard diner coffee, severely limits the addressable market, making the volume required to justify heavy capital expenditure unlikely to materialize quickly.

Relying on investor money to buy an existing six-store entity and then immediately fund aggressive expansion leaves no margin for error. If the niche appeal does not translate in new cities, the entire capital structure could collapse. The risks identified—niche demand, high price sensitivity, heavy capital needs, and reliance on external funding—are not merely hurdles but fundamental threats to viability in the current climate. Proceeding as proposed ignores the mismatch between the product's premium positioning and the broader market's price expectations. The lack of a phased approach or a proof-of-concept for inter-city expansion makes the immediate commitment of investor funds irresponsible.

DECISION: NO-GO
