The company faces a critical juncture after two years of losses and shrinking market share. Continuing current operations guarantees further decline, as the existing product line fails to resonate with consumers or stem financial bleeding. The proposal suggests a radical departure: a low-priced, translucent all-in-one computer that eliminates the diskette drive and legacy ports in favor of CD-ROM, USB, and internet connectivity.

While the risks are substantial, they stem from challenging established norms rather than technical impossibility. The concern regarding buyer expectations for diskette drives is valid but short-sighted; the industry trajectory clearly favors higher-capacity optical media and network distribution. Clinging to obsolete technology to satisfy temporary comfort will only delay the inevitable transition and burden the hardware with unnecessary costs and complexity. Similarly, the scarcity of USB peripherals is a temporary market gap that this very product could help close by driving adoption. If the ecosystem does not shift, no new standards will ever take hold.

The financial constraint is the most pressing issue, leaving little room for error. However, investing in a slightly improved version of a failing strategy is a guaranteed loss. A bold, differentiated product offers the only plausible path to reversing public perception and capturing attention in a crowded market. The unique design and simplified architecture could reduce manufacturing costs while creating a distinct brand identity that competitors lack.

Rejecting this proposal means maintaining the status quo, which has already proven unsustainable. The fear of incompatibility with older printers and drives must be weighed against the necessity of leading the market forward rather than following it into obsolescence. The potential reward of redefining the consumer computer category outweighs the risk of sticking to a dying model. Immediate action is required to halt the financial hemorrhage and regain relevance.

DECISION: GO
