The proposal presents a high-stakes initiative with catastrophic failure modes that currently outweigh potential benefits. The core concept relies on a global digital currency backed by bank deposits and government securities, governed by a consortium of partners. However, the identified risks are not merely operational hurdles; they are existential threats to the project's viability.

Regulatory opposition from the US and Europe regarding monetary sovereignty is a primary blocker. Unlike standard fintech products, a global currency issued by a private entity directly challenges state control over money supply and policy. Given the company's existing scrutiny over privacy scandals and antitrust issues, regulators are highly unlikely to grant the necessary approvals. The lack of trust in the company's data handling further complicates any argument for entrusting them with global financial infrastructure.

Furthermore, the governance model depends on large partner companies remaining committed. The proposal explicitly notes that partners may withdraw. If key financial or commercial partners exit due to regulatory pressure or reputational risk, the basket backing the currency could become unstable, leading to a loss of peg and a potential run on the asset. This fragility makes the system unsafe for two billion users.

Finally, the complexity of differing money-laundering rules across jurisdictions creates an almost insurmountable compliance barrier. A unified global currency requires unified compliance standards, which do not exist. Attempting to navigate conflicting national laws without a clear, pre-approved framework invites immediate legal action and fines.

Launching now, as proposed, ignores these fundamental structural flaws. The combination of hostile regulatory environments, fragile partner dependence, and the company's damaged reputation creates a scenario where failure is more probable than success. Proceeding would expose the organization to severe legal, financial, and reputational damage. The project cannot move forward in its current form without resolving these foundational issues, which requires significant revision and likely a complete restructuring of the governance and regulatory strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
