The proposal presents a high-risk venture in a hostile 2008 market environment. While the concept of instant streaming addresses the convenience gap left by piracy, the financial structure is fundamentally flawed. The requirement for large upfront payments to major labels creates an immediate, massive cash burn before a single user subscribes. Given that ad revenue per listener is explicitly noted as low, the free tier will likely operate at a significant loss, requiring the paid tier to subsidize it heavily.

However, the assumption that most users will convert to a paid plan contradicts the stated reality that free piracy remains easy and widespread. If consumers can access music for free illegally without consequence, the friction of paying a subscription fee, even for an ad-free experience, will result in conversion rates too low to cover the substantial licensing guarantees. The equity swap offers some relief by reducing immediate cash outlay, but it dilutes ownership in a company whose primary asset—user base monetization—is unproven and threatened by established free alternatives.

Launching simultaneously in several European countries amplifies these risks by multiplying operational costs and legal complexities across different jurisdictions without validated unit economics in a single market. The combination of high fixed costs (label advances), low variable revenue (ads), and uncertain conversion (piracy competition) creates a path to insolvency before product-market fit can be established. The risks outweigh the potential first-mover advantage because the underlying business model relies on user behavior changing drastically despite the continued availability of free, illegal options. Approval in the current form exposes the company to unsustainable financial liability.

DECISION: NO-GO
