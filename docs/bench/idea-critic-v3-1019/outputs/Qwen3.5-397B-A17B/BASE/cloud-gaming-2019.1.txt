The proposal presents a high-risk entry into the cloud gaming market with a fundamentally flawed value proposition. The core offering requires users to pay a monthly subscription fee while also purchasing games at full price, despite already owning these titles on other platforms. This double-dipping model creates an immediate barrier to adoption, especially when combined with the technical limitations of streaming. Since performance relies entirely on the user's internet connection, inevitable input lag and quality fluctuations will frustrate gamers accustomed to local hardware precision.

Furthermore, the company's historical reputation for discontinuing products undermines consumer confidence. Gamers are unlikely to invest in a library of digital goods on a platform they fear may be shuttered prematurely. This trust deficit is compounded by the lack of exclusive content; without unique titles to drive demand, there is little incentive for players to switch from established console ecosystems that offer superior reliability and ownership rights.

The plan to build proprietary game studios adds significant upfront cost and time-to-market delays without guaranteeing hit titles. Given the absence of a network effect, existing files, or specialized tools to mitigate these risks, launching now would likely result in substantial financial loss and reputational damage. The combination of a hostile pricing structure, technical vulnerabilities, low publisher support, and brand skepticism makes immediate approval untenable. The strategic gaps are too wide to bridge without a complete restructuring of the business model and risk mitigation strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
