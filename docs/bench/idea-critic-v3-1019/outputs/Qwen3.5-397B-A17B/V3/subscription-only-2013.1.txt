**Claim:** Replace perpetual licenses with a subscription-only model for creative software.

**Steelman:** Recurring revenue stabilizes cash flow, funds continuous innovation, and aligns vendor success with user retention, preventing the "upgrade cliff" of periodic releases.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal ignores a fatal blocker: **immediate revenue collapse and mass churn.** The facts state customers "object loudly" to renting and will switch to rivals or piracy if forced. Unlike enterprise infrastructure tools where switching is hard, creative tools rely on user habit and portfolio portability; friction here drives users to competitors instantly.

The "better way" test fails. A forced, immediate cutover (as proposed) sacrifices the installed base's lifetime value before subscription recurring revenue can mature. The risk of "revenue dip" is not a risk; it is a certainty that threatens solvency given the scale of "thousands of dollars" per unit lost. The proposal offers no "grandfathering" for existing users, no hybrid tier, and no migration incentive. It assumes coercion works where preference dictates otherwise.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, revenue is down 40%. Loyal users have migrated to a competitor's perpetual model or cracked versions. Brand reputation is destroyed among the core prosumer base. The company cannot fund the "continuous updates" promised because the cash cow was slaughtered before the subscription engine spun up.

**Edge:** None identified. Being first to force a hostile transition is not an advantage; it is a strategic error. The proposal lacks a kill criterion or a phased approach. It demands an irreversible door (alienating the entire current base) based on the hope that holdouts will eventually return, which contradicts the stated fact that they will switch to rivals.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the blocker test. It relies on forcing a behavior change that the evidence says will result in immediate defection. Without a hybrid option, a grandfather clause, or a phased rollout to test price elasticity, this is a suicide pact, not a strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
