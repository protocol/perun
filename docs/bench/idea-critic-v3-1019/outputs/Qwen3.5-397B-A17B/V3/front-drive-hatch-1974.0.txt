**Steelman:** Replace a declining, niche rear-engine car with a modern, efficient front-wheel-drive hatchback to survive the oil crisis and capture mass-market demand, despite high retooling costs and cultural attachment to the legacy model.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces severe headwinds: shrinking profits, high capital expenditure for new tooling, and a lack of internal expertise in water-cooled, front-drive architectures. The risk of alienating the loyal base is real, as the new car sacrifices the unique character of the incumbent. However, the "do nothing" alternative is fatal; reliance on a falling sales volume for a single aging model during an energy crisis guarantees insolvency. The oil crisis acts as a forced market shift toward fuel efficiency, rendering air-cooled, rear-engine designs increasingly obsolete regardless of sentiment.

While the company lacks experience with the new technology, this is a survivable execution risk compared to the existential risk of product irrelevance. The outside designer mitigates the risk of stylistic stagnation. The high cost of retooling is a barrier, but it is a necessary investment to access the larger, efficiency-conscious market segment that now dominates due to economic conditions. The proposal correctly identifies that the current trajectory leads to zero; the proposed path offers a chance at recovery, provided the transition is managed carefully.

**Blockers:** NONE. The financial strain is severe but not a factual impossibility; it is a constraint to be managed, not a stopper.
**Risks:** Execution failure due to inexperience; cash flow crunch during retooling; brand alienation. These are managed by the urgency of the market shift.
**Edge:** First-mover advantage in pivoting a heritage brand to modern efficiency standards before competitors with similar legacy burdens act.

The plan beats "do nothing" because the status quo is a slow death. The risks are high, but they are the price of survival. The proposal addresses the core threat (market irrelevance) directly.

DECISION: GO
