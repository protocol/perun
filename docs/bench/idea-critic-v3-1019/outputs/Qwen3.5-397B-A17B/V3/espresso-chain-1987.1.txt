**Steelman:** Acquiring the roaster secures supply chain control and brand heritage, allowing the operator to scale a differentiated premium espresso concept beyond local limits before competitors saturate the niche.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests. The core assumption—that a niche, high-priced product can sustain heavy capital expansion in distant cities—is contradicted by the stated market reality: the majority of the country drinks cheap drip coffee. There is no evidence of demand in target cities, only the operator's past success in a few local bars. The "edge" is weak; being a former director of a different entity offers no distribution advantage, and "Italian-style" is a product feature, not a moat against established diners or future chains.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids massive debt and the risk of expanding an unproven concept into hostile markets.
2.  **Licensing/Franchising:** Test the brand in new cities using other people's capital before buying the roaster.
3.  **Acquire Only Bars:** Buy the roaster only after proving the expanded chain model works without it.

**Pre-mortem:** The chain expands rapidly, burning investor cash. Distant customers reject the high price point in favor of familiar, cheap coffee. The heavy fixed costs of the roaster become a anchor, dragging the profitable local bars into bankruptcy within two years.

**Blockers:**
*   **Market Mismatch:** The proposal admits the product is a niche with a high price barrier in a mass-market, low-price country. No data proves this niche is large enough to support multi-city expansion.
*   **Capital Risk:** Raising heavy investor capital for an unproven geographic expansion creates a fatal cash-flow mismatch if adoption lags.
*   **Missing Edge:** No unique asset (distribution, cost advantage, proprietary tech) guarantees winning against incumbents in new cities.

**Verdict:** The plan relies on unproven demand in new markets while taking on maximum fixed costs (roaster + new leases) and financial leverage. It bets the existing local success scales nationally without a pilot. The risk of ruin outweighs the potential upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
