**Steelman:** Pivot from a failing game to a proven internal tool to capture enterprise value before capital runs out, leveraging existing tech rather than burning cash on a dead product.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Fit:** The tool solves *game dev* coordination, not general business workflows. Enterprise buyers (2013) demand security compliance (SOC2), Active Directory integration, and SLAs the team cannot currently promise. "Searchable history" is table stakes, not a differentiator against entrenched email or nascent competitors like HipChat.
2.  **Capability Gap:** The team has zero B2B sales, support, or compliance experience. Building the product is easy; selling it to risk-averse IT departments is a completely different business they have never practiced.
3.  **Strategic Contradiction:** Investors backed a game studio. Pivoting to B2B SaaS changes the risk profile entirely without their explicit buy-in. Using remaining venture money for an unapproved strategy risks legal action or immediate funding cutoff.
4.  **Morale & Execution:** Laying off most staff removes the very engineers needed to harden the tool for enterprise grade. A demoralized, skeletal team cannot execute a complex pivot while hunting for new customers.

**Better Way:**
*   **Do Nothing (Status Quo):** Continue burning cash on the game. Verdict: Fatal, as profitability is unreachable.
*   **Acquisition:** Sell the internal tool's IP to an existing player (e.g., Atlassian, Microsoft) rather than building a company around it. This monetizes the asset without requiring a sales org or compliance stack.
*   **Staged Pivot:** Secure investor approval and a bridge round *specifically* for the pivot before laying anyone off. Validate demand with LOIs from three non-game businesses before closing the game studio.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   **Blocker:** No explicit written approval from investors to change the company mandate from "games" to "enterprise software." Without this, the proposal is unauthorized use of funds.
*   **Edge:** NONE. The proposal cites no unique asset rivals lack, only an internal tool that likely lacks enterprise features. Being first is not claimed; being faster is irrelevant without distribution.

**Verdict:** The proposal fails the authorization test and assumes market fit without evidence. It attempts to start a new company with the wrong team structure and no capital approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
