The claim is that a 550-seat double-decker will succeed despite hub congestion and a market shift toward point-to-point twin-jets. The steelman: only a hub-and-spoke model requiring massive capacity between saturated major airports justifies this asset, assuming airlines cannot solve congestion via frequency or secondary airports.

**Skeptic:** The proposal admits the market trend is moving *away* from very large aircraft toward smaller, flexible twins. The "congestion" argument cuts both ways: if gates are full, airports cannot easily modify infrastructure for a plane requiring special gates, creating a chicken-and-egg adoption barrier. The $10.7B budget is a sunk cost before a single unit flies; any overrun threatens the maker's solvency. The rival's ability to stretch an existing jumbo offers a cheaper, lower-risk alternative for the same niche.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "stretch existing" options preserve capital. Investing in efficiency upgrades for current mid-size fleets addresses the point-to-point trend directly. A smaller, longer-range twin-engine jet captures the growing market segment without requiring airport reconstruction.

**Kill Criteria:** If the market for 500+ seaters is smaller than projected (plausible given the trend), the unit economics collapse. If even two launch customers defer due to infrastructure costs, the program loses momentum. The pre-mortem is clear: the plane becomes a niche luxury for a few hubs while the rival dominates the high-volume point-to-point routes with lower operating costs.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first to 550 seats is not an edge if the demand isn't there. "Strategic necessity" against a rival's stretch is fear-based, not value-based. The proposal relies on the assumption that hub congestion forces size increases, ignoring that congestion often forces frequency decreases or route shifts. The risks listed (small market, infrastructure hurdles, rival response) are not mitigated; they are merely acknowledged. Without a verified commitment that airports *will* upgrade gates, the plane cannot operate where needed.

Blockers: The trend toward point-to-point flying directly contradicts the hub-centric volume requirement. Infrastructure modification is a prerequisite, not a post-launch fix.
Risks: Massive budget overrun, limited market size, rival flexibility.
Edge: None identified beyond being larger.

DECISION: NO-GO
