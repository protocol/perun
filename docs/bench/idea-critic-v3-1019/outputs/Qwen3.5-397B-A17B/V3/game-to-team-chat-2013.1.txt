**Steelman:** Pivoting from a failing game to a commercialized internal chat tool leverages existing assets to capture enterprise demand before market saturation, utilizing remaining venture capital for a focused B2B launch.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces immediate blockers. First, **product-market fit is unproven**; an internal tool built for a small game studio lacks the security, compliance, and scalability required by enterprises. Second, **team capability mismatch** is critical: game developers rarely possess the sales cycles, support infrastructure, or domain knowledge for B2B software. Third, **competitive landscape**: by 2013, established players (and emerging giants) already dominate this space; entering without a distinct advantage is suicidal.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing/Shutdown:** Preserve remaining capital for investors rather than burning it on a high-risk pivot with no validated demand.
2.  **Sell Assets:** License the technology or acquire a small B2B team instead of rebuilding culture and skill sets from scratch.
3.  **Pivot (Proposed):** Requires hiring expensive enterprise sales/engineering talent immediately, depleting the "leftover" money before revenue materializes.

**Pre-mortem:**
Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: The team builds features gamers understand, not what CIOs buy. Morale collapses post-layoffs, key engineers leave for stable studios, and the burn rate outpaces enterprise sales cycles. The "internal tool" proves too brittle for external multi-tenant use, requiring a rewrite that exhausts funds.

**Edge Assessment:**
The proposal claims no specific edge. "We built it for ourselves" is not a competitive advantage in enterprise software; it is a limitation. Being first is irrelevant if the product doesn't meet enterprise standards. Fear of wasting the remaining money is not an edge; it is a sunk-cost fallacy driving a bad bet.

**Verdict:**
This is a classic "solution looking for a problem" scenario. The team lacks the core competencies (enterprise sales, security, compliance) and the product lacks validation outside a narrow internal use case. The risks (morale, skill gap, competition) are certainties, not possibilities. The remaining capital is better returned to investors or used to wind down gracefully than gambled on a pivot with no defined route to winning against incumbents.

DECISION: NO-GO
