**Steelman:** Shifting to subscription-only aligns revenue with continuous value delivery, eliminates version fragmentation, and creates predictable recurring revenue essential for long-term sustainability, despite short-term friction.

**Analysis:**
The proposal faces immediate, verified blockers based solely on the provided facts. First, the market reaction is not speculative; customers "object loudly," indicating active resistance that threatens immediate churn. Second, the value proposition fundamentally degrades for a specific segment: users "lose access" upon payment cessation, whereas the perpetual model offered permanent utility. This creates a hard barrier for cost-sensitive users or those with intermittent needs, directly fueling the risk of migration to "cheaper rivals."

Third, the financial trajectory is explicitly negative in the near term ("revenue will dip"). Without a disclosed cash reserve, financing strategy, or projected timeline to recover this dip, the plan fails the solvency check. A strategy that guarantees revenue loss while simultaneously driving customers to competitors and piracy lacks a viable bridge to its promised upside.

**Alternatives:**
The "do nothing" option (maintaining perpetual + optional subscription) preserves the existing revenue base while allowing organic migration, avoiding the forced churn and revenue cliff. A hybrid model could capture recurring revenue without the binary "access vs. none" ultimatum that drives users to piracy.

**Edge Assessment:**
The proposal claims no unique asset (e.g., unmatched features, locked-in ecosystem) that forces users to accept these terms despite the availability of rivals and pirated copies. "Continuous updates" are a standard industry expectation, not a defensible moat against the loss of ownership. The plan relies on coercion rather than superior value, which is unsustainable when alternatives exist.

**Verdict Logic:**
The combination of guaranteed revenue decline, explicit customer exodus to competitors, and the removal of product access creates a fatal flaw. The plan assumes customers will capitulate despite having viable exit routes. Without a mechanism to mitigate the revenue dip or prevent the loss of access (e.g., a read-only mode), the proposal is self-sabotaging.

Blockers: Guaranteed revenue dip with no recovery plan; active customer migration to rivals due to loss of access.
Edge: NONE.

DECISION: NO-GO
