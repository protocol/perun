The claim is that a social media giant can successfully launch a global, basket-backed digital currency in 2019 via an association model. The steelman: leveraging massive distribution and a multi-asset reserve creates a stable, inclusive payment rail that bypasses traditional banking friction.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The primary obstacle is not technical execution or partner alignment, but the immediate, existential reaction from sovereign states. A private entity issuing a global currency backed by government securities is functionally a shadow central bank. In the current climate of antitrust scrutiny and privacy scandals, regulators in the US and EU will not view this as a benign utility but as a direct threat to monetary sovereignty and financial stability.

The "association" governance structure does not mitigate this; it may exacerbate antitrust concerns by formalizing a cartel of large corporations controlling money supply. The risk of partners withdrawing under regulatory pressure is not a mere possibility but a near-certainty given the stated political headwinds. Unlike a standard product launch, this cannot be "moved fast and broken"; a regulatory cease-and-desist order would freeze the entire reserve and destroy user trust instantly.

The proposal lacks a viable path to regulatory approval in major markets before launch. Waiting for clarity or piloting in permissive jurisdictions first would fundamentally alter the "global" nature of the initial launch, reducing the immediate upside while retaining the reputational blast radius. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a forced shutdown after mobilizing billions of users. There is no edge here that outweighs the unified opposition of global financial regulators; being first or having distribution is irrelevant if the legal gate is slammed shut.

This is a one-way door with a high probability of catastrophic failure due to external veto points that the proposer cannot control or negotiate away in the current political environment. The plan assumes a cooperative regulatory stance that contradicts the stated facts of ongoing scrutiny.

DECISION: NO-GO
