**Steelman:** Bridge the 32-bit gap immediately to retain users and developers without delaying the true next-gen hardware.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cannibalization & Confusion:** The add-on ($160) competes directly with the upcoming standalone 32-bit console. Consumers facing a $160 stopgap versus a full new system in 12 months will likely delay all spending, crushing add-on sales and starving the new console's launch momentum.
2.  **Developer Fragmentation:** Forcing developers to support three tiers (16-bit, Add-on, New 32-bit) dilutes resources. With a rushed holiday library, the add-on launches with weak software, reinforcing the "wait for the real thing" narrative.
3.  **Brand Damage:** Releasing a rushed, underpowered bridge product signals desperation and technical confusion, eroding trust before the flagship launch.
4.  **No True Edge:** The proposal admits the schedule is "very tight" and the library "small." There is no asset here that guarantees success; the "edge" is merely speed to a compromised market.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, the add-on has sold poorly due to consumer hesitation. Developers ignored the small install base to focus on the upcoming native 32-bit hardware. The brand is associated with a confusing, expensive upgrade path rather than innovation. The core 16-bit sales dip as users freeze purchases.

**Verdict Logic:**
*   **Blocker:** The proposal explicitly states buyers may wait for the new console. If the target audience refuses the intermediate step, the business case collapses. The risk of cannibalizing the upcoming flagship's hype outweighs the marginal gain of a rushed holiday season.
*   **Edge:** NONE. Being first with a compromised, expensive adapter is not an advantage over a clean, scheduled next-gen launch.
*   **Alternative:** Focus marketing on the current 16-bit library's maturity and build hype for the definitive 32-bit experience coming next year.

The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test. Doing nothing preserves brand clarity and developer focus for the true generation leap.

DECISION: NO-GO
