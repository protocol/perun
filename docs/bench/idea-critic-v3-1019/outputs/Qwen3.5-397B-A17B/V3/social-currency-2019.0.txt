**Steelman:** A consortium-backed global stablecoin integrated into a ubiquitous messaging platform could drastically reduce remittance costs and bank the unbanked, leveraging existing network effects for immediate scale.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "fit" and "blocker" tests. The entity faces active antitrust and privacy scrutiny in its primary markets (US/EU). Introducing a currency basket governed by an association of partners does not neutralize the perception of monetary sovereignty threat; it amplifies it. Regulators view this not as a utility but as a geopolitical challenge from an untrusted actor. The "partners" are fragile; at the first sign of regulatory hostility, they will withdraw to protect their own licenses, collapsing the reserve backing.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids immediate existential regulatory confrontation.
2.  **Domestic-First Pilot:** Launch in a single, friendly jurisdiction with limited scope to prove compliance before global ambition.
3.  **Infrastructure Only:** Provide the tech stack for banks to issue their own currencies, removing the sovereignty threat.

**Pre-mortem:** The project launches, the US Treasury and EU Commission issue joint cease-and-desist orders citing systemic risk and AML gaps. Partners flee. The company faces broken antitrust promises, leading to massive fines and a forced breakup. The currency never circulates.

**Evidence & Logic:** The proposal admits regulators see this as a "threat to monetary sovereignty." No amount of association governance mitigates a sovereign state's defense of its currency, especially when the proposer is under active investigation for privacy and trust. The "edge" is distribution, but distribution is useless if the product is illegal. The risks listed (regulatory threat, partner withdrawal) are not mere possibilities; they are near-certainties given the current political climate described.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Sovereignty Conflict:** The core value proposition (global scale) triggers the primary blocker (regulatory ban).
2.  **Trust Deficit:** Privacy/antitrust scandals make the entity unfit to govern a financial public good in the eyes of necessary regulators.
3.  **Partner Instability:** The governance model relies on entities with no incentive to fight a regulatory war for this specific project.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first or large does not override sovereign monetary policy.

**Decision:** The plan attempts to solve a technical problem (payments) while ignoring the fatal political constraint (sovereignty + mistrust). It cannot succeed as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
