**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's "risks" are execution hurdles, not factual contradictions. The financial constraint is severe, but the plan addresses it by targeting a high-margin consumer segment with a simplified bill of materials (removing legacy ports/drives reduces cost).

**Risks:** High. Market education is required for USB and disk-less operation. Early adopter friction is guaranteed if peripheral compatibility fails. However, these are market risks inherent to innovation, not flaws in the logic of the proposal itself. The "do nothing" alternative guarantees continued share loss and financial bleed.

**Edge:** DIFFERENTIATION. In a sea of beige boxes with identical legacy ports, a translucent, colorful design creates immediate visual distinction. Removing the diskette drive and legacy ports forces a clean break, lowering manufacturing complexity and cost while signaling a future-focused product. This specific combination (design + port elimination) is an asset rivals clinging to compatibility cannot easily copy without cannibalizing their own legacy lines.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because the company ran out of cash before the brand perception shifted, or because the supply chain for USB peripherals lagged too far behind launch. The kill criterion must be strict: if pre-orders or first-quarter sales do not cover the tooling costs within 90 days, the line is cut.

**Decision Logic:** The status quo is death. The proposal offers a singular, defensible path to reverse perception and reduce costs simultaneously. The risks are real but manageable compared to the certainty of decline. The edge is clear and unique.

DECISION: GO
