**Steelman:** Launching a $199 console with built-in online play one year before the market leader captures the "first-mover" advantage in networked gaming and leverages price sensitivity to rebuild retailer trust despite financial fragility.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts provided in the prompt:
1.  **Financial Blocker:** The company is in "financial trouble" and has "little room for selling hardware at a loss." A $199 price point in 1998 for a modem-equipped console almost certainly requires a loss-leading strategy to gain share. Without capital to absorb these losses, the launch will accelerate bankruptcy rather than prevent it.
2.  **Content Blocker:** "Several big third-party publishers are not committing games." A console without major titles cannot compete, regardless of price or online features. The risk of a barren library is a current fact, not a future possibility.
3.  **Market Timing Risk:** While being first is an advantage, the leader's imminent announcement (one year out) creates a "wait-and-see" dynamic. If the hardware is underpowered or lacks games (as indicated), early adoption will be minimal, leaving the company exposed when the leader arrives.

**Edge Assessment:**
The only potential edge is the built-in modem and lower price. However, "being first" is not an edge if the product is financially unsustainable and lacks software. The proposal explicitly states the constraints that make execution impossible: no money for loss-leading and no games from key partners. These are not risks to be managed; they are fatal flaws in the current plan. The pre-mortem is clear: the company runs out of cash before achieving critical mass, damaging retailer relations further when units sit unsold.

**Kill Criteria:**
The plan lacks a mechanism to secure publisher commitments or financing before launch. Without a confirmed path to profitability at $199 or secured AAA titles, the venture collapses.

**Verdict:**
The proposal asks to execute a capital-intensive launch without the necessary capital or content. It violates the constraint of financial survival.

DECISION: NO-GO
