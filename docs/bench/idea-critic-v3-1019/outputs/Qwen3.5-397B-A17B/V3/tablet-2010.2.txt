**Steelman:** A 10-inch device fills the gap between phones and laptops by leveraging an existing app ecosystem at a premium but accessible price point, avoiding the failures of previous complex or under-powered tablets.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **"Just a big phone":** Valid criticism if the OS isn't adapted for larger screens. However, the proposal implies a new form factor, not a scaled UI. The risk is execution, not concept.
2.  **No camera/multitasking:** These are cost and complexity constraints, not fatal flaws. Early adopters prioritize content consumption (video, web, books) over production or communication features initially. Removing them lowers the price to the critical $499 threshold.
3.  **Past failures:** Previous tablets failed due to high price, poor battery life, clunky PC OSes, or lack of apps. This proposal directly addresses price (sub-$500), ecosystem (existing phone apps), and simplicity (mobile OS). The "cheap laptop" alternative runs desktop OSes unsuited for casual, instant-on media consumption.
4.  **Market Fit:** The "do nothing" option cedes the living room and casual computing space to laptops and phones. The "better way" of waiting for tech to mature risks losing first-mover advantage in defining the category.

**Pre-mortem:** The device fails if the app experience feels stretched or if consumers refuse to pay $499 for a device that can't replace a laptop. However, the "edge" is the immediate availability of thousands of optimized mobile apps and a price point significantly lower than historical tablet failures or comparable laptops.

**Blockers:** None found in the text. The limitations (no camera, no multitasking) are strategic trade-offs to hit the price and battery targets, not oversights.
**Risks:** Consumer confusion on use case; app scaling issues.
**Edge:** Existing massive app store + optimized mobile OS + aggressive pricing = a unique value proposition distinct from both phones and laptops.

The proposal survives the attack by turning previous tablet weaknesses (price, OS, apps) into strengths through strategic constraint.

DECISION: GO
