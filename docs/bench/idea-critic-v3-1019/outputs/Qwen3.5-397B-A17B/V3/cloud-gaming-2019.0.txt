The claim is that a non-gaming infrastructure giant can successfully launch a premium cloud gaming service with proprietary studios in 2019. The steelman posits that global data center proximity solves latency better than competitors, and brand trust overcomes the "product graveyard" reputation.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test on economics and user value. The model demands users pay a monthly subscription *plus* full price for games they likely already own on other platforms. This double-dip strategy offers no cost advantage over buying a console, which provides superior latency and ownership without recurring fees. The "edge" of data centers is neutralized by the "last mile" reality: streaming quality depends on the user's home internet, which the company cannot control, directly validating the raised risk of input lag ruining gameplay.

Furthermore, the lack of exclusive content is a fatal flaw. Publishers are incentivized to keep exclusives on established consoles where margins are proven, not experiment on an unproven streaming platform from a company known for killing products. The pre-mortem is clear: the service launches with a thin library, high effective cost, and lag complaints, leading to low retention and eventual shutdown, reinforcing the negative reputation.

The alternative of "do nothing" or licensing technology to existing gamers is safer. Building studios is a high-cost, slow-yield endeavor that contradicts the need for immediate content depth. There is no verified demand for renting games at full price while paying a subscription. The plan relies on changing entrenched consumer behavior without a compelling price or quality incentive.

Blockers: The economic model (subscription + full game price) is inferior to console ownership; latency relies on user ISP, not company infrastructure; no exclusive content pipeline exists.
Risks: Brand distrust leads to low adoption; publisher apathy starves the library.
Edge: NONE. Data center proximity does not overcome last-mile latency or content deficits.

DECISION: NO-GO
