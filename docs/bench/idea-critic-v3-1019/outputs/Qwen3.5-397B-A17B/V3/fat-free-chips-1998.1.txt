**Steelman:** Launching a nationally advertised, fat-free chip line leverages a unique, regulator-approved ingredient to capture the diet market before competitors, accepting higher costs and label warnings as barriers to entry.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "edge" test. The core asset is a fat substitute that causes digestive issues severe enough to mandate a warning label. In the food industry, a mandatory warning on the front of a package regarding digestive effects is not a minor friction point; it is a product disqualifier for the mass market. The "heavy advertising" budget will likely amplify awareness of the side effects rather than the taste benefit.

The risks are not hypothetical; they are baked into the regulatory approval. Consumer groups already oppose the ingredient, providing ready-made negative press. Furthermore, the product costs more than regular chips while offering a potentially unpleasant physical experience. The "diet" angle is fragile; if the trend shifts from low-fat to low-carb or natural ingredients (a plausible near-future shift), the product becomes obsolete instantly.

There is no "cheaper, safer" version of this specific plan because the flaw is intrinsic to the ingredient's mechanism of action (passing through undigested). A "do nothing" or "limited regional test" approach is superior. A national launch with heavy ad spend exposes the company to massive sunk costs if the warning label triggers consumer avoidance. The proposal assumes consumers will ignore a government-mandated health warning in exchange for "fat-free" status, a dangerous assumption given the history of fat substitutes (e.g., Olestra) which eventually faced market rejection due to exactly these gastrointestinal issues.

The plan lacks a credible path to overcoming the label stigma. Without a proven consumer desire to tolerate digestive side effects, the national launch is a high-cost gamble with a low probability of sustained adoption. The "edge" is actually a liability.

**Verdict:**
Blocker: The mandatory warning label regarding digestive effects creates a fundamental barrier to mass-market adoption that heavy advertising cannot overcome and may worsen.
Risk: High cost of goods sold combined with potential consumer backlash and shifting diet trends.
Edge: NONE. Being first with a problematic ingredient is not an advantage.

DECISION: NO-GO
