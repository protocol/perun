**Steelman:** Acquiring the roaster secures supply control and brand heritage, allowing the operator to scale a premium espresso concept beyond local limits by leveraging an existing multi-store footprint.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Remain a small operator; risk being outpaced by emerging national chains.
2.  **Organic Growth:** Open new stores slowly without acquisition; avoids debt but loses immediate brand equity and roasting capacity.
3.  **Acquisition (Proposed):** High capital intensity and investor dilution, but offers immediate vertical integration and a platform for rapid geographic expansion.

**Fit:** Assumes a shifting market where consumers will trade up from cheap drip coffee to premium espresso. The "niche" status is the opportunity, not the blocker, provided the brand can educate consumers.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the capital raised is insufficient to fund the cash-burn of distant expansion before profitability, or if the regional brand fails to resonate in new cities, leaving the entity over-leveraged with unsold inventory. This is a **one-way door**; once capital is raised and brands merged, reversal implies bankruptcy or fire-sale.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal admits the product is a niche and costs significantly more than the standard. However, no evidence suggests the target demographic in *other cities* exists or wants this specific Italian brand. The "edge" is the operator's experience, but size and being first are not edges. The reliance on external investors for a speculative expansion into unknown markets without a proven multi-city pilot is a structural flaw. The plan lacks a capped, time-boxed first step; it demands full commitment immediately.

**Kill Criteria:** Without a verified demand signal outside the current local area, the risk of capital misallocation is absolute. The proposal offers no "go-if" condition to test new markets before full acquisition and renaming.

**Verdict:** The plan conflates local success with national viability. The financial risk (heavy capital, investor reliance) outweighs the unproven upside in distant cities. The lack of a staged approach creates a fatal exposure.

DECISION: NO-GO
