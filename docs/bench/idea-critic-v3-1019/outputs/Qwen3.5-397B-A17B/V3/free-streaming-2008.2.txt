**Claim:** Launch a freemium, ad-supported desktop music streaming service in Europe (2008) by licensing major labels via upfront cash and equity.

**Steelman:** Only a legitimate, instant, free alternative can displace piracy; equity alignment secures catalog access while deferring some cash risk, creating a defensible moat against illegal competitors.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unit Economics Failure:** In 2008, mobile ad rates are insufficient to cover per-stream royalty costs implied by "almost any song." If labels demand large minimum guarantees regardless of usage, the burn rate will exceed ad revenue before user scale is achieved.
2.  **Substitution Gap:** Piracy is not just about cost; it is about ownership (MP3 files) and zero friction. A streaming app requiring installation, internet connectivity, and ads offers a worse user experience than "free" piracy for the majority of the market.
3.  **Capital Misallocation:** Paying upfront cash to entities with falling sales transfers scarce startup capital to incumbents without guaranteeing user conversion. If "most users never pay," the paid tier cannot subsidize the free tier's licensing costs.
4.  **No Edge:** The proposal relies on "legitimacy" as the differentiator. However, history shows users rarely pay a premium (in attention or money) for legality alone when a superior, free illegal alternative exists. There is no stated technical advantage or exclusive content that piracy cannot match.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Contradiction:** The model requires high licensing costs (upfront + royalties) but admits low ad revenue and low conversion. The math does not close without massive, unproven scale.
*   **Missing Edge:** No asset exists to beat piracy on convenience or cost; legitimacy is not a sufficient edge in 2008.

**Risks:** Rapid cash depletion due to minimum guarantees; failure to convert free users; labels reneging if sales don't rebound immediately.

**Verdict:** The proposal bets on changing user behavior via legality alone while ignoring the immediate negative cash flow per user. Without a proven mechanism to make ad revenue exceed royalty costs, or a unique feature piracy cannot replicate, this is a capital destruction strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
