The claim is that a national launch of fat-free chips using an undigested fat substitute will succeed despite known deterrents. The steelman: regulatory approval and unique technology create a first-mover advantage in the growing diet market, outweighing label concerns.

However, the blocker test fails immediately on evidence already present in the proposal. The "edge" relies on consumer demand for fat reduction, yet the proposal admits three fatal contradictions: the required label explicitly warns of digestive effects (directly undermining the health promise), consumer groups are actively opposing the ingredient (creating reputational headwinds), and the product costs more than regular chips (removing price sensitivity as a lever). Furthermore, the market context is shifting away from the core value proposition ("dieters' attention may shift from fat").

A pre-mortem reveals the most likely failure mode: consumers try the product once, experience the advertised digestive issues, and the brand becomes synonymous with unpleasant side effects, killing repeat purchase rates essential for CPG success. The "do nothing" or "wait" alternative is superior because it avoids sinking capital into a product where the primary differentiator (fat-free) is becoming less relevant while the primary liability (digestive side effects) is mandated by law to be highlighted on the pack.

The proposal offers no mitigation for the label warning, no strategy to counter activist opposition, and no explanation for why consumers would pay a premium for a product with known gastrointestinal risks. The risks are not hypothetical; they are stated facts within the proposal itself. The label requirement is a confirmed negative that removes the route to upside (health-conscious mass adoption) because the mechanism of action (undigested fat) causes the very effect the label warns against. This is not a risk to be managed; it is a structural flaw in the product-market fit for a national mass-market launch.

There is no named edge that survives these stated facts. Being first with a product consumers are warned against and activists oppose is not an advantage.

Blockers: Stated facts show target users face mandated warnings of negative effects, active opposition, and higher prices with no clear compensating benefit as trends shift.
Risks: Low repeat purchase due to side effects; brand damage from activist campaigns.
Edge: NONE.

DECISION: NO-GO
