**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant UK/Ireland teen network for $850M instantly secures a demographic gateway, leveraging instant-messaging integration to lock in users before a larger rival consolidates the market.

**Attack:** The price represents a massive premium over current small revenues, betting entirely on future monetization that has never been proven at this scale. The "larger rival" growing fast suggests the target may already be losing the network-effect war; buying the #2 player in a winner-take-all market is often a value trap. Furthermore, the acquirer's history of shifting strategies creates execution risk: integrating a distinct social culture into a portal legacy often kills the very engagement being purchased. Social networks are fragile; the moment acquisition news breaks, user churn can accelerate if the community senses "corporate" changes, rendering the asset worthless before integration completes. The cash outlay removes flexibility to pivot if the teen migration trend shifts (as it did historically with platform changes).

**Better Way:** A minority stake or partnership to test integration without the $850M sunk cost and cultural shock. Alternatively, build organic growth tools within the existing IM service rather than buying a potentially declining asset.

**Kill Criteria:** If user growth on the target network is not >20% year-over-year excluding paid marketing, or if the larger rival's growth rate exceeds the target's by >2x, the acquisition thesis fails.

**Edge:** NONE. Being a large portal is not an edge in social networking; it is often a liability. The proposal relies on "strategic necessity" and fear of the rival rather than a demonstrated unique advantage in retaining teen users. The only clear fact is the high cost and the volatility of the asset.

**Verdict:** The combination of an unproven revenue model at this price point, a shifting acquirer strategy, and the high probability of cultural/technical integration failure constitutes a blocker. The risk of buying a peaking asset while a stronger rival grows is too high for a full cash buyout.

DECISION: NO-GO
