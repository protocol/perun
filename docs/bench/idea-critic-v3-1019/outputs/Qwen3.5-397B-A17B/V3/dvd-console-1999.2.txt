**Steelman:** Bundling DVD playback and backward compatibility creates a superior value proposition that justifies a loss-leading price to capture market share, betting on volume and software royalties to offset hardware losses.

**Attack:** The proposal explicitly admits the unit sells at a loss, supply is constrained by chip yields, and a rival has a one-year head start. Entering a price war with inferior margins, limited inventory, and a delayed launch against an entrenched competitor is financially suicidal. The "edge" relies entirely on future software sales from a user base you cannot physically build due to supply constraints. If chip yields limit supply, you cannot achieve the volume required to make the loss-leading strategy work. Furthermore, difficult architecture risks alienating developers precisely when exclusive content is needed to overcome the rival's time advantage.

**Better Way:** Delay launch until yields improve to ensure supply meets demand, or remove the DVD drive to hit a profitable price point and earlier release date. Do not launch a loss-leading product you cannot manufacture in sufficient quantities.

**Kill Criteria:**
1.  **Blocker:** Hardware BOM cost exceeds retail price *and* chip yields are unproven. This creates a scenario where every unit sold deepens losses while supply shortages prevent market penetration.
2.  **Risk:** Developer friction combined with a rival's one-year head start could result in a "dead on arrival" software library.
3.  **Edge:** None identified. Being first with DVD is irrelevant if units are unavailable; backward compatibility is weak if the rival has a year of exclusive new titles.

**Decision Logic:** The plan fails the blocker test. Selling a product at a loss is only viable if you can scale volume immediately to recoup costs via ecosystem lock-in. The proposal admits supply constraints (chip yields) that make scaling impossible at launch. You would be losing money on every unit while failing to capture significant market share due to inventory shortages, allowing the rival to solidify dominance. This is a guaranteed cash burn with no immediate path to recovery.

DECISION: NO-GO
