**Steelman:** Acquiring the US portal instantly grants massive scale, bypassing years of organic growth to challenge English-language dominance, leveraging the telecom's capital to create a global media powerhouse.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the fundamental test of value creation. The target ranks behind rivals in traffic, meaning the acquirer is paying a premium for a declining asset, not a market leader. Paying $12.5 billion—a massive multiple of revenue—for a secondary player destroys shareholder value immediately. The logic relies on the "bigger is better" fallacy without a credible mechanism to reverse the target's competitive decay.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" option preserves $12.5 billion in equity. Alternatively, organic investment or smaller, targeted acquisitions of emerging technologies offer higher upside with negligible integration risk compared to a cross-continental merger of two struggling giants.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume it is 2002. The integration has stalled due to cultural clashes between Spanish telecom management and US web engineers. The portal's traffic has continued to bleed to faster, more innovative rivals. The stock price has collapsed under the weight of goodwill write-downs, as the anticipated synergies proved illusory. The company is now distracted, over-leveraged, and irrelevant in the US market.

**Fit & Edge:** This moves a "global presence" north star but ignores the "profitable growth" constraint. The proposal cites no unique edge; size, money, and fear of falling behind are not edges. The acquirer has no proven ability to operate US consumer web products, and the target has no defensible moat.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Valuation Mismatch:** Paying a high multiple for an asset with inferior traffic and no growth trajectory is a mathematical destroyer of value.
2. **No Strategic Edge:** The plan offers no specific asset or capability that rivals lack; it merely combines two weak positions.

**Risks:** Integration failure, culture clash, stock volatility, continued traffic erosion.

**Edge:** NONE. The proposal relies on financial engineering rather than product superiority or operational excellence.

**Kill Criteria:** If the combined entity does not achieve top-3 US traffic within 18 months, divest immediately.

**Decision:** The lack of a competitive edge and the destructive valuation make this an immediate failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
