**Steelman:** Acquiring the roaster secures supply chain control and brand heritage, allowing the operator to scale a premium espresso concept beyond local limits before competitors saturate the market.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the mismatch between the proposed "heavy capital" expansion into distant cities and the unproven demand for high-priced espresso in a drip-coffee dominant culture. Expanding geographically before validating the model in non-local markets risks exhausting investor capital on real estate and logistics for a niche product.

*Alternative 1 (Do Nothing):* Maintain current small bars; zero financial risk, but capped growth.
*Alternative 2 (Cheaper/Safer):* Acquire the roaster to secure branding and supply, but restrict expansion to the current region or adjacent neighborhoods only. This tests the "premium espresso" thesis without the massive overhead of distant city entry.

**Pre-mortem:** The venture fails because the operator raises funds based on the assumption that espresso will translate nationally like drip coffee. Investors demand rapid store count growth to justify valuation, forcing openings in cities where customers reject the $3+ price point. Cash flow turns negative within 18 months; the roaster asset cannot cover the debt service of failed retail leases.

**Edge Analysis:** The operator has specific experience with *small* Italian-style bars, not multi-city retail management or national brand building. There is no evidence provided that this specific team possesses a distribution advantage, unique cost structure, or existing demand in target cities. "Being first" is not an edge if the market isn't ready; "strategic necessity" is not an edge without a proven unit economics model at scale.

**Verdict:**
Blockers: The plan relies on unproven national demand for a niche, high-cost product to justify the heavy capital required for distant expansion. The proposal lacks a pilot step to validate this demand outside the current locale before committing to a multi-city rollout.
Risks: Capital exhaustion, cultural rejection of price point, operational overreach.
Edge: NONE (Past success is local; no stated advantage for national scaling).

The proposal attempts a high-risk, one-way door (national expansion) without a validated two-way door (regional pilot). It fails the "cheaper alternative" test: buying the brand is sound, but the immediate multi-city expansion is not.

DECISION: NO-GO
