The claim is that a 10-inch tablet running an existing phone OS at $499 will succeed where previous tablets failed. The steelman: this device is not a "big phone" but a distinct third category for media consumption and light productivity, leveraging a mature app ecosystem to solve the "no apps" failure of prior tablets.

**Skeptic:** The proposal admits critical deficits: no camera and no multitasking. In 2010, competitors are rapidly adding cameras to tablets. Multitasking is a core expectation for a device priced between a phone and a laptop. Earlier tablets failed due to poor software and awkward form factors; while the app store solves the software gap, the hardware limitations (no camera) and OS constraints (no multitasking) validate the critic's fear that this is merely an oversized phone with missing features. The price ($499) is high for a device lacking standard features found in cheaper netbooks.

**Better-way:** The "do nothing" option avoids the sunk cost of a potential flop in a historically dead category. A cheaper alternative exists: wait for component costs to drop or add the missing features (camera, multitasking) before launch. A smaller, cheaper device might reduce risk, but the proposal argues for a specific 10-inch premium niche. However, launching with known deficits against established netbooks and improving competitors is risky.

**Kill-criteria:** If the device cannot run multiple apps or take photos, it fails the "laptop replacement" and "premium media device" positioning. The risk of being perceived as "just a big phone" is high without unique capabilities. The "edge" is the app ecosystem, but if the hardware/OS limits prevent using those apps effectively (e.g., side-by-side usage), the edge is neutralized.

**Decision:** The proposal launches with known, significant feature gaps (no camera, no multitasking) in a competitive market where these are standard or expected. The "edge" of the app store is undermined by the inability to use them fully on a larger screen. The risks are not theoretical; they are built into the initial spec. A launch in this state invites immediate criticism and comparison to more capable netbooks or future competitor tablets.

DECISION: NO-GO
