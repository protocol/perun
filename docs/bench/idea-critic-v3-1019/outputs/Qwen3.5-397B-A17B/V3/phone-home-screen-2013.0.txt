**Steelman:** A dedicated launcher and partner device could deepen mobile engagement by surfacing social content immediately, bypassing the need for a full OS while leveraging existing hardware partnerships.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "edge" test. It relies on the assumption that users want a social network to dominate their primary interface, yet the stated risks explicitly note users may resist this takeover and privacy critics are wary. Historically, attempts by single-app giants to replace general-purpose home screens have failed because users value app diversity and neutrality over walled-garden immersion.

The "partner phone" constraint severely limits reach to a "handful of models," creating a fragmented experience that contradicts the goal of capturing "more of people's time" at scale. Pushing other apps out of view creates immediate friction, inviting users to revert to the stock launcher. This is a high-effort, low-reversibility move (building hardware partnerships and maintaining a launcher) with a weak value proposition: it solves a problem users haven't complained about (accessing the app) by creating new problems (privacy concerns, reduced utility).

The "edge" is nonexistent; being first or having brand size does not overcome the fundamental mismatch between a specific social utility and a general-purpose device interface. The risks are not merely theoretical; they are structural flaws in the product-market fit for a home screen replacement. A cheaper, safer alternative exists: optimize the standalone app and widgets within the existing OS ecosystem, which respects user choice and reaches all devices immediately.

**Blockers:**
1.  **User Resistance:** The proposal admits users may not want the social network taking over the home screen; no evidence suggests otherwise.
2.  **Limited Scale:** Restricting availability to a "handful of phone models" prevents meaningful impact on total attention time.
3.  **No Competitive Edge:** Brand size is not an edge when the product forces a workflow users haven't requested.

**Risks:** Privacy backlash, rapid uninstall rates, partner dependency.
**Edge:** NONE.

DECISION: NO-GO
