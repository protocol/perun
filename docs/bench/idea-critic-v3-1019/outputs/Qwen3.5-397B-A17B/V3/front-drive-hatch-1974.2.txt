**Steelman:** The company must replace its declining, obsolete rear-engined platform with a modern, efficient front-wheel-drive hatchback to survive the oil crisis and shifting market demands, despite high retooling costs and cultural attachment to the legacy model.

**Analysis:**
The status quo is a death spiral. Sales of the core product are falling precisely when the market (driven by the oil crisis) demands the efficiency this proposal offers. Continuing to invest in the old rear-engined, air-cooled technology is a bet against macroeconomic reality. While retooling is expensive, doing nothing guarantees obsolescence as profits shrink further.

The "loyal buyer" risk is real but manageable; character cannot save a car that fails basic economic utility during a fuel shortage. The lack of experience with water-cooled, front-drive systems is a execution risk, not a strategic veto. Every manufacturer faced this learning curve; delaying entry only cedes ground to competitors who adapt faster. The external designer brings necessary objectivity to break internal stagnation.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because the company half-hearted the transition, trying to appease legacy fans while under-investing in the new platform's quality. However, the alternative—clinging to the old car until sales hit zero—is a guaranteed failure.

**Edge:** The company has a strong brand identity and a pressing need that aligns perfectly with the global shift toward efficiency. Being forced into innovation by crisis often yields the most focused, successful products. The "do nothing" option has no upside.

**Blockers:** NONE. The financial pain of retooling is unavoidable regardless of the path; it is better spent on the future than patching the past.
**Risks:** Execution complexity in new engineering; alienating a niche segment of purists. These are operational hurdles, not strategic stoppers.
**Kill Criteria:** If the new prototype cannot match competitor fuel efficiency within 18 months, pivot to licensing technology.

The proposal addresses the only viable path to survival. Hesitation is more costly than the retooling bill.

DECISION: GO
