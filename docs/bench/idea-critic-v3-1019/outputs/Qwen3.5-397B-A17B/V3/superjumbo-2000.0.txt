**Steelman:** A double-deck airliner solves hub congestion by moving more passengers per slot, leveraging a first-mover advantage in the 550-seat category before the rival responds.

**Attack:** The proposal assumes hub-and-spoke growth will outpace point-to-point trends. However, airlines are explicitly shifting to smaller twin-engine jets for direct routes. Relying on 50 orders from six customers is a fragile foundation if the broader market for 550-seat planes is niche. The $10.7 billion budget is massive; if airport infrastructure modifications (gates, taxiways) slow adoption, the asset becomes stranded. The rival stretching an existing jumbo is a cheaper, faster counter-move that negates the capacity advantage without new development risk.

**Better Way:** Do nothing or develop a smaller, efficient wide-body that fits current gates and serves both hub and point-to-point routes. This captures the efficiency trend without requiring billions in infrastructure changes from customers.

**Fit:** The strategy conflicts with the observed market shift toward point-to-point travel. A 550-seat jet is a bet on continued hub dominance, which the premise suggests is eroding.

**Pre-mortem:** The plane launches, but airports delay upgrades. Airlines cancel orders because they cannot deploy the aircraft on enough routes. The rival releases a stretched version of their existing jet at half the development cost, capturing the remaining high-capacity demand. The project bankrupts the division.

**Blockers:** NONE (The 50 firm orders provide immediate financial cover, technically allowing a "GO" on paper).
**Risks:** High dependency on airport infrastructure upgrades; market trend misalignment (point-to-point vs. hub); massive capital exposure ($10.7B); rival counter-product.
**Edge:** NONE. Being first to 550 seats is not an edge if the market wants 250 seats. "Strategic necessity" against a rival is not an edge.

**Decision Logic:** While firm orders exist, the strategic edge is nonexistent because the product fights the primary industry trend (point-to-point). The risk of stranded assets due to airport limitations and the rival's ability to counter cheaply makes this a catastrophic bet. The "edge" relies on a market behavior (hub growth) that is explicitly stated as declining.

DECISION: NO-GO
