**Steelman:** Replacing the home/lock screen with social feeds maximizes engagement on existing hardware by making social interaction the primary phone function, bypassing the need for a proprietary OS.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Value Mismatch:** Users treat the home screen as a launcher for *tasks* (maps, camera, utility), not a passive feed. Forcing a social stream here creates friction for non-social tasks, likely driving users to disable the overlay immediately.
2.  **Distribution Trap:** Limiting availability to "a handful of phone models" and a single partner device caps the addressable market to a fraction of the network's user base, preventing the network effects required to sustain the platform.
3.  **Privacy Backlash:** An "always-on" layer with deep system integration validates privacy critics' worst fears, risking regulatory scrutiny and brand damage that outweighs marginal engagement gains.

**Better Ways:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Focus on optimizing the existing mobile app, which respects user context and works on all devices.
*   **Widget Strategy:** Provide optional home screen widgets that show previews without hijacking the entire interface, offering value without hostility.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, adoption is <2% of the partner phone's users. The majority uninstall the launcher within 48 hours due to battery drain and inability to quickly access non-social apps. The partner phone fails to gain traction against competitors, leaving the project as a costly distraction.

**Edge:** NONE. Being "first" to hijack the home screen is not an advantage if the user experience is degraded. There is no unique asset here that overcomes the fundamental mismatch between a social feed and a device launcher. The proposal relies on the hope that users *will* change their behavior, rather than evidence that they *want* to.

**Kill Criteria:** If pilot users do not retain the launcher as their default after 7 days without financial incentive, the premise is false.

**Decision:** The proposal forces a high-friction experience on a limited audience while inviting significant privacy backlash. It solves a distribution problem by creating a usability problem.

DECISION: NO-GO
