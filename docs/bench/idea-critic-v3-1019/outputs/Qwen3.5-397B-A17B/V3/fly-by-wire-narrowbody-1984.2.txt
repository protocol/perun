**Steelman:** A consortium can disrupt a duopoly by leveraging digital fly-by-wire and commonality to offer superior economics, using state aid to bridge the profitability gap.

**Attack:** The core risk is not technical failure but market rejection of unproven automation. Pilots and regulators may refuse certification or operation, grounding the fleet before entry. Furthermore, the incumbent's ability to cut prices could erase the efficiency advantage of the new design. The consortium lacks the cash reserves to survive a price war or prolonged certification delay.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids catastrophic loss but cedes the high-volume narrow-body market forever, keeping the consortium dependent on wide-bodies.
2.  **Incremental Upgrade:** Modify an existing airframe with partial automation. Lower risk, but fails to achieve the step-change in operating costs needed to dislodge the incumbent.
3.  **Joint Venture:** Partner with the American maker. Dilutes control and brand, likely rejected by government stakeholders.

**Fit:** Aligns with the strategic necessity of capturing the highest-volume market segment to ensure long-term survival.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because regulators mandate dual-control backups, negating weight savings, while the incumbent slashes prices by 20%, making the new jet unprofitable for launch customers.

**Evidence:** Historical data shows civil aviation moves slowly on safety; no prior civil fly-by-wire exists. Government aid is confirmed but finite.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks are severe but known; the "never used before" status is the very edge required to leapfrog the incumbent's legacy designs. The lack of profitability is a structural feature of startups/consortia entering capital-intensive fields, solved here by the stated government aid.

**Edge:** First-mover advantage in full digital flight controls offers a definitive pilot training and maintenance cost reduction that legacy aluminum designs cannot match without total redesign.

**Kill Criteria:** If Type Certification is not granted within 48 months of launch, or if launch orders fall below 50 units due to pilot union resistance, terminate the program.

**Decision:** The potential upside of owning the narrow-body market outweighs the execution risk, provided the government backstop holds. The technological leap is the only viable path to victory.

DECISION: GO
