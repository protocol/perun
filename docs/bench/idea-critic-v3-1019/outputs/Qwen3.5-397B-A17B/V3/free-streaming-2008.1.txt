**Claim:** Launch a freemium music streaming service in Europe in 2008 by licensing major labels via upfront cash and equity.

**Steelman:** Only a legal, instant, and free-to-start alternative can displace piracy at scale; equity alignment secures catalog access despite low initial ad yields, betting on conversion and market dominance before competitors emerge.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "edge" test. In 2008, the "free piracy remains easy" risk is not a future uncertainty but a present blocker. Piracy offers zero friction and zero cost. To compete, the service must match that frictionlessness while monetizing via ads with historically low CPMs. The math does not work: high fixed costs (label minimums) plus low variable revenue (ads) plus low conversion rates (users accustomed to free) creates a structural cash burn that equity cannot indefinitely mask without proven user growth.

The "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the sequencing. Paying large upfront minimums before validating that users will tolerate ads or convert to paid is a one-way door that depletes runway immediately. The proposal assumes labels will accept equity in lieu of guaranteed cash flow during a sales decline, yet labels hold the leverage; they are unlikely to accept high-risk equity over guaranteed minimums when they can simply sue pirates or wait for a deeper-pocketed competitor.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Unit Economics:** Ad revenue per listener in 2008 is insufficient to cover the per-stream licensing costs implied by "major catalog" deals, creating a loss on every free user that conversion rates cannot mathematically offset quickly enough.
2.  **Competitive Disadvantage:** The product cannot be "instant" and "free" while paying high royalties; piracy remains superior on price and friction. The proposal offers no unique asset (exclusive content, superior tech) to overcome this.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first to market with a licensed model is not an edge if the unit economics are negative and the user experience is inferior to the incumbent (piracy).

**Verdict:** The plan relies on optimistic conversion rates and label benevolence regarding equity, ignoring the immediate cash-flow mismatch. It bets the company on unproven ad yields against entrenched free alternatives.

DECISION: NO-GO
