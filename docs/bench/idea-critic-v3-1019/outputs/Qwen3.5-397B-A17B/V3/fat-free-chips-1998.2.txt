The claim is that a national launch of fat-free chips using an undigested fat substitute will succeed despite known deterrents. The steelman: first-mover advantage in the "fat-free" category captures dieters before attention shifts, leveraging regulatory approval as a safety signal.

However, the blocker test fails. The proposal admits three critical facts that directly contradict the path to upside:
1.  **Mandatory Negative Labeling:** The required statement about digestive effects is not a minor risk; it is an active disincentive printed on the package, directly scaring the target health-conscious buyer.
2.  **Price Disadvantage:** The product costs more than regular chips, removing the standard commodity incentive to switch.
3.  **Active Opposition:** Consumer groups are already mobilized against the ingredient, guaranteeing negative PR that heavy advertising may amplify rather than suppress.

The "Edge" is nonexistent. Being first is not an edge when the first product carries a warning label and a price premium. The risk of "dieters' attention shifting" is secondary to the immediate friction of the label and price. A cheaper, safer alternative is "do nothing" or a limited regional test to verify if the label actually kills sales, rather than a costly national rollout.

The pre-mortem is clear: The launch proceeds, consumers read the label, try the chip once, experience the warned effects, and the brand becomes synonymous with "digestive distress." The heavy advertising spend accelerates awareness of the flaw rather than the benefit. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed national brand launch that poisons the company's reputation for future health products.

There is no factual basis in the proposal to believe the marketing spend can overcome a mandatory warning label and a higher price point simultaneously. The plan relies on hope, not an asset rivals lack.

Blockers: The mandatory warning label and higher price directly contradict the value proposition for the target audience.
Risks: Consumer backlash, wasted ad spend, brand damage.
Edge: NONE.

DECISION: NO-GO
