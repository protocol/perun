This is not merely cost-saving; it delivers a tangible product advantage (wider seating) that rivals with dedicated, narrower fuselages cannot match without a total redesign. In commercial aviation, passenger comfort directly influences airline preference once basic range/cost thresholds are met.

The "stretched engineering staff" is a resource constraint, not a factual blocker, provided the company prioritizes this over lower-value initiatives. The risk of rivals locking in airlines is mitigated by the fact that airlines operate fleets for decades; a superior seat configuration offers a compelling reason to wait or switch, breaking the "first-mover" lock-in. The "few launch orders" criticism reflects the current market state, not the potential demand for a demonstrably better product.

The "do nothing" alternative guarantees zero market share in a growing segment, ceding dominance to competitors. The "cheaper alternative" of building a narrow five-abreast plane would result in a "me-too" product with no advantage to justify its late entry.
