The claim is that a non-gaming infrastructure giant can profitably launch a 4K cloud gaming service with proprietary studios in 2019. The steelman posits that global data center proximity solves latency better than competitors, and brand trust overcomes the "product graveyard" reputation.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts inherent to the plan. First, the economic model is broken: charging full price for games *plus* a subscription removes the primary value proposition of cloud gaming (accessibility/cost), especially when users must re-purchase libraries they may already own on other platforms. Second, the technical constraint of 2019 consumer internet infrastructure makes guaranteed 4K streaming with low input lag impossible for a global mass market; the proposal admits quality depends on user connections, yet promises console-quality performance, a direct contradiction. Third, the content strategy is weak: lacking exclusive publisher support and having a reputation for killing products, the company cannot secure the library density required to retain subscribers against established console ecosystems.

The "edge" is nonexistent. Data center count does not equal last-mile performance. Being first to market with a flawed pricing model is not an advantage. The risks are not merely potential; they are structural certainties based on the stated constraints. The pre-mortem is clear: the service launches with high churn due to lag and pricing, studios fail to produce hits without established talent or IP, and the product is shuttered within two years, reinforcing the negative brand reputation.

There is no cheaper or safer alternative within this specific configuration because the core value proposition (high cost, high friction, uncertain performance) is fundamentally misaligned with market realities. The plan does not beat "do nothing" or "partner only" strategies. Since the proposal contains internal contradictions regarding performance guarantees versus dependency on user internet, and the financial model lacks a competitive edge, it cannot proceed as written.

DECISION: NO-GO
