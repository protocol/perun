Steelman: A rapid, low-cost entry leveraging 64-bit novelty to capture early adopters before dominant rivals saturate the market.

**Blockers:**
1. **Software Void:** The proposal admits "few games" and "hard to program." A console without a robust library fails immediately; hardware specs are irrelevant to consumers without software.
2. **Resource Mismatch:** "Little cash for marketing" contradicts the need to educate developers and consumers on a new, difficult architecture against two wealthy rivals.
3. **Timing Trap:** Launching just one year ahead of massive competitors with weak third-party support invites a "bridge" failure, where the platform dies before reaching critical mass.

**Risks:**
High probability of developer abandonment due to technical difficulty; rapid obsolescence once rival 64-bit systems arrive with better support; brand damage from another failed hardware push.

**Edge:**
NONE. Being "first" to 64-bit offers no sustainable advantage if the ecosystem is empty. "First" without software is a liability, not an asset. The proposed price ($249) is aggressive but meaningless without games to play.

**Analysis:**
The plan relies on hardware superiority to overcome a lack of software and marketing. History and market dynamics dictate that platform success depends on the ecosystem, not bit-width. The "hard to program" constraint ensures the game library will remain thin, causing the "few games" risk to materialize instantly. With limited cash, the company cannot subsidize development or market the complexity away. Launching in only two cities limits feedback loops and revenue generation, failing to build the momentum needed to survive the impending rival launches. This is a "one-way door" expenditure of scarce resources with a near-certain failure mode: a shelf of unsold hardware and angry licensees. The cheaper alternative is to license the technology or exit the hardware business entirely to preserve remaining capital.

DECISION: NO-GO
