**Steelman:** Leveraging an existing community-built map and a proven IP to launch a location-based game creates a viral loop where physical movement drives monetization, potentially expanding a niche genre into a mainstream phenomenon.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker is technical capacity: servers built for a "modest" audience will collapse under the load of a "famous" franchise's user base, causing immediate churn and brand damage. Safety risks (walking while distracted) invite liability and public backlash, potentially halting operations. The "niche" history of location games suggests the total addressable market may not support the infrastructure costs required for a global hit, especially with free-to-play revenue relying on a tiny converter rate.

Alternatives include: (1) Licensing the IP for a traditional non-location game (lower risk, proven revenue); (2) A limited regional pilot to stress-test servers and safety protocols before global launch; (3) Doing nothing to preserve capital.

**Pre-mortem:** The game launches globally, servers crash on day one due to unexpected volume, players cannot access purchased items, safety incidents occur immediately, and the studio faces lawsuits while the partner franchise terminates the contract.

**Evidence & Fit:** The proposal admits servers are insufficient for the projected scale. It acknowledges safety concerns and revenue fragility without offering mitigation. The "edge" relies entirely on the IP's fame, which increases the likelihood of server failure rather than guaranteeing success. There is no evidence that location gaming has moved from niche to mass market; the proposal hopes to *create* that shift but lacks the operational readiness to survive it.

**Blockers:**
1. **Infrastructure Mismatch:** Current servers cannot handle the traffic spike inherent to partnering with a "famous" franchise.
2. **Unmitigated Liability:** Safety risks are identified but no plan (e.g., warnings, gameplay pauses) is proposed.
3. **Revenue Gamble:** No financial model proves the niche can support the required scale.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first with a famous IP is not an edge if the product crashes on launch. The proposal relies on hope rather than verified capacity.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. The infrastructure gap alone guarantees failure at scale. A capped pilot (go-if) was not proposed; the current ask is for a full launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
