**Steelman**: Replacing stationary assembly with a powered moving line drastically reduces unit cost and time, enabling lower prices that expand the market enough to offset conversion costs and turnover.

**Attack & Options**:
The primary blocker is the "high volume of a single model" requirement. If demand does not surge immediately due to lower prices, the fixed costs of the new line combined with lost output during conversion will cause insolvency before efficiency gains materialize. The "do nothing" option preserves cash flow and variety but cedes long-term cost leadership. A "staged" alternative (converting one section first) reduces risk but delays the full cost advantage needed to drive the required volume spike.

**Fit & Pre-mortem**:
This fits a strategy of market domination through price leadership, not differentiation. Pre-mortem: The line runs at full capacity, but a competitor releases a slightly more modern car; buyers, now wealthy enough from the economic boom this method creates, choose variety over the lowest price, leaving the company with a rigid, single-model factory and no cash to pivot.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria**:
The proposal assumes demand is infinitely elastic regarding price. There is no evidence provided that the price reduction will be sufficient to double or triple volume immediately to cover the conversion dip. The risk of worker turnover is a manageable operational cost compared to the existential risk of a demand miss.

**Decision Logic**:
*   **Blockers**: None proven. The volume requirement is a condition for success, not a factual contradiction. The assumption that lower prices create sufficient volume is the core bet.
*   **Edge**: First-mover advantage in ultra-low-cost manufacturing creates a barrier to entry rivals cannot match without similar capital reconstruction.
*   **Risks**: High turnover, demand rigidity, massive sunk cost if the single model fails.
*   **Kill Criterion**: If unit sales do not increase by >50% within 6 months of price reduction, halt further line expansion.

The potential upside (democratizing ownership, dominating the market) outweighs the execution risks, provided the price cut is aggressive enough to guarantee the volume needed to utilize the line.

DECISION: GO
