**Steelman:** The company must immediately replace its aging, declining rear-engined icon with a modern, efficient front-wheel-drive hatchback to survive the oil crisis and shifting market demands, despite high retooling costs and limited internal experience.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces severe blockers. First, **financial fragility**: executing a full model replacement, new tooling, and external design during an oil crisis while profits are shrinking creates an existential cash-flow risk. If the new car fails to gain immediate traction, the company lacks the reserves to survive. Second, **competence gap**: the manufacturer has "little experience" with the proposed powertrain layout. Developing a complex new architecture while simultaneously managing a brand transition invites catastrophic quality or delay issues. Third, **market timing**: launching an expensive new product exactly when overall car demand is shrinking due to the oil crisis contradicts basic economic caution; consumers may delay purchases regardless of the car's efficiency.

While the "do nothing" option leads to slow death as the old car's sales fall, the proposed "all-in" swap risks sudden death. The risks (loyalists rejecting the new character, execution failure due to inexperience, and market collapse) are not merely theoretical; they are grounded in the stated lack of experience and current financial constraints. There is no stated "edge" or asset (such as a prototype already proven, a partner to share tooling costs, or a waiting list) that guarantees this specific high-risk pivot will succeed where recent models have failed. The proposal relies on hope that the new design will instantly overcome brand loyalty and economic headwinds without a phased approach or proof of capability.

Without a verified path to fund the retooling or evidence that the new design resonates with the shrinking market, the plan fails the blocker test. The combination of zero margin for error, lack of relevant experience, and hostile economic timing makes this specific proposal too dangerous to approve as written.

DECISION: NO-GO
