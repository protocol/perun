**Steelman**: Replacing stationary assembly with a moving powered line will drastically reduce unit cost and time, enabling lower prices that expand the market enough to offset conversion costs and turnover.

**Attack & Alternatives**:
The primary blocker is the "high volume of a single model" requirement. If demand does not instantly surge due to lower prices, the fixed cost of the new line combined with lost production during conversion creates a liquidity crisis. The alternative—"do nothing" or incremental station improvements—avoids this binary risk but forfeits the cost advantage. A "mixed model" approach is impossible with this specific mechanical design; it demands total standardization.

**Fit & Pre-mortem**:
This fits a "volume leader" strategy but fails a "variety leader" strategy. In a pre-mortem: The line is built, but a competitor releases a slightly more stylish car. Because the line cannot adapt without massive retooling, sales stall. The company is stuck with high fixed costs and a product buyers now view as obsolete. The "savings passed to buyers" assumes price is the only constraint; if style or features become the primary driver, the efficiency gain is irrelevant.

**Evidence & Edge**:
The proposal relies on the assumption that demand is infinitely elastic regarding price. There is no evidence provided that buyers will accept zero variety. The stated edge is cost leadership. However, cost leadership is only an edge if the product remains acceptable. If the "one design" becomes a liability rather than a virtue, the cost advantage cannot save the venture.

**Decision Logic**:
*   **Blockers**: The plan requires immediate, massive volume to justify the conversion cost and disruption. The proposal admits rivals offer variety and buyers "may tire" of one design. This is not a risk; it is a stated market condition that contradicts the volume assumption. Without proof that price sensitivity overrides the desire for variety, the core economic engine fails.
*   **Risks**: High turnover, conversion downtime, competitor adaptation.
*   **Edge**: Lower price (contingent on volume).

The proposal asks to bet the company on a single unproven assumption: that lower price alone guarantees the necessary volume despite zero variety. The stated risk that buyers may tire of one design is a direct threat to the volume required for the line to pay off. This is a blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
