The proposal attempts to resolve a trust crisis by retaining the very mechanism causing it. The core failure is assuming that adding controls to an unwanted feature satisfies users who fundamentally object to the feature's existence. The "apology" admits fault, yet the plan refuses to remove the fault, creating a dissonance that fuels rather than quells protest.

The risks are not merely hypothetical; they are active and escalating. "Growing quickly" protest groups indicate a tipping point where retention is threatened not by inconvenience, but by perceived violation. In a social network, trust is the primary asset; once eroded, privacy controls are often viewed as insufficient damage control rather than a solution. The argument that "nothing new is shared" ignores the psychological impact of forced visibility. Users do not want to manage a feed of their private lives; they want those lives to remain private by default.

The "better way" analysis reveals a critical omission: the option to disable the feed entirely or revert to an opt-in model is absent. Keeping the feed active while apologizing signals that the platform values engagement metrics over user consent. This validates the protesters' claims that the company is intrusive. A true apology requires removing the source of harm, not just installing a fence around it.

The pre-mortem is clear: if the feed remains mandatory (even with filters), the narrative becomes "they heard us and ignored us," accelerating member exodus and giving rivals a definitive wedge to split the user base. The "edge" of this plan is non-existent; it offers no competitive advantage, only a diluted version of the status quo. There is no scenario where keeping the intrusive element survives the trust test when the alternative (removal or opt-in) directly addresses the complaint.

The proposal fails the blocker test: the stated fact that protests are "growing quickly" contradicts the strategy of keeping the trigger alive. Without an option to turn the feed off completely, the plan guarantees continued backlash.

DECISION: NO-GO
