The claim is that shifting from perpetual licenses to a subscription-only model in 2013 will secure the company's future despite immediate revenue dips and customer backlash. The steelman: recurring revenue creates predictable cash flow, funds continuous innovation, and eliminates the "upgrade cliff" where users skip versions, ultimately increasing lifetime value per customer.

Alternatives include maintaining a hybrid model (perpetual + subscription) or delaying the shift until market readiness improves. A hybrid approach mitigates churn but preserves the volatile revenue cycle the proposal seeks to eliminate. Doing nothing retains current users but cedes long-term growth to competitors who successfully monetize updates.

Fit analysis assumes the market in 2013 is ready for "renting" creative tools, a significant cultural hurdle given the proposal's own admission of loud objections. The pre-mortem reveals a catastrophic failure mode: if the monthly price point does not mathematically recover the lost upfront capital within 18 months, the company faces a liquidity crisis during the transition dip. The "one-way door" nature of abandoning perpetual licenses means regaining trust if the model fails is nearly impossible.

Evidence check: The proposal admits revenue will dip and users may leave. Without a verified financial model showing that the Net Present Value (NPV) of the subscription stream exceeds the immediate loss of perpetual sales plus churn costs, the core economic claim is unsupported. The "edge" is purely structural (recurring revenue), not based on proven demand for this specific pricing in this specific year.

Blocker: The proposal lacks a concrete, validated financial bridge showing how the company survives the admitted revenue dip and churn without running out of cash. A strategic shift of this magnitude requires proof that the unit economics work *before* burning the bridge of perpetual sales. The risks listed (churn, piracy, revenue dip) are not just possibilities; they are stated outcomes that, without a quantified mitigation plan, constitute a failure of the business case.

Risks: Liquidity crisis during transition, permanent brand damage, accelerated piracy.
Edge: NONE (structural benefit only; no proven demand advantage cited).

DECISION: NO-GO
