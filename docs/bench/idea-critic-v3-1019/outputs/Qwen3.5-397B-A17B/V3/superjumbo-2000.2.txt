**Steelman:** A 550-seat double-decker solves hub congestion by moving more passengers per slot, leveraging a first-mover advantage in the very-large-aircraft (VLA) segment before rivals react.

**Attack:** The proposal hinges on the assumption that hub-and-spoke traffic will continue to grow faster than point-to-point demand. However, the prompt explicitly states airlines are *already* shifting to point-to-point routes using smaller twin-engine jets. Building the largest aircraft in history directly contradicts the stated market trajectory. If airports are congested, the industry solution described is bypassing hubs entirely, not making them larger.

**Alternatives:** 
1. **Do Nothing/Smaller Jet:** Develop a highly efficient twin-engine jet (250–300 seats) that matches the stated market shift. This avoids massive airport infrastructure costs and offers airlines route flexibility.
2. **Rival Stretch:** If the rival stretches their existing jumbo, they achieve similar capacity at a fraction of the $10.7B development cost and risk.

**Blockers:** 
- **Market Contradiction:** The core premise (need for massive capacity) fights the evidence (shift to smaller, point-to-point jets). 
- **Infrastructure Friction:** Requiring global airports to modify gates creates a massive adoption barrier for a niche product.
- **Cost vs. Flexibility:** $10.7B is sunk into a single configuration while the market demands flexibility.

**Edge:** None. Being first into a shrinking or stagnant VLA segment is a liability, not an asset. The "congestion" argument is solved by the industry's current move toward point-to-point flying, not by doubling down on hubs.

**Pre-mortem:** In 15 years, the fleet sits grounded because airlines prefer direct flights that bypass hubs, and the high operating costs per seat cannot be justified without full loads, which are hard to gather on specific routes. The rival dominates with flexible, efficient twins.

**Verdict:** The proposal bets against the explicit trend provided in the text. It requires the market to reverse its current behavior to succeed.

DECISION: NO-GO
