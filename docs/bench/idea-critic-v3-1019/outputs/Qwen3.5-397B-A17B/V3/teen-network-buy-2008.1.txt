**Verdict: NO-GO**

The proposal fails the blocker test on valuation and strategic fit. The core claim relies on acquiring growth to reach young users, yet the target's revenue is explicitly "small relative to the $850 million price." This implies a massive multiple based entirely on future user retention, directly contradicting the stated risk that "social networks can lose users quickly." Paying a premium for an asset with high churn risk and low current monetization is financially indefensible without a proven mechanism to stabilize the user base, which the proposal does not provide.

Furthermore, the "better way" analysis favors inaction or organic development. The portal's strategy has "changed several times," indicating internal instability. Integrating a volatile external asset into a shifting strategic framework amplifies execution risk. A cheaper, safer alternative is to leverage the existing "large ad business" to build native social features or partner via revenue share, avoiding an $850 million cash outlay for an unproven integration.

The presence of a "larger rival social network... growing fast" suggests the target may already be losing the network effect war. In social networking, the second mover often captures negligible value compared to the leader. Acquiring the #2 player at a peak price while a stronger competitor accelerates is a classic value-destruction scenario.

**Blockers:**
1. **Valuation Mismatch:** $850M price vs. "small" revenue creates an unjustifiable risk profile given the high churn possibility.
2. **Strategic Instability:** The buyer's history of frequent strategy changes makes successful integration of a complex social platform unlikely.
3. **Competitive Disadvantage:** A larger, faster-growing rival threatens the target's core asset (the user network) immediately post-acquisition.

**Edge: NONE.** The proposal cites no unique asset, cost advantage, or proven demand that rivals lack. It relies on "strategic necessity" and fear of missing the youth demographic, which are not edges.

DECISION: NO-GO
