The claim is that a radical simplification of the PC, removing legacy standards for a new ecosystem, will reverse financial losses despite high execution risk. The steelman: legacy complexity bloated costs and confused users; a bold, distinct product targeting the emerging internet era can reclaim margins and mindshare faster than incremental updates, provided the ecosystem gap is bridged by software distribution.

Alternatives include continuing the current fragmented lineup (high cost, shrinking share) or a modest refresh retaining legacy ports (missed differentiation, continued margin erosion). Doing nothing guarantees continued losses. This proposal bets on a one-way door: once legacy ports are removed, return is impossible without a total redesign. The cost of delay is another year of losses and irrelevance.

However, the blocker test reveals a critical flaw in the current facts. The proposal relies on USB and the internet for peripherals and software, yet the evidence states "few USB peripherals exist yet" and users' existing hardware "will not connect." This is not merely a risk; it is a functional gap that prevents the product from serving the immediate needs of the target market (replacing existing setups). Without a guaranteed, simultaneous launch of essential USB peripherals or a robust software bundling strategy to offset the lack of a diskette drive for installation, the product fails its primary utility test upon release. The financial constraint ("little room for a miss") makes betting on an undeveloped ecosystem fatal.

The edge is potential distinctiveness and cost reduction, but currently, this is theoretical. The "Edge" is NONE until the peripheral gap is solved. A "go" decision requires a verified plan to bundle necessary USB hardware or software installation methods that do not rely on the missing diskette drive. As proposed, with known incompatibilities and no mitigation for the missing ecosystem, the plan fails.

Blockers: Functional incompatibility with user's existing hardware and lack of available peripherals at launch.
Risks: Financial ruin if adoption lags; reviewer backlash over missing standard features.
Edge: NONE (distinctiveness exists, but utility is compromised).

DECISION: NO-GO
