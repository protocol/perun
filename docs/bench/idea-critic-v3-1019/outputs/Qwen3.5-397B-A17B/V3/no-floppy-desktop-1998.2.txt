**Verdict: REVISE (simulated); Final Output: NO-GO**

The proposal attempts to solve shrinking market share and financial losses by launching a radical, low-priced all-in-one computer that deliberately removes industry-standard connectivity (floppy drives, legacy ports) in favor of nascent technology (USB, CD-ROM, Internet).

**Steelman:** The only path to survival is a disruptive, simplified product that leverages emerging standards to cut costs and appeal to new users, forcing the market forward rather than catering to legacy expectations.

**Critical Blockers:**
1.  **Market Reality vs. Assumption:** The proposal assumes buyers will accept the absence of a floppy drive despite explicit evidence that "buyers and reviewers expect" one. In a shrinking market, alienating the existing installed base (who cannot connect current printers/drives) while the peripheral ecosystem for USB is "few," creates a immediate adoption cliff.
2.  **Financial Fragility:** The company has lost money for two years with "little room for a miss." Launching a product that knowingly lacks features critical to the current purchasing decision is not a calculated risk; it is a gamble with the company's remaining capital. If the market does not shift instantly, the company fails.
3.  **Missing Edge:** The proposal cites "low-priced" and "translucent case" as differentiators. However, without functional compatibility (the ability to use existing peripherals), price and aesthetics are insufficient to overcome the utility gap for a mass-market consumer computer. There is no proven demand for a computer that cannot talk to the user's current hardware.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, reviewers pan the lack of a floppy drive and legacy ports, enterprise and education buyers (key volume drivers) reject it due to peripheral incompatibility, sales flatline, and the company exhausts its cash reserves within 12 months.

**Conclusion:** While the vision of a USB-only future is strategically sound long-term, executing this specific transition *now*, given the company's precarious financial state and the immature peripheral market, presents an unacceptable blocker. The plan fails the "must beat do nothing" test because the likelihood of catastrophic failure outweighs the potential upside in the current context. A phased approach or a higher-end model to subsidize the transition is required before betting the firm on this specific configuration.

DECISION: NO-GO
