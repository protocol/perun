**Steelman:** Acquiring the US portal instantly grants critical mass, brand recognition, and a user base that would otherwise take a decade to build organically, justifying the premium as a market-entry fee.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on three concrete blockers derived strictly from provided facts. First, the target "ranks behind larger rivals in traffic." In the portal business, network effects dictate that users flock to the leader for better search results and ad density. Buying the #2 or #3 player at a premium does not solve the scale disadvantage; it merely monetizes a declining asset. Second, the price is a "large multiple of its revenue" paid entirely in "volatile" stock. If the acquirer's stock drops post-announcement (a high probability given the volatility noted), the deal economics collapse, or the acquisition becomes dilutive without guaranteed synergies. Third, the "hard" integration of two continents introduces massive execution risk that directly threatens the value of the volatile currency used to pay for the deal.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or organic growth option avoids overpaying for a secondary asset. A cheaper, simpler path is a strategic partnership or minority stake to test US appetite without assuming full integration risk or paying a control premium for a lagging traffic rank.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 24 months. Cause: The combined entity still trails the market leader in traffic, advertisers migrate to the higher-volume rival, and the stock price crashes due to missed integration targets and the initial overpayment. The "global portal" vision dissolves because the US foothold was bought, not earned, at the peak of a bubble.

**Edge:** NONE. Being a Spanish-language provider offers no inherent advantage in a general US web portal war against established English-language incumbents. The proposal relies on "strategic necessity" rather than a specific asset that guarantees winning the US market.

**Blockers:** 
1. Target traffic ranks behind rivals (fundamental business model flaw).
2. Price is a large multiple of revenue paid in volatile stock (financial instability).
3. High integration complexity across continents (execution risk).

**Risks:** Advertiser churn, culture clash, stock devaluation.

**Decision:** The plan buys a disadvantaged position at a premium using unstable currency.

DECISION: NO-GO
