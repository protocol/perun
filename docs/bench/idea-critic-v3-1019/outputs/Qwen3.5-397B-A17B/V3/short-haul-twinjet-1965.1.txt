**Steelman:** Entering late with a superior product (wider cabin) leveraging existing tooling to capture a distinct comfort niche despite competitor head-starts.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal acknowledges lateness and resource strain but offers a specific architectural advantage (cross-section reuse) that directly addresses the "late" disadvantage by reducing development time and cost while offering a tangible customer benefit (6-abreast vs. 5-abreast) that rivals cannot match without redesigning their own fuselages. The risk of locked-in airlines is mitigated by the fact that airlines prioritize operational economics and passenger preference; a wider cabin is a durable competitive edge for short-haul density and comfort.

**Risks:** Engineering stretch is real but manageable if the cross-section reuse significantly lowers the design burden compared to a clean-sheet design. Launch orders being few is typical for new segments; the goal is to secure enough to sustain production until the market matures. Rival lock-in is not absolute; airlines frequently switch suppliers for better product fit.

**Edge:** The specific, hard-to-copy asset of the existing large-jet cross-section allows for a wider cabin (6-abreast) than the incumbent 5-abreast rivals. This is a product advantage, not just a timing or marketing play. It turns a "late" entry into a "better" entry.

**Decision Logic:** The plan beats "do nothing" (ceding the segment entirely) and "rush a copy" (matching rivals' 5-abreast). The reuse strategy lowers the barrier to entry enough to make the late launch viable. The wider cabin is the killer feature that justifies the risk.

**Kill Criteria:** If wind tunnel testing or structural analysis proves the reused cross-section cannot support the required wing-engine configuration without prohibitive weight/cost penalties, stop. If major airlines explicitly reject the 6-abreast layout in favor of the rivals' 5-abreast due to fleet commonality with existing small jets, pivot.

**Leading Metric:** Number of launch orders secured within 12 months of formal program launch, specifically from carriers not already committed to rivals.

DECISION: GO
