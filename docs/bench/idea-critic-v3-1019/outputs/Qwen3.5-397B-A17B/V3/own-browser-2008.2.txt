**Steelman:** Building a proprietary browser with superior speed (new JS engine) and stability (multi-process) protects the search company's core revenue by preventing a single OS vendor from monopolizing web access, despite high execution risks.

**Analysis:**
The proposal addresses an existential threat: if the OS vendor controls the browser, they can exclude the search company entirely. The "do nothing" option relies on an open-source partner that the search company funds but does not control, creating a fragile dependency. The dominant competitor's preinstallation is a barrier, but not an absolute one; history shows users will switch for significantly better performance and stability.

The risks are severe but manageable as "kill criteria" rather than blockers. The strain on the open-source partner is a political cost, not a technical failure; it can be mitigated by continuing funding while diverging technically. The lack of desktop software experience is a valid execution risk, yet the core competency (web standards, JavaScript optimization) is adjacent to existing strengths. The "users rarely switch" heuristic fails when the value proposition (speed/safety) is order-of-magnitude better.

**Edge:** The company possesses the specific engine technology and financial resources to sustain a free product solely to protect its search monopoly, a motivation rivals lacking a search revenue stream do not share. The multi-process architecture solves a specific pain point (crashes) that incumbents ignore.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal; the risks are known uncertainties, not proven impossibilities.
**Risks:** Execution failure, partner backlash, low adoption. These are addressed by setting strict kill criteria: if market share does not reach a specific threshold within 24 months, or if the partner relationship collapses legally, the project pivots or stops.
**Decision Logic:** The cost of delay is total dependency on a hostile OS vendor. The upside is survival and independence. The plan beats "do nothing" because it secures the distribution channel directly.

DECISION: GO
