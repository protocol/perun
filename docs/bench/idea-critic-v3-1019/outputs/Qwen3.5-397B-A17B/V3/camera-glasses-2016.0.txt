However, the core claim—that a software photo company should *sell* hardware—relies on the assumption that novelty demand will sustain the $130 price point long enough to justify scaling manufacturing. The proposal admits the demand risk but proceeds to "scale up" anyway if the pilot shows initial traction. Without a defined kill criterion for the pilot (e.g., "stop if repeat purchase rate < X%"), the "scale up" phase is a runaway risk.

While the "Edge" is weak and risks are high, there is no hard *blocker* (a fact showing the plan fails as proposed). The risks are known and partially mitigated by the pilot structure. The lack of a specific kill criterion is a flaw, but not a blocker under the strict definition of a fact already in hand that proves failure.
