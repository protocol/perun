Steelman: Leverage existing global data centers to deliver console-quality gaming without hardware, monetizing via subscription and full-price game purchases while building exclusive content through owned studios.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Value Proposition Failure:** Charging a monthly fee *plus* full price per game creates a cost structure higher than owning a console (one-time hardware, competitive software pricing). Users will not pay a premium for inferior convenience (lag/quality dependence).
2.  **Technical Reality:** Streaming console-quality games requires latency lower than most global internet connections can guarantee in 2019. Input lag fundamentally breaks the core loop for the target "console-quality" experience.
3.  **Content Gap:** Publishers are withholding exclusives due to the company's reputation for killing products. Without exclusives, the library cannot justify the premium cost or the technical compromise.

**Risks:**
High churn due to technical performance; inability to recoup massive studio setup costs; brand damage from another discontinued service.

**Edge:** NONE.
The proposal cites global data centers as an asset, but without a specialized low-latency network protocol (not mentioned) or a superior pricing model, raw proximity does not equal playable streaming. Being "first" or "large" is not an edge here. The "do nothing" alternative (waiting for 5G/better compression or entering via acquisition later) offers better risk/reward. The proposed model (Sub + Full Price) has no path to profitability given the stated constraints.

The strongest attack is the economic model: no rational consumer pays a subscription fee to rent infrastructure, then pays full retail price for software, to receive a degraded product (laggy video) compared to the local alternative. This fails the basic unit economics of user adoption.

DECISION: NO-GO
