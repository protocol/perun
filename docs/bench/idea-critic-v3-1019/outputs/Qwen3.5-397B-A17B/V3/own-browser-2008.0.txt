**Steelman:** Releasing a fast, multi-process, open-source browser secures the web's growth against OS vendor dominance, protecting the search company's core revenue model despite execution risks.

**Analysis:**
The proposal addresses an existential threat: if the web stagnates due to slow browsers, the search business fails. The "do nothing" option relies entirely on partners who may lack the incentive or capacity to push performance boundaries aggressively. While the company lacks desktop shipping experience, the technical differentiators (speed via a new engine, stability via process isolation) directly target user pain points that drive switching behavior. History shows users will switch for significant performance gains.

The risk to the existing open-source partnership is real but manageable; competition often spurs innovation across the ecosystem. The preinstallation barrier is high, but not insurmountable if the product is demonstrably superior and free. The "one-way door" nature of this investment is mitigated by open-sourcing the code, ensuring the technology benefits the wider web even if the specific brand struggles.

**Blockers:** None. The facts presented do not prove failure; they highlight challenges that the proposed technical advantages are designed to overcome.
**Risks:** Execution complexity, partner friction, adoption inertia. These are addressed by the open-source strategy (leveraging community for testing/adoption) and the clear value proposition (speed).
**Edge:** The company has a unique financial imperative to make the web faster, aligning its core revenue motive directly with product success, unlike pure browser vendors who may monetize differently. The proposed architecture solves the specific performance bottlenecks of the era.

**Kill Criteria:** If the browser fails to achieve a measurable market share (e.g., 1-2%) within 18 months of launch, or if the development costs exceed a defined cap without delivering the promised performance benchmarks, the project should be pivoted to purely funding the existing open-source partner.

**Decision:** The potential upside of securing the web's future outweighs the execution risks. The technical approach is sound and directly addresses the market need.

DECISION: GO
