**Steelman:** A licensed, instant-streaming desktop app with a free ad-supported tier and a premium ad-free tier can displace piracy in Europe by offering superior convenience and legality, securing label buy-in via upfront cash and equity to align incentives.

**Attack & Options:**
The claim faces three immediate blockers based on the provided facts. First, the economic model is fragile: "ad revenue per listener is low" combined with "most users may never pay" suggests the free tier will bleed cash while the paid tier fails to subsidize it. Second, the cost of entry is prohibitive; labels demanding "large minimum payments" creates a massive fixed cost before a single user is verified. Third, the value proposition is weak against the status quo; if "free piracy remains easy," the friction of installing a desktop app and viewing ads may not be enough to convert users unless the catalog is 100% complete and the UX is flawless, which the proposal does not guarantee.

Alternatives include: (1) Do nothing and let piracy erode sales further (low cost, high risk of total market collapse); (2) Launch a web-based player to reduce friction (cheaper, more accessible than desktop); (3) Negotiate a revenue-share-only deal to eliminate upfront liability (harder to sell to labels, but safer for the startup).

**Pre-mortem:** The venture launches, burns through the upfront payments to labels, fails to convert free users to paid due to the ease of piracy, and cannot cover the minimum guarantees with low ad yields. The company runs out of cash within 18 months.

**Blockers:**
1. **Unit Economics:** Low ad revenue + low conversion rate cannot cover large minimum label payments.
2. **Competitive Friction:** Desktop installation is a higher barrier than existing easy piracy methods.
3. **Capital Risk:** Upfront payments create irreversible burn before product-market fit is proven.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first to license is not an edge if the cost structure guarantees insolvency and the delivery mechanism (desktop) is inferior to the competitor (piracy/web). The proposal relies on users paying for what they can get free with minimal friction, without a demonstrated mechanism to force that behavior change.

DECISION: NO-GO
