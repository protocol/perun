**Steelman:** Acquiring the third-most-visited site instantly grants massive distribution and user-generated content, creating a dominant ad inventory that organic growth could never match at this speed.

**Attack:**
1.  **Valuation Mismatch:** The target's revenue is "small" relative to the $3.6B price. This implies an astronomical multiple based on speculative future ad monetization, not current cash flow. If the stock market corrects from "record highs," the acquisition currency (stock) collapses, destroying the deal's logic before integration begins.
2.  **Product-Market Friction:** Advertisers explicitly dislike "amateur content." Unlike curated portal pages, user pages lack brand safety guarantees. If the core inventory is toxic to premium advertisers, the projected revenue synergy is a fantasy.
3.  **Community Backlash:** Changing terms of service to force ads risks alienating the very users who create the value. A mass exodus of page owners would render the acquired asset worthless, leaving the buyer with a $3.6B shell.
4.  **Opportunity Cost:** At record market highs, capital is expensive. Deploying $3.6B in equity for an unproven ad model ignores cheaper, lower-risk alternatives to boost page views (e.g., content partnerships, organic feature development).

**Edge Analysis:** The proposal claims an edge in "scale," but scale without monetization viability is a liability, not an asset. Being "first" to buy a losing business model is not a strategic advantage. The "edge" relies entirely on the assumption that advertiser sentiment can be forced to change, which contradicts the stated risk.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial:** Revenue does not support the price; the multiple is unjustified by current facts.
*   **Market:** Advertiser aversion to amateur content directly blocks the primary revenue stream.
*   **Operational:** Risk of user revolt upon TOS changes threatens asset integrity.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test. The cost of capital is too high, the revenue model is unproven and contested by the market, and the integration risk could destroy the asset. This is a speculative bet on a market peak, not a sound business acquisition.

DECISION: NO-GO
