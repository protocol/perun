The proposal argues that entering the small jet market late is viable by leveraging a unique comfort advantage: a six-abreast cabin derived from existing large-jet tooling, surpassing the rivals' five-abreast configuration. This strategy bets that airlines and passengers will prioritize seat width over the competitors' head start.

However, critical flaws threaten this approach. First, the "late mover" disadvantage is severe; with rivals already securing orders and a year's lead, the window to lock in major carriers may have closed. Airlines prioritize fleet commonality and delivery speed; a new, unproven airframe with delayed availability offers little incentive to switch from established suppliers. Second, the constraint of "stretched engineering staff" creates a high probability of development slippage. If the team is already overloaded, adding a complex new program risks delaying not only this jet but also the core large-jet programs that fund the company. Third, reusing a large-jet cross-section for a short-haul aircraft may result in an inefficient design—potentially too heavy or aerodynamically compromised compared to rivals who optimized their airframes specifically for this role.

The assumption that "wider seats" alone will displace incumbent orders is unverified. Without a guaranteed launch customer or a clear path to accelerate delivery despite staff shortages, the project risks becoming a costly drain on resources with no market share to show for it. The rivals' lead allows them to refine their product and lock in supply chains, creating a barrier that a derivative design cannot easily breach.

To proceed without resolving the staffing bottleneck or securing anchor orders would be reckless. The proposal lacks a concrete plan to alleviate the engineering strain or a committed buyer to justify the investment against the competitive moat already built by rivals. The risk of cannibalizing focus from profitable large-jet lines while failing to gain traction in the small-jet market is too high.

DECISION: NO-GO
