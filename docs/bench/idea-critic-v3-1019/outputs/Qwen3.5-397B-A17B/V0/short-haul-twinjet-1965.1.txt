The proposal attempts to enter a market where the company is already a year behind rivals with established order books. The primary risk is that being late allows competitors to lock in major airlines, rendering a new entry unviable despite technical superiority. The argument relies entirely on the assumption that a wider six-abreast cabin is a decisive enough advantage to overcome first-mover dominance and convince airlines to switch suppliers or delay existing commitments.

However, the "better-way" analysis reveals a critical flaw in the timing strategy. Entering a market late requires a disruptive advantage that immediately offsets the switching costs for airlines. While a wider cabin is a comfort benefit, it does not guarantee the operational economics or route flexibility needed to displace incumbent aircraft already on order. Furthermore, the proposal admits engineering staff are stretched; launching a complex new program while resources are diverted to larger programs increases the risk of delays, cost overruns, and performance shortfalls. If the new jet slips its schedule, the "late" entry becomes a "too late" failure, leaving the company with high development costs and no market share.

The pre-mortem indicates a high probability of failure: the company spends billions developing a plane that arrives just as rivals lower prices to defend their turf, offering airlines a "good enough" five-abreast solution at a discount. The kill criterion here is clear: if the value proposition relies solely on cabin width without a corresponding leap in fuel efficiency or maintenance costs to justify the delay, the project will not secure enough launch orders to reach break-even. The current facts show few launch orders and significant internal resource constraints. The risk of locking capital into a losing race against entrenched competitors outweighs the potential benefit of a slightly wider cabin. The company should not stretch its engineering team for a speculative catch-up game where the odds are stacked against late entrants.

DECISION: NO-GO
