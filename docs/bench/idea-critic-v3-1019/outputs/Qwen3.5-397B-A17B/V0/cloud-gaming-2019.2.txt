The proposal fails the "better-way" and "fit" tests immediately. The economic model is broken: charging a monthly subscription *plus* full price for games creates a cost structure higher than owning a console, removing the primary incentive for users to switch. Competitors offer libraries included in the fee; this model offers nothing but friction.

The technical risk is fatal for the stated value proposition. "Console-quality" gaming requires near-zero input lag. Streaming 4K video over the public internet introduces variable latency that makes fast-paced games unplayable, regardless of data center proximity. The proposal admits this dependency but offers no mitigation, promising a premium experience on infrastructure it does not control.

Strategically, the fit is poor. The company has a documented reputation for terminating products, which erodes consumer trust required for a platform relying on long-term library ownership. Furthermore, building proprietary game studios is a capital-intensive, high-risk venture with long lead times, distracting from the core competency of infrastructure. The lack of exclusive titles at launch means there is no "must-have" content to drive adoption against established incumbents who already have ecosystems and exclusives.

The "do nothing" or "partner-only" alternative is superior. Licensing the streaming technology to existing publishers without the burden of content creation or a confusing double-dip pricing model would test the market with significantly less capital exposure. The current plan attempts to solve three hard problems simultaneously (infrastructure latency, content creation, and consumer pricing psychology) with a strategy that loses on price and performance compared to the status quo.

The strongest attack survived is merely that the company has data centers; however, having servers does not equate to solving the "last mile" latency problem for real-time interaction. Without a revised pricing model that includes games in the subscription, a concrete technical solution for input lag, and a credible plan to secure exclusives, this initiative will burn capital while alienating users.

DECISION: NO-GO
