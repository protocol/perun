The claim is that a dual-tier streaming service, launched in Europe with major label licensing via upfront cash and equity, will successfully combat piracy and generate revenue. The steelman: only a legal, instant, and free-to-start alternative can displace entrenched piracy habits, and securing major catalogs is the non-negotiable key to legitimacy.

However, the unit economics appear fatal. Ad revenue per listener in 2008 is too low to cover the "large minimum payments" demanded by labels, creating an immediate cash burn. If "most users never pay," the free tier becomes a subsidy for the labels that accelerates insolvency. The proposal trades scarce cash (upfront payments) and future ownership (equity) for content that users currently steal for free, without a guaranteed conversion mechanism strong enough to bridge the gap between low ad yields and high licensing costs.

The "better way" analysis suggests a fatal flaw in the cost structure. Negotiating revenue-share-only deals (no upfronts) with indie labels first would validate demand without catastrophic fixed costs. Alternatively, a desktop-only P2P legal layer could reduce bandwidth costs while offering convenience over piracy. The current plan assumes labels will accept equity in lieu of sufficient cash flow, but labels facing falling sales are likely to demand guaranteed liquidity, creating a mismatch between their needs and the startup's capacity.

The pre-mortem reveals the company runs out of cash within 18 months: user growth is high, but ARPU (Average Revenue Per User) remains below the per-stream royalty obligation due to the heavy mix of non-paying users and low ad rates. The "upfront payment" requirement acts as a kill switch before product-market fit is proven. Without a verified path where Lifetime Value (LTV) exceeds the sum of upfront amortization and per-stream costs, the venture is structurally insolvent from day one. The risk of labels demanding more cash mid-contract upon seeing low conversion is extreme.

This is a one-way door with high capital destruction. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of signing these specific terms now. The leading metric (conversion rate) cannot save the model if the base ad revenue doesn't cover the minimum guarantees.

DECISION: NO-GO
