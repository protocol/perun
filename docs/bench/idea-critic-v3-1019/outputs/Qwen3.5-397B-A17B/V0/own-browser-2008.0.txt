Steelman: Releasing a fast, multi-process, open-source browser in 2008 directly protects the search company's core revenue by preventing a single OS vendor from monopolizing web access, even if it risks current partnerships and requires mastering desktop shipping.

**Skeptic**: The claim that users "rarely switch" is the primary failure mode. If adoption stays below 1% due to pre-installation dominance, the engineering cost yields no defensive value. The risk of alienating the open-source partner is real but secondary to the existential threat of OS lock-in. The "never shipped desktop software" constraint is a capability gap, not a logical impossibility; it demands extreme discipline in scope (MVP only).

**Better-way**: Alternatives include doubling funding to the existing open-source partner or building a toolbar. However, toolbars are declining, and relying solely on a partner cedes control of the rendering engine's future to a group without the search company's specific performance incentives. Doing nothing guarantees dependence on the OS vendor's whims. The proposed path is the only one securing full stack control.

**Kill-criteria**: This is a one-way door with high irreversibility once code is public and teams are hired.
*Pre-mortem*: Failure occurs if the browser launches >12 months late or fails to achieve 2% market share within 24 months, rendering the defensive moat ineffective.
*Cost of Delay*: High. Every month the OS vendor tightens integration, the switching cost for users increases exponentially. Waiting for "perfect readiness" ensures the window closes.

**Evidence & Fit**: The proposal aligns with the north star: maximizing web usage. The technical differentiators (speed, stability via separate processes) address the exact pain points of the era's dominant browser. While the partnership strain is a valid cost, it is a tactical negotiation issue, not a strategic blocker. The lack of desktop experience is mitigated by open-sourcing, leveraging external contributors to fill gaps.

**Decision**: The existential risk of OS monopoly outweighs the execution risks and partnership friction. The proposal must proceed immediately to establish a foothold before the window closes. Cap initial scope strictly to the engine and basic UI to mitigate the "never shipped desktop software" risk.

DECISION: GO
