The claim is that a non-gaming infrastructure giant can successfully launch a premium cloud gaming service with proprietary studios in 2019. The steelman: leverage global data centers to deliver low-latency 4K streaming, overcoming hardware barriers while monetizing via subscriptions and full-price game sales.

However, the attack reveals fatal fractures. First, the value proposition is inverted: users pay a monthly fee plus full price for games they must repurchase, creating a cost structure higher than owning a console, with no ownership rights. Second, the technical dependency on user internet connections introduces input lag and quality variance that the provider cannot control, directly undermining the "console-quality" promise. Third, the company's reputation for terminating products creates a trust deficit; publishers will withhold exclusives, and gamers will avoid building a library on a platform likely to be shuttered.

Alternatives exist: licensing technology to existing gamers, acquiring an established studio without launching a full platform, or waiting for global bandwidth to improve. The "do nothing" option avoids massive capex for an unproven market fit.

The pre-mortem is clear: the service launches, suffers from lag complaints, fails to secure key exclusives due to trust issues, and sees low adoption because the pricing model is punitive. Within 18 months, the division is marked as non-core and shut down, wasting the studio investments. The cost of delay is minimal compared to the cost of failure; waiting allows competitors to validate the market and bandwidth to mature.

There is no evidence provided that the pricing friction or latency issues are solvable at scale in 2019. The assumption that users will accept double-payment (subscription + full game price) is unsupported by market behavior. The kill criteria are missing, but the fundamental unit economics and trust barriers are already sufficient to halt. This is a one-way door with high irreversible costs and low probability of success given the stated constraints.

DECISION: NO-GO
