The claim is that a $160 32-bit add-on for an aging 16-bit console, rushed for holiday release, will successfully bridge the gap to a next-generation system arriving in one year without cannibalizing sales or damaging the brand.

**Steelman:** The add-on maximizes the installed base's remaining value, generating immediate cash flow and keeping developers engaged during the transition, provided the price is low enough to impulse-buy.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cannibalization & Confusion:** Releasing a stopgap 32-bit device one year before the "real" next-gen console creates a disastrous message. Consumers will rationally delay spending $160 on a compromised interim solution, waiting for the superior native hardware. This risks freezing sales of both the old console and the new add-on.
2.  **Developer Fragmentation:** Forcing developers to rush games for a niche add-on while simultaneously preparing for the true next-gen splits limited resources. A small, rushed launch library fails to justify the $160 price point, leading to high attach-rate failure.
3.  **Brand Erosion:** Rushing hardware to meet a holiday deadline invites technical flaws. If the add-on is perceived as a cynical cash grab or performs poorly, it tarnishes the reputation of the upcoming flagship console.
4.  **Opportunity Cost:** The engineering and marketing bandwidth consumed by this add-on detracts from ensuring the next-generation console's launch is flawless. The "cheaper" path is focusing entirely on the inevitable future rather than propping up the past.

**Better Way:** Aggressively discount the existing 16-bit library to squeeze final revenue and redirect all engineering/marketing toward a strong, unified launch for the 32-bit successor. Do not fragment the ecosystem.

**Kill Criteria:** If the add-on captures less than 15% of the installed base or causes a >10% dip in standard console sales during the holiday quarter, the strategy is a failure. The leading metric is pre-order conversion for the next-gen console; if it drops post-announcement of the add-on, the confusion is fatal.

The risk of consumer paralysis and brand dilution outweighs the potential short-term revenue. The market will wait for the true next generation.

DECISION: NO-GO
