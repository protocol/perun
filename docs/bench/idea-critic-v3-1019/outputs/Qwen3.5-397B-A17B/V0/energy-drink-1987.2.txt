Steelman: Repositioning a functional tonic as a premium lifestyle beverage creates a new high-margin category by targeting aspirational demographics rather than utility workers.

However, the proposal fails the "fit" and "evidence" checks required for approval. First, the target audience shift is unproven: manual workers tolerate bitter/medicinal tastes for function, whereas students and clubbers prioritize palatability. Without reformulation data confirming the "sweet tonic" profile appeals to Western youth, taste rejection is a near-certain failure mode. Second, the pricing strategy contradicts the "no existing category" risk. Charging a premium for an unknown product category removes the primary incentive (low price) for trial. Consumers rarely pay more for uncertainty. Third, the regulatory flag is treated as a footnote rather than a gate. If caffeine or taurine levels trigger bans in key markets, the supply chain collapses before launch.

The "do nothing" alternative (selling to the existing worker base) has proven revenue. The "cheaper path" is a limited test run with the current formulation before investing in rebranding and carbonation. The proposal skips this validation, demanding a full-scale launch based on assumptions about cultural transferability that history often disproves.

A pre-mortem reveals the most likely failure: the product launches as an expensive, strange-tasting soda that fits neither the energy niche (due to flavor) nor the soft drink niche (due to price and lack of brand equity). The "extreme sports" angle is a marketing narrative, not a product feature, and cannot compensate for a potentially unpalatable core experience.

The kill criteria are missing. There is no defined metric for taste acceptance, no cap on marketing spend before proving unit economics, and no pivot plan if regulators block the specific stimulant blend. Launching without a verified flavor profile for the new demographic and without a regulatory clearance strategy is reckless. The evidence provided supports the existence of the liquid, not the viability of the new business model.

DECISION: NO-GO
