The proposal attempts to launch a global currency backed by a basket of assets, governed by a corporate association, within a messaging app serving two billion users. The steelman argument is that this creates a seamless, low-cost global payment rail leveraging existing network effects to drive financial inclusion.

However, the failure hypothesis is immediate regulatory interdiction. The premise assumes partners will remain committed and regulators will tolerate a private entity issuing a sovereign-scale currency. This contradicts the stated facts: the company is already under intense antitrust and privacy scrutiny, and regulators explicitly view this as a threat to monetary sovereignty. Unlike a standard product launch, this cannot be "moved fast and broken" because the breakage involves national security and financial stability.

The "do nothing" alternative avoids triggering a coordinated global regulatory backlash that could jeopardize the core advertising business. A "better way" would be to limit scope to a single, friendly jurisdiction or act purely as a wallet interface for existing currencies, rather than issuing a new global asset. The current plan ignores the "one-way door" nature of antagonizing major governments; once trust is shattered with regulators, it cannot be regained.

The strongest attack is that the governance model (an association of partners) offers no credible shield against sovereign power. If the US or EU decides this is a threat, they will ban it, causing partners to withdraw instantly due to compliance risk. The proposal lacks a kill criterion for regulatory pushback because the pushback is the primary event, not a secondary risk. Launching now guarantees a high-profile defeat that strengthens the case for breaking up the parent company. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the existential risk of proceeding.

DECISION: NO-GO
