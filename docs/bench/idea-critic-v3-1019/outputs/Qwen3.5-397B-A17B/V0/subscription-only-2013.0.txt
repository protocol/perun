The claim is that replacing perpetual licenses with a subscription-only model maximizes long-term viability despite immediate friction. The steelman: recurring revenue stabilizes cash flow, funds continuous innovation, and aligns vendor-customer incentives better than episodic upgrades.

However, the attack reveals critical fragility. The proposal ignores the "renting tools" objection as mere noise rather than a structural barrier; for creative professionals, tools are capital assets, not operating expenses. Losing access upon payment cessation creates an existential threat to user workflows, driving them to competitors or piracy permanently, not temporarily. The admitted revenue dip during transition is not just a "dip" but a potential liquidity crisis if the churn of perpetual buyers outpaces subscription acquisition. Furthermore, the proposal offers no "do nothing" comparison: maintaining a hybrid model (perpetual + subscription) captures risk-averse users while testing subscription appetite, whereas the proposed "subscription-only" mandate forces a binary choice that alienates the core base immediately.

The pre-mortem shows failure via mass defection: users refuse to trust a model where stopping payment erases their ability to open old files. The cost of delay (waiting to see market reaction) is lower than the cost of immediate, irreversible alienation. There is no kill criterion defined for when the revenue dip becomes fatal, nor a leading metric to track conversion elasticity before burning the perpetual bridge. The evidence provided admits loud objections and revenue risks but proceeds anyway, treating verified customer resistance as an assumption to be overcome rather than a constraint to be respected.

Without a hybrid option, a defined liquidity runway for the revenue dip, or a rollback plan if churn exceeds a specific threshold, the proposal is a high-stakes gamble with undefined stop conditions. It fails the "better-way" test by ignoring the cheaper, safer path of a parallel hybrid launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
