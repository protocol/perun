**Steelman:** A 10-inch device fills the gap between phones and laptops by leveraging an existing app ecosystem at a mass-market price, avoiding the failures of previous complex, expensive tablets.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **"Just a big phone":** Valid concern, but the value isn't screen size alone; it's portability + media consumption + instant-on apps that laptops lack. The "no camera" constraint forces focus on content consumption/creation, not capture, reducing cost.
2.  **"No multitasking":** A deliberate constraint to ensure battery life and simplicity, differentiating it from cluttered PC experiences. Early adopters prioritize fluidity over background processes.
3.  **"Past failures":** Previous tablets failed due to high price, poor OS (desktop ports), and lack of apps. This proposal flips all three: cheap ($499), mobile-native OS, thousands of existing apps.
4.  **"Cheap laptops":** Laptops require keyboards, boot times, and file management. This targets the "couch" use case laptops ignore.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
Fits the strategy of ecosystem expansion. If it fails, it will be because enterprise users demand multitasking/file systems immediately, or the app UI looks stretched and unusable. However, the "do nothing" option cedes the living room to competitors. The cost of delay is losing the definition of this new category.

**Evidence & Metrics:**
The phone's popularity proves the OS and app model work. The $499 price point undercuts all viable competitors while maintaining margin. Success depends on developer uptake for larger screens, not new hardware features.

**Kill Criteria:**
If developer tools for screen scaling aren't ready by launch, the experience fails. Leading metric: App adoption rate specifically optimized for tablet resolution within 90 days.

**Verdict:**
The objections are features, not bugs. Simplicity, price, and ecosystem are the moat. The risks of "missing features" are outweighed by the risk of over-engineering like past failures. The market gap is real; previous attempts missed it due to execution, not concept.

DECISION: GO
