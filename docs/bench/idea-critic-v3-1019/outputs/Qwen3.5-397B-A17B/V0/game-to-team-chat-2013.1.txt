The claim assumes an internal game-dev chat tool can displace entrenched enterprise communication suites without business expertise or a differentiated mechanism. The steelman: the team's unique coordination pain points during game development reveal unmet needs in broader business workflows that generic email and legacy chat ignore, and their remaining venture capital provides a runway to pivot before total failure.

However, the "better-way" analysis exposes fatal flaws. The status quo (enterprise email/chat) is deeply embedded, sticky, and "good enough." Entering this market requires massive sales infrastructure and trust, which a game studio lacks. The "do nothing" option (shutting down) preserves remaining capital for investors rather than burning it on a low-probability pivot into a saturated market. The risk of laying off staff destroys the very institutional knowledge needed to build a complex B2B product, while simultaneously signaling instability to potential enterprise customers.

The pre-mortem is clear: six months post-pivot, the team has burned 40% of remaining cash building features enterprises won't pay for, unable to close deals due to lack of sales channels and brand credibility. The "one-way door" of layoffs is irreversible; once talent is scattered, reassembly is impossible. The cost of delay in shutting down is merely the burn rate, whereas the cost of delay in launching a B2B product is missing the market window entirely while competitors solidify.

Crucially, the proposal lacks evidence that the internal tool solves a *unique* problem for *other* businesses, not just game developers. Assuming "searchable history and integrations" are sufficient differentiators against 2013 incumbents is an unsupported claim. The team has no verified path to distribution or monetization in the B2B sector. The strongest attack—that the team is solving a problem they understand (game dev) for a market they don't (general enterprise)—survives because the proposal offers no mechanism to bridge this gap.

Without a validated customer discovery process, a specific niche beachhead (beyond "businesses"), or a sales strategy, this is a gamble with remaining investor funds on a hypothesis that contradicts the team's core competencies.

DECISION: NO-GO
