The claim is that replacing perpetual licenses with a subscription-only model will secure the company's future despite immediate customer backlash and revenue dips. The steelman is that recurring revenue creates a predictable valuation multiple and funds continuous innovation, preventing the "feast or famine" cycle of major version releases while aligning vendor success with user retention.

However, the attack reveals critical fragility. The proposal ignores the "rentership fatigue" of 2013, where professional creatives viewed tools as capital assets, not operating expenses. Forcing this shift risks a mass exodus to competitors still offering ownership or to piracy, as the "loss of access" upon non-payment is a non-starter for studios requiring guaranteed archival access to old projects. The assumption that revenue will merely "dip" rather than collapse underestimates the churn of price-sensitive freelancers and small studios who cannot absorb indefinite monthly costs. Furthermore, the "continuous updates" value proposition is weak if users feel forced to pay just to maintain current functionality without significant new features.

The "do nothing" alternative (hybrid model) preserves the high-margin perpetual cash cow while growing the subscription segment organically. A "cheaper way" is to offer a discounted "legacy perpetual" tier for resistant users while pushing subscriptions as the default for new buyers, reducing friction without burning bridges. The pre-mortem shows a scenario where 30% of the installed base cancels or pirates the software within six months, causing a liquidity crisis that the projected recurring revenue cannot fill fast enough. The cost of delay is low; waiting to gauge market sentiment or offering a hybrid transition reduces the risk of irreversible brand damage.

Without a guaranteed bridge loan to cover the projected revenue dip and a concrete retention strategy for the "ownership" segment, this all-or-nothing approach is a gamble with the company's solvency. The failure mode is not just reduced growth, but active contraction and loss of market share to rivals who respect the user's desire for asset ownership.

DECISION: NO-GO
