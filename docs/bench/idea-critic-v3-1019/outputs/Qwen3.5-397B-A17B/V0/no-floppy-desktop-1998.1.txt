The claim is that a radical simplification of the PC, removing legacy ports and drives in favor of USB and CD-ROM, will reverse financial losses and shrinking market share despite high compatibility risks.

**Steelman:** By eliminating costly legacy components and targeting the emerging internet-centric home user with a distinct, friendly aesthetic, the company can achieve higher margins per unit and capture a niche ignored by competitors focused on enterprise compatibility.

**Attack:** The failure mode is immediate market rejection. Buyers expect diskette drives for file transfer and installation; reviewers will pan the lack of legacy ports as unusable for existing peripherals. With only two years of losses remaining before potential insolvency, a single product miss is fatal. The ecosystem of USB peripherals is not yet mature enough to support a "port-less" machine. If the core audience cannot plug in their current printer or scanner, they will not buy this computer, regardless of its color or price.

**Better Way:** A transitional model retaining the diskette drive and one legacy port while introducing USB would mitigate compatibility backlash while still testing the new form factor. Alternatively, focusing on the professional market where the brand still holds sway might stabilize cash flow before attempting a consumer revolution.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This strategy assumes the market is ready to abandon floppy disks entirely in 1998. If this assumption is false, inventory will pile up, and the company will face liquidation. The cost of delay is continued loss of share, but the cost of error is total collapse.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders from key retail partners do not cover 50% of initial production costs within 60 days, or if early reviews cite "lack of floppy drive" as a dealbreaker in more than 30% of major publications, the project must be halted immediately to preserve remaining capital.

The risk of total failure due to ecosystem unreadiness is too high given the company's fragile financial state. The proposal ignores the transitional nature of the market.

DECISION: NO-GO
