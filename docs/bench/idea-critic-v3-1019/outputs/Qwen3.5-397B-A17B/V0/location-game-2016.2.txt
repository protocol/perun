The claim posits that a small studio can successfully partner with a famous franchise to launch a mass-market, location-based AR game, overcoming historical niche limitations, server capacity constraints, and safety liabilities.

**Steelman:** Leveraging an existing, devoted community's map data and a major IP's brand recognition creates a unique barrier to entry that justifies the infrastructure risk, converting a niche mechanic into a cultural phenomenon if safety and scale are managed.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Infrastructure Collapse:** The proposal admits servers only handle a "small audience." A famous franchise implies instant mass adoption. Without a pre-committed, elastic cloud architecture and load-testing plan, the launch will result in immediate downtime, destroying trust and revenue. *Action: Require proof of scalable infrastructure contract before approval.*
2.  **Safety Liability:** "Walking while staring" invites injury and lawsuits. Unlike niche hobbies, mass adoption guarantees incidents. *Action: Mandate legal review of liability caps and in-game safety warnings.*
3.  **Revenue Fragility:** Free-to-play relies on "whales." If the player base is casual walkers rather than collectors, ARPU (Average Revenue Per User) may not cover server costs for non-paying users.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves the stable, modest base. A "web-based prototype" could test scale without full app commitment. However, the IP partnership window is likely time-sensitive; delaying may lose the license.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
Assuming failure: The game launches, servers crash within hour one, a high-profile accident occurs, and the IP holder sues for brand damage. The "niche" history of location games suggests the "mass market" assumption is the weakest link. However, the combination of a *famous* IP and *existing* map data creates a specific convergence not present in previous failed niche attempts.

**Decision:**
The risk of server failure is existential but solvable with capital. The safety risk is manageable via UX. The "niche" constraint is broken by the IP's reach. The cost of delay (losing the IP deal) outweighs the technical risks, provided the studio secures immediate infrastructure funding. The proposal lacks the specific infrastructure plan, making the current iteration dangerous, but the strategic opportunity is too significant to reject entirely if the gap is filled. However, as presented, it lacks the critical "how" for scaling.

DECISION: NO-GO
