The claim is that a $299–$349 console with a built-in touchscreen, branded as a successor to a hit motion-control system, will reverse sales declines despite weak third-party support and an imminent rival launch.

**Steelman:** A distinct, moderately priced hybrid device leveraging existing brand equity can capture casual gamers fleeing to tablets by offering a dedicated, living-room-friendly experience with unique touch inputs that phones cannot replicate.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Identity Crisis:** Branding it as a successor ("short suffix") while selling at a mid-tier price with year-old HD graphics invites the exact confusion feared: consumers may view it as an accessory rather than a new generation, cannibalizing the installed base without expanding it.
2.  **Software Void:** Hardware novelty fails without software. Lukewarm publisher interest and a thin launch lineup are fatal when rivals offer robust ecosystems. A "better way" is to delay until key first-party titles are ready or secure exclusive indie partnerships, rather than rushing to beat a hardware cycle.
3.  **Timing:** Launching immediately before rivals release true next-gen hardware positions this product as obsolete on day one. The "do nothing" option (supporting the current console with cheaper peripherals) preserves margin better than a likely flopped hardware launch.

**Pre-mortem:** The product fails because it satisfies neither hardcore fans (weak graphics) nor casuals (too expensive compared to tablets, too complex). The "touchscreen gimmick" becomes a battery-draining burden, and the confusing branding prevents clear marketing messaging.

**Verdict:** The proposal relies on hardware differentiation to solve a software and ecosystem problem. The pricing strategy places it in a no-man's-land between cheap mobile devices and powerful home consoles. Without a guaranteed "killer app" lineup or clarified branding to distinguish it from an accessory, the risk of market rejection is extreme. The "short suffix" naming convention specifically exacerbates the identified risk of consumer confusion.

**Dissent Ledger:** Proponents argue unique input methods create new markets; however, history shows input novelty alone rarely sustains a platform without deep software support.

**Remaining Risk:** High probability of brand dilution and inventory write-downs within 12 months.

DECISION: NO-GO
