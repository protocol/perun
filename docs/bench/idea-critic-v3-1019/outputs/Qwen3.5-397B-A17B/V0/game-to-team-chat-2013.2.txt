The claim rests on the assumption that an internal game-dev chat tool can displace entrenched enterprise communication solutions without business expertise. While the studio has remaining capital, pivoting from a B2C game to a B2B infrastructure play represents a fundamental strategy break. The "do nothing" alternative (shutting down entirely) preserves capital but yields zero return; this pivot risks burning the remaining venture money on a product with no distribution channel and no sales team.

The strongest attack is the market fit mismatch: tools built for creative, technical game developers often lack the governance, security, and compliance features required by general enterprises. Furthermore, the team's lack of business-software experience is a critical gap; building software is not selling it. The risk of morale collapse post-layoff could cripple the rapid iteration needed to productize the tool. However, the existing tool provides a tangible asset (the "steelman": a functional, integrated messaging system) that reduces initial build time compared to starting from scratch. The cost of delay is high, as remaining funds deplete monthly, but a rushed launch without a go-to-market strategy guarantees failure.

The pre-mortem reveals failure via inability to acquire paying customers despite a working product, due to lack of sales infrastructure. This is a two-way door only if the pivot is treated as a capped experiment. Without a defined kill criteria (e.g., "acquire 10 paying beta customers within 90 days"), the project becomes a money pit. The proposal lacks specific metrics for success or a plan to address the sales gap. Yet, given the game is already unprofitable and funds remain, the option value of attempting a pivot exceeds the certainty of immediate closure, provided the scope is strictly limited to validating market demand before full commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
