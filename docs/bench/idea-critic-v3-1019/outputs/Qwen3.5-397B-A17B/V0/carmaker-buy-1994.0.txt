The claim is that acquiring a loss-making British volume carmaker for £800m will successfully broaden a premium German brand's range into small cars and 4WDs. The steelman: this is the fastest route to immediate 4WD capacity and small-car volume, bypassing years of internal R&D.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current proposal. Since this partner supplies "much" of the engineering, their exit creates an immediate capability gap that £800m investment cannot instantly fill, risking product continuity. Third, brand overlap suggests internal cannibalization rather than net growth. Finally, low productivity in British plants implies the heavy investment required will be consumed by operational fixes rather than new model development, delaying the strategic goal.

The "do nothing" or "build internally" alternatives, while slower, avoid inheriting structural losses, hostile partners, and productivity black holes. The cost of delay is high, but the cost of this specific acquisition appears higher due to the high probability of immediate engineering collapse and continued financial drain. The proposal lacks a confirmed retention agreement with the Japanese partner or a specific productivity turnaround plan, making the £800m outlay a gamble on fixing known, active failures rather than buying proven capacity.

Without resolving the Japanese partner's withdrawal threat and defining a credible path to profitability for the loss-making units, the acquisition threatens to drain resources from the core premium business without delivering the intended volume or capability. The risks are not hypothetical; they are stated current conditions.
