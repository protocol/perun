The claim proposes partnering with a famous franchise to launch a free-to-play, location-based creature-collecting game, monetizing via in-app purchases despite a history of niche market performance and limited server capacity.

**Steelman:** Leveraging an established IP converts a niche mechanic into a mass-market phenomenon, where viral social proof overcomes server limits through phased rollout, and safety concerns are mitigated by community norms rather than technical guards.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Infrastructure Collapse:** The studio's servers handled a "small audience." A famous franchise brings millions instantly. Without a proven, auto-scaling cloud architecture (not mentioned), the launch will result in immediate, reputation-killing downtime.
2.  **Safety Liability:** "Walking while staring" creates tangible physical risk. Without explicit insurance coverage or gameplay mechanics that penalize unsafe movement (e.g., speed locks), the studio faces lawsuits that could bankrupt a small spin-out.
3.  **Revenue Fragility:** Free-to-play relies on <5% of users paying. If the "devoted" base does not convert at scale, operational costs for map maintenance and licensing fees will exceed revenue.
4.  **Alternatives:**
    *   *Do Nothing:* Preserve current stability; avoid licensing debt.
    *   *Web-First Demo:* Test IP appeal without app store friction or heavy server load.
    *   *Acquisition:* Sell the tech to the IP holder rather than operating the game.

**Pre-mortem:** The game launches, servers crash within hour one, news cycles highlight player injuries, and the licensing agreement triggers penalties for downtime, forcing shutdown.

**Verdict:** The proposal ignores the catastrophic mismatch between current infrastructure and projected load. It treats safety as a minor note rather than a primary existential risk. The revenue model is unproven at this scale for this specific genre. The "famous franchise" element increases stakes without addressing the foundational cracks in the studio's operational capacity.

**Kill Criteria Missing:** No cap on initial user intake, no defined safety protocol, and no fallback if server load exceeds 10x current capacity.

DECISION: NO-GO
