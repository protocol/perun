The proposal attempts to disrupt a duopoly dominated by an entrenched American incumbent with a proven, low-risk product. The core claim rests on introducing unproven digital fly-by-wire technology to commercial aviation to achieve commonality and efficiency.

**Steelman:** A technologically superior, common-cockpit narrow-body will reduce operator training costs and fuel burn enough to overcome pilot resistance to unproven automation and undercut the incumbent's pricing power via state-backed financing.

**Attack:**
1.  **Technology Risk:** Civil aviation is conservatism-personified. Introducing the first civil fly-by-wire system creates a massive certification hurdle and potential pilot union backlash. If regulators delay approval due to safety fears, the project bleeds cash without revenue.
2.  **Market Dynamics:** The incumbent can engage in predatory pricing, leveraging their established cash flow to starve a non-profitable consortium. With only a few launch customers, the loss of a single order jeopardizes the entire business case.
3.  **Financial Fragility:** Relying on government aid introduces political risk; a change in administration could cut funding mid-development. The consortium lacks the organic profitability to self-fund overruns, which are guaranteed in first-of-kind tech integration.

**Better Way:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid the catastrophic risk of betting the entire consortium on an unproven architecture in a price-sensitive market.
*   **Incremental Upgrade:** Develop a conventional control narrow-body with high commonality to existing wide-bodies, reducing pilot retraining needs without the regulatory lightning rod of fly-by-wire.
*   **Niche Focus:** Target a specific regional gap rather than a head-on assault on the 150-seat staple.

**Pre-mortem:** The aircraft is certified three years late due to fly-by-wire software validation issues. Pilots refuse to fly it without extensive (and expensive) simulator time. The incumbent drops prices by 15%. Two launch customers cancel. The consortium runs out of state aid and collapses.

**Verdict:** The combination of unproven critical technology, aggressive incumbent counter-moves, and fragile financing creates a failure mode that threatens the consortium's existence. The "commonality" benefit does not outweigh the existential risk of being the first to fail with fly-by-wire in this segment.

DECISION: NO-GO
