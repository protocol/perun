The claim that this tool should replace email via a 100,000-user preview fails the steelman test: the strongest defensible version is that it serves as a niche collaboration layer, not an email successor. The proposal ignores fatal friction points. First, network effects are negative at this scale; if invitations are scarce and contacts aren't present, the utility is zero, guaranteeing high churn. Second, performance issues in current browsers mean the core "real-time" promise breaks for many, creating a frustrating first impression that is hard to recover from. Third, lacking interoperability with existing email isolates users rather than merging workflows, directly contradicting the value proposition of "merging" communication.

The "do nothing" or "pilot smaller" alternatives are superior. A 100,000-user blast amplifies confusion when the use case is undefined. A better path is a closed beta of 500 users within single organizations where network density is guaranteed, allowing the team to fix performance and clarify the interface before exposing structural flaws to a massive audience. The cost of delay is low; email isn't going anywhere this quarter. However, the cost of a failed public launch is brand damage and entrenched user perception that the product is "broken" or "confusing."

The pre-mortem is clear: the preview launches, users invite friends who don't join or can't use the slow interface, early adopters abandon it within 48 hours, and the narrative solidifies as "another failed email killer." There is no kill criterion mentioned to stop the bleed if engagement drops below a viable threshold. Without interoperability, performance fixes, and a clear use case, expanding now is betting on a broken foundation. The evidence provided (confusion, scarcity, slowness, isolation) overwhelmingly suggests the product is not ready for a 100,000-user stress test intended to validate replacement viability.

DECISION: NO-GO
