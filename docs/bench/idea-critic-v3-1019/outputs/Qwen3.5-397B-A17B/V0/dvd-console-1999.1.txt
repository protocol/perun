The claim is that adding DVD playback and backward compatibility while pricing near rivals will secure market leadership despite immediate hardware losses. The steelman: this creates an unbeatable value proposition (game console + affordable DVD player) that drives rapid adoption, subsidizing future software profits.

However, the "better-way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the "price near rival" constraint combined with "sell at a loss." If chip yields are low, supply will be severely constrained. Selling a high-demand, loss-leading unit at a low price during a supply crunch maximizes financial bleed without maximizing market share, as you cannot meet demand anyway. A "do nothing" or "premium launch" alternative suggests pricing higher initially to capture early adopters willing to pay for scarcity, mitigating losses until yields improve.

The pre-mortem assumes the rival launching a year earlier has already locked up key third-party developers. If the new architecture is indeed hard to program, and developers are already committed to the incumbent, the library will be thin at launch. Without a strong game library, the DVD feature becomes the primary selling point, positioning the device as a cheap DVD player rather than a premier gaming destination, eroding the brand's premium status.

The strongest attack survived is the concern over developer friction; history shows powerful tools can overcome initial complexity if the install base is guaranteed. However, the install base depends on supply, which depends on yields. The current plan ties low price to low supply, creating a "loss spiral" where every unit sold deepens the deficit without guaranteeing market dominance if the rival's head start proves insurmountable.

The proposal lacks a specific kill criterion for the hardware loss per unit. Without a cap on total subsidy exposure or a clear path to profitability via software attachment rates within the first year, the financial risk is unbounded. The plan assumes volume will solve the yield and cost issues, but if the rival's earlier launch captures the critical mass of developers, the volume may never materialize.

DECISION: NO-GO
