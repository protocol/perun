Steelman: Repositioning a functional tonic as a premium lifestyle beverage creates a high-margin category where none exists, leveraging existing IP to capture early-adopter spend.

However, the proposal fails the "fit" and "evidence" checks required for approval. First, the target audience shift is unvalidated. The product's current success relies on manual workers and drivers who prioritize utility and stamina. Students and clubbers prioritize taste, social signaling, and mixability. A "sweet tonic" adapted from an industrial context may clash violently with the palates of a leisure market accustomed to sugary sodas or bitter mixers. The proposal admits consumers "may dislike the taste" but offers no sensory data or concept testing to prove this specific demographic will accept it.

Second, the pricing strategy contradicts the value proposition for a non-existent category. Launching a small can at a price "well above colas" requires established brand equity or a proven functional benefit that consumers urgently desire. In 1987, without an existing "energy drink" framework in the region, consumers have no reference point for why this small can is worth a premium. They will likely compare it directly to colas (cheaper, larger, trusted taste) and reject it as poor value.

Third, the regulatory risk is treated as a footnote rather than a gate. If caffeine and taurine restrictions exist in the home country or key expansion markets, the formulation could be illegal or require costly reformulation that alters the taste further. Launching before resolving regulatory status invites immediate recall or fines.

The "do nothing" or "pilot" alternative is superior. Instead of a full launch, the company should run a limited regional test with the specific target demographic to validate taste acceptance and price elasticity. Alternatively, license the brand for a co-packaged mixer rather than a standalone premium SKU.

As proposed, this is a high-cost bet on unverified assumptions about taste preference and category creation. The risks of product rejection and regulatory blockage are too high to approve a full launch without prior validation data.

DECISION: NO-GO
