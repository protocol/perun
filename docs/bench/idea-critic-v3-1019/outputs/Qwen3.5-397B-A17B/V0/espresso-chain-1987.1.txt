**Steelman:** Acquiring the roaster secures vertical integration, allowing the former director to control supply quality and margins while rebranding existing profitable bars to establish immediate brand equity before capital-intensive expansion.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on a fragile assumption: that a niche, high-priced product can sustain rapid geographic expansion in a market dominated by cheap alternatives. The "heavy capital" requirement for distant cities creates a cash-flow trap; if new locations fail to gain traction quickly due to cultural resistance to expensive espresso, the debt service to investors could bankrupt the entire operation, including the original successful bars. Furthermore, renaming existing bars risks alienating their current local customer base before the new brand proves its value elsewhere. The plan treats "expansion" as the goal rather than a validated demand, ignoring the possibility that the roaster's value lies solely in its current six-store footprint, not as a national franchise engine.

**Better Way:** Instead of immediate multi-city expansion, the buyer should acquire the roaster and rebrand only the existing bars. The "expansion" phase should be conditional on achieving specific same-store sales growth and margin targets over 12 months. This reduces the capital raise requirement and tests the brand's transferability without betting the company on unproven markets.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed with the current "expand to other cities" mandate. The risk of over-leveraging on an unproven national demand for high-priced espresso is too high given the 1987 market context. The proposal lacks a phased approach, demanding full commitment to a speculative growth strategy before validating the core rebranding success. The absence of a "stay small" or "phase one only" option makes this a reckless bet on investor patience in a niche market.

DECISION: NO-GO
