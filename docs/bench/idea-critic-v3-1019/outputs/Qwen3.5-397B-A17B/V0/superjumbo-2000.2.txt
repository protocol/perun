The claim assumes hub-and-spoke congestion will persist and that airlines prioritize seat count over frequency. The steelman is that a 550-seat double-decker captures unmatched efficiency on saturated trunk routes where slot constraints prevent adding more flights.

However, the "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the market premise. The industry is shifting toward point-to-point travel using smaller, efficient twin-engine jets, bypassing congested hubs entirely. Building the largest possible aircraft doubles down on the hub model precisely as the market fragments. The rival's potential to stretch its existing jumbo offers a lower-capital alternative to capture any remaining demand for size without the $10.7 billion development risk.

The pre-mortem is clear: if fuel prices rise or slot constraints ease through infrastructure updates rather than capacity increases, the aircraft becomes too expensive to operate per seat compared to frequent smaller flights. The requirement for widespread airport modifications creates a adoption barrier that limits the addressable market, creating a vicious cycle where low volume drives up unit costs.

The strongest attack survived is the existence of 50 firm orders. Yet, these orders come from only six customers, likely heavily incentivized, and do not guarantee broader market viability. The risk of budget overrun is extreme given the novel double-deck engineering and airport modification dependencies. The "do nothing" option allows the company to wait for the point-to-point trend to mature before committing capital, preserving liquidity.

This proposal bets the company on a static view of airport congestion and a centralized travel model that is already eroding. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of building a white elephant. The kill criteria for this project would likely trigger immediately upon the first sign of further market fragmentation or fuel price volatility. Without a verified path to profitability in a point-to-point world, the risk profile is unacceptable.

DECISION: NO-GO
