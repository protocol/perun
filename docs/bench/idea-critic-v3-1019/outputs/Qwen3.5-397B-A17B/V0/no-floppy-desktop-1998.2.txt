The claim is that a radical simplification of the personal computer, removing legacy storage and ports in favor of USB and internet connectivity, will reverse financial losses and shrinking market share despite high execution risk.

**Skeptic:** The premise relies on unproven consumer readiness to abandon floppy disks and existing peripherals. With two years of losses, the company lacks the runway for a market education failure. If USB peripheral adoption lags or users cannot migrate data easily, the product becomes unusable for its target audience, accelerating the decline. The "translucent case" is a cosmetic differentiator that does not solve the functional gap.

**Better-Way:** A safer alternative is a transitional model retaining the floppy drive and legacy ports while introducing USB, allowing gradual migration. Another option is focusing on the profitable professional segment with incremental updates rather than betting the entire turnaround on a risky consumer redesign. "Do nothing" preserves cash but guarantees continued share erosion.

**Kill-Criteria:** This is a one-way door; once legacy ports are removed, they cannot be added to shipped units without a full redesign. The cost of delay is losing the holiday season window, but the cost of error is bankruptcy. The proposal lacks a verified backup plan if peripheral compatibility blocks sales. There is no defined metric to halt production if early reviewer feedback highlights the missing drive as a dealbreaker.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the product fails because schools and businesses cannot transfer files from older machines, and reviewers pan the lack of a standard drive. The company burns its remaining cash on inventory it cannot sell.

**Verdict:** The proposal bets the entire company on an ecosystem shift that has not yet fully occurred. While the vision is coherent, the financial fragility makes the "no room for a miss" constraint fatal when combined with unverified user behavior. The removal of essential, ubiquitous functionality (floppy) without a mature alternative (USB peripherals) creates an unacceptable adoption barrier for a company that cannot afford a miss.

DECISION: NO-GO
