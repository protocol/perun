**Steelman:** Leveraging an existing, devoted community and user-generated map to launch a proven franchise mechanic creates immediate market fit with low content costs.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Infrastructure Collapse:** The proposal admits servers only handle a "small audience." A famous franchise triggers massive, instant load. Without a verified scaling plan, the launch will crash, destroying reputation permanently. *Alternative:* Soft-launch in one city to stress-test before global release.
2.  **Safety Liability:** "Walking while staring" invites lawsuits and public backlash if injuries occur. The proposal lists this as a risk but offers no mitigation (e.g., speed locks, warnings). *Alternative:* Delay launch until safety protocols are engineered and tested.
3.  **Revenue Fragility:** Relying on a "small share" of payers is dangerous if the user base is niche. If the famous franchise fans do not convert to location-based gamers, revenue fails to cover server costs. *Alternative:* Secure upfront licensing guarantees rather than pure revenue share.
4.  **Niche Ceiling:** Location games have historically stayed niche. Scaling to "famous" levels requires breaking this ceiling without evidence it can be done.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, servers buckle under day-one traffic, causing data loss. A high-profile injury lawsuit gains traction due to lack of safety features. The niche audience proves too small to sustain server costs, and the famous franchise partner terminates the deal due to brand damage.

**Fit & Evidence:** The proposal relies on assumed synergies between "devoted players" and "famous franchise fans" without data proving overlap. It treats known technical limits (small server capacity) as manageable rather than fatal blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed unless: (1) A concrete, funded infrastructure scaling plan is presented; (2) Specific safety features are defined and built; (3) A soft-launch strategy replaces the immediate global rollout.

**Verdict:** The current plan ignores critical, known failure points (server capacity, safety) that guarantee catastrophic failure if the game succeeds. Approving this "as proposed" is negligence.

DECISION: NO-GO
