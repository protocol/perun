Steelman: Acquiring the third-most-visited site instantly grants massive distribution, converting raw traffic into ad revenue through scale rather than immediate profitability.

However, the claim fails the evidence and fit tests. First, the financial premise is unsupported: the target's revenue is explicitly "small" relative to the $3.6 billion price tag. This implies a payback period so distant it relies entirely on the assumption that "record high" stock valuations will persist indefinitely. If the market corrects, the acquisition currency collapses while the debt or equity dilution remains, destroying shareholder value.

Second, the revenue model contradicts the asset's nature. Advertisers actively dislike "amateur content" due to brand safety risks and low conversion intent. Monetizing millions of uncurated personal pages requires expensive moderation infrastructure not mentioned in the proposal. Without it, CPMs (cost per mille) will remain too low to justify the purchase price.

Third, the integration risk is critical. Changing terms of service to force advertising on "page owners" who expect free, ad-free hosting invites mass exodus. If users migrate elsewhere, the acquired traffic evaporates, leaving the portal with an overpriced, empty shell. The proposal offers no retention strategy or pilot data proving users will tolerate ads.

Finally, the timing is dangerous. Buying at a "record high" market peak using overvalued stock is a classic value-destruction mechanism. The "do nothing" alternative or a cheaper, organic growth strategy avoids betting the company on a bubble. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the risk of immediate overpayment.

The strongest attack survived is the scale argument, but it fails because the specific mechanism to monetize that scale (ads on amateur pages) is fundamentally broken by advertiser preferences and user backlash. The kill criteria are clear: if advertiser CPMs for user-generated content do not match professional portal rates within six months, the thesis fails. Given the current facts, the risk profile is unacceptable.

DECISION: NO-GO
