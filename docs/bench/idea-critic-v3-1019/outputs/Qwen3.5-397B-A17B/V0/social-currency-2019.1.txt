The claim is that a global digital currency backed by a basket of assets, governed by an association, and integrated into a messaging app with two billion users can launch successfully despite regulatory, privacy, and partner risks. The steelman is that this creates a seamless, low-cost global payment rail leveraging existing trust in the basket's assets and the platform's reach.

However, the proposal fails the fit check against the stated context. The company is under active scrutiny for privacy scandals and antitrust issues. Launching a financial instrument that rivals monetary sovereignty while under such scrutiny is not a risk; it is a near-certain trigger for immediate, coordinated global regulatory blockade. The "association" governance model does not neutralize the threat perceived by central banks when the underlying distribution channel is a single, distrusted tech giant.

The pre-mortem is clear: regulators in the US and EU freeze the project before launch, citing systemic risk and lack of trust. Partners, fearing secondary regulatory fallout, withdraw. The cost of delay is zero compared to the cost of a very public, catastrophic failure that cements antitrust actions. The "do nothing" or "wait for regulatory clarity" alternative is superior because it preserves capital and avoids creating a focal point for global regulatory hostility.

The strongest attack survived is the idea that the asset basket provides stability. While true financially, it is irrelevant politically; the threat is not the currency's stability, but the platform's control over its distribution. No kill criteria or leading metrics can mitigate a sovereign ban enacted on day one. The proposal assumes regulatory permission is a hurdle to be cleared rather than a fundamental blocker given the current political climate.

This plan commits real money and reputation to a venture with a high probability of immediate prohibition. It ignores the verified state of regulatory hostility.

DECISION: NO-GO
