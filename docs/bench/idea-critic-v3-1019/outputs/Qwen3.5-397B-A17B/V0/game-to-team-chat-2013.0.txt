**Steelman:** Pivoting transforms a failing game asset into a viable B2B messaging platform by leveraging existing technology before venture capital depletes.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The proposal assumes technical fit equals market fit. In 2013, the enterprise space is crowded with entrenched giants (email, legacy chat) and emerging well-funded rivals. The team lacks sales channels, compliance frameworks, and B2B product experience. "Searchable history" is a feature, not a moat.

*Alternative 1 (Do Nothing/Status Quo):* Continue burning cash on the game. **Verdict:** Fatal. Money runs out; zero probability of turnaround given current traction.
*Alternative 2 (Sell IP):* Attempt to sell the game engine or tool internally. **Verdict:** Low likelihood; niche assets rarely fetch life-saving sums without a bidding war.
*Alternative 3 (Pivot):* High risk of failure due to inexperience, but the only path with non-zero upside given the remaining capital.

**Pre-mortem:** The pivot fails because the team builds a "game dev tool" rather than an "enterprise tool," ignoring security, admin controls, and sales cycles. Morale collapses post-layoffs, causing key engineers to quit before the product ships. Investors refuse follow-on funding without a commercial lead.

**Fit & Evidence:** The constraint is "most venture money is left." This provides a runway for a pivot but not for prolonged experimentation. The internal tool's existence proves technical capability but not commercial viability. The risk of layoffs is real but secondary to the certainty of insolvency if the game continues.

**Decision Logic:**
This is a one-way door with high uncertainty. However, the alternative is a guaranteed slow death. The "no business experience" risk is mitigated by the urgency: they must learn fast or die. The proposal correctly identifies that the game is not working. While the odds are low, they are strictly better than zero. The capital on hand allows for a time-boxed attempt to find product-market fit in a new sector.

**Kill Criteria:** If no paying pilot customer is secured within 6 months, or if 50% of the remaining cash is burned without a clear path to $10k MRR, the company must dissolve immediately.

DECISION: GO
