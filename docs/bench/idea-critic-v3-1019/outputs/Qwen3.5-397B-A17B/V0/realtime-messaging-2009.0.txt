The proposal fails the steelman test: it claims a "replacement for email" while admitting zero interoperability with existing email. In 2009, email's value is its universal network; a walled garden with 100,000 users cannot replace it. The "confusing interface" and "slow performance" indicate the product is not ready for a public preview, let alone a strategic pivot.

The network effect risk is fatal. If invitations are scarce and utility requires contacts to be present, growth will stall immediately. Users invited to a slow, confusing tool that cannot message their actual colleagues will churn, creating negative word-of-mouth that poisons future expansion. The "do nothing" alternative (improving current tools) or a "private beta" (fixing speed and clarity before any public invite) are superior options.

The pre-mortem is clear: launching now results in a public failure where users reject the tool as a buggy, isolated toy. The cost of delay is low; the cost of a botched launch is the permanent loss of credibility for this product line. The kill criteria are already met by the reported risks: confusion, slowness, and isolation are showstoppers.

There is no evidence that 100,000 users will tolerate a broken value proposition. The assumption that "preview" excuses poor performance is unsupported; first impressions define category entrants. Without interoperability, the tool is not a communication platform but a niche editor.

Strongest attack survived: None. The proposal collapses under the weight of its own admitted deficits. It attempts to solve a network problem with a product that lacks network viability.

Dissent ledger: The scale (100k) amplifies existing flaws rather than validating the concept.
Remaining risk: Total reputational damage and wasted engineering capacity on a non-starter.

DECISION: NO-GO
