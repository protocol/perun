**Steelman:** Acquiring the US portal instantly grants massive scale, bypassing years of organic growth to challenge market leaders, leveraging the telecom's capital to create a global Spanish-English gateway.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on valuation, strategic fit, and execution risk.
1.  **Valuation:** Paying $12.5 billion for a traffic lagger implies a speculative multiple disconnected from current revenue. You are buying past hype, not future cash flow.
2.  **Strategic Fit:** A Spanish-language telecom provider gains little from a general US portal where it lacks brand equity. The audiences do not naturally overlap enough to justify the premium.
3.  **Execution:** Integrating distinct cultures across continents while managing volatile stock currency for payment creates a high probability of value destruction. The "global portal" concept ignores the trend toward specialized, local content.
4.  **Opportunity Cost:** $12.5 billion could build infrastructure or acquire smaller, targeted assets with clear synergies rather than a bloated, declining generalist platform.

**Better Way:** Do nothing. Organic expansion or targeted partnerships in specific US Hispanic markets offer lower risk and clearer ROI than a massive, undifferentiated acquisition.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-deal, the stock has collapsed due to integration failures and realized overpayment. The portal continues losing share to nimbler rivals, and the core telecom business is distracted by managing a foreign subsidiary it does not understand.

**Kill Criteria:** If the target's traffic growth is negative year-over-year, or if the price-to-revenue multiple exceeds industry leaders without a clear path to margin improvement, the deal must be rejected.

**Verdict:** The risks overwhelmingly outweigh the speculative benefits. The price is too high for a secondary player, and the strategic logic is weak.

DECISION: NO-GO
