The proposal fails the "better-way" and "fit" tests immediately. The pricing model is fundamentally broken: charging a monthly subscription *plus* full price for individual games creates a double-dip friction that destroys value compared to owning a console (one-time hardware cost, lower game prices) or competing services (subscription includes library). Users will not pay a premium for the inconvenience of streaming while retaining the cost burden of ownership.

The "skeptic" hat highlights fatal assumptions. First, it assumes internet infrastructure in 2019 is universally sufficient for lag-free 4K streaming; in reality, variable latency ruins gameplay, creating a churn risk that no amount of marketing can fix. Second, it assumes publishers will grant exclusives to a company with a documented reputation for terminating products. Studios will not bet their IP on a platform likely to be sunset within three years, leaving the catalog weak.

The "kill-criteria" analysis shows no viable path to success under these constraints. The cost of building game studios is massive and slow, delaying time-to-value while burning cash. The "do nothing" or "partner-only" alternative is superior: license the tech without the capital expenditure of content creation, or fix the pricing model first. Launching with this specific economic structure and reputational handicap guarantees low adoption and high churn.

The strongest attack survived is actually a failure of the proposal itself: there is no scenario where users accept "rent plus buy" pricing for an inferior, lag-prone delivery method when superior, cheaper alternatives exist. The risk of reputational damage from another failed product launch outweighs the potential first-mover advantage, which is nullified by the flawed business model.

This plan requires a complete strategic pivot on pricing and content strategy before any capital is committed. As proposed, it is a capital destruction event.

DECISION: NO-GO
