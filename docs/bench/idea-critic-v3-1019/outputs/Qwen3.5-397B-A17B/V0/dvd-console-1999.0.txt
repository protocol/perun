Steelman: Bundling DVD playback and backward compatibility creates a superior value proposition that accelerates market adoption, offsetting initial hardware losses through software licensing and ecosystem lock-in, despite supply and development hurdles.

**Skeptic Analysis**: The claim relies on the assumption that consumers value DVD playback enough to drive console choice over price or availability. If chip yields remain low, supply constraints could cede market share to the earlier rival launch. The "loss leader" strategy fails if software attach rates do not meet projections quickly enough to cover the hardware subsidy. However, the trend toward home theater integration supports the DVD hypothesis.

**Better-Way Alternatives**: 
1. *Do Nothing*: Launch a game-only box. Risk: Obsolescence as DVD players become standard; missed opportunity to be the living room hub.
2. *Premium Pricing*: Price higher to cover costs. Risk: Slows adoption, reduces install base, hurts long-term software revenue.
3. *Drop Backward Compatibility*: Lowers cost/complexity. Risk: Alienates existing user base, fractures ecosystem.
The proposed path beats these by maximizing the install base, which is the primary revenue driver for this business model.

**Fit & Pre-mortem**: Fits the vision of dominating the living room. Failure scenario: Supply shortages at launch allow the rival to establish an unassailable lead. Mitigation: Aggressive marketing of the "all-in-one" value to create demand that outweighs supply constraints temporarily. The cost of delay is losing the "next-gen" narrative to the rival.

**Kill Criteria**: 
- If chip yields remain below 60% after two quarters, pivot to a reduced-feature SKU.
- If software attach rate is <2.5 titles per console within six months, halt hardware subsidies.
- Leading metric: Weekly sell-through rate vs. rival launch windows.

The strongest attack is the financial risk of selling hardware at a loss. This survives because the historical data for this market shows profits are almost exclusively derived from software royalties, not hardware margins. The loss is a calculated customer acquisition cost. The rival's head start is a risk, but the feature gap (DVD/Backward Compatibility) provides a differentiator strong enough to overcome it.

DECISION: GO
