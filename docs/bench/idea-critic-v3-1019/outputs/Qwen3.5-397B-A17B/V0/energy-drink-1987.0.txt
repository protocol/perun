**Steelman:** Repositioning an existing Asian tonic into a premium, carbonated "energy drink" for youth creates a high-margin category leader before competitors react, leveraging proven ingredients to solve fatigue for a new demographic.

**Skeptic Attack:** The core failure hypothesis is **category rejection**. In 1987, "energy drink" is not a recognized concept in the home region; consumers associate caffeine with coffee or cola, not niche tonics. The proposal admits no such category exists. Launching at a price "well above colas" for a "small slim can" creates a severe value perception gap. If consumers cannot immediately justify the premium for an unknown taste profile targeting "extreme sports" (a nascent cultural signal in '87), adoption will stall. The risk of regulatory restriction on caffeine/taurine is secondary to the primary risk of zero demand.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" or "minor reformulation" option is safer. Alternatively, license the formula for a functional additive to existing cola lines rather than building a new distribution channel for an unproven category. Entering via a niche B2B channel (e.g., gyms, specific events) before a mass-market premium launch would validate taste and price elasticity without the blast radius of a national rollout.

**Pre-mortem & Kill Criteria:** Assume failure: The product launches, sits on shelves due to confusing positioning, and is delisted within six months as "too expensive and weird." This is a **one-way door** regarding brand reputation and sunk marketing costs. The cost of delay is low; being second allows learning from early adopters' mistakes.

**Verdict:** The proposal relies on creating a category ex nihilo with high price friction and unverified taste appeal for a completely new demographic. The evidence provided (risks of taste, category absence, price sensitivity) directly contradicts the assumption of immediate success. No kill criteria or pilot plan is offered to mitigate the "no category exists" risk. The gap between the current market reality (1987) and the proposed future state is too wide to bridge with a single blind launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
