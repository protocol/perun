**Steelman:** The proposal bets the company's survival on a radical technological and stylistic pivot to a fuel-efficient, modern hatchback, arguing that clinging to the obsolete rear-engine air-cooled legacy guarantees extinction as regulations tighten and oil prices soar.

**Attack:** The timing is catastrophic. Retooling for an entirely new powertrain architecture (front-engine, water-cooled) requires massive capital expenditure precisely when profits are shrinking and market demand is contracting due to the oil crisis. The company lacks institutional knowledge in this layout, introducing high execution risk. Furthermore, alienating the loyal base that cherishes the current car's unique character could destroy the brand's core revenue stream before the new model gains traction. If the new car fails to resonate immediately, the company faces insolvency with no fallback.

**Better Way:** Evolve the existing platform. Invest in incremental efficiency improvements to the current air-cooled engine and update the styling to meet modern safety norms without the prohibitive cost of new tooling. This preserves cash, leverages existing manufacturing expertise, and maintains brand loyalty while waiting for the economic climate to stabilize before attempting a full architectural shift.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch fails. The cause is likely a combination of delayed production due to unfamiliar engineering challenges, a price point too high for a crisis market, and a design that feels too foreign to existing customers. The "do nothing" or "evolutionary" path minimizes these specific failure modes.

**Fit:** While the north star is long-term viability, this specific proposal ignores the immediate constraint of liquidity. A "go" decision here assumes the company can survive the cash burn of retooling during a recession, which is unverified and highly improbable given the stated shrinking profits.

**Verdict:** The proposal demands an irreversible, high-cost bet on an unproven (for this maker) technology during an economic downturn, ignoring the safer, cash-preserving alternative of evolving the current successful asset. The risk of total collapse outweighs the potential gain.
