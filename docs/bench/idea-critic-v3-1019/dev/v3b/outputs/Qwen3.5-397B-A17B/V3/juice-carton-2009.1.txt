The proposal claims a nationwide, simultaneous redesign of the primary shelf cue for a loyal, older demographic will succeed despite recognized risks of non-recognition. The steelman is that a modern aesthetic, paired with heavy advertising and a functional cap innovation, will refresh the brand and attract new buyers without alienating the core base.

However, the attack reveals a fatal blocker. The proposal explicitly admits the old orange image is the "main shelf cue" and the buyer base is "older." Changing the single most critical visual anchor for a demographic resistant to change, while removing the recognizable fruit entirely in favor of an abstract glass and rotated text, creates a high probability of immediate sales collapse. Advertising cannot instantly retrain millions of habitual shoppers to find a product they previously identified in seconds. The "vertically rotated brand name" further reduces legibility for older eyes.

The "better way" analysis shows the "do nothing" or "evolutionary change" options are superior. A phased rollout or retaining the orange motif while updating secondary elements would test the new design without betting the entire franchise on a single, irreversible, nationwide switch. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a failed national launch where shoppers simply walk past the unrecognizable carton.

The pre-mortem is clear: six months post-launch, sales have plummeted because loyalists could not find the product, and new buyers were not sufficiently acquired to offset the loss. The "edge" cited is purely aesthetic, not functional or demand-driven. There is no evidence provided that the current design is suppressing growth; the risk is entirely downside.

Blockers: The plan removes the primary shelf cue for a loyal, older demographic simultaneously nationwide, guaranteeing recognition failure.
Risks: High cost of retooling, potential permanent brand damage, advertising inefficacy against habit.
Edge: None stated beyond aesthetic preference.

DECISION: NO-GO
