Steelman: Leverage dominant mobile distribution to disrupt handheld gaming via a hybrid device, accepting hardware compromises for market access.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal; the conflicts are strategic risks, not impossibilities. The price premium and awkward form factor are deliberate trade-offs, not errors in logic.

**Risks:** High. Removing the battery to swap games creates severe friction compared to the leader's hot-swappable cartridges. The $300 price point is double the competitor's, demanding justification beyond telephony. Awkward ergonomics may limit call utility, alienating the core mobile base while failing to woo hardcore gamers. Publisher apathy could starve the platform of content, rendering the hardware irrelevant regardless of phone sales.

**Edge:** The proposer is the largest mobile phone maker. This provides an installed base and distribution network the dedicated game company cannot match. If the "phone" utility is primary and gaming is secondary, the device succeeds by being the only option that does both, forcing publishers to eventually comply due to user volume.

**Analysis:** The proposal survives the "do nothing" test only if the goal is market diversification, not immediate gaming dominance. The "better way" of licensing games to existing phones is unavailable in this 2003 context due to technical limits. The pre-mortem suggests failure via content drought, but the kill criterion is clear: secure publisher commitments before launch. Since the proposal acknowledges these risks explicitly, they are managed variables, not hidden flaws. The edge (mobile scale) is unique and sufficient to warrant the attempt, provided the company accepts it will not beat the leader on pure gaming merits initially.

The plan is sound because it leverages an asymmetric advantage (mobile dominance) against a competitor's weakness (lack of phone tech), despite significant usability handicaps.
