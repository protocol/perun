**Steelman:** Committing now secures a decade-long lead in hybrid technology, forcing competitors to play catch-up while establishing the brand as the definitive eco-innovator before regulations mandate it.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "edge" test. With fuel cheap and no regulatory mandate, the market signal is nonexistent; customers will not pay a premium for unproven reliability. The "bold public schedule" creates a one-way door: if the novel transmission or NiMH batteries fail durability tests (a high probability given they are unproven), the brand suffers catastrophic reputational damage and warranty bankruptcy. A cheaper, safer alternative exists: develop the technology silently as a compliance car or limited fleet to validate durability without tying the entire corporate future to a 1997 mass-production deadline. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a public failure.

**Blockers:** 
1. **No Market Edge:** Cheap fuel and zero regulatory pressure mean the product offers no compelling value proposition to offset higher costs and reliability risks.
2. **Unproven Core Tech:** Committing to mass production before validating battery durability and transmission reliability contradicts basic engineering risk management.
3. **Alternative Superiority:** A staged approach (pilot fleet) achieves the learning objective without the existential risk of a public mass-market flop.

**Risks:** Massive warranty exposure, brand destruction if the first units fail, and capital misallocation away from profitable core lines.

**Edge:** None stated. "First mover" status is worthless if the product is unreliable or too expensive for the current market conditions. Fear of future regulation is not a current edge.

**Decision Logic:** The plan commits real money to a one-way door based on assumed future demand rather than verified current need. The lack of a near-term demand signal and the unproven nature of the core components constitute a factual blocker. The proposal must be revised to a capped, time-boxed pilot program with clear kill criteria based on durability data before mass production commitment. As proposed, it is a gamble, not a strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
