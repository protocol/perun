**Steelman:** A premium, global-only mobile network captures high-value business travelers underserved by fragmented, land-based cellular networks in 1998, generating enough revenue to service $5 billion in debt before ground networks achieve ubiquity.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the unit economics and market timing test. The addressable market is severely constrained by the $3,000 handset cost and multi-dollar per-minute call rates, limiting adoption to a tiny fraction of the projected user base required to service the debt. Ground cellular networks are expanding rapidly, directly eroding the "global gap" advantage in the very cities where business travelers congregate. Furthermore, the technical limitations (bulk, poor indoor performance) degrade the user experience below the threshold expected by premium clients. The debt structure demands immediate, massive scale that the high-price/low-performance product cannot deliver. The "global" feature is a nice-to-have for most, not a must-have at this price point, especially when the primary use cases (office-to-office, city-to-city) are increasingly covered by cheaper, better-performing ground alternatives.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing/Wait:** Avoid launching with a flawed product; wait for handset costs to drop and ground gaps to clarify, though this ignores the sunk debt pressure.
2.  **Pivot to Niche/Gov:** Target specific government, maritime, or aviation sectors where ground cellular is impossible, rather than the general business traveler.
3.  **Price/Subsidy Slash:** Drastically subsidize handsets and lower call rates to drive volume, risking even higher immediate cash burn but potentially capturing market share before ground networks close the gap.

**Pre-mortem:** By 2000, subscriber growth lags projections by 80% due to price resistance and superior ground coverage in key hubs. Cash flow cannot cover interest payments. The company defaults on its loans, assets are liquidated for pennies on the dollar, and the constellation becomes space debris.

**Blockers:** The mathematical mismatch between the debt service schedule and the realistic adoption curve at the proposed price point. The rapid expansion of ground cellular invalidates the core value proposition in high-density areas.

**Edge:** None identified. The first-mover advantage is negated by inferior technology and prohibitive cost.

**Kill Criteria:** If subscriber acquisition costs exceed $5,000 per user or if ground coverage reaches 60% of major international business hubs within 18 months, the project is insolvent.

DECISION: NO-GO
