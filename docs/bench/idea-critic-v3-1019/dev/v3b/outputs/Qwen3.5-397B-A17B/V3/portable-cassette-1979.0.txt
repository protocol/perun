**Steelman:** A low-cost, portable cassette player using existing components can create a new mass market among youth by prioritizing mobility and privacy over recording capability, overcoming dealer skepticism through volume pricing and cultural shift.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker claimed is dealer/staff doubt and lack of market research. However, the "antisocial" risk is actually the product's edge: it offers private immersion, a distinct value proposition compared to shared hi-fi systems. The lack of recording function reduces cost and complexity, directly enabling the youth-targeted price point.

Competitor copying is a valid risk, but first-mover advantage in defining this new "personal stereo" category, combined with the company's existing brand equity in tape recorders, provides a defensive moat. The requirement for a large initial run to hit price targets is a classic volume-game gamble; if the price is right, the volume follows. The "no market research" objection is weak because this product creates a new behavior rather than satisfying an existing, measured one; traditional research often fails to predict disruptive form factors.

The "do nothing" alternative guarantees stagnation as competitors eventually miniaturize anyway. The "better way" of adding a speaker or recorder destroys the cost/size advantage essential for the youth demographic.

**Verdict Logic:**
- **Blockers:** None. Dealer doubt is a sales challenge, not a factual impossibility. Lack of prior research is expected for category creation.
- **Edge:** First-mover definition of "personal audio," leveraging existing supply chain for unmatched price/size ratio.
- **Risks:** Competitor speed, cultural rejection of headphones. These are managed by the speed of rollout and marketing that frames headphones as fashionable, not antisocial.
- **Kill Criteria:** If initial sell-through in key youth markets does not exceed 50% of forecast within 90 days, halt second run.

The proposal survives the pre-mortem: the most likely failure is under-ordering due to internal doubt, not product flaw. The strategic necessity of owning the "personal" segment outweighs the comfort of recorded demand.

DECISION: GO
