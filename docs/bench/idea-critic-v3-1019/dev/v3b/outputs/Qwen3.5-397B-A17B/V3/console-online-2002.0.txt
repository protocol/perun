**Steelman:** A unified, paid online service creates a defensible moat through network effects and recurring revenue, differentiating the console via superior user experience (identity, voice, matchmaking) despite higher friction.

**Analysis:**
The proposal bets on a future state (broadband ubiquity) rather than the current reality (dial-up dominance). The "blocker" is the mismatch between the mandatory broadband requirement and the installed base in 2002. However, this is a strategic gamble to define the high-end market segment, not a fatal flaw if the target demographic is early adopters.

The "free" expectation of rivals is a short-term advantage for them but a long-term liability regarding service quality and security. By centralizing identity and voice, the proposal solves fragmentation that plagues publisher-led models. The operational risk (new hosting/support capabilities) is significant but surmountable compared to the strategic risk of having no online strategy at all.

**Pre-mortem:** The service fails if the subscription price point exceeds the perceived value of the unified features or if broadband adoption stalls for another decade. However, waiting for "perfect" conditions cedes the definition of online console gaming to competitors.

**Edge:** First-mover advantage in creating a cohesive, console-wide social layer. Once a user's identity and friends list are established here, switching costs become prohibitive, locking in the ecosystem. The "walled garden" approach, while restrictive, ensures a consistent quality of service that fragmented, free alternatives cannot guarantee.

**Kill Criteria:** If broadband penetration in target markets does not reach 15% within 18 months, or if subscriber churn exceeds 40% in the first year, the model must pivot to a hybrid (free tier) or sunset.

**Verdict:** The risks are real but represent the cost of leadership. The alternative (doing nothing or copying the fragmented free model) guarantees commodity status. The proposal forces the market forward.

DECISION: GO
