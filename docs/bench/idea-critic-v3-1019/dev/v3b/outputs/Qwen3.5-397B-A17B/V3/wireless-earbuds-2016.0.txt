**Steelman:** Removing the headphone jack forces a transition to wireless audio, creating a market for premium, seamlessly integrated earbuds that leverage proprietary silicon for superior user experience, despite higher costs and form-factor risks.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists between the proposal's goals and the stated risks. The risks are execution challenges, not proof of failure.

**Risks:** High price point ($159) vs. free alternatives; potential consumer ridicule of design; loss anxiety; battery limitations; backlash from jack removal. These are valid concerns but do not inherently prevent success if the value proposition (convenience, integration) is demonstrated.

**Edge:** Proprietary wireless chip enables automatic pairing and ecosystem lock-in, a distinct advantage competitors lacking custom silicon cannot immediately replicate. The "odd" design becomes a signature differentiator if marketed as futuristic rather than defective.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because the industry wasn't ready for the jack's removal, causing backlash that outweighs the convenience of wireless. However, being first allows the company to define the category standards before rivals react.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal bets on a one-way door (removing the jack) requiring a two-way door solution (wireless buds) to mitigate user pain. The $159 price is justified by the R&D in custom silicon and the strategic necessity of owning the audio experience post-jack. The "do nothing" option is unavailable once the jack is removed; the only alternative is licensing inferior third-party tech or bundling cheap adapters, both weakening the brand's premium stance. The risks are manageable through marketing (reframing design) and product iteration (battery/case). The edge (custom chip integration) is the critical wedge to make the high price palatable.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders miss internal targets by >40% due to price/design complaints, pivot to a lower-cost SKU or include a dongle prominently. Leading metric: Adoption rate of auto-pairing feature within first 30 days.

DECISION: GO
