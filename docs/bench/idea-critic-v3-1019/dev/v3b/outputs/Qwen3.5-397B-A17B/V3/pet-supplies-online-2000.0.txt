**Steelman:** Aggressively scale market share via IPO proceeds to dominate the pet supply sector before competitors, accepting short-term losses to secure long-term monopoly power.

**Attack:** The proposal contains a fatal mathematical contradiction. Selling products below cost ("negative gross margins") while simultaneously increasing volume through national TV ads and acquiring a competitor creates a scenario where every additional unit sold increases total losses. This is not a path to dominance; it is an acceleration of insolvency.

The plan assumes capital markets will remain open indefinitely to fund these mounting losses. However, the proposal itself admits capital markets could close. If markets tighten while the company is burning cash on TV ads, below-cost sales, and warehouse construction, liquidity will vanish instantly. The "edge" claimed is speed, but the mechanism (selling below cost) destroys the very capital required to sustain that speed.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing/Status Quo:** Continue organic growth without the massive burn rate.
2.  **Profitability Focus:** Raise prices to cover shipping and COGS before expanding.
3.  **Targeted Acquisition:** Buy the competitor using stock or debt only after proving unit economics work.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-IPO, the stock crashes as quarterly losses widen due to the TV campaign and below-cost pricing. Investors flee as the "growth at all costs" narrative fails to show a path to profitability. The company cannot raise follow-on funding, cannot pay suppliers, and enters bankruptcy with expanded, underutilized warehouses.

**Blockers:**
*   **Negative Unit Economics:** Selling below cost while scaling volume guarantees accelerated cash burn.
*   **Capital Dependency:** The entire plan collapses if the single assumption of "perpetually open capital markets" proves false.
*   **No Path to Profit:** The proposal offers no mechanism to ever turn a profit, relying entirely on future market dominance that may never materialize if funds run out.

**Edge:** None stated. Being the fastest to bankruptcy is not a competitive advantage. Fear of competitors spending heavily is not a strategy; it is a reaction that ignores basic arithmetic.

**Kill Criteria:** Immediate halt if unit economics (price minus COGS minus shipping) remain negative after the first quarter of expanded operations.

DECISION: NO-GO
