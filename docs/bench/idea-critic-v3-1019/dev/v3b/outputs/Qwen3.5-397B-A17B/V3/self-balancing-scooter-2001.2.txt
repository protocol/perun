**Steelman:** A novel, self-balancing electric transporter will create a new urban mobility category, leveraging high-profile backing to overcome initial skepticism and achieve mass adoption despite regulatory ambiguity.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" and "evidence" tests. In 2001, $5,000 is prohibitive for a toy and impractical for commuting compared to bicycles or cars. The "institutional buyer" market (security, tourism) is too small to sustain a 40,000-unit factory; this volume requires mass consumer adoption which the price point precludes.

Regulatory uncertainty is a fatal blocker, not a risk. Without clear legal status on sidewalks or roads, the product faces immediate bans in major cities, destroying utility. The "conspicuous" factor further limits early adopters to novelty seekers, not daily commuters.

The pre-mortem is clear: The company builds a massive factory, faces regulatory crackdowns in key markets, fails to find enough institutional buyers to fill capacity, and cannot lower prices due to high fixed costs. The "edge" of being first is negated by being too expensive and legally vulnerable. A staged approach (pilot programs, regulatory clarity, smaller production run) is the only viable path; building for 40,000 units now is reckless.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Regulatory Void:** No legal framework exists for operation; high probability of immediate bans.
2.  **Market Mismatch:** $5,000 price point excludes mass market; institutional demand cannot support 40k unit capacity.
3.  **Premature Scaling:** Factory size assumes demand that has not been proven or legally enabled.

**Risks:** Public ridicule, safety liabilities, rapid obsolescence if regulations shift.
**Edge:** First-mover technology advantage (insufficient to overcome blockers).

**Decision Logic:**
The plan commits to high fixed costs (factory) before resolving the primary blocker (legality) or validating the market size at the proposed price. It bets on a market that does not yet exist legally or economically. The "do nothing" or "pilot first" alternative is strictly superior.

DECISION: NO-GO
