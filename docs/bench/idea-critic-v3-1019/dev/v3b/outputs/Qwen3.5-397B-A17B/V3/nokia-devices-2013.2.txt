**Steelman:** Acquiring the handset division secures the primary hardware partner, prevents defection to the rival platform, and aligns incentives to improve the user experience, potentially slowing market share erosion.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "edge" test. The core problem is identified as a lack of apps and developers driven by the market leader's dominance. Owning a failing hardware division with falling sales does not solve the ecosystem deficit; it adds a massive integration burden (large workforce, cultural clash) and capital expense (€5.4B) to a company already struggling with low single-digit share.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Misaligned Solution:** The critical failure mode is the app ecosystem. Hardware ownership does not compel developers to write apps for a dying platform. History shows that controlling both layers cannot compensate for a lack of third-party support when the install base is shrinking.
2.  **Financial Drain:** Acquiring a business with falling sales consumes cash needed for software innovation or marketing, likely accelerating the decline rather than halting it.
3.  **Competitor Reaction:** Rival handset makers, fearing favoritism or direct competition from the OS owner, may accelerate their own switch to the rival platform, instantly collapsing the remaining hardware diversity for this OS.

**Risks:** Integration failure, alienation of other partners, and the high probability that the acquired unit continues to lose money without reversing the platform's fate.

**Edge:** None stated. "Strategic necessity" or fear of the rival's move is not an edge. The proposal offers no unique asset, cost advantage, or proven demand that rivals lack. The "do nothing" or "partner-only" alternative preserves cash and avoids the integration risk while facing the same ecosystem headwinds.

**Decision Logic:**
The plan attempts to solve a software/network-effect problem with a hardware acquisition of a declining asset. The costs are immediate and certain; the benefits are speculative and unlikely to address the root cause (app availability). The risk of triggering a partner exodus is a concrete blocker that outweighs the theoretical benefit of vertical integration. Without a named edge showing why *this* specific combination will succeed where the organic partnership failed, the deal destroys value.

DECISION: NO-GO
