**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee ($79) eliminates per-order friction, locking in loyalty and increasing purchase frequency enough to offset shipping losses through volume and margin expansion.

**Blockers:** NONE. The "unproven economics" is a risk, not a factual blocker, as the model relies on behavioral shifts (frequency) that cannot be proven without a launch. The short-term margin hit is a known cost of acquisition, not a failure of the plan itself.

**Risks:** Heavy buyers may cause shipping costs to exceed revenue on individual orders; significant short-term margin compression; reputational damage if the program is reversed after customer adoption; total failure if frequency does not increase sufficiently to cover fixed fee costs.

**Edge:** Per-order fees are explicitly identified as the "biggest friction." Removing this specific barrier directly targets the primary constraint on repeat business. Competitors charging per order remain disadvantaged on convenience for frequent shoppers.

**Analysis:**
The proposal addresses the stated root cause (friction) with a direct solution (free shipping). The risks raised are inherent to any subscription model attempting to monetize behavior change; they are uncertainties to be managed via kill criteria, not reasons to reject the strategy before testing. The "hard to reverse" concern is mitigated by the fact that memberships are annual; the company can simply stop selling new memberships or change terms for renewals, limiting exposure to one year of legacy contracts. The short-term margin hit is the necessary investment to secure long-term lifetime value (LTV). Doing nothing preserves the "biggest friction," guaranteeing stagnant repeat rates. A cheaper alternative (discounted shipping) fails to remove the psychological friction of paying per transaction.

**Kill Criteria:** If, after 12 months, the incremental gross margin from increased frequency does not exceed the total shipping subsidy cost, terminate new sales.

**Verdict:** The plan survives the attack because the risks are manageable hypotheses rather than disqualifying facts, and the edge directly solves the stated problem.

DECISION: GO
