**Steelman:** Embedding general-purpose compute into mass-market GPUs creates a ubiquitous, low-cost supercomputer fabric, unlocking scientific markets that CPUs cannot serve efficiently, securing a decade-long moat before rivals react.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "pay now, maybe later" test. Adding silicon area and toolchain costs to *every* unit raises prices in a hyper-competitive gaming market where performance-per-dollar is the sole purchasing driver. With no committed customers and niche demand, this subsidizes a hypothetical future market with definite present margins. The engineering distraction risks losing the core gaming race against the main rival; if the core business bleeds share to fund a ghost market, the company lacks the revenue to sustain the program. Academic experimentation with hacked APIs proves interest but not willingness to pay for dedicated silicon. The "cheaper alternative" is to wait for scientific demand to crystallize before committing hardware, or to use FPGA/ASIC partnerships for early adopters without bloating consumer chips.

**Pre-mortem:** By 2006, the gaming division has lost 15% market share due to higher unit costs and diverted engineering focus. The scientific toolchain is unfinished, and academics remain on hacked drivers because the new hardware offers no immediate software advantage. The company burns cash on a feature no consumer asked for, forcing a retreat and write-down.

**Edge:** None stated. "Strategic necessity" is not an edge. The proposal relies on the assumption that scientific computing will shift to parallel architectures before the company runs out of cash from gaming losses.

**Blockers:** 
1. No committed customers to validate the price premium.
2. Direct threat to core gaming margin and market share in a two-horse race.
3. High fixed cost (silicon + toolchain) applied to 100% of units for a 0% current revenue segment.

**Risks:** Engineering distraction, delayed time-to-market for gaming parts, toolchain abandonment.

**Verdict:** The plan bets the core business on an unproven market without a bridge to profitability. It violates the principle of staging investment against validated demand.

DECISION: NO-GO
