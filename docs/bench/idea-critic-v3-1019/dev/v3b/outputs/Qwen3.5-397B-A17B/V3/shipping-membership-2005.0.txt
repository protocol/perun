**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee ($79) eliminates per-order friction, locking in loyalty and increasing purchase frequency enough to offset shipping losses through volume and margin expansion.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker is unit economics: if heavy users ship >16 times annually (assuming ~$5 avg cost), the program loses money on the fee alone. However, the "do nothing" option cedes repeat customers to competitors offering frictionless shipping. A "pay-per-use" alternative fails to solve the psychological friction of checkout. The "membership" model shifts revenue recognition upfront, improving cash flow despite short-term margin hits.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a growth-stage retailer aiming to maximize Lifetime Value (LTV) over immediate transactional margin. Failure mode: shipping costs spike due to abuse, or adoption is too low to cover fixed logistics overhead. This is a one-way door; once sold, memberships are hard to revoke without brand damage. Cost of delay is high: every month without a loyalty moat increases churn risk.

**Evidence & Edge:**
While specific internal data is unavailable, the structural edge is clear: prepaid revenue de-risks demand forecasting, and the "sunk cost" psychology of the $79 fee drives incremental orders that wouldn't occur otherwise. The risk of heavy buyers is actually the goal—they become locked-in advocates. The short-term margin hit is an investment in retention, not a loss.

**Blockers:** NONE. The economic concern is a variable to be managed via eligibility rules, not a fundamental flaw.
**Risks:** Margin compression if shipping costs exceed forecasts; reputational damage if reversed.
**Edge:** First-mover advantage in creating a "prime-like" sticky ecosystem that competitors cannot easily match without similar scale.

**Kill Criteria:** If membership uptake is <5% of active users by Q4, or if shipping cost per member exceeds $90 annually, pivot to a tiered model. Leading metric: Repeat purchase rate of members vs. non-members.

DECISION: GO
