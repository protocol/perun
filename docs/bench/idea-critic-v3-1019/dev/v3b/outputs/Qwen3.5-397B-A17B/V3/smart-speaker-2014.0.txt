Steelman: A dedicated, always-listening home speaker creates a new "ambient computing" category, leveraging existing retail relationships to overcome the poor phone launch by offering superior audio and frictionless shopping integration that mobile cannot match.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of a category is an opportunity for first-mover advantage, not a factual barrier. The poor phone sales reflect a crowded smartphone market, not an inability to build hardware. While voice recognition risks are real, they are execution challenges, not proof of impossibility.

**Risks:** High consumer privacy skepticism regarding always-on microphones; technical failure in noisy environments; potential brand damage if the "gimmick" fails to deliver utility. The invitation-only model limits initial data collection needed to improve recognition algorithms.

**Edge:** The retailer possesses a massive existing catalog of physical goods and a loyal membership base willing to try exclusive offers ($99 price point). Unlike phone assistants, this device offers premium audio quality and is positioned specifically for the home environment where shopping list creation and music consumption are high-frequency activities. The "invitation only" approach creates scarcity and manages rollout risk.

**Analysis:** The proposal correctly identifies a gap between mobile assistants and home audio systems. The pricing strategy ($199 standard, $99 member) effectively monetizes the existing loyalty program while lowering the barrier to entry for early adopters. The risks are significant but manageable through iterative software updates and clear privacy controls. The "do nothing" alternative cedes the emerging smart home market to competitors. The pre-mortem suggests failure would stem from privacy backlash or poor voice accuracy, but these are addressable with transparent user controls and focused R&D. The cost of delay is high, as competitors are likely exploring similar form factors.

The plan beats "do nothing" by establishing a foothold in a nascent, high-potential category using unique assets (membership, catalog) that competitors lack. It is a two-way door; if it fails, the brand can pivot or discontinue with limited long-term damage compared to the strategic loss of missing the category entirely.

DECISION: GO
