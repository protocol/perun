**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled e-reader removes friction for book buyers and solves publisher piracy fears via a closed ecosystem, creating a new high-margin revenue stream that complements rather than cannibalizes print.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces four critical objections: lack of hardware expertise, publisher resistance to low pricing, historical failure of e-readers, and print cannibalization.

1.  **Hardware Inexperience:** While a valid risk, the core value is not the device itself but the seamless delivery mechanism. Partnering with an existing manufacturer for the hardware while retaining control over the software and store mitigates the "never designed hardware" blocker. The cellular connection is the differentiator; it must be subsidized as a customer acquisition cost, not a hardware profit center.
2.  **Publisher Margins:** Publishers fear piracy and margin erosion. The "single low price" must be structured as an agency model where publishers set prices, and the platform takes a fixed cut, ensuring they do not feel squeezed. The closed system directly addresses piracy fears better than PDFs on PCs.
3.  **Past Failures:** Previous e-readers failed due to poor screens, difficult content transfer, and limited catalogs. This proposal specifically addresses screen quality ("paper-like") and distribution (cellular, huge catalog). The failure of predecessors validates the need for *this* specific combination, not the concept itself.
4.  **Cannibalization:** Print and digital serve different use cases (collectibility/tactility vs. portability/immediacy). Data from music transitions suggests digital often expands the total market rather than just shifting it. The risk of *not* entering is ceding the digital future to competitors.

**Blockers:** None. The hardware gap is solvable via partnership; publisher concerns are addressed by the closed ecosystem and pricing model flexibility.
**Risks:** Execution complexity in supply chain; initial subsidy costs. These are managed risks, not blockers.
**Edge:** The company possesses the world's largest book catalog and existing customer trust, an asset no pure hardware manufacturer has. The "paper-like" screen plus ubiquitous cellular access creates a unique value proposition unavailable on general-purpose devices of this era.

**Kill Criteria:** If pilot units show <10% monthly active usage of the cellular download feature within 6 months, or if major publishers refuse to license titles for the platform, the project halts.

**Decision:** The strategic necessity of owning the digital reading interface outweighs the operational risks. The unique assets (catalog, brand) provide the edge required to succeed where others failed.

DECISION: GO
