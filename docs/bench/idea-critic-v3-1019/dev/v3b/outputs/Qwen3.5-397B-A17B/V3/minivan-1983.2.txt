**Steelman:** Leverage existing compact-car assets to create a novel, garage-friendly passenger van, capturing an uncontested market segment before competitors react, using minimal capital via factory conversion.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary objection is the non-existence of the category and fear of commercial stigma. However, the "no category" argument is a strength, not a blocker; it indicates a blue-ocean opportunity where the firm defines the rules rather than fighting incumbents. The risk of buyers perceiving it as a commercial van is mitigated by the explicit positioning as a family vehicle (station wagon alternative) and car-like driving dynamics.

Financial constraints are severe, but the proposal explicitly addresses this by reusing the compact-car platform and converting an existing factory. This minimizes R&D and CapEx, making the project feasible despite low cash reserves. The platform limitation on space is a necessary trade-off to achieve the "garage-fit" constraint, which is the product's core value proposition.

Rival copycats are a future risk, but first-mover advantage in defining a new segment (the "minivan") creates brand ownership that is difficult to dislodge quickly. The alternative ("do nothing") leaves the company dependent on declining station wagon sales with no growth vector.

**Blockers:** None. The lack of cash is addressed by the low-cost approach; the lack of category is the opportunity.
**Risks:** Market education costs; platform space constraints limiting cargo utility.
**Edge:** Unique fit for home garages combined with car comfort on a shoestring budget, enabled by existing assets rivals cannot replicate without similar legacy platforms.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal beats "do nothing" by creating a new revenue stream with minimal investment. It addresses the cash constraint through asset reuse. The risks are manageable execution challenges, not fatal flaws. The potential upside (saving the company, defining a decade-defining vehicle class) vastly outweighs the cost of a converted factory line.

DECISION: GO
