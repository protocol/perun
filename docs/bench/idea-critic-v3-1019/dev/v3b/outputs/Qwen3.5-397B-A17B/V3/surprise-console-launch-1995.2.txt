**Steelman:** Launching in May 1995 secures a critical four-month head start to establish market presence and developer mindshare before the competitor arrives, accepting limited inventory as a strategic trade-off for timing.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" test. A full national launch is impossible due to stock constraints, but a "limited early access" program creates the same headline advantage without alienating excluded retailers. By framing the May shipment as an exclusive "founder's edition" or pilot rather than a general launch, the company mitigates retailer retaliation while still beating the competitor to market.

However, the **blocker** is the software gap. Hardware without games is a toy, not a console. The proposal admits "few games ready" and developers were not planned for this date. Launching a hard-to-program 3D machine with an empty library at a premium price ($399 vs. rival's $299) invites immediate consumer rejection. The "edge" of being first is nullified if the first impression is a barren, overpriced device. The pre-mortem is clear: early adopters buy, find no games, return the unit, and cement a reputation of failure before the rival even ships.

The "do nothing" option (stick to September) allows time for game development, aligning hardware availability with software readiness. The cost of delay (losing four months of exclusivity) is high, but the risk of a botched launch destroying brand equity is higher. The proposal lacks a kill criterion for software readiness; it assumes the hardware date dictates success regardless of content.

**Verdict:**
The plan as proposed creates a high probability of market rejection due to the software void and price disadvantage. The head start is valuable, but not at the cost of launching a broken value proposition. The proposal must be revised to decouple the "announcement" from the "general availability," or delayed until software matches hardware. As it stands, launching an empty console at a premium price is a strategic error.

DECISION: NO-GO
