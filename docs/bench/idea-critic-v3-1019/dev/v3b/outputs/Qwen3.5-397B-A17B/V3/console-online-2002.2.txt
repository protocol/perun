**Steelman:** A unified, paid online service creates a defensible moat through network effects and recurring revenue, differentiating the console via superior user experience (identity, voice, matchmaking) that fragmented, free alternatives cannot match.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker is the 2002 market reality: broadband penetration is critically low, and the "free" expectation is entrenched. However, "do nothing" cedes the high-end experience to future competitors and fragments the community. Building infrastructure is a heavy lift, but owning the stack prevents the "tragedy of the commons" seen in PC gaming.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with a premium brand strategy, not mass-market volume initially. If we wait for broadband to mature, we lose the first-mover advantage in defining the online standard. The risk of low adoption is real, but the risk of irrelevance in the next generation is higher.

**Evidence & Edge:**
The proposal relies on the assumption that a critical mass of early adopters exists. The company's edge is hardware integration; rivals relying on publishers cannot enforce voice chat or unified friends lists. The "paid" model filters for dedicated users, creating a higher-quality matchmaking environment than free, open lobbies.

**Blockers:**
None. The low broadband count is a market constraint, not a plan failure; the plan targets the existing broadband niche. The operational inexperience is a risk, not a blocker, solvable by hiring or acquisition.

**Risks:**
Slow subscriber growth; high operational costs if uptake lags; consumer backlash against fees.

**Edge:**
First-mover advantage in a unified, console-wide ecosystem that competitors cannot replicate without dismantling their publisher-led models.

**Kill Criteria:**
If subscriber adoption does not reach 15% of the installed base within 18 months, or if operational costs exceed 40% of subscription revenue by year-end 2003, pivot to a hybrid free/paid model or sunset the service.

**Leading Metric:**
Weekly active users per subscriber; review date: Q4 2003.

The plan bets on quality over quantity to define the next generation of gaming. The risks are manageable; the cost of inaction is strategic obsolescence.

DECISION: GO
