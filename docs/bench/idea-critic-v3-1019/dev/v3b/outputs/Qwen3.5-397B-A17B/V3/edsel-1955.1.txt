**Steelman:** Launching a distinct mid-priced division leverages existing manufacturing to capture a growing market segment, preventing customer defection to competitors while maximizing brand portfolio coverage.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on the assumption that the mid-priced segment will continue growing into 1957. However, the acknowledged "recession risk" directly contradicts this. In an economic downturn, the mid-market is historically the first to collapse as consumers trade down to economy models or delay purchases entirely. Furthermore, building a new dealer network requires massive fixed capital expenditure exactly when liquidity is most precious. The risk of "overlap" suggests internal cannibalization without a clear differentiation strategy beyond styling, which is insufficient if purchasing power evaporates.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; risk losing customers to competitors but avoid catastrophic fixed costs during a potential recession.
2.  **Badging Strategy:** Utilize existing dealers to sell mid-priced variants under current franchises, avoiding new infrastructure costs while testing demand.
3.  **Delay:** Postpone launch until economic indicators stabilize, sacrificing first-mover advantage for survival certainty.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If a recession hits in 1957 (a plausible external shock), the new division becomes a cash incinerator with low volume and high overhead. The "massive advertising" spend becomes sunk cost with no ROI. The failure mode is not poor execution, but correct execution of a strategy misaligned with the macroeconomic cycle. The proposal admits the segment "could shrink" yet proceeds with irreversible infrastructure investment.

**Blockers:**
*   **Macro-Economic Contradiction:** Proceeding with high-fixed-cost expansion while explicitly acknowledging a high-probability recession risk that targets the specific price segment.
*   **Infrastructure Timing:** Committing to new dealer networks before economic stability is verified creates a liquidity trap.

**Edge:** None stated. "Perceived gap" is not an edge; it is a hypothesis. No unique asset or cost advantage is cited that would allow this division to survive a downturn where others might fail.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It commits irreversible capital to a cyclical segment immediately prior to a predicted downturn without a staged entry or hedging mechanism.

DECISION: NO-GO
