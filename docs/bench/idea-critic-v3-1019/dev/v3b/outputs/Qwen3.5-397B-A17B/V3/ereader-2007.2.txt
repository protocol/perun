**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled e-reader removes friction for book buyers and solves publisher piracy fears via a closed ecosystem, creating a new high-margin revenue stream that complements rather than cannibalizes print.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces four critical objections: lack of hardware expertise, publisher resistance to low pricing, historical failure of e-readers, and internal cannibalization.

1.  **Hardware Inexperience:** While valid, this is a "build vs. buy" execution risk, not a strategic blocker. The core value is the cellular distribution network and content integration, not the silicon design. Partnering or contracting manufacturing mitigates this.
2.  **Publisher Margins:** The "low price" risk is actually the primary edge. Publishers fear piracy, not low prices per se. A DRM-protected, seamless delivery system justifies lower prices by guaranteeing sales volume they currently lack. The free cellular connection is the subsidy that unlocks this volume.
3.  **Historical Failure:** Previous e-readers failed due to poor UX (slow screens, required PC connection). This proposal specifically addresses those failures with "paper-like" tech and direct cellular access. The market context has shifted; the failure of the past does not predict the failure of a fundamentally different value proposition.
4.  **Cannibalization:** This is a feature, not a bug. If the device converts non-readers or captures incremental digital sales, total revenue rises. If it shifts print buyers to digital, the company retains the customer and gains higher marginal profit per unit (no printing/shipping costs).

**Edge:** The company's massive catalog and existing publisher relationships provide an insurmountable content moat that dedicated hardware startups lacked. The "free cellular" model aligns incentives: the device is a loss leader for high-margin content.

**Kill Criteria:** If publisher sign-up for the initial launch catalog is below 50% of top titles, or if the hardware unit cost cannot be subsidized to under $200 while maintaining content margins, the plan fails.

**Verdict:** The risks are executional and manageable; the strategic fit is perfect. The "cannibalization" fear is a sunk-cost fallacy regarding print logistics. The edge (content + distribution) is unique to this proposer.

DECISION: GO
