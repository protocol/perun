**Steelman:** A search company can disrupt mature email by offering 1,000x storage and superior search, monetizing via contextual ads, leveraging existing infrastructure to offset high storage costs.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's "risks" are not blockers because they are either solved by the proposer's unique assets or are standard market entry hurdles that the specific value proposition overcomes. The cost of storage is a barrier only to those without the proposer's scale; for a search company, marginal storage costs are low compared to the value of indexed data. Privacy objections are a reputational risk, not a technical failure, and are mitigated by the "free" value exchange which users have historically accepted.

**Risks:** 
1. **Privacy Backlash:** Scanning content for ads may trigger regulatory or user trust issues.
2. **Unit Economics:** If ad click-through rates on email are lower than search, the model fails.
3. **Incumbent Response:** Competitors could match storage, eroding the differentiator.

**Edge:** The proposer is a *search* company. This is the decisive fact. They possess the unique algorithmic capability to make 1GB of mail usable (search) and the existing ad infrastructure to monetize it immediately. No other incumbent combines search dominance with ad tech. The "mature market" is actually stagnant, with users conditioned to delete mail; removing that pain point creates immediate, viral demand (fueled by the invitation-only scarcity).

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal beats "do nothing" because it captures high-value user data and engagement time. It beats "smaller storage" because the marginal cost to the proposer is negligible while the marketing impact is massive. The risks are manageable: privacy is addressed by transparency (a policy choice), and economics rely on the proposer's core competency (ads). The lack of direct revenue at launch is irrelevant given the ad-model trajectory. The specific combination of massive storage + search + ad tech is a unique fit for this specific proposer that rivals cannot instantly copy without pivoting their core architecture.

**Kill Criteria:** If, after 12 months, the cost per user exceeds ad revenue per user by more than 20% despite 100M active users, pivot to a tiered model.

**Leading Metric:** Daily active users searching their mail (validates the "search" differentiator).

DECISION: GO
