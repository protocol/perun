**Steelman:** Merging dominant content with the fastest-growing distribution channel creates an unbeatable ecosystem, hedging dial-up decline with cable broadband while monetizing assets across both.

**Attack:** The proposal rests on a fatal asymmetry: valuing a volatile, unproven dial-up model at a premium to swap for stable, cash-generating physical assets. The "broadband future" explicitly undermines the internet provider's core revenue (dial-up) while relying on the partner's cable infrastructure, which may face regulatory or partnership blockers. The cultural clash between a agile tech firm and a traditional media giant risks paralyzing integration. Most critically, the internet provider's shareholders taking a majority stake means the conglomerate's stable value is being exchanged for a bubble that could burst before synergies materialize.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Conglomerate retains stability; internet provider risks obsolescence but keeps upside.
2.  **Partnership Only:** License content without merging balance sheets, avoiding cultural/valuation risk.
3.  **Cable-First Build:** Conglomerate accelerates its own broadband rollout without diluting equity.

**Pre-mortem:** In 24 months, the dot-com bubble bursts. The internet provider's stock collapses 80%, wiping out the "majority" value held by new shareholders. The merged entity is saddled with massive debt (implicit in stock swaps), cultural infighting stalls cross-promotion, and cable partners deny access. The conglomerate's pristine balance sheet is destroyed by the tech sector's crash.

**Blockers:**
*   **Valuation Mismatch:** Swapping hard assets for a volatile, peak-bubble valuation is financially reckless. The dial-up decline is inevitable as broadband grows; the merger accelerates this cannibalization without guaranteeing cable access.
*   **Regulatory/Cable Risk:** No guaranteed network access nullifies the primary distribution synergy.

**Edge:** None. The "content + distribution" thesis is generic; rivals can license content without merging entire corporate structures. The specific advantage claimed (cable delivery) is not owned by the combined entity.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It exchanges certainty for speculation at the exact moment the speculation is most fragile.

DECISION: NO-GO
