**Steelman:** Acquiring the startup secures a free, open mobile OS to accelerate mobile web adoption, directly expanding the search inventory required for long-term ad revenue growth, despite high upfront costs and adoption barriers.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists between the $50M cost and the company's cash position (implied by market dominance in 2005). The lack of a direct OS revenue model is not a blocker because the proposal explicitly ties success to indirect search ad yield, not OS licensing.

**Risks:** High carrier resistance, handset maker inertia, multi-year payback, and zero guaranteed adoption. The "open source" strategy relies on ecosystem coordination that historically fails without a hardware anchor or carrier subsidy.

**Edge:** As a search advertiser, the proposer uniquely monetizes *any* increased web usage regardless of the OS vendor. Unlike handset makers who need margin, the proposer only needs traffic. Owning the platform prevents competitors from locking down mobile search access, a defensive necessity even if adoption is slow.

**Analysis:** The "no revenue model" objection is a category error; the OS is infrastructure, not a product. The $50M price for a six-person team is high but negligible compared to the cost of being excluded from the mobile web future. The "do nothing" alternative risks ceding the next computing platform to rivals who might restrict search access. While carrier resistance is real, an open-source model bypasses traditional licensing negotiations by appealing to manufacturers seeking to avoid paying incumbents. The long payback aligns with infrastructure bets. The edge (monetizing traffic vs. selling units) makes this viable where it would fail for a hardware company.

**Kill Criteria:** If no major handset manufacturer commits to the platform within 24 months, or if mobile search query volume from the OS does not exceed 1% of total mobile search within 3 years, the project must be pivoted or shut down.

**Decision:** The strategic necessity of securing mobile search distribution outweighs the execution risks and lack of direct monetization. The cost is affordable, and the defensive value is high.

DECISION: GO
