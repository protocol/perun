**Steelman:** Replacing the flagship formula with a demonstrably superior-tasting alternative is a rational move to halt share loss and align the product with verified consumer preference.

**Attack:** The proposal confuses "sips" with "cases." Blind taste tests measure immediate sensory pleasure, not long-term consumption satisfaction or brand equity. By withdrawing the original entirely, the plan converts a product decision into an existential threat to consumer identity. The testing protocol contained a fatal flaw: it did not disclose the removal of the original, meaning participants evaluated the new taste as an addition, not a substitution. This invalidates the data for a replacement strategy.

**Better Way:** A "line extension" strategy introduces the sweeter formula alongside the original, capturing new users without alienating the core base. This preserves the option to pivot later based on real-world sales rather than lab conditions.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch fails catastrophically. The cause is not the taste, but the perceived arrogance of removing a cultural icon. Loyalists feel betrayed, leading to boycotts, hoarding of old stock, and a media narrative of corporate overreach. The "sweeter" win in a sip test becomes irrelevant against the backlash of loss.

**Blockers:**
1. **Data Invalidity:** The core evidence (taste tests) explicitly excluded the condition of "removal," making it useless for a replacement decision.
2. **No Reversibility:** Withdrawing the original eliminates the fallback, turning a manageable risk into a binary gamble with no safety net.

**Edge:** None. The rival's sweetness is an asset, but the incumbent's heritage is a larger one that this plan actively destroys.

**Kill Criteria:** If the plan proceeds, it must include a public commitment to reintroduce the original if sales drop below a specific threshold within 30 days. Without this, the risk is unmanaged.

**Verdict:** The proposal relies on flawed data regarding the specific action of removal and ignores the non-rational value of brand heritage. It creates a one-way door with no justification other than a sip test.

DECISION: NO-GO
