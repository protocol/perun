Steelman: A converged device leveraging existing music dominance can redefine the mobile market if design control is retained to ensure a superior user experience, even at a premium price with limited initial distribution.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of phone experience is a risk, not a factual blocker, as the core competencies (hardware design, software integration, music ecosystem) are already proven. The premium price targets a specific segment rather than the mass market immediately. Exclusivity is a strategic trade-off for unprecedented carrier concessions (revenue share, design control), not a failure condition.

**Risks:** High price may limit volume; lack of third-party apps reduces initial utility; keyboard-less interface may face resistance from business users; single carrier limits reach. These are valid uncertainties but do not contradict the core hypothesis that a superior, integrated experience creates its own demand.

**Edge:** Unmatched integration of hardware, software, and content (music) that competitors fragmented across device makers, carriers, and software vendors cannot replicate. The brand's existing loyalty in music provides a ready beachhead. Carriers are currently losing control to handset manufacturers; this proposal flips the power dynamic entirely in the proposer's favor, securing a recurring revenue stream rarely seen in hardware.

**Decision Method:**
1.  **Alternatives:** Licensing the OS (loses control), adding a keyboard (compromises screen real estate and modern interface), or waiting (allows competitors to erode music player sales). The proposed path is the only one that secures the ecosystem.
2.  **Fit:** Aligns perfectly with the vision of converging communication and media.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** If it fails, it will be due to price sensitivity or network reliability, not the lack of a keyboard.
4.  **Kill Criteria:** If the carrier refuses the revenue share or demands design changes, the deal dies. Since the proposal states the carrier *agrees*, this condition is met.

The "no experience" objection is outweighed by the unique leverage of the music brand and the structural advantage of the carrier deal. The lack of apps is a v1 constraint, not a fatal flaw, given the strength of the native features.

DECISION: GO
