**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled e-reader removes friction for book buyers and solves publisher piracy fears via a closed ecosystem, creating a new high-margin revenue stream that complements rather than replaces print.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces four critical objections: lack of hardware expertise, publisher resistance to low pricing, historical failure of e-readers, and cannibalization of print sales.

1.  **Hardware Inexperience:** While a valid risk, the company can mitigate this by partnering with established manufacturers rather than building factories, focusing internal resources on the user experience and store integration where they hold dominance.
2.  **Publisher Margins:** The "low price" risk is actually the core value proposition. Publishers fear piracy, not low prices per se. A closed system with DRM addresses the piracy fear directly. The margin squeeze is a strategic choice to drive volume and lock-in, not a fatal flaw.
3.  **Past Failures:** Previous e-readers failed due to poor screens, high costs, and difficult content acquisition (requiring a PC). This proposal specifically solves all three: paper-like screens, free cellular delivery, and a massive existing catalog. The "do nothing" option guarantees zero share in a potential digital future.
4.  **Cannibalization:** This is a false blocker. If the company does not cannibalize its own print sales with a superior digital product, a competitor will. It is better to capture the digital margin internally than to lose the customer entirely.

**Kill Criteria & Edge:**
The primary edge is the existing massive catalog and customer trust, which no new entrant possesses. The risk of delay is losing the first-mover advantage in a defined digital ecosystem. There are no factual blockers in the proposal text; the risks are execution challenges, not reasons to stop. The "do nothing" alternative yields a guaranteed loss of future relevance.

**Verdict:**
The proposal addresses the specific reasons for past failures (usability, connectivity, content availability). The risks are manageable through partnerships and strategic positioning. The opportunity cost of waiting exceeds the cost of potential failure.

DECISION: GO
