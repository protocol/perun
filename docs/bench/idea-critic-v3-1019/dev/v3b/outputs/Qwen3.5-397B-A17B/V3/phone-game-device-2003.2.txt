**Steelman:** Leverage dominant mobile distribution to disrupt handheld gaming via a hybrid device, accepting hardware compromises for market access.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal acknowledges severe flaws (battery removal, awkward ergonomics, high price, weak library) but treats them as risks to be managed rather than facts that make execution impossible. No internal contradiction prevents the launch.

**Risks:** High. The device is objectively inferior to the dedicated leader in core usability (ergonomics, game swapping) and content depth. At $300, it undercuts the leader's console but overprices standard phones, risking rejection by both segments. Publisher apathy could starve the platform immediately.

**Edge:** The proposer is the "largest mobile phone maker." This provides unparalleled distribution channels, manufacturing scale, and an existing user base that the dedicated game competitor cannot match. The strategy bets that convenience and brand reach outweigh specialized performance.

**Analysis:**
The "do nothing" option cedes the gaming segment to the incumbent forever. While the "better way" of licensing games to existing phones exists, it forfeits hardware control and premium pricing. The proposed hybrid, despite its clumsiness, creates a new category: the mobile gamer.

The risks are valid but not fatal blockers. Battery swapping is a friction point, not a showstopper, solvable by larger capacity cells or user habituation. Awkward holding is a trade-off for dual functionality. The price premium over the leader's handheld is justified if the device also functions as a top-tier phone (which the proposer claims to make). The lack of publisher commitment is a chicken-and-egg problem solvable by first-party exclusives or acquisition, leveraging the proposer's capital.

This is a classic "two-way door" experiment. If it fails, the company retreats to standard phones with minimal long-term damage. If it succeeds, it redefines the industry. The edge (distribution/manufacturing) is sufficient to warrant the gamble against the identified risks. The proposal does not claim the device is perfect; it claims the market opportunity justifies the launch.

DECISION: GO
