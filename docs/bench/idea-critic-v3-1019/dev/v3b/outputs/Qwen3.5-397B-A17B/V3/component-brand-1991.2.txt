**Steelman:** Transform an invisible commodity into a consumer demand driver by subsidizing partner ads, forcing retailers to stock computers with this specific chip to satisfy end-user requests, thereby breaking price-based competition with clones.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary objection is that B2B component advertising is unprecedented and may alienate manufacturers who resent brand dilution or loss of creative control. However, the "do nothing" alternative guarantees continued commoditization, where superior engineering loses to clone makers using similar part numbers. Without this campaign, the company cannot capture value from its R&D lead. A cheaper alternative, such as technical certification seals alone, lacks the financial leverage to ensure partner compliance and consumer visibility.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a growth stage requiring differentiation. If this fails, it will likely be due to insufficient partner adoption or a failure to translate the logo into perceived quality. Yet, the cost of delay is catastrophic: every quarter without brand equity allows clones to cement a "good enough" perception, eroding pricing power permanently.

**Evidence & Edge:**
While no direct precedent exists for component branding, the logic holds: if end-users demand a specific ingredient (like fabric content or tire brands), retailers dictate terms to assemblers. The company's edge is its market leadership; only the dominant player can afford this subsidy and offer partners enough volume to justify the co-op arrangement. Clone makers cannot match the budget or the brand recognition potential.

**Blockers, Risks, Edge:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction prevents execution; risks are manageable.
*   **Risks:** Partner resentment, budget drain, unproven consumer response. These are mitigated by making participation voluntary and tying subsidies to verified ad spend.
*   **Edge:** Market dominance allows funding a moat that competitors cannot cross, turning a technical spec into a consumer preference.

**Decision:**
The plan addresses an existential threat (commoditization) with a high-upside, reversible tactical spend (marketing budget). The lack of precedent is the opportunity, not a blocker.

DECISION: GO
