**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 validates demand for a head-worn display while mitigating initial privacy backlash through exclusivity, creating a pathway to mass-market adoption.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "everyday consumer product" positioning test against its own constraints. A $1,500 price point excludes the mass market by definition, contradicting the stated goal of general public availability. More critically, the identified risks are not merely hurdles but fundamental blockers for the proposed use case: without a "clear everyday use," the device is a solution seeking a problem. Privacy objections to a always-on camera in public spaces create immediate social friction that an "invitation-only" program cannot resolve once scaled; early adopters do not normalize surveillance for the general population if the core utility is undefined. The short battery life further undermines "everyday" reliability. The plan attempts to price-discriminate its way out of a product-market fit void, but high cost combined with low utility and high social risk guarantees rejection beyond a tiny niche of tech enthusiasts.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Avoids reputational damage from a failed consumer launch.
2.  **Enterprise Pivot:** Target specific industrial use cases (logistics, manufacturing) where utility is clear, price is secondary, and privacy concerns are manageable. This beats the consumer plan on viability.
3.  **Delayed Launch:** Wait until battery tech improves and use cases are proven elsewhere.

**Edge:** None identified. Being first with an undefined utility and high social cost is a disadvantage, not an edge.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Positioning:** Cannot be both "$1,500 exclusive" and "everyday consumer product."
*   **Undefined Utility:** No clear everyday use case invalidates the mass-market premise.
*   **Social/Privacy Friction:** Unresolved public acceptance issues block scaling.

**Risks:** Brand damage, inventory write-downs, regulatory scrutiny on privacy.

**Verdict:** The proposal lacks a coherent path from early adopter to mass market due to undefined utility and insurmountable social barriers.

DECISION: NO-GO
