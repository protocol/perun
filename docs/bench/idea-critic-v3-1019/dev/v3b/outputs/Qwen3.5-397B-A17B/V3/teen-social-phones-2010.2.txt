**Steelman:** Launching two carrier-bound, data-plan-dependent social phones without apps or games leverages an acquired team's momentum to capture the youth market before the main platform arrives, betting that social connectivity alone justifies smartphone-tier pricing.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" and "fit" tests immediately.
1.  **Value Mismatch:** The target demographic is price-sensitive, yet the cost structure (full data plan + hardware) equals a full smartphone. Without apps or games—the primary drivers of smartphone utility and youth engagement—the value proposition is negative compared to existing feature phones or upcoming smartphones.
2.  **Cannibalization & Timing:** Launching niche devices months before the company's main platform creates internal competition for carrier shelf space and marketing air cover, likely confusing consumers and diluting the main launch.
3.  **Operational Fragility:** The acquiring team already faces delays due to software switches. Adding a complex dual-device launch with a carrier partner, while managing known internal conflict between teams, drastically increases execution risk.
4.  **No Edge:** There is no stated asset (exclusive content, unique network effect) that rivals or the company's own upcoming smartphones lack. "Social networking" on a locked-down device is not a defensible edge against open platforms.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserve capital and carrier goodwill for the main platform launch later in the year.
*   *Software Only:* Port the social features to existing popular handsets to validate demand without hardware risk.
*   *Delay:* Align the social project's release with the main platform to share costs and marketing.

**Blockers:** The unit economics do not work for the target user (high cost, low utility). The strategic timing undermines the company's primary annual revenue driver.

**Risks:** Carrier rejection, inventory write-downs, brand confusion, exacerbated internal conflict.

**Edge:** None identified. The proposal relies on "strategic necessity" rather than a demonstrable advantage.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot cannot prove youth will pay smartphone rates for a non-smartphone experience within 3 months, stop. However, given the fundamental mismatch in value and timing, proceeding as proposed guarantees failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
