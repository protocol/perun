**Steelman:** Dominate the portable market by prioritizing affordability, battery endurance, and a killer software library over raw graphical fidelity, effectively creating a distinct, lower-cost category.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal appears suicidal: a monochrome screen against color rivals, modest hardware, and the company's history of single-game devices suggest obsolescence. However, the "blocker" test reveals no fatal flaws in the *economic* logic. The rival's color screen likely incurs high power consumption and cost, forcing a higher price point and frequent battery changes. By accepting lower specs, this plan achieves a reversible two-way door with a massive price advantage ($89.95) and superior utility (battery life).

The "single-game" history is a process failure, not a hardware one; bundling a sticky puzzle game directly addresses this retention risk. The $20M marketing spend is the critical variable: it must convince parents that "play anywhere, forever" is superior to "pretty colors, dies quickly." If the target audience is children (dependent on parental purchase), battery cost and durability outweigh screen resolution. The thin margins are mitigated by volume and software attach rates, a proven model in the home console division.

**Risks:** Developers may ignore the platform if they perceive it as technologically backward. The puzzle game might fail to resonate.
**Edge:** Unbeatable price-to-utility ratio for the mass market; first-mover advantage in establishing a dedicated third-party ecosystem for a low-cost device.
**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders do not cover 50% of the marketing spend within 60 days, halt production expansion. Leading metric: Software attach rate per unit sold.

The objection that "monochrome is outdated" fails because it ignores the constraint of battery physics and price sensitivity. The cheaper, simpler path *is* the proposed path; doing nothing cedes the mass market to a premium niche competitor.

DECISION: GO
