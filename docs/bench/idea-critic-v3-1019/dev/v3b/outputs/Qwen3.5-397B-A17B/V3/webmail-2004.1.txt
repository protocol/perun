**Steelman:** Offering 1,000x more storage than incumbents with superior search creates a defensible moat via user lock-in, monetized by high-intent contextual ads that offset infrastructure costs.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly addresses the primary constraint (storage cost) by pairing it with a specific revenue model (text ads). While privacy concerns are valid, they are reputational risks, not factual blockers preventing launch, especially given the "invitation-only" rollout which limits initial exposure. The claim that the market is "mature" is contradicted by the stated user behavior of routinely deleting emails due to storage caps; this indicates unmet demand, not saturation.

**Risks:** Privacy backlash from scanning content; high upfront capital expenditure for storage before ad revenue scales; potential regulatory scrutiny. These are managed by the phased "invitation-only" launch, allowing for iterative policy adjustment and cost monitoring.

**Edge:** The proposer is a "search company." This provides a unique, asymmetric advantage in building the "fast search across all mail" feature and optimizing the ad-matching algorithm, capabilities entrenched email incumbents lack. Competitors are constrained by their legacy architecture and low-storage business models.

**Decision Method:**
1.  **Fit:** Aligns with a search company's core competency (indexing and ads).
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the high-value data layer to future competitors. "Small storage" fails to differentiate.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if ad yield per user < storage cost per user. However, email text is high-signal for advertisers, likely yielding higher CPMs than generic web search.
4.  **Evidence:** User behavior (deleting mail) proves the current market offering is broken.
5.  **Kill Criteria:** If ad revenue per user does not cover 50% of storage costs within 12 months, pivot or cap growth.

The "gigabyte" leap is a classic disruptor move: changing the unit economics to make the competitor's model obsolete. The risks are real but manageable; the opportunity to own the primary communication dataset for ad targeting is too significant to pass given the specific edge in search technology.

DECISION: GO
