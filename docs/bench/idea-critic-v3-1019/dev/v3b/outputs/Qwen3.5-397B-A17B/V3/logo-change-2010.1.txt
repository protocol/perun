**Steelman:** A sudden, total rebrand shocks the market into perceiving the retailer as contemporary, avoiding the "uncanny valley" of a partial transition.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on a "one-way door" action (total rollout) without a "two-way door" test (customer validation). In 2010, social media accelerates backlash; alienating loyal customers who identify with the historic square risks immediate revenue loss that a "contemporary" aesthetic cannot quickly recover. The claim that "no advance announcement" prevents negativity is inverted: silence breeds suspicion and feels like a betrayal of heritage, not an evolution.

**Better Way:** A phased rollout starting with digital channels or a limited capsule collection allows measurement of sentiment and sales impact before committing physical inventory and signage. This preserves the option to revert if the "contemporary" look fails to resonate.

**Fit & Evidence:** The goal is to look contemporary, but the method ignores the core asset: recognition. The risks raised (attachment, instant backlash, lack of testing) are not hypothetical; they are verified patterns in rebranding failures where heritage is discarded without consent. The proposal offers no edge—no evidence that this specific demographic desires a stark break from tradition—only an assumption that "new" equals "better."

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales dip 15% as core customers defect to competitors, and social sentiment turns toxic. The company is forced to quietly reintroduce elements of the old logo, admitting the "bold move" was a miscalculation.

**Blockers:** The lack of customer testing on a high-stakes, irreversible change is a factual blocker. You cannot claim a design works for a specific demographic without data from that demographic.

**Risks:** Immediate brand alienation, social media crisis, revenue drop.
**Edge:** None stated; the proposal relies entirely on internal aesthetic preference.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot test shows <5% positive sentiment shift or >2% intent-to-churn increase, the project stops.

**Decision:** The proposal fails the blocker test. It demands an irreversible commitment based on unverified assumptions while ignoring explicit, high-probability risks.

DECISION: NO-GO
