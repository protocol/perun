**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can overwrite the "kids' value" brand identity, forcing adult trial of a premium burger that generates higher margins and diversifies revenue.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests. The chain's core equity is low-price value for families; a premium price point directly contradicts the established value proposition that drives volume. The risk that adults perceive the brand as "fast, not premium" is not a mere risk but a structural blocker supported by the brand's existing image. Furthermore, committing the "largest advertising budget in the industry" before validating demand violates basic unit economics; it bets the company on an unproven hypothesis rather than testing price elasticity or product fit in limited markets. The "do nothing" or "limited test" alternatives offer safer paths to the same insight without catastrophic downside. There is no stated edge (e.g., unique ingredient, exclusive partnership) that explains why *this* specific brand, known for toys and value, would win in a premium segment against established competitors. Fear of stagnation is not an edge.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Brand-Price Mismatch:** The core customer base expects value; a premium price alienates them while failing to attract adults who distrust the brand's premium capability.
2. **Unverified Spend:** Committing maximum industry ad spend before proof of concept creates a non-recoverable sunk cost if the product fails (which the risks suggest is likely).

**Risks:** Total brand dilution, alienation of the core family demographic, massive financial loss from unproven ad spend.

**Edge:** NONE. The proposal cites no unique asset or market signal suggesting this brand can overcome its "cheap/kids" perception.

**Decision Logic:** A plan that contradicts the brand's core value proposition, lacks a defined competitive edge, and demands maximum financial exposure before validation contains fatal blockers. The risks are not hypothetical; they are inherent to the strategy's mismatch with the brand's current reality.

DECISION: NO-GO
