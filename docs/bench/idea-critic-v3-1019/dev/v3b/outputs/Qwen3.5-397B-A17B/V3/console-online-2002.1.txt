Steelman: A unified, paid online service creates a defensible moat through network effects and recurring revenue, differentiating the console via superior user experience (identity, voice, matchmaking) that fragmented, free alternatives cannot match.

**Skeptic:** The claim rests on two fragile assumptions: that broadband adoption will accelerate fast enough to sustain a critical mass in 2002, and that users will pay for a utility competitors give away. The company lacks operational history in hosting; failure here damages the core hardware brand. However, the "free" alternative is currently disjointed and inferior, offering a wedge for quality-focused early adopters.

**Better-way:** "Do nothing" cedes the online future to rivals, commoditizing the console. A hybrid model (free basic, paid premium) dilutes the unified identity value proposition. A publisher-led model repeats the current fragmented status quo. The only viable alternative is delaying launch until broadband penetration hits a specific threshold, but this risks losing the first-mover advantage in defining the standard.

**Kill-criteria:** If broadband household penetration is below 5% by launch, the addressable market is too small to sustain server costs. If the attach rate for the subscription is below 10% of active console owners within six months, the model fails. The one-way door is the massive upfront infrastructure spend; the reversibility lies in shutting down servers later, though brand damage remains.

**Decision:** The proposal identifies a clear strategic edge: unified experience vs. fragmentation. While risks regarding broadband adoption and operational inexperience are real, they are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The "free" status quo is a weakness, not a strength, as it offers poor user experience. The potential to lock in a high-value ecosystem justifies the risk. The lack of operational history is a gap to be filled, not a reason to abstain from a category-defining move. No blocker exists that invalidates the core thesis given the long-term strategic necessity.

DECISION: GO
