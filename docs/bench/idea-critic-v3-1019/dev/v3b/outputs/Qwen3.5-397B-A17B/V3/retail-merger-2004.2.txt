**Steelman:** Acquiring the larger chain provides immediate scale and real estate to pivot the restructured discounter into a broad-line retailer before big-box rivals dominate, betting that merged operations will reverse declining sales through format conversion.

**Attack:** This is a "merger of equals" in distress, not a strength acquiring weakness. Both entities suffer declining sales and underinvestment; combining them aggregates two failing business models rather than fixing either. The proposal admits cost savings are "uncertain" and customer bases differ, suggesting integration friction will outweigh synergies. Converting old, underinvested stores into a new format requires significant capital expenditure exactly when the acquirer has exited restructuring with likely limited liquidity. Big-box rivals are growing precisely because they offer the modern experience these aging chains lack; buying legacy baggage accelerates obsolescence. The plan relies on selling older brands in discount stores, a tactic that rarely offsets the massive operational distraction of merging corporate functions and retrofitting physical locations.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Preserve cash, focus on optimizing the existing restructured footprint, and avoid taking on the target's debt and decay.
2. *Organic Growth:* Invest capital in refurbishing current stores and upgrading supply chains to compete directly with big-box rivals without integration risk.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** The strategy contradicts the need for agility post-restructuring. Assuming failure: integration costs balloon, customer confusion drives further sales drops, and the combined entity runs out of cash while trying to fix two broken networks. The "edge" is nonexistent; there is no unique asset or proven demand here, only the hope that size alone solves structural decline.

**Blockers:** 
1. Both chains have declining sales; no evidence suggests the combination reverses this trend.
2. Cost savings are explicitly admitted as "uncertain."
3. Customer mismatch creates high integration risk with no proven bridge.
4. Capital required for conversion conflicts with post-restructuring financial fragility.

**Edge:** None identified. Fear of big-box growth is not an edge.

**Verdict:** The proposal aggregates risks without a clear mechanism for value creation. It fails the blocker test on multiple fronts: uncertain synergies, conflicting customer bases, and capital intensity amidst decline.

DECISION: NO-GO
