**Steelman:** A mandatory client centralizes updates and anti-cheat, solving critical delivery and integrity failures that boxed media cannot address, while creating a future digital distribution platform.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that limited 2003 broadband adoption and player hostility toward mandatory online checks will cause sales to collapse faster than cheating destroys the brand. However, the status quo ("do nothing") guarantees continued revenue loss from piracy and unplayable buggy discs, which already alienates users. Physical patches are logistically unsustainable. Alternatives like optional downloaders fail to stop cheaters or ensure version uniformity. The "retail partner drop" risk is mitigated by the fact that popular titles drive shelf space, not vice versa; retailers need these games more than the developer needs specific shelf slots.

**Pre-mortem & Door:**
If this fails, it will be due to a catastrophic launch bug preventing login, not the concept itself. This is a one-way door for the specific new titles mandated, but reversible for the company overall if the client is decoupled later. The cost of delay is high: every month without centralized anti-cheat devalues the multiplayer ecosystem, and every physical recall burns cash.

**Evidence & Edge:**
The proposal addresses verified pain points: inability to patch boxed goods and rampant cheating. The edge is not "being first" (competitors may copy), but leveraging existing hit franchises to force adoption where a new entrant could not. The mandatory nature is the feature, not a bug, ensuring 100% update compliance and a unified anti-cheat baseline that voluntary systems cannot match.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (broadband, dislike of accounts) are market frictions, not factual contradictions. The trend lines favor connectivity, and the current solution is broken.
**Risks:** Launch stability, retail friction, user backlash.
**Edge:** Existing popular IP provides the leverage to mandate a behavioral shift competitors lack.

**Decision:**
The proposal beats "do nothing" because the current distribution model is fundamentally defective for software requiring updates and integrity checks. The risks are executional, not strategic, and are outweighed by the necessity of solving the core product failures.

DECISION: GO
