Steelman: A $180 tabletop stereoscopic 3D console with red monochrome displays fills the market gap between handheld and home generations, leveraging existing IP before the next home console arrives in two years.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal admits severe flaws (red-only visuals, discomfort, eye strain, sparse launch library), but these are known risks, not hidden facts contradicting the plan's feasibility. The hardware can be built at the stated price.

**Risks:** High. The "red-on-black" limitation and ergonomic issues threaten mass adoption. Eye strain complaints could damage the brand. A weak launch lineup reduces immediate revenue. However, these are execution and reception risks, not proof the product cannot exist or sell *some* units.

**Edge:** First-mover advantage in affordable consumer 3D gaming. It utilizes current assets to generate revenue during the transitional period before the next home console, preventing a content drought. The low $180 price point lowers the barrier to entry compared to full home systems.

**Analysis:** The proposal explicitly acknowledges its own weaknesses. The "do nothing" alternative yields zero revenue in this segment and wastes the interim period. Cheaper alternatives (accessories for existing consoles) do not offer the same dedicated 3D experience. While the product is flawed, it is a calculated, low-cost experiment ($180 unit price suggests tight margins but low capital risk relative to a full console) to test 3D viability. The two-year window provides a defined runway. The risks are manageable through marketing positioning (niche novelty) and rapid iteration if initial sales warrant it. No factual contradiction prevents launch; the downsides are inherent to the chosen technology stack, which was a deliberate trade-off for cost.

**Kill Criteria:** If unit sales fail to reach profitability within 6 months, or if medical complaints exceed 1% of units sold, halt production.

**Decision:** The plan survives the attack because it accepts its limitations as trade-offs for speed and cost. It beats "do nothing" by capturing a niche market and maintaining momentum.

DECISION: GO
