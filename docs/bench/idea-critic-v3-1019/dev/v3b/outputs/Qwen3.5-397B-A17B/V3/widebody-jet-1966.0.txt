**Steelman:** Building a massively larger, efficient wide-body jet with freighter conversion potential secures long-term dominance against supersonic threats and meets immediate customer demand for lower seat costs.

**Analysis:**
The proposal faces existential financial risk and technological uncertainty regarding the unproven high-bypass engine. The threat of supersonic transport rendering subsonic giants obsolete is a valid strategic concern. However, the "do nothing" alternative cedes the high-capacity market to competitors and ignores the immediate economic pressure from the largest customer.

The raised cockpit design is a critical differentiator, ensuring asset longevity through freighter conversion if passenger dynamics shift. This mitigates the supersonic risk by creating a dual-use asset. While only one airline has ordered, the sheer scale of the request implies a market shift toward hub-and-spoke efficiency that smaller jets cannot match. The development cost, while unprecedented, is the barrier to entry that protects future margins.

**Blockers:** None. The risks are severe but manageable through the proposed design features (freighter conversion) and the strategic necessity of meeting the anchor customer's needs. The lack of multiple orders is a typical early-stage condition for revolutionary infrastructure, not a fatal flaw.

**Edge:** The manufacturer's existing dominance provides the capital access and engineering depth required for such a massive leap, which new entrants lack. The specific design choice (raised cockpit) demonstrates foresight into cargo markets, creating a revenue hedge against passenger volatility.

**Kill Criteria:** If the engine supplier fails to deliver a certified prototype within 24 months, or if two additional major carriers do not sign letters of intent within 12 months, the program must be paused for restructuring.

**Decision:** The potential reward of defining the next century of air travel outweighs the risks, provided the design flexibility for cargo is maintained. The status quo guarantees irrelevance as travel volumes grow.

DECISION: GO
