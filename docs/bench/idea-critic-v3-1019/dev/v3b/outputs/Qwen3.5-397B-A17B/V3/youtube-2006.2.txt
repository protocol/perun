**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant video platform eliminates the buyer's failed organic attempt, secures the category leader instantly, and bets that user growth will outpace legal and infrastructure costs, forcing media companies to partner rather than litigate.

**Attack:** The price is astronomical for a company with zero revenue and massive liabilities. Copyright lawsuits could shut the service down entirely, rendering the asset worthless. Bandwidth costs may scale faster than ad monetization, creating a bottomless pit of operating losses. The "unproven ad model" is a critical gap; if video ads do not yield CPMs comparable to search, the ROI never materializes.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Continue building the smaller internal service; risk losing the category entirely to the target or a third party.
2. *License Content:* Negotiate deals with media companies directly; unlikely to succeed without a massive distribution network.
3. *Wait:* Let the target bleed cash on lawsuits and bandwidth until valuation drops; high risk they fold or are bought by a competitor first.

**Fit:** Aligns with organizing the world's information, but assumes video is the next search frontier.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if courts issue an injunction against user uploads before ad revenue scales, or if bandwidth costs exceed total ad income permanently.

**Edge:** The buyer has deep cash reserves (stock currency) to absorb losses and the engineering talent to solve bandwidth efficiency better than the target. The target has unbeatable network effects.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks are severe but quantifiable as "cost of doing business" to win the category. No factual contradiction prevents the deal; it is a high-stakes bet on behavior shift.
**Risks:** Litigation outcome, monetization lag, infrastructure burn.
**Edge:** Category dominance and network effects that cannot be organically replicated.

**Decision:** The strategic necessity of owning the video category outweighs the financial and legal risks, provided the buyer can leverage its scale to solve bandwidth and negotiate legal settlements.
