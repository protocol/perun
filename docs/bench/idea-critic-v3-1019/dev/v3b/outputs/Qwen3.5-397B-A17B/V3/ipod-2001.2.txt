**Steelman:** A premium, high-capacity device creates a seamless ecosystem lock-in for a niche user base, establishing a beachhead before mass-market licensing resolves.

**Attack & Options:**
The proposal fails the "addressable base" test. With a small computer share and no consumer experience, restricting playback to proprietary software shrinks the potential market to near zero. Cheaper competitors exist; while they sell poorly, they set a price anchor this $400 device shatters. The "do nothing" option preserves capital. A "better way" would be licensing software to Windows immediately or launching a lower-capacity, lower-price flash prototype to test demand.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This contradicts the constraint of "no consumer experience." Assuming the company can source custom hard drives and build a supply chain in under a year without prior vendor relationships is a fatal assumption. Pre-mortem: The device launches late, the hard drive supply fails, and the high price prevents adoption among the tiny installed base, resulting in massive write-offs. The cost of delay is low; the music download market is not yet legal or scaled. Waiting for licensing deals reduces execution risk.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
No evidence suggests consumers will pay a 2-3x premium for a device that only works with a minority of computers. The core claim—that a closed system can succeed before open licensing exists—is unsupported by the stated facts (poor sales of existing players, tiny computer share).

**Blockers:**
1. **Market Size:** The intersection of "owns specific computer" and "wants $400 player" is too small to sustain production costs.
2. **Supply Chain:** No verified path to source 5GB drives in <1 year without consumer hardware history.
3. **Value Prop:** No legal download ecosystem exists to justify the hardware investment yet.

**Edge:**
None stated. "Integration" is a feature, not a market edge when the platform share is minimal.

**Verdict:**
The plan relies on unproven demand, impossible supply timelines, and a self-limiting distribution model. It bets the company on a closed ecosystem before the content exists.

DECISION: NO-GO
