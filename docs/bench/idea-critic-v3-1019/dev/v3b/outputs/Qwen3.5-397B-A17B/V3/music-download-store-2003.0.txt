**Steelman:** A dominant hardware vendor leverages its installed base to launch a legal, per-song music store, securing label licenses to convert file-sharers into paying customers despite thin margins and platform restrictions.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal addresses the existential threat of piracy by offering a superior, legal alternative integrated with the best-selling hardware. While margins are thin, the strategic value of driving hardware sales and securing ecosystem lock-in outweighs immediate software profitability. The "own computers only" restriction is a feature, not a bug, in 2003; it differentiates the hardware in a commoditizing market.

**Risks:** Labels withdrawing licenses is a valid concern, but the alternative (unchecked piracy) offers them zero revenue, making partnership their least-worst option. Rivals betting on subscriptions face higher friction and consumer resistance to recurring fees for ownership; the per-song model aligns better with existing consumer behavior. Free file-sharing remains a competitor, but convenience and integration often beat "free" for mainstream users.

**Edge:** The company controls the entire vertical stack: the device people already want, the software interface, and now the content pipeline. No rival can replicate this seamless hardware-software-content integration. The existing sales momentum of the portable player provides an immediate distribution channel that standalone software rivals lack.

**Decision Logic:** The cost of delay is catastrophic; waiting for a "perfect" cross-platform or subscription model cedes the market to piracy permanently. The "do nothing" option guarantees hardware commoditization as music becomes free. This plan creates a defensible moat. The risks are manageable execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The edge (vertical integration) is unique and potent.

**Kill Criteria:** If major labels refuse licensing within 6 months, or if attach rate (songs per device) remains below 5 after one year, the strategy must pivot to subscription or licensing the store to other devices.

DECISION: GO
