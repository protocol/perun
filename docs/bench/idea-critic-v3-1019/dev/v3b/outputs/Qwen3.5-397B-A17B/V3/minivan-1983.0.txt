**Steelman:** A cash-strapped carmaker can survive by creating a new "minivan" category using existing compact-car parts and a converted factory, offering car-like driving in a garage-friendly package before rivals react.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker is the claim that "no such vehicle category exists." Historically, the absence of a category often signals unmet demand rather than lack of interest. Families currently compromise between bulky vans and cramped wagons; a garage-fit vehicle solves a specific, painful constraint.

The financial risk ("little cash") is mitigated by the proposal's core mechanism: reusing an existing compact-car platform and converting a factory. This avoids the capital expenditure of a clean-sheet design and new tooling, making it the cheapest possible entry into a new segment. If the platform cannot support the weight or space requirements, the project fails, but compact platforms of this era often had unused capacity relative to body-on-frame vans.

The risk of buyers seeing it as a "commercial van" is a marketing challenge, not a product flaw. Positioning it as a family vehicle with car amenities (carpeting, easy entry) differentiates it immediately from work trucks. Rivals copying the idea is inevitable but irrelevant to the initial go/no-go; being first allows the company to define the segment and capture early adopters while competitors are still retooling.

The "do nothing" alternative guarantees continued decline toward collapse. The "full-size van" alternative ignores the garage constraint. The "station wagon" alternative ignores the capacity need. This proposal is the only path that addresses the specific gap in the market with the limited resources available.

**Blockers:** NONE. The platform limitations are a design constraint, not a showstopper, given the target size.
**Risks:** Marketing misfire (commercial perception); slower than expected adoption. These are manageable via positioning and do not prevent launch.
**Edge:** First-mover advantage in a newly defined category using low-cost existing assets, directly addressing a known family pain point (garage fit) that incumbents ignore.

DECISION: GO
