Steelman: A clear cola leverages test market enthusiasm to capture health-conscious consumers seeking purity, creating a defensible niche before competitors react.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The core objection is not merely risk, but a fundamental contradiction in the product's value proposition relative to category norms. Cola flavor is psychologically inextricable from dark color; removing the color removes the sensory cue that validates the flavor promise. Test markets, often driven by novelty and limited availability, notoriously overstate long-term demand for gimmick products. The "positive response" is likely a curiosity spike, not a repeat-purchase signal.

The "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the "do nothing" comparison. Launching nationwide with a "large marketing campaign" incurs massive sunk costs for a product that may actively confuse the brand's core identity. If shoppers associate the flagship brand with this failed experiment, cannibalization becomes brand erosion, not just sales shifting. The risks listed by the proposal itself (novelty fade, flavor-color disconnect, copycats) are not mitigated; they are accepted as costs of doing business. This is reckless.

A cheaper, safer alternative exists: a limited regional rollout without a massive national campaign, designed specifically to test repeat purchase rates over six months, not just initial trial. The current proposal demands a binary, irreversible national commitment based on weak leading indicators. The "edge" is nonexistent; being the second-largest maker does not grant immunity from category laws, and "purity" is an unproven driver for cola specifically.

The evidence provided (test market positivity) is insufficient to overcome the structural barrier of flavor-color expectation. The proposal asks for a high-cost, low-reversibility decision based on data known to be flawed in this specific context (novelty bias). There is no named asset that guarantees this specific extension will succeed where others have failed due to similar sensory mismatches.

Blockers: The psychological link between cola flavor and dark color contradicts the product design; test market data is likely inflated by novelty.
Risks: High cannibalization of flagship, rapid novelty fade, competitor copying.
Edge: NONE.

DECISION: NO-GO
