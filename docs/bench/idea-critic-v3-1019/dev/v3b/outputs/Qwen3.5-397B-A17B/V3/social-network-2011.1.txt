**Steelman:** Leveraging massive existing distribution (search, mail) to solve cold-start problems, using unique video tech to carve a niche before scaling.

**Attack:**
1.  **History as Data:** Three prior failures indicate a structural inability to execute social products, not just bad timing. Repeating the same "build it and they will come" strategy ignores this evidence.
2.  **Forced Integration Risk:** Injecting social layers into utility products (search, mail) risks degrading core user experience. If users feel coerced, backlash increases churn in profitable core businesses.
3.  **Incentive Distortion:** Tying executive bonuses to adoption metrics encourages gaming the system (e.g., dark patterns, forced migrations) rather than building genuine value, potentially accelerating user resentment.
4.  **Network Effect Reality:** The incumbent's hundreds of millions of users create a moat that "circles" and "hangouts" cannot easily breach unless they offer 10x utility immediately. Differentiators are features, not platform shifts.
5.  **Opportunity Cost:** A company-wide push diverts engineering talent from core search dominance during a critical era of mobile transition.

**Better Way:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Maintain core dominance; let social experiments remain small, isolated skunkworks projects without brand risk.
*   **Acquire:** Buy an existing growing network rather than building from scratch against a entrenched rival.
*   **Pivot:** Focus video hangouts as a standalone communication tool (B2B or niche) rather than a broad social graph challenger.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   If core product satisfaction drops >5% post-integration.
*   If active daily users in the beta do not reach 20% of invitees within 30 days.
*   If the cost per acquired active user exceeds 10x the industry average.

**Verdict:** The proposal ignores three prior failures, risks core revenue streams via forced integration, and relies on incentive structures prone to manipulation. The "edge" is distribution, but history suggests this company cannot convert distribution into social retention. The risks outweigh the speculative upside given the track record.

DECISION: NO-GO
