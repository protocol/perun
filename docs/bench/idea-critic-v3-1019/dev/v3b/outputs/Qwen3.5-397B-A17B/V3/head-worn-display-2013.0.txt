**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 validates demand for a head-worn computer while mitigating mass-market backlash through scarcity and early-adopter social buffering.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "everyday consumer" positioning test against its own constraints. A $1,500 price point excludes the mass market required to normalize the device socially; without normalization, the "social acceptance" risk intensifies rather than fades. The "invitation-only" mechanism contradicts the goal of an "everyday consumer product" by creating exclusivity that prevents the critical mass needed to solve the privacy and social stigma objections. Furthermore, short battery life and lack of clear everyday use cases are fundamental product failures, not launch strategy problems; no amount of phased rolling out fixes a device that cannot last a workday or solve a common problem. The risks listed are not launch hurdles but core viability blockers.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids reputational damage from a failed consumer push; preserves tech for enterprise use where privacy/battery are manageable.
2.  **Enterprise Pivot:** Target industrial/medical fields where $1,500 is standard, utility is high, and camera privacy is a feature, not a bug.
3.  **Prototype Only:** Keep as a research demo until battery and use-case clarity improve.

**Fit:** The strategy assumes a market ready for ubiquitous computing in 2013, which contradicts the stated risks of privacy and social acceptance.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sells only to tech enthusiasts, is banned from restaurants and cinemas due to privacy fears, battery life frustrates daily use, and it is discontinued within two years as a social failure.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Edge:** The proposal offers no asset or advantage to overcome the stated lack of everyday use or privacy hostility.
*   **Contradiction:** "Everyday consumer" positioning is incompatible with "$1,500 invitation-only" access and unresolved core utility deficits.

**Risks:** Social backlash, privacy regulation, rapid obsolescence due to battery limits.

**Edge:** NONE. Fear of falling behind is not an edge.

**Verdict:** The plan attempts to sell an unfinished product (poor battery, no use case) to a market explicitly hostile to its primary feature (camera).

DECISION: NO-GO
