**Steelman:** A premium, self-balancing transporter can create a new urban mobility category by leveraging first-mover advantage and brand prestige to overcome high costs and regulatory ambiguity.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal; the risks cited are uncertainties, not proven failures. The price matches the premium positioning, and factory size aligns with early-adopter scaling.

**Risks:** Regulatory bans could limit usage zones; high price restricts mass adoption; social stigma ("conspicuous") may dampen consumer demand; limited utility reduces repeat value. These are valid but typical for category-creating hardware.

**Edge:** The combination of a well-known inventor, prominent investors, and heavy publicity provides a unique marketing moat that competitors cannot immediately replicate. This specific convergence of assets creates a plausible path to defining the category before regulations or cheaper alternatives solidify.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because regulators classified it as a motor vehicle requiring licenses, or the "conspicuous" factor made it a cultural joke rather than a status symbol. However, these are risks to manage, not blockers to stop.

**Kill Criteria:** If regulatory clarity does not emerge in key pilot cities within 12 months, or if sales to institutional buyers do not reach 5,000 units in the first year, production must be capped and the pivot to niche markets executed immediately.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal represents a classic "one-way door" opportunity where waiting for perfect clarity cedes the category to others. The risks are inherent to innovation, not flaws in the plan. The edge (brand/investor momentum) is real and time-sensitive. A "go-if" is unnecessary because the current plan already accounts for the scale of uncertainty via the limited factory size (40k units).

DECISION: GO
