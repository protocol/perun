Steelman: A hybrid form factor leverages the maker's unique handheld heritage to create a distinct market niche, avoiding direct specification wars with powerful rivals while offering versatility that phones cannot match.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal; the financial losses stem from the previous home console, which this plan explicitly replaces with a new category. The "weak specs" are a strategic trade-off for mobility and cost, not a failure of engineering.

**Risks:** Third-party support may lag due to architectural differences; supply constraints could dampen launch momentum; a thin game lineup risks weak software attach rates initially. These are execution risks, not fundamental flaws.

**Edge:** The maker possesses a deep library of iconic first-party franchises that drive hardware sales regardless of third-party breadth. The hybrid concept resolves the "confusing positioning" of the past by unifying home and portable play into a single, clear value proposition: play anywhere. Competitors lack a credible hybrid offering, leaving this niche uncontested.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the price point exceeds the perceived value of the weaker specs or if the detachable controllers are seen as gimmicky rather than functional. However, pricing parity with rivals neutralizes the spec disadvantage by shifting the comparison to versatility.

**Kill Criteria:** If first-year sell-through does not exceed the previous console's lifecycle average by 20% within 18 months, or if third-party title count remains below 50 major releases by year-end, the strategy must pivot to aggressive price cuts or service bundling.

**Decision Method:** The "do nothing" alternative guarantees continued erosion by phones and irrelevance in the home market. Cheaper alternatives (software-only or accessories) do not address the hardware deficit. This plan beats "do nothing" by creating a new category where the maker holds the advantage. The risk of confusion is mitigated by the clarity of the hybrid message. The edge (first-party IP) directly addresses the thin launch lineup risk.

DECISION: GO
