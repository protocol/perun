**Steelman:** A mandatory client solves critical update/cheat failures and creates a defensible distribution moat, turning a vulnerability into a platform advantage despite short-term friction.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that limited 2003 broadband and retail backlash will kill adoption. However, "do nothing" guarantees continued revenue loss from piracy and inability to patch, which is fatal for live-service potential. The alternative of optional patches fails to stop cheaters. A "retail-only" approach ignores the digital shift already beginning. The risk of retail partners dropping titles is real but manageable if the publisher's hits are strong enough to force carrier status; the risk of copying is mitigated by first-mover network effects.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the north star of recurring revenue and IP protection. If this fails, it will be due to poor client execution (bugs/crashes) rather than the concept itself. The cost of delay is high: every month without anti-cheat devalues the multiplayer ecosystem.

**Evidence & Edge:**
The proposal addresses a verified operational failure (cheating/updates) that physical boxes cannot fix. The edge is the publisher's existing hit games, which provide the initial user base required to make the client viable, a leverage point competitors and retailers lack. The "mandatory" nature is the feature, not a bug, ensuring 100% patch compliance.

**Blockers:** NONE. The cited risks (broadband, retail) are market conditions, not factual contradictions of the plan's mechanics. Broadband was growing rapidly in 2003; early adopters (the core gaming demographic) had access. Retail pushback is a negotiation tactic, not a hard stop for essential software.
**Risks:** Execution quality, user friction. These are managed via kill criteria (e.g., if client crash rate >5% in beta, halt rollout).
**Edge:** Proprietary hits force adoption; competitors cannot retroactively mandate a client for existing installed bases as effectively.

**Decision:**
The plan beats "do nothing" which accepts inevitable decay. The risks are executional, not fundamental. The strategic upside of owning the distribution layer outweighs the transitional friction.

DECISION: GO
