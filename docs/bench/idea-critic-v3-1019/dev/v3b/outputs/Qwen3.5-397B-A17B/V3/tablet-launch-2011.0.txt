**Steelman:** Leverage the computer maker's brand and the acquired OS to enter the tablet market immediately, accepting a small app catalog initially while aggressively courting developers to close the gap with the dominant leader.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "edge" test. The market leader dominates with a massive app ecosystem, which is the primary driver of tablet adoption. The proposal admits the app catalog is a "small fraction" of the leader's yet suggests pricing "in line with the market leader." Without a price advantage or a superior ecosystem, there is no rational reason for a consumer to choose this unproven device over the established category king.

The risks are not merely "unproven status"; they are structural blockers. An operating system with "almost no market share" creates a negative network effect: users won't buy it without apps, and developers won't build apps without users. Heavy investment in advertising cannot overcome a fundamental product deficit (lack of apps) when a superior alternative exists at the same price point.

The "leadership transition" adds execution risk to an already fragile strategy. The proposal offers no "cheaper, simpler" alternative, such as launching at a significantly lower price point to force adoption or delaying launch until the app catalog reaches a critical threshold. It attempts to fight a network-effect battle with a marketing budget, which historically fails when the incumbent's moat is this wide.

**Blockers:**
1. **Price-Value Mismatch:** Pricing equal to the market leader despite having a fraction of the apps creates an immediate economic disqualifier for consumers.
2. **No Defined Edge:** Brand heritage does not compensate for a missing core utility (apps) in a two-sided market.

**Risks:**
Massive capital expenditure on advertising yielding negligible market share; developer outreach failing due to lack of installed base.

**Decision:**
The plan lacks a viable wedge. It requires a price disruption or a delayed launch contingent on app growth. As proposed, it is a direct assault on a fortified position with inferior ammunition.

DECISION: NO-GO
