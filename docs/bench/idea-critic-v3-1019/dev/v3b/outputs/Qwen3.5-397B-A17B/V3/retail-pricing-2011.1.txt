**Steelman:** Leveraging a proven electronics retail model to modernize a tired department store brand, eliminating margin-eroding promotions and creating a curated, shop-in-shop experience to attract higher-value traffic.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores a fundamental mismatch in customer psychology and inventory dynamics. Electronics are high-consideration, low-frequency purchases where price transparency builds trust; the proposed model works because customers do not "wait" for a TV sale in the same way they wait for apparel. The department store's core base is explicitly "coupon-driven" and trained to defer purchases until discounts appear. Removing coupons instantly removes their purchase trigger without a guaranteed replacement demographic. Furthermore, apparel has high seasonality and rapid trend decay compared to electronics; a "everyday price" on last season's clothing is a liability, not a stable asset.

**Better Way:** A "do nothing" baseline retains current, albeit falling, revenue. A superior alternative is a hybrid transition: maintain the coupon framework for the legacy base while testing "everyday low price" zones in 5-10 locations to validate if the new model can acquire enough new customers to offset the inevitable loss of the old base before a full chain-wide rollout.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales collapse by 40%. Legacy customers stop visiting entirely, feeling alienated by the lack of expected discounts. New, younger shoppers do not arrive in sufficient numbers because the store's location and brand perception remain "old department store," not "trendy boutique." Inventory piles up as non-discounted goods become stale. The company runs out of cash before the rebrand can take hold.

**Blockers:**
1. **Customer Behavior Mismatch:** The core revenue source (discount-trained shoppers) is removed without evidence they will adapt or be replaced.
2. **No Pilot:** Rolling out a radical behavioral shift across the entire chain simultaneously eliminates the ability to course-correct based on data.

**Edge:** None stated. The CEO's past success in a different category (electronics) is not a transferable asset here without validation.

**Decision:** The plan bets the company on an unproven hypothesis regarding customer migration while actively alienating the existing revenue base. The lack of a pilot makes this an unnecessary gamble.

DECISION: NO-GO
