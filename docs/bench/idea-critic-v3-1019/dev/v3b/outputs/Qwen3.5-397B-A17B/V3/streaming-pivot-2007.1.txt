The claim is that adding a free, limited streaming service in 2007 protects a profitable DVD monopoly against future disruption. The steelman: this is a low-cost "two-way door" experiment to retain subscribers and gather data before giants dominate, leveraging existing cash flow to subsidize a necessary transition.

However, the "no extra charge" model paired with high licensing costs and a small, old catalogue creates immediate financial drag without clear retention upside. If video quality is poor and broadband is scarce, the feature offers negligible user value, failing to act as a effective moat. The risk of cannibalization is real if the streaming option, however poor, signals that the DVD model is dying, potentially accelerating subscriber churn before the streaming tech is viable.

The "do nothing" alternative preserves current margins entirely. A "better way" would be to launch streaming as a paid add-on or a distinct beta for a small segment, isolating costs and validating demand without devaluing the core product or inflating licensing bills for a feature most cannot yet use. Entering a price war for rights against deep-pocketed tech entrants with an inferior product is a strategic error.

The pre-mortem reveals failure: the company burns cash on licenses for a feature few use, while signaling to the market that DVDs are obsolete, causing profitable subscribers to leave for competitors who offer better streaming or stick with DVDs longer. The cost of delay is low; the technology and broadband penetration will improve, allowing a stronger entry later with better economics. There is no named "edge" here; the company lacks a content advantage, a tech advantage, or a distribution advantage in streaming compared to incoming rivals. The proposal relies on fear of missing out rather than a defensible position.

Blockers: The economics (high cost, low value) contradict the goal of profitability. The strategy (free access to inferior product) lacks a mechanism to prevent cannibalization or attract new users.
Risks: Accelerated decline of the core business; wasted capital on rights.
Edge: None identified; the brand is tied to physical media.

DECISION: NO-GO
