**Steelman:** A dominant hardware vendor leverages its installed base to launch a legal, à la carte music store at $0.99/song, securing major label licenses to combat piracy and lock users into its ecosystem, despite low margins and platform exclusivity.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces four critical threats: ubiquitous free alternatives, fragile label relationships, razor-thin margins, and confinement to a single hardware platform. However, the "free" alternative carries legal risk and poor user experience (searching, viruses, incomplete files), which a seamless paid store directly addresses. The risk of labels withdrawing licenses is mitigated by their desperate need for a legal revenue stream to replace collapsing CD sales; they are partners in survival, not just licensors.

Regarding margins: the goal is not immediate high profit per song, but to drive hardware sales. The music store acts as a loss leader or break-even utility that increases the value of the portable player, justifying a premium hardware price. The "own computers only" restriction is initially a bug but strategically functions as a feature to create a unique selling proposition for the hardware, differentiating it from rivals pushing subscriptions (which suffer from ownership anxiety and connectivity issues in 2003).

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because the price point remains too high compared to "free" or because the catalog is incomplete. However, the integration of hardware, software, and store creates a moat that disjointed subscription services cannot match.

**Edge:** The proposer controls the entire vertical stack (device, OS, store). This allows for a frictionless user experience ("buy and play in one click") that no third-party software or subscription rival can replicate on the device. The existing successful player provides the distribution channel.

**Blockers:** None. The risks are manageable strategic bets, not factual impossibilities.
**Risks:** Label renegotiation, slow adoption due to price.
**Kill Criteria:** If major labels refuse licensing or hardware sales do not increase within 12 months.

The proposal turns a hardware company into a platform owner, solving the piracy problem with convenience rather than litigation.

DECISION: GO
