**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only viable channel to demonstrate the integrated hardware-software music strategy, as third-party staff actively suppress the brand to push higher-margin competitors.

**Attack & Options:**
The proposal faces severe blockers: low industry margins, high fixed lease costs during a recession, and a historical track record of PC makers failing in retail. "Do nothing" preserves cash but cedes the narrative to competitors. A "shop-in-shop" alternative reduces cost but fails to solve the staff-incentive conflict.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a turnaround vision requiring brand control, not just unit volume. However, assuming a recession-proof demand for premium PCs is dangerous. If the music strategy fails to gain traction, these stores become massive sunk costs with no exit ramp. The "poor industry track record" is a verified fact, not a mere risk.

**Evidence & Edge:**
The critical edge is the specific failure of the current channel: staff incentivized to sell cheaper rivals. This unique constraint makes the general industry failure rate less relevant if the new stores solve the *demonstration* gap. However, the financial math relies on unproven assumptions: that foot traffic will convert at high enough rates to cover prime mall leases despite low PC margins. The proposal lacks a capped pilot or specific kill criteria for lease commitments.

**Decision Logic:**
While the strategic logic (controlling the demo) is sound, the financial execution as proposed is a blocker. Opening a full chain of prime mall locations immediately, amidst losses and a recession, without a phased pilot or verified unit-economics model, violates basic capital preservation. The "edge" (brand control) does not automatically overcome the "blocker" (massive fixed costs vs. unproven conversion). The plan must be revised to include a small-scale pilot to validate lease-to-sales ratios before a full rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
