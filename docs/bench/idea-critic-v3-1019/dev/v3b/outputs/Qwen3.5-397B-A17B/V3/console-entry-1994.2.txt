Steelman: A hardware incumbent can pivot to a 3D CD-console by leveraging superior developer tools and lower media costs to disrupt a duopoly, despite lacking platform experience.

**Skeptic:** The proposal admits zero experience as a platform holder. Success relies entirely on third-party developers abandoning established relationships with incumbents who control loyal user bases and iconic IP. The "easier tools" claim is unproven; if the SDK is immature at launch, the ecosystem collapses. Hardware margins are negative; without immediate software volume, cash burn will be catastrophic.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" option avoids massive R&D sunk costs in a losing war. A cheaper alternative is licensing the CD technology to existing players rather than building a standalone console, capturing media revenue without platform risk. Entering via a partnership with a non-competing arcade manufacturer could share the burden.

**Kill-Criteria:** If the incumbent cannot secure exclusive launch titles from top-tier third parties before the rival's 3D launch, the window closes. If hardware subsidy exceeds $150 per unit without a path to recoup via software royalties within 18 months, the model fails.

**Pre-mortem:** The console launches with weak games; developers cite difficult documentation. The rival's brand loyalty holds firm. The company is stuck with warehouses of unsold hardware and no recurring software revenue, forcing a total exit from the consumer market.

**Edge:** The only stated edge is "cheaper disc manufacturing." This is a cost advantage, not a product advantage. It does not guarantee users will switch or developers will port. Fear of the rival's upcoming 3D launch is not an edge; it is a threat.

**Blockers:** NONE (facts allow the attempt).
**Risks:** High execution difficulty, unproven demand for non-first-party IP, potential for massive losses due to hardware subsidies.
**Edge:** NONE (cost savings do not equal market win).

Without a proven asset to attract developers or a unique product feature beyond media format, the plan relies on hope rather than leverage. The risks outweigh the unproven upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
