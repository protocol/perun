The claim asserts that combining two declining internet media assets will create a scaled competitor to dominant ad platforms. The steelman is that only by merging these specific distressed assets can the carrier achieve the minimum scale required to survive in mobile advertising, leveraging the carrier's distribution to reverse traffic declines.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The core mechanism relies on reversing years of falling traffic and ad revenue in both the acquirer's existing unit and the target. There is no evidence provided that the carrier possesses the product capability, culture, or management stability to arrest these declines, given its lack of consumer media history and the target's repeated management failures. The "scale" being purchased is shrinking; buying a larger decline does not create a viable competitor against growing incumbents.

The "better way" analysis highlights that doing nothing avoids deploying $4.8 billion into a deteriorating market where the buyer has no comparative advantage. A cheaper alternative would be investing in the carrier's core connectivity or partnering with an existing successful platform rather than attempting to build a media empire from two failing parts. The pre-mortem is clear: integration drags down the carrier's focus while the combined ad revenue continues to slide, resulting in a massive write-down.

The fatal flaw is the assumption that scale alone creates competitiveness. In advertising, relevance and growth trajectory matter more than raw legacy size. The proposal offers no "edge"—no unique asset or capability this specific buyer brings that rivals lack—to justify why this combination would succeed where previous management teams failed. The risks (revenue decline, integration) are not merely potential; they are active, verified trends that the plan does not credibly reverse. This is a classic "falling knife" acquisition driven by strategic fear rather than a demonstrable path to value.

Blockers: The buyer has no history in consumer media; both assets have verified, multi-year declining revenue and traffic; no mechanism to reverse decline is proven.
Risks: Integration failure, continued ad market share loss to dominants.
Edge: None stated; "strategic necessity" is not an edge.

DECISION: NO-GO
