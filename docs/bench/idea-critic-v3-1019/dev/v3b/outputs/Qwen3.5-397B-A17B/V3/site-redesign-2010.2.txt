Steelman: A complete rewrite with a new surfacing algorithm and publisher focus, launched via big-bang migration, will reverse traffic decline by modernizing the platform and attracting premium content.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Irreversibility:** The "move all users at once" approach creates a one-way door. If the new voting mechanics alienate the core community (a stated risk), there is no rollback path to restore traffic or trust.
2.  **Contradictory Incentives:** The proposal favors publisher content while acknowledging the loyal community values existing voting mechanics. History shows that displacing core user incentives in social platforms often triggers immediate exodus, accelerating the very traffic decline this aims to fix.
3.  **Engineering Risk vs. Competitive Threat:** A full rewrite is a massive engineering undertaking with high failure rates. Executing this while a rival is growing divides focus; if the rewrite stalls or fails, the company loses ground to the competitor with no interim gain.

**Risks:**
*   Community revolt due to changed voting mechanics.
*   Technical debt or bugs in a from-scratch rewrite delaying launch.
*   Publisher content failing to engage existing users.

**Edge:**
None stated. The proposal relies on the assumption that "new" equals "better" without evidence that the specific changes (publisher focus) address the root cause of slowing traffic rather than exacerbating it. Fear of the rival's growth is a motivator, not a strategic edge.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "one-way door" test. A big-bang launch of a rewritten core product, where the primary risk is alienating the existing user base, is strategically unsound. The lack of a phased rollout or A/B testing mechanism means the company is betting its entire remaining value on an unproven hypothesis. The "publisher focus" directly conflicts with the "community values" constraint, creating a high probability of user churn. Without a reversible pilot or a phased migration to validate the new surfacing logic, the risk of catastrophic failure outweighs the potential upside. The correct move is a staged rollout or a parallel launch, not a forced immediate migration.

DECISION: NO-GO
