**Steelman:** A clear cola leverages positive test data to capture demand for a "purer" alternative, diversifying the portfolio against slowing category growth before competitors act.

**Attack:** The core claim relies on test markets translating to national repeat purchase. However, the proposal admits shoppers psychologically link cola flavor with dark color. If the product violates this sensory expectation, initial trial (driven by novelty) will not convert to repeat sales. The risk of cannibalization is high: if the clear cola fails to retain users, it merely erodes the flagship's shelf space and marketing focus without adding net volume. Furthermore, a "large marketing campaign" increases the cost of failure; if the product is a novelty fad, the sunk cost will be substantial. The proposal offers no kill criteria to distinguish between a viable new habit and a short-term curiosity. Without a mechanism to verify repeat rates before a full nationwide spend, the plan bets the brand's momentum on an unproven sensory shift.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Preserve flagship margin; avoid cannibalization.
2. *Staged Rollout:* Launch in limited regions with capped marketing to measure repeat rates before national commitment.
3. *Proposed Plan:* High spend, high risk of rapid failure if novelty fades.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the blocker test. The admitted risk that "novelty buyers may not repeat" combined with the sensory contradiction (clear vs. cola flavor) creates a high probability of a failed launch that cannibalizes the core brand. A nationwide launch with a large campaign is irreversible once spent. The proposal lacks a "go-if" condition based on verified repeat purchase data from the test markets. It demands a binary choice (launch or don't) rather than a measured step. The edge is undefined; being second-largest does not grant immunity from sensory backlash. The cost of delay (waiting for better data) is lower than the cost of a failed national rollout.

Blockers: High probability of non-repeat due to sensory mismatch; lack of kill criteria for novelty fade.
Risks: Cannibalization, competitor copying, wasted marketing spend.
Edge: None stated beyond test market positivity, which is acknowledged as potentially inflated.

DECISION: NO-GO
