**Steelman:** The current discount model erodes brand equity and trains customers to devalue merchandise; a decisive pivot to everyday low pricing (EDLP) with branded shops will restore margin integrity and attract a broader demographic, leveraging the CEO's proven electronics retail playbook.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the specific behavioral conditioning of the existing customer base. Unlike electronics, where specifications drive value, this department store's volume relies on the psychological trigger of "saving" via coupons. The customer base is older and explicitly "coupon-driven." Removing the discount mechanism removes the primary purchase incentive for the core revenue engine. Furthermore, the CEO's past success was in a different category (electronics) with different margin structures and customer expectations; transposing that strategy without adaptation is a category error.

**Better Way:** A "do nothing" approach maintains cash flow but accelerates brand decay. A superior alternative is a phased pilot: test EDLP in 5-10 stores while maintaining the coupon model elsewhere. This validates whether the brand has enough equity to sustain full-price sales before risking the entire chain. The cheapest alternative is a hybrid model, introducing everyday pricing on select private-label goods while keeping national brands on promotion.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Assuming the goal is long-term viability, the fit is poor because the timeline (immediate chain-wide rollout) assumes customer loyalty exceeds price sensitivity. If this fails, the pre-mortem shows a immediate 30-40% revenue drop as coupon shoppers defect to competitors, with insufficient new customers arriving to fill the gap. The cost of delay (waiting for a pilot) is negligible compared to the cost of a chain-wide collapse.

**Blockers:** The lack of a pilot is a critical blocker. The proposal relies on an unverified assumption that the electronics CEO's strategy translates directly to soft goods with a different demographic. The evidence (historical reliance on discounts) contradicts the core claim that customers will accept full price immediately.

**Edge:** There is no stated edge (asset, cost advantage, or proven demand) that guarantees this specific chain can win with EDLP against established competitors who already offer low prices. Fear of falling behind is not an edge.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the blocker test due to the absence of a pilot and the high probability of alienating the core customer base without a proven replacement demand source.

DECISION: NO-GO
