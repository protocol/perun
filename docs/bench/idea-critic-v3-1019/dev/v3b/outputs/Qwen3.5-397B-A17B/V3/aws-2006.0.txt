**Steelman:** Leverage existing, underutilized spiky infrastructure to create a high-margin revenue stream that amortizes fixed capital costs, turning a liability into a competitive moat.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Continue bearing full capital costs for retail peaks alone. This guarantees lower overall margins and leaves infrastructure idle during troughs.
2.  **Sell Hardware/Software:** Compete directly with incumbents (Dell, Oracle) on their terms. Fails due to lack of brand trust in enterprise IT and no differentiation.
3.  **The Proposal (Utility Computing):** The "unproven" market is the opportunity, not a blocker. Incumbents are locked into high-margin, upfront license/models and cannot pivot to low-margin, pay-per-use without cannibalizing their core. The retailer's specific pain point (spiky demand) creates a natural cost advantage for variable pricing that smooths out their own curve.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if reliability SLAs exceed internal retail requirements, causing brand damage, or if the culture cannot support developer relations. However, these are execution risks, not structural blockers. The "distraction" risk is mitigated because the underlying tech stack is identical; only the billing and access layers differ.

**Evidence & Edge:**
*   **Fact:** Infrastructure is already built and "spiky." Idle capacity has near-zero marginal cost.
*   **Edge:** No incumbent has this specific excess capacity profile combined with the desperation to monetize it to justify thin margins. They are optimized for steady-state enterprise sales, not elastic utility.
*   **Blocker Check:** No factual blocker exists. "Unproven market" is a hypothesis, not a disqualifier; the low marginal cost of goods sold (COGS) allows for price experimentation that incumbents cannot match. "No experience" is true but surmountable; the core competency is running the infrastructure, which is already proven internally.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal beats "do nothing" by monetizing sunk costs. It beats alternatives by exploiting a structural cost advantage incumbents cannot replicate. The risks are manageable execution hurdles, not fundamental flaws. The potential upside (creating a new high-margin asset class) vastly outweighs the capital risk, which is already sunk.

**Kill Criteria:** If utilization of rented capacity does not exceed 40% within 18 months, or if retail site reliability drops below 99.9% due to resource contention, pivot or shut down the unit.

DECISION: GO
