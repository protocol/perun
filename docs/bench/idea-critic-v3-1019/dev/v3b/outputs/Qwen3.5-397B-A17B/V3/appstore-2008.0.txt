**Steelman:** A curated, single-vendor native app store with a 30% revenue share creates a secure, high-quality ecosystem that differentiates the hardware, despite the CEO's prior skepticism of web apps and risks of carrier pushback or review bottlenecks.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal directly contradicts the CEO's stated belief that web apps are sufficient, requiring a strategic pivot justification. However, rival platforms already possess open ecosystems; relying solely on web apps in 2008 risks immediate obsolescence as competitors leverage native performance. The "polish" concern is valid but mitigated by the proposed mandatory review process, which acts as a quality gate rather than a blocker. Security and stability risks are inherent to native code but are manageable via the single-store constraint, which prevents sideloading. Carrier objections are a political hurdle, not a technical blocker, and can be negotiated given the phone's market potential. The 30% cut aligns incentives for maintenance while funding the review infrastructure.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing (Web Only):** Lowest risk, but cedes the native performance advantage to rivals, likely resulting in market irrelevance.
2.  **Open Distribution:** Maximizes developer speed but invites malware and instability, destroying the brand's "polish" value proposition.
3.  **Proposed Model:** Balances openness with control. It beats "do nothing" by enabling native capabilities rivals offer, and beats "open distribution" by preserving stability.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the review process becomes so slow that developers abandon the platform, or if carriers block the device entirely. The kill criterion is developer churn due to review latency exceeding two weeks.

**Edge:** The company controls both hardware and software, allowing tighter integration than fragmented rivals, justifying the curated approach. The "polish" brand asset is the very thing the review process protects.

**Blockers:** NONE. The strategic contradiction is resolved by the competitive necessity of matching rival ecosystems.
**Risks:** Review bottlenecks, carrier friction.
**Edge:** Integrated hardware/software control enabling superior user experience.

DECISION: GO
