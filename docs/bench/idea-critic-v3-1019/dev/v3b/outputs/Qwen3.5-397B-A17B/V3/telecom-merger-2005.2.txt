**Steelman:** Acquire the target to capture its sticky, high-value business segment and push-to-talk dominance, accepting short-term integration friction as the price of entry.

**Attack:** The core flaw is not the price tag but the technological chasm. In 2005, merging incompatible network architectures (e.g., CDMA vs. GSM or proprietary iDEN vs. standard cellular) is not a software patch; it requires a physical, multi-year overlay or complete rip-and-replace. The proposal admits the target's tech is "limited for data," yet the industry is pivoting to mobile data. You are buying a legacy voice feature set on dying hardware.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" alternative preserves capital. A superior alternative is a commercial roaming/MVNO agreement to resell the target's push-to-talk service without owning the incompatible infrastructure. This captures the revenue stream without the $36B goodwill risk or the engineering nightmare of running two disjoint networks.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 2008. The cause: integration costs ballooned as engineers struggled to bridge incompatible standards, while the target's unique hardware became obsolete faster than anticipated. The "high-value" customers churned because the merged network offered inferior data speeds compared to competitors who standardized early. The massive goodwill becomes a write-down.

**Fit & Edge:** The strategy lacks a defined path to unify the technology. The "edge" is merely owning a customer list that can be accessed via contract, not equity. The cost of delay is low; the cost of a botched integration is existential. The proposal relies on the hope that integration will work, rather than a proven mechanism.

**Blockers:** The incompatibility of networks creates a technical ceiling that prevents the "combined company" from realizing synergies quickly. The reliance on legacy equipment contradicts the industry shift to data.

**Risks:** Massive integration cost, goodwill impairment, technology obsolescence.

**Edge:** None identified beyond what a partnership could achieve.

**Kill Criteria:** If a technical unification roadmap cannot be produced within 90 days showing a single standard migration path, the deal must be aborted.

The proposal fails to demonstrate how the technological incompatibility will be resolved without destroying the value being purchased.

DECISION: NO-GO
