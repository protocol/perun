**Steelman:** Acquiring a proven, modern object-oriented OS core from a small vendor is the only viable path to rapidly replace a failing internal OS, leveraging external innovation to arrest market share decline when internal attempts have twice failed.

**Options Analysis:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Continued market share erosion and eventual irrelevance as the dated OS becomes untenable.
*   **License Another Vendor:** Surrenders control of the core roadmap and differentiation to a competitor or third party; likely costly long-term.
*   **Acquire Target (Proposal):** High upfront cost ($100M+) for low-revenue asset, but gains full ownership of modern IP and key talent (founder).

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
The strategy fits a "survival and renewal" north star. Assuming the target's technology is technically superior and portable.
*   *Pre-mortem:* Integration fails due to cultural clash with the difficult founder; migration tools are insufficient, causing developer exodus; the purchase price drains cash needed for marketing the new OS.
*   *Door Type:* One-way door (acquisition is hard to unwind).
*   *Cost of Delay:* High. Every quarter without a modern OS accelerates market share loss.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
*   *Claim:* Internal replacement failed twice. *Fact:* Given.
*   *Claim:* Target has modern OO OS. *Fact:* Given.
*   *Blocker Check:* Is there a cheaper alternative reaching the same upside? Licensing avoids integration risk but sacrifices control; given the company's desperate need for a unique turnaround asset, control is likely paramount. The "high price" is relative to current revenue, not strategic value of survival. The "few units sold" is irrelevant if the tech is sound for the buyer's scale.
*   *Edge:* The buyer has the distribution channel and brand the target lacks; the target has the tech the buyer lacks. This symbiosis creates the only plausible path to a modern OS in time.

**Decision:**
No factual blocker exists. The risks (migration, founder personality) are execution challenges, not deal-breakers, manageable via the advisor role rather than full re-integration immediately. The cost of inaction exceeds the acquisition premium.

**Kill Criteria:** If within 18 months the new OS core cannot run 80% of existing critical apps or developer sign-ups do not grow by 20%, halt further migration investment.

DECISION: GO
