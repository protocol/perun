**Blockers:** NONE. No existing fact contradicts the core claim that a low-cost, sporty coupe can capture the emerging youth demographic. The mechanical sharing reduces development risk and cost, directly addressing the "cheap" constraint while the styling targets the "dull" pain point.

**Risks:** Unproven volume demand for sporty small cars; potential perception of cheapness due to parts sharing; high upfront marketing spend before revenue validation. These are valid uncertainties but do not constitute factual blockers. They are mitigated by the low base price (reducing buyer friction), the extensive options list (allowing margin recovery and customization), and the specific targeting of a known, growing demographic (postwar boomers entering the market).

**Edge:** The proposal leverages existing, proven mechanicals to minimize R&D cost and time-to-market, enabling a competitive low base price that incumbents cannot easily match without new platforms. The "long hood, short deck" styling creates a distinct visual category separate from the economy donor car, countering the "cheap" perception. The massive launch campaign is necessary to define this new category quickly before competitors react.

**Decision Method:**
1.  **Steelman:** A uniquely styled, affordable sports car using proven economy components can unlock a massive, underserved youth market.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the youth segment to competitors. A clean-sheet design is too costly and slow. A simple restyle of the existing sedan fails to capture the "sporty" essence. This plan beats "do nothing" by capturing first-mover advantage.
3.  **Fit:** Aligns with capturing the emerging baby boomer market.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure would stem from styling that still feels "economy" or pricing that misses the sweet spot. The options list and specific styling cues address this.
5.  **Evidence:** Demographic data (baby boom entry) is the primary driver. Mechanical reliability is inherited.
6.  **Kill Criteria:** If first-year sales do not meet X units or brand perception surveys still link it strongly to the economy model, pivot marketing or trim levels.

The risks are inherent to innovation but are managed by the strategy's core strengths: low cost, high differentiation, and targeted appeal. The potential upside of defining a new market segment outweighs the risks of execution.

DECISION: GO
