Steelman: A hardware incumbent can pivot to a 3D CD-console by leveraging lower disc costs and superior developer tools to displace cartridge-based incumbents, despite lacking platform experience.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal acknowledges the lack of experience and competitor strength but offers a structural cost advantage (CD vs. cartridge) and a specific wedge (developer tools) to overcome them. No factual contradiction exists in the text preventing entry.

**Risks:** High. Failure to secure third-party titles would render the hardware useless. Selling hardware at a loss requires deep capital reserves not explicitly confirmed here, though implied by "consumer electronics maker" status. An incumbent launching a rival 3D system simultaneously creates a direct feature war against a brand with loyal users.

**Edge:** The shift from cartridges to CDs offers a verified manufacturing cost advantage and higher storage capacity, directly addressing developer pain points (cost and complexity). This is a tangible product advantage rivals lacking CD technology cannot immediately match without their own hardware revision.

**Analysis:** The "do nothing" option yields zero growth in the emerging 3D market. The "cheaper alternative" of licensing technology to an existing partner was explicitly cancelled by that partner, forcing the "build own" path. The risks are executional (winning devs, managing losses), not structural flaws in the premise. The edge (CD economics/tools) is sufficient to justify the bet given the blocked partnership. The proposal correctly identifies the battlefield: third-party developer mindshare.

**Kill Criteria:** If third-party game announcements do not exceed five major titles within 12 months of announcement, or if hardware losses exceed 20% of the division's annual budget before reaching 1 million units sold, the project must pivot to software-only or cease.

**Decision:** The plan survives the pre-mortem. The cancellation of the partnership makes this the only viable path to remain in the gaming sector. The cost advantage is real, and the risk of incumbents is inherent to any market entry.

DECISION: GO
