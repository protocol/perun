**Steelman:** A dominant Japanese 50cc manufacturer can create a new US commuter segment by bypassing traditional motorcycle dealers to sell small, affordable bikes via sporting-goods stores, despite the current US cultural preference for large machines and potential reliability issues with their larger models at highway speeds.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly acknowledges the risk of larger models failing at US highway speeds but strategically pivots to selling *small* 50cc step-throughs, which do not require sustained high-speed highway travel. The "small market" citation refers to the existing *large* motorcycle market, not the untapped commuter segment this proposal aims to create. The foreign currency risk is a known constraint, not a factual contradiction of the plan's viability.

**Risks:** Cultural rejection of small bikes; brand obscurity; operational complexity of non-traditional distribution; currency exposure. These are valid execution risks but do not invalidate the core logic. The pre-mortem suggests failure would stem from refusing to adapt marketing to "easy riding" rather than mechanical flaws.

**Edge:** The company holds a monopoly on reliable, mass-produced 50cc technology. Competitors are fixated on the high-margin, heavy-metal segment, leaving the low-end commuter niche completely undefended. Using sporting-goods stores bypasses the "rough image" of traditional dealerships, aligning the product with leisure and utility rather than rebellion.

**Decision Logic:** The plan beats "do nothing" because the domestic market is saturated while the US has a latent, unaddressed need for cheap transportation. It beats importing larger models because it avoids the identified mechanical failure mode (highway stress) and cultural headwinds. The distribution channel innovation directly solves the image problem. The risks are manageable through a staged rollout (testing the waters before committing massive currency reserves). The edge (unique product fit + novel channel) outweighs the risks of entry.

**Kill Criteria:** Halt expansion if initial sales via sporting-goods stores fail to reach break-even within 12 months or if warranty claims on the 50cc engine exceed 5% in the first year.

DECISION: GO
