Steelman: A dedicated high-volume factory enables a $2,500 price point, successfully migrating scooter families to four-wheel safety despite land protests and stigma.

**Blockers:**
*   **Land Risk:** Farmer protests threaten the dedicated factory timeline. If the site is delayed, the high-volume cost model collapses before production starts. The proposal offers no alternative site or phased rollout to mitigate this single point of failure.
*   **Demand Mismatch:** Marketing explicitly as "the cheapest" may alienate aspirational buyers who view car ownership as a status upgrade from scooters. If the brand becomes synonymous with poverty rather than value, volume targets will miss, breaking the economies of scale required to sustain the $2,500 price.

**Risks:**
*   Safety perceptions could trigger regulatory scrutiny or consumer avoidance if accidents occur, regardless of actual crashworthiness.
*   Used cars and premium scooters offer better value propositions for slightly higher costs, squeezing the target demographic.

**Edge:**
*   First-mover advantage in the ultra-low-cost segment creates a massive moat if volume is achieved immediately.
*   Unique product-market fit for families currently exposed to weather and safety risks on two-wheelers.

**Analysis:**
The proposal hinges entirely on the new dedicated factory achieving immediate high volume to justify the $2,500 price. However, the identified land protests present a credible, immediate threat to breaking ground. Without the factory, the cost structure fails. Furthermore, the marketing strategy ("cheapest") directly conflicts with the psychological drivers of emerging market consumers, who often prioritize status and durability over rock-bottom pricing. The proposal treats these critical vulnerabilities as manageable risks rather than existential threats. There is no "go-if" condition to secure land rights first, nor a pivot strategy if the "cheapest" branding fails. Proceeding now locks capital into a site under protest and a brand narrative that may repel the very customers it seeks to attract. The cost of delay to resolve land issues and test branding is lower than the cost of a failed factory launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
