Steelman: Acquiring the dominant cross-platform messaging app at a premium is the only viable defense against the existential threat of mobile displacement, securing the network effect before a competitor does.

**Blockers:** NONE. The $19B price tag, while 12x the prior valuation, reflects the strategic necessity of neutralizing a category winner with hundreds of millions of users. No factual evidence suggests the target is unsellable or legally blocked; the risks are financial and executional, not structural failures of the premise. The "no monetization" risk is irrelevant if the asset prevents the buyer's core business from becoming obsolete.

**Risks:** Massive overpayment if user growth stalls; inability to monetize without alienating the user base; privacy regulatory hurdles; founder departure post-acquisition. These are severe financial and operational risks, but they do not invalidate the strategic imperative. They are manageable through earn-outs, integration planning, and long-term horizon shifts.

**Edge:** The buyer possesses the capital to close immediately and the desperate need to own the primary mobile communication layer. Competitors lack the same urgency or balance sheet depth to outbid. The target's refusal to run ads confirms a pure utility focus that aligns with retaining users, which is the exact asset the buyer lacks.

**Kill Criteria:** If user monthly active growth drops below 10% year-over-year within 18 months, or if regulatory action forces a breakup within 24 months, the acquisition thesis fails.

**Decision Logic:** This is a one-way door with a high cost of delay. Waiting for a "better price" or "monetization proof" cedes the mobile future to a rival. The cost of doing nothing (displacement) exceeds the cost of overpaying. The proposal addresses the single most critical strategic vulnerability. The risks are priced into the deal but do not constitute blockers.

DECISION: GO
