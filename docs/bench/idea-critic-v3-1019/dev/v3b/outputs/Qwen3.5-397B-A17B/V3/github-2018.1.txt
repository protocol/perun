Steelman: Acquiring the dominant code-hosting platform secures the industry's central nervous system, neutralizing a potential rival while capturing the world's largest developer graph, despite short-term cultural friction and financial losses.

**Skeptic**: The buyer's historical hostility creates a unique "poison pill" of distrust that standard independence promises cannot instantly dissolve. If even 15% of high-value users defect to the free competitor due to fear (not just feature gaps), the network effect—the asset's core value—degrades rapidly. The target's lack of permanent leadership suggests internal instability that a stock-heavy deal might exacerbate if the buyer's stock dips post-announcement.

**Better-Way**: "Do nothing" preserves capital and avoids triggering a community exodus. Alternatively, a deep commercial partnership or minority stake could secure integration benefits without the $7.5B price tag or the immediate trigger of a full acquisition. Given the target is loss-making, paying a premium for control now assumes the buyer can fix operations faster than the market can erode the user base.

**Kill-Criteria**: This is a one-way door; once the brand is merged or trust broken, it cannot be restored. The cost of delay is low; the free competitor is not yet dominant, and the target has no other serious suitors. Waiting 12 months for the target to prove monetization or for community sentiment to cool reduces risk without losing the asset.

**Decision Logic**:
*   **Blockers**: None definitive, but the "distrust" risk is existential and unmitigated by the proposal's vague "run independently" clause.
*   **Edge**: The buyer's distribution could monetize the target better than it can alone.
*   **Verdict**: The price ($7.5B) for a loss-making entity amidst active community revolt and leadership vacuums is speculative. The proposal lacks a concrete, binding mechanism (e.g., charter, board seats for community) to guarantee the "independence" required to stop defection. Without a specific kill criterion for user retention or a capped earn-out based on active users, the risk of paying for a hollowing asset is too high.

DECISION: NO-GO
