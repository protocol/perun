**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men avoiding both full-sugar guilt and diet stigma, leveraging a premium price to signal innovation before the low-carb trend fades.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "satisfy neither group" test. Core cola drinkers demand full sugar for taste; diet buyers demand zero calories for health. A hybrid risks tasting inferior to the original while retaining enough carbs to alienate strict dieters. The "young men" segment is unproven to care about carb counts specifically, rather than just calories or taste. Furthermore, entering a premium price point during a potential "fad" creates high inventory risk if the trend collapses. The rival's similar launch suggests a crowded niche that may not exist, turning this into a fight for scraps rather than new growth.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do nothing:* Protect core brand equity and wait for clear data on the low-carb longevity.
2. *Diet extension:* Improve the taste of the existing zero-calorie option, which already serves the health-conscious without alienating purists.
3. *Limited test:* Run a small regional pilot before a global campaign to validate the "half-sugar" value proposition.

**Fit:** This assumes the low-carb diet is a structural shift, not a temporary craze. If it is a fad, the manufacturing retooling and marketing spend become sunk costs with no long-term revenue.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-launch, sales are flat. The product is described by consumers as "watered-down coke" by loyalists and "still has too much sugar" by dieters. The premium price prevented trial, and the campaign failed to define a distinct identity.

**Blockers:** No evidence provided that young men specifically want *half* sugar rather than *no* sugar or *better-tasting* diet soda. The risk of cannibalizing both core and diet lines without net new volume is high.

**Edge:** None demonstrated. Being second to a rival in a questionable category is a disadvantage, not an edge.

**Kill Criteria:** If a regional pilot does not show >15% repeat purchase rate among young men within 3 months, terminate immediately.

**Verdict:** The proposal lacks proof of a distinct market need and carries high cannibalization risk against two established pillars.

DECISION: NO-GO
