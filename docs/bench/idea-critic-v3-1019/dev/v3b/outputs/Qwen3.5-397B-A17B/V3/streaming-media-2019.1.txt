**Steelman:** Sacrificing immediate, high-margin licensing revenue to build a direct-to-consumer monopoly asset, betting that lifetime subscriber value exceeds the sum of current licensing fees and execution costs.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on a fatal arithmetic gap. The company earns "billions" in largely pure-profit licensing fees today. Replacing this with a "low price" subscription model requires acquiring tens of millions of users just to match current revenue, ignoring the massive cost of content hosting, marketing, and the "multi-year operating loss" required to fund it. The leading competitor has a decade-long head start and over 100 million subscribers; undercutting them on price invites a price war the incumbent can win due to scale, while the challenger burns cash. Furthermore, cannibalizing existing cable/theatrical revenue shrinks the total addressable market before the new stream is proven. The "technology platform" risk is not merely operational; building a global, stable streaming infrastructure from scratch often takes years and billions in unexpected overruns, delaying the very audience growth needed to justify the burn.

**Better Way:** A hybrid approach. License content selectively to maintain cash flow while launching a niche, premium-tier service for superfans, avoiding a blunt "all-in" price war. Alternatively, acquire an existing tech platform rather than building one, reducing execution risk.

**Kill Criteria:** If the projected subscriber acquisition cost exceeds the lifetime value of a subscriber within 24 months, or if licensing revenue drops faster than subscriber growth, the strategy destroys value. The lack of a defined path to profitability beyond "building an audience" is a blocker. The proposal assumes market share will automatically convert to profit despite negative unit economics at launch.

**Edge:** None stated. Owning content is an asset, but distribution without a proven low-cost acquisition channel is a liability. The "low price" strategy is a commodity play, not a differentiated edge.

**Verdict:** The plan trades guaranteed billions for speculative future gains with high execution risk and undefined unit economics. It fails the "beat do nothing" test because the cost of capital for the required burn likely exceeds the risk-adjusted return of the licensing model.

DECISION: NO-GO
