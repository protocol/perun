**Steelman:** A complete rewrite with a publisher-focused algorithm and immediate migration is the only way to reverse traffic decline and modernize the platform before a rival captures the market.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the blocker test on three counts. First, it ignores the primary asset: the loyal community. Changing the core voting mechanics they value, while simultaneously favoring publishers over community submissions, creates a high probability of mass user exodus, accelerating the traffic decline it aims to fix. Second, the "big bang" migration eliminates reversibility; if the new algorithm fails to engage users, there is no fallback, turning a calculated risk into an existential threat. Third, the engineering risk of a full rewrite diverts all resources from immediate growth tactics needed to counter the rival, with no interim value delivery.

**Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option preserves the community but accepts slow decline. A superior alternative is an iterative approach: A/B test the new surfacing logic on a small traffic segment while keeping the core voting mechanics intact for the majority. This allows data-driven validation of the publisher-focus hypothesis without alienating the base. A phased migration allows rollback if engagement drops.

**Fit & Edge:** The proposal lacks a defined edge. "Strategic necessity" against a rival is not an edge; it is a fear response. The plan assumes the traffic slowdown is due to product stagnation rather than market saturation or content quality, an unverified assumption. Without evidence that the current community wants more publisher content, this pivot attacks the wrong problem.

**Blockers:** 
1. The plan removes the core feature (voting mechanics) valued by the only remaining loyal segment.
2. The "all at once" migration removes the ability to rollback, violating basic risk management for a core product change.
3. No evidence is provided that the traffic slowdown is caused by the surfacing algorithm rather than external factors.

**Risks:** Total community revolt, irreversible technical failure, accelerated loss to rivals during the rewrite blackout.

**Edge:** NONE stated.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot of the new surfacing logic on 5% of traffic does not increase time-on-site within 30 days, the rewrite halts.

DECISION: NO-GO
