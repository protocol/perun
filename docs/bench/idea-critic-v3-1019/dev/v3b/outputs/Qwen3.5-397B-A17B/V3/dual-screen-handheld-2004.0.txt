**Steelman:** Differentiate via novel interaction and price to expand the market rather than competing on raw graphical power, leveraging a dual-screen form factor to offer unique gameplay experiences unavailable on the rival's superior hardware.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that the dual-screen, touch-based architecture creates an insurmountable barrier for third-party developers, leading to a software drought. However, maintaining the older handheld on sale mitigates this risk by preserving the existing installed base and developer revenue stream during the transition. The "gimmick" risk is neutralized if the second screen is integral to game mechanics (e.g., maps, inventory, distinct perspectives) rather than decorative.

Regarding cost: adding a second screen and touch layer typically increases Bill of Materials (BOM). The proposal claims a *lower* price than the rival. This is only feasible if the rival's "stronger graphics" rely on expensive components (high-end GPU, fast memory) that this proposal intentionally omits. If the processor and memory costs are sufficiently low, the screen addition does not break the price target.

The "unproven" nature of touch games is a risk, but also the edge: the rival is fighting a war on established terrain (graphics), while this proposal changes the battlefield. If the goal is broadening the audience beyond core players, intuitive touch controls are a stronger vector than graphical fidelity.

**Blockers:** None identified in the text. The cost contradiction (two screens vs. lower price) is resolved by the strategic omission of expensive graphical hardware.
**Risks:** Developer confusion; market perception of gimmickry.
**Edge:** First-mover advantage in mass-market touch interaction; price parity despite feature expansion; segmented market coverage via legacy device retention.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal successfully identifies a non-consensus path (interaction > graphics) that aligns with the goal of audience expansion. It includes a specific mitigation for the supply-side risk (keeping the old unit on sale). The risks are executional, not fundamental flaws in the thesis. The edge (unique gameplay + lower price) is sufficient to justify the bet against a superior graphical rival.

DECISION: GO
