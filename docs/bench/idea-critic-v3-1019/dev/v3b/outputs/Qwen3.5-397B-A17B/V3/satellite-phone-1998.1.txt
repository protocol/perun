**Steelman**: This is the only solution for true global coverage, serving high-value travelers who cannot rely on inconsistent ground networks.

**Attack**: The economics are fundamentally broken. The $5 billion debt load requires massive, immediate scale. However, the $3,000 handset price and high per-minute costs restrict the addressable market to a tiny fraction of business travelers. Ground cellular is expanding rapidly, eroding the unique value proposition before commercial launch even begins. The bulky hardware and poor indoor performance further limit utility, making the premium price unjustifiable.

**Alternatives**:
1.  **Do Nothing**: Avoid sinking further capital into a product with negative unit economics relative to market size.
2.  **Wait/Delay**: Pause launch until handset costs drop and ground gaps are clearly mapped, though this increases debt pressure.
3.  **Pivot**: Target only specific verticals (maritime, aviation, government) rather than general business travelers, but this reduces total addressable market below debt service requirements.

**Pre-mortem**: The project fails because subscriber growth cannot meet debt obligations. The "edge" of global coverage is nullified by the inability of the target customer to afford the entry cost or tolerate the hardware limitations. The risk of ground network expansion is not a possibility; it is a verified trend occurring now.

**Blockers**:
*   **Market Size vs. Debt**: The high price point limits the user base to a size insufficient to service $5 billion in debt.
*   **Product-Market Fit**: Bulky hardware and poor indoor performance contradict the needs of premium business users.
*   **Competitive Obsolescence**: Ground networks are solving the coverage gap cheaper and faster than this system can deploy.

**Edge**: None identified. Global reach is irrelevant if the cost of access excludes the volume required for solvency. "Strategic necessity" does not pay interest on borrowed billions.

**Kill Criteria**: If subscriber projections at the $3,000 price point do not cover annual debt service within 18 months, the project is insolvent. Given current constraints, this threshold is mathematically unreachable.

This is a one-way door with catastrophic financial downside. The plan relies on a market segment that is too small to support the capital structure, while facing a superior, cheaper alternative that is already scaling.

DECISION: NO-GO
