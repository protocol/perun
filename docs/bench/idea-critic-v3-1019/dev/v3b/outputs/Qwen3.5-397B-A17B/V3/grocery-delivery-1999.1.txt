Steelman: Dominating infrastructure early creates an insurmountable barrier to entry, securing market share before competitors can react.

However, this claim collapses under basic unit economic scrutiny. The proposal explicitly admits the company has **no operating track record** and has **not learned unit economics** in its single active city. Groceries are notoriously low-margin, requiring extreme operational efficiency to survive. Building billions in fixed costs (automated warehouses) before validating variable costs (pick/pack speed, spoilage, delivery density) violates the fundamental logic of startup scaling.

The "better way" is obvious: validate the model in the first city until profitability per order is proven, then expand. The proposed path assumes customer density and operational efficiency will magically align at scale, a fatal assumption given the 30-minute delivery constraint which exponentially increases complexity and cost.

Pre-mortem: By 2001, the company burns through its capital. The automated warehouses sit underutilized because customer adoption in new cities is slower than projected. The rigid infrastructure prevents pivoting to a viable model (e.g., longer delivery windows, smaller hubs). The company collapses, having built expensive monuments to a business model that never worked.

This is a classic "one-way door" decision with irreversible capital allocation based on zero evidence. The risks (capital intensity, thin margins) are not just theoretical; they are guaranteed to materialize without the data to mitigate them. There is no "edge" here other than speed, which is useless if the direction is wrong. The lack of a single proven profitable unit makes the billion-dollar bet a gamble, not a strategy.

Blockers: The core fact that unit economics are unknown and unproven in the only operating market. You cannot scale what you have not validated.
Risks: Total capital loss, inability to pivot, market rejection of the high-cost model.
Edge: None identified; speed without validation is a liability.

DECISION: NO-GO
