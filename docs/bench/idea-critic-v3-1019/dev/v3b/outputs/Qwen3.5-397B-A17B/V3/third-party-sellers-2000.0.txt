**Steelman:** Transforming from a pure retailer into a hybrid marketplace creates immediate inventory depth and revenue via fees without capital expenditure, leveraging existing traffic to solve profitability constraints faster than organic inventory growth.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary fear is cannibalization: used/competitor items undercutting first-party sales. However, for an unprofitable retailer, inventory carrying costs and capital tie-up are the true killers. If a third-party seller fulfills demand at a lower price, the retailer avoids the cost of goods sold, storage, and shipping for that unit, while collecting a fee. The margin on the fee often exceeds the net profit of a discounted first-party sale after overhead.

Regarding trust and service quality: The risk is real but manageable via policy (performance thresholds, bans) rather than a reason to reject the model entirely. Publisher objection is a negotiation hurdle, not a structural flaw; they care about volume, which this model increases. The "do nothing" alternative guarantees continued losses and limited selection. The "cheaper alternative" of drop-shipping lacks the unified page experience that drives conversion.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the platform allows rampant fraud or counterfeit goods early on, destroying brand equity before network effects kick in. This is a one-way door for reputation but a two-way door for inventory strategy (sellers can be removed).

**Edge:** The retailer already owns the customer relationship and the product page traffic. Competitors lack this specific aggregation of intent. By monetizing the "long tail" of inventory they cannot afford to stock, they turn fixed costs into variable revenue.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited are operational challenges inherent to the model, not facts proving the model fails. The financial logic of shifting from capital-intensive retail to asset-light marketplace fees directly addresses the "not yet profitable" constraint.

**Risks:** Brand dilution if quality control fails; supplier friction.
**Kill Criteria:** If customer complaint rates regarding third-party items exceed 2% of total orders within 12 months, or if first-party sales volume drops >15% without offsetting fee revenue growth.

**Decision:** The proposal converts a cost center (inventory risk) into a profit center (fees) and addresses the core profitability bottleneck.

DECISION: GO
