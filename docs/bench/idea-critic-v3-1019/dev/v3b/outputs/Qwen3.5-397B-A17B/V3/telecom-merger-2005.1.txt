**Steelman:** Acquiring the target secures a sticky, high-margin enterprise segment (construction/business) via its unique push-to-talk service, creating immediate revenue synergy despite technical friction.

**Attack & Options:**
The core claim fails the **blocker test**. The proposal admits the networks use "different, incompatible technologies" and the target is "limited for data." In 2005, the industry trajectory is unequivocally toward data convergence (3G/4G) and IP-based services. Acquiring a network explicitly described as "limited for data" and requiring the buyer to "run one on legacy equipment" creates a permanent structural drag.

*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; compete for enterprise customers via software partnerships or MVNO agreements without inheriting stranded legacy infrastructure.
*   **Alternative:** Build/buy a compatible PTT application layer over the buyer's existing network rather than purchasing an incompatible physical network.
*   **Cost of Delay:** Minimal; the target's "popular" status suggests it is not imminently failing, allowing time for a compatible solution.

**Pre-mortem:** The integration drags on for years due to incompatibility. The legacy network becomes a cash incinerator requiring dual maintenance teams. The "high-value" customers churn because the combined entity cannot offer modern data services on the legacy footprint, while the buyer's core network is distracted by integration hell. The $36 billion price tag loads the balance sheet with goodwill that must be written down when the legacy tech proves unusable for future data growth.

**Evidence & Fit:** The proposal itself provides the blocker: "incompatible technologies" and "limited for data." A strategy requiring the operation of known-inferior, non-convergent legacy hardware contradicts the fundamental north star of a national wireless carrier in 2005 (data expansion). The "edge" is merely buying revenue that is technologically stranded.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Technical Incompatibility:** The proposal admits the networks cannot natively integrate, forcing costly, parallel operations indefinitely.
2.  **Strategic Mismatch:** Acquiring "limited for data" assets in a data-centric market era creates immediate obsolescence risk.
3.  **Financial Risk:** $36B valuation heavily weighted toward goodwill on assets with no clear migration path.

**Edge:** None stated beyond current revenue, which is at risk due to the tech limitation.

**Kill Criteria:** If a compatible software-only PTT solution cannot be prototyped within 6 months, the M&A is unnecessary.

DECISION: NO-GO
