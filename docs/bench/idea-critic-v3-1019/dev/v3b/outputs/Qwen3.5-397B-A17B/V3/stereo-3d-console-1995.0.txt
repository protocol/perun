Steelman: A low-cost ($180) bridge product leveraging existing tech to maintain market presence between major console generations.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Value Proposition Failure:** The device is neither portable (handheld definition) nor a home console (requires tabletop/eyepiece), alienating both user bases.
2.  **Hardware Defect:** Red monochrome displays are a severe regression from contemporary color standards, creating an immediate negative perception.
3.  **Health Liability:** Explicit acknowledgment of eye strain and headaches presents a reputational and legal risk unacceptable for a family-oriented brand.
4.  **Content Void:** "Few games at launch" guarantees poor software attachment rates, dooming the hardware regardless of price.

**Risks:**
*   Cannibalization of the upcoming home console release within two years.
*   Brand erosion by associating the company name with discomfort and visual inferiority.

**Edge:**
*   None identified. The $180 price point does not compensate for the functional deficits and health risks. Being "first" to market with flawed stereoscopic 3D offers no sustainable advantage if the user experience is painful.

**Pre-mortem:**
Launched in 1995, the product is ridiculed by press for its screen quality and physical discomfort. Sales stall immediately due to lack of software and negative word-of-mouth regarding health effects. The company is forced to discount heavily within six months, taking a loss on every unit while distracting engineering resources from the critical next-generation home console. The project is discontinued within a year, remembered as a costly failure.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal fails the "beat do nothing" test. Doing nothing preserves capital and brand reputation while waiting for the true next-generation home console. The identified blockers (comfort, health, display quality, software library) are fundamental to the product's core function, not edge cases that can be patched. No kill criteria can save a product where the primary user interaction causes physical pain. The cost of delay is negative; launching now actively harms the company's future position.

DECISION: NO-GO
