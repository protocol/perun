Steelman: Acquiring the handset division secures the primary hardware channel for the minority OS, preventing defection to the dominant rival and buying time to solve the app ecosystem deficit through vertical integration.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal acknowledges falling sales and integration risks but posits that losing the hardware partner entirely is a faster path to irrelevance than the cost of acquisition. No factual contradiction exists in the prompt preventing the transaction.

**Risks:** High integration friction with a large, struggling workforce; potential alienation of other handset manufacturers who may fear favoritism or competition; the core possibility that owning hardware does not solve the underlying lack of developer interest (the "app gap"). The market leader's network effect remains the primary threat.

**Edge:** Immediate control over the user experience and release cadence, eliminating the principal-agent problem between OS vendor and device maker. It stops the immediate bleed of the largest partner switching sides, which would likely trigger a death spiral for the OS.

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition fails if the combined entity burns cash on hardware losses while developers still ignore the platform, leading to a stranded asset. However, the alternative—watching the main partner defect—is an guaranteed, immediate loss of market presence.

**Decision Logic:** This is a two-way door in strategy (assets can be sold later) but a one-way door in market timing (once the partner switches, they are lost forever). The cost of delay is the collapse of the hardware base. While the app ecosystem is the ultimate hurdle, you cannot attract developers without users, and you cannot keep users without a committed hardware partner. The €5.4B price buys the *option* to fix the ecosystem; refusing the deal removes the platform entirely. The risks are executional, not existential to the thesis.

DECISION: GO
