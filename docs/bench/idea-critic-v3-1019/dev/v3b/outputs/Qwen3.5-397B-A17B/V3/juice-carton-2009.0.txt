**Steelman:** A modernized, minimalist design will signal premium quality and attract new demographics, with advertising overcoming any temporary loss of shelf recognition.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the specific function of the current asset: the "orange with a straw" is the primary visual cue for a loyal, older demographic. Removing this cue creates a high probability of search failure on the shelf. The plan relies on advertising to re-educate millions of habit-driven buyers instantly, which is historically inefficient compared to visual continuity. A nationwide, simultaneous launch eliminates the ability to test recognition failure or adjust packaging before total inventory commitment. The risk is not just "confusion," but immediate sales collapse among the core base who cannot find the product they intend to buy.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain current shelf presence; zero risk of recognition loss.
2.  **Evolutionary Redesign:** Modernize elements while retaining the iconic "orange with straw" cue to preserve shelf visibility.
3.  **Staged Pilot:** Test the new design in a limited region to measure actual recognition drop-off before national rollout.

**Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits the buyer base is "loyal and older," yet the strategy actively dismantles their primary navigation tool. No evidence suggests this demographic seeks minimalism over familiarity. The cost of re-education via ads likely exceeds the cost of a gradual transition.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales have dropped 20%. Cause: Loyal shoppers walked past the new carton, assuming the brand was out of stock or switched to a competitor, because the visual anchor was removed. Advertising failed to override deep-seated shelf-search habits.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The old image is the "main shelf cue."
*   **Failure:** Removing the main cue for a loyal/older base without a transition period guarantees recognition failure.
*   **Edge:** None provided; "minimalist" is an aesthetic preference, not a proven sales driver for this specific demographic.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It sacrifices the single most valuable asset (recognition) for an unproven aesthetic gain, with no safety valve (pilot) and a dangerous "all-at-once" execution.

DECISION: NO-GO
