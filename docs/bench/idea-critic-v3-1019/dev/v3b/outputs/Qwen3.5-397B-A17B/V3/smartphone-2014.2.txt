**Steelman:** A differentiated hardware feature (3D/shopping) justifies a premium price and carrier exclusivity, overcoming the app gap by targeting high-value shoppers rather than general users.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "edge" test. The "3D-like display" is a niche gimmick with no proven mass-market demand, while the "one-touch shopping" feature is software that could run on existing dominant platforms (iOS/Android) where the users already are. Building a forked OS without mainstream apps creates a fatal barrier to entry; users will not abandon their ecosystem for a single utility feature, especially at a flagship price point. The "saturated market" risk is compounded by offering a worse core experience (missing apps) for the same price as competitors. The carrier exclusivity limits reach precisely when network effects are needed to sustain a third platform. There is no asset here that rivals lack; in fact, rivals possess the critical asset: the app ecosystem.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Preserve capital; avoid high burn rate of hardware + OS development.
2. *Cheaper Path:* Release the shopping camera feature as an app on existing phones to validate demand without hardware risk.
3. *Staged Approach:* Launch a low-cost device to gain user share before attempting a premium flagship.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-launch, sales are negligible because users cannot access banking or social apps. The carrier drops exclusivity due to poor volume. The project is written off as a costly distraction from the core retail business.

**Blockers:** 
- No ecosystem advantage: Users reject a premium-priced phone missing mainstream apps.
- Differentiation mismatch: 3D display does not solve a primary user pain point sufficient to switch platforms.
- Price/Value failure: Flagship pricing requires flagship utility, which the app gap prevents.

**Edge:** None identified. Being an online retailer does not confer an advantage in manufacturing premium smartphones or convincing users to switch OS ecosystems.

**Kill Criteria:** If pilot testing shows <5% of target users are willing to delete a mainstream app to use the shopping feature, the project stops.

DECISION: NO-GO
