**Steelman:** Leveraging existing spiky infrastructure to sell unused compute capacity creates a high-margin revenue stream that subsidizes retail capital costs, turning a cost center into a profit center before competitors realize the model's viability.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "edge" test. The retailer possesses capital and hardware, but lacks the core assets required to win a developer platform war: brand trust with engineers, a history of running external services at scale, and a sales channel for B2B infrastructure. The "spiky" internal build is optimized for retail workloads, not the multi-tenant isolation, security, and billing granularity external clients demand. Retrofitting this infrastructure introduces massive complexity and reliability risks that directly threaten the core retail business during peak seasons.

The "do nothing" alternative preserves focus on the core retail margin, which is already under investor scrutiny. Adding a low-margin, high-complexity business line without relevant experience invites catastrophic distraction. The market is unproven, and incumbents have deep relationships with the target demographic. The retailer's brand is associated with goods, not reliable enterprise infrastructure; this mismatch is a fatal blocker for early adoption by serious developers.

The pre-mortem is clear: The platform launches, suffers an outage during a retail peak event due to resource contention or configuration errors, causing retail revenue loss and reputational damage. Investors punish the stock for both the failed experiment and the core business disruption. The "cheap" alternative of using existing hardware ignores the engineering cost of building a secure, multi-tenant control plane, which is the actual product, not the servers themselves.

There is no named edge where this specific retailer beats established vendors or proves the market better than a focused startup. The risks (distraction, margin dilution, reliability) are not merely theoretical; they are structural mismatches between the company's current capabilities and the proposed business model. The proposal relies on the assumption that "having servers" equals "having a cloud business," which is a fundamental category error.

Blockers: Lack of developer trust brand, no multi-tenant architecture, high risk to core retail reliability.
Risks: Capital misallocation, investor backlash, operational collapse during peak retail.
Edge: None identified; hardware ownership is not a differentiator without the software layer.

DECISION: NO-GO
