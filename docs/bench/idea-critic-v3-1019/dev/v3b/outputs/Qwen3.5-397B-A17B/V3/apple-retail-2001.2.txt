**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only viable path to demonstrate the integrated hardware-software music strategy, as third-party staff actively undermine the premium value proposition, making current losses structural rather than cyclical.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal acknowledges low industry margins and high lease costs but posits that the unique "digital-music strategy" requires an experience channel that resellers cannot provide. No factual contradiction exists between the stated losses and the need to change the sales model; indeed, the losses support the claim that the current channel is failing.

**Risks:** High fixed costs during a recession could accelerate cash burn if unit volume does not materialize quickly. Channel conflict may cause immediate revenue drops if resellers retaliate. However, these are execution risks, not logical blockers. The "poor track record" of others applies to commodity sellers, not a differentiated ecosystem play.

**Edge:** The company's specific asset is a tightly integrated product line centered on a new digital-music workflow that competitors lack. This differentiation justifies the high cost of real estate by converting foot traffic into high-margin ecosystem adoption, a leverage point unavailable to generic PC makers. The 3-5% share indicates a niche strong enough to sustain boutique locations but too small to survive commoditization in big-box stores.

**Kill Criteria:** If same-store sales do not cover operating expenses within 18 months, or if the new channel fails to increase overall brand margin by 200 basis points within two years, the expansion must halt.

**Decision:** The plan beats "do nothing" because the current trajectory is confirmed losses driven by channel misalignment. Cheaper alternatives (online only) fail the specific requirement of physical product demonstration for the music strategy. The edge is distinct, and the risks are manageable via tight site selection and inventory control.

DECISION: GO
