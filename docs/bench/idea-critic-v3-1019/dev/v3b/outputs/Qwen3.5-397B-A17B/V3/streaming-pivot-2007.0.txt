The claim is that a profitable DVD leader should add free streaming in 2007 despite high costs, poor quality, and cannibalization risks.

**Steelman:** Free streaming acts as a defensive moat to retain subscribers against inevitable digital entrants, converting a physical logistics advantage into a future-proof digital relationship before competitors lock out content rights.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economic Suicide:** Licensing fees are high while the catalogue is weak. Adding this cost to the existing subscription without raising prices destroys margin on seven million users immediately.
2.  **Cannibalization without Upside:** Users switching from high-quality DVDs to poor streaming reduces satisfaction while increasing corporate costs. This accelerates the decline of the cash cow without generating new revenue.
3.  **Market Timing:** Broadband is not universal; the addressable market is too small to justify the infrastructure spend. Entering now burns capital against tech giants who can sustain losses longer.
4.  **Strategic Dilution:** Competing on technology and content licensing against retail/tech giants ignores the company's core competency: physical distribution efficiency.

**Better Way:** Maintain the profitable DVD monopoly. License streaming rights only for a separate, premium-tier product once broadband penetration and video quality mature. Do not subsidize a inferior product for existing users.

**Kill Criteria:** If the initiative reduces overall operating margin within two quarters or fails to attract 5% of subscribers within a year, it must be terminated. However, the initial premise fails the profitability test immediately.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blocker:** The proposal mandates a cost increase (licensing) and revenue stagnation (no extra charge) that mathematically reduces profitability based on current facts.
*   **Edge:** None identified. The company has no cost advantage in streaming compared to tech entrants.
*   **Risk:** High probability of accelerating bankruptcy by degrading the core business model prematurely.

The proposal asks to trade definite, high-margin profits for speculative, low-quality digital presence in an immature market. It violates the principle of funding transformation from excess cash flow; instead, it jeopardizes the cash flow itself. The strategic risk is real, but the proposed tactic ensures defeat by fighting the enemy on their terms before the battlefield is ready.

DECISION: NO-GO
