**Steelman:** Acquiring the startup secures a free, open mobile OS to prevent search monopolization on emerging devices, betting that ubiquitous mobile web access will multiply ad inventory enough to justify the $50M entry cost and multi-year timeline.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the immediate blocker test. The acquirer is a search-ad company with **no hardware business**, yet the mobile market in 2005 is strictly controlled by carriers and handset makers who have zero incentive to adopt an unproven, third-party OS from an outsider. Without a pre-existing hardware partner or carrier agreement (none cited), the OS will never reach users. An OS without distribution is worthless; it cannot drive the mobile web usage required to generate the proposed ad revenue.

The "open-source" angle is a weak lever against entrenched incumbents (Nokia, Microsoft, Palm) and carrier gatekeepers who prefer walled gardens. The $50M price tag for a six-person team with no revenue model and no path to distribution represents a pure speculation on a market shift the acquirer cannot influence alone. The risks (adoption, carrier resistance, no revenue model) are not merely "risks"; they are fatal structural gaps in the current plan. The cheaper alternative is "do nothing" or wait for the market to mature, as the acquirer lacks the specific assets (hardware relationships, carrier leverage) to make this specific bet win *now*.

There is no named "edge" explaining why this specific acquirer can succeed where hardware-focused competitors are struggling to define the space. Fear of missing the mobile wave is not an edge. Without a concrete distribution strategy or a committed hardware partner, the core claim that this acquisition will drive ad revenue collapses.

**Blockers:** No distribution channel (carriers/handset makers) identified; acquirer lacks hardware business to force adoption.
**Risks:** Multi-year payback, no revenue model, carrier resistance.
**Edge:** NONE.

DECISION: NO-GO
