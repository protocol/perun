**Steelman:** A modernized, minimalist design will signal premium quality and attract new, younger demographics, outweighing the risk of alienating existing loyalists through a bold, nationwide refresh supported by heavy advertising.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "shelf cue" test. For a low-involvement, habit-driven purchase like orange juice, visual recognition happens in under two seconds. The straw-in-orange is a unique, ownable asset built over years; removing it destroys the primary heuristic shoppers use to locate the product. The risks identified (loss of recognition, older demographic confusion) are not merely "risks" but **blockers**: they directly contradict the mechanism of sales in this category.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "evolutionary change" options are superior. A cheaper, safer alternative is to retain the iconic straw-in-orange image while subtly updating typography or background colors. This captures freshness without severing the neural link to the brand. A "big bang" nationwide launch removes the ability to test recognition failure before total commitment. If recognition drops even 10%, the cost of re-acquiring those lost customers via advertising exceeds the design savings.

**Fit & Evidence:** No evidence is provided that the current design suppresses growth or that the target "younger" demographic avoids the brand due to the artwork. The assumption that "minimalist" equals "premium" in the grocery aisle is unproven for this specific buyer base. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a failed relaunch.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Fact:** The old image is the main shelf cue. 
2. **Fact:** The buyer base is older and loyal (high sensitivity to change). 
3. **Result:** Removing the cue causes immediate recognition failure, leading to lost sales that advertising cannot instantly fix.

**Edge:** None. The proposal offers no unique advantage over competitors who can also adopt minimalism, while sacrificing the brand's only distinct visual asset.

**Kill Criteria:** If a blind shelf-test shows >5% increase in time-to-locate or >10% drop in selection rate versus the old pack, the project must stop. Since this test was not run prior to the nationwide proposal, the plan is unsafe.

**Verdict:** The plan sacrifices a proven asset for an unproven aesthetic gain in a category where habit rules. It must be revised to include iterative testing and retention of the core visual cue.

DECISION: NO-GO
