The claim is that a $1B+ mobile-only, premium short-form subscription service will succeed despite market saturation and fatigue. The steelman: High production value and orientation-agnostic technology solve the quality gap in short-form, converting ad-tolerant users to subscribers.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The core assumption—that users will pay a premium subscription for short-form content when free, abundant alternatives exist—is contradicted by the stated fact of "subscription fatigue" and "no proven demand." The "edge" cited (A-list talent, high budgets, vertical/horizontal tech) does not overcome the fundamental mismatch between the proposed monetization model (subscription) and the consumption habit (short-form, typically ad-supported or free). The technology feature is a hygiene factor, not a differentiator strong enough to justify a new subscription in a fatigued market.

Alternatives like an ad-supported model with premium tiers or licensing content to existing platforms were not seriously weighed against the "do nothing" baseline of letting studios fund projects within existing ecosystems. The cost of delay is low; the cost of failure is catastrophic ($1B+). The pre-mortem is clear: the service launches, acquires few subscribers due to price and fatigue, burns cash on content, and shuts down as investors lose confidence.

There is no named edge that proves this specific plan can win where others have struggled or where the market behavior (free short-form) directly opposes the revenue model. Fear of missing out on "mobile-only" is not an edge. The risks (unproven demand, price sensitivity, competition) are not just risks; they are active blockers based on current market facts provided.

Blockers: Stated "no proven demand" and "subscription fatigue" directly contradict the revenue model; no evidence provided that "high budget" changes this behavior.
Risks: Capital intensity, talent retention, platform dependency.
Edge: NONE. The proposed features do not constitute a defensible advantage against free alternatives.

DECISION: NO-GO
