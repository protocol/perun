Steelman: Replacing the flagship formula with a demonstrably superior-tasting product will reverse market share loss by satisfying actual consumer preference.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The evidence cited (blind taste tests, surveys, focus groups) is fundamentally flawed because it isolates taste from brand equity. The testing protocol explicitly withheld the critical variable: the permanent removal of the original formula. This omission invalidates the data for a "replacement" decision. Consumers may prefer the sweeter taste in a vacuum, but the proposal ignores the verified risk of severing emotional attachment and brand heritage. The "edge" claimed (superior taste) is negated by the execution method (total withdrawal), which transforms a product upgrade into a cultural erasure.

A cheaper, safer alternative exists: launch the sweeter formula as a new line extension while retaining the original. This captures the taste-preference upside without triggering the heritage backlash. The current plan bets the entire century-old brand on a metric (blind taste) that the proposal's own risk section admits is incomplete. The risk of "emotional attachment" is not a hypothetical; it is a direct consequence of the "no fallback" strategy. Since the testing did not measure reaction to the *loss* of the original, the core claim that the new formula will succeed as a *replacement* is unsupported by the provided evidence.

Blockers: The primary evidence (taste tests) explicitly excluded the decision's defining constraint (removal of the original), rendering the data inapplicable to the proposed action.
Risks: Mass consumer alienation, bottler revolt, permanent brand damage.
Edge: None identified for total replacement; taste advantage only applies to addition, not substitution.

The plan commits irreversible brand damage based on incomplete data. The failure scenario is clear: consumers reject the change not because the drink tastes worse, but because the choice was removed. Without a pilot that includes the "removal" variable or a fallback option, the proposal is a high-stakes gamble with a flawed premise.

DECISION: NO-GO
