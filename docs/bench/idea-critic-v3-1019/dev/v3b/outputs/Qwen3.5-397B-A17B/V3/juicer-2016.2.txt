**Steelman:** A premium, connected juicer creates a recurring revenue moat by guaranteeing freshness and convenience, justifying a high upfront hardware subsidy through lifetime subscription value.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unit Economics Failure:** At $699, the device targets early adopters, yet the recurring cost (proprietary bags) likely exceeds fresh produce plus manual juicing. Consumers rarely accept a permanent premium for a commodity input (fruit) unless the convenience is absolute. Pre-packed chopped fruit has a short shelf life; logistics costs for cold-chain or preservative-free delivery will crush margins or force prices too high for mass adoption.
2.  **Unnecessary Friction:** Mandatory Wi-Fi for a mechanical pressing function introduces failure points (connectivity loss, server downtime) without delivering proportional value. "Checking freshness" via an app is a solution in search of a problem; users can smell or see spoiled fruit. This connectivity requirement signals "planned obsolescence" and privacy intrusion, deterring the very demographic willing to spend $699 on quality.
3.  **Market Size vs. Burn:** Tens of millions in funding creates pressure for hyper-growth. The intersection of users who: (a) want juiced produce, (b) refuse to buy fresh fruit/blenders, (c) accept a $699 entry fee, and (d) tolerate a subscription lock-in is vanishingly small. This is a niche product with enterprise-level burn requirements.
4.  **Better Way:** Sell a high-quality non-connected press compatible with standard bags or fresh produce. Validate the subscription model separately. If the hardware must be smart, make connectivity optional, not a gatekeeper for basic function.

**Pre-mortem:** The company runs out of cash 18 months post-launch. Hardware sales stall due to price; subscription churn spikes when users realize the per-ounce cost is 5x retail produce. The mandatory cloud dependency becomes a PR liability when servers lag or shut down, bricking expensive countertop units.

**Edge:** None identified. Being "well-funded" is not an edge; it is a runway that encourages overspending on a flawed unit economics model. No proprietary technology prevents competitors from launching a cheaper, non-connected version that accepts the same bags (if reverse-engineered) or fresh fruit.

**Blockers:**
*   Unit economics likely negative for the consumer (subscription vs. fresh).
*   Mandatory connectivity creates unnecessary churn and support costs.
*   Total Addressable Market too small to support the implied valuation/burn rate.

DECISION: NO-GO
