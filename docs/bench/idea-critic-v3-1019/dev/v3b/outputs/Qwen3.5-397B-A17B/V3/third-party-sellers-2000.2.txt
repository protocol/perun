**Steelman:** Transforming from a pure retailer into a hybrid marketplace leverages existing traffic to generate immediate, high-margin fee revenue without inventory risk, effectively monetizing long-tail demand the retailer cannot profitably stock itself.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary fear is cannibalization: used or competitor items undercutting the retailer's own new stock. However, for an unprofitable company, the alternative is maintaining high inventory costs for slow-moving items that tie up capital. "Do nothing" preserves control but guarantees continued losses on marginal SKUs. A "retailer-only" model cannot scale variety without destroying margins.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
The strategy fits the survival stage: cash flow over control. If we assume the retailer refuses to let sellers win the "Buy Box" on items where the retailer holds cheap inventory, the cannibalization risk is managed. The pre-mortem failure scenario is not lost sales, but a trust collapse due to counterfeit goods or poor shipping from third parties, driving away core customers. This is mitigated by strict seller vetting and holding fees in escrow until delivery confirmation.

**Evidence & Edge:**
The proposal addresses the specific constraint of "not yet profitable." Inventory ownership is the cost driver; fees are pure profit. The edge is the existing customer base searching for these items already. If they can't find them here, they leave; if they find them via a third party here, the site captures a fee. The risk of supplier objection is real but secondary to solvency.

**Blockers, Risks, Edge:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The math of fees vs. inventory carrying costs favors the marketplace model for an unprofitable entity.
*   **Risks:** Brand damage from bad sellers; supplier retaliation. These are operational hurdles, not structural blockers.
*   **Edge:** Immediate monetization of search traffic without incremental inventory cost.

**Decision:**
The proposal moves the north star (profitability) by swapping low-margin inventory risk for high-margin fee income. The risks are manageable via policy (seller standards) rather than structural rejection.

DECISION: GO
