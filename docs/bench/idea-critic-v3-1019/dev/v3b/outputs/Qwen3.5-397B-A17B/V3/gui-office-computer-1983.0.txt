The proposal claims a $10,000 graphical computer will succeed with office workers in 1983 despite being significantly slower and more expensive than existing business PCs, having minimal software, and facing internal competition from a cheaper alternative.

**Steelman:** The graphical interface offers such a revolutionary ease-of-use advantage that office workers and management will pay a 300%+ premium over text-based competitors, ignoring speed and software gaps, while the company cannibalizes its own future cheaper product.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At several times the cost of leading business PCs, the unit economics fail for mass office deployment. The "graphical" benefit does not currently offset the hard cost difference for spreadsheet or word processing tasks where speed is critical.
2.  **Performance Liability:** A "slow" machine frustrates productivity. Office workers measure value in throughput; a slow GUI is worse than a fast command line for data entry.
3.  **Software Void:** Business utility depends on applications (VisiCalc, WordStar). "Little third-party software" means the machine is a toy, not a business tool.
4.  **Internal Cannibalization:** Launching a $10k slow unit now undermines the imminent cheaper, faster internal alternative. It signals confusion in strategy and wastes marketing capital on a losing spec.
5.  **Market Fit:** In 1983, the "office worker" buyer is the IT director, not the end-user. They buy specs and compatibility, not mouse-driven novelty at a luxury price point.

**Better Way:** Do nothing with this specific configuration. Wait for the cheaper internal machine, or re-engineer this unit to match competitor speeds and lower the price to within 20% of the market leader before launching.

**Kill Criteria:** If the machine cannot run standard business software at competitive speeds, or if the price exceeds 1.5x the market leader, it cannot penetrate the office market.

**Verdict:** The proposal fails the blocker test. The price is unjustifiable given the performance deficit and lack of software. The existence of a cheaper internal alternative makes this launch strategically incoherent. This is a solution looking for a problem that the market (and the company's own R&D) is already solving more efficiently.

DECISION: NO-GO
