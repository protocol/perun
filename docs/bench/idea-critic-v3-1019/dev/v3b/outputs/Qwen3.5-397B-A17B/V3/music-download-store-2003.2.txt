**Steelman:** A dominant hardware vendor can monetize pervasive piracy by offering a frictionless, legal alternative at a psychological price point ($0.99), leveraging exclusive hardware integration to lock in users despite label dependency and low margins.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that free file-sharing renders any paid model obsolete. However, piracy suffers from poor discovery, malware risks, and inconsistent quality. A curated store solves these pain points, creating value beyond the file itself. The risk of label withdrawal is mitigated by the vendor's unique leverage: they control the most popular playback device. Labels need this distribution channel more than the vendor needs any single label.

Regarding margins, the goal is not immediate software profit but hardware ecosystem expansion. The store acts as a subsidy to drive high-margin player sales. The "own computers only" restriction is not a bug but a feature; it creates a differentiated user experience that competitors backing subscriptions (which require constant connectivity and higher monthly commitments) cannot match in 2003's bandwidth environment.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the music ecosystem to pirates, eventually eroding hardware differentiation as players become commoditized.
2.  **Subscription model:** Premature for 2003 dial-up/early broadband limits; consumers prefer ownership.
3.  **Higher price point:** Would fail to compete with the "free" alternative psychologically.

**Edge:** The vendor owns the endpoint (the player). This vertical integration allows for a seamless "buy-click-listen" flow that fragmented PC-based rivals cannot replicate. The hardware install base provides an immediate distribution moat.

**Verdict:**
No factual blockers exist. The low margin is a strategic choice to fuel hardware dominance, not a flaw. The restriction to own hardware is a competitive advantage. The timing addresses the specific friction of early 2000s piracy. The risk of label conflict is real but manageable given the vendor's market position.

**Kill Criteria:** If major labels collectively refuse licensing within 6 months, or if attach rate (songs per user) remains below 5 after one year, pivot to a subscription model or licensing OS to third parties.

DECISION: GO
