**Steelman:** A low-cost, camping-oriented crossover built on an existing minivan platform can capture a niche youth market at 30,000 units/year despite styling concerns.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the primary viability test. Early customer clinics explicitly rated the styling poorly; in the automotive sector, styling is the primary purchase driver for the targeted "youthful" demographic, not utility. Launching a vehicle with known aesthetic rejection is a direct path to inventory bloat. Furthermore, the platform is aging, and cost cuts have already compromised the design, suggesting the final product will feel cheap rather than adventurous. The lack of real off-road ability negates the "outdoor" branding, leaving the vehicle with no functional edge over standard minivans or emerging compact SUVs. The "camping options" (tent/cooler) are low-barrier accessories that competitors can easily replicate, offering no defensible moore.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids sinking capital into a product with pre-validated market rejection.
2.  **Platform Refresh:** Delay launch to update the platform and fix design compromises (increases cost/time but fixes the core product flaw).
3.  **Re-style:** Invest heavily in a new front/end design to overcome clinic feedback (contradicts the "cost cut" narrative).

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-launch, sales are 12,000 units (40% of break-even). The angular styling is mocked in press reviews, the interior feels dated due to the aging platform, and the "camping" package is ignored by serious outdoorsmen who require actual off-road capability. The division loses millions annually, forcing a premature discontinuation that damages brand reputation.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Early clinics rated styling poorly.
*   **Fact:** No real off-road ability despite outdoor positioning.
*   **Fact:** Design compromised by cost cuts on an aging platform.

**Edge:** None identified. Low price is insufficient to overcome aesthetic rejection in this segment.

**Verdict:** The proposal relies on a product that has already failed its most critical validation step (customer styling approval) and lacks a unique functional advantage. Proceeding guarantees a financial loss against the 30,000-unit break-even target.

DECISION: NO-GO
