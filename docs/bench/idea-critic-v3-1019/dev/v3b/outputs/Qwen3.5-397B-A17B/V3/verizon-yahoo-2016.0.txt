**Steelman:** Combining two distressed media assets creates sufficient scale and data to challenge duopoly leaders, leveraging the carrier's distribution to reverse decline.

**Attack:** The claim fails on momentum and fit. Both assets exhibit structural decay (falling traffic/revenue, management churn); combining them aggregates decline rather than creating growth. The "scale" required to compete with dominant platforms involves network effects and technological moths this merger does not address. The acquirer (wireless carrier) lacks core competency in consumer media, increasing integration risk while distracting from primary infrastructure business. The $4.8B price tag assumes a turnaround capability neither party has demonstrated.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; avoid integrating two failing cultures.
2.  **Organic Build:** Invest $4.8B in proprietary mobile ad tech tailored to carrier data (higher control, lower integration risk).
3.  **Acquire Tech, Not Content:** Buy a specific ad-tech stack rather than a bloated, declining portal.

**Fit:** Contradicts the "north star" of competing with dominant platforms; dominant platforms win via superior algorithms and user engagement, not just aggregated legacy traffic. This is a "one-way door" with high irreversibility.

**Pre-mortem:** In 24 months, ad revenue continues to slide due to market shifts toward programmatic mobile video (where these legacy portals are weak). Integration stalls due to culture clash. The $4.8B becomes a write-down, distracting leadership from the core wireless business.

**Blockers:**
*   **Momentum Mismatch:** Buying declining revenue to solve a growth problem is mathematically unsound without a proven turnaround mechanism (none cited).
*   **Competency Gap:** Acquirer has zero history in consumer media; execution risk is maximal.
*   **Market Reality:** Two declining assets do not equal one growing competitor against entrenched tech giants.

**Edge:** None identified. "Scale" here is an illusion of size without health. Fear of missing out on mobile media is not a strategic edge.

**Kill Criteria:** If projected synergies rely solely on cost-cutting rather than revenue growth, the thesis collapses. No leading metric suggests this combination reverses the multi-year traffic decline.

**Verdict:** The proposal bets on a "roll-up" strategy in a sector requiring innovation. It buys problems, not solutions.

DECISION: NO-GO
