**Steelman:** A third-place console maker can win by abandoning the graphics arms race to serve an untapped mass market of non-gamers with a low-cost, motion-controlled device, creating a blue ocean rather than fighting for hardcore share.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces severe skepticism: hardcore gamers may reject it as a "toy," third-party publishers might ignore the non-HD hardware, and the motion controller is unproven. However, these are risks, not blockers. The current market trajectory (competing on polygon counts) favors entrenched leaders; continuing that fight guarantees remaining in third place. The "blocker" test requires evidence that the casual market does not exist or cannot be monetized. No such fact exists; the absence of HD and third-party AAA titles is a feature of the cost strategy, not a flaw, enabling a price point competitors cannot match without losing money.

The "do nothing" alternative ensures continued irrelevance in the high-end segment. The cheaper path is exactly this proposal: lower hardware specs reduce the bill of materials, allowing aggressive pricing to drive install base volume, which monetizes via first-party software and accessories. The pre-mortem suggests failure only if the controller is unusable or the software library is empty at launch; the proposal mitigates this by launching with simple, family-friendly titles designed specifically for the hardware.

The edge is clear: competitors are locked into high-cost, high-complexity architectures serving a saturated core demographic. This plan leverages a unique input method to expand the total addressable market rather than dividing existing demand. While margins per unit are thin, the volume potential among non-gamers offers a higher ceiling than the niche high-end market. The risks (perception, third-party support) are manageable through marketing and strong first-party exclusives, whereas the risk of staying the course is certain stagnation.

**Blockers:** NONE. The cited risks are uncertainties inherent to innovation, not factual contradictions.
**Risks:** Controller adoption friction, slow third-party uptake.
**Edge:** First-mover advantage in the casual mass market with a cost structure rivals cannot match without cannibalizing their high-end lines.

DECISION: GO
