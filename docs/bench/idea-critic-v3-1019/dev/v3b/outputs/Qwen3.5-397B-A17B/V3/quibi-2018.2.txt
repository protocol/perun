The claim proposes raising >$1B for a mobile-only, premium short-form subscription service in 2018, relying on high production values and orientation-agnostic tech to overcome subscription fatigue.

**Skeptic:** The core assumption—that users will pay a premium subscription for short-form content when abundant free ad-supported alternatives exist—is unproven and contradicts 2018 market signals. "Subscription fatigue" is explicitly cited as a rising concern, yet the plan adds another paid silo rather than solving discovery or bundling. The "technology feature" (vertical/horizontal adaptability) is a hygiene factor, not a differentiator sufficient to drive paid conversion.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" or "wait" option is stronger given the unproven demand. A cheaper, lower-risk alternative is licensing content to existing platforms (YouTube, Snapchat) to validate willingness-to-pay for premium short-form before building a standalone tech stack and burning $1B on production. The proposed path ignores the proven success of ad-supported models for short attention spans.

**Kill-Criteria:** If a pilot with A-list talent cannot demonstrate a conversion rate significantly higher than industry averages for long-form SVOD within 6 months, the model fails. The pre-mortem reveals failure via cash burn before achieving critical mass, driven by high customer acquisition costs in a saturated market.

**Blockers:** The proposal lacks evidence of demand for *paid* short-form specifically; the cited risk ("no proven demand") is a factual blocker, not just a risk, because the entire revenue model depends on it being false. The $1B ask is premature without a validated unit economy.
**Risks:** Intense competition for phone attention; subscription fatigue; high production costs without guaranteed retention.
**Edge:** None stated beyond "veteran executive" and "A-list talent," which are inputs, not market advantages.

Without a proven mechanism to convert free short-form viewers to paid subscribers, the plan bets $1B on an unverified hypothesis while ignoring cheaper validation paths.

DECISION: NO-GO
