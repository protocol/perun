**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee converts variable shipping friction into fixed revenue, locking in loyalty and increasing purchase frequency to offset margin compression.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Retain per-order friction; risk churn to competitors offering flat rates.
2.  **Free Shipping Threshold:** Require minimum spend; protects margin but fails to solve friction for small, frequent orders.
3.  **Proposal:** $79 annual fee. Highest risk, highest potential lock-in.

**Fit:** Aligns with a "customer obsession" north star by removing checkout friction. Assumes the retailer has sufficient logistics capacity to handle increased volume without service degradation.

**Pre-mortem:** The program fails if heavy buyers dominate adoption, causing shipping costs to exceed the $79 cap by a wide margin before the retailer can adjust pricing or terms. Because memberships are prepaid and promised for a year, reversing the offer mid-cycle creates immediate reputational damage and refund liabilities.

**Evidence & Economics:**
*   **Claim:** Economics are unproven.
*   **Analysis:** Without historical data on elasticity, the break-even point is unknown. If the average shipping cost is $8, a member needs only 10 orders to breach the fee. If the fee drives frequency from 5 to 15 orders, the retailer loses $40 per customer on shipping alone, ignoring product margins.
*   **Delay Cost:** Waiting allows competitors to capture the "free shipping" mindshare.

**Blockers:**
*   **NONE:** No factual contradiction exists in the prompt preventing a trial. The "unproven" nature is a risk, not a blocker, provided the experiment is capped.

**Risks:**
*   Adverse selection (only heavy buyers join).
*   Inability to pivot quickly due to annual commitments.
*   Short-term margin erosion.

**Edge:**
*   First-mover advantage in removing psychological checkout friction.
*   Upfront cash flow from membership fees aids working capital.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal addresses the primary friction point. While risks are high, they are manageable if treated as a controlled bet rather than a blanket rollout. The "unproven" status demands a pilot, but the strategic necessity to solve the repeat-customer friction outweighs the fear of margin hit. The proposal implies a launch; approving it requires accepting the risk profile as the cost of innovation.

**Kill Criteria:** If membership uptake exceeds 5% of the base but net margin per member remains negative after 6 months, terminate new signups.

DECISION: GO
