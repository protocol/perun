**Steelman:** Switching to the dominant architecture solves the thermal and performance bottleneck preventing thin laptop production, securing the product's future competitiveness despite transition friction.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists between the supplier's failure and the need to switch. The risks cited are execution challenges, not impossibilities.

**Risks:** Sales freeze during the announcement window; developer friction slowing ecosystem adoption; performance penalties in translated apps diluting user experience; brand erosion as hardware commoditizes; operational trauma from repeating a past painful transition. These are valid but manageable through phased rollouts and robust translation tools.

**Edge:** The current roadmap is physically incapable of delivering the required form factor (thin/cool). Only this move unlocks the immediate product necessity. The proposer controls both hardware and the translation layer, offering a unified solution competitors cannot match.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the translation layer is too slow for pro users, causing a mass exodus to native PC competitors before native apps arrive. Or, if the announcement halts sales for too long, starving cash flow before new units ship.

**Door Type:** Two-way door technically (could switch back), but practically one-way due to massive sunk costs and brand perception shifts once announced.

**Cost of Delay:** High. Every quarter without thin, cool laptops cedes market share to rivals who already have them. The supplier's lag is a bleeding wound.

**Kill Criteria:** If the translation layer cannot run key professional applications at >80% native speed within 12 months, pivot to a hybrid lineup or abandon the full switch. Review date: 12 months from project start.

**Leading Metric:** Percentage of top 100 applications running natively vs. translated at launch. Review date: Launch day.

The proposal addresses an existential hardware deficit. While the risks are severe, the status quo guarantees product irrelevance in the emerging thin-laptop category. The translation layer mitigates the software gap sufficiently to bridge the transition.

DECISION: GO
