**Steelman:** Paying $19B secures the dominant mobile messaging layer to prevent displacement of the core social network, buying time to integrate users before competitors lock them in.

**Blockers:** NONE. The price is extreme but not a factual blocker given the strategic necessity of preventing obsolescence. No evidence suggests the deal cannot close or the technology is flawed.

**Risks:** Massive overpayment relative to current revenue; inability to monetize without alienating the user base; regulatory scrutiny on privacy; key founder departure post-acquisition; cultural clash between a 50-person team and a giant.

**Edge:** The buyer possesses the only balance sheet capable of funding this defense immediately. The target's refusal to advertise proves user loyalty, creating a defensible moat that rivals cannot easily breach with ad-supported models. The network effect of hundreds of millions of active users is the asset, not current cash flow.

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition fails if integration destroys the app's simplicity, causing mass user exodus to the next competitor, leaving the buyer with a $19B loss and no strategic gain.

**Door:** One-way door. Once spent, capital is gone; reversing the deal is impossible.

**Cost of Delay:** High. If a competitor acquires the target or the target pivots to directly compete, the buyer's core mobile relevance could collapse within quarters.

**Kill Criteria:** If monthly active users drop by >10% within 12 months post-close, or if the founder leaves before integration milestones are met, initiate immediate strategic review for divestiture or pivot.

**Leading Metric:** Daily Active Users (DAU) growth rate of the acquired app, reviewed quarterly.

**Decision:** The threat of displacement outweighs the financial absurdity of the price. In platform wars, losing the distribution layer is fatal. This is a defensive monopoly play, not a revenue investment.

DECISION: GO
