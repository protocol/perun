**Steelman:** Acquire the leading mobile-native photo network at $1B to instantly neutralize the primary threat to the buyer's desktop dominance and secure the mobile future, accepting that revenue is secondary to strategic positioning and user migration speed.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The valuation lacks traditional financial justification (no revenue, tiny team), creating a massive immediate write-down risk. However, the "blocker" test reveals no factual failure: the cost of delay is likely catastrophic. If the target continues growing independently, it becomes the default mobile photo layer, rendering the buyer's existing product irrelevant. Competitors cannot replicate this network effect quickly. The "no business model" risk is mitigated by the buyer's established ad infrastructure, which can be applied post-acquisition. The cultural risk is real but manageable compared to the existential threat of losing the mobile shift.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** High probability of the target displacing the buyer's core utility within 24 months. Cost: Existential.
2.  **Build Competitor:** Too slow; network effects favor the incumbent mobile app. Cost: High failure rate.
3.  **Acquire:** Expensive upfront, but buys time, users, and eliminates the competitor instantly.

**Edge:** The buyer possesses the cash reserves and ad monetization engine the target lacks, while the target possesses the mobile engagement the buyer desperately needs. This specific combination creates a monopoly defense no other player can match.

**Kill Criteria:** If user growth stalls by >50% within 6 months post-integration, or if key engineering talent exodus exceeds 40%, the strategic thesis fails. Leading metric: Daily Active Users (mobile) retention rate at 90 days.

**Verdict:** The price is staggering, but the cost of losing the mobile transition is higher. The lack of revenue is irrelevant given the strategic necessity of owning the network graph before the IPO locks in a higher public valuation or competitor interest. The "blocker" of high price is outweighed by the "edge" of immediate market control.

DECISION: GO
