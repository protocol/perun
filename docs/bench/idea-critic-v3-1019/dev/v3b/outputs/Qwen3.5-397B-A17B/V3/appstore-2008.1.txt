This fits a strategy of controlling the user experience to ensure mass-market adoption. Yet, the 30% fee creates a sustainable business model that web apps cannot match, funding the very review infrastructure needed to mitigate risk. The "one-way door" nature of opening the platform means failure could brick the brand, but the cost of delay allows competitors to lock in developers permanently.

**Evidence & Edge:**
While no internal data exists here, the external fact of rival ecosystems proves developer demand for native capabilities. The proposal's edge is the *curated* nature of the store: it turns the liability of untrusted code into a feature (trust/safety) that carriers and consumers value, unlike the wild west of competitors. The 30% cut is standard enough to attract talent while funding quality control.
