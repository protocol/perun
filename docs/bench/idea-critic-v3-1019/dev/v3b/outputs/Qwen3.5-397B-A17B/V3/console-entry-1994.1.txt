**Steelman:** A consumer electronics giant leverages its manufacturing scale and existing developer relationships to bypass cartridge costs, offering a superior 3D platform that attracts third-party studios priced out by incumbents.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal acknowledges the lack of platform experience and incumbent loyalty but identifies a specific, exploitable wedge: the economic friction of cartridge manufacturing for third-party developers. The hardware margin risk is inherent to the industry model, not a unique flaw.

**Risks:** High execution risk due to zero prior platform ownership; potential failure to secure "killer apps" without first-party studios; aggressive counter-moves from the two entrenched rivals launching competing 3D hardware simultaneously. The market is a duopoly resistant to new entrants.

**Edge:** The proposer possesses a distinct cost advantage in optical disc production compared to cartridge-based rivals. This allows for cheaper software pricing and higher margins for external developers, creating a financial incentive for third parties to support a new, unproven platform despite incumbent loyalty. The "easier tools" claim suggests a developer-first approach that incumbents may have neglected.

**Analysis:** The "do nothing" option cedes the emerging 3D CD market to rivals. While the risks are severe, the "blocker" test requires a fact showing the plan *cannot* work. The existence of loyal customers is a hurdle, not a blocker, if the economic value proposition for developers is strong enough to fragment the software library. The proposal correctly identifies that winning the developer war is the prerequisite to winning the console war. The risks are manageable through aggressive developer support and marketing, whereas the opportunity cost of missing the CD transition is existential. The edge (manufacturing cost/tools) directly attacks the incumbents' primary weakness (cartridge cost/complexity).

DECISION: GO
