**Steelman:** A mandatory client solves critical update and cheating failures while creating a future distribution monopoly, outweighing short-term friction.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact provided proves the plan fails as proposed; broadband growth trends (2003) support feasibility, and retail pushback is a transition cost, not a functional blocker.

**Risks:** Player churn due to mandatory accounts; retail partner retaliation; initial client instability; limited market reach due to connectivity gaps; competitive replication. These are execution risks, not proof of failure.

**Edge:** Direct control over the game environment eliminates cheating and update lag immediately. Owning the client creates a sticky platform asset competitors cannot easily replicate once network effects begin, turning a utility into a revenue stream via third-party sales.

**Pre-mortem:** The client launches with game-breaking bugs during peak hours, causing a PR disaster that drives players to pirated versions lacking the client. Retailers drop the games in protest, shrinking physical revenue before digital scales. Broadband adoption stalls in key regions, locking out a significant user base.

**Door Type:** Two-way door for the software requirement (can be patched or relaxed), but one-way for reputation if the launch fails catastrophically.

**Kill Criteria:** If active users on the client drop below 60% of the installed base within 90 days of launch, or if retail revenue declines by more than 40% year-over-year without digital offset, revert to optional updates and renegotiate retail terms.

**Leading Metric:** Daily Active Users (DAU) successfully connecting to the client versus total estimated installs. Review date: 30 days post-launch.

**Decision:** The proposal addresses existential threats (cheating, update fragmentation) that cannot be solved via boxes alone. The risks are manageable through staged rollouts and robust testing. The strategic upside of owning the distribution channel justifies the friction.

DECISION: GO
