**Steelman:** A stock-for-stock merger creates a top-three global automaker by combining German engineering prestige with US mass-market profitability and truck dominance, achieving immediate scale and platform synergies.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The "merger of equals" claim is the weakest link; historical data suggests such structures often devolve into power struggles due to incompatible governance (German two-tier vs. US unitary) and labor cultures (co-determination vs. at-will). The US partner's profitability relies entirely on a cyclical light-truck boom; entering this deal at the cycle's peak exposes the combined entity to massive downside when the cycle turns. Platform sharing between premium and mass-market brands risks diluting the German brand's exclusivity without guaranteeing cost savings significant enough to offset integration chaos.

Alternatives include a strategic alliance for specific platform sharing (lower risk, no cultural clash) or waiting for a US market correction to acquire assets cheaper without assuming legacy labor liabilities. Doing nothing preserves the German firm's margins and brand equity while avoiding the integration tax.

**Pre-mortem:** In five years, the entity has destroyed value. The "equal" governance caused decision paralysis during a downturn. The truck boom collapsed, dragging down the combined stock. Cultural friction led to an exodus of key engineering talent from the German side, who viewed the mass-market focus as a degradation of standards. The premium brand suffered reputational damage from perceived "cheapening" via shared components.

**Blockers:** None explicitly proven false yet, but the "cyclical peak" timing is a severe structural risk. The "equals" structure is a governance time bomb given the stated cultural differences.

**Edge:** Only if the German firm desperately needs North American manufacturing footprint *now* and cannot build it organically. However, the proposal admits the US partner is "highly profitable at the moment," implying a premium purchase price at the top of the cycle.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal buys high on a cyclical peak, merges incompatible cultures under a fragile "equals" governance model, and risks brand dilution. The risks (cultural collapse, cycle turn, brand erosion) vastly outweigh the theoretical synergies. The lack of a clear mechanism to resolve the "equals vs. takeover" ambiguity is a fatal flaw.

DECISION: NO-GO
