Steelman: Repositioning a game console as an "Entertainment System" with non-game peripherals and strict quality control can bypass retailer stigma and rebuild a collapsed market.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly addresses the primary market failure (retailer refusal) through rebranding, bundling, and financial guarantees. No factual contradiction exists between the plan's mechanisms and the stated market conditions.

**Risks:** High financial exposure from the stock take-back promise; potential developer friction due to lockout chips; execution risk in maintaining the "toy" illusion against cheap home computers. However, these are managed risks, not show-stoppers. The take-back promise is the necessary cost of re-entry; the lockout is the mechanism for quality assurance to prevent another crash.

**Edge:** The only viable path forward. Doing nothing yields zero revenue in a dead category. Selling it as a standard console guarantees immediate rejection by retailers who view "video games" as toxic. The specific combination of rebranding (distancing from the "fad"), bundling (adding perceived value beyond gaming), and inventory guarantees (removing retailer risk) directly neutralizes the three specific causes of the 1983 collapse: market saturation, poor quality software, and retailer hostility. No cheaper alternative exists because the core barrier is psychological and financial, not technical.

The pre-mortem confirms that without the take-back promise, retailers will not stock the item, causing immediate failure. Without the lockout, low-quality software returns, repeating the 1983 crash. The plan's constraints are its strengths. The edge is the total alignment of the product strategy with the specific scars of the North American market.

DECISION: GO
