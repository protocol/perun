**Steelman:** Launching a dedicated, ultra-low-cost car at $2,500 will successfully migrate scooter families to four-wheel safety, leveraging volume to offset thin margins.

**Blockers:** NONE based strictly on provided facts. The farmer protests are a risk to timeline, not a factual proof of failure; safety doubts are perception, not verified defects; cheaper alternatives exist but lack four-seat enclosed safety.

**Risks:** Land acquisition delays could stall the dedicated factory; the "cheapest" label may stigmatize the brand, reducing demand among aspirational buyers; aggressive cost-cutting might eventually validate safety fears if an incident occurs.

**Edge:** First-mover advantage in creating a distinct "lowest-price new car" category, directly addressing the specific unmet need of scooter families upgrading to four seats, which used cars and scooters cannot fully satisfy.

**Analysis:**
The proposal targets a clear demographic shift (scooter to car) with a price point no competitor matches. While the "cheapest" marketing carries reputational risk, it also creates immediate market clarity. The existence of cheaper used cars or scooters does not block this plan because they do not offer the same value proposition (new, four-seat, dedicated family vehicle). The land protests are an execution hurdle, not a fundamental flaw in the product-market fit. The safety concerns are hypothetical without evidence of actual failure.

The "do nothing" option leaves the target demographic on scooters. The alternative of a higher-priced car loses the volume strategy essential to this margin structure. A staged launch is implicit in any factory ramp-up. The core claim—that there is demand for a safe, four-seat vehicle at this price—remains unrefuted by the listed risks. The risks require management (community relations, rigorous safety testing transparency, branding nuance), but they do not constitute a blocker that proves the plan will fail as proposed. The edge of being the undisputed lowest-price option is strong enough to proceed, provided the execution risks are actively managed.

DECISION: GO
