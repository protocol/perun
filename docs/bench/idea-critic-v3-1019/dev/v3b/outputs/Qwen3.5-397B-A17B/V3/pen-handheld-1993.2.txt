**Steelman:** Launching a $700 pen-based PDA with heavy CEO publicity will define a new handheld category before competitors can react, leveraging first-mover advantage despite technical immaturity.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts provided in the text. First, the core mechanism (handwriting recognition) is explicitly "unreliable in testing." Shipping a primary input method that does not work reliably invalidates the product's main value proposition immediately. Second, the form factor is "too big for a pocket," contradicting the essential requirement for a "handheld" or personal device. Third, the ecosystem is empty ("little software") and the category is "unproven," meaning there is no existing demand to sustain a high-price, high-friction launch.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids reputational damage from a flawed flagship launch; preserves capital.
2.  **Delay & Refine:** Wait until recognition is reliable and the device fits a pocket. This sacrifices first-mover status but ensures a viable product.
3.  **Pilot:** Release a developer kit or limited run to test the category without heavy CEO-backed publicity.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to poor reviews citing unusable input and awkward size. Sales flatline due to the $700 price point and lack of software. The brand becomes synonymous with hype over utility, delaying the actual adoption of the category by years. The "heavy publicity" amplifies the failure rather than masking the defects.

**Edge:** There is no stated edge. "Leading a new category" is an aspiration, not an asset. Being first with a broken product is a disadvantage, not an edge. The risks listed are not merely potential; they are current states ("is unreliable," "is too big") that directly prevent the product from functioning as intended.

**Blockers:**
1.  Core input mechanism is unreliable (fact).
2.  Physical design fails portability requirement (fact).
3.  No software ecosystem to support high price (fact).

**Risks:** Category unproven, high price sensitivity.
**Edge:** NONE.

The plan commits significant resources to a product that fails its primary functional requirements. A cheaper, staged alternative (fix the tech, shrink the size) must precede a full launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
