**Steelman:** Replacing the flagship with a objectively superior-tasting formula will halt share loss by winning blind preference, securing the future through product merit.

**Attack:** The proposal conflates "taste preference" in isolation with "purchase intent" for a heritage brand. Blind tests removed the critical variable: the emotional contract and identity consumers hold with the original. By withdrawing the original entirely, the company transforms a product choice into a cultural loss, triggering reactance. The testing methodology was fatally flawed because it did not disclose the removal of the original, hiding the very risk that will cause failure.

**Better Way:** A "line extension" strategy. Launch the sweeter formula as "New Cola" while retaining the original as "Classic." This captures the taste-preference segment without alienating the heritage base. It preserves the option to pivot later if data supports it, rather than burning the bridge immediately.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume launch fails. Why? Because loyalists perceive the removal as an arrogant erasure of their history, leading to boycotts and media backlash that outweighs the gain from switchers. The "sweeter taste" advantage vanishes when the product is viewed as a betrayal.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Methodology Failure:** The core evidence (taste tests) is invalid for this specific decision because it excluded the consequence (removal) which drives the primary risk (emotional backlash).
2. **No Fallback:** Withdrawing the original eliminates the safety net, making the error irreversible.

**Edge:** None. The proposal relies on a metric (blind taste) that explicitly ignores the brand's actual competitive advantage (heritage/emotion).

**Kill Criteria:** If consumer sentiment regarding "loss of original" exceeds positive sentiment for "better taste" in a disclosed survey, stop.

**Verdict:** The plan bets the entire franchise on a partial dataset while ignoring the stated risk of emotional attachment. It forces a one-way door based on incomplete evidence.

DECISION: NO-GO
