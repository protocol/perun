**Steelman:** A dedicated luxury brand with a distinct dealer network and an all-new flagship sedan can overcome economy-car stigma, undercut European rivals on price while matching quality, and capture market share despite high development costs and a limited initial lineup.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker is brand perception: can an economy manufacturer convincingly sell luxury? History suggests "badge engineering" fails, but a completely separate brand and dealer network directly addresses this by creating physical and psychological distance from the economy parent. The risk of buyers refusing to pay luxury prices is mitigated by the significant price undercut; value becomes the entry drug for refinement.

Regarding the thin lineup: launching with a single flagship is a focused spearhead, not a weakness, provided the vehicle is exceptional. It concentrates R&D and marketing spend. The high development cost is a sunk reality; the decision hinges on future ROI. If the car matches European quality at a lower price, the value proposition is compelling enough to disrupt incumbents who rely on heritage rather than pure value-per-dollar.

The "separate dealer network" cost is high but necessary. Sharing showrooms with economy models would instantly dilute the luxury claim. This infrastructure investment is the price of admission, not an optional extra.

**Pre-mortem:** The venture fails if the flagship sedan does not objectively match European refinement. If the "engineered from scratch" claim is marketing fluff and the car feels cheap, the low price will confirm bias rather than overcome it. However, the proposal explicitly states the engineering goal is to match leaders on quality. Assuming execution meets this technical target, the market gap for "attainable luxury" is wide open.

**Edge:** The ability to price below incumbents while matching quality creates a unique value wedge that heritage brands cannot match without cannibalizing their own high-margin lines.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks (stigma, cost, lineup) are addressed by the proposal's specific structural choices (separate brand/dealers, focused flagship, value pricing).
**Risks:** Execution failure in build quality; slow brand adoption.
**Edge:** Superior price-to-quality ratio unachievable by legacy luxury makers.

DECISION: GO
