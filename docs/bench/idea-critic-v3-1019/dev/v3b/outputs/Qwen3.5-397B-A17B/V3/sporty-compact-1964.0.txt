**Steelman:** Leverage existing compact mechanics to deliver affordable sporty styling for emerging young buyers, monetizing customization via options and scaling demand through aggressive marketing.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly addresses the "cheap" perception risk by pairing distinct sporty styling (long hood, short deck) with shared mechanicals, a proven strategy for margin expansion without re-engineering core reliability. The "unproven demand" risk is mitigated by the low base price point and the demographic tailwind of the baby boom entering the market; the financial exposure is capped by the shared platform, making the upside asymmetric.

**Risks:** Heavy upfront ad spend could erode margins if initial volume lags; excessive option complexity might stall production ramp-up. These are execution risks, not fundamental flaws, manageable through phased marketing spend and strict supply chain oversight.

**Edge:** First-mover advantage in the "affordable sporty" segment using an already-proven, reliable mechanical base. Competitors lack the specific combination of low-cost infrastructure and targeted styling for this new demographic. The "dull" reputation of the donor car is neutralized by the new body style, while its reliability serves as a hidden asset.

**Decision Method:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the emerging youth segment to rivals. Building a clean-sheet sports car violates the "low price" constraint. A mere trim package on the existing dull model fails the "sporty styling" requirement. This proposal is the only viable path to the stated goal.
2.  **Fit:** Aligns perfectly with the demographic shift (baby boom) and the need for higher-margin variants on low-margin volume cars.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** If it fails, it is due to pricing the options too high or misjudging the aesthetic appeal, not mechanical failure.
4.  **Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders do not cover 20% of launch ad spend within 60 days, pause further media buying.

The plan beats "do nothing" by capturing a new market segment with minimal incremental engineering cost. The risks are standard for a new product launch and are outweighed by the strategic necessity of owning the youth entry-point.

DECISION: GO
