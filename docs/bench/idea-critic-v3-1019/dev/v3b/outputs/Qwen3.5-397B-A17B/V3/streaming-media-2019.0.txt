**Steelman:** Direct-to-consumer streaming captures long-term value and control that licensing to rivals forfeits, justifying short-term losses to build a scaled audience in a shifting market.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on a fatal arithmetic gap. It plans to fund "multi-year operating losses" while simultaneously eliminating the "billions" in licensing revenue that currently fund operations. The company has no history of running a technology platform, introducing massive execution risk against entrenched incumbents with 100M+ subscribers. Undercutting the market leader on price while burning cash creates a unit economics trap: every new subscriber increases the loss magnitude without a clear path to profitability. The "cannibalization" of cable and theatrical revenue is not a side effect; it is an immediate destruction of the cash cow required to subsidize the new venture. Without the licensing income, the balance sheet cannot sustain the required burn rate to reach "tens of millions" of subscribers before capital runs dry.

**Alternatives:** 
1. **Do Nothing:** Continue collecting billions in licensing fees while the market shifts; risky but cash-positive.
2. **Hybrid:** Launch a service but keep licensing key titles to rivals to maintain cash flow; slower growth but survivable.
3. **Proposed Plan:** Immediate cash incineration with unproven tech and no revenue bridge.

**Fit:** While the vision aligns with industry trends, the financial mechanics described guarantee insolvency before scale is reached. The "edge" is merely content ownership, which rivals can replicate by outbidding or waiting for the proposer to collapse.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Funding Gap:** Eliminating billions in licensing revenue removes the capital source needed to fund the proposed multi-year losses.
2. **Execution Void:** No existing capability to run the required technology platform at scale.
3. **Unit Economics:** Pricing below market leader while burning cash ensures negative contribution margin per user.

**Edge:** None stated beyond existing library, which is insufficient to overcome the financial structural failure.

**Kill Criteria:** If licensing revenue drops by >50% before the service reaches 40M subscribers and positive cash flow, the company faces liquidity crisis.

**Verdict:** The plan destroys the funding mechanism required to execute itself.

DECISION: NO-GO
