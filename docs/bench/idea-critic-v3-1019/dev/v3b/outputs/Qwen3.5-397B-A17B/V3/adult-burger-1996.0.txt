**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can overwrite the "kids' brand" perception, forcing adult trial of a premium burger that generates higher margins.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "Fit" and "Edge" tests. The brand's core asset is value and family fun; a premium price point directly contradicts the established value proposition that drives volume. The "edge" cited is merely spending power, not a product or structural advantage. History shows that when a value brand attempts to pivot upmarket without a sub-brand, it confuses the core customer base while failing to convince the target demographic, who view the brand as inherently "cheap."

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Protect current margins and brand clarity.
2.  **Sub-brand/Test:** Launch a limited-time offer in select adult-demographic markets to validate demand before committing industry-record budgets.
3.  **Proposal As-Is:** High risk of alienating the core base while failing to convert adults due to brand dissonance.

**Pre-mortem:** The campaign launches, costs balloon, and adults perceive the burger as overpriced for the brand, while families feel priced out or confused. The company burns cash on an image shift that requires years to sustain, leading to a sharp stock decline and a forced retreat to value menu items.

**Blockers:**
*   **Brand-Price Mismatch:** The stated fact that customers come for "value" contradicts a "premium price" strategy.
*   **Unproven Demand:** Heavy spending is planned *before* demand is shown, violating basic unit economics for a new category entry.
*   **Lack of Edge:** Spending more than competitors is not a sustainable competitive advantage if the product-brand fit is broken.

**Risks:** Total brand dilution, catastrophic cash burn, failure to retain core customers.

**Edge:** NONE. The proposal offers no unique asset other than budget size, which is a liability if the hypothesis is wrong.

**Verdict:** The plan bets the company on an unproven assumption that advertising alone can override deep-seated brand positioning. It lacks a staged approach, ignores the core value constraint, and commits irreversible capital before validation.

DECISION: NO-GO
