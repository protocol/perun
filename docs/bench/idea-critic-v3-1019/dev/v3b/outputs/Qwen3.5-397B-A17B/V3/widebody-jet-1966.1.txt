**Steelman:** Building a massively larger, convertible wide-body jet secures dominance against supersonic threats and meets urgent capacity demand, leveraging a single anchor customer to justify unprecedented capital risk.

**Attack:** The proposal bets the company's survival on three unproven pillars: an engine that does not yet exist, a factory costing more than the firm's net worth, and a market shift away from the anticipated supersonic future. With only one customer committed, a single order cancellation triggers insolvency. The "freighter conversion" feature adds complexity and cost without a confirmed secondary market. If supersonic travel becomes the standard as predicted, this subsonic giant becomes a stranded asset before it pays back its debt. The cost per seat advantage is irrelevant if the aircraft cannot be sold to anyone other than the initial requester.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Wait for supersonic clarity; risk losing the airline to competitors.
2. *Stretch Current Model:* Lower risk, but fails to meet the "2.5x size" requirement.
3. *Consortium:* Share development costs with another manufacturer to mitigate financial ruin.

**Fit:** Aligns with growth but contradicts the industry's expected supersonic pivot.

**Pre-mortem:** The engine fails certification, delaying delivery by years. The airline cancels due to changing routes. The company collapses under debt service before the first unit flies.

**Blockers:** None explicitly proven false yet, but the financial exposure is absolute.
**Risks:** Total insolvency, engine failure, supersonic obsolescence, single-customer dependency.
**Edge:** First-mover advantage in high-capacity subsonic travel; unique convertible design.

**Decision Logic:** While the risks are existential, the "do nothing" option cedes the high-growth subsonic market to competitors if the supersonic prediction fails. The convertible design hedges against freighter demand. The single customer is a risk, but also the necessary catalyst for such a leap. The potential reward (monopoly on high-capacity travel) outweighs the status quo, provided the company can secure financing. The proposal represents a necessary, albeit terrifying, evolution to maintain market leadership.

DECISION: GO
