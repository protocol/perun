**Steelman:** Leverage seven million subscribers and existing cash flow to seed a digital transition before broadband saturation makes entry impossible, accepting short-term margin pressure to secure long-term relevance.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "edge" test. The catalogue is weak, quality is poor, and rights are expensive. Entering now against future tech giants with a inferior product risks brand damage without securing loyalty. Cannibalizing a highly profitable DVD monopoly for a loss-leading, low-value streaming service destroys immediate shareholder value with no guaranteed upside. The "strategic necessity" argument is speculative; the company could wait for technology to mature and costs to drop, using its DVD profits to buy a later entrant or license content when leverage is higher.

**Better Way:** Do nothing for 24 months. Monitor broadband adoption and licensing cost curves. Use current profits to maximize DVD efficiency. Enter only when a viable, high-quality catalogue is affordable or when subscriber churn to competitors becomes measurable.

**Pre-mortem:** In 2010, the company has spent hundreds of millions on licensing, alienated studios by depressing DVD margins, and lost subscribers to superior tech platforms that entered later with better interfaces and content. The DVD business collapsed faster than expected due to internal cannibalization, leaving the firm with neither a dominant physical nor digital presence.

**Blockers:** NONE (The plan is executable).
**Risks:** High licensing costs, brand erosion from poor quality, accelerated DVD decline, capital burn against deeper-pocketed rivals.
**Edge:** NONE. The proposal cites no unique asset (tech, exclusive data, or cost advantage) that allows this specific firm to win against future tech entrants. "Existing subscribers" is a distribution channel, not a product advantage if the product is inferior.

**Decision Logic:** A sound plan requires a named edge to justify the risk of cannibalization and capital expenditure against larger future competitors. This proposal relies on fear of missing out ("strategic risk") rather than a concrete advantage. Without an edge, the "do nothing" or "wait and see" alternative dominates because it preserves the profitable core while avoiding premature investment in a nascent, low-quality market.

DECISION: NO-GO
