**Steelman:** Launching a $700 pen-based PDA with heavy CEO publicity creates a new handheld category by betting that superior input methods outweigh current technical limitations and high costs.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts provided in the text. First, the core mechanism (handwriting recognition) is explicitly "unreliable in testing." Releasing a primary input feature known to be broken guarantees a poor user experience, regardless of marketing. Second, the device violates a fundamental constraint for "handheld" utility: it is "too big for a pocket." Third, the ecosystem is empty ("little software"), and the category is "unproven," meaning there is no existing demand to justify the $700 price point.

**Alternatives:**
The "do nothing" option avoids sinking costs into a flawed prototype. A "better way" would be to delay launch until recognition is reliable and the form factor fits a pocket, or to target a vertical market where size and price are less sensitive, rather than a mass-market "personal" device.

**Pre-mortem:**
Assume failure: The device launches, users cannot reliably input data due to recognition errors, the device is inconvenient to carry, and no developers build apps because the installed base is small. The result is a costly recall or brand damage associated with broken promises.

**Edge:**
The proposal cites "heavy publicity by the chief executive" as an asset. However, publicity amplifies both success and failure; promoting a broken, oversized, expensive product with no software will accelerate negative word-of-mouth, not create an edge. There is no stated fact showing a unique asset, cost advantage, or proven demand that rivals lack.

**Conclusion:**
Blockers exist: the core technology does not work as required, the form factor contradicts the use case, and the price lacks justification. These are not risks to be managed; they are fatal flaws in the current plan.

DECISION: NO-GO
