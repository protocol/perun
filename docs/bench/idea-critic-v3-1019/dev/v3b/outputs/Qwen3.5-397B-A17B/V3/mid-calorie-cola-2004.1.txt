**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men avoiding both full-sugar guilt and diet stigma, leveraging a premium price to signal innovation before the low-carb trend fades.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests. First, the core assumption—that young men want a "halfway" drink—is unproven and contradicts the binary nature of diet choices (indulgence vs. restriction). A half-sugar product risks satisfying neither group, cannibalizing existing sales without growing the base. Second, the premium pricing directly conflicts with the goal of driving trial among a demographic known for price sensitivity, especially for an unproven flavor profile. Third, the "low-carb" catalyst is explicitly identified as a potential fad; building a major campaign and production line on a transient trend invites massive stranded costs. Finally, a rival's similar launch suggests a crowded entry point where differentiation is weak, turning the premium price into a liability rather than an asset.

**Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option preserves margins while observing if the rival's similar drink succeeds or fails, avoiding the cost of a large campaign. A cheaper alternative is a limited regional test without a premium price hike to validate demand before committing to a national rollout.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: The product launches, young men reject the taste and price, diet drinkers stay loyal to zero-calorie options, and the low-carb trend collapses within a year, leaving unsold inventory and a damaged brand perception of "confused" positioning.

**Decision Logic:** There is no named edge proving this specific proposer can win against a rival with a similar product in a fad-driven market. The risks (cannibalization, trend volatility, price resistance) are not mitigated by kill criteria or a staged approach; the proposal demands a full launch immediately. The lack of evidence for the specific "young male" demand gap, combined with the high cost of a large campaign on a potentially fleeting trend, constitutes a blocker.

Blockers: Unproven demand for "mid-calorie" vs. binary diet choices; premium price deters trial in a fad market; rival saturation.
Risks: Trend collapse, brand confusion, cannibalization.
Edge: NONE stated.

DECISION: NO-GO
