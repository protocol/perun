**Steelman:** Acquiring the agency and platform instantly grants the scale and technology needed to challenge the search leader's emerging dominance before the window closes.

**Attack:** The $6 billion cash price represents a massive premium in a fast-changing market, creating immediate financial risk. The core conflict is structural: owning an agency while serving competitors alienates existing agency customers, potentially driving them to rivals. Furthermore, display ad technology evolves rapidly; buying a static asset today may result in obsolete infrastructure tomorrow, whereas the search leader's move leverages existing data dominance rather than just buying distribution.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid debt and customer conflict, but cede the display market to the competitor.
2.  **Partner/Build:** Develop tech internally or form alliances; slower, cheaper, avoids customer conflict, but risks missing the strategic window.
3.  **Acquire Technology Only:** Buy the platform without the agency arm to mitigate customer conflicts, though this reduces immediate scale.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If the market shifts away from current display standards or if major agency clients defect due to the conflict, the $6 billion becomes a sunk cost with no path to ROI. The "fast-changing" nature of the tech suggests the acquisition might lock the company into a dying paradigm. The premium paid assumes current conditions persist, which the proposal explicitly doubts.

**Blockers:**
*   **Customer Conflict:** No resolution offered for retaining existing agency clients who will view the buyer as a competitor.
*   **Valuation vs. Volatility:** The fixed $6 billion cost contradicts the acknowledged "fast-changing" risk; the price assumes stability the market lacks.
*   **Strategic Redundancy:** Competing via acquisition duplicates the rival's move without the rival's inherent search-data advantage.

**Edge:** None stated. Fear of falling behind is not an edge. The proposal admits the technology is volatile and the price is high, offering no unique asset to overcome these specific deficits.

**Kill Criteria:** If agency churn exceeds 10% within 12 months or display revenue fails to grow 20% YoY by Q2 2008, the strategy is failing.

**Verdict:** The unresolved channel conflict and excessive premium in a volatile market constitute clear blockers. The plan relies on hope rather than a defensible advantage.

DECISION: NO-GO
