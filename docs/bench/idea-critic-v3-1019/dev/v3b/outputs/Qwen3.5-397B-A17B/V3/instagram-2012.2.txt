**Claim:** Acquire the photo-sharing app for $1B to neutralize a mobile threat and capture its user base.

**Steelman:** At $1B, the price buys immediate dominance in mobile photo sharing, removing the primary existential threat to the buyer's core business before an IPO highlights mobile weakness. The cost is justified as a defensive monopoly rent rather than a revenue multiple.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The valuation lacks fundamental support (no revenue, tiny team). However, the "do nothing" alternative carries catastrophic risk: if the app continues growing, it becomes the default camera, rendering the buyer's photo product irrelevant post-IPO. Building a competitor internally is too slow given the app's network effects and two-platform momentum. A "wait and see" approach fails because the target's value scales exponentially with user growth; waiting increases the price or allows a rival to buy it.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if regulators block the deal (killing the IPO narrative) or if the integration destroys the culture, causing user exodus. The "one-way door" nature demands certainty on cultural preservation.

**Evidence & Edge:** The buyer possesses the cash/stock liquidity post-IPO to close immediately. The edge is the unique ability to absorb a $1B loss to protect a multi-billion dollar core franchise, which no other bidder can match without jeopardizing their own stability. The threat is verified by the app's rapid cross-platform expansion.

**Blockers:** None. The lack of revenue is a feature of the strategy (buying growth/defense), not a bug. Regulatory risk is present but manageable as a calculated gamble compared to certain competitive erosion.

**Risks:** Cultural destruction, regulatory delay, overpayment if mobile trends shift.
**Edge:** Exclusive financial capacity to execute a defensive monopoly buy that secures the core business's future valuation.

**Kill Criteria:** If user growth stalls by >50% within 6 months post-acquisition, or if key founders depart within 90 days, initiate immediate divestiture or fire-sale. Leading metric: Daily Active Users (DAU) retention rate vs. pre-acquisition baseline.

DECISION: GO
