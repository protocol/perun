Furthermore, granting the target's leaders control over the buyer's animation unit invites significant culture clash and internal resistance, potentially destabilizing the broader organization. The founder becoming a major shareholder complicates governance, potentially creating a power center rivaling existing management.

Losing this partner to a competitor would cede the animation market dominance for a generation. The buyer's own hand-drawn unit is already failing; organic recovery is unproven and slow. In the entertainment sector, hit-driven economics often defy standard P/E ratios; the value lies in the franchise potential and library, not just annual output. The "high price" is relative to the cost of irrelevance. While culture clash and key-person risks are real, they are manageable through retention packages and operational autonomy, whereas losing the studio to a rival is irreversible. The strategic necessity of securing the premier content engine outweighs the financial stretch, provided the integration respects the studio's creative independence.
