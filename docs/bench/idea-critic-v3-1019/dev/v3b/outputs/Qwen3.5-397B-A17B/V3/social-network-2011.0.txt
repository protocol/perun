**Steelman:** Leveraging dominant search distribution to force a differentiated social layer (circles, hangouts) can overcome network effects where standalone attempts failed.

**Attack:** The core claim—that integration overcomes user inertia—contradicts the company's own history of three failed social attempts despite having this same distribution. Tying executive bonuses to adoption creates a perverse incentive to prioritize vanity metrics (sign-ups) over genuine engagement, likely exacerbating the "forced integration" risk and annoying the existing user base. The incumbent's network effect is not just a barrier; it is the product value. A "better" feature (circles) does not automatically displace a "locked-in" network. The proposal treats distribution as a silver bullet, ignoring that previous failures suggests the problem is fundamental user behavior, not just visibility.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve core search/mail utility; avoid brand damage from another high-profile social failure.
2.  **Niche Acquisition:** Buy a small, engaged community rather than building a generic network from scratch.
3.  **Feature-Only:** Keep "circles" and "hangouts" as standalone utilities within existing products without forcing a full profile migration.

**Pre-mortem:** By 2013, the platform launches with high initial sign-ups due to forced integration but sees near-zero organic engagement. Users feel coerced, leading to public backlash and churn. Executives collect short-term bonuses based on flawed adoption metrics before the project is quietly shelved as the fourth failure. The cost of delay is minimal; the market leader is not threatened by a forced clone.

**Blockers:**
*   **Historical Precedent:** Three prior failures with the same distribution advantage suggest the hypothesis "integration solves adoption" is false.
*   **Incentive Misalignment:** Bonus structures tied to adoption invite gaming the system rather than building value.

**Edge:** None identified. Differentiators are feature-level, not network-level. Fear of missing out is not an edge.

**Verdict:** The plan repeats past failures with higher reputational risk and distorted incentives. It lacks a credible mechanism to break the incumbent's lock-in beyond "force," which historically fails.

DECISION: NO-GO
