**Steelman:** A premium, connected juicer creates a recurring revenue moat by guaranteeing freshness and convenience via proprietary packs, justifying a high upfront device cost through lifetime subscription value.

**Attack & Options:**
The claim fails on unit economics and market fit. At $699, the device targets early adopters, yet the recurring cost of proprietary bags likely exceeds fresh produce, destroying the value proposition for daily use. The "connected" feature adds failure points (Wi-Fi dependency) without solving a core user pain (freshness is visual/temporal, not app-verifiable).
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids massive CAPEX on custom engineering and inventory risk for perishable goods.
*   **Cheaper Alternative:** Sell a high-quality mechanical press ($200) compatible with standard produce; test subscription demand separately without hardware lock-in.
*   **Fit:** A "smart" appliance contradicts the north star of simplicity in kitchen tools; complexity increases breakage and returns.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because the attach rate for subscriptions collapses after three months due to price fatigue, leaving users with an expensive, single-function brick that cannot accept grocery store fruit. The hardware margin is erased by warranty claims on the custom press mechanism.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
No evidence is provided that consumers will pay a premium for app-verified freshness over visual inspection. The $699 price point creates a narrow total addressable market while requiring mass-market subscription adoption to sustain the business model—a contradiction. The requirement for internet connectivity introduces a blocker: if servers dip or Wi-Fi fails, the core value (freshness check) vanishes, rendering the device less useful than a $50 manual press.

**Edge:** None stated. Being "well-funded" is not an edge; it is fuel for a potentially flawed engine. No unique asset or proven demand is cited.

**Verdict:**
Blockers: High unit cost vs. unproven subscription LTV; connectivity creates single point of failure without clear user benefit.
Risks: Low retention, high return rate, inventory spoilage.
Edge: None.

DECISION: NO-GO
