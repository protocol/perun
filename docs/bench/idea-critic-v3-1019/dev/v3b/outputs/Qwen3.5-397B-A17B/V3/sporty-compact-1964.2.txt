**Steelman:** Leverage existing compact mechanics to deliver affordable sporty styling for a new demographic, maximizing margin via options and scale via massive advertising.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly mitigates the primary financial risk (unproven demand) through the use of existing mechanical parts (low development cost) and a low base price. The "cheap" perception risk is countered by distinct sporty styling and a long options list, allowing buyers to upscale the feel. The heavy ad spend is a calculated bet on volume, not a sunk cost before production.

**Risks:** Demand uncertainty remains, but the low-cost foundation limits downside. If the style fails, the loss is contained to marketing and tooling, not expensive new engineering. The "economy" stigma is a branding challenge, not a fatal flaw, solvable by the proposed design language.

**Edge:** First-mover advantage in a nascent "affordable sporty" segment created by a specific demographic shift (postwar boomers). The ability to customize via options captures higher margins from enthusiasts while the low entry price captures volume. Using proven mechanics ensures reliability and speed to market, which competitors with clean-sheet designs cannot match immediately.

**Decision Logic:** The plan beats "do nothing" by capturing a growing market segment with minimal incremental engineering risk. It beats "clean-sheet sporty car" by being significantly cheaper and faster to launch. It beats "wait and see" because the demographic window is opening now; delay cedes the category to rivals. The risks are manageable given the low structural cost basis. The kill criteria are implicit in the options strategy: if customization doesn't drive margin, the model fails, but the low base price ensures volume floor.

DECISION: GO
