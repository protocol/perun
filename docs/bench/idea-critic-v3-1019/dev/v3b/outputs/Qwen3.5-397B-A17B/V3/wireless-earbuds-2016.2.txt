Steelman: Removing the headphone jack forces a transition to wireless audio, allowing the company to control the ecosystem, eliminate legacy ports to save internal space, and sell a high-margin accessory that solves the resulting user problem seamlessly.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists between the proposal's costs/revenue and the company's financial survival. The risks cited are behavioral (ridicule, loss anger), not structural failures.
**Risks:** High price point ($159) vs. free wired alternatives; potential consumer backlash over perceived forced obsolescence; battery anxiety (5 hours); physical loss of small units. These are execution and adoption risks, not proof the plan fails.
**Edge:** Proprietary chip enables automatic pairing, creating a user experience (frictionless connection) that generic Bluetooth competitors cannot match at launch. This justifies the premium over "free" but inferior wired options.

**Pre-mortem:** The product fails if the market views the jack removal as purely cost-cutting without providing a superior wireless alternative. However, the proposal includes the specific solution (custom chip, charging case) to mitigate the "odd look" and battery concerns. The "easy to lose" risk is inherent to the form factor but does not prevent the product from functioning or selling to early adopters.

**Alternatives:**
1. *Do nothing:* Keep the jack. Risk: Falling behind on water resistance, internal space optimization, and wireless trends. Loses the accessory revenue stream.
2. *Include dongle only:* Cheaper, but fails to establish a new premium category or ecosystem lock-in.
3. *Delay launch:* Allows competitors to set the standard first.

The proposal beats "do nothing" because it converts a necessary hardware constraint (removing the jack) into a strategic advantage (ecosystem lock-in + revenue). The risks are real but manageable through marketing and product design; they do not constitute a blocker. The price is high, but justified by the proprietary technology and convenience.

DECISION: GO
