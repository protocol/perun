The claim is that a clear cola, validated by positive test markets, should launch nationwide immediately with a major campaign to counter slowing growth. The steelman: this extension leverages existing distribution to capture a "purer" niche, reversing growth stagnation before competitors act.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The stated risks are not merely potential; they are structural flaws in the core assumption that "clear equals purer" for cola drinkers. History and category logic suggest consumers equate cola flavor with dark color; removing the color may break the flavor expectation, causing the "positive response" in test markets to be a novelty spike rather than sustainable demand. The proposal admits novelty buyers may not repeat, yet relies on a "large marketing campaign" to drive a national launch. This is a mismatch: heavy spend cannot fix a fundamental product-market fit issue if the repeat rate is low.

Furthermore, the risk of cannibalizing the flagship is a direct financial blocker. If the clear cola merely shifts existing customers from the high-margin flagship to a new SKU without expanding the total user base, the net value is negative, especially given the high cost of the proposed national campaign. The test market data is explicitly flagged as potentially overstating demand, yet the proposal proceeds as if the data is definitive. There is no "edge" cited—no unique asset or proven demand that rivals cannot copy immediately (as noted in the rival copy risk).

The alternative "do nothing" or a limited regional rollout preserves capital while testing the repeat rate, which is the true unknown. A national launch with a large campaign is a one-way door with high sunk costs based on unverified repeat behavior. The plan lacks a specific kill criterion for the repeat rate before scaling. Without evidence that the "purer" positioning drives long-term loyalty distinct from novelty, the plan exposes the company to significant brand dilution and financial loss.

DECISION: NO-GO
