**Steelman:** Sacrificing immediate, high-margin licensing revenue to build a direct-to-consumer monopoly asset is the only way to survive the industry's shift away from linear TV and third-party distribution.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" and "fit" tests. The plan assumes the company can endure years of massive operating losses while simultaneously losing billions in guaranteed licensing income from rivals. This creates a "double burn" scenario: cash outflows for platform build/marketing plus immediate inflow cessation.

The "better way" is a hybrid model: retain licensing for non-core content or specific windows while launching a premium, higher-priced niche service. This funds growth without requiring a multi-year blackout of primary revenue streams. The proposal's reliance on a "low price" to dislodge a competitor with 100M+ subscribers ignores the immense switching costs and content depth gap; price alone rarely breaks network effects in streaming without unlimited content velocity, which this entity lacks compared to pure-play rivals.

Furthermore, the execution risk is a blocker. The company has never operated a global consumer technology platform at this scale. Building this while managing a transition away from their core competency (licensing) introduces catastrophic operational friction. The pre-mortem is clear: the company runs out of cash before reaching the subscriber density required for profitability because the licensing cliff hits immediately, while subscriber growth is linear and slow.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Financial Contradiction:** The simultaneous loss of licensing revenue and addition of operating losses creates a cash hole likely exceeding the company's risk tolerance, contradicting the "fund it" assumption without explicit capital sources.
2.  **Strategic Gap:** No evidence provided that a "low price" overcomes the incumbent's content lead; this is a commoditized race to the bottom without a differentiated edge.

**Edge:** None stated. Fear of falling behind is not an edge.

**Kill Criteria:** If licensing revenue drops >20% in Year 1 without a corresponding 15% subscriber growth rate, the model fails.

**Verdict:** The proposal demands a binary, irreversible pivot based on unproven execution capabilities and a flawed financial model that ignores the value of the existing licensing ecosystem.

DECISION: NO-GO
