**Steelman:** A premium, connected juicer creates a recurring revenue stream by locking users into a convenient, freshness-guaranteed subscription model, justifying high R&D through hardware-software integration.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unit Economics & Value:** At $699, the device cost is prohibitive for a single-function appliance. Proprietary pre-packed bags inevitably carry a massive premium over fresh produce due to packaging, cold-chain logistics, and margin stacking. Consumers will reject a per-juice cost significantly higher than buying fruit or visiting a juice bar.
2.  **Friction vs. Convenience:** Requiring Wi-Fi to verify "freshness" introduces catastrophic friction. If the network fails, the device bricks. Users will not tolerate connectivity dependencies for a basic mechanical function (pressing fruit). This is a solution in search of a problem; visual inspection suffices for freshness.
3.  **Market Timing & Behavior:** In early 2016, the "connected home" novelty is wearing off against utility. The market for single-use countertop appliances is shrinking, not growing. The subscription model assumes users want less control over their ingredients, contradicting the health-conscious demographic likely to juice.
4.  **Better Way:** Sell a high-quality, offline mechanical press. Allow users to input their own produce. If a subscription is needed, offer pre-portioned *ingredients* without the proprietary bag lock-in or internet gatekeeping.

**Pre-mortem:** The company burns cash on custom engineering and cold-chain logistics. Early adopters return the device when the app glitches or the subscription cost becomes absurd compared to grocery bills. The "smart" feature becomes a liability, not an asset. Inventory of proprietary bags becomes worthless waste.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price Dissonance:** $699 hardware + premium subscription exceeds the total cost of ownership for alternatives.
*   **Unnecessary Connectivity:** Internet dependency for freshness checking creates a single point of failure with no user benefit.
*   **Locked Ecosystem:** Proprietary bags limit market size to only those willing to pay a monopoly tax on ingredients.

**Edge:** None identified. The proposed advantages (freshness check, convenience) are outweighed by cost and friction.

**Kill Criteria:** If the cost per serving exceeds $8, or if the device requires internet for basic operation, the value proposition collapses.

DECISION: NO-GO
