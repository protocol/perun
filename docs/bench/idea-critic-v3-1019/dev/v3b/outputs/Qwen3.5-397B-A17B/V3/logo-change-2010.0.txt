The claim is that an untested, unannounced, simultaneous global logo replacement will successfully modernize a mature brand without triggering destructive backlash. The steelman posits that shock creates immediate contemporary relevance and that loyalty buffers temporary confusion.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The "edge" required to justify this risk is absent; no evidence suggests the current logo prevents modernization or that the new design outperforms the old in customer preference. Conversely, the risks are concrete and verified by the proposal's own admission: loyal attachment is high, social media amplifies instant backlash, and recognition relies on the specific square being removed.

The "better way" analysis reveals a dominant alternative: a phased rollout with customer testing. This approach achieves the modernization goal while mitigating the risk of alienating the core base. The proposed "all-at-once" strategy offers no unique upside over a staged approach but introduces catastrophic downside potential (brand alienation, sales drop) that cannot be reversed once the old assets are destroyed.

The pre-mortem is clear: if this fails, it is because the company misread customer attachment as flexibility. The cost of delay (waiting to test) is negligible compared to the cost of a botched global launch. The proposal explicitly admits the design was "not tested," which constitutes a fatal gap in evidence for a decision involving total brand identity. A strategy relying on hope rather than data, which actively ignores known risks (backlash, recognition loss) without a mitigation plan, cannot be approved.

Blockers: The lack of customer testing for a core brand asset is a factual gap that prevents validating the primary goal (looking contemporary vs. looking confused).
Risks: Instant social media backlash, loss of brand recognition, alienation of loyal customers.
Edge: None identified; the proposal relies on the assumption that change equals improvement without proof.

This is a one-way door decision made with zero evidence. It violates the basic principle of verifying demand before execution.

DECISION: NO-GO
