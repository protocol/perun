**Steelman:** Launching a $10,000 GUI-based computer in 1983 captures the high-end office market by offering unprecedented usability despite higher costs and lower speed.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the blocker test immediately. The price ($10,000) is several times higher than leading business PCs, which already dominate the market. For office workers, speed is a primary productivity metric; a "slow" machine undermines the core value proposition of efficiency. Furthermore, the lack of third-party software creates a barren ecosystem, rendering the hardware less useful than established competitors.

Most critically, an internal alternative exists: a cheaper machine with a similar interface under development. This internal option offers the same usability benefits (GUI/mouse) without the prohibitive price tag or the stated performance penalties (implied by "cheaper" and likely newer architecture). Choosing the expensive, slow, software-poor option over the cheaper, internal alternative violates basic economic rationality. The "edge" claimed (usability) is present in the cheaper internal option, negating any justification for the $10,000 price point.

The risk of market rejection is not theoretical; it is a near-certainty given the price-performance mismatch. The existence of a superior internal alternative acts as a definitive blocker. Launching this specific configuration now would cannibalize resources from the better internal project and likely result in immediate commercial failure. There is no scenario where the "slow, expensive, software-less" unit wins against the "cheaper, similar interface" unit currently in the pipeline.

**Blockers:**
1. Price is multiples of competitors without corresponding value.
2. Performance (slow) contradicts office productivity needs.
3. Internal alternative offers same UI benefits at lower cost.

**Risks:** Total market rejection; resource diversion from superior internal project.

**Edge:** None identified; the proposed advantages are negated by the internal alternative.

**Kill Criteria:** If a cheaper internal alternative with similar UI exists (Verified: Yes), do not launch the expensive version.

DECISION: NO-GO
