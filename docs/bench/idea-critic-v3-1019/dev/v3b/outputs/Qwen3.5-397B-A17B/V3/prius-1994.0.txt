Steelman: Committing now secures a decade-long technological moat and brand definition before regulators force the issue.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal; the constraints are risks, not impossibilities. The technology (NiMH, power-split) is novel but physically viable.

**Risks:** High R&D burn with negative near-term ROI due to cheap fuel; unproven battery durability could trigger massive warranty liabilities; market indifference to efficiency without regulatory pressure.

**Edge:** First-mover advantage in volume production. By locking in the supply chain and patenting the power-split architecture before competitors react, the firm defines the segment. Waiting for demand signals or regulatory mandates cedes this strategic ground to rivals who might move first.

**Analysis:**
The "do nothing" option preserves cash but guarantees commodity status in a future where efficiency inevitably becomes valuable (either via regulation or fuel volatility). The cost of delay is the loss of the "hybrid" brand association. While battery durability is unproven, a bold public schedule acts as a forcing function to solve reliability faster than a quiet R&D program. The lack of current demand is a feature, not a bug; it allows the company to shape consumer expectations rather than fight for share in an established category.

The pre-mortem suggests failure occurs if the battery degrades rapidly, destroying brand trust. However, this is a mitigation target, not a stopper. The kill criterion must be strict: if prototype packs cannot meet a specific cycle-life threshold by the tooling freeze date, the program pivots to a mild-hybrid or delays launch, but the *commitment* to the architecture stands.

This is a two-way door in strategy (you can slow production) but a one-way door in opportunity (you cannot easily become the "first" later). The edge of defining the category outweighs the financial risk of being early, provided the engineering team is empowered to solve the durability gap under the pressure of the public schedule.

DECISION: GO
