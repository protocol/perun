**Steelman:** Leveraging vertical integration of hardware and a newly acquired OS to create a differentiated ecosystem, using aggressive marketing to overcome the "chicken-and-egg" app problem before competitors solidify their moats.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces a catastrophic coordination failure. The market leader dominates with a massive app catalog; users buy tablets for apps, not just hardware. A "small app catalog" is a fatal defect, not a minor risk, because developer outreach takes years to yield results, while consumer adoption requires day-one utility. Investing heavily in advertising a product with low utility (few apps) burns capital without generating sustainable demand.

Furthermore, the operating system has almost no market share, meaning no existing user base to migrate. Leadership transition introduces strategic instability exactly when long-term ecosystem commitment is required. The "unproven" perception is rational; shoppers will not pay market-leading prices for an inferior ecosystem.

**Better Way:**
The "do nothing" or "delay" option is superior until the app catalog reaches a critical mass or the OS gains traction in another segment (e.g., phones) to leverage cross-device synergy. Launching now guarantees a high-profile failure that could poison the brand for future attempts. A cheaper, safer alternative is to license a mature OS or delay launch to build software leverage first.

**Kill Criteria & Verdict:**
*Blocker:* The core value proposition (apps) is missing at launch, and advertising cannot fix a product gap. The financial math of high ad spend vs. low retention due to poor app support fails.
*Risk:* Total capital loss on marketing; brand damage.
*Edge:* None identified in the text; vertical integration is a potential future edge but irrelevant without software.

This plan attempts to buy market share for a broken value proposition. It violates the basic rule of platform businesses: supply (apps) must precede or coincide with demand (users), not follow it years later.

DECISION: NO-GO
