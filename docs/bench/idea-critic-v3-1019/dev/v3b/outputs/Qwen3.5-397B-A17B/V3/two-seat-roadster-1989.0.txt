Steelman: A mid-size maker can profitably revive the affordable two-seat roadster by leveraging lightweight, front-engine/rear-drive simplicity to serve an underserved niche that mass manufacturers have abandoned.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal acknowledges the shrinking segment and lack of heritage but posits that "affordable" and "lightweight" create a new value proposition distinct from failed premium attempts. No internal fact contradicts the ability to build such a car.

**Risks:** High. The segment has shrunk; safety regulations increase costs; the brand lacks sports-car credibility; sales may rely entirely on a finite pool of enthusiasts. These are valid execution risks, not proof of impossibility.

**Edge:** Mass-market makers have vacated the space, leaving zero direct competition for an "affordable" entry. The company's mid-size status allows for lower overhead than premium specialists, potentially enabling a price point that re-expands the market rather than fighting for scraps. The "dead niche" assumption by competitors creates a blue-ocean opportunity if the cost base is kept low.

**Pre-mortem:** The car launches to critical acclaim but fails commercially because safety compliance bloats the price, pushing it into competition with established used sports cars. Alternatively, the "enthusiast" base is too small to sustain production volumes, and the lack of brand heritage prevents attracting casual buyers.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal explicitly identifies its own risks (shrinking market, safety costs, heritage gap). These are not hidden blockers but known variables to be managed. The "affordable" constraint is the key differentiator; if the company can meet it despite safety rules, the lack of competition offers a clear path to profitability. The risk of fading sales after the initial enthusiast wave is a lifecycle reality, not a reason to abort, provided the initial run is sized correctly. Since no factual blocker exists and the edge (vacated niche + cost advantage) is plausible, the plan survives the attack. Kill criteria must be set for price-to-weight ratio and first-year sell-through rates to mitigate the identified risks.

DECISION: GO
