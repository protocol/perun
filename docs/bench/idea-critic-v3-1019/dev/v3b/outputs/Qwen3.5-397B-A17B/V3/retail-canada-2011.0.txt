The claim is that rapid entry via 130–150 leased sites in a new country will succeed despite unproven logistics and an entrenched competitor. The steelman: leveraging existing leases accelerates market presence, capturing price-sensitive customers before competitors react, justifying the operational risk.

However, the "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the proposed pace. Opening nearly all stores within two years while simultaneously building a new cross-border supply chain and inventory system creates a single point of catastrophic failure. If the unproven inventory system falters (a near-certainty in complex cross-border rollouts), empty shelves across 150 locations will permanently brand the retailer as unreliable in a new market. The entrenched competitor can easily outlast a supply-chain-starved entrant.

The pre-mortem is clear: the expansion fails because the organization cannot learn cross-border logistics, regulation, and bilingual labeling fast enough to support 150 stores simultaneously. The cost of delay is low; the cost of a botched launch is total market exit. A cheaper, safer alternative exists: a phased pilot of 10–15 stores to validate the supply chain and inventory system before committing capital to the remaining leases. This proposal demands betting the entire international strategy on unverified operational assumptions.

The strongest attack survived? None. The proposal offers no edge other than "first mover" speed, which is illusory without functional logistics. Fear of missing out is not an edge. The lack of a proven inventory system for this specific cross-border context is a concrete blocker, not a manageable risk. The plan commits real money without a capped, time-boxed first step.

Blockers: Unproven inventory/supply chain system scaled to 150 stores immediately; no pilot data.
Risks: Regulatory delays, bilingual labeling errors, competitor price wars.
Edge: None stated beyond speed, which is negated by operational fragility.

Kill criteria: Do not proceed without a successful 6-month pilot of ≤15 stores proving the inventory system works cross-border.

DECISION: NO-GO
