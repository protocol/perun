**Steelman:** Launching two carrier-locked, data-plan-dependent social phones without apps or games leverages an acquired team's momentum to capture the youth market immediately, bypassing the delay of the main platform.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" test. The target demographic is price-sensitive, yet the required data plan costs as much as a smartphone itself. A "do nothing" or "wait" approach avoids cannibalizing the main platform launching later this year. The "better way" is to integrate social features into the upcoming main platform or release a Wi-Fi-only device, removing the prohibitive data barrier.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This contradicts the likely north star of ecosystem growth; locking users to one carrier with no app store prevents network effects. Assuming success leads to a pre-mortem where the devices sit unsold because parents refuse the high data cost for a limited-function phone, and the internal conflict causes the delayed software switch to fail again. The "one-way door" risk is brand damage among young users who associate the company with overpriced, useless hardware.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The facts provided contain a direct blocker: the target buyers are price-sensitive, but the total cost (device + data plan) exceeds that of a full smartphone with an app store. There is no edge presented; "strategic necessity" is not an edge when the product is objectively inferior to existing smartphones at a higher effective price point. The internal conflict history suggests execution risk, but the economic mismatch is the immediate failure point.

**Blockers:**
1. Target users are price-sensitive, but the required data plan makes the total cost higher than a full smartphone.
2. No app store or games removes the primary value proposition of a "smart" device for youth.

**Risks:**
Cannibalization of the main platform launching later; internal team conflict causing further delays.

**Edge:**
NONE. The proposal offers no unique asset or cost advantage over existing smartphones.

**Decision:**
The plan commits real resources to a product that is more expensive and less functional than the competition for the specific target audience. It lacks a viable path to adoption.

DECISION: NO-GO
