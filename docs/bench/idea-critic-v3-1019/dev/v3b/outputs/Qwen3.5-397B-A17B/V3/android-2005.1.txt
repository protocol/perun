**Steelman:** Acquiring the startup secures a free, open mobile OS to accelerate mobile web adoption, directly expanding the search inventory required for long-term ad revenue growth, despite high upfront costs and adoption hurdles.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists between the $50M cost and the company's cash position (implied by 2005 search ad dominance). The lack of a direct OS revenue model is not a blocker because the proposal explicitly ties success to indirect search ad yield, not OS licensing.

**Risks:** High carrier resistance, handset maker inertia, multi-year payback, and zero direct revenue. These are execution risks, not factual failures. The "open source" strategy specifically attempts to mitigate adoption friction by removing licensing costs for partners.

**Edge:** The proposer is a search-advertising monopoly with massive capital and a desperate need to prevent search stagnation as usage shifts to mobile. Competitors (handset makers) lack the incentive to prioritize open web browsing over their own walled gardens or services. Only a search player benefits enough from universal web access to subsidize the OS entirely. This asymmetry makes the bet viable for this specific buyer when it would fail for a traditional software vendor.

**Analysis:** The proposal acknowledges all major risks (adoption, revenue, timeline). The strategy (open source) aligns with the goal (web usage growth). The cost ($50M) is negligible relative to the potential loss of search relevance in a mobile-first future. The "no hardware business" constraint is actually an advantage: it assures partners the OS vendor is not a competitor seeking to sell devices, lowering political resistance compared to offers from existing handset makers.

**Decision Logic:** The claim survives the pre-mortem. The primary failure mode (nobody adopts it) is addressed by the open-source leverage and the unique non-competitive stance of the buyer. The financial risk is capped at $50M plus team retention, while the downside of missing the mobile shift is existential. The edge (search revenue alignment) is unique and sufficient.

DECISION: GO
