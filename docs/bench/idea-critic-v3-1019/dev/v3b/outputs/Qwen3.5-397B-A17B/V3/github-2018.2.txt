**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant code-hosting platform secures the industry's central nervous system, preventing a rival from locking it down and forcing the buyer's integration into the open-source workflow despite cultural friction.

**Attack:** The target is loss-making, leaderless, and hated by its users who actively prefer a free alternative. Paying $7.5 billion in stock for a distressed asset with high defection risk and antitrust scrutiny appears financially reckless and culturally suicidal. The "independent" promise may not survive internal pressure.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Save $7.5B; risk a competitor buying the target or the target collapsing, leaving a vacuum.
2.  **Build/Buy Smaller:** Impossible to replicate the network effect; smaller acquisitions lack the critical mass.
3.  **Acquire:** High cost and integration risk, but captures the definitive platform.

**Fit:** Aligns with the buyer's pivot from hostility to embrace; essential to prove sincerity and control the pipeline.

**Pre-mortem:** Developers flee to the free competitor immediately upon announcement; regulators block the deal; the target remains a money pit with no synergy realized.

**Evidence:** Dominance is implied; no other platform holds comparable gravity. The price is high but strategic.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of a CEO and losses actually lower the barrier to acquisition compared to a profitable, entrenched rival. The community distrust is a management challenge, not a deal-breaker if autonomy is strictly maintained.

**Risks:** Developer defection, regulatory blocks, cultural clash. These are manageable via strict operational independence and long-term monetization patience.

**Edge:** Only this buyer has the capital to absorb the losses and the strategic desperation to prove its open-source conversion. The network effect is an unassailable moat that justifies the premium.

**Decision:** The strategic necessity of owning the primary open-source hub outweighs the immediate financial negatives and cultural risks. The "independent" operating model is the critical kill criterion for success.

DECISION: GO
