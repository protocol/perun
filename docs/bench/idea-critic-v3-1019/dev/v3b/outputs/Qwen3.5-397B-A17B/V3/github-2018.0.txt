**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant code-hosting platform secures the critical infrastructure of modern software development, preventing a competitor from capturing it and accelerating the buyer's transformation from open-source hostile to open-source leader.

**Attack:** The target is unprofitable, leaderless, and culturally toxic toward the buyer. Paying $7.5B (a massive premium) for a loss-making entity risks immediate developer defection to free alternatives, regulatory blockage, and total value destruction if the community revolts.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid financial risk but cede the developer ecosystem to rivals; the buyer remains an outsider.
2.  **Build/Buy Smaller:** Cheaper, but fails to capture the network effect of the dominant player; too slow.
3.  **Acquire (Proposed):** High cost and cultural friction, but the only path to instant centrality in the developer workflow.

**Fit:** Aligns with the strategic pivot to embrace open source; doing nothing contradicts the new vision.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the buyer imposes restrictive licensing or monetization too quickly, triggering a mass exodus to GitLab or Bitbucket. The "one-way door" risk is high; once the brand is tainted, trust is hard to regain. However, the cost of delay is losing the platform war entirely.

**Evidence & Edge:** The target's dominance is the edge; no other asset offers this gravity. The buyer's resources can solve the leadership vacuum and absorb losses temporarily. The "free competitor" lacks the same network density. Regulatory scrutiny is a risk, not a blocker, given the horizontal nature of the tools.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact provided proves the deal cannot close or that defection is inevitable if managed correctly.
**Risks:** Cultural integration, developer churn, antitrust delays. These are mitigated by running the target independently and maintaining free tiers.
**Edge:** Unique opportunity to own the primary funnel for global developer talent and tooling adoption.

**Decision:** The strategic necessity of owning the primary developer platform outweighs the financial and cultural risks, provided the target runs autonomously. The cost of missing this window exceeds the acquisition premium.
