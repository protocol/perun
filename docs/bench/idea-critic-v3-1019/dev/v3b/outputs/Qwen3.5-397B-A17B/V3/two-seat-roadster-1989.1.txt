**Steelman:** A mid-size maker can profitably revive the affordable two-seat roadster by leveraging lightweight, simple engineering to serve an underserved niche of enthusiasts ignored by mass-market trends.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces immediate structural blockers. First, the "affordable" claim conflicts with the reality of 1989 safety regulations; complying with crash and rollover standards for a low-volume convertible typically destroys the cost advantage required for an "affordable" price point. Second, the market data indicates a shrinking segment; entering a declining niche without a unique technological edge or heritage brand premium is a classic value trap. The risk that sales will fade after early enthusiasts buy is not just a risk but a near-certain trajectory given the limited total addressable market.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids sinking R&D into a shrinking, regulation-heavy segment.
2.  **Derivative Approach:** Modify an existing chassis from the current lineup rather than a dedicated lightweight platform, though this compromises the "pure roadster" feel.
3.  **Wait/License:** Wait for regulatory clarity or license a design from an existing specialist to test waters without full capital commitment.

**Fit & Edge:**
The proposal lacks a defined "edge." The company has no sports-car heritage in this class, and "inspired by" is not a product advantage. Without a proprietary cost-breaking manufacturing method or a confirmed waiting list of buyers, the plan relies on hope rather than asset leverage. The "lightweight" goal is a desired outcome, not a proven capability that guarantees compliance within budget.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Cost vs. Regulation:** No evidence provided that an "affordable" price is mathematically possible under 1989 safety rules for a low-volume convertible.
2.  **Market Trajectory:** Entering a shrinking segment without a distinct competitive moat contradicts basic portfolio strategy.

**Verdict:**
The proposal fails the blocker test. It assumes a cost structure that likely cannot exist given the regulatory environment and targets a shrinking market without a defensible unique selling proposition. The risks are not merely "possible" but structurally inherent to the plan as stated.

DECISION: NO-GO
