**Steelman:** Acquiring a dormant, high-recognition space-fantasy franchise for $4B enables a unified content pipeline (films, parks, products) that organic development cannot match in speed or cultural impact, despite creative transition risks.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker is the $4B price tag against mixed recent performance and the loss of the founding creative voice. However, the "do nothing" alternative yields zero synergy; building a new franchise from scratch carries higher failure risk and slower ROI. The "cheaper way" (licensing) is impossible as the target is privately held and the founder is retiring, forcing a binary buy-or-lose scenario.

The risk of fan rejection is real but manageable; the franchise's core mythology remains intact even if recent entries faltered. The merchandise licensing issue limits immediate upside but does not prevent long-term reintegration upon contract expiry. The critical edge is the buyer's unique capacity to monetize across theme parks and consumer products immediately, subsidizing film production risks that a standalone studio could not bear. The $4B cost is high relative to film output alone but reasonable when amortized across a multi-decade ecosystem strategy.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the new trilogy alienates the core fanbase, rendering the brand toxic for parks. This is mitigated by strict adherence to established lore rather than reinvention.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** None. The price is steep but justified by the exclusive asset availability and cross-segment leverage.
*   **Risks:** Creative execution, fan backlash, high upfront capital. These are execution risks, not structural flaws.
*   **Edge:** Only this specific buyer can unlock the full value of the IP across parks and products immediately, creating a moat competitors cannot replicate.

The proposal represents a two-way door regarding future film slates (films can be paused), but a one-way door on asset acquisition (once sold, it is gone). Given the strategic necessity of fresh IP for the parks and the unavailability of comparable assets, the potential upside of reviving a beloved universe outweighs the financial premium and creative risks.

DECISION: GO
