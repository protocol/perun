**Steelman:** Separating the brands isolates the declining DVD unit's operational constraints, allowing the streaming business to pivot rapidly without legacy drag, while forcing distinct P&L accountability.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the immediate context: a 60% price hike occurred just two months prior. Splitting the billing and accounts now compounds this friction, appearing not as strategic clarity but as a deceptive unbundling to justify the earlier hike. The "loss of cross-service recommendations" is not a minor risk; it actively degrades the value proposition for the hybrid user, accelerating churn. Furthermore, forcing existing customers to migrate to a new brand, create new accounts, and manage separate queues creates a massive service disruption that will be interpreted as hostility. The rationale that "streaming should be free to pursue its own strategy" does not require a consumer-facing brand split; internal management separation achieves this without alienating the user base. The "do nothing" alternative (internal separation only) captures the management benefits without the customer experience penalty.

**Edge:** The only potential edge is forcing a clean break to accelerate DVD sunset. However, the proposal lacks a mechanism to mitigate the perceived betrayal of existing dual-subscribers. Without a migration concierge or a grandfathered unified billing option, this executes as a penalty on loyal users.

**Blockers:**
1. **Timing:** Executing a complex account split immediately following a 60% price increase guarantees a perception of bad faith, likely triggering mass cancellation rather than segment acceptance.
2. **Value Destruction:** Severing data sharing removes the primary justification for a combined subscription, making the high price indefensible.

**Risks:** Catastrophic churn, brand reputation collapse, regulatory scrutiny regarding deceptive billing practices.

**Decision:** The plan fails the "fit" test against current customer sentiment and creates a self-inflicted wound during a fragile pricing transition. The strategic goal (management separation) can be achieved internally without the consumer-facing fracture.

DECISION: NO-GO
