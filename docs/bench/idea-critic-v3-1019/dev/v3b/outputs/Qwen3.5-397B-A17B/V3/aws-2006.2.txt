**Steelman:** Leveraging existing, spiky internal infrastructure to sell pay-per-use computing could transform a cost center into a high-margin revenue stream, utilizing idle capacity during non-peak retail periods.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "Edge" test. The retailer possesses zero experience running a developer platform, while incumbents dominate with established trust and tooling. The "spiky" infrastructure designed for retail workloads is architecturally misaligned with the steady, multi-tenant reliability required by external enterprise developers. 

The market is explicitly "unproven," yet the capital requirements are massive. Investors are already questioning heavy spending; adding a speculative, distinct business line with high reliability risks invites severe capital punishment. The "do nothing" alternative (optimizing retail margins) offers certain value, whereas this plan bets the company's financial stability on an unverified hypothesis with no competitive moat. The risk of distraction from the core thin-margin retail business is catastrophic if the new venture fails to gain traction quickly.

There is no named asset that guarantees victory here. "Having servers" is not an edge when the product is "trust and ecosystem," which the retailer lacks. The pre-mortem is clear: the platform suffers outages during retail peaks (conflicting priorities), developers flee due to lack of features, and the capital burn accelerates investor exodus.

**Blockers:** 
1. No proven demand for hourly server rentals (market unproven).
2. Zero internal capability for developer platform operations.
3. Direct conflict with investor sentiment regarding capital spend.

**Risks:** Margin dilution, core business distraction, reliability failures.

**Edge:** NONE. Internal infrastructure usage is a cost advantage only if the product works; it is not a differentiator against established vendors.

**Conclusion:** The proposal attempts to enter a capital-intensive, trust-based market without the necessary experience, proof of demand, or investor support. It violates the principle of betting on known strengths. The potential for catastrophic distraction and capital loss outweighs the theoretical upside of utilizing idle capacity.

DECISION: NO-GO
