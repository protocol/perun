**Steelman:** Separating the brands isolates the declining DVD asset, allowing the streaming business to pivot without legacy constraints or brand drag.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the immediate context: a 60% price hike occurred just two months prior. Executing a split now—forcing separate logins, bills, and queues—validates customer fears that the company is intentionally devaluing the DVD service to kill it or extract maximum cash before abandonment. The "confusion" risk is understated; this feels like a bait-and-switch. Customers perceive this not as strategic clarity, but as punishment for wanting both services.

**Better Way:** Maintain a single account and brand umbrella while internally separating management teams. Absorb the friction of combined billing temporarily to stabilize trust after the price shock. If separation is inevitable, delay it until the price hike backlash subsides or offer a grandfathered "combined" tier to retain high-value users.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** The strategy assumes streaming's growth outweighs DVD churn. However, splitting the data (ratings/recommendations) destroys the unique value proposition of a unified library. In a pre-mortem, the split launches, social media erupts over the "two login" hassle on top of the price hike, churn spikes across both services, and the stock collapses because the market reads the move as desperation, not focus.

**Blockers:** The timing relative to the price increase is a factual blocker. Proceeding now confirms negative customer hypotheses about company intent.
**Risks:** Mass churn, brand reputation destruction, loss of cross-selling data.
**Edge:** None identified that outweighs the timing disaster.

**Decision:** The plan fails the "do no harm" test in the current climate. The strategic logic of separation may be sound in a vacuum, but executing it immediately after a massive price increase is catastrophic. The proposal lacks a mitigation strategy for the compounded customer anger. It must be revised to decouple the branding split from the pricing event or delayed significantly.

DECISION: NO-GO
