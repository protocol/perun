**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant video platform eliminates the primary competitor, secures the category leader despite current losses, and leverages the buyer's infrastructure to solve bandwidth and monetization challenges that the target cannot survive alone.

**Blockers:** NONE. The $1.65B price is steep for a revenue-less entity, but in 2006, no other asset commands this level of user engagement or cultural relevance. The copyright litigation risk is existential but manageable for a cash-rich acquirer who can negotiate licenses or settle, whereas the target would likely be shut down by courts if independent. The "unproven ad model" is a solvable engineering problem for the buyer, not a reason to abandon the audience.

**Risks:** Massive integration failure; copyright lawsuits costing billions; bandwidth costs continuing to outpace ad revenue; cultural clash between a small startup and a large corporation. These are severe but do not constitute immediate factual impossibility.

**Edge:** Only this specific buyer has the search distribution, existing ad sales machinery, and server infrastructure to turn the traffic into profit quickly. No other potential acquirer possesses the balance sheet to absorb the legal liability and the technical scale to fix the bandwidth bleed simultaneously. Doing nothing cedes the future of online video to a rival or leaves the market fragmented.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal fails if integration stalls for 12 months, allowing a new competitor to emerge, or if media companies secure an injunction that removes all premium content permanently. However, the cost of delay is losing the network effect forever; a competitor buying the target would be catastrophic.

**Kill Criteria:** If user growth stalls for two consecutive quarters post-acquisition, or if legal judgments exceed $500M within the first 18 months, initiate divestiture or shutdown of the brand. Leading metric: Daily active users and ad revenue per thousand impressions (RPM) measured quarterly.

The proposal survives the attack because the alternative (losing the category) is worse than the financial risk. The buyer is the only entity capable of solving the target's fatal flaws.

DECISION: GO
