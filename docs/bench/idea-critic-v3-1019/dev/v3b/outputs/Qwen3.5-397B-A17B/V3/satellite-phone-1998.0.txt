**Steelman:** A global, gap-free mobile network captures high-value travelers ignored by fragmented, ground-only cellular providers, justifying premium pricing to service heavy debt.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" and "market fit" tests. Ground cellular is expanding rapidly, offering significantly lower infrastructure costs and better indoor penetration without the latency of low-earth orbit. The $3,000 handset price and high per-minute rates restrict the addressable market to a tiny fraction of business travelers, making the subscriber growth required to service $5 billion in debt mathematically improbable. The "bulkiness" and indoor failure modes are technical blockers that directly contradict the needs of the target demographic (seamless connectivity).

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing/Wait:** Let ground networks mature; costs drop, coverage improves naturally.
2.  **Niche B2B/Government:** Target specific industries (shipping, aviation, defense) rather than general consumer travelers, accepting lower volume but higher reliability needs.
3.  **Roaming Aggregator:** Build software/services atop existing fragmented networks instead of owning the physics layer.

**Pre-mortem:** The project launches, subscribers miss targets by 80% due to price and device usability. Debt payments consume cash flow within 18 months. The company enters bankruptcy, selling assets for pennies on the dollar. This is a one-way door with catastrophic financial downside.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unit Economics:** The cost structure ($5B capex + debt service) requires a market size that the $3,000 price point explicitly excludes.
*   **Technical Fit:** Indoor failure renders the product useless for a core use case (office/travel hub connectivity).
*   **Competitive Trajectory:** Ground networks are solving the coverage gap cheaper and faster than this proposal can scale.

**Edge:** None identified. Being first to global coverage is not an edge if the product is unusable indoors and unaffordable for the mass market required to sustain the debt.

**Kill Criteria:** If subscriber acquisition costs exceed $2,000 per user or if indoor success rates remain below 90% after pilot, stop immediately.

The risk of immediate financial collapse due to insufficient market adoption at the proposed price point is a confirmed blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
