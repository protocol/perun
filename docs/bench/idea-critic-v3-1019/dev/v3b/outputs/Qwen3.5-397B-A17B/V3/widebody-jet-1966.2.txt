**Steelman:** Building a massively larger, wide-body jet with a convertible freighter design captures emerging high-volume travel demand and secures a dominant market position before competitors or supersonic alternatives mature.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces existential financial risk; the development and factory costs exceed all prior company history, threatening bankruptcy if sales stall. Relying on a single customer and an unproven engine supplier compounds this fragility. Furthermore, the industry expectation that supersonic travel will render subsonic giants obsolete poses a strategic timing threat.

However, the "do nothing" option carries greater long-term danger. If air travel grows as projected, current fleets will lack the capacity to meet demand efficiently, ceding the market to any rival who dares to build big. The supersonic threat is speculative and likely limited to niche luxury routes due to sonic boom restrictions over land, whereas the economic imperative for high-capacity subsonic travel is immediate.

The raised cockpit design is a critical differentiator, ensuring the asset retains value as a freighter even if passenger dynamics shift, directly addressing the obsolescence risk. While the engine is unproven, high-bypass technology offers the only viable path to the required fuel efficiency and noise reduction for such a large airframe. The single order provides the necessary anchor to justify the gamble, with the expectation that other carriers will follow once the prototype proves viable.

**Blockers:** None. The financial scale is terrifying but not a factual impossibility; it is a manageable execution challenge given the market trajectory.
**Risks:** Cash flow exhaustion, engine delays, supersonic disruption. These are mitigated by the freighter conversion capability and the sheer magnitude of the projected demand growth.
**Edge:** First-mover advantage in the high-capacity segment and a dual-use design that hedges against passenger market volatility.

The potential reward of defining the next era of aviation outweighs the catastrophic but avoidable risk of stagnation.

DECISION: GO
