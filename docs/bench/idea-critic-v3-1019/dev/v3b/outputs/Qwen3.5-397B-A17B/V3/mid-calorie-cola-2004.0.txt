**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men avoiding both full-sugar guilt and diet stigma, leveraging a premium price to signal innovation before the low-carb trend fades.

**Attack & Options:**
The core failure mode is **positioning ambiguity**. If the product satisfies neither the taste seeker (full sugar) nor the calorie counter (diet), it becomes shelf dead weight. The proposal admits users wanting fewer calories already buy diet; thus, the "half-sugar" benefit is weak unless taste is significantly superior to diet cola. However, halving sugar often compromises the mouthfeel and preservation profile of cola, risking a "flat" taste that alienates the target young male demographic who prioritize flavor over marginal carb counts.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
Assuming the low-carb trend is a fad (a stated risk), launching a premium product dependent on this specific macro-nutrient anxiety is dangerous. If the trend collapses in 12 months, the premium pricing becomes unjustifiable. The "do nothing" option (waiting to see if the rival fails or the trend solidifies) preserves capital. The rival's similar launch suggests a crowded niche where differentiation is minimal.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
No evidence is provided that young men *want* a mid-calorie option specifically; they may prefer zero-calorie if taste allows, or full-sugar if they aren't dieting. The proposal relies on the assumption that "half-sugar" is a desirable attribute rather than a compromise. The premium price acts as a barrier to trial for a product with an unproven value proposition.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blocker:** The value proposition is contradictory. It targets a gap (mid-calorie) that may not exist if the primary driver is either taste (full sugar) or health (diet/zero). Without proof that "half-sugar" tastes better than diet cola to young men, the product fails its primary utility test.
*   **Risk:** High probability of being a "zombie" product—too sweet for dieters, not sweet enough for traditionalists.
*   **Edge:** None identified. Being second to a rival in a potentially fad-driven niche with a compromised formula is not a winning edge.

The plan fails the blocker test: the core assumption that a mid-calorie segment exists and prefers this specific formulation over existing options is unsupported and contradicted by the risk that it satisfies neither group.

DECISION: NO-GO
