**Steelman:** Combining German engineering prestige with US mass-market profitability creates a dominant global entity through platform synergies and balanced governance.

**Attack:** The "merger of equals" structure is likely a facade masking a cultural collision that will paralyze decision-making. The German firm's premium positioning relies on exclusivity and cost control, while the US partner's current profits depend entirely on a cyclical light-truck boom that may burst. Integrating rigid German labor structures with flexible US management styles historically leads to exodus of talent and brand dilution. The proposal assumes the truck boom is permanent and cultures are compatible; both are unverified high-risk assumptions.

**Better Way:** Maintain independence. The German firm can enter the US mass market via a dedicated subsidiary or joint venture without inheriting the US partner's cyclical exposure or cultural baggage. The US firm should capitalize on the boom while it lasts rather than risking it on complex integration.

**Kill Criteria:** If the light-truck market contracts by >15% within 24 months, the combined entity's profitability collapses. If key engineering leadership from either side departs within year one, the "synergy" thesis fails.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** None definitively proven yet, but the cultural mismatch is a near-certain operational failure point given the "equals" governance which prevents decisive leadership during conflict.
*   **Risks:** High. Cyclical dependency, brand dilution, integration paralysis.
*   **Edge:** None. The proposed "scale" is offset by the complexity of managing two opposing operational models. The "complementary" nature is theoretical, not proven.

The proposal relies on the continuity of a cyclical boom and the success of a notoriously difficult "merger of equals" between disparate national cultures. The risks of value destruction through integration failure and market cycle reversal outweigh the theoretical benefits of scale. The lack of a clear dominant leadership structure to resolve inevitable cultural conflicts makes this a high-probability failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
