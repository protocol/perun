Steelman: Launching a dedicated, ultra-low-cost factory enables mass motorization for families currently on scooters, creating a new market segment.

**Blockers:** NONE. The farmer protests are a risk to timeline, not a factual impossibility of the business model. Cost-cutting concerns are inherent to the price point but do not preclude launch if minimum safety standards are met.

**Risks:** High. Land acquisition delays could stall production. The "cheapest" label may stigmatize the brand, reducing aspirational value. Safety perceptions could deter risk-averse families. Used cars and scooters offer proven, lower-cost alternatives with established reliability.

**Edge:** First-mover advantage in a massive, underserved demographic (scooter families). A dedicated high-volume factory allows unit economics impossible for competitors adapting existing platforms. The price point ($2,500) is a psychological and financial threshold that unlocks credit access for millions.

**Analysis:**
The proposal beats "do nothing" because the status quo leaves the target market on unsafe scooters. Alternatives like used cars lack the warranty and specific design for four-seat family safety this vehicle offers. While the "cheapest" marketing carries reputational risk, it also provides undeniable clarity and viral potential. The land protests are a local execution hurdle, not a strategic flaw; they require management, not project cancellation.

The core assumption—that families will trade scooters for a car at this price—is supported by the sheer volume of two-wheeler ownership in India. The risk of safety doubts is mitigated by the fact that scooters are significantly less safe; even a basic car is an upgrade. The "cheapest" tag can be reframed as "most accessible."

Delaying for perfect conditions or higher safety margins destroys the unique value proposition: affordability. The cost of delay allows competitors to enter or the economic window to close. This is a two-way door; if safety scandals emerge, production can halt, but missing the launch window forfeits the segment entirely.

The edge (dedicated volume economics) outweighs the risks (protests, perception), provided the launch proceeds with aggressive crisis management for land issues and transparent safety communication.

DECISION: GO
