**Steelman:** Secure a generational talent before the market prices him, using controversy and exclusivity to create a cultural phenomenon that transcends sports performance, effectively manufacturing demand where none currently exists.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails standard risk calculus: the subject is unproven, the financial exposure is catastrophic relative to the budget, and the product strategy relies on regulatory violation. Conventional wisdom dictates waiting for professional validation. However, the "unproven" status is precisely the arbitrage opportunity; waiting guarantees competitor bidding wars. The injury risk is real but unhedgeable in any talent acquisition. The uniform violation is not a bug but a feature: fines generate news cycles that money cannot buy, framing the brand as rebellious and player-centric.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing/Status Quo:** Continue losing share to established basketball brands. Safe, but guarantees irrelevance in the category.
2.  **Sign Established Star:** Proven performance, lower injury uncertainty. Cost: prohibitive (likely higher than this rookie's deal) with less creative control and no "origin story" narrative.
3.  **Team Endorsement:** Lower risk, diluted impact. Fails to create a standalone cultural icon.

**Fit & Edge:**
The company dominates running but lacks basketball cultural cache. The edge is speed of conviction: acting before the rookie plays a single minute secures exclusivity and defines the brand's attitude immediately. The "risk" of fines becomes the marketing engine. The lack of professional footage forces the narrative to focus entirely on the athlete's persona and the shoe's design, creating mystery.

**Kill Criteria & Verdict:**
Blockers: None. The facts presented (rookie status, high cost, rule-breaking) are the very mechanisms of the strategy, not flaws.
Risks: Injury, league ban. These are managed by the five-year term (locking value early) and the high-reward potential of the first year alone.
Edge: First-mover advantage on a unique cultural archetype; turning regulatory friction into free media.

This is a one-way door that requires total commitment. Half-measures (waiting for a few games, negotiating safer terms) destroy the premise. The proposal demands an all-in bet on intuition over data. Given the goal to stop losing ground, the only path to leadership is a discontinuous jump.

DECISION: GO
