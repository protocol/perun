**Steelman:** Paying $19B eliminates an existential mobile threat and secures hundreds of millions of active users who reject the buyer's current model, buying immediate relevance over building it slowly.

**Attack:** The price is 12x the prior valuation with zero revenue and no monetization path. The target explicitly refuses advertising, the buyer's core engine. Paying this premium for a "fee-only" app with 50 employees creates massive dilution and integration risk if founders leave or regulators block data merging.

**Alternatives:**
1. *Do Nothing:* Risk continued erosion of the core user base as messaging shifts to the target.
2. *Build/Buy Small:* Attempt to clone features or buy a smaller competitor; likely too slow given the target's network effects.
3. *Staged Deal:* Offer a lower upfront cash component with heavy earn-outs tied to retention and revenue milestones to mitigate the valuation gap.

**Fit:** Aligns with the "mobile-first" survival imperative but contradicts fiscal discipline and proven unit economics.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, regulators impose strict data silos preventing cross-app monetization, founders depart within 18 months, and the $1 fee model caps revenue growth. The buyer is left with an expensive, isolated app and angry shareholders.

**Blockers:** None definitively proven in the text; the price is extreme but not mathematically impossible if the strategic value of stopping displacement is infinite.
**Risks:** Massive overpayment, regulatory breakup, cultural clash, inability to monetize without violating the target's ethos.
**Edge:** Only the buyer has the cash reserves to pay this premium immediately to stop a competitor from being acquired by a rival or going public independently.

**Decision Logic:** This is a one-way door with high delay cost (losing the mobile generation). While the financials are absurd, the strategic necessity of owning the primary mobile communication layer for hundreds of millions of users outweighs the lack of current revenue. The "no-go" alternative is potential irrelevance. The deal must proceed but requires immediate, aggressive kill criteria regarding user retention and a capped integration timeline.

DECISION: GO
