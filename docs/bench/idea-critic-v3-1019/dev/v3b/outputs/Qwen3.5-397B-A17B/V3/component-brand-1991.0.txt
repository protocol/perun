**Steelman:** Transform an invisible component into a consumer demand driver by subsidizing OEM advertising, forcing buyers to request the brand by name and locking out clones.

**Attack:**
1.  **Channel Conflict:** Computer makers (the direct customers) may resent ceding brand control or view the subsidy as manipulative, potentially retaliating by favoring competitors.
2.  **Budget Cannibalization:** Diverting a large share of the marketing budget to unproven consumer ads risks starving proven B2B sales channels. If consumer demand does not translate to immediate sales, the company faces a revenue cliff with no fallback.
3.  **Measurement Lag:** Unlike B2B, consumer sentiment is hard to link directly to chip orders. The "unprecedented" nature means there are no benchmarks for ROI, making it impossible to know if the program is failing until quarters later.
4.  **Clone Resilience:** Clone makers using similar part numbers may simply ignore the campaign or undercut prices further, rendering the brand premium ineffective if performance gaps are negligible.

**Better Way:** Instead of a blanket subsidy, run a limited pilot with one major partner to validate consumer pull before committing the full budget. Alternatively, invest in technical certification seals that OEMs can voluntarily display, reducing friction and resentment.

**Kill Criteria:** If pilot partners do not see a measurable increase in premium-priced unit sales within two quarters, or if more than 20% of targeted OEMs refuse participation due to brand conflict, the program must terminate.

**Edge:** First-mover advantage in component branding creates a psychological moat; once consumers associate "quality" with a specific logo, clones become perceived as risky regardless of actual specs. The unprecedented nature is the opportunity, not just the risk.

**Verdict:** The risk of channel conflict is real but manageable through contract incentives rather than mandates. The budget risk is mitigated by the fact that current differentiation is zero, leading to commoditization. Doing nothing guarantees margin erosion. The potential to own the consumer mindshare outweighs the execution risks, provided strict kill criteria are enforced immediately upon launch.

DECISION: GO
