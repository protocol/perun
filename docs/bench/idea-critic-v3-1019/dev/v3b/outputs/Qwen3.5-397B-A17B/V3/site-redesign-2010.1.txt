**Steelman:** A full rewrite with a publisher-focused algorithm and immediate migration is the only way to reverse traffic decline and compete with a growing rival before it is too late.

**Attack:** This proposal fails the blocker test on three counts. First, it contradicts verified community values: the core users prize the existing voting mechanics. Changing this to favor publishers alienates the loyal base that generates the content, likely accelerating traffic loss rather than fixing it. Second, the "all-at-once" migration creates a one-way door with no rollback; if the new algorithm fails, the site cannot revert without losing data or trust during the transition. Third, the engineering risk of a ground-up rewrite delays any market response while the rival grows.

**Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option preserves the community but accepts slow decline. A superior alternative is a staged rollout: keep the current engine for existing users while A/B testing the new publisher-focused surfacing on a small segment. This validates the algorithm's impact on traffic and community sentiment without risking the entire platform. It is cheaper, reversible, and faster to market than a full rewrite.

**Edge:** The proposal claims urgency due to a rival, but urgency demands speed and safety, not a high-risk "big bang" launch. The stated facts show the community is the asset; the plan jeopardizes that asset to chase an unproven publisher angle.

**Blockers:** 
1. The plan explicitly changes mechanics the core community values, creating a high probability of user exodus.
2. The "move all users at once" constraint eliminates the ability to rollback, violating basic risk management for a core product change.

**Risks:** Total community collapse; engineering delay allowing rival to dominate; unproven assumption that publisher content drives more value than community curation.

**Edge:** None identified. The plan relies on fear of the rival rather than a demonstrated advantage in the new approach.

**Decision:** The combination of alienating the core user base and removing the safety net of a rollback constitutes a fatal flaw. The plan must be revised to include staged testing and community preservation before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
