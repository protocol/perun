**Steelman:** Acquire the target to capture its sticky, high-value business segment and push-to-talk dominance, accepting short-term integration friction to secure long-term revenue diversity.

**Attack:** The core flaw is the $36 billion price tag relative to the target's "limited data" capability in a market rapidly shifting toward mobile data (2005 context). The proposal admits the technologies are "incompatible," requiring the acquirer to run a legacy network indefinitely. This creates a permanent dual-operating cost structure that erodes the margin gains from the new customers. The "large goodwill" implies paying a massive premium for a technology asset that is technically obsolete and must be maintained rather than leveraged. The incompatibility prevents the primary synergy usually driving such mergers: network consolidation cost savings. Instead, costs increase while the acquired revenue base faces existential threat from emerging data-centric competitors.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid $36B debt and operational complexity; compete for business users via organic product development.
2.  **Partner/Resell:** License the push-to-talk service without acquiring the incompatible network infrastructure.
3.  **The Proposal:** High cost, high technical debt, no immediate synergy.

**Fit:** If the north star is "network efficiency" or "data growth," this fails. It only fits a "revenue diversification" goal, but at a price that likely destroys shareholder value given the technical drag.

**Pre-mortem:** Five years post-merger, the legacy network drains cash, the business customers churn to superior data-enabled devices, and the acquirer is left with $36B in debt and two fractured networks.

**Blockers:** The "incompatible technologies" combined with "limited data" in 2005 is a structural blocker to synergy. The cost to integrate exceeds the value of the limited customer base when the technology is a dead end. There is no clear path to migrate these users to the modern network without losing the very feature (push-to-talk on that specific hardware) they pay for.

**Edge:** None stated. Acquiring a legacy tech stack at a premium is not an edge; it is a liability.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the cost-benefit analysis due to permanent operational drag and lack of technical synergy.

DECISION: NO-GO
