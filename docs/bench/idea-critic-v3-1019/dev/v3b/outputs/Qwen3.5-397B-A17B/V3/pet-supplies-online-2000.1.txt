**Steelman:** Aggressively scale market share and infrastructure via an IPO to dominate the pet supply sector before competitors can react.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Negative Unit Economics:** Selling products below cost while shipping heavy, low-margin items creates a scenario where every additional sale increases total losses. No amount of volume fixes a negative gross margin; it only accelerates cash burn.
2.  **Capital Market Reality:** The proposal assumes capital markets will remain open to fund infinite losses. Given the stated risks of market closure and heavy competitor spending, relying on future equity raises to cover operational deficits is a fatal assumption.
3.  **Acquisition Timing:** Buying the largest competitor immediately after an IPO, while simultaneously burning cash on TV ads and below-cost pricing, requires unprecedented capital efficiency that contradicts the negative margin structure.

**Risks:**
*   **Cash Insolvency:** The combination of below-cost pricing, expensive national TV spots (especially major sports broadcasts), and warehouse construction creates an immediate liquidity crisis if the IPO underperforms or markets tighten.
*   **Competitor Response:** Competitors are already spending heavily; a price war initiated by a company with negative margins will drain reserves faster than rivals with better unit economics.
*   **Operational Bloat:** Building more warehouses before proving a profitable delivery model locks in high fixed costs.

**Edge:**
None identified. The proposal relies on "growth at all costs" without a mechanism to ever achieve profitability. Speed and scale are not advantages when the core transaction destroys value.

**Decision Logic:**
The plan fails the fundamental test of business viability: it loses money on every unit sold. The strategy depends entirely on continuous access to public capital markets to subsidize operations, ignoring the explicit risk that these markets could close. The "edge" of being first or biggest is irrelevant if the company goes bankrupt before achieving dominance. The pre-mortem is clear: the company runs out of cash within 12-18 months as investors refuse to fund deepening losses. There is no kill criterion that saves this; the foundational math is broken.

DECISION: NO-GO
