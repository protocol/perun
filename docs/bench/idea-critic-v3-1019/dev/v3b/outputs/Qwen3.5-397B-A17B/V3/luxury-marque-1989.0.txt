**Steelman:** A dedicated luxury brand with a distinct dealer network and an all-new flagship, priced below European incumbents, can capture value without diluting the parent's economy reputation.

**Analysis:**
The proposal correctly identifies that the parent brand's "economy" equity is a blocker, not an asset, for luxury sales; a separate brand is mandatory. The "separate dealer network" cost is not a bug but a feature: luxury buyers demand a premium purchase experience incompatible with high-volume economy lots. While European incumbents hold prestige, they are vulnerable on price and perceived value. Entering with a flagship engineered to match their quality but sold at a discount creates an immediate "value luxury" wedge.

**Risks & Rebuttals:**
*   **Brand Perception:** Mitigated entirely by the separate brand identity.
*   **Thin Line-up:** A single, perfect flagship is a stronger launch statement than a diluted range of mediocre models; it focuses engineering resources and marketing clarity.
*   **Sunk Costs:** Development spend is irrelevant to the forward-looking decision; the metric is future ROI.
*   **Incumbent Prestige:** Prestige is fragile when confronted with objectively superior value. The "cheap" tag can be inverted to "smart luxury."

**Kill Criteria Check:**
No factual blocker exists in the text. The risks cited are standard market entry challenges, not fatal flaws. The strategy addresses the core conflict (brand dilution) via structural separation. The pricing strategy exploits a clear gap in the incumbent's defense. The "thin line-up" is a strategic focus, not a failure mode, for a debut.

**Edge:**
The parent company's core competency in "reliable, economical" manufacturing suggests a supply chain capable of producing high-quality components at lower costs than European rivals, enabling the aggressive pricing while maintaining margins. This cost advantage is the specific edge required to win.

**Verdict:**
The plan survives the pre-mortem. The separation of brand and channel solves the primary adoption barrier. The single-model focus ensures quality parity. The price undercutting is the catalyst.

DECISION: GO
