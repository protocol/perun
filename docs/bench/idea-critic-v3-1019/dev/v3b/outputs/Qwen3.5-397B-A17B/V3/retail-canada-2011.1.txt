**Steelman:** Rapidly acquiring 130–150 sites leverages immediate scale to preempt the entrenched competitor, betting that speed outweighs the complexity of unproven logistics and bilingual compliance.

**Attack:** The proposal conflates "having leases" with "being operational." Opening nearly all stores within two years requires a supply chain and inventory system that are explicitly "new" and "not yet proven." In retail, inventory accuracy and shelf availability are the primary drivers of retention; failing these in a new regulatory environment (bilingual labeling) while simultaneously scaling logistics creates a single point of catastrophic failure. The "larger, entrenched competitor" can easily exploit stockouts or compliance fines to crush a newcomer before it stabilizes. The pace assumes execution perfection in an area where the retailer has zero prior experience (cross-border).

**Alternatives:** A "beachhead" strategy (5–10 stores) would validate the supply chain and regulatory compliance with capped downside. "Do nothing" preserves capital until a partner or smaller pilot de-risks the model. The current plan beats "do nothing" only if speed is the sole determinant of success, which is unlikely given the operational dependencies.

**Pre-mortem:** Twelve months in, 40% of stores are understocked due to customs delays; the new inventory system crashes under multi-currency/multi-language load; fines accumulate for labeling errors. The brand's "loyal domestic following" does not transfer to a foreign market experiencing poor execution. Cash burn from rent on underperforming stores forces a distress sale.

**Blockers:** The core claim relies on a "new supply chain" and "new inventory system" functioning at full scale immediately. There is no evidence these systems work, let alone in a cross-border context. This is a factual gap that invalidates the timeline.

**Risks:** Regulatory non-compliance, inventory collapse, brand damage in a new market.

**Edge:** None stated. Low prices and a domestic loyal following are not unique advantages against an entrenched local rival with established logistics.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot of 5 stores cannot achieve 95% shelf-fill and regulatory compliance within 6 months, the wider rollout must stop.

**Decision:** The proposal demands a "go" on a massive capital commitment while admitting the core operational engines are unproven. This violates the basic principle of verifying the engine before building the chassis.

DECISION: NO-GO
