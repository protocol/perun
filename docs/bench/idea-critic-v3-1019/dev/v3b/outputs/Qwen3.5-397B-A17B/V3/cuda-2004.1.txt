**Steelman:** Embedding general-purpose compute capability in mass-market GPUs now creates a ubiquitous, low-cost parallel supercomputer, positioning the company to dominate an emerging scientific computing market before rivals react, transforming a cyclical gaming hardware vendor into a foundational platform provider.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal acknowledges the lack of current paying customers and the cost burden, but these are strategic bets, not factual contradictions. The core business (gaming) funds the R&D; the "distraction" risk is inherent to any pivot but not a definitive blocker if the upside is real.

**Risks:** High unit cost reduces margins; engineering focus shifts from pure graphics performance; the software ecosystem may fail to materialize; academic interest may not convert to commercial demand; competitors might copy the feature without the early cost burden.

**Edge:** First-mover advantage in hardware architecture. By baking capability into every consumer chip, the company creates an installed base of millions of parallel processors overnight, solving the distribution problem that plagues niche scientific hardware. Competitors focused solely on the graphics race cannot easily retrofit this architecture later.

**Pre-mortem:** The program fails because the compiler toolchain is too difficult for scientists to use, or because gaming performance lags the rival due to resource diversion, causing the core revenue stream to collapse before the new market matures.

**Decision Method:**
1.  **Fit:** Aligns with a "platform" vision, moving beyond commodity graphics.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves margins but cedes the future to whoever programs the GPU first. "Wait for demand" ensures the company is a follower, not a leader, in a high-margin sector.
3.  **Cost of Delay:** Waiting until a market exists allows competitors to establish standards and lock in developers.
4.  **Kill Criteria:** If gaming market share drops >5% due to performance deficits, or if no significant non-graphics code runs on the hardware within 24 months of launch, pivot or halt.

The potential to create an entirely new computing paradigm outweighs the near-term margin pressure, provided the core gaming business remains competitive. The "no market yet" condition is the very reason to act now to create it.

DECISION: GO
