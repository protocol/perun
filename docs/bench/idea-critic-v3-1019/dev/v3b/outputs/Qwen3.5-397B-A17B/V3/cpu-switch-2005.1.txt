**Steelman:** Switching to the dominant architecture solves the thermal/performance ceiling blocking thin laptops, using a translation layer to preserve the existing software ecosystem during a two-year transition.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the "sales freeze" risk: announcing a future switch typically halts current sales immediately, creating a revenue gap before new units ship. However, staying on the current roadmap guarantees product irrelevance in the emerging thin-and-light category; the "do nothing" option yields zero upside. Rebuilding the entire software stack internally is cost-prohibitive compared to leveraging the dominant architecture's native developer base.

**Pre-mortem:** If executed poorly, the translation layer performs too slowly for pro users, and the brand becomes just another generic PC maker. The "painful earlier transition" suggests organizational fatigue, yet the technical debt of the old architecture is now an existential threat.

**Evidence & Fit:** The supplier's failure to deliver cool/efficient chips is a hard constraint. The dominant architecture offers the only proven path to the required power efficiency. The translation layer mitigates the "developer rebuild" risk, turning a hard stop into a manageable degradation.

**Blockers:** None. The risks (sales freeze, slow translation, brand dilution) are severe but manageable via marketing cadence and engineering focus; they do not invalidate the core necessity of the move. The alternative is product stagnation.

**Edge:** This maker controls both hardware and software, allowing tighter integration of the translation layer than competitors attempting similar feats. The distinctiveness risk is outweighed by the ability to finally ship competitive thin hardware.

**Decision:** The proposal addresses a fatal hardware limitation with a viable, albeit risky, migration path. The risks are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The cost of delay is losing the entire thin-laptop market segment.

**Kill Criteria:** Halt if the translation layer fails to achieve >80% native speed for key applications within 12 months, or if initial pre-orders for the new architecture miss targets by >40% due to consumer confusion.

**Leading Metric:** Percentage of top 100 applications running natively vs. translated by Month 18.

DECISION: GO
