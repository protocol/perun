**Steelman:** A cylindrical, always-listening speaker at $199 ($99 for members) creates a new "ambient computing" category, leveraging existing retail relationships to recover from a failed phone launch via a focused, invitation-only rollout.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly mitigates the "failed hardware launch" risk through an invitation-only release (limiting blast radius) and a aggressive pricing tier for members. The lack of a prior category is the opportunity, not a blocker, provided the device solves a specific need (music/lists) better than phones.

**Risks:** Privacy rejection of always-on microphones; voice recognition failure in noisy environments; category education costs. These are valid execution risks but do not invalidate the core hypothesis given the staged rollout.

**Edge:** The retailer possesses a massive installed base of paying members who can be targeted with the $99 price point, creating an immediate early-adopter cohort that phone-based assistants cannot match on price or form factor integration. The "always-listening" aspect offers convenience phones (which sleep or require activation) cannot.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if voice accuracy drops below 90% in real-world home noise, causing users to revert to phones. However, the invitation-only phase allows for iterative improvement before mass marketing.

**Kill Criteria:** If <40% of invited members activate the device within 30 days, or if return rates exceed 15% due to privacy concerns or accuracy issues, the program halts. Leading metric: Daily Active Usage (DAU) per invited unit.

**Decision Logic:** The plan beats "do nothing" (which cedes the emerging smart home market) and "wait" (competitors are already exploring voice). The staged approach converts high risks into manageable experiments. The specific pricing strategy and member-only initial access directly address the company's recent credibility gap.

DECISION: GO
