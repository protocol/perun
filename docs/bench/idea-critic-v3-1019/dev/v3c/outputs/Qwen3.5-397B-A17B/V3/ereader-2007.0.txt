**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled e-reader removes friction for book buyers and solves publisher piracy fears via a closed ecosystem, creating a new high-margin revenue stream that complements rather than replaces print.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the company's total lack of hardware experience, suggesting high failure risk. However, "do nothing" cedes the digital future to competitors who will eventually erode print dominance. Licensing software to existing hardware makers is a cheaper alternative but fails to control the user experience or guarantee the "one-click" simplicity required for mass adoption. The risk of cannibalization is real but acceptable; owning the digital transition is superior to losing sales to third-party devices.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the core vision of being the world's largest bookseller, extending reach rather than changing the product. If this fails, it will likely be due to device cost or battery life, not lack of demand. The "one-way door" nature of hardware investment demands a strict kill criterion: if device adoption does not drive a net increase in total title sales (print + digital) within 18 months, the program stops.

**Evidence & Edge:**
While earlier e-readers failed, they lacked seamless connectivity and a massive existing catalog. The company's edge is not technology, but its exclusive relationships with publishers and a library of millions of titles ready for immediate download. The cellular subsidy is a customer acquisition cost, justified by lifetime book value.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of experience is a risk, not a factual impossibility, and can be mitigated by partnering with manufacturers while retaining design control.
**Risks:** Hardware margin compression, publisher pushback on pricing, print cannibalization.
**Edge:** Unmatched catalog depth and existing publisher trust that no new entrant possesses.

**Decision:**
The proposal beats "do nothing" because it secures the company's position in the inevitable digital shift. The risks are manageable compared to the existential risk of irrelevance. The single low price and free connectivity are the specific differentiators that make this viable where previous attempts failed.

DECISION: GO
