The claim is that a 2013 head-worn display with a camera, voice control, and small screen should launch as an everyday consumer product via an invitation-only program at $1,500.

**Steelman:** An exclusive, high-priced launch creates scarcity and validates the category, allowing early adopters to normalize the form factor before mass market release.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "everyday consumer" fit test against its own stated facts. In 2013, a $1,500 price point excludes the mass market by definition, contradicting the positioning. The identified risks are not merely hurdles but fundamental blockers: privacy objections to a always-on camera create immediate social hostility; short battery life prevents all-day utility required for an "everyday" device; and the lack of a clear use case means the value proposition is speculative. The "invitation-only" tactic masks the absence of product-market fit rather than solving it. A cheaper, staged alternative exists: target specific enterprise or developer niches where the high cost and limited battery are acceptable trade-offs for specialized utility, rather than forcing a premature consumer launch that invites regulatory and social backlash.

**Blockers:** 
1. The stated price ($1,500) contradicts the "everyday consumer" positioning, making the core claim internally inconsistent.
2. The stated risk of "no clear everyday use" confirms the product lacks the primary driver for mass adoption.
3. Privacy and social acceptance risks are active, not theoretical, in 2013, threatening brand reputation before scale.

**Risks:** Regulatory bans on wearable cameras; public rejection leading to a "glasshole" stigma; high return rates due to battery limitations.

**Edge:** None stated. Being first with an unproven form factor without a solved use case is not an edge; it is a liability.

**Decision:** No-go. The plan commits to a mass-market narrative that the price, battery life, and lack of use cases explicitly undermine. The invitation-only layer does not fix the fundamental mismatch between the product's capabilities and the demands of an everyday consumer. Pivot to a focused developer/enterprise pilot to define the use case before attempting a consumer narrative.

DECISION: NO-GO
