Steelman: A premium, keyboard-less touchscreen phone integrating a proven music player and full web browser can disrupt a market stagnating under carrier-controlled, keyboard-centric designs, provided one carrier cedes design control for exclusivity.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of phone experience is a risk, not a factual blocker, as the core competencies (miniaturization, UI, music) are already proven in existing products. The premium price is justified by the unique value proposition (full internet + music) absent in competitors.

**Risks:** No third-party apps at launch limits utility; exclusivity caps initial market share; business users may reject the lack of a physical keyboard. These are valid execution risks but do not invalidate the core thesis that the current market options are inferior.

**Edge:** The company owns the leading digital music player brand and has a history of defying industry norms (e.g., removing floppy drives, adopting USB early). Competitors are locked into carrier mandates and legacy keyboard designs, creating a vacuum for a user-centric, software-controlled device. The "no keyboard" feature is a differentiator, not a deficit, enabling a larger screen interface that rivals cannot match without scrapping their entire hardware lineage.

**Decision Logic:** The "do nothing" option guarantees erosion of the music player business as phone makers add mediocre music features. The proposed path is the only one that protects the core business while opening a new revenue stream via service revenue sharing. The exclusivity deal mitigates carrier resistance by offering them a unique product to drive subscriptions. The lack of apps is a temporary state solvable post-launch; the hardware/software integration is the immediate moat.

**Kill Criteria:** If the carrier refuses the revenue-share model or demands design control, the deal fails. If pre-orders do not indicate strong demand despite the premium price, the rollout should be paused.

DECISION: GO
