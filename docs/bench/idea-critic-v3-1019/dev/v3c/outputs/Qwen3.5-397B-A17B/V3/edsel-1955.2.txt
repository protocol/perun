**Steelman:** Launching a distinct mid-priced division captures a growing market segment, leverages existing manufacturing scale, and prevents customer defection to competitors by offering a logical upgrade path within the corporate family.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on two fragile premises: that the mid-priced segment will continue growing despite looming recession risks, and that consumers will accept a new, unproven brand over established rivals during economic uncertainty. Building a dedicated dealer network requires massive upfront capital with a long payback period; if the recession hits at launch (1957), sales will collapse while fixed infrastructure costs remain, bleeding cash from the profitable low and premium lines. Furthermore, "extensive research" often confirms biases rather than predicting shifts in sentiment during downturns. The risk of internal cannibalization is high if the new brand's pricing overlaps with the entry-premium models, confusing buyers and diluting marketing spend.

**Alternatives:** 
1. **Do Nothing:** Maintain current portfolio; risk losing customers to competitors but preserve capital and avoid fixed overhead expansion.
2. **Badge Engineering:** Introduce mid-priced models under existing brand umbrellas to test the waters without the cost of a new dealer network or separate brand identity.
3. **Staged Launch:** Delay the dealer network build-out; sell through existing channels first to validate demand before committing to irreversible infrastructure.

**Fit & Edge:** The plan assumes continued economic expansion. The stated "edge" is merely being second-largest and having research; neither guarantees success in a contraction. No unique asset (cost, distribution, or product advantage) is cited that protects against a downturn better than competitors. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of failure.

**Blockers:** None explicitly proven in the text, but the recession risk combined with irreversible dealer investment creates a potential financial trap if the timing is off.
**Risks:** High fixed costs at launch, segment contraction, brand confusion, capital drain.
**Edge:** None identified beyond scale, which does not mitigate timing risk.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal commits to high-fixed-cost infrastructure (dealerships) and a new brand identity immediately, right before a potential recession. This is a one-way door with massive downside. The lack of a staged approach or alternative distribution method makes the plan too rigid for the stated risks. A "go" decision requires a capped, time-boxed pilot or a revised strategy that defers the dealer network until demand is proven in a softening market. As proposed, it fails the reversibility and risk-adjusted return test.

DECISION: NO-GO
