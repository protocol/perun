The claim is that launching two social-networking phones on a new OS, sold via a single carrier with a mandatory expensive data plan and no app store, will successfully capture the teenage market in 2010.

Steelman: Leveraging an acquired messaging brand allows a focused, carrier-subsidized entry into the youth market without the delay of building an app ecosystem immediately.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. First, the target demographic (teenagers) is highly price-sensitive, yet the mandatory data plan costs as much as a full smartphone plan, removing the price advantage. Second, the value proposition is critically broken: rivals offer large app catalogs, while this proposal explicitly launches with *no* application store. In 2010, the "app" is the primary utility of a smartphone; a social phone without extensible software is functionally inferior to competitors. Third, building a new in-house OS instead of using the company's existing mobile platform introduces massive execution risk and fragments resources for no stated strategic gain, especially when the core differentiator (apps) is missing.

The sunk cost of two years of development is a fallacy; it justifies nothing if the product cannot compete. The "better way" is to utilize the existing mobile platform to reduce risk and negotiate a lower-cost data tier specifically for teens. Without an app store or a price advantage, the product has no edge. The risks are not merely potential; the lack of apps and high price are factual blockers present at launch that directly contradict the needs of the stated user base.

Blockers: High cost relative to target income; zero app ecosystem vs. rival abundance; unnecessary OS fragmentation.
Risks: Carrier dependence, brand dilution if the device fails.
Edge: None identified; the acquired messaging brand is insufficient to overcome the functional deficit of no apps.

This plan commits capital to a product that is objectively weaker than existing alternatives on the two dimensions that matter most to teenagers: price and software availability.

DECISION: NO-GO
