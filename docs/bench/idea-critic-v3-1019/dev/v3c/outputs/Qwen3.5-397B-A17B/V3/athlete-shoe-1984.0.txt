**Steelman:** Secure a generational talent before the market prices him, using controversy and exclusivity to manufacture demand that transcends on-court performance, effectively creating a new product category rather than competing in an existing one.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The objections regarding injury, inexperience, and league fines are valid risks but function as features of this specific strategy, not blockers.
1.  **Unproven/Injury Risk:** Every established star was once unproven. The premium paid is for *potential* dominance. If he fails, the loss is financial; if he succeeds, the brand owns the decade. No alternative "safe" player offers this ceiling.
2.  **League Fines:** The proposal explicitly includes rule-breaking colorways. This is not an oversight; it is a calculated provocation. Fines generate news cycles, framing the shoe as rebellious and desirable to the core youth demographic. Paying the fines becomes part of the marketing spend.
3.  **Budget Share:** Concentrating the budget is necessary to break through noise. A diluted spend on multiple mediocre endorsers guarantees invisibility.
4.  **Longevity:** While signature lines often fade, the "rule-breaking" narrative and the sheer magnitude of the bet create a cultural moment that sustains initial sales long enough for performance to take over.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Continue losing share; brand stagnates as a "running only" company.
*   *Sign Established Star:* Prohibitive cost, less creative control, no "discovery" narrative.
*   *Sign Multiple Rookies:* Dilutes impact; none receive the focus required to become a superstar.

**Edge:**
The competitor's caution is this proposal's advantage. Rivals will not match the offer due to risk aversion, allowing exclusive access to the asset. The "edge" is the willingness to monetize controversy and absorb short-term regulatory friction for long-term brand definition.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited are inherent to the high-reward strategy and are mitigated by the narrative value of the controversy itself.
**Risks:** Career-ending injury in year one; league bans the shoe entirely (not just fines).
**Kill Criteria:** If the league bans the shoe permanently (not just fines) before launch, or if the player suffers a season-ending injury in the first 10 games, re-evaluate the contract structure.

**Decision:** The plan beats "do nothing" and relies on a unique asymmetry: competitors cannot copy the risk profile.

DECISION: GO
