**Steelman:** Acquiring the premium tea brand instantly grants category leadership and growth, leveraging the buyer's massive distribution to scale revenue faster than organic expansion allows.

**Attack:** The core value of the target is its "cult" status, built on quirky independence and loyal niche distributors. The proposal explicitly plans to erase these differentiators by forcing mass-market advertising and supermarket distribution. This is not integration; it is assimilation. History shows that when mass-market giants absorb cult brands and apply their standard playbook, the unique brand equity evaporates, leaving a commoditized product facing fierce competition from established soft-drink rivals who already dominate the proposed channels. The $1.7 billion price tag pays for growth that the proposed strategy is likely to destroy. The "edge" claimed (distribution scale) directly attacks the asset being purchased (exclusive image).

**Alternatives:** 
1. **Do Nothing:** Avoids destroying the target's value while allowing the buyer to develop its own tea line slowly.
2. **Autonomous Operation:** Acquire but guarantee distributor independence and forbid mass-market advertising for a set period to preserve the brand's soul.
3. **The Proposal:** High risk of killing the goose that lays the golden eggs.

**Fit:** The strategy contradicts the north star of preserving the premium brand's loyalty. Moving to mass channels invites direct combat with Coke and Pepsi tea lines, where the buyer has no unique advantage other than the distribution they plan to force.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-acquisition, sales flatten as core fans reject the "corporate" image, independent distributors defect, and the brand becomes just another SKU on a supermarket shelf, failing to justify the $1.7 billion outlay. The cost of delay is low; the cost of immediate execution is the total loss of the acquired brand's premium positioning.

**Blockers:** The plan relies on a contradiction: using mass-market tactics to grow a brand whose value derives from anti-mass-market exclusivity. No evidence suggests the buyer can maintain the "cult" image while implementing the proposed operational changes.

**Edge:** None identified. The buyer's scale is a disadvantage in this specific context, not an asset.

**Kill Criteria:** If independent distributor retention drops below 80% in six months, or if brand sentiment scores decline by 15% post-rebrand, the integration must halt.

DECISION: NO-GO
