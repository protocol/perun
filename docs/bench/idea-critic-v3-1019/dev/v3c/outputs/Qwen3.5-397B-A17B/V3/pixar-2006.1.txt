**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio secures the industry's most valuable creative engine, preventing a rival partnership and instantly revitalizing the buyer's struggling hand-drawn unit by installing proven leadership.

**Attack:** The $7.4 billion price tag relies entirely on the continued dominance of a small creative team producing only one film annually. This creates extreme concentration risk: if the specific leaders depart or the next film underperforms, the acquisition multiple becomes indefensible. Furthermore, an all-stock deal making the founder a major shareholder introduces governance friction; the buyer cedes significant control to the very individuals whose retention is critical yet uncertain. The "culture clash" risk is not merely operational but existential; imposing conglomerate processes on a boutique hit-maker often destroys the magic being purchased.

**Alternatives:** A long-term distribution-only deal or a minority equity stake would secure content flow without the massive balance sheet exposure or integration trauma. "Do nothing" risks losing the partnership but preserves capital for organic rebuilding or alternative targets.

**Fit & Edge:** The buyer lacks a current animation edge; this purchase imports one instantly. However, the edge is fragile, tied to individuals who now hold leverage as major shareholders. The cost of delay is high (rival partnership), but the cost of overpayment is catastrophic.

**Blockers:** None explicitly fatal based *only* on provided facts, as the strategic necessity is clear.
**Risks:** Key person dependency, valuation bubble, governance conflict with the founder, culture destruction.
**Edge:** Immediate access to a proven hit-making machine that the buyer cannot currently replicate internally.

**Decision Logic:** While the risks are severe, the alternative (losing the partner to a rival while having no internal success) threatens the conglomerate's long-term viability in animation. The high price is the market rate for monopoly-quality assets. The governance risk is mitigated by the founder's financial incentive to see the stock rise post-merger. The strategic necessity outweighs the financial caution in this specific "buy or die" context.

DECISION: GO
