**Steelman:** Acquiring the startup secures a free, open mobile OS to accelerate mobile web adoption, directly expanding the search advertising market before competitors lock it down.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces immediate structural hostility. Carriers control distribution and currently extract high margins from closed ecosystems; they have zero incentive to adopt a free OS that commoditizes their gateway. Handset makers, reliant on carrier relationships and existing licensing revenues, face high switching costs for an unproven platform with no hardware partner commitment. The $50 million price tag for a six-person team with no revenue model is a massive premium on pure speculation.

The "open source" strategy assumes developers and manufacturers will rally around a platform owned by an advertising company with no hardware credibility. History suggests incumbents will fracture any open standard rather than cede control to a search rival. The payback period relies on mobile search becoming dominant years before the infrastructure (data networks, smartphone usability) supports it. The risk of total adoption failure is high; if carriers block it, the asset becomes a costly science experiment with no path to recoup the investment.

However, the cost of *not* acting is potentially existential if mobile search does explode and a competitor controls the OS layer. The $50 million is a relatively small "option premium" against the total market cap of a search monopoly. While the execution risks (carrier resistance, lack of hardware partners) are severe blockers for immediate success, they are not facts that prove the plan fails *as proposed*; they are the exact hurdles the acquisition is meant to solve by removing the cost barrier for manufacturers. The edge is the proposer's unique ability to monetize usage rather than software licenses, aligning incentives with manufacturers who hate paying royalties.

**Blockers:** NONE (Risks are high but inherent to the strategic bet, not factual contradictions).
**Risks:** Carrier blockade, manufacturer apathy, multi-year cash burn.
**Edge:** Monetization via ads allows free distribution, undercutting licensed OS competitors.

**Kill Criteria:** If no major handset maker commits to a prototype within 18 months, terminate the project. Leading metric: Number of active developer builds per month.

DECISION: GO
