The claim is that a premium burger, high price, and record ad spend will shift a child-centric brand to adults. The steelman: a massive, visible push can overwrite brand perception faster than organic growth, using scale to force a new identity.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The core contradiction is the brand asset itself: the chain's equity is "child-centered" and "value." A premium product priced above the core menu directly conflicts with the "value" expectation of its existing base, while the "child" image actively repels the target adult demographic seeking sophistication. The proposal admits adults see the chain as "fast, not premium," yet offers no mechanism to change this perception other than spending money. History shows that throwing budget at a fundamental brand mismatch often accelerates failure by highlighting the dissonance to both groups: adults ignore it as inauthentic, and families feel alienated by the price shift.

The "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the "do nothing" comparison. Doing nothing preserves the profitable core. This plan risks cannibalizing the core value proposition without a proven demand signal. The risks listed (price deterrence, image mismatch) are not merely potential issues; they are structural barriers confirmed by the proposal's own context. There is no "edge" listed—no unique asset, cost advantage, or existing adult demand—that suggests this specific chain can win where others might fail. Being first or spending the most is not an edge when the product contradicts the brand's DNA.

The pre-mortem is clear: the launch fails because the advertising amplifies the brand's inability to be premium, wasting the largest budget in industry history on a message the market rejects as incompatible with the source. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a failed rebrand that damages the core business. Without a pilot to prove adults will pay a premium at *this* specific brand, the full-scale launch is a bet against verified consumer psychology.

Blockers: The brand's child-centric image and value positioning directly contradict the premium adult target; no evidence suggests ad spend alone can invert this.
Risks: Alienating core family customers; wasting budget on inauthentic messaging.
Edge: None identified; scale and spend are not advantages here.

DECISION: NO-GO
