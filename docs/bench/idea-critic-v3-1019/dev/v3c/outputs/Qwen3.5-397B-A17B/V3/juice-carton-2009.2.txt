The claim is that a radical visual overhaul of a legacy brand's packaging, executed via a big-bang national launch, will succeed despite losing its primary shelf cue. The steelman: the current "orange with straw" icon is dated, and a modern, minimalist aesthetic with a functional orange-shaped cap will rejuvenate the brand, attract new buyers, and signal quality without altering the product itself.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The stated facts identify the old image as the "main shelf cue" for a "loyal and older" demographic. In fast-moving consumer goods, removing the primary visual anchor for an entrenched, aging audience is a proven failure mode, not a risk. The proposal offers no evidence that the new "minimalist design" functions as an equally strong cue, nor does it account for the "costly" nature of a simultaneous nationwide switch which eliminates the ability to learn from regional failures.

The "better way" analysis highlights a superior alternative: a phased rollout or A/B testing in select markets to verify if the new design actually drives recognition and sales before committing to a expensive, irreversible national change. The current plan ignores the "do nothing" or "evolutionary change" options which preserve shelf equity while testing modernization.

The pre-mortem is clear: six months post-launch, sales have collapsed because loyal shoppers walked past the unrecognizable cartons, and the new design failed to attract enough new buyers to offset the loss. The cost of delay (waiting to test) is negligible compared to the cost of a failed national relaunch. There is no named "edge" in the proposal—no asset, data, or unique advantage—that suggests this specific brand can defy the standard retail rule that disrupting shelf cues alienates existing customers. The risks are not merely theoretical; they are direct consequences of the stated facts.

Blockers: The plan removes the stated "main shelf cue" for the core "older" demographic without validation that the new design works, contradicting the goal of maintaining sales.
Risks: High cost of nationwide failure; alienation of loyal base.
Edge: None provided.

DECISION: NO-GO
