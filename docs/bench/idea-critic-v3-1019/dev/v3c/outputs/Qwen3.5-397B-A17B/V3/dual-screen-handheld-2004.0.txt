**Steelman:** Differentiate via novel interaction (dual-screen/touch) and price to expand the market beyond core gamers, rather than losing a specular hardware war against a well-funded rival.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that "unusual hardware confuses developers," leading to a software drought. However, the proposal mitigates this by keeping the older handheld on sale, preserving the existing developer ecosystem and revenue stream while the new platform matures. Matching the rival's graphics (Option A) is a no-go; it cedes the advantage to a better-funded opponent in a commodity fight. Doing nothing (Option B) allows the rival to define the generation.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a "blue ocean" strategy: making the competition irrelevant by changing the rules. If this fails, it will be because first-party studios fail to demonstrate the touch screen's unique value, causing third parties to flee. The risk of being dismissed as a "gimmick" is real but necessary; safe products do not create new markets. The cost of two screens is offset by avoiding the R&D arms race for high-end graphics processors.

**Evidence & Edge:**
The edge is not technical superiority but *asymmetric positioning*. The rival targets the high-end core; this proposal targets the lapsed and non-gamer with lower price and intuitive touch controls. The "gimmick" risk is the price of admission for a new input method. Since the legacy system remains active, the company retains cash flow to subsidize the transition. The lack of proven touch games is an opportunity for first-mover definition, not a reason to abstain.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (developer confusion, gimmick perception) are execution challenges, not factual impossibilities. The proposal explicitly addresses the developer risk via the legacy device strategy.
**Risks:** Third-party adoption lag; consumer confusion on value prop.
**Edge:** Unique input method creates a defensible niche; lower price point expands total addressable market; dual-product strategy hedges risk.

This is a two-way door with a capped downside (legacy sales continue) and massive upside (market expansion).

DECISION: GO
