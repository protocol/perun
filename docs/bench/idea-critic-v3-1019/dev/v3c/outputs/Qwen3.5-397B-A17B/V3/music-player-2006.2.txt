**Steelman:** Leverage brand loyalty and superior hardware (screen, wireless sharing) to disrupt a complacent leader by removing friction (DRM) and integrating content with device.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal acknowledges the late entry and ecosystem dominance but posits that the user experience gap (DRM, screen, sharing) is wide enough to overcome switching costs. No factual contradiction exists in the text preventing execution.

**Risks:** High. Abandoning the industry DRM standard may alienate record labels, potentially starving the store of content. Late entry requires massive marketing spend to dislodge entrenched habits. Wireless sharing could face regulatory or licensing hurdles not yet resolved.

**Edge:** The proposer is a software company with the capacity to build a seamless desktop-to-device ecosystem, unlike hardware-only rivals. The specific combination of a larger screen, wireless sharing, and a DRM-free store addresses specific pain points (portability, ownership feeling) that the dominant player's locked ecosystem ignores. Being late is irrelevant if the product category is redefined from "MP3 player" to "music lifestyle device."

**Kill Criteria:** If major labels refuse to license content for the DRM-free store within 6 months, or if wireless sharing functionality is legally blocked by regulators before launch, the project must pivot to a licensed-DRM model or halt. Leading metric: Percentage of top 100 albums available in the store at launch; target >80%.

**Decision Logic:** The "do nothing" option guarantees irrelevance as the market shifts. The risks are executional, not fundamental flaws in the thesis. The edge (ecosystem integration + UX superiority) directly attacks the incumbent's weakness (locked, fragmented experience). The proposal includes a marketing budget to counter late entry. The removal of DRM is a high-risk, high-reward differentiator that aligns with consumer desire for ownership, providing a clear wedge into the market.

DECISION: GO
