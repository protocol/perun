**Steelman:** Vertical integration is the only remaining lever to prevent the sole hardware partner from defecting to the dominant rival, preserving the operating system's existence despite the handset division's declining sales.

**Attack:** The core failure mode is treating a software ecosystem crisis as a hardware ownership problem. The market leader wins due to developer adoption and app availability, not device manufacturing control. Buying a failing handset maker with falling sales for €5.4 billion consumes critical capital without solving the "app gap." History suggests integrating a large, distressed hardware workforce distracts engineering focus from the actual bottleneck: platform attractiveness. Furthermore, this move likely alienates remaining third-party handset partners, pushing them closer to the rival platform out of fear of competition, thereby accelerating the very isolation the deal aims to prevent. The "edge" is illusory; owning a shrinking slice of hardware does not create the network effects required to compete with the incumbent's developer moat.

**Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option avoids burning cash on a depreciating asset but risks immediate partner defection. A better alternative might be licensing the OS more aggressively or pivoting strategy entirely, rather than propping up a dying hardware unit. However, if the immediate threat is the *single* remaining partner switching sides tomorrow, this acquisition acts as a defensive blockade, not a growth engine.

**Verdict:** The proposal lacks a credible mechanism to reverse the ecosystem decline. It solves a short-term loyalty issue (preventing switch) by creating a long-term financial and operational anchor (owning a losing business). The risks of integration distraction and partner alienation outweigh the temporary benefit of controlling one flailing device line. Without a simultaneous, funded plan to solve the app deficit, this is a costly delay of the inevitable, not a turnaround strategy. The edge is negative: it trades liquidity for a liability without fixing the root cause.

DECISION: NO-GO
