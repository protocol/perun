The claim is that a $180 tabletop stereoscopic 3D console with red monochrome displays will successfully bridge the gap between handheld and home-console generations. The steelman is that this device leverages existing technology to create a unique, immersive 3D experience at a lower price point than home consoles, filling a market void before the next generation arrives.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The "better-way" analysis reveals that "do nothing" is superior because the proposed product offers a degraded user experience (red-on-black visuals, eye strain, discomfort) compared to existing handhelds and the impending home console. The risks are not merely potential; they are inherent design flaws that directly contradict the core value proposition of gaming: enjoyment and comfort. Eye strain and headaches are not edge cases but likely outcomes of the fundamental technology choice, creating a negative feedback loop that will kill software adoption.

Furthermore, the "fit" with the company's strategy is poor. Launching a niche product with few games just two years before a major home console release cannibalizes marketing focus and developer resources without offering a compelling reason for consumers to adopt a stopgap technology. The "edge" is nonexistent; being first with uncomfortable, monochrome 3D is not an asset but a liability that could damage the brand's reputation for quality.

The pre-mortem is clear: the product launches, reviewers panned the visual fidelity and physical discomfort, sales plummet due to lack of software and consumer fatigue, and the company is left with excess inventory and a distracted development pipeline just as the true next-generation console needs full support. The cost of delay (waiting for better 3D tech or the next console) is zero compared to the cost of launching a flawed product. There is no observable metric or kill criterion that can save a product fundamentally broken in its user experience.

Blockers: The core technology causes physical discomfort and visual degradation, making the product unusable for extended periods.
Risks: Low software adoption, brand damage, resource diversion from the next-gen console.
Edge: None identified; being first with inferior technology is not a competitive advantage.

DECISION: NO-GO
