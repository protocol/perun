**Steelman:** An upfront $79 fee locks in loyalty and increases purchase frequency, offsetting shipping losses through higher volume and reduced price sensitivity.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the **unit economics mismatch**. In 2005, two-day shipping costs for heavy/bulky items often exceed $15–$20. A customer ordering only 4–5 times annually breaks even; anyone ordering more generates immediate, uncapped losses. Unlike digital subscriptions, marginal costs here are high and variable. The proposal lacks a cap on eligible items or a minimum order value, exposing the retailer to arbitrage by "heavy buyers" who will maximize order frequency to extract value, directly causing the cited margin hit.

**Do Nothing** preserves current margins but cedes convenience to competitors. A **Better Way** would be a tiered membership (e.g., free shipping only on orders over $50) or a lower fee with per-order discounts, limiting downside risk while testing elasticity. The current "all eligible orders" definition is too broad for an unproven model.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, heavy users account for 40% of volume but only 10% of fee revenue. The company burns cash on every shipment to this segment. Reversal is impossible without massive PR backlash from paying members, forcing a continuation of losses or a buyout of contracts.

**Fit & Edge:** While loyalty is a valid north star, the proposal assumes frequency equals profit. It fails to account for the *cost* of that frequency. There is no stated edge (e.g., proprietary logistics lowering costs below market rates) that allows the retailer to absorb unlimited two-day shipping costs profitably. The "unproven economics" risk is not just a risk; it is a likely outcome given the uncapped liability.

**Blockers:**
1. **Uncapped Liability:** No maximum order limit or item exclusion creates a mathematical certainty of loss on high-frequency/heavy-item shoppers.
2. **Missing Unit Economics:** No data proves the $79 fee covers the average shipping cost of the anticipated increased frequency.

**Edge:** NONE stated.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot shows average shipping cost per member exceeds $79 within 90 days, stop immediately.

Without a cap on eligibility or proof that increased volume offsets the per-unit loss, this is a subsidized logistics scheme, not a business model.

DECISION: NO-GO
