**Steelman:** A cash-strapped carmaker can survive by creating a new "minivan" category using existing compact-car parts and a converted factory, offering car-like driving in a garage-friendly package before rivals react.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker cited is financial: the company has "little cash." However, the proposal explicitly mitigates this by reusing an existing compact-car platform and converting a factory rather than building new tooling. This drastically reduces capital expenditure compared to launching a clean-sheet vehicle. The risk of buyers confusing it with a commercial van is addressed by the "front-wheel-drive" and "car-like" positioning, differentiating it from rear-wheel-drive truck-based vans of the era.

The objection that "no such category exists" is actually the opportunity; entering a blue ocean is preferable to fighting in the saturated station wagon market. While rivals could copy the idea, the first-mover advantage in defining the segment, combined with the specific fit for home garages, creates a defensible niche. The platform limitation on space is a necessary trade-off to achieve the garage-fit constraint, which is the core value proposition for families.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because the marketing failed to distinguish the vehicle from cheap commercial vans, or the conversion of the factory took too long, burning the remaining cash. However, doing nothing guarantees continued decline in the station wagon segment. The cost of delay is high, as competitors are always one design cycle away.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** None. The cash constraint is addressed by the low-cost engineering approach.
*   **Risks:** Market confusion and competitor copying. These are manageable through branding and speed.
*   **Edge:** Unique combination of car handling, garage fit, and family capacity using existing assets.

The proposal turns a liquidity crisis into a strategic pivot by leveraging underutilized assets (platform, factory) to create a high-margin product in an uncontested space. The risks are outweighed by the certainty of failure if the current trajectory continues.

DECISION: GO
