**Steelman:** Leverage existing compact mechanics to deliver affordable sporty styling for a emerging demographic, monetized via customization and amplified by massive launch visibility.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly addresses the "cheap" perception risk through distinct sporty styling (long hood, short deck) and targets a specific, growing demographic (postwar boom) rather than relying on general demand. The unproven volume demand is the core bet, not a factual contradiction; the low base price limits downside exposure if volume lags.

**Risks:** Demand uncertainty is real but mitigated by the low entry price and shared R&D costs. Parts sharing could dilute brand prestige, but the styling differentiation counters this. Heavy upfront ad spend creates financial pressure if initial sales miss targets.

**Edge:** First-mover advantage in a new "pony car" segment combining economy reliability with aspirational aesthetics, targeting a massive, underserved generational cohort with high customization potential.

**Analysis:**
The "do nothing" option cedes the emerging youth market to competitors. Alternatives like building a ground-up sports car violate the "low price" constraint; upgrading the existing compact fails the "sporty" requirement. This plan uniquely balances cost control (shared mechanics) with market appeal (styling/options).

The risks are managed by the business model: a low base price ensures broad accessibility even if option uptake is slow, while the long options list protects margins. The advertising blitz is necessary to define the new category. The lack of proven volume demand is inherent to innovation; waiting for proof would mean missing the window. The proposal correctly identifies the tension between economy roots and sporty image but offers a viable design solution.

No factual blocker exists. The strategy aligns with the demographic shift and leverages existing assets efficiently. The risks are acceptable given the potential market creation.

DECISION: GO
