The claim is that launching the world's cheapest car at $2,500 from a new dedicated factory will successfully transition scooter families to cars. The steelman is that extreme affordability unlocks a massive, untapped market segment, achieving volume sufficient to offset razor-thin margins through a specialized high-efficiency plant.

However, three critical blockers exist in the proposal's own facts. First, the "cheapest" positioning creates a fatal perception trap: in safety-sensitive purchases like family transport, "cheapest" often signals "dangerous," directly validating the raised risk that buyers will reject the brand due to quality doubts. Second, the dedicated factory is currently immobilized by farmer protests; without a resolved site, high-volume targets are physically impossible to meet, rendering the cost structure invalid. Third, the alternatives (scooters and used cars) are not just cheaper but proven; scooters offer lower operating costs, and used cars offer higher perceived status and safety for similar capital outlay. The proposal offers no specific "edge"—such as a unique distribution channel, a verified safety rating, or a pre-order book—to overcome these entrenched preferences.

A cheaper, safer alternative is a phased launch using an existing facility to bypass the land protest blocker, paired with a marketing pivot from "cheapest" to "smart family upgrade" to avoid the quality stigma. The current plan fails the "beat do nothing" test because the stated facts (protests, safety doubts, strong substitutes) suggest the dedicated factory will sit idle while the brand becomes synonymous with unsafe cut corners. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the sunk cost of a halted factory and a tainted brand.

Blockers: Unresolved land protests halt production; "cheapest" branding likely repels safety-conscious families; no defined edge over used cars.
Risks: Margin erosion, brand damage, regulatory scrutiny on safety.
Edge: None stated; price alone is not an edge when safety is questioned.

DECISION: NO-GO
