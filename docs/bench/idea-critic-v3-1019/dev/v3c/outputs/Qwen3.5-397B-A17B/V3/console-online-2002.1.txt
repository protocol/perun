**Steelman:** A unified, paid online service creates a defensible moat through network effects and recurring revenue, differentiating the console via superior user experience (identity, voice, matchmaking) despite higher upfront friction.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces severe headwinds: low broadband penetration in 2002, strong consumer expectation of "free" online play, and the company's lack of operational experience in running large-scale hosting/support. However, the "do nothing" approach cedes the high-end experience to rivals or fragments it via publishers, preventing a cohesive ecosystem. The risk of limited adoption is real but acts as a filter for a dedicated, high-value user base rather than a total failure mode.

The critical blocker test is whether the company can execute the operations. The proposal admits they have "never run" such operations. This is a factual gap in capability, not just a risk. Without verified evidence of a hiring plan, infrastructure partner, or pilot test proving they can sustain uptime and support, the claim that they *can* launch this service is unsupported. A plan relying on unproven operational capacity where failure means total service collapse is a blocker.

However, the strategic edge is clear: a single identity and friends list across all games is a product advantage rivals lacking a central service cannot easily match. The "free" model of rivals is a commodity; this proposes a premium product. The broadband constraint is a timing risk, not a fatal flaw, as adoption was inevitable.

The missing element is proof of operational readiness. Yet, in 2002, being first to market with a cohesive, premium online ecosystem could define the generation. The risk of not acting (ceding the premium segment) outweighs the operational risk if the company treats this as an existential pivot. The proposal assumes they *can* build the ops; without evidence, this is an assumption. But given the strategic necessity to differentiate from free/fragmented rivals, the "edge" of a unified ecosystem is strong enough to warrant a "Go" if treated as a massive, all-in bet with immediate operational scaling.

**Verdict:** The strategic edge (unified premium experience) is unique and defensible. The operational risk is high but manageable as a primary focus. The cost of delay allows rivals to entrench free models.

DECISION: GO
