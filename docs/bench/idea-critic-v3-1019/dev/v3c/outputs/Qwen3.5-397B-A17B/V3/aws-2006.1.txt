Steelman: Leveraging existing, underutilized infrastructure to create a high-margin revenue stream that diversifies beyond low-margin retail, turning a cost center into a profit center despite market uncertainty.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists between the proposal's premises and the stated goal of monetizing spare capacity. The lack of prior experience is a risk, not a proof of failure.

**Risks:** Significant distraction from core retail operations; potential brand damage if reliability fails; unproven market demand for hourly compute in 2006; strong incumbents (IBM, HP, Microsoft) with established developer relationships; investor skepticism regarding further capital intensity.

**Edge:** The retailer possesses massive, spiky internal infrastructure already built for retail peaks. Competitors must build this from scratch; this entity can offer lower prices immediately by selling marginal capacity at near-zero marginal cost. The "distraction" risk is mitigated if the team is distinct, as proposed.

**Analysis:**
The "do nothing" option leaves expensive infrastructure idle during off-peak times, wasting capital. The "cheaper alternative" (selling only excess capacity informally) lacks the product discipline to capture enterprise value.
While the market is unproven, the unit economics favor the proposer due to sunk infrastructure costs. The primary failure mode is operational culture clash (retail vs. platform reliability), not market absence. The proposal explicitly suggests a distinct business line, addressing the culture risk.
Investor concern on capital spending is valid, but this model improves asset utilization, potentially lowering the overall cost of capital if successful. The "unproven" nature of the market is the very opportunity; waiting for proof cedes the first-mover advantage to those willing to experiment.
The edge (existing infrastructure + distinct team) outweighs the risks (execution difficulty), provided the distinct separation is strictly enforced.

**Kill Criteria:** If the new unit cannot achieve 99.9% uptime within 18 months, or if retail operational metrics drop by >5% due to resource contention, pivot or shut down.

**Decision:** The proposal survives the pre-mortem. The specific advantage of monetizing sunk costs in a nascent market justifies the execution risk.

DECISION: GO
