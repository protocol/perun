**Steelman:** Rapidly convert venture capital into dominant market share and infrastructure before competitors lock out the category, accepting deep short-term losses to secure a monopoly position for future profitability.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Negative Unit Economics:** Selling products below cost while shipping heavy, low-margin items creates a scenario where every additional sale increases total losses. Scaling this model via national TV and new warehouses accelerates cash burn without a path to positive gross margin.
2.  **Capital Market Timing:** The proposal assumes proceeds from an IPO will fund this expansion. However, the admission that "capital markets could close" combined with negative margins makes an successful IPO highly improbable; investors rarely fund proven money-losing scales without a clear margin correction plan.
3.  **Acquisition Logic:** Buying a largest competitor while engaging in a price war is redundant if the goal is market share via loss-leading; it adds integration risk and debt without fixing the core unit economic flaw.

**Risks:**
*   Competitors with deeper pockets can sustain the price war longer.
*   Irreversible infrastructure spend (warehouses) becomes stranded assets if funding dries up.
*   Brand perception may solidify around "cheap" rather than "valuable," making future price hikes impossible.

**Edge:**
No valid edge is presented. Being first or largest is irrelevant if the underlying business model loses money on every transaction. Speed cannot compensate for a broken engine.

**Decision Analysis:**
The proposal fails the fundamental test of viability: it plans to scale a negative margin business using capital that is at high risk of being unavailable. The "edge" of speed is negated by the certainty of accelerated insolvency. The risks are not merely potential; the negative gross margin is a stated fact that guarantees failure under the proposed strategy. There is no "go-if" condition that can salvage this specific combination of actions (IPO + below-cost pricing + heavy expansion) because the math does not work even if the IPO succeeds. The only viable path is a complete revision of the pricing and unit economics strategy before any expansion.

DECISION: NO-GO
