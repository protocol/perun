**Steelman:** Leverage existing compact mechanics to deliver affordable sporty styling for emerging young buyers, monetizing customization via options and scale via massive advertising.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists between the proposed mechanics and the goal of low pricing; the "cheap" perception is a risk, not a proven blocker, mitigated by the "sporty" styling directive.

**Risks:** Unproven volume demand for sporty compacts; brand dilution from economy parts; high sunk cost if the launch campaign fails to convert. These are inherent to market creation but do not invalidate the logic.

**Edge:** The proposal uniquely combines a verified demographic shift (postwar boom entering market) with a cost-advantaged production base (existing reliable mechanics). Competitors lack either the specific styling focus for this segment or the cost structure to hit the "low base price" while offering customization. The "long hood/short deck" design directly addresses the "dull" reputation of current compacts.

**Analysis:**
1.  **Fit:** Directly targets the stated North Star of capturing young buyers with sporty styling at low cost.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the emerging segment. Building a new platform destroys the cost advantage. The proposed path is the cheapest route to the upside.
3.  **Evidence:** The demographic trend is stated as fact. The mechanical reliability is stated as fact.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** If it fails, it is because the styling did not sufficiently mask the economy roots or the ad spend missed the cultural moment. However, the options list allows price discrimination to recover marketing costs.
5.  **Decision Logic:** The plan beats "do nothing" by addressing a specific, growing demographic with a differentiated product at a margin-friendly cost basis. The risks are managed by the low base cost of the underlying platform and the potential high margin of the options list. The edge (demographic timing + cost structure) is clear.

**Kill Criteria:** If first-year sales do not cover the launch campaign cost within 18 months, pivot to fleet sales or discontinue the convertible. Leading metric: Units sold per advertising dollar spent in Q1.

DECISION: GO
