**Steelman:** A clear cola leverages positive test data to capture demand for a "purer" alternative, diversifying the portfolio before soft-drink growth stalls further.

**Attack:** The core failure mode is category confusion. Cola flavor is psychologically anchored to dark color; removing the color may signal "diet" or "chemical" rather than "pure," causing immediate rejection after the first novelty purchase. Test markets often overstate demand by attracting curiosity buyers who do not convert to repeat volume. A national launch with a large marketing campaign locks in high fixed costs before proving unit economics. If the product cannibalizes the flagship without bringing in new users, net revenue drops while costs rise. Rivals can copy the formulation quickly, eroding any first-mover advantage if the concept proves viable.

**Better Way:** The proposal ignores the "do nothing" option and cheaper alternatives. A staged rollout in limited regions without a massive national campaign would validate repeat purchase rates at lower risk. Alternatively, licensing the concept or co-branding could test waters without full infrastructure commitment. The current plan bets the brand equity on an unproven psychological shift (color vs. flavor association) without a safety net.

**Fit & Edge:** The strategy aims to counter slowing growth, but the edge is weak. Being the second-largest maker provides distribution, not a unique product advantage. "Purity" is a claim easily replicated by competitors or achieved by existing clear beverages. There is no defensible moat against copycats.

**Blockers:** None explicitly fatal based *only* on provided text, but the risk profile is extreme.
**Risks:** High probability of novelty fade, flagship cannibalization, and consumer rejection due to color-flavor mismatch.
**Edge:** Distribution scale only; no unique product or cost advantage cited.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal demands a national launch with large marketing spend based solely on test market enthusiasm, which is historically unreliable for novelty food/beverage items. It fails to address the fundamental consumer psychology risk (color = flavor) or provide a kill criterion for stopping if repeat rates lag. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed national launch. Without a phased approach or proof of repeat purchase beyond the test phase, the risk of destroying brand equity and capital outweighs the potential upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
