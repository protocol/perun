**Steelman:** Acquiring the agency and platform instantly grants critical scale and technology to challenge the search leader's recent move, preventing permanent market exclusion.

**Attack:** The $6 billion cash price represents a massive premium in a fast-changing market, creating immense goodwill risk if display technology shifts again. More critically, buying an agency directly conflicts with the buyer's existing agency customers, who may flee to competitors to avoid funding a rival's infrastructure. The "fast-changing" nature of the sector suggests the acquired tech could be obsolete quickly, making the high fixed cost a fatal anchor. While the competitor's move creates urgency, paying a premium for a conflicting asset in a volatile market fails the "cheaper/safer alternative" test; building or partnering avoids the customer conflict and reduces upfront exposure.

**Fit:** This moves the north star of "competing in display," but the method undermines the existing business base (agency clients).

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 2009: The acquired agency clients left immediately due to conflict, revenue collapsed, and the platform tech was superseded by a new standard, leaving the company with $6B less cash and no market share gain.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits "conflicts with existing agency customers" and "fast-changing technology." These are not risks; they are stated facts that contradict the viability of the purchase as proposed.

**Blockers:** 
1. Stated conflict with existing agency customers threatens core revenue without a mitigation plan.
2. Stated "fast-changing market" combined with a "large premium" creates a high probability of immediate asset impairment.

**Risks:** Technology obsolescence, integration failure, culture clash.

**Edge:** None stated beyond reacting to a competitor; being first or big is not an edge here given the conflicts.

**Decision:** No-go. The stated conflicts and market volatility, combined with the massive premium, create a blocker that the proposal does not answer. A smaller, non-conflicting technology purchase or organic build is the safer path to the same upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
