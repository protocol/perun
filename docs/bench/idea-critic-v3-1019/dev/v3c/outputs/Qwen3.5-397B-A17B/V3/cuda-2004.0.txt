**Steelman:** Embedding general-purpose compute into mass-market GPUs creates a ubiquitous, low-cost supercomputing fabric, unlocking scientific markets that CPUs cannot serve efficiently, securing a decade-long architectural lead over rivals focused solely on graphics.

**Attack & Options:**
The proposal fails the immediate financial test: it raises unit costs for a nonexistent market while distracting engineering from the existential war against the primary rival in gaming. The "do nothing" option preserves margin and focus; the "wait" option allows the company to adopt programmability only after academic experimentation proves a paying customer base exists. History suggests niche academic demand rarely justifies silicon-wide tax without early enterprise anchors.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This contradicts the stated core business (gaming/workstation graphics). If executed, the most likely failure mode is a "hollow victory": the hardware ships, but the software ecosystem remains too difficult for scientists, leaving the company with higher costs, slower graphics performance due to area trade-offs, and no new revenue. The cost of delay is low; the cost of premature commitment is total margin erosion during a competitive crunch.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
No large paying market exists; users are currently "hacking" APIs, indicating high friction and low willingness to pay. There are no committed customers to underwrite the R&D. The proposal relies on the assumption that "if we build it, they will come," which is a blocker when core margins are threatened. There is no named edge (e.g., a specific algorithm only this architecture can run) that guarantees winnability against CPU clusters or future specialized accelerators.

**Decision:**
This is a one-way door with massive sunk costs and no verified demand. The risks (cost, distraction, niche demand) are confirmed facts, not hypotheticals. The "edge" is speculative. A sound approach would be a capped, time-boxed experiment on a single high-end SKU, not a mass-market mandate. As proposed, the plan lacks a kill criterion, a leading metric, and a verified path to ROI. It bets the core business on a phantom market.

DECISION: NO-GO
