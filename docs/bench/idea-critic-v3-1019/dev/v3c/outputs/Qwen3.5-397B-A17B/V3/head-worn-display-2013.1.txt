**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 validates demand for a head-worn display while mitigating mass-market backlash, positioning the device as an everyday consumer product despite known privacy and utility gaps.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "everyday consumer" positioning test against its own constraints. A $1,500 price point excludes the mass market required to normalize social acceptance, yet the privacy risks and lack of clear everyday use cases are inherent to the form factor, not the price tier. The "invitation-only" strategy creates an elite early-adopter class that heightens social resentment rather than building broad acceptance. Crucially, the proposal admits "no clear everyday use for most people" yet plans to position it as an "everyday consumer product." This is a direct contradiction: you cannot market a solution to a non-existent problem as a daily necessity. The risks (privacy, battery, social stigma) are not solvable by an invitation list; they are fundamental product flaws that will cause the early adopters—the very group needed to champion the device—to reject it once the novelty fades. The "cheaper alternative" is to delay the consumer launch until a killer app exists, rather than forcing a premium hardware sale on a confused market.

**Blockers:** The stated fact that there is "no clear everyday use for most people" directly contradicts the core claim that it can be positioned as an "everyday consumer product." Without a defined use case, the $1,500 price is unjustifiable, and the privacy invasion becomes purely extractive with no reciprocal value.

**Risks:** Social backlash, privacy regulation, high return rates due to utility mismatch.

**Edge:** None identified. Being first with a camera in public without a clear use case is a liability, not an asset.

**Kill Criteria:** If early adopters cannot articulate a daily use case within 30 days, the product fails.

**Decision:** The plan commits to a mass-market narrative ("everyday consumer") while admitting the product lacks the fundamental requirement for that narrative (everyday use). This guarantees market rejection and brand damage.

DECISION: NO-GO
