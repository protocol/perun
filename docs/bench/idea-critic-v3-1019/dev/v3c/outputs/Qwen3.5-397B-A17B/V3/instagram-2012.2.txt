**Steelman:** Acquiring the category-defining mobile photo leader neutralizes an existential threat to the buyer's core business and instantly secures mobile dominance for $1B, a premium justified by strategic necessity rather than current revenue.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker is valuation: $1B for zero revenue and ~13 employees implies a massive multiple on future potential that may never materialize. However, the "Do Nothing" option carries a higher risk: allowing a competitor to own the fastest-growing mobile engagement layer could cannibalize the buyer's desktop ad monopoly. The alternative of building a competing product internally has already failed (the buyer's "weak mobile product").

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the north star of organizing the world's information via social connection. If this fails, it will be due to cultural assimilation destroying the very innovation that made the target valuable, or regulatory blocks. The cost of delay is likely catastrophic; waiting six months could see the target's valuation double or a rival bidder emerge.

**Evidence & Edge:**
The evidence is the target's explosive user growth on a single platform and successful cross-platform expansion. The buyer's edge is unparalleled distribution and capital to scale the infrastructure immediately. While the business model is unproven, the *attention* model is verified by user metrics.

**Blockers:** None. The lack of revenue is a feature of the stage, not a flaw in the asset. The cultural risk is manageable via operational independence.
**Risks:** Integration friction, regulatory scrutiny, overpayment if growth stalls.
**Edge:** Only this buyer can monetize this specific user base at scale immediately upon integration.

**Decision:**
This is a two-way door in strategy but a one-way door in market positioning. The cost of losing the mobile transition outweighs the financial risk of overpaying. The acquisition secures the ecosystem against fragmentation.

DECISION: GO
