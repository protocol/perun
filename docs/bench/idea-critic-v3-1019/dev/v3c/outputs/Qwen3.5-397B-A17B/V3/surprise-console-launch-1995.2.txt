**Steelman:** Launching in May 1995 secures a critical four-month head start to establish market presence and mindshare before the competitor arrives, accepting limited inventory and higher price as the cost of being first.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "edge" test. Being first provides no lasting advantage if the product is fundamentally inferior at launch.
1.  **Product-Market Mismatch:** Shipping with "few games" and a machine "hard to program" guarantees a negative user experience. Early adopters will buy, play the sparse library, and reject the hardware before the competitor arrives. This burns brand equity rather than building it.
2.  **Channel Alienation:** Shipping to "a few retailers" while excluding others invites immediate retaliation from major partners who will prioritize the competitor's full launch in September.
3.  **Price/Value Gap:** At $399 versus the rival's $299, the console demands a superior library to justify the premium. The proposal explicitly admits the library will be weak. The math does not work: High Price + Low Content = Market Rejection.
4.  **Developer Friction:** Forcing an early date on unprepared developers results in low-quality launch titles, reinforcing the perception of a weak platform.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing (Wait for September):** Launches with a fuller library, unified retail support, and competitive pricing. Risks losing the "first" headline but gains a viable product.
*   **Limited "Developer" Release:** Distribute units only to press and developers in May without retail sales. This generates buzz without alienating retailers or disappointing paying customers with an empty store shelf.

**Verdict:**
The plan creates a "one-way door" disaster: once retailers are angered and early users disappointed by a high-priced, game-less console, the September competitor will crush the weakened launch. There is no blocker that prevents waiting, and the stated facts (high price, few games, angry retailers) constitute a concrete failure scenario. The "edge" of being first is negated by the inability to retain customers.

**Kill Criteria:** If the launch cannot support a minimum viable library of 10 strong titles and 80% retail distribution, the launch date must slip.

DECISION: NO-GO
