The claim is that replacing a discount-driven model with everyday low pricing (EDLP) and branded shops will reverse falling sales for a mid-market department store whose customers are explicitly trained to wait for coupons.

**Steelman:** The current promo cycle erodes margins and brand value; a clean, simplified pricing strategy modeled after successful electronics retailing will restore trust, increase basket size, and attract new shoppers tired of game-playing.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the specific behavioral conditioning of the existing base. These customers do not just *like* discounts; their purchase timing is entirely dictated by them. Removing coupons without a transition period or pilot effectively tells the core revenue source to stop shopping immediately. The "electronics store" success is a false analogy: electronics buyers expect stable prices on commodities, whereas this demographic views department store goods as discretionary purchases only justified by a "deal."

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Continue margin erosion but maintain traffic volume.
2.  **Pilot Program:** Test EDLP in 10% of stores to measure churn vs. margin gain before a full rollout.
3.  **Hybrid Transition:** Gradually reduce coupon frequency while introducing "fair price" markers, allowing customers to adapt over 12–18 months.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, same-store sales collapse by 25%. The core demographic vanishes, having migrated to competitors offering deep discounts. New customers fail to materialize because the "branded shop" concept lacks differentiation against big-box retailers, and the store is now perceived as "expensive" rather than "fair." The lack of a pilot means the failure is chain-wide, leaving no cash reserves to pivot back.

**Blockers:** The proposal admits "most sales have historically come from items sold at a discount" yet offers no evidence that this specific customer base will accept non-discounted pricing. The absence of a pilot for a strategy that contradicts the primary historical revenue driver is a fatal flaw. The risk is not theoretical; it is a direct conflict with verified customer behavior.

**Edge:** None identified. The CEO's past success in a different category (electronics) does not transfer to a demographic dependent on promotional psychology in soft goods.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot cannot be run, the risk of total chain-wide failure outweighs the potential margin upside. The plan as proposed guarantees a shock to the system with no safety valve.

DECISION: NO-GO
