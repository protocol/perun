**Steelman:** Building a massively larger, convertible wide-body jet secures the dominant manufacturer's future against capacity constraints and potential supersonic displacement, leveraging a single anchor customer to justify unprecedented capital expenditure.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces existential financial risk; the development and factory costs dwarf all prior company history, threatening bankruptcy if the market shifts. The reliance on an unproven high-bypass engine introduces technical failure modes that could ground the fleet. Furthermore, the industry consensus predicts supersonic travel will render large subsonic jets obsolete, potentially stranding this asset before it pays off. Currently, only one airline has committed, creating dangerous single-customer dependency.

However, the "do nothing" alternative is worse. If air travel grows as projected, current fleets cannot meet demand efficiently, ceding the market to competitors or limiting industry growth. The supersonic threat is speculative and likely limited to niche routes due to sonic booms and operating costs, leaving the subsonic high-volume market open. The raised cockpit design for freighter conversion is a critical strategic hedge: if passenger demand wanes, the asset retains value in cargo, mitigating the obsolescence risk. The anchor customer's request signals immediate, validated demand for lower seat-mile costs, which only scale can achieve.

**Blockers:** None. The financial scale is terrifying but necessary to maintain dominance; the technical risks are manageable through the supplier partnership; the supersonic threat is unlikely to cannibalize the entire high-volume market.
**Risks:** Catastrophic cost overrun, engine failure, slower-than-expected adoption by other airlines.
**Edge:** First-mover advantage in high-capacity efficiency, convertible design flexibility, and a locked-in launch customer providing immediate revenue visibility.

The potential upside of defining the next century of air travel outweighs the risks, provided the company accepts that failure means extinction, but success means monopoly. The convertible feature specifically neutralizes the strongest objection regarding supersonic displacement.

DECISION: GO
