Steelman: Launching early in May 1995 at $399 secures a four-month head start to establish market presence before the competitor arrives, aiming to capture early adopters and retailer shelf space despite limited software.

**Blockers:**
*   **Developer Alienation:** The proposal admits developers "did not plan for the early date." Launching without games guarantees a barren library, punishing the very partners needed for long-term success and likely causing them to prioritize the rival's better-supported platform.
*   **Retailer Retaliation:** Shipping to "only a few retailers" explicitly invites retaliation from excluded major chains. In 1995, console success relied entirely on broad retail distribution; alienating key gatekeepers before the main launch is a fatal strategic error.
*   **Price Disadvantage:** At $399, the unit is $100 (33%) more expensive than the rival. Without a superior game library to justify the premium (which the proposal admits is missing), price-sensitive consumers will wait for the cheaper, better-supported competitor.

**Risks:**
*   Hardware complexity for 3D combined with rushed timelines increases the likelihood of buggy launch units, damaging brand reputation immediately.
*   A "soft launch" with high prices and no games may generate negative word-of-mouth that poisons the well for the full September release.

**Edge:**
*   The only potential edge is being first to market. However, being first with an overpriced product and no software is not an advantage; it is a liability that hands the narrative to the competitor.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal fails the "must beat do nothing" test. Waiting until September allows time to secure developer support, finalize the full retail distribution network, and potentially adjust pricing strategy. The current plan actively destroys relationships with developers and retailers while offering consumers no value proposition other than "available now" for a high price with no games. The risks are not merely executional; they are structural flaws that guarantee a failed launch window and compromised long-term position. There is no scenario where alienating your supply chain (developers) and distribution channel (retailers) simultaneously leads to success.

DECISION: NO-GO
