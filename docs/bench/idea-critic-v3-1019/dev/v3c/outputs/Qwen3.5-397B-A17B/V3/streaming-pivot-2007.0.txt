**Steelman:** Leverage the existing seven-million-subscriber base and cash flow from the profitable DVD business to seed a streaming platform now, accepting initial losses, limited catalog, and technical imperfections to secure early market positioning before dominant tech giants lock out independent players.

**Analysis:**
The proposal faces severe headwinds: high licensing costs, poor video quality, limited broadband penetration, and an inferior content library compared to future entrants. Cannibalization of the core DVD revenue stream is a near-certainty. However, the "do nothing" alternative carries a fatal flaw: it cedes the digital transition entirely to well-funded technology and retail competitors who will eventually make physical media obsolete. Waiting for perfect conditions (universal broadband, HD quality, cheap rights) ensures the company arrives too late to compete.

The strategic edge is not the current streaming product, which is weak, but the proprietary relationship with seven million paying customers. This installed base provides immediate distribution and data that new entrants lack. By bundling streaming at no extra charge, the company converts a potential churn risk into a retention tool while learning the streaming economics at a manageable scale. The high licensing costs and technical debt are acceptable "tuition" for entering the market early.

**Blockers:** None. The financials rely on the existing profitable DVD business subsidizing the transition, which is explicitly stated as current reality.
**Risks:** Accelerated erosion of DVD margins before streaming becomes profitable; inability to secure future content rights against deeper-pocketed rivals.
**Edge:** Direct access to seven million subscribers and a profitable core business to fund the pivot, unlike pure-play tech entrants who must buy customers.

**Decision Logic:**
This is a two-way door experiment with one-way door consequences if delayed. The cost of delay is total market irrelevance. While the current streaming offering is poor, it is the only viable path to evolve the business model before the core asset (DVDs) becomes stranded. The risks are real but manageable compared to the existential threat of inaction. The proposal correctly identifies the need to move despite imperfections.

DECISION: GO
