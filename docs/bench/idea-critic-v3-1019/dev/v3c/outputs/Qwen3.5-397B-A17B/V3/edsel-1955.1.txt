**Steelman:** Launching a distinct mid-priced division captures a growing market segment, leverages existing manufacturing scale, and prevents customer defection to competitors by offering a branded upgrade path within the corporate family.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on the assumption that current market growth will persist through the 1957 debut. However, the mid-priced segment is historically the first to contract during economic downturns, and a recession risk is explicitly acknowledged. Launching a capital-intensive venture requiring a new dealer network exactly when consumer discretionary spending may collapse creates a fatal mismatch between fixed costs and revenue potential. Furthermore, "extensive research" often reflects past desires, not future purchasing power. The cost of establishing a new dealer network is irreversible and creates a massive overhead burden before a single unit is sold. If the economy turns, the company will be stuck with high fixed costs, unsold inventory, and underutilized dealers, threatening the financial stability of the entire corporation. The "perceived gap" may be a value trap where customers either trade down to reliable low-priced models or hold existing vehicles rather than trade up in uncertainty.

**Alternatives:** 
1. **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital and wait for economic clarity; risk losing share but avoid catastrophic loss.
2. **Badge Engineering:** Introduce mid-priced models under existing brands/dealers to test demand without new infrastructure.
3. **Delayed Launch:** Postpone debut until recession risks clear, using the time to refine the product.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the blocker test. Proceeding with a massive, irreversible infrastructure investment (new dealers) and a full brand launch immediately prior to a potential recession, based on extrapolated growth trends, ignores the cyclicality of the auto market. The downside risk (corporate financial distress) vastly outweighs the upside (segment share) given the timing. The proposal lacks a contingency for immediate economic contraction or a phased entry strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
