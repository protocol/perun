**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men avoiding full-sugar drinks but rejecting the taste of diet cola, leveraging a premium price to signal innovation before the low-carb trend fades.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "satisfy neither group" test. Core cola consumers demand full sugar for taste; diet consumers demand zero calories for health. A half-sugar product offers inferior taste to the former and insufficient calorie reduction to the latter, creating a product with no distinct value proposition. The "young men" segment is not a monolith that ignores taste or calorie counts in favor of a middle ground. Furthermore, pricing a potentially compromised taste profile at a premium increases the barrier to trial for a product that likely delivers a worse experience than the established market leaders (full sugar or zero). The rival's similar launch suggests a race to the bottom in a niche that may not exist, rather than validation of demand. If the low-carb trend is a fad, the window for ROI is too narrow to justify a large campaign and new SKU complexity.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain share in growing diet and stable full-sugar segments without risking brand dilution.
2.  **Reformulate Existing Diet:** Improve the taste profile of the existing diet cola to attract taste-sensitive dieters without confusing the portfolio.
3.  **Limited Test:** Run a small regional test before a large campaign (not proposed).

**Fit:** Does not clearly move a north star of volume or profit given the high risk of cannibalization and low probability of net-new adoption.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, young men try it once due to marketing, find the taste "off" compared to regular cola and the calorie count too high compared to diet, and never repurchase. Inventory is discounted, damaging brand equity.

**Blockers:** The core claim that a half-sugar drink satisfies a unique, large enough need state is unsupported by the stated risks which highlight the dichotomy of consumer preferences (taste vs. calories).
**Risks:** Fad trend, cannibalization, price resistance, confused positioning.
**Edge:** None identified; being first or second to a potentially non-existent market is not an edge.

DECISION: NO-GO
