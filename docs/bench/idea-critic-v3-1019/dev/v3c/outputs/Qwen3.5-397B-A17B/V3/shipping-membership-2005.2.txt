**Steelman:** An upfront $79 fee buys loyalty and frequency, turning shipping from a transactional friction into a fixed-cost advantage that locks out competitors.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is unit economics: if heavy buyers increase frequency by >30% while margins are thin, the retailer bleeds cash on every "loyal" order. However, the alternative—"do nothing"—guarantees continued friction where every repeat purchase triggers a decision tax, capping lifetime value. A "pay-per-use" discount fails to solve the psychological barrier of recurring fees.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a growth-stage strategy prioritizing market share over immediate quarterly margin. If this fails, it will be because the retailer underestimated the elasticity of demand (users ordering unnecessarily just because shipping is "free") or overestimated the retention stickiness. The "hard to reverse" risk is real; cancelling a paid membership triggers immediate churn and reputational damage. Yet, waiting for "proven economics" via small pilots is impossible here; the model only works at scale due to network effects in logistics and purchasing behavior.

**Evidence & Edge:**
No external data exists to verify the 2005 context, but the proposal's edge is structural: it aligns retailer and customer incentives perfectly. The retailer wants volume; the customer wants to maximize their sunk $79. Competitors charging per order cannot match the marginal cost perception of zero. The risk of margin hit is not a blocker but a calculated investment in switching costs. The "unproven" nature is the very reason to act first; being the pioneer creates the category definition.

**Blockers:** NONE. The financial risk is a feature of the model, not a flaw, provided the retailer has the cash runway to absorb the initial margin dip for long-term lock-in.
**Risks:** Over-indexing on low-margin goods; inability to raise prices later.
**Edge:** First-mover advantage in creating a psychological "free shipping" baseline that competitors must match but cannot afford.

**Kill Criteria:** If membership uptake is <5% of active users after 12 months, or if fulfillment costs per member exceed $150 annually, terminate the program.
