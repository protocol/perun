Steelman: Rapidly acquiring 130–150 sites allows immediate scale to undercut the entrenched competitor before they can react, leveraging the retailer's domestic loyalty as a springboard.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The plan requires mastering cross-border logistics, regulation, and bilingual labeling while simultaneously deploying an unproven inventory system and supply chain. Doing all this while opening nearly all stores within two years creates a single point of catastrophic failure: operational collapse. If the new inventory system glitches (highly probable for unproven tech under load) or logistics stall, 150 stores open with empty shelves or compliance fines. The entrenched competitor can easily wait out supply hiccups; the entrant cannot survive them at this scale.

The "pace" is not just a risk; it is a structural flaw. A cheaper, safer alternative exists: open 5–10 pilot stores to validate the supply chain and inventory system in the new regulatory environment before committing capital to 140 more. This staged approach preserves the upside while removing the blocker of unproven operations at scale. The proposal's claim that speed is necessary to win ignores that speed without functional logistics guarantees loss.

Blockers: Unproven inventory system and supply chain deployed at full scale (150 stores) simultaneously with market entry; no pilot phase to validate cross-border logistics or regulatory compliance.
Risks: Entrenched competitor price war, bilingual labeling errors, lease liabilities on empty stores.
Edge: None stated beyond "first expansion"; domestic loyalty does not automatically transfer across borders without operational proof.

The plan commits real money without a capped, time-boxed first step. It bets the entire expansion on systems that have never run in this context. This is a one-way door with a high probability of failure due to execution complexity, not market demand.

DECISION: NO-GO
