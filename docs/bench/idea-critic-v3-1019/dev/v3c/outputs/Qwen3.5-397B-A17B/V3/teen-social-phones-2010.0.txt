**Steelman:** Launching two carrier-locked, data-plan-dependent social phones without apps or games leverages an acquired team's momentum to capture the youth market before the main platform arrives, despite higher unit costs.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" and "fit" tests immediately.
1.  **Value Mismatch:** The target demographic is price-sensitive, yet the requirement for a full smartphone data plan makes the total cost of ownership equal to a full smartphone. Without an app store or games, the device offers less utility than the competing smartphone at the same price point.
2.  **Cannibalization & Timing:** Launching this niche product shortly before the company's main new platform creates internal competition and confuses the market narrative.
3.  **Execution Risk:** The history of conflict between the core team and the acquired team, combined with previous delays due to software switches, suggests high operational fragility. Removing apps/games removes the primary driver of data usage, undermining the carrier's incentive to support the plan.
4.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" option (waiting for the main platform) or a "software-only" approach (an app on existing phones) avoids hardware risk, carrier dependency, and channel conflict while reaching the same users cheaper.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Viability:** No evidence provided that price-sensitive youth will pay smartphone rates for a feature-phone experience.
*   **Strategic Fit:** Launching a limited device right before a major platform launch dilutes marketing focus and risks cannibalizing the flagship release.
*   **Operational Capacity:** Known team conflict and prior technical delays indicate a high probability of missing the launch window again.

**Edge:** None identified. Being "first" with a limited device is not an edge if the value proposition is inferior to existing smartphones.

**Verdict:** The plan requires customers to pay a premium price for a degraded experience (no apps/games) while creating internal strategic conflict. The "do nothing" or "software-first" alternatives achieve the goal with lower risk and cost. The blockers regarding price sensitivity and strategic timing are fatal to the proposal as written.

DECISION: NO-GO
