**Steelman:** A hybrid device leverages existing IP to escape the losing home-console war and the eroding handheld market by creating a unique, portable home experience that rivals cannot match.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly accepts modest specs and a thin launch lineup as constraints, not hidden variables. The price parity with superior hardware is a strategic gamble on uniqueness, not a math error.

**Risks:** Third-party abandonment is real given the power gap; consumer confusion remains high after the previous failure; supply chains for a novel form factor are unproven. However, these are execution risks, not logical contradictions. The "do nothing" alternative guarantees continued financial bleeding from two failing product lines.

**Edge:** The maker possesses the world's strongest first-party franchises. Competitors compete on teraflops; this plan competes on *where* and *how* games are played. No rival offers a seamless transition between TV and handheld. This uniqueness justifies the price despite lower raw power, provided the software quality is high.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the "hybrid" message is muddled or if the battery life renders handheld mode unusable. The proposal's risk list acknowledges positioning and supply, showing awareness.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders do not cover initial manufacturing costs by launch month, or if attach rate of first-party software falls below 1.5 units per console in Q1, the strategy must pivot to aggressive price cuts or software bundling.

**Decision Logic:** The status quo is financial decline. The hardware disadvantage is neutralized by the form-factor monopoly. The edge (IP + unique utility) outweighs the spec deficit. The risks are manageable compared to the certainty of loss by staying the course. The proposal survives the attack because the alternative is surrender.

DECISION: GO
