**Steelman:** Acquire the leading mobile photo threat at $1B to instantly secure mobile dominance, neutralize a competitor before IPO leverage increases, and import critical mobile talent/culture that internal teams cannot replicate quickly enough.

**Analysis:**
The valuation is extreme relative to revenue (zero), but standard revenue multiples fail for network-effect assets where user growth and engagement define future monopoly power. Waiting for a business model or revenue proof risks the target going public, raising capital, and becoming an entrenched, independent rival with higher acquisition costs later. The "do nothing" option preserves cash but accepts a high probability of losing the mobile photo war to a focused, faster competitor.

The primary blocker is cultural destruction; if the acquirer's management absorbs and stifles the target, the asset's value (talent and product velocity) evaporates. However, this is a mitigation issue, not a fundamental flaw in the acquisition thesis. Regulatory review is a delay risk, not a deal-killer for a vertical acquisition of this size in 2012. The lack of a business model is irrelevant if the strategic goal is defensive dominance and user acquisition rather than immediate ROI.

**Edge:** The buyer has the cash/stock liquidity to pay a premium that no other bidder can match, and the urgent need to stop a specific, proven threat to its core business. The target has the user momentum but lacks the scale to monetize or defend against a copycat war indefinitely.

**Kill Criteria:** If the target's daily active users decline by >20% within 6 months post-acquisition, or if key founding engineers depart within 3 months, the strategic value is compromised, triggering a review of integration tactics.

**Verdict:** The cost of delay (competitor IPO, entrenched rivalry) exceeds the financial risk of overpayment. The strategic necessity of owning the mobile layer justifies the premium.

DECISION: GO
