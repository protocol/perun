**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant video platform eliminates the buyer's smaller, failing video effort, instantly captures the market leader, and bets that scale will solve monetization and legal hurdles faster than organic growth.

**Attack:** The price ($1.65B) is astronomical for a company with near-zero revenue and massive operating losses (bandwidth). The core asset (user uploads) is legally toxic; pending lawsuits from major media companies could result in injunctions shutting the site down or damages exceeding the purchase price. The "ad model" is unproven at this scale; bandwidth costs may outpace any possible ad revenue. The buyer's own smaller service suggests they already struggle to make video profitable.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Continue building the smaller internal service; avoid massive debt/equity dilution and legal liability.
2. *License Content:* Partner with media companies for legitimate content, avoiding copyright chaos, though lacking user-generated volume.
3. *Wait:* Let the legal clouds burst; acquire assets cheaper post-litigation or let the market correct.

**Fit:** Aligns with organizing world's information, but the financial and legal risk threatens the entire corporation's stability.

**Pre-mortem:** In 24 months, lawsuits force a payout larger than the acquisition cost, bandwidth bills bankrupt the unit, and advertisers flee due to brand safety issues around pirated content. The stock price collapses.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Legal Existence Risk:** Copyright infringement is not a "risk" here; it is the site's primary function. If courts rule against fair use (likely in 2006 context), the business model is illegal.
2. **Unit Economics:** No path to profitability is demonstrated; bandwidth costs per view likely exceed ad CPMs.
3. **Valuation:** Paying $1.65B for negative cash flow and massive contingent liability violates basic fiduciary duty without a guaranteed legal shield.

**Edge:** None stated. Being "largest" means nothing if the content must be removed.

**Decision:** The proposal lacks a viable solution to the existential legal threat and demonstrates no unit economics. The potential liability exceeds the purchase price.

DECISION: NO-GO
