**Steelman:** A search company can disrupt mature email by offering 100x storage (1GB vs MBs) and superior search, monetizing via contextual ads, using invitation-only growth to manage costs and generate demand.

**Attack & Analysis:**
*   **Cost Blocker?** In 2004, storage costs are plummeting. While 1GB/user seems high, the marginal cost is low enough if ad revenue per active user exceeds it. The "routine deletion" behavior of users on other platforms proves the constraint is artificial, not a user need. Removing it unlocks value.
*   **Privacy Risk:** Scanning mail for ads is a genuine friction point. However, users have already accepted free services funded by ads (e.g., search, portals). The trade-off (privacy for massive utility/free cost) has precedent. The risk is reputational, not fatal, provided transparency exists.
*   **Market Entry:** Incumbents are vulnerable because they compete on features users don't need (more folders) rather than the core utility: finding old messages. Their business models rely on storage scarcity; attacking this weakness is the only viable entry vector.
*   **Revenue Delay:** No direct revenue at launch is acceptable given the invitation-only model. This caps initial infrastructure spend while creating artificial scarcity (marketing hype). The "no revenue" critique ignores the customer acquisition cost savings of viral invites.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if ad targeting accuracy is too low to cover storage, or if a privacy backlash becomes regulatory. However, the search company's core competency is ad matching, making the first unlikely. The second is a manageable PR challenge compared to the product advantage.

**Edge:** Only a search company possesses the indexing technology to make 1GB usable and the ad infrastructure to monetize it immediately upon scale. Competitors cannot match the search speed or ad relevance without pivoting their entire architecture.

**Verdict:** The proposal attacks a specific incumbent weakness (storage scarcity) with a unique asset (search/ad tech). The risks are known and manageable; the "do nothing" option cedes the future of communication data to rivals. The invitation model effectively gates the financial risk.

DECISION: GO
