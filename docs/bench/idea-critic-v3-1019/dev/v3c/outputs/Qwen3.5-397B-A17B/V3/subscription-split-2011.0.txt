**Steelman:** Separating the declining DVD unit from high-growth streaming allows distinct strategies, prevents the legacy cost structure from dragging down the streaming valuation, and isolates brand damage from the price hike.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the immediate context: a 60% price increase just occurred. Splitting the billing and brands now compounds the friction. Customers facing a massive hike will perceive a second bill and a forced new account not as strategic clarity, but as a "double penalty" and an attempt to hide the true cost of their combined usage. The loss of shared recommendations devalues the bundle further, removing the only remaining synergy. The "confusion" risk is understated; this looks like a breakup forced on angry users. The rationale cites "different management," which can be achieved internally without fracturing the customer experience during a pricing crisis. The edge (strategic freedom) does not outweigh the blocker: executing a structural separation immediately after a aggressive price hike creates a compounding negative event that likely triggers mass churn. The market has not absorbed the first shock; adding a second operational fracture is premature.

**Blockers:** 
1. Timing: Implementing a complex billing/account split immediately following a 60% price hike creates compounded customer friction and perceived hostility, likely accelerating churn beyond models predicting standard decline.
2. Value Destruction: Severing data sharing eliminates the cross-service recommendation engine, reducing the perceived value of holding both subscriptions precisely when customers are most price-sensitive.

**Risks:** Brand fragmentation, increased support costs for two systems, user fatigue from dual logins.

**Edge:** None identified that cannot be achieved via internal organizational restructuring without customer-facing separation at this specific moment.

**Kill Criteria:** If customer churn exceeds 5% in the first month post-split or support tickets regarding billing confusion exceed 10% of the active base, the separation must be paused and billing re-integrated.

**Decision:** The strategic logic of separation may be sound long-term, but the tactical execution timing relative to the price hike is fatal. The proposal fails to account for the cumulative burden on the customer.

DECISION: NO-GO
