**Steelman:** A $180 tabletop stereoscopic 3D console using red monochrome displays bridges the gap between handheld and home generations, leveraging existing IP while the next home console matures.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The proposal fails the "better-way" and "fit" tests. The "gap" it fills is a value void, not a market opportunity.
1.  **Product-Market Mismatch:** Red-on-black visuals are a severe downgrade from contemporary color handhelds and home TVs. "Neither portable nor comfortable" destroys the handheld value prop; requiring an eyepiece while tabletop isolates the user, preventing the social play central to home consoles.
2.  **Health Liability:** Documented risks of eye strain and headaches are not mere "risks" but fundamental flaws in the core mechanism (stereoscopy via eyepiece without head tracking or full color). This invites returns and brand damage.
3.  **Content Vacuum:** "Few games at launch" guarantees low attachment rates. Without a killer app, the hardware is a gimmick.
4.  **Cannibalization & Obsolescence:** With a true next-gen home console due in two years, this device competes for internal resources and consumer wallet share, only to be rendered obsolete by the very system it was meant to bridge.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital and brand reputation. Focus resources on the imminent home console and existing successful handhelds.
*   **Software Bridge:** Release enhanced games for current hardware rather than fragmenting the ecosystem with inferior hardware.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The hardware causes physical discomfort (headaches/strain) and offers a visually inferior experience (red monochrome) compared to 1995 standards.
*   **Fact:** The form factor satisfies neither portability nor social home-play needs.
*   **Fact:** Lack of launch software ensures commercial failure.

**Edge:** None. Being first to market with uncomfortable, monochrome 3D is not an advantage; it is a liability. Cost savings ($180) do not offset a broken user experience.

**Verdict:** The proposal attempts to solve a timing problem with a defective product. The risks are confirmed flaws, not uncertainties. The "bridge" leads to a dead end that damages the brand before the real next generation arrives.

DECISION: NO-GO
