**Steelman:** A premium, high-capacity device restricted to a niche ecosystem can succeed if it offers a user experience so superior that it justifies the price and creates a gateway to the ecosystem, rather than competing on specs alone.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" and "addressable base" tests on current facts. Cheaper players exist but suffer from poor usability and low capacity; however, this proposal compounds the problem by restricting software compatibility to a small-share computer platform. In early 2001, legal downloads are non-existent, meaning the device relies on users ripping CDs. While the 5GB drive is a technical advantage, the $400 price point targets a mass market that currently rejects digital music due to complexity and cost.

The critical blocker is the **distribution constraint**. Limiting the device to owners of a small-share computer excludes the vast majority of potential early adopters (Windows users). Without a cross-platform solution, the total addressable market is too small to sustain the supply chain risks or justify the premium component costs. The "ecosystem lock-in" strategy is premature before the ecosystem itself has proven demand for digital media.

Furthermore, the "one-way door" risk is high: sourcing custom components for a short timeline without a guaranteed volume base threatens cash flow. If the device flops (likely given the compatibility wall), the hardware investment is sunk with no secondary market.

**Edge:** The only potential edge is seamless integration, but this is nullified by the lack of a legal music source and the platform restriction. Being first to 5GB is not an edge if the software barrier prevents usage.

**Blockers:**
1. **Market Size:** Compatible computer base is too small to support the volume needed for the $400 price point.
2. **Content Gap:** No legal download infrastructure exists to drive hardware urgency.
3. **Platform Exclusion:** Ignoring the dominant OS (Windows) artificially caps adoption.

**Risks:** Supply chain overextension; consumer rejection of high price without content ecosystem.

**Verdict:** The plan as proposed ignores the fundamental mismatch between the high price, the tiny compatible user base, and the lack of content. It must be revised to include cross-platform support and a content strategy before proceeding.

DECISION: NO-GO
