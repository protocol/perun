**Steelman:** A complete rewrite with a publisher-focused algorithm and forced migration is the only way to reverse traffic decline and modernize the platform before a rival captures the market.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts within the text. First, the core community explicitly values the existing voting mechanics; changing these to favor publishers directly attacks the primary asset (loyal users) causing the very traffic loss it aims to fix. Second, a "move all users at once" strategy with "no rollback" creates a single point of failure; if the new algorithm repels users, the damage is immediate and irreversible. Third, while a rival is growing, responding with maximum engineering risk and maximum user alienation is a strategic mismatch. The proposal offers no evidence that publishers drive more value than the existing community, nor does it provide a kill criterion or pilot plan to validate the new sorting logic.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" baseline regarding the voting mechanism is safer than active alienation. A staged approach (A/B testing the new surface on a small segment) would validate the publisher hypothesis without risking the entire user base. A full rewrite is unnecessary to test algorithmic changes; these can often be layered onto existing infrastructure.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch occurs, long-time users revolt over the changed voting dynamics, traffic plummets further due to the lack of rollback, and the engineering team is stuck maintaining a broken new codebase while the rival site absorbs the displaced community.

**Blockers:** 
1. The plan removes the specific feature (existing voting mechanics) identified as the community's core value.
2. The "all at once" migration eliminates the ability to recover from failure.
3. No pilot or metric is defined to prove publisher content improves traffic before total commitment.

**Edge:** None stated; fear of a rival is not an edge.

**Kill Criteria:** Without a phased rollout plan and validation that publisher content increases retention among current users, the risk of total community collapse is certain.

DECISION: NO-GO
