**Steelman:** Transforming every consumer chip into a general-purpose compute engine creates a ubiquitous hardware base that, once software matures, unlocks a massive parallel processing market unreachable by CPUs, securing long-term dominance beyond the cyclical gaming sector.

**Attack:** The proposal demands raising unit costs and diverting critical engineering resources from a fierce, existential rivalry in the core gaming market to build a toolchain for a non-existent customer base. The "edge" is purely speculative: academic experimentation with hacked APIs does not prove commercial demand, nor does it guarantee developers will abandon established CPU workflows. The cost is immediate and certain (lower margins, potential loss of gaming share); the benefit is distant and unproven.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain cost leadership in gaming; wait for clear market signals before adding silicon area.
2.  **Targeted High-End:** Add programmable features only to expensive workstation cards where unit cost impact is negligible and scientific users already exist.
3.  **Software-First:** Invest in compilers for existing hardware architectures without altering silicon, validating demand before committing to new tape-outs.

**Pre-mortem:** By 2006, the company has lost significant gaming market share to its rival due to feature delays caused by the compute diversion. The scientific toolchain remains unused by industry because the performance overhead on consumer chips is too high for serious work, and the specialized workstation market was ignored. The company burns cash on university programs with no commercial return, leaving it weaker in its primary revenue stream.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Edge:** There is no verified demand or committed customer base to justify the silicon cost increase on *every* unit.
*   **Strategic Contradiction:** Diverting engineering focus during a "core race" with a main rival threatens the sole current revenue source.
*   **Economic Failure:** Raising unit costs without an immediate price premium or volume guarantee violates basic unit economics.

**Verdict:** This is a classic "boil the ocean" strategy with no beachhead. The risk of collapsing the core business to chase a phantom market is unacceptable without a single paying anchor tenant or a proven performance advantage over CPUs for specific workloads.

DECISION: NO-GO
