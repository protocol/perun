Steelman: Acquiring the dominant cross-platform messaging app at a premium is the only viable defense against the existential threat of mobile displacement, securing the network effect before a competitor does.

**Skeptic**: The price ($19B) represents a >12x increase over the prior $1.5B valuation in just one year, with no revenue to justify the multiple. The target has 50 employees and refuses ads, making monetization purely hypothetical. Founder retention is a single point of failure; if they leave, the asset's value (culture and trust) evaporates. Privacy regulations could fracture the unified global network.

**Better-way**: Alternatives include building a competing messenger (cheaper, but slow and likely too late), acquiring a smaller rival (lower risk, but insufficient scale), or doing nothing (accepting mobile displacement). However, given the "hundreds of millions" of users already entrenched, no smaller alternative reaches the same defensive upside. Doing nothing cedes the mobile future to a competitor who controls the primary communication layer.

**Kill-criteria**: This is a one-way door; once spent, the capital is gone. If the founders depart within 24 months, the acquisition fails. If regulatory bodies force a breakup due to privacy/data silos, the value collapses. The cost of delay is total loss of relevance in mobile messaging.

**Decision Analysis**:
*   **Blockers**: None. The high price is a feature of the scarcity, not a bug; the lack of revenue is typical for infrastructure-level user acquisition at this scale. The fear of displacement is validated by the target's growth.
*   **Edge**: The buyer has the cash/stock to close immediately, which no other entity can match. The target's refusal to monetize proves user loyalty, which is the actual asset being purchased.
*   **Risks**: Massive overpayment, integration failure, regulatory scrutiny. These are managed by locking up founders and operating the app independently.
*   **Verdict**: The strategic necessity of owning the mobile communication layer outweighs the financial absurdity. The cost of losing the mobile transition exceeds $19B.

DECISION: GO
