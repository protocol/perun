**Steelman:** Leveraging the existing seven-million-subscriber base and brand trust to seed a digital distribution channel now, accepting short-term margin pressure and catalogue limitations to secure long-term relevance before incumbents lock out access.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The "do nothing" option preserves current profits but guarantees obsolescence as broadband adoption curves upward and tech giants (Amazon, Apple) normalize instant access. Waiting for quality or catalogue parity cedes the customer relationship to competitors. The "paid add-on" alternative fails because friction reduces adoption velocity; the goal is data and habit formation, not immediate streaming revenue.

**Pre-mortem:** The initiative fails if licensing costs bleed cash faster than digital engagement reduces DVD churn, or if the poor quality experience drives subscribers to cancel entirely rather than hybridize. However, the proposal explicitly accepts these risks as the cost of entry.

**Evidence & Fit:** The core asset is the subscriber relationship, not the physical disc. In 2007, broadband is nascent but inevitable. Studios are hesitant now but will become gatekeepers later; securing rights early, even for older titles, establishes the legal and technical framework required for the future. Cannibalization is a feature, not a bug: it is better to cannibalize your own profitable DVD business with your own streaming service than to have a competitor do it for you.

**Blockers:** None. The financial constraints (high licensing, tech cost) are known hurdles, not show-stoppers, given the company's existing profitability. The strategic risk of large entrants is mitigated only by moving first with an installed base.

**Edge:** Seven million active billing relationships and a recommendation algorithm trained on physical rental data provide a personalization advantage pure-tech entrants lack. This data moat justifies the transition cost.

**Decision:** The plan survives the attack because the cost of delay (irrelevance) exceeds the cost of execution (margin compression). The "go-if" condition is inherent: execute immediately to lock in user habits.

**Kill Criteria:** If streaming adoption does not reach 10% of the subscriber base within 18 months, or if licensing costs exceed 15% of total revenue without correlating to reduced DVD churn, pivot to a licensed-content-only model or spin off the division.

DECISION: GO
