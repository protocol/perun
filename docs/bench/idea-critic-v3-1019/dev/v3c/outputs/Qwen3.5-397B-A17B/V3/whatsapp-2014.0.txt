**Steelman:** Paying $19B secures dominance in mobile messaging, neutralizing an existential threat to the core business by acquiring hundreds of millions of engaged users who have rejected ads, despite the target's minimal revenue and tiny team.

**Attack:** The price represents a >12x increase over the prior valuation in just one year, with no proven monetization path other than a $1 fee the market may not tolerate at scale. The target's explicit refusal to show advertising contradicts the buyer's primary revenue engine, creating a fundamental strategic clash. With only ~50 employees, the asset is entirely human capital; if founders leave, the $19B buys nothing but a fragile app shell. The "existential threat" argument justifies any price, removing financial discipline. However, the cost of delay—losing the entire mobile cohort to a competitor—is potentially infinite if the threat is real.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Risk irrelevance as users migrate; save $19B cash.
2.  **Build/Buy Small:** Attempt to replicate features or buy a smaller rival; likely too slow given the target's network effects.
3.  **Acquire:** Pay the premium to instantly own the network and deny it to rivals.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of universal connectivity but assumes the buyer can integrate a culture that hates ads without destroying the product's value.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if regulators block the deal due to privacy concerns, or if the founders quit post-acquisition, causing user exodus. The one-way door nature of spending $19B cash/stock leaves no room for error.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blocker:** None. The high price and lack of revenue are features of buying a monopoly-in-waiting, not bugs, provided the threat is genuine.
*   **Edge:** Only this buyer has the cash flow and distribution to absorb the loss leader and monetize via the broader ecosystem later.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If monthly active users drop >10% within 12 months post-close, initiate divestiture or pivot.

The fear of displacement outweighs the financial absurdity. In platform wars, the winner takes all; losing the mobile layer is fatal.

DECISION: GO
