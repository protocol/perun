**Steelman:** Leverage dominant mobile distribution to disrupt the handheld market by converging communication and gaming in a single $300 device, accepting hardware compromises to secure first-mover advantage in mobile gaming.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" test. The market leader's handheld is significantly cheaper, and this device compromises core utility (calling awkwardness, battery removal for game swaps) to match features the leader already dominates with a superior library. Publishers are uncommitted, meaning the "huge library" advantage of the incumbent remains unchallenged at launch.

The "edge" cited is merely size and distribution, which are not product advantages if the device is functionally inferior for both primary use cases (calling and gaming). A device that is awkward to hold for calls violates the primary North Star of a phone manufacturer: communication usability. The risk of alienating the core mobile user base for an unproven gaming foothold is a concrete blocker.

The pre-mortem is clear: The device launches, gamers reject it due to high price and poor ergonomics compared to the dedicated leader, while mobile users reject it due to call awkwardness and battery inconvenience. Publishers remain with the incumbent due to low install base. The product becomes a niche failure, damaging the brand's reputation for usability.

There is no "go-if" condition here that can be met now; the fundamental design conflicts with the stated risks (awkwardness, battery swap) which are inherent to the proposed form factor. The "do nothing" or "wait for technology to mature" option is superior, as it avoids launching a compromised product into a hostile market. The lack of publisher commitment is a fatal flaw that cannot be solved by the current plan.

**Blockers:** Core utility (calling) is degraded by gaming features; price is non-competitive against a superior incumbent; no publisher support secured.
**Risks:** Brand damage from poor usability; total market rejection by both gamers and callers.
**Edge:** None identified; distribution alone cannot overcome product inferiority.

DECISION: NO-GO
