**Steelman:** Paying $19B secures the dominant mobile messaging layer to prevent existential displacement of the core business, buying time to integrate a network effect that cannot be built organically.

**Options:**
1. **Do Nothing:** Risk rapid user migration to the target, rendering the buyer's mobile strategy obsolete.
2. **Build/Buy Competitor:** Impossible to replicate hundreds of millions of active users and network effects quickly; other targets lack scale.
3. **Acquire:** Immediate neutralization of the threat and ownership of the primary mobile identity graph.

**Fit:** Aligns with the survival imperative (North Star: maintain relevance in mobile shift). The cost is extreme, but the cost of failure is total irrelevance.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if regulators block the deal, founders leave immediately post-payout, or privacy scandals erupt. However, waiting increases regulatory scrutiny and founder leverage. The "door" is one-way; once the target matures or IPOs, the price becomes infinite (unavailable).

**Evidence & Math:** The $19B price is ~12x the prior valuation, but based on ~450M users, the cost is ~$42/user. For a company facing displacement, the Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) to replace these engaged users organically would exceed this amount over a decade. Revenue is irrelevant; strategic utility is the asset. The $1 fee proves user commitment, not a business model.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal. The price is high, but not factually impossible given the strategic necessity.
**Risks:** Integration friction, regulatory review, founder retention. These are execution risks, not deal-breakers given the alternative is market exit.
**Edge:** Only the buyer has the cash reserves and stock currency to close a deal of this magnitude immediately. Competitors cannot match the bid.

**Decision:** The fear of displacement is validated by the target's growth. Organic build is too slow. The premium paid is the "cost of survival." While monetization is undefined, owning the channel allows future optionality that non-ownership does not. The deal must be capped with strict founder vesting and privacy compliance guarantees, but the strategic directive stands.

DECISION: GO
