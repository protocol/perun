**Steelman:** Acquiring a modern, object-oriented OS core from a small vendor is the only viable path to rapidly replace a failing, dated system, leveraging external innovation to halt market share loss when internal attempts have twice failed.

**Skeptic & Better-Way Analysis:**
The target's low sales volume is irrelevant; the asset's value lies in its architecture, not its commercial traction. Internal development is proven ineffective (two failures). Licensing a competitor's system surrenders strategic control and differentiation, likely accelerating decline. The "high price" is a function of desperation; without this acquisition, the company's valuation trends toward zero. The founder's difficult history is a manageable personnel risk compared to the existential threat of product obsolescence. Migration risk exists but is lower than the certainty of failure with the current OS.

**Pre-mortem & Kill Criteria:**
Failure scenario: Integration stalls due to cultural clash with the founder, or the technical bridge between old and new architectures proves too costly. However, the alternative (doing nothing) guarantees failure. The "do nothing" option is blocked by the falling market share and failed internal projects.

**Edge:**
This specific target offers a modern, object-oriented foundation that aligns with future computing trends, which the current vendor cannot match. No other available license provides this specific technological leap combined with the availability of the original architect (the founder) to guide integration.

**Blockers:** NONE. The financial cost is high, but the cost of inaction is total collapse. The risks are executional, not fundamental flaws in the strategy.
**Risks:** Founder friction, migration complexity. These are managed by defining the founder's role strictly as an advisor and committing to a phased migration.
**Edge:** Unique access to a modern core and its creator when no internal or licensed alternative exists.

**Decision:**
The proposal beats "do nothing" and "licensing" because it secures ownership of the necessary technology stack. The price is justified as a survival premium. The risks are manageable compared to the certainty of decline without action.

DECISION: GO
