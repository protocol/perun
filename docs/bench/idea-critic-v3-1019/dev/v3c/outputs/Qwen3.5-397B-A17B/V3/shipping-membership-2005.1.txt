**Steelman:** An upfront $79 fee locks in loyalty and increases order frequency, offsetting shipping losses through higher volume and reduced price sensitivity.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker is the unit economics gap. If heavy buyers increase frequency by >30% while margins are thin, the $79 cap fails to cover variable shipping costs, creating a loss on every additional order. Unlike digital subscriptions, this has high marginal costs per use. The "hard to reverse" risk is critical: once sold, the liability exists for a full year regardless of cash flow strain.

Alternatives include:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain friction but protect margins.
2.  **Threshold-Based Free Shipping:** Encourages larger basket sizes without uncapping frequency.
3.  **Paid Membership with Caps:** Limits free shipping to X orders/year to control downside.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This assumes customers value speed over cost enough to pay upfront. If the retailer lacks the cash reserves to absorb the initial margin hit while waiting for volume ramp-up, the program causes insolvency before success. A pre-mortem reveals failure if the attachment rate is low (only heavy buyers join) and their incremental shipping costs exceed $79 + incremental gross profit.

**Evidence & Verification:**
The proposal admits economics are "unproven." Launching a company-wide, hard-to-reverse financial commitment without a pilot or modeled break-even frequency is a violation of basic capital allocation. There is no evidence provided that the $79 fee covers the *average* shipping cost of a *heavy* user. Without a verified model showing that increased frequency yields net-positive contribution margin after shipping, this is a speculative bet with unlimited downside liability.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Unproven Economics:** No data shows $79 covers the shipping costs of the target "heavy buyer" segment.
2.  **Irreversibility:** The "hard to reverse" nature combined with potential negative unit economics creates an uncontrollable liability.
3.  **Missing Kill Criteria:** No pilot, cap, or metric is defined to stop the bleed if costs exceed revenue.

**Edge:**
None stated. First-mover advantage in loyalty is not an edge if the unit economics are negative.

**Verdict:**
The proposal lacks the fundamental proof required to justify a hard-to-reverse, high-liability financial product. It must be modeled and piloted before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
