**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant code-hosting platform instantly grants the historically hostile buyer legitimacy in the open-source community, neutralizing a rival ecosystem and securing the primary pipeline for developer tools, provided the target remains operationally independent.

**Skeptic:** The core claim relies on the community accepting the buyer's "pivot" despite historical hostility. If the acquisition triggers a mass exodus to free alternatives due to distrust, the $7.5 billion price tag purchases a hollowed-out brand with negative revenue, destroying shareholder value. The lack of a permanent CEO at the target suggests internal instability that could accelerate defection during integration.

**Better-Way:** "Do nothing" avoids the $7.5 billion outlay and the immediate reputational backlash, allowing the buyer to build organic trust over time. Alternatively, a strategic partnership or minority stake could secure integration benefits without triggering the "hostile takeover" narrative that drives users to competitors. Given the target is loss-making, waiting for a lower valuation or a different suitor might yield better terms.

**Kill-Criteria & Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Developers migrate en masse within six months, citing privacy fears and cultural mismatch, leaving the buyer with a costly, unused platform. The deal is a one-way door; once bought, the brand damage is irreversible. The cost of delay is losing the asset to a competitor, but the cost of error is billions in wasted stock and cemented distrust.

**Blockers:** NONE explicitly proven in the text, but the "vocal distrust" and "loss-making" status are severe headwinds.
**Risks:** Mass developer defection, regulatory block, integration failure, overpayment for a bleeding asset.
**Edge:** The buyer's vast distribution and resources could stabilize the target's operations and monetize features the target couldn't afford, *if* trust is maintained.

However, the proposal lacks a concrete mechanism to guarantee independence or arrest user defection beyond a verbal promise. Buying a loss-making, leaderless asset hated by its users for a premium price without a verified retention strategy is speculative gambling, not strategy. The risk of immediate value destruction via community flight outweighs the potential upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
