**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men avoiding both full-sugar guilt and diet stigma, leveraging a premium price to signal innovation before the low-carb trend fades.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The core claim fractures under the "satisfy neither" risk. Historical data in beverages suggests binary choices (regular vs. diet) dominate; a middle ground often confuses consumers rather than liberating them. If the target is young men rejecting "diet" labels, the product must taste indistinguishable from regular, rendering the "half-sugar" benefit negligible to the low-carb demographic who still count carbs. Conversely, strict low-carb dieters will reject 50% sugar content entirely.

The "do nothing" alternative is strong: let the rival test the water. If the trend is a fad (as suspected), being second avoids the sunk cost of a massive campaign for a dying category. If the trend persists, the market will validate the need before this company commits capital.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because the value proposition is unclear. Marketing spends millions explaining why this isn't diet, but sales remain flat because core regular drinkers don't care about half-sugar, and dieters want zero-sugar. The premium price further restricts trial, preventing the volume needed to sustain shelf space.

**Blockers:**
1. **Contradictory Value:** The product attempts to serve two mutually exclusive needs (taste of regular, benefits of diet) without fully satisfying either. No evidence is provided that a "middle" segment exists at a scale large enough to justify a new line.
2. **Fad Risk:** The proposal admits the low-carb trend may be temporary. Launching a permanent SKUs line and major campaign on a potentially transient trend is a capital allocation error.
3. **Price Barrier:** Premium pricing for a compromised product (half-sugar) lacks a clear justification compared to established full-sugar or zero-sugar options.

**Edge:** None identified. Being a large cola maker does not guarantee success for a confused value proposition. First-mover advantage is negated by the rival's simultaneous entry.

**Verdict:** The proposal lacks a defined, viable customer segment and relies on a potentially fleeting trend with high execution risk. The "satisfy neither" objection is a fundamental product flaw, not a manageable risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
