The proposal commits to a $200M annual capacity (40k units × $5k) before resolving fundamental market and regulatory blockers. The "edge" relies entirely on publicity and inventor reputation, which are not sustainable competitive advantages against the stated risks.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Regulatory Void:** The legal status is "unclear." Launching a vehicle without defined operating zones creates immediate liability and potential bans, rendering the inventory unsellable.
2.  **Unproven Demand:** Uses beyond institutional buyers are unverified. Scaling to 40k units assumes mass consumer adoption for a $5k device that makes riders feel "conspicuous" and offers low utility (cannot carry much). This contradicts basic adoption curves for high-friction, high-cost novelties.
3.  **Price vs. Utility:** At $5k, the value proposition is weak compared to existing transport (cars, bikes) given the limitations. No evidence suggests consumers will pay a premium for reduced utility and social stigma.

**Risks:**
*   High fixed costs from a large factory create massive downside if sales miss targets.
*   Safety incidents due to regulatory ambiguity could destroy the brand instantly.
*   The "conspicuous" factor may limit usage to niches, failing to justify factory scale.

**Edge:**
None identified. Publicity and investor backing do not equate to product-market fit or regulatory clearance. Being first with an unproven, legally ambiguous, high-cost device is a liability, not an asset.

**Pre-mortem:**
The company builds 40k units, regulators ban sidewalk use in major cities, consumers refuse to pay $5k for a toy they look ridiculous on, and the company collapses under inventory debt.

**Kill Criteria Missing:**
The proposal lacks a pilot phase, regulatory validation, or a smaller initial production run to test demand. It jumps straight to full-scale manufacturing.

**Verdict:**
The plan fails the "must beat do nothing" test. A cheaper, safer alternative is a limited pilot to resolve legal status and prove consumer demand before committing to factory-scale CAPEX. The current proposal ignores concrete failure scenarios that are highly probable given the facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
