**Steelman:** Repositioning a game console as an "Entertainment System" with non-game toys and strict quality control via lockout chips can bypass retailer bias against the collapsed 1983 video game market, securing distribution where pure consoles cannot.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is retailer refusal; however, bundling a robot and light gun reclassifies the product as a toy, accessing a different shelf space and buyer mindset. The alternative—waiting for the market to recover or launching as a computer—fails because home computers are too complex for the mass market, and delay cedes ground to incumbents. The "fad" perception is the enemy; the proposal directly attacks this by hiding the "video game" identity.

**Pre-mortem & Evidence:**
If this fails, it will be because the toy bundle feels gimmicky without strong software, or the lockout chip stifles the library needed to sustain interest. However, the take-back promise directly neutralizes the retailer's financial risk, forcing them to stock the unit. The lack of a track record is mitigated by the Japanese market leadership (implied quality) and the low-risk offer to retailers.

**Kill Criteria & Edge:**
*Blockers:* None identified in the text; the proposal explicitly addresses the stated barriers (retailer fear, fad perception).
*Risks:* Developer anger over lockout chips is a long-term risk but secondary to securing initial distribution.
*Edge:* The unique combination of toy bundling (masking the console nature) and a financial guarantee (take-back) creates a path to market that competitors lacking the capital for such guarantees or the agility to rebrand cannot match.

**Decision:**
The plan survives the skeptic test because it does not ask retailers to believe in "video games" again; it asks them to stock a high-margin toy with zero downside risk. The lockout chip ensures quality control, preventing a repeat of the 1983 software glut that caused the crash. The risks are manageable compared to the total loss of doing nothing.

DECISION: GO
