The claim is that a clear cola will succeed as a major national brand extension based on positive test market data. The steelman is that clarity signals purity to health-conscious consumers, creating a distinct category that drives incremental volume despite cannibalization risks.

However, critical blockers exist. First, the core assumption that "cola flavor" can be decoupled from "dark color" in consumer psychology is unproven at scale; if shoppers perceive clear cola as tasting different or inferior due to lack of color cues, repeat purchase will collapse after the novelty fades. Second, the proposal admits test markets often overstate demand for novel items, yet relies on them as the primary evidence for a nationwide rollout. Third, the risk of cannibalizing the flagship brand is high without a proven mechanism to retain novelty buyers long-term.

Better ways exist: a staged regional rollout to verify repeat purchase rates before committing to a costly national campaign, or positioning it as a limited-time offering to test durability without diluting the master brand. The cost of delay is low compared to the risk of a failed national launch damaging brand equity.

The strongest attack is that the product solves a non-existent problem (color impurity) while violating a deeply held category schema (cola = dark). If the sensory experience contradicts the visual cue, cognitive dissonance will reject the product regardless of marketing spend. The proposal offers no kill criteria for early failure nor a leading metric beyond initial trial.

Without evidence that the "purity" positioning overrides entrenched flavor-color associations, and given the high probability of novelty decay, the plan fails the blocker test. The edge is undefined; being second-largest is not an advantage in creating a new sub-category against a dominant incumbent who can easily copy the format.

DECISION: NO-GO
