**Steelman:** A dominant Japanese 50cc manufacturer can create a new US commuter segment by bypassing traditional motorcycle dealers to sell small, accessible bikes via sporting-goods stores, despite current US preferences for large machines and concerns about brand anonymity and product durability.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly acknowledges the risks (brand unknown, preference for big bikes, durability questions, currency costs) but offers a structural workaround (non-traditional distribution) and a product fit (easy-to-ride) that targets a non-competing demographic (commuters/students vs. heavy motorcycle enthusiasts). No fact provided proves the plan fails as proposed; the risks are hypotheses to be tested, not proven failures.

**Risks:** The 50cc engines may fail on US highways; the brand may remain unknown; cultural preference for "rough" large bikes may reject the step-through design; foreign currency expenditure may strain finances if sales lag. These are execution risks, not blockers, as the proposal targets a specific niche (commuters/students) distinct from the existing "large machine" market.

**Edge:** The company leads its home market with this specific product, proving product-market fit for the "small, easy-to-ride" value proposition. The strategy to use sporting-goods stores creates a distribution channel inaccessible to competitors focused on traditional motorcycle dealerships, allowing entry without direct head-to-head competition with established US brands.

**Decision Logic:** The plan beats "do nothing" by attempting to create a new category rather than fighting for share in a small, hostile existing market. The alternative of selling larger models is already flagged as risky due to durability concerns. The "do nothing" option guarantees zero growth in the US. The proposed path has a clear edge (home market leadership + unique distribution) and manageable risks given the targeted niche.

**Kill Criteria:** If sales via sporting-goods stores do not reach profitability within 12 months, or if engine failure rates on US roads exceed 5% in the first year, the venture must pivot or cease US operations.

DECISION: GO
