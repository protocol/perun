Steelman: A premium, keyboard-less touchscreen phone integrating a proven music player and full web browser can disrupt a market dominated by carrier-controlled, keyboard-centric business devices, provided one carrier cedes design control and shares revenue for exclusivity.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of phone experience is a risk, not a factual blocker, as the core competencies (hardware design, software integration, music ecosystem) are already proven in the proposer's existing PC and music player lines. The premium price targets a gap left by utilitarian business phones, and the "no keyboard" constraint is the specific differentiator against current market leaders, not a flaw. Exclusivity is the necessary cost to secure the unprecedented demand for carrier revenue sharing and design autonomy.

**Risks:** High price sensitivity; limited initial reach due to single-carrier exclusivity; lack of third-party apps at launch; consumer resistance to removing physical keyboards. These are mitigated by the "killer app" integration (music + web + email) which existing phones fragment, and the revenue-share model which aligns carrier incentives despite exclusivity.

**Edge:** The proposer uniquely owns the dominant digital music ecosystem and a loyal customer base already accustomed to their hardware/software integration. No rival combines a leading music brand, industrial design capability, and a full desktop-class browser in a single handheld device. Competitors are locked into carrier-dominated design cycles and lack the integrated music entitlement.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders or first-quarter sales do not exceed the total addressable market of the proposer's existing high-end music player customers by at least 20% within 18 months, or if the carrier partner attempts to override software updates or design choices before the exclusivity term ends, the venture must be pivoted to a multi-carrier model or discontinued.

**Leading Metric:** Activation rate of the built-in music player and web browser among new phone users versus legacy feature phones, reviewed quarterly.

DECISION: GO
