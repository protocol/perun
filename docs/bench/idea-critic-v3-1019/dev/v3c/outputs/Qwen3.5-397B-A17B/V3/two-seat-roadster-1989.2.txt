**Steelman:** A mid-size maker can profitably revive the affordable two-seat roadster by leveraging lightweight simplicity against bloat, targeting underserved enthusiasts despite segment shrinkage.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists between the proposal's premises and the stated risks; the risks are inherent to the strategy, not proof of failure. The "no heritage" claim is a marketing hurdle, not a manufacturing blocker.

**Risks:**
1.  **Shrinking Segment:** The market has contracted; volume will be low.
2.  **Regulatory Cost:** Safety compliance may erode the "affordable" price point.
3.  **One-Hit Wonder:** Sales may collapse after initial enthusiast purchases.
4.  **Brand Fit:** Lack of sports-car heritage may limit premium pricing power.

**Edge:**
Mass-market competitors have abandoned the niche due to complexity and margin targets, creating a vacuum for a focused, lightweight, front-engine/rear-drive vehicle that prioritizes driving dynamics over features. The proposer's "mid-size" status offers sufficient supply chain leverage to produce affordably where micro-breweries cannot.

**Analysis:**
The proposal explicitly acknowledges the primary risks (shrinking segment, safety costs, lack of heritage). These are not hidden blockers but known variables the strategy must navigate. The "affordable" and "lightweight" directives directly counter the regulatory cost risk. The "enthusiast" target accepts the limited buyer pool. The lack of heritage is a positioning challenge, not a feasibility stopper. The pre-mortem scenario (sales fading after early adopters) is a valid lifecycle concern for niche cars but does not invalidate the initial launch if the business case accounts for a limited run. Since the proposal admits these faults and still argues for the opportunity based on a market gap, the claim survives the attack. The decision rests on whether the company accepts a low-volume, high-passion product to fill a portfolio gap. Given the explicit acceptance of risks and the clear identification of a competitor-abandoned niche, the plan is sound enough to proceed to detailed financial modeling and prototyping.

**Kill Criteria:** Stop if prototype regulatory compliance costs exceed 15% of the target MSRP or if pre-orders from the enthusiast target group miss projections by >40% within 6 months of reveal.

DECISION: GO
