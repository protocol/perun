**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled e-reader removes friction for book buyers and solves publisher piracy fears via a closed ecosystem, creating a new high-margin revenue stream that complements rather than replaces print.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the company's total lack of hardware experience, which historically leads to costly delays and quality failures. However, the "do nothing" alternative cedes the digital future to competitors who will eventually erode the core print business. Building a PC-dependent software solution fails because it ignores the "browse and download anywhere" value proposition that drives impulse purchases. The risk of cannibalization is real but acceptable; owning the digital transition is superior to letting a third party dictate terms and margins later.

**Pre-mortem & Evidence:**
If this fails, it will be because the device is too expensive or the cellular subsidy burns cash before adoption scales. The proposal mitigates piracy concerns (a key publisher blocker) through a dedicated device, securing the content library necessary for success. The "paper-like" screen addresses the specific fatigue complaints of earlier failed e-readers.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** None. Lack of experience is a risk, not a factual impossibility, and can be mitigated by partnering with existing manufacturers (an implicit step in execution).
*   **Edge:** The company possesses the world's largest book catalog and existing customer trust, an asset no electronics manufacturer has. This content moat creates a unique value proposition: the device is useless without the store, and the store is best accessed via this specific device.
*   **Risks:** Hardware execution, margin compression, and channel conflict. These are managed by the high strategic necessity of owning the customer interface for digital goods.

The proposal beats "do nothing" because the cost of delay is the loss of the entire digital market segment. It beats a software-only approach by solving the specific usability and piracy hurdles that have stalled the market. The unique combination of content dominance and a purpose-built delivery mechanism provides a defensible edge.

DECISION: GO
