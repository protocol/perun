**Steelman:** Launching two carrier-locked, data-plan-dependent social phones without apps or games leverages an acquired team's momentum to capture the youth market before the main platform arrives, despite higher costs.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" and "fit" tests immediately. The target demographic is explicitly price-sensitive, yet the hardware requires a data plan costing as much as a smartphone itself. This creates an insurmountable barrier to entry. Furthermore, removing apps and games strips the device of the primary utility young users demand, leaving only a single social function available elsewhere on cheaper devices.

The "do nothing" or "wait" alternative is superior. The company's main platform launches later that same year; fragmenting the ecosystem now with a limited, expensive niche product cannibalizes attention and resources from the core launch. The history of internal conflict between teams suggests execution risk is high, and the lack of an app store guarantees a poor user experience compared to competitors.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Economic Mismatch:** Price-sensitive users cannot or will not pay smartphone-tier data rates for a limited device.
2.  **Value Deficit:** No apps/games removes the core value proposition for the target demographic.
3.  **Strategic Conflict:** Launching a niche product immediately prior to the main platform dilutes focus and risks internal friction without a clear path to profitability.

**Edge:**
None identified. Being first with a limited, expensive product is not an advantage; it is a liability. The "social" aspect is not unique enough to justify the hardware and data constraints.

**Kill Criteria:**
If the projected uptake requires users to ignore the cost disparity between this plan and existing smartphones, the premise is false. The risk of brand damage among youth due to a sub-par, expensive experience outweighs the potential gain of early entry.

**Verdict:**
The proposal asks to enter a price-sensitive market with a premium-priced, feature-poor product while distracting from a major upcoming launch. It ignores basic unit economics and user behavior.

DECISION: NO-GO
