**Steelman:** Leverage existing compact mechanics to deliver a stylish, customizable coupe/convertible at a low entry price, targeting young buyers with massive advertising to create a new segment.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that sharing parts with a "dull" economy car will taint the sporty image, causing the product to fail despite styling. However, the proposal mitigates this by decoupling the *mechanical* reality from the *perceived* value through distinct styling (long hood, short deck) and a customization model. The "cheap" mechanical base is actually the strategic edge: it guarantees low cost and reliability while allowing the options list to drive margin.

The risk of unproven demand is real, but the "do nothing" alternative guarantees missing the emerging demographic shift entirely. The heavy ad spend is a calculated bet to define the category before competitors react; delaying to test demand softly allows rivals to capture the mindshare. The proposal explicitly addresses the "cheap look" risk via design differentiation and the "financial risk" via a long options list that shifts revenue burden to willing buyers rather than inflating the base price.

There is no factual contradiction in the proposal. The mechanics support the price; the styling supports the image; the ads support the launch velocity. The risks are executional, not structural. The plan beats "do nothing" because it creates a unique market position (sporty yet affordable) that competitors cannot immediately match without retooling. The edge is the speed to market using existing reliable components, freeing capital for the brand-building ad campaign.

**Blockers:** NONE. The cited risks are managed by the proposed design and pricing strategy.
**Risks:** Demand volume uncertainty; brand perception drift.
**Edge:** First-mover advantage in a new "pony car" segment using proven, low-cost mechanics to underprice potential rivals.

**Kill Criteria:** If first-year sales do not cover the launch ad spend plus 20%, halt further variant development. Leading metric: Options take-rate per unit (target >40% attachment).

DECISION: GO
