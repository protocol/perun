**Steelman:** Transforming from a pure retailer into a hybrid marketplace creates immediate inventory depth and revenue via fees without capital expenditure, leveraging existing traffic to monetize long-tail demand that current margins cannot support.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary fear is cannibalization: used or cheaper third-party items stealing new sales. However, for an unprofitable retailer, the alternative is stagnation. "Do nothing" preserves margin percentage on a shrinking or static volume while competitors potentially build network effects elsewhere. A "walled garden" (retailer only) limits selection and increases inventory risk. The "marketplace" model shifts inventory risk to sellers while capturing rent on every transaction, even those undercutting the retailer's own price. If a customer buys a cheaper used book from a third party on the site, the retailer earns a fee with zero cost of goods sold, likely yielding higher net profit than selling the new copy itself.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits the survival stage: cash flow and selection matter more than controlling every variable. Failure mode: rogue sellers destroy trust. Mitigation: strict performance metrics and immediate suspension for bad actors. The risk of supplier objection is real but manageable; suppliers care about volume, and this model expands total addressable market by offering price-tiered options (new vs. used) on a single page, capturing customers who would otherwise leave for eBay or other secondary markets.

**Blockers, Risks, Edge:**
*Blockers:* NONE. The math of fee-based revenue on zero-inventory sales outweighs the margin loss on displaced new units for an unprofitable entity.
*Risks:* Brand dilution from poor seller service; supplier channel conflict.
*Edge:* Existing traffic and customer trust provide the liquidity needed to jumpstart a marketplace, a barrier new entrants lack.

**Decision:**
The proposal addresses the core constraint (profitability) by monetizing traffic rather than just inventory. The cannibalization argument fails because the fee on a lost sale is often more profitable than the margin on the won sale, given the absence of inventory carrying costs.

DECISION: GO
