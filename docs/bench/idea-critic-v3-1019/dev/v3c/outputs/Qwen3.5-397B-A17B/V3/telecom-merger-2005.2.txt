**Steelman:** Acquiring the target secures a sticky, high-margin enterprise segment (construction/business) via its unique push-to-talk service, creating an immediate revenue moat that organic build-out cannot match quickly.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is **technology incompatibility**. Running two distinct networks forces duplicated operational expenditures (OpEx) and delays synergy realization. The "integration cost" risk is not theoretical; merging incompatible legacy equipment often leads to service degradation, churn among the very business customers being courted, and massive capital expenditure (CapEx) to force a unified standard.

*Alternative (Do Nothing/Organic):* Build a proprietary push-to-talk solution on the buyer's existing network.
*Cost:* Lower upfront cash outlay than $36B.
*Risk:* Slow time-to-market; failure to replicate the specific network effect or brand loyalty of the target's service.
*Reversibility:* High (can stop development).

*Alternative (Partnership):* Commercial agreement to resell the target's service without equity merger.
*Cost:* Variable revenue share.
*Risk:* Less control, lower long-term margin.
*Reversibility:* High.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal fails because integration drags on for years. The legacy network becomes a cash drain rather than a bridge. Business customers, frustrated by inconsistent service during the migration, defect to competitors. The $36B price tag creates massive goodwill that must be written down when the projected synergies fail to materialize due to technical debt.

**Fit & Edge:** While the customer base is valuable, the proposal admits the technologies are incompatible and the target is "limited for data." In 2005, data growth is the critical vector. Buying a legacy, incompatible network contradicts the strategic necessity of a unified, data-capable infrastructure. The "edge" is purely the installed base, but the cost to migrate that base to a profitable, unified technology may exceed the lifetime value of the customers, especially given the high risk of churn during transition.

**Blockers:** The $36B valuation assumes successful integration of incompatible tech. No evidence is provided that the migration cost won't erase the acquisition's value. The "large goodwill" risk indicates the price already premiums the synergy that is technically jeopardized.

**Decision:** The technical incompatibility creates a high probability of value destruction that outweighs the benefit of the installed base. The cheaper, safer path is organic development or partnership until a unified technology standard is viable.

DECISION: NO-GO
