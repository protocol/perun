**Steelman:** A premium, gap-filling network for global travelers where terrestrial coverage fails, monetizing urgency before ground networks mature.

**Attack:** The unit economics collapse under the stated constraints. At $3,000 per handset and multi-dollar per-minute rates, the addressable market is too narrow to service $5 billion in debt, especially given the "bulky" hardware and poor indoor performance which degrade the core value proposition. The primary risk—rapid terrestrial expansion—is not a possibility but an accelerating reality that directly erodes the window for ROI. The proposal relies on a high-margin niche that ground networks are rapidly commoditizing at a fraction of the cost.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid sinking further capital into a high-fixed-cost model facing immediate obsolescence.
2.  **Pivot to B2B/Government:** Ignore consumers; sell exclusively to maritime, aviation, and defense sectors where indoor use is irrelevant and price sensitivity is lower.
3.  **Delay Launch:** Wait for handset miniaturization, risking debt default but improving product fit.

**Fit:** This conflicts with the implicit north star of sustainable growth; it chases a shrinking premium window with a product inferior to the emerging substitute (ground cellular).

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-launch, subscriber growth misses targets by 60% due to price and bulk. Ground coverage reaches 80% of business hubs. The company cannot service debt and enters bankruptcy, selling assets for cents on the dollar.

**Blockers:**
*   **Debt Service Mismatch:** The projected revenue from a luxury-priced, niche product cannot mathematically cover the interest on $5 billion borrowed capital within the required timeframe.
*   **Substitute Velocity:** The "spreading fast" ground network directly invalidates the "worldwide" necessity before the payback period ends.

**Edge:** None stated. Being first with an expensive, bulky product is not an edge when a cheaper, better alternative is scaling simultaneously.

**Kill Criteria:** If subscriber acquisition costs exceed $1,500 per user or if ground coverage hits 50% of major international business hubs within 18 months, terminate expansion immediately.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the blocker test. The debt load requires mass adoption that the price point and product limitations explicitly prevent.

DECISION: NO-GO
