**Steelman:** Launching a $700 pen-based PDA with heavy CEO-backed publicity creates a new handheld category by betting that superior input methods outweigh current technical limitations and high costs.

**Attack & Options:**
The proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts provided:
1.  **Core Mechanism Failure:** Handwriting recognition is explicitly "unreliable in testing." For a device where this is the *main* input, this is not a risk; it is a functional defect that renders the primary value proposition unusable.
2.  **Form Factor Mismatch:** The device is "too big for a pocket," contradicting the essential utility of a "handheld" or "personal" digital assistant intended for mobile use.
3.  **Ecosystem Void:** "Little software" and an "unproven category" at a "high" price point ($700 in 1993 is a premium tier) creates a dead-on-arrival scenario. Without proven demand or apps, the high price cannot be justified, especially when the core feature (writing) does not work reliably.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Avoids sinking capital into a broken prototype.
*   *Delay/Revise:* Wait until recognition is reliable and the form factor fits a pocket.
*   *Cheaper Path:* License the technology to an existing player rather than leading a category with an unfinished product.

**Pre-mortem:**
Assume failure one year post-launch. The cause is clear: early adopters return the device because it cannot accurately transcribe notes, and the size prevents daily carry. The heavy publicity amplifies the embarrassment, damaging the brand's reputation for quality. The "edge" claimed is merely being first, but being first with a broken core mechanism is a liability, not an asset. Fear of falling behind or "strategic necessity" are not edges.

**Verdict:**
There is a definitive blocker: the primary input method is unreliable, and the physical design violates the basic requirement of portability. These are not risks to be managed; they are facts that negate the product's viability. No amount of publicity can fix a device that fails its primary function.

DECISION: NO-GO
