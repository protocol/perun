The claim is that offering 1GB of free storage, funded by content-matched ads, will disrupt a mature email market despite high infrastructure costs and privacy concerns.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly addresses the "mature market" by solving the primary user pain point (storage exhaustion) which incumbents ignore. The cost of storage is a variable expense matched to user growth, not a fixed upfront blocker. The "no direct revenue" is a feature of the ad-model strategy, not a flaw.

**Risks:** 
1. **Privacy Backlash:** Scanning mail for ads may trigger regulatory or user trust issues. 
2. **Unit Economics:** If ad yield per user is lower than storage/server costs at scale, the model fails.
3. **Adoption Friction:** Invitation-only growth might be too slow to reach critical mass before funding runs out.

**Edge:** The proposal leverages the search company's existing core competency (fast search and ad matching) to solve a specific, unaddressed frustration (deleting mail) that incumbents are incentivized to ignore (they sell storage upgrades). No other player can deploy this specific combination of search technology and ad infrastructure at zero marginal development cost.

**Decision Logic:**
The "do nothing" alternative yields no entry into the communication layer. The "cheaper" alternative (standard storage) fails to differentiate. The pre-mortem suggests failure comes from privacy regulation or low ad yield, but these are manageable risks via terms of service and algorithmic optimization, not absolute blockers. The invitation-only launch mitigates server load while creating demand. The edge (search + ads + unused storage capacity) is unique to this proposer. The risks are real but do not invalidate the core hypothesis that users value storage over privacy trade-offs in a free tier.

DECISION: GO
