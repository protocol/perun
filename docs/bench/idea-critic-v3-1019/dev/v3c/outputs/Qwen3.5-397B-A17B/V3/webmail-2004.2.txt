The claim is that offering 1,000x more storage than incumbents, funded by content-matched ads, will disrupt the mature email market despite high infrastructure costs and privacy risks.

**Steelman:** By making storage scarcity irrelevant and leveraging superior search, the service creates a unique value proposition that justifies ad-scanning privacy trade-offs, allowing rapid user acquisition via exclusivity to offset initial lack of revenue.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economic Blocker:** In 2004, a gigabyte of storage was expensive. If users do not delete emails (the core value prop), costs scale linearly and perpetually per user. Text ads may not generate sufficient CPM to cover long-term storage marginal costs, especially if engagement is low. The proposal lacks a unit-economics model proving Lifetime Value > Lifetime Storage Cost.
2.  **Privacy Barrier:** Scanning private correspondence for ads is a fundamental shift from the norm. A single high-profile leak or misuse scandal could destroy trust instantly. The "invitation-only" launch mitigates scale risk but does not solve the inherent privacy contradiction if the business model relies on pervasive scanning.
3.  **Market Entrenchment:** Incumbents have network effects (everyone already has an address). Switching costs are high. Unless the search capability is demonstrably magical, "more storage" alone may not drive mass migration away from established providers.

**Better Way:** Offer a tiered model (e.g., 500MB free, paid upgrades) to validate willingness to pay before committing to unlimited gigabyte scaling, or limit ad-scanning to non-sensitive metadata to reduce privacy backlash.

**Kill Criteria:** If pilot data shows cost-per-user exceeds ad-revenue-per-user by >20% after 6 months, or if privacy complaints exceed 1% of the invitee base, the project must pivot to a paid model or shut down.

**Verdict:** The proposal bets the company on unproven storage economics and a controversial privacy model in a saturated market. While the upside is massive, the lack of a confirmed path to profitability per user and the existential reputational risk of email scanning constitute a blocker. The unit economics are assumed, not proven.

DECISION: NO-GO
