**Steelman:** A mandatory client solves critical update and cheating failures, creating a defensible platform that transforms a distribution weakness into a recurring revenue store, outweighing short-term friction.

**Attack & Options:**
The "do nothing" option guarantees continued revenue leakage from piracy and high support costs for patching, eventually eroding the player base. Alternatives like optional patchers fail to stop cheaters who simply disable them. While retail pushback and broadband limits are real risks in 2003, they are transitional. The core blocker test is whether the value of a secure, updated game exceeds the friction of installation for the *core* audience. For popular PC titles, the audience is already technically inclined and desperate for fair play; they will tolerate the hurdle. Competitors copying the model validates the market rather than killing it; first-mover advantage in building the user network is the decisive edge.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the north star of maximizing lifetime value per user. If we assume failure, it is because the client is too bloated or buggy at launch, driving users to pirated, static versions. However, this is an execution risk, not a strategic flaw. The cost of delay is immediate: every month without anti-cheat accelerates community churn.

**Evidence & Edge:**
The proposal cites specific operational failures (updates, cheating) that current physical media cannot solve. The edge is the developer's existing hit games; they provide the initial traffic no other store has. Mandatory adoption forces the network effect required to make the store viable. Risks like broadband penetration are improving trends, not static blockers.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks are manageable execution challenges or market conditions that favor early adoption.
**Risks:** Retail channel conflict, initial client stability, user friction.
**Edge:** Existing popular IP forces adoption where a new entrant could not; solves the unsolvable piracy/update loop of physical media.

**Kill Criteria:** If the client causes a >20% drop in active users within 6 months post-launch due to technical failures, pivot to an optional model. Leading metric: Weekly Active Users on the client vs. total sales.
