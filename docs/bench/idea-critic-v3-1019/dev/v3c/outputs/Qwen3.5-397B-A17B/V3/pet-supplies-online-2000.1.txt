**Steelman:** Aggressive capital deployment via IPO will secure dominant market share and eliminate competition before rivals can react, accepting short-term losses for long-term monopoly power.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on continuous access to public capital markets to fund negative gross margins. This is a fatal dependency. Selling products below cost creates a "pay-to-acquire" loop where every new customer deepens the cash burn. If capital markets close—as explicitly flagged in the risks—the company cannot fund operations, let alone advertising or acquisitions. The plan assumes infinite liquidity to sustain a model where revenue per unit is less than cost per unit.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve current runway; likely lose share to better-funded rivals.
2.  **Profitability Focus:** Raise prices to positive margins, accept slower growth, and survive a market downturn.
3.  **Targeted Acquisition:** Buy the competitor using stock rather than cash, avoiding immediate liquidity drain.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** In a dot-com crash scenario (highly probable given the era and heavy spending by all competitors), this strategy guarantees insolvency. The "edge" claimed is speed, but speed without a path to unit economics is merely faster failure. The proposal lacks a kill criterion for customer acquisition cost or a timeline to positive margins.

**Blockers:**
*   **Negative Unit Economics:** Selling below cost means scaling increases losses linearly. No factual evidence suggests this trend reverses without a miracle in supply chain costs, which are stated as "heavy."
*   **Capital Dependency:** The entire plan collapses if the IPO window slams shut or post-IPO stock drops, preventing secondary raises.

**Risks:** Competitors engaging in the same price war; shipping costs eroding any potential margin recovery; inability to integrate acquisitions quickly enough.

**Edge:** None identified. Being first to lose money at scale is not a competitive advantage. The proposal offers no asset, cost structure, or proprietary technology that rivals lack.

**Decision Logic:** A plan requiring perpetual external funding to sustain negative margins, executed during a period of known market volatility and competitor overspending, fails the basic viability test. It bets the company on a market condition (open capital) that is explicitly at risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
