The claim is that a 3D display and visual search justify a flagship-priced, carrier-exclusive smartphone on a forked OS lacking mainstream apps in a saturated two-platform market.

**Skeptic:** The proposal assumes users value 3D navigation and camera-based shopping enough to overcome the absence of key applications and pay a premium price. In a duopoly market, the "app gap" is a primary driver of consumer choice; without the leading platform's mainstream apps, the device fails the basic utility test for the mass market, regardless of novel hardware. The "unsubsidised-equivalent" price removes the only historical lever (subsidy) that successfully moved customers to inferior hardware.

**Better-way:** The "do nothing" option preserves capital. A cheaper alternative is licensing the dominant OS to ensure app compatibility while differentiating via the camera/shopping features, or launching the visual search software on existing iOS/Android devices first to prove demand before building hardware. Building a walled garden with a smaller app store contradicts the retailer's strength (selection) and ignores Chesterton's Fence: the two dominant platforms exist because of network effects, not lack of innovation.

**Kill-criteria:** If the device cannot run the top 50 mainstream apps, it cannot compete as a primary smartphone. The one-way door is the massive sunk cost of hardware manufacturing and carrier exclusivity contracts. The cost of delay is low; waiting allows the retailer to validate the 3D/shopping features on other platforms first.

**Blockers:** The combination of "no mainstream apps," "flagship price," and "carrier exclusivity" creates a fatal mismatch. The stated facts confirm the market is saturated and dominated by two platforms; entering with a fragmented OS and a niche feature set at a premium price point has no path to the necessary volume. There is no stated edge (distribution, cost, or unique asset) that overcomes the app deficit.

**Edge:** None identified that neutralizes the app gap. Being a successful retailer does not translate to smartphone platform viability without the ecosystem.

DECISION: NO-GO
