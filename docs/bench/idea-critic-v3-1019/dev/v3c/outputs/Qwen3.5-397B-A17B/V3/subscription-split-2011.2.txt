The proposal to split the DVD and streaming services into separate brands, accounts, and billing systems fails the edge test and introduces fatal execution risks without solving the core problem.

**Blockers:**
1. **No Named Edge:** The proposal cites "different management" and "declining DVD business" as reasons to split. However, internal organizational structure is not a customer-facing edge. Splitting the brand does not inherently make streaming faster, cheaper, or more content-rich than rivals. It merely fractures the user experience.
2. **Contradictory Evidence:** The rationale acknowledges "customer confusion" and "reaction to the price increase" as risks. Executing a split immediately after a 60% price hike compounds the negative sentiment. It forces loyal customers—who likely subsidized the transition—to manage two logins and bills, effectively punishing them for the previous pricing error. This confirms the fear of backlash rather than mitigating it.
3. **Value Destruction:** Severing shared ratings and recommendations destroys the primary data advantage of a combined service. In a nascent streaming market, cross-pollinating viewing data is critical for algorithmic improvement. Deliberately blinding the streaming engine to DVD user preferences (and vice versa) weakens the product's core mechanism for retention.

**Risks:**
The pre-mortem is clear: Customers perceive this not as strategic focus, but as a "double dip" scheme to extract more money while degrading service convenience. The operational cost of maintaining two brands, two tech stacks, and two marketing funnels will likely outweigh the management clarity gained, especially when the DVD unit is already declining.

**Verdict:**
The plan mistakes an internal accounting desire for a market strategy. It solves a management inconvenience by creating a customer experience disaster. The "edge" claimed (freedom to pursue strategy) can be achieved internally without fracturing the brand or alienating the user base during a pricing crisis. There is no scenario where forcing friction on the customer immediately after a price hike leads to growth.

DECISION: NO-GO
