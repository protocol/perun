**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only viable channel to demonstrate the integrated hardware-software music strategy, as third-party retailers lack the incentive or space to explain the value proposition, and current losses stem from product obscurity rather than price.

**Attack & Options:**
The proposal faces immediate blockers: high fixed lease costs during a recession and a industry-wide history of failed manufacturer-owned stores. However, the "do nothing" option guarantees continued margin erosion and brand invisibility in crowded big-box environments where staff actively push competitors. Alternatives like "shop-in-shop" kiosks fail to solve the staffing incentive problem; dedicated online stores cannot replicate the tactile music demo required for adoption in 2001.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This moves the North Star from "selling boxes" to "selling an ecosystem." If this fails, it will be due to over-expansion before proving the unit economics of a single location. The cost of delay is ceding the emerging digital music narrative to software-only competitors who will commoditize the hardware.

**Evidence & Edge:**
The edge is not "being first" or "having money," but the unique integration of device and music service that competitors cannot replicate on their floors. The proposal's risk of fixed costs is real, but the risk of irrelevance is fatal. The "poor track record" cited applies to manufacturers selling generic commodities; no one has tested a manufacturer selling a fully integrated lifestyle experience in a controlled environment.

**Blockers:** NONE. The financial risks are severe but are execution risks, not fundamental flaws in the logic that the current channel is broken for this specific strategy.
**Risks:** Recession impacting foot traffic; channel conflict causing reseller retaliation.
**Edge:** Only company-controlled spaces can faithfully demonstrate the proprietary music workflow that drives hardware sales.

**Decision:**
The plan beats "do nothing" because the current channel actively works against the product's core differentiator. The high cost is the price of survival. Proceed, but strictly cap the initial rollout to verify unit economics before scaling.

DECISION: GO
