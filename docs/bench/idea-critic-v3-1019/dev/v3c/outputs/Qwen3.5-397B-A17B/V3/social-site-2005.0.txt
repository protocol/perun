**Steelman:** Acquiring the custom-page social network for $580M grants immediate dominance in a high-growth demographic, leveraging the conglomerate's ad infrastructure to monetize users the target cannot reach alone.

**Attack:** The price represents a massive premium over current revenue with no proven path to profitability given the "hard to monetize" youth segment. Brand-safety risks from user-generated content could contaminate the conglomerate's premium inventory, deterring existing high-value advertisers. The "uneven" technology suggests high integration costs and potential scalability failures under conglomerate-scale traffic. A college-focused rival indicates the market is fracturing; buying the legacy "custom page" model may be acquiring a dying asset just as behavior shifts to feed-based interaction.

**Better Way:** Do nothing or pursue a minority stake to test monetization without assuming full technical debt and brand risk. Alternatively, build a proprietary youth-focused platform using internal content assets, avoiding the $580M upfront cash outlay.

**Fit:** While it expands reach, it conflicts with the conglomerate's likely need for brand safety and stable revenue. The "separate unit" strategy fails if ad integration requires deep technical coupling with the unstable codebase.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-acquisition, advertiser boycotts occur due to unsafe user content; the platform crashes during peak traffic due to legacy code; the youth audience migrates to the mobile-first rival, leaving the conglomerate with a $580M write-down.

**Blockers:**
1. **Financial:** No evidence provided that $580M can be recouped given "modest revenue" and "hard to monetize" users.
2. **Technical:** "Uneven" technology poses a critical integration risk for ad delivery.
3. **Strategic:** Brand-safety issues threaten core revenue streams.

**Edge:** None stated beyond scale; being first does not guarantee winning if the model is flawed.

**Kill Criteria:** Halt if due diligence cannot prove a 3-year path to positive ROI excluding synergy assumptions, or if brand-safety automation cannot be guaranteed pre-close.

**Decision:** The proposal lacks evidence to overcome the financial premium and brand-risk blockers. The technical debt and shifting competitive landscape make this a high-probability failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
