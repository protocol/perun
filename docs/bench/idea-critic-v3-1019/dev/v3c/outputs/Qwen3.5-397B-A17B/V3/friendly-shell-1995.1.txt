**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly targets "high-end PC" owners, neutralizing the memory constraint for the intended initial market segment. The risk of patronizing experienced users is irrelevant as the product is not marketed to them. The upcoming OS release is a competitive risk, not a factual blocker to launching a distinct product line now.

**Risks:** High hardware barrier limits total addressable market immediately; potential perception as a toy; dependency on third-party content which currently lacks; risk of cannibalization or obsolescence by the parent company's own upcoming OS update.

**Edge:** The company's dominant market position provides immediate distribution leverage and brand trust. No competitor currently offers a unified, metaphor-based interface for novices, creating a first-mover advantage in the "home ease-of-use" segment before the next generic OS wave arrives.

**Steelman:** A premium, metaphor-driven interface sold as an add-on allows the company to capture the underserved home novice market immediately using existing high-end hardware, generating revenue and user feedback before integrating successful elements into the core OS later.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The argument that "waiting for the next OS release is cheaper and safer" was tested. It fails because the delay cedes the "easy home computing" narrative to competitors and ignores the immediate revenue potential from the installed base of high-end machines. Waiting guarantees zero near-term growth in this specific demographic; launching now captures it.

**Kill Criteria:** Halt development if sales to high-end users do not reach 50,000 units within six months, or if internal testing confirms the cartoon metaphor reduces task completion speed for novices compared to standard menus.

**Decision:** The proposal addresses a verified gap (novice difficulty) with a targeted solution that bypasses current hardware limitations by segmenting the market. The risks are manageable via positioning, and the edge (distribution + first-mover) is distinct.

DECISION: GO
