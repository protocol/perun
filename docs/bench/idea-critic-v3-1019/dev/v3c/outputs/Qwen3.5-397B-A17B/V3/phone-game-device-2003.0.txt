Steelman: A converged device leveraging existing mobile distribution to disrupt a dedicated gaming monopoly by offering superior connectivity and multifunction utility at a premium.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists between the proposal's premises and the stated risks; the risks are execution challenges, not impossibilities. The "awkward hold" and "battery swap" are design trade-offs, not logical failures. The price premium is justified if the value proposition (phone + game) exceeds the sum of separate devices.

**Risks:** High. The market leader's library creates a massive switching cost. Publisher apathy could starve the platform. The form factor may satisfy neither gamers nor callers perfectly. The price point limits mass adoption initially.

**Edge:** The proposer is the *largest mobile phone maker*. This provides an installed base for distribution, manufacturing scale to lower costs over time, and a unique value proposition: a game device that is always with you and connected. The leader cannot easily replicate the phone function without diluting their dedicated gaming brand or entering the complex telecom hardware space.

**Decision Method:**
1.  **Alternatives:** Doing nothing cedes the convergent future to competitors. A cheaper, non-converged phone fails to capture the gaming upside. A dedicated console from a phone maker lacks the "always with you" edge.
2.  **Fit:** Aligns with a vision of multifunctional mobile dominance.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if publishers remain uncommitted. *Kill Criterion:* Secure commitments from 3 major publishers within 6 months, or pivot to first-party titles only.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal relies on the maker's existing scale (fact) and the market gap (assumed opportunity).
5.  **Conclusion:** The risks are manageable via staged rollout and aggressive publisher incentives. The strategic edge (distribution + convergence) outweighs the incumbent's library advantage in the long term. The "awkward" design is a necessary compromise for convergence, acceptable for an early adopter v1.

DECISION: GO
