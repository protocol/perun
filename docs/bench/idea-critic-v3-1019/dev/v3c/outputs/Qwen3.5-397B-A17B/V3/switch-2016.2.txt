**Steelman:** A hybrid device leverages the maker's unique IP and portability strength to create a new market category, bypassing direct specification wars with powerful rivals.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker is the **price-to-performance ratio**. Launching a modest-power device at parity with significantly more powerful rivals risks immediate rejection by core gamers who prioritize fidelity. The "confusing positioning" risk is critical; after a previous failure, clarity is mandatory. A "thin first-year game line-up" from only first-party studios is insufficient to sustain momentum if third-party support lags due to weak hardware.

*Alternative:* Launch at a lower price point to reflect the hardware gap, or delay until a stronger software library is secured. However, delaying cedes the handheld market entirely to phones.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a "blue ocean" strategy but fails if the value proposition ("play anywhere") does not outweigh the graphical compromise. If the hybrid nature is marketed poorly, it alienates both handheld and home audiences. The cost of delay is total erosion of the handheld lineage.

**Evidence & Edge:**
The proposal lacks evidence that consumers will accept current-gen pricing for last-gen performance. The only distinct **edge** is the specific form factor and the maker's iconic franchises, which rivals cannot replicate. However, relying solely on first-party titles in year one is a fragile bet against established ecosystems.

**Blockers:**
1. **Pricing Mismatch:** No justification provided for pricing a modest chip equal to powerful rivals without a clear value-add beyond form factor.
2. **Software Gap:** A thin first-party lineup is a known failure point for new platforms; no concrete third-party commitments are cited to mitigate this.

**Risks:** Market confusion, supply constraints, developer apathy.

**Decision:**
The proposal as stated contains a fatal pricing and content mismatch. Without a confirmed strategy to secure third-party support or a pricing adjustment that acknowledges the hardware deficit, the risk of repeating the previous financial loss is too high. The "edge" is not strong enough to overcome the "blocker" of poor price-to-performance perception at launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
