**Steelman:** Acquire the target to capture its high-value business segment and push-to-talk dominance, accepting $36B cost and technical debt as the price of immediate market share.

**Attack:** The core flaw is the assumption that "combining" incompatible networks creates value rather than destroying it. In 2005, data capabilities are the emerging growth vector; the target's network is explicitly "limited for data." Merging forces the buyer to maintain two parallel, incompatible infrastructures indefinitely or undertake a prohibitively expensive, high-risk migration of legacy hardware. The $36 billion valuation likely prices in the customer base while underestimating the "integration tax" of running dual legacy systems. Goodwill of this magnitude becomes a massive write-down risk if the promised synergies fail to materialize due to technical friction.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid $36B outlay and integration chaos; grow data organically.
2.  **Partner/Resell:** License the target's push-to-talk service without acquiring the incompatible network backbone.
3.  **Build/Buy Tech Only:** Acquire the push-to-talk application layer or build a compatible solution on the buyer's modernizing network, leaving the target's legacy infrastructure to decay.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-merger, integration costs have tripled, customer churn spikes due to service degradation during migration, and the legacy network drains capital needed for 3G/4G data rollout. The "high-value" customers leave for competitors with superior data offerings.

**Blockers:** The proposal lacks a credible, costed migration path for the incompatible technology that preserves the specific push-to-talk utility customers pay for. Without a defined endpoint where the legacy network is retired, the entity operates permanently at a structural cost disadvantage. The "limited data" attribute of the target contradicts the industry's trajectory, making the acquisition a bet on a dying technological paradigm.

**Edge:** None stated. Market share alone is not an edge if the underlying asset depreciates faster than the revenue it generates.

**Kill Criteria:** Halt if a detailed technical audit reveals the cost to migrate the push-to-talk service to the buyer's architecture exceeds 20% of the deal value, or if churn projections during integration exceed 15%.

DECISION: NO-GO
