**Steelman:** Transforming from a pure retailer into a hybrid marketplace creates immediate inventory depth and variety without capital expenditure, turning fixed inventory risk into variable commission revenue while capturing transactions even when the retailer cannot offer the lowest price.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary fear is cannibalization: used copies undercutting new sales. However, for an unprofitable retailer, losing a sale to a third-party seller (earning a fee) is superior to losing the customer entirely to a competitor or a user abandoning the search due to high price or stockouts. The proposal explicitly accepts lower control over service quality; this is a critical vulnerability. If third-party sellers fail, the platform's brand absorbs the reputational damage, potentially destroying the trust required to sustain the core retail business.

The "growing range of goods" suggests the retailer lacks scale in non-core categories. Allowing competitors there leverages their inventory to fill gaps immediately. Yet, the proposal lacks a mechanism to enforce service standards or handle disputes, which is fatal when inviting competitors onto the same product pages. Without a defined escrow system, strict performance metrics, or a clear "fulfillment by platform" requirement, the risk of brand erosion outweighs the potential commission revenue. The objection regarding publisher backlash is valid but manageable; the objection regarding uncontrolled customer experience is existential.

**Blockers:** No defined mechanism to guarantee third-party service quality or protect brand trust against misbehaving sellers.
**Risks:** Supplier retaliation, brand dilution, internal channel conflict.
**Edge:** Converting fixed inventory costs to variable revenue; capturing long-tail demand without capital outlay.

The plan fails the blocker test because it invites existential brand risk without the necessary operational guardrails (kill criteria for seller performance, escrow, or mandatory service levels). Approving this "as proposed" ignores the specific risk of brand destruction via unvetted third parties. It requires a revised operational model before execution.

DECISION: NO-GO
