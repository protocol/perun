**Steelman:** Launching a high-end, mouse-driven computer at $10,000 in 1983 targets a niche willing to pay for superior usability despite performance and software deficits.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the blocker test immediately. The price point ($10,000) is several times higher than leading business PCs, creating an insurmountable cost barrier for the stated target of general "office workers." In 1983, business procurement is driven by compatibility and cost-efficiency; this unit offers neither. The admitted slowness and lack of third-party software render it functionally inferior to cheaper alternatives for actual business tasks (spreadsheets, word processing).

Crucially, an internal alternative exists: a cheaper machine with a similar interface under development. This internal option represents a direct **better way**. It captures the usability upside (graphical interface) without the prohibitive cost or the baggage of the current hardware's performance limitations. Launching the expensive, slow model first cannibalizes the future cheaper model's market entry while likely failing to gain traction due to price.

The "edge" claimed (graphical interface) is not unique to this specific hardware configuration, especially given the internal competitor. There is no evidence that office workers in 1983 value a mouse enough to pay a 300%+ premium over established terminals, especially when the machine runs slowly. The risk of brand damage from releasing an overpriced, underpowered product is high.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Price Mismatch:** $10,000 exceeds market tolerance for "office workers" by a factor of several, contradicting the target demographic's budget reality.
2.  **Internal Substitution:** A cheaper internal alternative with the same core benefit (GUI) makes this specific high-cost implementation redundant and strategically unsound.
3.  **Functional Deficit:** Slowness and lack of software prevent core business utility, regardless of interface novelty.

**Edge:** None identified that justifies the price premium over existing or incoming internal solutions.

**Kill Criteria:** If a cheaper internal alternative exists with similar features, the high-cost variant cannot launch as the primary business offering.

**Decision:** The plan proposes a product that is too expensive, too slow, and lacks software support for its intended audience, while a better, cheaper alternative is already in the pipeline. It must not proceed as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
