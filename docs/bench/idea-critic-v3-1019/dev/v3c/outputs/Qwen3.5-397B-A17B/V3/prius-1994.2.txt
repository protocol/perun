**Steelman:** Committing now secures a decade-long technological lead, defines the brand as an innovator, and creates an insurmountable barrier to entry before competitors react.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "edge" test. With cheap fuel and no regulatory mandate, the market signal is negative. The "edge" cited is merely being first, which the guidelines explicitly reject as a valid advantage without proven demand. The core technology (battery durability, novel transmission) is unproven in volume production, creating massive warranty liability. This is a one-way door with high irreversible cost and no immediate upside.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain current profitable ICE lines; avoid catastrophic warranty costs.
2.  **Staged Pilot:** Develop the technology quietly for fleet/limited release to validate durability before a public mass-production commitment.
3.  **Wait:** Let competitors absorb the initial R&D risk and market education costs, then enter with a refined, cheaper solution once regulations or fuel prices shift.

**Pre-mortem:** By 1999, the program is canceled after billions in losses due to battery failures in cold weather and consumer refusal to pay a premium for fuel savings they don't value. The brand suffers reputational damage from unreliable "future tech."

**Blockers:**
*   **No Edge:** "Being first" is not an edge; demand is unproven and currently non-existent given cheap fuel.
*   **Contradictory Evidence:** The proposal admits "no near-term demand signal" and "unproven durability" yet demands a full mass-production commitment. This violates the requirement that the plan must work *as proposed* on stated facts.
*   **False Premise:** The plan assumes a bold public schedule drives adoption; history shows it drives skepticism when the product is unproven and expensive.

**Risks:** Massive warranty exposure, brand damage from reliability issues, capital misallocation away from profitable segments.

**Decision Logic:** A sound plan requires a named edge (asset, cost advantage, or shown demand). This plan has none. It relies on fear of falling behind and "strategic necessity," which are explicitly disqualified as edges. The unproven technology combined with zero market pull constitutes a factual blocker: the numbers do not work, and the core claim (that this will succeed as proposed) is contradicted by the lack of demand and technical validation.

DECISION: NO-GO
