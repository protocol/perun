Steelman: Rapidly acquiring 130–150 sites allows immediate scale to challenge the entrenched competitor before they can react, leveraging the retailer's domestic loyalty.

**Blockers:**
1. **Unproven Core Systems:** The proposal admits the supply chain and inventory systems are "not yet proven." Deploying unverified infrastructure across 150 international sites simultaneously creates a single point of catastrophic failure. If the system breaks, the entire network stalls with no fallback.
2. **Pace vs. Learning:** Opening "nearly all" stores within two years while simultaneously learning cross-border logistics, regulation, and bilingual labeling removes the ability to iterate. Errors in the first 10 stores will be replicated across the remaining 140 before corrections can be made.
3. **Capital Concentration:** Committing to 130–150 leases upfront eliminates flexibility. If price perception fails or the competitor retaliates aggressively, the retailer is locked into massive fixed costs in a foreign jurisdiction without a pilot to validate demand.

**Risks:**
Entrenched competitor retaliation, regulatory delays, bilingual labeling errors, and price perception mismatches. These are compounded by the lack of a phased approach.

**Edge:**
First-mover advantage in the discount segment for this specific retailer is negated by the existence of a larger, entrenched local competitor. There is no unique asset cited that guarantees success against an incumbent who already knows the local regulations and supply chains.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "one-way door" test. It combines maximum speed with maximum uncertainty (unproven systems, new market). A cheaper, safer alternative exists: a phased rollout (e.g., 10–20 stores) to prove the supply chain and inventory systems before committing to the full lease portfolio. The current plan bets the entire international venture on assumptions that have not been tested in the target environment. The risk of total systemic collapse due to unproven technology and operational inexperience outweighs the potential speed benefit.

DECISION: NO-GO
