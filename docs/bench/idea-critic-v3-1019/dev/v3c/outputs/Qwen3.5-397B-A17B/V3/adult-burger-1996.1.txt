**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can overwrite the "kids' meal" brand association, forcing adults to trial a premium product that proves the chain's culinary maturity.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on the assumption that awareness, not perception or product fit, is the bottleneck. Evidence suggests the barrier is deep-seated brand identity ("fast," "for kids") and a value-oriented customer base. A higher price point directly contradicts the established value proposition. The "largest advertising budget" approach ignores the risk of burning capital to drive traffic for a product the core demographic rejects and the target demographic distrusts coming from this specific brand.

**Better Way:** Test the product in a limited market with targeted, lower-cost media to validate demand before committing to an industry-record budget. Alternatively, introduce a "premium" line at a mid-tier price point to bridge the value gap.

**Pre-mortem:** The campaign launches, awareness spikes, but conversion remains flat. Adults perceive the burger as overpriced fast food, not genuine premium dining. The brand dilutes its core value image without gaining adult loyalty. The massive spend yields negative ROI, and the product is quietly discontinued, leaving the brand confused.

**Fit:** This contradicts the "value" north star implicit in a fast-food chain's core model. It attempts to change the brand rather than extend it.

**Blockers:**
1. **Brand-Product Mismatch:** The stated fact that "adults see the chain as fast, not premium" is a fundamental blocker that advertising alone rarely overcomes, especially when the price increases.
2. **Economic Contradiction:** Raising prices above the core menu for a demographic attracted by "value" lacks a proven mechanism for success.

**Edge:** None stated. Being first with a big budget is not an edge if the product-market fit is unproven and the brand equity works against the premium positioning.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It commits maximum resources to a strategy that directly fights the brand's current gravity without a validated bridge. The risks are not just executional; they are foundational to the brand's identity.

DECISION: NO-GO
