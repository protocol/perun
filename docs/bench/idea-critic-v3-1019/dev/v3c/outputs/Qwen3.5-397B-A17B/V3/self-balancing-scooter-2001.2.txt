**Steelman:** A novel, self-balancing transporter with strong backing can create a new personal mobility category despite high cost and regulatory ambiguity.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "edge" and "blocker" tests. The "edge" cited is publicity and investor prominence, which the framework explicitly rejects as valid advantages over rivals or proof of demand. Being first or well-funded is not a moat.

The risks listed are not merely "unproven"; they are structural blockers for a mass-market launch at $5,000.
1.  **Legal Status:** If the device is illegal on sidewalks and roads, it has nowhere to operate. This is a factual blocker, not a risk to be mitigated later.
2.  **Use Cases:** "Uses beyond institutional buyers are unproven" contradicts the plan for a 40,000-unit factory. Institutional sales alone cannot justify this capacity without confirmed contracts, which are not cited.
3.  **Price vs. Utility:** At $5,000, the device competes with used cars. With no cargo capacity and high social conspicuousness, the value proposition for a general consumer is nonexistent based on the provided facts.

The proposal attempts to skip validation by relying on hype ("heavy publicity"). A cheaper, safer alternative is a limited pilot with institutional buyers to prove utility and resolve legal status before committing to mass production. The current plan commits real money to a capped, time-boxed step? No, it commits to a full factory.

**Blockers:**
*   Legal prohibition on public pathways removes the operating environment.
*   Lack of proven consumer demand at the $5,000 price point.
*   Factory capacity (40k units) vastly exceeds confirmed institutional interest.

**Risks:** Social stigma, limited utility, high cost of failure.

**Edge:** None identified. Publicity and funding are not product advantages.

The plan does not beat "do nothing" or a "pilot first" approach. It commits to high fixed costs without solving the primary constraint: where and why a mass market would use this.

DECISION: NO-GO
