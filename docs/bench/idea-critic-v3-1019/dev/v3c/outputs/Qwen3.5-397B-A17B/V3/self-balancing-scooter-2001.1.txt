**Steelman:** A novel, self-balancing transporter leveraging high-profile backing can create a new personal mobility category, justifying a premium launch despite regulatory ambiguity.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the blocker test. The "unclear legal status" on sidewalks and roads is not a mere risk; it is a fundamental **blocker**. If the device cannot legally operate where people walk or drive, its utility collapses to zero for the mass market, rendering the $5,000 price point and 40,000-unit factory capacity immediately unjustifiable. A product requiring new legislation before use cannot sustain a full-scale factory launch; it requires a pilot in controlled, private environments first.

Furthermore, the "edge" is weak. Being first, having publicity, or possessing investor backing are not sustainable advantages against the stated realities: high cost, limited utility (cannot carry much), and social friction (riders feeling conspicuous). The "do nothing" or "pilot first" alternatives are superior because they avoid the catastrophic sunk cost of a 40,000-unit factory before proving demand or legality. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of building inventory for a product that may be banned from public spaces.

The pre-mortem is clear: The company builds the factory, launches, and immediately faces regulatory bans in major cities, leaving them with massive fixed costs and unsellable inventory. The kill criteria for a "GO" would require resolved legal pathways in target markets, which are explicitly stated as missing.

**Blockers:** Legal status on public infrastructure is undefined, preventing core use cases.
**Risks:** High price, unproven demand, social stigma, limited utility.
**Edge:** None identified beyond temporary publicity and first-mover status, which do not overcome regulatory barriers.

The plan commits to full-scale production before validating the single most critical dependency: legality. This violates the principle of staging investment against uncertainty. The correct path is a constrained pilot, not a full launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
