**Steelman:** Switching to the dominant architecture solves the thermal/performance bottleneck preventing thin laptops, using a translation layer to preserve the existing software ecosystem during a two-year transition.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the "freeze" risk: announcing a future switch cannibalizes current sales before the new units ship. However, staying on the lagging roadmap guarantees failure in the emerging thin-and-light category; "do nothing" yields no competitive product. The alternative of a hybrid line (splitting the brand) increases complexity and dilutes the "distinctiveness" concern more than a unified shift.

Regarding developer friction: history shows developers follow market share. Once the hardware advantage is proven, rebuilding native apps becomes a priority, not a barrier. The translation layer bridges this gap. While translated performance is lower, native performance on the new chips outweighs the speed of the old architecture. The "painful earlier transition" is a sunk cost; avoiding change due to past trauma is a strategic error when the current path leads to obsolescence.

**Fit & Edge:**
The company's edge is vertical integration (hardware + OS + silicon strategy). This allows them to control the translation layer's quality and the user experience in a way clone-makers cannot. The distinctiveness shifts from "unique chip" to "unique experience on superior hardware."

**Pre-mortem & Kill Criteria:**
Failure mode: The translation layer is too slow for pro users, causing an exodus.
Mitigation: The two-year window allows simultaneous shipping of native and translated apps.
Kill Criterion: If native app adoption by key developers does not reach 20% within 12 months of the first ship date, the transition timeline must be accelerated or the translation performance re-architected.

**Decision:**
The risk of sales freeze is real but manageable via marketing cadence; the risk of product irrelevance is fatal. The proposal addresses the core constraint (thermal/efficiency) that the current supplier cannot solve. The edge (integration) mitigates the transition pain. No factual blocker exists that invalidates the core thesis; the risks are execution challenges, not show-stoppers.

DECISION: GO
