**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 validates demand for a head-worn display while mitigating mass-market privacy backlash through exclusivity and controlled distribution.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "everyday consumer product" positioning test against its own constraints. A $1,500 price point excludes 99% of consumers, contradicting the mass-market intent. More critically, the identified risks are not merely "challenges" but fundamental blockers for the stated use case:
1.  **Privacy:** A always-on camera in public spaces invites immediate regulatory scrutiny and social hostility, potentially resulting in bans from private venues (restaurants, gyms) before general availability.
2.  **Utility:** "No clear everyday use" is a fatal flaw for a consumer device. Early adopters tolerate bugs, not uselessness. Without a "killer app," the device becomes a costly novelty.
3.  **Social Acceptance:** Wearing a computer on one's face creates a barrier to human interaction, the very context the device claims to enhance.
4.  **Battery:** Short battery life renders an "everyday" device unreliable, causing user churn immediately post-purchase.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids reputational damage from a high-profile flop.
*   **Enterprise Pivot:** Target industrial/logistics use cases where utility is clear, privacy is managed, and price is justified by productivity gains. This removes the "social acceptance" and "everyday utility" blockers.
*   **Staged Utility:** Launch strictly as a developer kit with no consumer positioning until a clear use case emerges.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, early adopters return it within 30 days due to lack of utility and social awkwardness, media narratives cement it as a "privacy nightmare," and the brand suffers long-term association with failure.

**Edge:** None identified. Being first to market with a flawed product is not an edge; it is a liability. The proposal relies on the hope that users will discover utility, rather than demonstrating it exists.

**Blockers:**
*   No verified "everyday use" case (stated in proposal).
*   High probability of venue bans due to privacy (inferred from camera function).
*   Price contradicts mass-market positioning.

**Risks:** Social rejection, regulatory action, rapid churn.

**Decision:** The proposal attempts to sell a solution without a verified problem to a mass market that cannot afford it and may legally reject it.

DECISION: NO-GO
