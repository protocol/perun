**Steelman:** Acquiring a modern, object-oriented OS core from a small vendor is the only viable path to rapidly replace a failing, dated system after two internal failures, leveraging external innovation to arrest market share decline.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Continued market share erosion and eventual irrelevance given two prior internal failures.
*   **License Alternative:** Perpetual licensing fees, lack of control over roadmap, and potential integration mismatches; ownership provides strategic autonomy.
*   **Internal Build (Again):** Proven failure mode; time-to-market is too slow against current decline.

**Blockers:** None. The low unit sales of the target are irrelevant; the asset being purchased is the technology and engineering talent, not current revenue. The high price relative to revenue is a standard premium for strategic IP acquisition, not a financial blocker. The founder's difficult history is mitigated by the limited "advisor" role rather than full operational control. Migration risk exists but is inferior to the certainty of failure with the current OS.

**Edge:** The target's architecture is explicitly "modern" and "object-oriented," directly solving the "dated" core problem that caused previous internal failures. The acquisition converts a fixed R&D failure into a purchased solution with a compressed timeline.

**Kill Criteria:** If due diligence reveals the object-oriented core cannot support the manufacturer's essential hardware drivers within 6 months, or if the founder refuses the advisor role, the deal terminates. Leading metric: Developer beta adoption rate of the new core within 9 months post-acquisition.

**Verdict:** The proposal addresses a critical existential threat where all other options have failed or are inferior. The risks are manageable compared to the status quo. The cost is justified by the strategic necessity of owning the core technology to ensure survival.

DECISION: GO
