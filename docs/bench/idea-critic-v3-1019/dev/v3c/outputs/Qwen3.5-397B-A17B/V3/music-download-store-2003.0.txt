**Steelman:** Leveraging a dominant hardware ecosystem to create the first legal, convenient, and reasonably priced alternative to piracy, using music as a loss-leader to drive device sales.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's constraints (proprietary format, low margin) are strategic features, not failures. Low margins are acceptable if the primary goal is ecosystem lock-in rather than direct store profit. Proprietary formats ensure the hardware remains the only way to access the purchased library, directly countering the "free" alternative with exclusivity.

**Risks:** Labels withdrawing licenses is a valid threat, but the alternative for them is zero revenue from piracy; the proposal offers a revenue stream they currently lack. Rival subscriptions address a different use case (rental vs. ownership); history suggests consumers prefer owning specific tracks. The "widespread free sharing" is the problem being solved, not a reason to abstain; the friction of piracy is the opportunity.

**Edge:** The company already sells the portable player well. This creates an immediate installed base with a verified willingness to pay for hardware. No rival has this specific hardware-software integration at scale in 2003. The "99 cents" price point psychologically undercuts the perceived risk of trying a legal service compared to album purchases.

**Decision Logic:** The "do nothing" option guarantees continued erosion of value via piracy and potential hardware commoditization. The proposed path monetizes the ecosystem and creates a moat. The risks are manageable compared to the existential threat of irrelevance in a digital music future. The low margin is a calculated subsidy for hardware dominance.

**Kill Criteria:** If major labels refuse to license within 6 months, or if attachment rates (songs per device) remain below 1.0 after one year, the strategy fails.

**Leading Metric:** Songs sold per active device per month. Review date: Q4 2003.

DECISION: GO
