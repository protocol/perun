**Steelman:** Differentiate via novel interaction (dual-screen/touch) and price to expand the market beyond core gamers, rather than losing a spec war against a superior graphical rival.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that unusual hardware will fracture the developer ecosystem, resulting in a software drought that kills the platform regardless of price. However, maintaining the older handheld on sale mitigates this by preserving the existing library and developer revenue stream while the new platform matures.

Matching the rival's graphics ("Do Nothing" relative to innovation) guarantees a loss in a commodity hardware battle where the competitor already holds the advantage. The "better way" is not to build a cheaper clone, but to change the game entirely. The risk of touch being "unproven" is precisely the opportunity; if it works, the rival's graphical superiority becomes irrelevant to the new use cases created.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a "blue ocean" strategy: making the competition irrelevant by offering unique value. If we assume failure, it is likely due to poor first-party software support failing to demonstrate the touch utility, not the hardware concept itself. The cost of delay is ceding the "innovator" narrative to the rival permanently.

**Evidence & Edge:**
The proposal relies on the fact that the rival cannot easily copy the dual-screen form factor without alienating their current high-fidelity user base or redesigning their entire architecture. The edge is the installed base of the older handheld, which subsidizes the transition and reduces the immediate pressure for the new device to have 1,000 launch titles. The "gimmick" accusation is a standard response to disruption; it is neutralized only by killer applications that utilize the second screen for gameplay, not just menus.

**Decision Logic:**
No factual blocker exists. The risks (developer confusion, unproven tech) are inherent to the strategy but are managed by the dual-console approach. The edge (market expansion, lower price, unique IP potential) outweighs the risk of sticking to a losing graphical arms race.

Blockers: NONE
Risks: Software adoption speed, developer learning curve.
Edge: Incumbent legacy library + unique form factor creating a new market segment.

DECISION: GO
