**Steelman:** Rapidly acquiring 130–150 sites leverages immediate scale to preempt the entrenched competitor, betting that speed outweighs the complexity of unproven cross-border logistics and new inventory systems.

**Attack:** The proposal conflates "market entry" with "instant saturation." Opening nearly all stores within two years while simultaneously building a new supply chain and inventory system creates a single point of catastrophic failure: operational collapse. If the new inventory system glitches (highly probable for unproven tech in a new regulatory environment), 130+ stores face stockouts simultaneously, destroying brand reputation before it establishes. The "larger, entrenched competitor" can easily outlast a supply-chain-starved newcomer. The risk is not just "pace"; it is the coupling of maximum physical exposure with minimum operational validation.

**Better Way:** A staged rollout (e.g., 10–15 stores) allows the supply chain and inventory system to stabilize under real load before scaling capital commitment. This preserves the "low price" value proposition by ensuring shelves are actually stocked.

**Fit & Edge:** The retailer's "loyal domestic following" offers no edge in a neighboring country with different regulations, labeling laws, and a dominant local rival. There is no stated asset (e.g., exclusive supplier contracts, proprietary tech already tested across borders) that guarantees success where others might fail. Being first to sign leases is not an edge if the stores cannot operate profitably.

**Pre-mortem:** One year in, the inventory system fails to handle bilingual labeling complexities, causing customs delays. 120 stores sit half-empty. Cash burn from leases and failed logistics exhausts the expansion budget. The brand is perceived as "empty" rather than "discount."

**Blockers:**
1. **Operational Coupling:** Scaling to 130+ stores before the supply chain/inventory system is proven creates a non-recoverable failure mode.
2. **Lack of Edge:** No specific advantage cited to overcome the entrenched competitor or regulatory hurdles.

**Risks:** Total capital loss, brand damage in a new market, inability to pivot due to long-term lease commitments.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. The scale is unjustified by the operational readiness.

DECISION: NO-GO
