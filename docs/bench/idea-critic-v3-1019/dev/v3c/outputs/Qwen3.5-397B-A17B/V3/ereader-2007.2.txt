**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled e-reader removes friction for book buyers and solves publisher piracy fears via a closed ecosystem, creating a new high-margin revenue stream that complements rather than cannibalizes print.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces severe execution risks: the company lacks hardware DNA, and dedicated devices have historically failed. However, the "do nothing" option cedes the digital future to competitors while print sales inevitably stagnate. The specific combination of a paper-like screen (addressing eye strain) and free cellular connectivity (removing the need for a PC) directly attacks the two primary adoption barriers of previous failed devices.

Regarding margins and publisher relations: a single low price point simplifies the consumer decision, driving volume that offsets lower per-unit margins. Piracy fears are mitigated by the proprietary format and seamless delivery, offering publishers a safer channel than open PDFs or early DRM-free attempts. While cannibalization is possible, it is preferable to lose a print sale to your own digital edition than to a pirate or a future competitor's device.

The "hardware inexperience" blocker is real but surmountable through partnership or acquisition, which is implied by the urgency of the market shift. The cost of delay is catastrophic; waiting for the market to mature means entering as a follower rather than defining the category. The edge lies in the massive existing catalog and customer trust, which no pure hardware manufacturer possesses.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of hardware experience is a risk, not a factual impossibility, given the company's resources.
**Risks:** Hardware supply chain failures, slower-than-expected adoption, publisher pushback on pricing.
**Edge:** Unmatched catalog depth, existing customer billing relationships, and brand trust in books specifically.

**Kill Criteria:** If device adoption does not reach 5% of active customers within 18 months, or if publisher participation remains below 50% of top titles by launch, pivot to a software-only app strategy.

**Leading Metric:** Number of titles downloaded per active device per month (engagement > hardware sales). Review date: 12 months post-launch.

DECISION: GO
