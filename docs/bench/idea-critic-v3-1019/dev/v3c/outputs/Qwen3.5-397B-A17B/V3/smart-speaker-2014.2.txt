**Steelman:** Leverage existing retail trust and member loyalty to pioneer a new home category, using invitation-only scarcity to manage early voice-recognition limitations while gathering unique household data.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly mitigates the "bad hardware launch" risk via invitation-only rollout and addresses price sensitivity with member discounts. The lack of a category is an opportunity for first-mover advantage, not a factual blocker.

**Risks:** Privacy backlash regarding always-on microphones; voice failure rates frustrating users; category education costs. These are valid execution risks but do not invalidate the core claim given the staged rollout.

**Edge:** The retailer possesses a massive existing base of paying members willing to trust the brand with shopping lists, a specific use case phone assistants do not fully own in the home context. The $99 member price creates an immediate value wedge competitors cannot match without a subscription ecosystem.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if privacy fears spike before trust is established. **Kill Criterion:** If opt-out requests exceed 15% of invited members within the first 30 days, halt broader rollout.

**Decision Method:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the smart home to tech giants. "Phone-only" misses the stationary home utility. This plan is the cheapest viable entry point compared to building a full phone ecosystem again.
2.  **Fit:** Aligns with expanding retail presence into the home (North Star).
3.  **Evidence:** Poor phone sales indicate a need for hardware reset, not abandonment; the member base provides the initial distribution channel missing from pure tech startups.
4.  **Door/Delay:** Two-way door (can stop production); cost of delay is high as competitors are likely exploring similar always-on concepts.

The plan survives the attack because it converts the company's specific assets (members, retail trust) into a defensive moat against the stated risks. The invitation model turns the "no category exists" risk into a controlled experiment.

DECISION: GO
