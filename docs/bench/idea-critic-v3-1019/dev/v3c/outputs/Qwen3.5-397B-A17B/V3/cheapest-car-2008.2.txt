**Steelman:** A dedicated high-volume factory enables a $2,500 price point, successfully migrating scooter families to four-wheel safety despite land protests and stigma.

**Attack & Options:**
The "cheapest" label is a fatal positioning error. In automotive markets, low price signals low safety, directly contradicting the core value proposition of protecting families. If buyers perceive the car as unsafe due to cost-cutting, they will retain scooters or buy used cars, which offer perceived higher quality at similar price points. The "do nothing" option (waiting for land issues to resolve or using existing capacity) avoids the sunk cost of a controversial new factory while the brand reputation is tested.

The land protests represent a critical execution blocker. A new dedicated factory cannot reach the necessary volume targets if construction is delayed or halted by civil unrest. Unlike a brownfield expansion, a greenfield site on contested land introduces unquantifiable timeline risks that destroy the thin margins of a ultra-low-cost model.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because the "cheapest" marketing alienates the aspirational middle class, while land delays prevent economies of scale, causing unit costs to exceed the $2,500 cap.

**Evidence & Constraints:**
The proposal admits buyers have cheaper alternatives (scooters, used cars). Without a distinct quality or safety advantage proven independently of the "cheap" label, demand is unproven. The factory site conflict is a stated fact, not a risk; it is an active barrier to the "high volume" requirement.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** Active land protests threaten the dedicated factory's completion; the "cheapest" positioning undermines the safety narrative required to convert scooter users.
*   **Risks:** Safety perception, margin erosion from delays, preference for used vehicles.
*   **Edge:** None stated. Being first to a low price point is not an edge if the product is stigmatized and the supply chain is contested.

The plan lacks a viable path to overcome the stigma of "cheapest" while simultaneously securing the land required for volume production. The combination of reputational headwinds and physical obstruction creates a failure mode that the current proposal does not address.

DECISION: NO-GO
