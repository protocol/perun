**Steelman:** Embedding general-purpose compute capability into every consumer GPU creates a ubiquitous, low-cost supercomputing fabric, positioning the company as the foundational hardware layer for future scientific and AI workloads before competitors recognize the shift.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the blocker test. The core failure mode is economic and strategic misalignment with the company's current reality. The company's revenue is almost entirely dependent on the gaming and workstation markets, engaged in a fierce, immediate "race" with a main rival. In this context, adding silicon area for features with **no committed customers** and **no large paying market** directly increases the unit cost of every product sold.

In a cutthroat commodity hardware race, increasing costs without an immediate, corresponding performance benefit for the primary customer base (gamers) is a fatal error. It risks ceding price/performance leadership to the rival, potentially destroying the core business that funds any future R&D. The "edge" cited is speculative future demand, while the "blocker" is present-day margin compression and competitive vulnerability.

The "better way" is to wait for the market to signal clear demand or for the core business to generate sufficient excess cash flow to subsidize such a moonshot without endangering its market share. Alternatively, explore software-only hacks or limited professional SKUs rather than burdening every mass-market unit.

While the vision of GPU computing is historically vindicated, the specific proposal to fund this via a mandatory cost increase on every consumer chip during a fierce competitive battle, with zero revenue visibility, constitutes a blocker. The risk of losing the core business outweighs the potential of a non-existent market. The proposal lacks a viable path to profitability or a safeguard for the core business.

**Blockers:** Increased unit cost on 100% of products with no immediate ROI; high risk of losing the core gaming market share to rivals during a critical competitive window; no committed customers to validate demand.
**Risks:** Engineering distraction; niche demand remaining niche; burning cash on unproven technology.
**Edge:** None currently verifiable; purely speculative first-mover advantage in a market that does not yet exist.

DECISION: NO-GO
