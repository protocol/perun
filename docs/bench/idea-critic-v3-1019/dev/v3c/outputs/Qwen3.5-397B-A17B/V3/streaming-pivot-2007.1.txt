The claim is that a profitable DVD leader should add free streaming in 2007 despite high costs, poor quality, and cannibalization risks. Steelman: This is a defensive necessity to retain subscribers and learn the medium before competitors make physical media obsolete, leveraging existing cash flow to fund the transition.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The "edge" is undefined; being first with an inferior product (small catalog, poor quality) against well-funded tech entrants is a liability, not an asset. The proposal admits broadband is not universal, limiting the immediate addressable market. Crucially, the plan offers "no extra charge," destroying potential revenue to subsidize a loss-leading service with high licensing fees, directly threatening the core profit engine without a clear migration path or kill criteria.

The "do nothing" alternative preserves the highly profitable monopoly while letting others bear the R&D cost of fixing streaming economics. A "better way" exists: license a small subset of content for a *premium* add-on to test willingness-to-pay, rather than giving it away free and inviting cannibalization. The current plan bets the entire company on a hypothesis that streaming can immediately compete on quality and catalog depth, which the proposal itself admits is false.

The strongest attack is the lack of a defined stopping point. If licensing costs spiral and cannibalization accelerates without new revenue, the plan has no tripwire. Without a premium tier to validate unit economics, this is not a strategic pivot; it is an uncontrolled bleed of capital into a nascent market where the company holds no advantage. The risks (licensing, cannibalization, tech cost) are not just "risks" here; they are guaranteed outcomes based on the stated facts, with no offsetting edge.

Blockers: The plan destroys margin on the core business to fund a service explicitly described as inferior to future competition, with no revenue model to offset licensing costs.
Risks: Accelerated cannibalization, cash burn, failure to differentiate against tech giants.
Edge: None identified; "existing subscribers" is a distribution channel, not a product advantage for a worse product.

DECISION: NO-GO
