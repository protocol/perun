**Steelman:** Launching a distinct mid-priced division captures a growing market segment between the company's existing low and premium lines, leveraging extensive consumer research to compete directly with the industry leader's successful mid-tier brands.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on the assumption that current growth trends will persist into the 1957 debut. However, the text explicitly acknowledges a "recession risk" and that the segment "could shrink." History demonstrates that mid-priced discretionary purchases are the first to collapse during economic downturns. Furthermore, the requirement to build a "new dealer network" creates massive fixed costs and channel conflict with existing franchises before a single unit is sold. If the market contracts at launch, the company faces high overhead with plummeting volume, threatening the financial stability of the entire corporation. The "extensive market research" reflects past desires, not future economic reality.

**Alternatives:** 
1. **Do Nothing:** Avoid capital expenditure on new dealers and tooling; preserve cash during potential recession.
2. **Badge Engineering:** Utilize existing dealer networks to sell mid-priced variants under current brands, reducing infrastructure risk.
3. **Delay:** Postpone launch until economic indicators stabilize post-1957.

**Fit:** While the strategy aims to fill a portfolio gap, it misaligns with economic cyclicality by committing to high fixed costs exactly when a downturn is anticipated.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch coincides with a recession; consumers trade down to lower-priced models or delay purchases. The new dealer network, lacking volume, goes bankrupt or demands costly bailouts. The brand becomes synonymous with failure, damaging the parent company's reputation.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blocker:** The combination of a known recession risk, a shrinking segment possibility, and the requirement for a costly, unproven dealer network creates a fatal financial exposure. The plan proceeds as if growth is guaranteed despite stated contrary evidence.
*   **Edge:** None cited that outweighs the macroeconomic threat.
*   **Verdict:** The proposal fails the blocker test. It commits irreversible infrastructure spend based on optimistic timing in a volatile market.

DECISION: NO-GO
