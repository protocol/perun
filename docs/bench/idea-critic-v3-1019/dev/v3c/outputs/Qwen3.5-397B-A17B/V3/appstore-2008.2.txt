**Steelman:** A curated, single-store model with a 30% fee balances developer monetization and user security, transforming the device from a closed appliance into a platform while maintaining brand polish against open rivals.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that centralized review creates a bottleneck, stifling innovation and angering carriers who lose control over content. However, the "walled garden" directly addresses the stated fear of instability; open distribution (the rival approach) would likely result in a fragmented, crash-prone experience that validates the CEO's previous skepticism of web apps. The 30% fee is high, but it funds the curation infrastructure necessary to guarantee the "polish" the executive team prizes.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Retain web-apps only. Risk: The device becomes obsolete as rivals offer deeper hardware integration via native code.
2.  **Open Sideloading:** Mimic rivals. Risk: High probability of malware and instability, damaging the brand's reputation for quality immediately.
3.  **Carrier-Managed Store:** Cede control to networks. Risk: Fragmented user experience and slow update cycles; carriers lack software curation expertise.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a strategy of premium differentiation. If we assume failure, it is because the review process is too slow, causing developers to abandon the platform for open rivals. However, the alternative (no apps) guarantees abandonment by power users. The cost of delay is total irrelevance in the emerging software ecosystem.

**Edge:**
The edge is not being first, but being *trusted*. Rivals have ecosystems, but they lack a unified, high-quality gatekeeper that assures users every app works flawlessly. This turns the "closed" nature from a bug into a feature.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks (workload, carrier friction) are operational hurdles, not fundamental flaws, provided the review team scales.
**Risks:** Review backlog slowing time-to-market; carrier retaliation.
**Edge:** Unified quality control that open rivals cannot match without fracturing their user base.

**Kill Criteria:** If developer sign-ups do not reach 500 within six months, or if the review backlog exceeds 14 days consistently by launch, pivot to a tiered review system. Leading metric: Number of approved apps in store at launch.

DECISION: GO
