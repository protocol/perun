**Steelman:** A dominant hardware vendor leverages its installed base to launch a legal, per-song store at $0.99, securing major label licenses to convert file-sharers into paying customers despite margin pressure and platform restrictions.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that free alternatives and rival subscription models will render a paid, per-song model uncompetitive. However, the "free" option carries legal risk and friction (viruses, poor quality, complex interfaces) that a segment of users will pay to eliminate. Rivals betting on subscriptions face a different adoption curve; the per-song model matches existing consumer mental models of ownership.

The margin constraint is real but secondary to the strategic imperative: without a legal source, the hardware itself becomes a liability in copyright lawsuits. The store is not primarily a profit center initially; it is an ecosystem moat. The restriction to own computers is a feature, not a bug, in 2003—it drives hardware sales, which carry the bulk of the margin. The risk of labels withdrawing licenses is mitigated by the vendor's unique leverage: they are the only partner offering a seamless path to monetization that protects the labels' core business while embracing digital distribution.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the north star of selling portable players. If this fails, it is because labels sue the hardware into oblivion or the interface is too clunky. The cost of delay is catastrophic: waiting cedes the legal digital market to fragmented rivals or entrenches piracy permanently.

**Blockers:** NONE. The cited risks (margin, competition, platform lock-in) are managed by the strategy of using software to sell high-margin hardware.
**Risks:** Label relations, slow adoption vs. free.
**Edge:** Integrated hardware/software control, massive existing user base, brand trust, and being the first to offer a legal, simple alternative at scale.

**Kill Criteria:** If major labels refuse licensing within 6 months, or if attach rate (songs per user) remains below 1 after 12 months, pivot or shut down the store. Leading metric: Weekly song sales volume.

DECISION: GO
