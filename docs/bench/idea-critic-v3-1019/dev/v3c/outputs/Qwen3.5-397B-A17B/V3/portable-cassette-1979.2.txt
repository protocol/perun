**Steelman:** A low-cost, portable, playback-only cassette device leveraging existing components can unlock a new youth demographic by prioritizing affordability and personal mobility over traditional hi-fi fidelity and recording utility.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Dealer Bias:** Staff skepticism reflects current adult-centric norms, not youth desire. The "antisocial" headphone critique ignores the emerging value of private, mobile soundscapes for commuters and students.
2.  **Market Research Gap:** No data exists because the category is undefined. Waiting for validation cedes first-mover advantage. The product *creates* the data upon launch.
3.  **Copycat Risk:** While competitors can copy the technology, they cannot copy the brand's momentum or the specific price-point achieved through the initial large production run. Speed to market is the only defensible moat here.
4.  **Financial Leverage:** The requirement for a large initial run to hit the target price is a high-stakes gamble. However, using existing parts minimizes R&D sunk costs. The risk is inventory, not development.
5.  **Strategic Fit:** This pivots the company from stationary "equipment" to lifestyle "accessories." It beats "doing nothing" (stagnation in a mature market) and "full recording" (too expensive/complex for the target).

**Pre-mortem:** The device fails if the price misses the youth budget by even a small margin, or if the battery life is insufficient for all-day portability. If the price holds, the lack of recording becomes a feature (simplicity), not a bug.

**Edge:** The proposer controls the supply chain of existing parts to hit a price point competitors cannot match immediately without retooling. The brand reputation for quality transfers trust to this new form factor.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of market research is a characteristic of disruptive innovation, not a fatal flaw. The dealer doubt is a distribution hurdle, not a product failure.
**Risks:** Inventory overhang if price target is missed; slow adoption due to cultural friction regarding headphones.
**Kill Criteria:** If the unit cost cannot be maintained within 10% of the target youth price point after the first production run, halt expansion.

The proposal addresses a latent need for personal mobility that current "hi-fi" definitions obscure. The risks are manageable; the opportunity to define a new category is singular.

DECISION: GO
