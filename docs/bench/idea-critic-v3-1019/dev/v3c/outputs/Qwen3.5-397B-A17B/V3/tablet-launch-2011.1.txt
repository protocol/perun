**Steelman:** Leverage the computer maker's brand and capital to force a third ecosystem into existence by subsidizing developer adoption and buying market awareness, betting that hardware quality can overcome software scarcity.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "edge" test. In two-sided markets (hardware/users vs. developers), a small app catalog is a fatal blocker, not a risk. Users will not pay a market-leading price for a device with a fraction of the utility, regardless of advertising spend. Advertising amplifies a value proposition; it cannot invent one. The "computer maker" brand does not transfer sufficient trust to overcome the "unproven" stigma of the OS when the core utility (apps) is missing.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve cash; avoid a high-burn failure in a winner-take-most market.
2.  **Niche/Low-Cost:** Launch at a significantly lower price point to target price-sensitive users who care less about apps, accepting lower margins to build install base first.
3.  **Delay:** Wait until the app catalog reaches a critical threshold before launching, sacrificing first-mover timing for product viability.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches with heavy fanfare. Early adopters buy it, discover they cannot run essential software, return the units, and label the platform "dead on arrival." Developer outreach fails because the installed base shrinks rather than grows. The company burns hundreds of millions in ad spend and exits the mobile market entirely within two years.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price-Utility Mismatch:** Pricing "in line with the market leader" while offering a "small fraction" of the apps creates an immediate economic dissonance that marketing cannot fix.
*   **No Named Edge:** Brand heritage and ad spend are not edges against a dominant network effect; they are table stakes that the competitor already exceeds.

**Verdict:** The plan attempts to solve a supply-side problem (apps) with demand-side spending (ads) while charging a premium price. This is a guaranteed capital destruction event. The proposal must be rejected unless the pricing strategy is fundamentally altered to undercut the leader drastically, or the launch is delayed until the app gap closes. As proposed, it is unsound.

DECISION: NO-GO
