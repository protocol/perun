Steelman: Acquiring the dominant code-hosting platform secures the industry's central nervous system, neutralizing a potential rival while converting a hostile open-source pivot into genuine credibility through stewardship rather than competition.

However, the claim fails the blocker test. The proposal admits the target is loss-making with no permanent CEO, yet offers no specific turnaround plan or named successor, relying entirely on the assumption that "running independently" preserves value. This is a fatal gap; independence without leadership accelerates decay. The $7.5 billion price tag for a money-losing asset lacks a defined path to profitability, making the ROI purely speculative.

The stated risk of "developer defection" is not merely a risk but an active, verified threat given the community's vocal distrust and the existence of free alternatives. The proposal offers no concrete mechanism to arrest this churn beyond a promise of autonomy, which history suggests is insufficient to quell cultural hostility. A cheaper, safer alternative exists: deepening the buyer's own hosted services or partnering without the massive balance-sheet exposure and integration debt of a full acquisition.

The "edge" cited is merely size and fear of falling behind, which the decision framework explicitly rejects as valid justification. There is no unique asset or cost advantage demonstrated that rivals cannot replicate organically. The plan commits real capital without a capped, time-boxed first step or a leading metric to validate the cultural integration hypothesis before full closure. The absence of a kill criterion for developer retention rates post-announcement means failure cannot be detected early enough to pivot.

Because the proposal relies on unverified assumptions regarding leadership stability and community tolerance, and fails to beat the "do nothing" or "partner" alternatives on verified financial grounds, it cannot proceed as written.

DECISION: NO-GO
