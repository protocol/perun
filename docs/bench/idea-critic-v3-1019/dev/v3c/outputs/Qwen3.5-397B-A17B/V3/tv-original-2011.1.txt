**Steelman:** Leverage proprietary viewing data to bypass the pilot system, commissioning a high-profile original series released entirely at once to differentiate the brand and lock in subscribers before licensing costs become unsustainable.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the "no pilot" approach combined with a two-season commitment. In 2011, no online-only service has proven it can produce prestige drama; broadcasters use pilots specifically to mitigate the massive financial risk of unknown audience reception for scripted content. While viewing data informs *what* users watch, it does not prove they will pay for or engage with *original* narrative content from this specific provider. The alternative—"do nothing" or sticking to licensed content—preserves capital but accepts rising costs and lack of differentiation. A cheaper, safer alternative exists: commission a single-season order or a traditional pilot to test production capability and audience response before committing 26 episodes.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
The proposal fits a "north star" of differentiation, but assumes data translates directly to original content success. If the show fails (high probability given the unproven model), the sunk cost of two seasons could cripple the budget, leaving no funds for recovery. The "all-at-once" release is an unproven distribution mechanic that may confuse audiences accustomed to weekly appointments.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal relies on internal viewing data as proof of demand. However, this data reflects consumption of *licensed* content, not original programming. There is no evidence that the specific director or star guarantees success in a streaming-only format. The lack of a pilot removes the industry-standard circuit breaker for failure.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** Yes. Committing to two full seasons without a pilot or prior original production track record creates an unacceptable financial exposure based on unverified assumptions about data translating to original hits.
*   **Edge:** None proven. Data on licensed content is not a defensible edge for original production success.
*   **Risk:** Catastrophic budget loss if the show fails; brand damage if quality is poor.

The plan fails the blocker test: the cost structure (2 seasons) is not justified by the evidence (licensed viewing data). A one-season order with a pilot phase would address the risk while testing the hypothesis.

DECISION: NO-GO
