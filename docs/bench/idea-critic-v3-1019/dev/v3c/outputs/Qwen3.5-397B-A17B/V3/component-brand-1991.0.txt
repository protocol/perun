**Steelman:** Transform an invisible component into a consumer demand driver by subsidizing OEM ads, forcing buyers to specify the brand and locking out clones.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces three structural threats: channel conflict (OEM resentment), budget cannibalization (diverting R&D or sales funds), and category inappropriateness (consumers don't buy chips). However, the "clone" threat described is existential; if buyers cannot distinguish the premium product from inferior copies, price becomes the only differentiator, destroying margins.

*   **Skeptic Check:** The premise that "buyers do not know which chip is in their computer" is the root cause of vulnerability. Without brand pull, the chip maker is entirely at the mercy of computer makers' pricing decisions. The risk of OEM resentment is real but manageable: the subsidy acts as a financial incentive that outweighs the loss of creative control. If an OEM refuses, they forfeit free marketing spend, a hard argument to reject internally.
*   **Better Way:** "Do nothing" guarantees commoditization. Alternative B2B sales pushes cannot solve an end-user perception problem. There is no cheaper path to differentiate a hidden component than making it visible at the point of purchase.
*   **Kill Criteria:** The program must be tied strictly to verified sales lift or specific "logo-in-ad" compliance. If OEMs fail to display the logo prominently, funding stops.

**Edge:** The chip maker holds the monopoly on the "premium" performance narrative. Clones cannot fake the brand equity once established. By shifting the battlefield from technical specs (where clones mimic) to consumer desire (where clones are invisible), the proposal creates a moat that competitors cannot cross without massive consumer spend of their own.

**Verdict:** The risks are executional, not strategic. The cost of inaction (commoditization) exceeds the cost of the program. The subsidy buys leverage over OEMs and creates a direct line to the end user, bypassing the confusion of similar part numbers.

DECISION: GO
