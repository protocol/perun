Steelman: Transform an invisible component into a consumer demand driver by subsidizing partner ads that feature a unified brand, forcing buyers to specify the chip by name.

**Skeptic**: The claim that consumers care about internal components contradicts the premise that they currently do not know which chip they own. If buyers are indifferent to performance specs, a logo alone may not create differentiation. However, the "unprecedented" nature of the risk is not a blocker; it is the source of potential monopoly power if successful. The cost risk is real but manageable if tied strictly to verified ad spend containing the logo.

**Better-Way**: Alternatives include direct price cuts to computer makers or technical certification programs. Price cuts erode margins without building brand equity. Certification lacks consumer visibility. "Do nothing" cedes mindshare to clone makers using similar part numbers, commoditizing the product. The proposed co-op model is the only path that aligns partner distribution with brand building while sharing costs.

**Kill-Criteria**: Failure occurs if computer makers refuse to alter ad creative or if consumer inquiries do not translate to retailer demand within two quarters. The proposal lacks a specific exit clause if partners resent the creative control.

**Edge**: The chip maker holds a unique position where supply is constrained to specific manufacturers. This leverage allows them to mandate logo placement as a condition of supply or favorable pricing, overcoming partner resentment. No competitor has attempted to bypass the OEM to own the end-user relationship.

**Decision Analysis**:
*   **Blockers**: NONE. The risks are executional, not fundamental flaws in the logic.
*   **Risks**: Partner friction, budget drain, consumer apathy. These are mitigated by making payments contingent on compliant ads and measuring lift via consumer recall surveys.
*   **Edge**: First-mover advantage in component branding creates a moat against clones that cannot match the consumer pull.

The plan beats "do nothing" because commoditization guarantees margin collapse. It beats price cuts because it builds long-term asset value. The lack of precedent is the opportunity, not the disqualifier.

DECISION: GO
