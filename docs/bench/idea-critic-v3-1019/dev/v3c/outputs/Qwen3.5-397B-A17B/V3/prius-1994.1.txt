Steelman: Committing now secures a decade-long technological moat and brand definition before regulators force the issue.

**Skeptic:** The proposal rests on unproven battery durability and a novel transmission with no volume precedent. With cheap fuel and no regulatory mandate, the immediate market demand is nonexistent. The financial exposure from warranty claims on unproven technology could outweigh early sales revenue. The "bold public schedule" creates irreversible reputational risk if the 1997 deadline slips due to technical hurdles.

**Better-way:** The "do nothing" option preserves capital until competitors validate the technology or regulations shift. A cheaper alternative is a limited fleet pilot to gather durability data without committing to mass production tooling. Waiting allows the company to license proven solutions later rather than bearing the full R&D burden alone.

**Kill-criteria:** This is a one-way door; mass production tooling cannot be easily repurposed. The cost of delay is low given the lack of competitive pressure. The pre-mortem reveals failure via catastrophic battery degradation rates discovered post-launch, destroying residual values and brand trust.

**Blockers:** 
1. No verified demand signal exists; customers prioritize price over unproven efficiency when fuel is cheap.
2. Battery durability is unknown, creating an undefined but potentially massive warranty liability.
3. The novel transmission lacks volume manufacturing proof, threatening the 1997 launch date.

**Risks:** High unit cost, supply chain immaturity for NiMH packs, and consumer confusion regarding hybrid value proposition.

**Edge:** None stated. Being first offers no advantage if the product is unreliable or too expensive for the current market. "Strategic necessity" is not an edge without a clear path to profitability or adoption.

The proposal commits massive capital to unproven technology in a market that has not asked for it, with no safeguard against technical failure. The lack of a pilot phase or measurable kill criteria before mass production commitment makes this financially reckless.

DECISION: NO-GO
