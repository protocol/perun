**Steelman:** Launch a niche, phone-tethered 7-inch device on an untested OS to validate a new platform quickly, leveraging existing business brand loyalty despite missing native apps.

**Attack:**
1.  **Value Contradiction:** The company leads in business email, yet the device lacks built-in email, calendar, or contacts unless paired with their phone. This alienates the core business demographic who may not own the phone or want a secondary device dependent on it.
2.  **Pricing Failure:** Matching the price of a rival's larger tablet with a superior app store offers no value proposition. Customers pay the same for less screen real estate, fewer apps, and broken standalone functionality.
3.  **Platform Risk:** An untested OS with no developer commitment creates a "ghost town" effect. Without native apps, the device is a peripheral, not a standalone product, severely limiting adoption beyond existing phone users.
4.  **Strategic Misfit:** "Business leader" launching a consumer-grade, crippled device confuses the market position. It cannibalizes the phone's value without offering a compelling tablet experience.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserve brand reputation; wait for the OS to mature or acquire apps before hardware launch.
*   *Cheaper Path:* Release the OS as an app on established platforms first to build developer interest before committing to hardware.
*   *Better Fit:* Launch with full native PIM (Personal Information Management) apps to honor the business brand promise, even if the app store is small.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Price equals rival's larger, feature-rich tablet.
*   **Fact:** Core brand promise (email) is missing as a standalone feature.
*   **Fact:** No developer commitment for the untested OS.

**Edge:** None identified. Being first with this specific configuration offers no advantage; it exposes the brand to failure on price, features, and ecosystem simultaneously. The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" or justify the cost to the brand.

**Verdict:** The plan as proposed guarantees market rejection due to price parity with superior rivals and the absence of the company's core competency (email) as a standalone feature.

DECISION: NO-GO
