**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant video platform eliminates the buyer's largest competitive threat, secures the definitive user-generated content library, and captures the emerging shift in online media consumption before rivals can react, justifying the premium as a strategic monopoly play.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts provided:
1.  **Financial Blocker:** The target has "no meaningful revenue" against a $1.65 billion price tag. The ROI is purely speculative, relying on an "unproven ad model." Paying billions for a cost center (high bandwidth bills) with no income violates basic valuation logic.
2.  **Legal Blocker:** "Media companies have threatened lawsuits" over "large volume" copyright infringement. This is not a future risk but an active, existential threat. The acquisition transfers massive liability to the buyer immediately, potentially resulting in judgments exceeding the purchase price or an injunction shutting down the core asset.
3.  **Strategic Redundancy:** The buyer "already runs its own video service." Instead of fixing their own unit economics and legal standing, they propose buying a larger version of the same broken business model.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids immediate liability and cash burn; allows the target to potentially collapse under litigation or bandwidth costs, allowing the buyer to acquire assets later for pennies or win by default.
*   **Build/Organic Growth:** Invest the $1.65B in scaling the existing smaller service with proper licensing deals and proven ad tech, avoiding the inherited legal poison pill.

**Pre-mortem:**
Assume failure in 24 months: The lawsuits materialize, forcing the removal of all popular copyrighted content, which drives users away. Without user traffic, the unproven ad model generates zero revenue. The buyer is left with massive bandwidth bills for an empty site and a depressed stock price due to the dilution used for the purchase.

**Edge:**
The proposal cites "largest destination" status as the edge. However, without a legal right to host the content driving that traffic, "largest" is merely "largest liability." There is no defensible moat, only a scale of infringement.

**Blockers:** Active, unresolved copyright litigation threats; zero revenue validating the valuation; unproven monetization.
**Risks:** Bandwidth costs exceeding ad yield; integration failure; regulatory scrutiny.
**Edge:** None identified that outweighs the legal and financial exposure.

DECISION: NO-GO
