**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant video platform eliminates the primary competitor, secures the category leader despite current losses, and bets on solving copyright/monetization post-acquisition using the buyer's superior capital and legal resources.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal; the high price and risks are stated features of the bet, not hidden flaws. The "no revenue" and "lawsuit" facts are known variables, not surprises that invalidate the core logic of buying dominance.

**Risks:** Massive copyright liability could bankrupt the entity; bandwidth costs may outpace ad revenue indefinitely; the $1.65B valuation assumes an ad model that does not yet exist at scale; cultural integration of a tiny startup into a large corporation may fail.

**Edge:** The buyer possesses the specific capital reserves, existing ad sales infrastructure, and legal engineering capacity to resolve the copyright impasse and monetize the traffic where the target cannot. The target's network effect (largest destination) is an asset the buyer cannot replicate organically in a reasonable timeframe.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if media lawsuits result in an injunction removing core content before a licensing framework is signed, or if user growth stalls once professional content replaces amateur uploads due to aggressive filtering.

**Door Type:** One-way door (acquisition is irreversible; stock is spent).

**Cost of Delay:** High. Waiting allows a competitor to bid or the target to collapse under legal pressure, losing the chance to own the category standard.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal explicitly acknowledges the risks (price, litigation, unproven model) and relies on the buyer's unique ability to solve them. Since the "edge" (capital/legal/ads) directly addresses the stated "risks," and no blocker proves the plan impossible as stated, the bet is rational for a market leader seeking category dominance. The lack of current revenue is the reason the price is negotiable relative to future potential, not a reason to abort.

**Kill Criteria:** If licensing deals with major media studios are not signed within 12 months, or if monthly active users decline by >10% post-integration, initiate divestiture or shutdown.
