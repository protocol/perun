**Steelman:** Secure dominant nationwide infrastructure immediately to create an insurmountable barrier to entry, betting that speed of scale outweighs the cost of early inefficiency.

**Attack:** The proposal commits $1 billion to fixed assets before validating the core hypothesis: that customers will adopt online grocery shopping at a density sufficient to cover thin margins. In June 1999, internet penetration and consumer trust in online transactions are nascent. Building "highly automated" warehouses assumes a stable, high-volume operational model that has never been proven in this sector.

**Better Way:** The alternative is a "crawl, walk, run" approach. Validate unit economics in the single existing metro area using flexible, lower-cost fulfillment centers. Only expand to a second city once the first demonstrates a path to profitability. This preserves capital to iterate on the model rather than locking it into potentially obsolete automation.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This strategy violates basic startup hygiene: it treats a high-uncertainty search problem as a known execution problem. If the model fails (e.g., customers prefer selecting their own produce, or delivery costs exceed margins), the company is left with a dozen expensive, specialized white elephants and no liquidity to pivot. The "first mover" advantage is illusory if the underlying economics do not work; being the biggest bankrupt player offers no strategic edge.

**Blockers:**
1.  **No Validated Learning:** The proposal explicitly ignores learning unit economics in the first market before scaling. This is a factual blocker; you cannot scale what you have not proven works.
2.  **Capital Mismatch:** Burning $1B on fixed costs in a low-margin, unproven category creates a fatal cash-flow gap before revenue can mature.
3.  **Assumed Demand:** The plan assumes customer behavior (density, slot adherence) that contradicts the historical reality of the era without evidence.

**Edge:** None. Speed without a working model is not an edge; it is an accelerator of failure. There is no asset, cost advantage, or proven demand cited that rivals cannot undercut once the model is actually defined.

**Verdict:** The plan lacks a verified foundation. Scaling unproven unit economics with billions in fixed costs is a guaranteed failure mode.

DECISION: NO-GO
