**Steelman:** Leverage existing retail loyalty and voice data to create a new home category, using exclusivity to manage early adoption risks while gathering behavioral data phone assistants cannot capture.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Cede smart home entry to competitors; rely solely on phone-based assistants which lack always-on, room-scale context.
2.  **Cheaper Alternative:** Launch a non-listening Bluetooth speaker ($50). Lower risk, but zero data collection and no strategic foothold in AI.
3.  **Proposal:** High risk, high reward. Creates a dedicated hardware footprint in the home.

**Fit:** Aligns with a "customer obsession" north star by embedding service directly into the living room, assuming users value convenience over privacy concerns.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if privacy fears trigger a media backlash or if voice failure rates exceed 20%, causing abandonment. The "always-on" feature is the primary friction point. However, the invitation-only rollout acts as a circuit breaker, limiting blast radius.

**Evidence & Analysis:**
*   **Privacy Risk:** Valid, but mitigated by the "invitation only" gate, allowing controlled feedback loops before mass marketing.
*   **Voice Failure:** A known 2014 constraint. The proposal accepts this as an iterative challenge rather than a blocker.
*   **Category Creation:** Lack of precedent is an edge, not a flaw; it defines the market rather than fighting for share in a saturated one.
*   **Past Hardware Failure:** The smartphone failure was likely due to market saturation and late entry. A novel form factor (cylinder) with a unique utility (always-on home hub) addresses different user needs than a handheld phone.
*   **Price:** $199 is premium but justifiable for early adopters; the $99 member price locks in loyalty and subsidizes data collection.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks are managed via the rollout strategy (invitation-only) and pricing tiers.
**Risks:** Privacy backlash, voice accuracy, category education. These are monitored via the limited launch.
**Edge:** Existing customer base for invitations, integrated shopping list utility that phone assistants lack due to lack of always-on presence, and first-mover advantage in a dedicated home device category.

**Kill Criteria:** If voice command failure rate >15% in the first 1,000 units or if cancellation rate due to privacy concerns exceeds 5% within 30 days, halt mass production.

**Decision:** The proposal turns previous hardware weakness into a focused, low-blast experiment with high strategic upside.

DECISION: GO
