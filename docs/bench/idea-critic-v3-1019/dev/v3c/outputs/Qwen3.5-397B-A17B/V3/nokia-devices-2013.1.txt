**Steelman:** Acquiring the struggling handset maker secures the primary hardware reference design, prevents its defection to the dominant rival, and signals commitment to developers, potentially slowing ecosystem erosion.

**Attack:** The core failure mode is not hardware supply but app scarcity. The market leader dominates because developers write for where the users are; users are where the apps are. Buying a declining hardware division (falling sales) for €5.4 billion consumes capital needed for developer incentives or marketing without solving the "chicken-and-egg" app problem. Integration risks are severe: merging a large, culturally distinct hardware workforce into a software-centric firm often creates distraction and bloat. Furthermore, this move may alienate *other* handset manufacturers using the OS, who may now view the platform owner as a direct competitor, pushing them toward the rival platform. The proposal assumes hardware control fixes an ecosystem deficit; history suggests it merely consolidates losses. The "edge" is undefined; being first to integrate vertically does not guarantee success if the underlying platform lacks developer momentum.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing/Partner:** Maintain arms-length relationships, avoiding integration costs and partner alienation.
2.  **Developer Fund:** Spend the €5.4 billion directly on subsidizing app development and user acquisition.
3.  **Acquire IP only:** Buy specific patents/assets without the liability of a falling sales division and massive workforce.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the "cheaper alternative" test. Spending billions on a losing hardware business to solve a software ecosystem problem is a strategic mismatch. The risk of alienating other partners outweighs the benefit of controlling one failing device line. The capital is better deployed to directly attack the app gap. Without a clear path to reversing the app deficit via hardware ownership, this is a defensive panic move, not a strategic win.

**Blockers:** Capital misallocation (hardware buy vs. software ecosystem need); potential alienation of remaining partner manufacturers.
**Risks:** Integration failure, continued sales decline, partner defection.
**Edge:** None stated; vertical integration alone is not an edge against a network-effect monopoly.

DECISION: NO-GO
