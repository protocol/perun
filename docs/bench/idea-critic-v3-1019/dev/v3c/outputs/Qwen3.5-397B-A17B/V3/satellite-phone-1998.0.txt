**Verdict: NO-GO**

The proposal fails the blocker test. The core economic model relies on a temporary scarcity of terrestrial coverage that is rapidly vanishing. Ground cellular networks are expanding faster than the satellite constellation can monetize its niche.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Market Erosion:** The primary value proposition (global connectivity) is being undercut by the very trend noted in the risks: the rapid spread of ground cellular. Waiting even one year drastically shrinks the addressable market.
2.  **Unit Economics Mismatch:** A $3,000 handset and high per-minute costs create a luxury product, yet the debt structure ($5B borrowed) demands mass-market adoption speed. The math does not close; the required subscriber growth rate is incompatible with the price point and limited use cases (poor indoor performance).
3.  **Operational Readiness:** The admission that sales channels are not ready is fatal for a capital-intensive launch. You cannot service debt with a product customers cannot buy.

**Risks:**
The "international business traveler" segment is too narrow to support $5B in debt service. Bulky hardware and poor indoor reception will lead to high churn among early adopters who expect premium reliability.

**Edge:**
There is no defensible edge. Being first with expensive, inferior technology (bulky, indoor-blind) against a rapidly improving, cheaper alternative (ground cellular) is a losing strategy. "Strategic necessity" or fear of falling behind does not overcome the fundamental unit economics failure.

**Conclusion:**
This is a one-way door with a high probability of total capital loss. The cheaper alternative is to wait, license the technology for niche government/military use only, or restructure the debt before a commercial launch that will likely trigger default. The current plan commits real money to a shrinking window of opportunity with unready operations.

DECISION: NO-GO
