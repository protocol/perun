**Steelman:** A mid-size maker can profitably revive the affordable two-seat roadster by leveraging lightweight simplicity against bloated competitors, capturing nostalgic demand before it vanishes entirely.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces immediate structural blockers. First, the "affordable" claim contradicts the reality of 1989 safety regulations; complying with crash standards for a low-volume convertible typically destroys the price advantage required to be "affordable." Second, the admission that the company lacks sports-car heritage removes the primary justification for a buyer to choose this unknown entity over established rivals, especially when the segment is already shrinking. Third, the risk of sales fading after early enthusiasts buy is not a risk but a certainty in a niche with no mass-market replacement demand; without a clear path to recurring buyers, the business case relies on a one-time spike.

**Alternatives:**
Doing nothing preserves capital in a declining segment. A cheaper alternative would be a sporty trim on an existing high-volume chassis rather than a dedicated new platform, reducing development risk and tooling costs.

**Fit & Edge:**
The proposal fails to identify a specific edge. Being "lightweight" is a design goal, not an asset the company currently possesses. "Strategic necessity" to fill a gap is not an edge if the gap exists because the economics are broken. The proposal offers no evidence of unmet demand at a profitable price point, only an observation that the cars have disappeared.

**Decision Logic:**
There is a clear blocker: the economic contradiction between "affordable," "low-volume," and "1989 safety compliance" without a stated technological breakthrough or subsidy. There is no edge: the company has no heritage, distribution advantage, or cost structure that proves it can win where others failed. The risks (shrinking market, one-time buyer pool) are treated as manageable uncertainties rather than fatal flaws in the volume model. Without a verified path to profitability beyond the initial enthusiast wave, the plan fails the blocker test.

DECISION: NO-GO
