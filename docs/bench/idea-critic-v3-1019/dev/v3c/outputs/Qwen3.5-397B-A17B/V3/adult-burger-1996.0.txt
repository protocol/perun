**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can overwrite the "kids' brand" perception, forcing adult trial of a premium burger that proves the food quality matches the new price point.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests. The brand's core asset is value and family fun; a premium price directly contradicts the established value proposition that drives volume. The risk that "adults see the chain as fast, not premium" is not a hypothetical risk but a structural barrier confirmed by the brand's own history. No evidence is provided that advertising alone can invert deep-seated brand architecture without alienating the existing high-volume customer base. The "largest budget in industry history" is a cost, not an edge; it increases the downside if the product-market fit is missing.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Protect current margins and brand clarity.
2.  **Sub-branding:** Launch the premium line under a different name or distinct storefront to avoid brand collision.
3.  **Pilot:** Test the burger in adult-heavy markets with minimal spend before committing to an industry-record budget.

**Pre-mortem:** The campaign launches, costs soar, and adults reject the burger as "too expensive for this place" while regulars feel abandoned by the price hike. The brand becomes confused, losing its core without gaining the new segment.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price-Brand Mismatch:** The proposal admits the brand is known for value/kids, yet sets a premium price without a mechanism to bridge this gap other than spending money.
*   **Unproven Demand:** Heavy spending is planned *before* demand is shown, violating basic capital allocation logic for an unproven segment.

**Edge:** None stated. Being first to spend heavily is not a competitive advantage if the product doesn't fit the brand.

**Decision:** The plan lacks a validated path to overcome the brand's inherent positioning against premium pricing. The risk of brand dilution and financial loss from unchecked spending is a confirmed blocker. A pilot or sub-brand strategy is required first.

DECISION: NO-GO
