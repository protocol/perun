**Steelman:** A premium, integrated device leveraging a niche computer base to solve music portability before legal downloads exist, betting on design and capacity over price.

**Attack:** The addressable market is mathematically insufficient. With a small computer market share and no consumer electronics experience, selling a $400 device (significantly above competitors) only to existing computer owners creates a fatal distribution bottleneck. Cheaper players are already failing; raising the price and restricting compatibility contradicts the only viable path to volume. The "walled garden" software requirement eliminates the broader PC market needed to justify the supply chain risk and development speed. Without licensed downloads, the value proposition relies entirely on CD ripping, a friction point unchanged by this hardware.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid capital expenditure on an unproven category with high sourcing risk.
2.  **Open Compatibility:** Build for Windows/Mac immediately to expand the base (requires different software strategy).
3.  **Lower Cost/Storage:** Match competitor pricing to gain foothold (reduces margin, ignores capacity edge).

**Fit:** This does not move a north star of "market expansion" as proposed; it contracts the potential user base to a fraction of an already small install base.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches to critical acclaim for design but sells fewer than 50,000 units in year one due to the $400 price point and Mac-only restriction. Inventory piles up as component costs drop, forcing a write-down. The brand is diluted by a failed consumer pivot.

**Blockers:**
*   **Market Size:** The compatible computer install base is too small to support the unit economics of a custom hard drive supply chain at this price point.
*   **Price vs. Value:** No evidence suggests users will pay a 2x premium for capacity when cheaper alternatives exist and content availability (legal downloads) is zero.

**Edge:** None stated. "Design" is not an edge against a 10x market size disadvantage. Being first is irrelevant if the market isn't ready.

**Verdict:** The proposal fails the basic math of total addressable market versus cost structure. It requires opening the ecosystem or waiting for legal music licensing before committing to hardware production.

DECISION: NO-GO
