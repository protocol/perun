**Steelman:** Switching to the dominant architecture secures a competitive thermal and performance roadmap essential for thin laptops, using a translation layer to mitigate immediate software incompatibility, despite short-term sales friction and brand dilution risks.

**Analysis:**
The status quo is a slow death; the current supplier cannot deliver the required efficiency for the target form factor. Without this move, the product line becomes physically obsolete compared to competitors. The "distinctiveness" risk is secondary to functionality; a distinct laptop that overheats or underperforms sells nothing.

The "painful earlier transition" is a sunk cost and a learning opportunity, not a veto. The proposal explicitly includes a translation layer, directly addressing the developer rebuild risk and user continuity. While announcing early may freeze current sales, this is a strategic cannibalization: it is better to freeze sales of your own aging models than to let competitors freeze your entire market share with superior hardware. The two-year timeline provides a reasonable window to manage the supply chain and developer relations.

The only true blocker would be if the translation layer performance was unacceptable, but the proposal acknowledges slower speeds for translated apps as a known trade-off, not a failure condition. Native apps will run at full speed, capturing the performance benefit. The risk of waiting (continued roadmap lag) outweighs the execution risk of switching. The "do nothing" option fails the primary goal of producing viable thin laptops.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks are managed trade-offs, not factual contradictions.
**Risks:** Sales dip during announcement, developer friction, brand perception shift.
**Edge:** Access to the only viable roadmap for thin, cool, efficient hardware, securing the product's physical future.

DECISION: GO
