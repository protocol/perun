**Steelman:** Acquiring the startup secures a free, open mobile OS to accelerate mobile web adoption, directly expanding the search advertising market before competitors lock down distribution.

**Options:**
1.  **Acquire ($50M+):** High cost, high risk, potential monopoly on future mobile search.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Low cost, but risks being locked out of the mobile ecosystem by handset makers or carriers.
3.  **Partner/License:** Lower upfront cost, but lacks control over the OS roadmap and openness required to drive web usage.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of maximizing search ad revenue by ensuring universal access to the mobile web.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if handset makers refuse to adopt the OS due to carrier pressure or if the open-source model fails to attract developer traction, leaving the $50M sunk with no revenue impact for years.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits no current revenue model and significant adoption hurdles. However, the "edge" is the proposer's unique position as a search-ad giant; only they can monetize a free OS indirectly through search volume, a model handset makers cannot replicate. Competitors rely on licensing fees, creating an opening for a free alternative.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of a direct revenue model is intrinsic to the strategy (indirect monetization), not a flaw. The high cost is justified if it prevents total exclusion from the mobile market.

**Risks:** Carrier resistance, slow adoption, long payback period. These are mitigated by making the OS open-source (reducing carrier/handset friction) and treating the acquisition as a defensive necessity.

**Edge:** The ability to subsidize the OS entirely through existing search profits, undercutting any competitor trying to charge for an OS.

**Decision:** The potential loss of the entire mobile search market outweighs the $50M risk. The open-source approach is the only viable wedge against entrenched carriers.

**Kill Criteria:** If no major handset manufacturer commits to the platform within 24 months, or if mobile search revenue from the platform remains <1% of total revenue after 5 years, terminate further investment.

DECISION: GO
