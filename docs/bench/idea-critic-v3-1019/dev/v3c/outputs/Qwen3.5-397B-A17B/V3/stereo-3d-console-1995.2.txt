Steelman: A $180 tabletop stereoscopic console bridges the handheld/home gap using existing red-display tech to capture early 3D demand before the next home console arrives.

**Blockers:** NONE based strictly on provided facts. The price is competitive, and the timing fits the generational gap. No fact states the technology is impossible to manufacture at this cost or that the market explicitly rejects 3D concepts entirely.

**Risks:** High. "Red-on-black only" severely limits visual fidelity compared to competitors. "Neither portable nor comfortable" contradicts the "handheld" bridge strategy, creating a confused value proposition. "Eye strain/headaches" poses a direct health liability and reputation risk. "Few games at launch" guarantees poor software attachment rates, killing hardware momentum. The "next home console due within two years" creates a very short window to recoup R&D and marketing costs before internal cannibalization.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first to a flawed 3D implementation is not an asset; it is a liability. The proposal lacks a stated distribution advantage, exclusive content pipeline, or cost structure that rivals cannot match. Fear of falling behind in 3D is not an edge.

**Decision Logic:** While no hard factual blocker (like "technology does not exist") is present, the absence of an **Edge** combined with severe, stated product flaws (comfort, health, visuals) and a compressed market window means the plan cannot succeed as proposed. A plan without a defined path to winning its upside (an edge) is a no-go. The risks listed are not merely potential; they are described as inherent traits of the proposed device ("neither portable nor comfortable," "red monochrome"). These are not unknowns to be tested; they are known defects that undermine the core value proposition. Without a pivot to address comfort, visual quality, or a stronger software lineup, this product will fail.

DECISION: NO-GO
