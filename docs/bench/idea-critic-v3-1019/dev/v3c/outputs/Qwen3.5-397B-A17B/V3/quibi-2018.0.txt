The claim assumes a market gap for premium, short-form subscription video despite abundant free alternatives and rising subscription fatigue. The steelman posits that high-production value and technical convenience (vertical/horizontal adaptability) create a unique value proposition sufficient to overcome price sensitivity and attention competition.

However, the "better-way" analysis reveals a critical flaw: the proposal ignores the dominant market reality of free, ad-supported short-form content which already satisfies the "under 10 minutes" demand. The cost structure (A-list talent, high budgets) creates an insurmountable barrier to profitability compared to low-cost user-generated competitors. The "do nothing" or "partner with existing platforms" alternative offers similar reach with drastically lower capital risk and no need to combat subscription fatigue directly.

The pre-mortem indicates failure due to inability to acquire subscribers at a cost lower than their lifetime value, given the prevalence of free substitutes. The "edge" cited (production quality and orientation technology) is not a defensible moat; competitors can replicate technical features quickly, and high production value does not guarantee engagement in short-form formats where volume and virality often trump polish.

The evidence provided highlights "no proven demand" and "subscription fatigue" as stated facts within the proposal itself. These are not mere risks but active blockers: the proposal admits the core assumption (demand for paid short-form) is unproven while simultaneously proposing a billion-dollar spend to test it. The high capital requirement ($1B+) combined with unproven unit economics and a saturated free market constitutes a factual contradiction to the plan's viability as proposed. The orientation feature is a commodity, not a strategic asset capable of shifting consumer behavior away from free options.

Blockers: The proposal admits "no proven demand" while requiring massive upfront capital; free alternatives already dominate the specific format (short-form); subscription fatigue directly contradicts the revenue model.
Risks: High churn, inability to scale content library fast enough, rapid technical commoditization.
Edge: None identified that overcomes the free alternative advantage.

DECISION: NO-GO
