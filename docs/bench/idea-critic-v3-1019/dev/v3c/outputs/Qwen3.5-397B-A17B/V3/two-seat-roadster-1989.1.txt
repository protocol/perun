**Steelman:** A mid-size maker can profitably revive the affordable lightweight roadster by exploiting a gap left by mass-market exit, targeting enthusiasts underserved by heavy, expensive alternatives.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker is the assumption that "affordable" and "safe/compliant" are compatible in low volumes. Safety regulations in 1989 increasingly demand structural rigidity and airbags, which add weight and cost, directly attacking the "lightweight" and "affordable" pillars. If the car becomes heavy or expensive to meet rules, it loses its edge against used classics or higher-tier sports cars.

*Do Nothing:* Avoids high retooling costs and brand risk but cedes the niche to potential future entrants or kit-car makers.
*Alternative:* Launch as a limited "halo" model with minimal marketing spend to test price elasticity before committing to mass production. This reduces downside if the "enthusiast fade" risk materializes quickly.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
The proposal lacks a stated "north star" beyond filling a gap. If the goal is volume profit, the shrinking segment is a fatal mismatch. If the goal is brand image, the lack of heritage makes credibility hard to earn.
*Pre-mortem:* The car launches, meets safety rules by adding 150kg, prices out of the "affordable" bracket, sells 2,000 units to early adopters, then collapses as the second-hand market floods. The company cannot recoup tooling costs.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal admits the segment has "shrunk for years" and sales "may fade." These are not just risks; they are stated trends contradicting the viability of a mass-market approach. No evidence is given that the company can produce a compliant car at a lower cost than incumbents who already exited. The "edge" is undefined; being first in a dying category is a liability, not an asset.

**Decision:**
There is a clear blocker: the conflict between regulatory costs and the "affordable/lightweight" value proposition in a shrinking market, with no proven cost advantage or heritage to sustain pricing power. The risks are treated as possibilities rather than structural hurdles. Without a capped pilot or a verified cost model proving compliance doesn't kill the margin, this is a speculative bet on a declining trend.

DECISION: NO-GO
