**Steelman:** Acquiring the agency and platform instantly grants the technology and scale needed to challenge the search leader's recent move, preventing permanent market exclusion.

**Attack:** The $6 billion cash price represents a massive premium in a fast-changing market, creating immense financial risk if display technology shifts again. The proposal admits a direct conflict with the buyer's existing agency customers; alienating these current revenue streams to chase a speculative display future jeopardizes the core business. Furthermore, buying a "fast-changing" asset at a peak premium often results in immediate obsolescence rather than sustained advantage. The "largest acquisition ever" suggests a lack of internal organic capability, forcing a high-stakes bet on integration success amidst known cultural and technical friction.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve cash and existing agency relationships; accept a secondary position in display while dominating search.
2.  **Partner/Invest:** Secure technology access or minority stakes without the $6 billion outlay or customer conflict.
3.  **Organic Build:** Develop internal capabilities slowly but without integration debt or customer alienation.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because the acquired technology became obsolete within two years, or the exodus of alienated agency customers erased the acquisition's value. The cost of delay is losing the "next big thing" narrative, but the cost of error is catastrophic capital destruction.

**Blockers:**
*   **Conflict of Interest:** The proposal explicitly states conflicts with existing agency customers. No mitigation is offered for retaining these clients post-acquisition. This is an immediate revenue threat.
*   **Valuation vs. Volatility:** Paying a large premium for assets in a "fast-changing" market violates basic investment hygiene; the asset could be worthless shortly after purchase.

**Edge:** None stated beyond "catching up" to a competitor's move. Fear of falling behind is not a strategic edge.

**Decision:** The explicit conflict with existing customers and the unchecked financial risk of a premium buy in a volatile sector constitute fatal flaws.

DECISION: NO-GO
