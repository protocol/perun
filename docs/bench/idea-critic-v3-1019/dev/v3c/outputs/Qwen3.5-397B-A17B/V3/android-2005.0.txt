**Steelman:** Acquiring the startup secures a free, open mobile OS to commoditize handset software, forcing widespread adoption of mobile web browsing and locking in search-ad dominance before competitors control the mobile gateway.

**Options:**
1.  **Acquire:** High upfront cost ($50M+), high execution risk, potential for massive strategic upside via ad revenue.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Low cost, but risks being locked out of the mobile ecosystem by handset makers or carriers, potentially capping future search growth.
3.  **Partner/License:** Lower cost, but lacks control over the OS direction and openness required to disrupt the market.

**Fit:** Aligns with the core mission of organizing information and monetizing access; mobile is the inevitable next frontier for search.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if handset makers refuse to adopt the OS due to carrier pressure or existing vendor contracts, leaving the company with an expensive, unused codebase. This is a **one-way door**; once acquired, the capital is spent and the open-source genie is out, but the strategic window may close if adoption stalls.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The lack of a direct revenue model for the OS is intentional (it's a loss leader for ads). Carrier resistance and handset maker hesitation are *risks*, not proven blockers, as the "free" value proposition is uniquely compelling against costly proprietary licenses.
*   **Edge:** Only a search-ad giant can afford to give away an OS to fuel its core business. Handset makers have no incentive to build their own OS if a robust, free alternative exists.
*   **Risks:** Long payback, adoption friction, carrier gatekeeping.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If major handset makers do not commit to the platform within 18 months of launch, pivot to a purely software-layer strategy or sunset hardware ambitions.

**Decision:** The potential to define the mobile internet landscape outweighs the financial risk. The "no revenue model" objection misunderstands the strategy: the OS *is* the distribution channel for the real product (search).

DECISION: GO
