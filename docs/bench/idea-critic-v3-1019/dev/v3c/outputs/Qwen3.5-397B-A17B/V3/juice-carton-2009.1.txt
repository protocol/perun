**Steelman:** The brand must modernize its visual identity to signal freshness and premium quality, betting that a sophisticated minimalist design will attract new buyers without alienating the core base, justified by a massive launch campaign to force recognition.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the primary function of the current packaging: instant shelf recognition for a loyal, older demographic. The "orange with a straw" is not merely decoration; it is the dominant cognitive cue. Removing it creates a high probability of "shelf invisibility," where loyalists bypass the product assuming it is out of stock or a different brand. The risk of sales collapse during the transition outweighs the aesthetic gain. A "large advertising campaign" is a lagging corrective measure; it cannot guarantee immediate shelf-level identification in the critical first seconds of a shopping trip. The cost of retooling nationwide simultaneously creates a single point of failure with no market feedback loop.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain sales stability; zero retooling cost.
2.  **Staged Pilot:** Test the new design in a limited geographic region to measure recognition failure rates before national rollout.
3.  **Hybrid Evolution:** Gradually introduce the glass/cap elements while retaining the iconic straw-orange until equity transfers.

**Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits the buyer base is "loyal and older," a segment statistically resistant to abrupt visual changes. No evidence is provided that the current cue is a liability rather than an asset. The assumption that "minimalist" equals "premium" for this specific demographic is unverified.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales have dropped 15%. Cause: Loyal customers could not locate the product on shelves due to the missing orange-straw cue, and the advertising spend was insufficient to retrain muscle memory quickly enough to prevent churn to competitors.

**Blockers:** The plan lacks a validation step proving the new design works for the stated "older" demographic before committing to a costly, irreversible nationwide print run. The risk of recognition failure is treated as a manageable marketing issue rather than a fundamental product-accessibility blocker.

**Edge:** None identified. Being first to a minimalist look is not an edge if it sacrifices shelf visibility.

**Verdict:** The proposal fails the blocker test by ignoring the specific vulnerability of its core demographic to visual discontinuity.

DECISION: NO-GO
