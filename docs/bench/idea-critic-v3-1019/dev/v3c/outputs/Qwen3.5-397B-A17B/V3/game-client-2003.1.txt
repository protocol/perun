**Steelman:** A mandatory client centralizes update delivery and anti-cheat enforcement, solving critical revenue leakage and integrity issues that retail distribution cannot address, while creating a future digital storefront.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal addresses an existential threat (cheating/update failure) with the only viable technical solution available in 2003. No fact provided proves the plan fails as proposed; constraints like broadband penetration are adoption hurdles, not logical blockers.

**Risks:** Retail channel conflict, user friction from mandatory accounts/downloads, initial client instability, and competitor imitation. These are execution and market risks, not flaws in the core logic. The "do nothing" alternative guarantees continued inability to patch games or stop cheaters, which is worse than the risk of retailer pushback.

**Edge:** The developer owns popular IP. Players willing to buy boxed games for these specific titles will tolerate the client to play them. This leverage allows the company to force a platform shift that competitors cannot yet match because they lack the "must-have" titles to drive client adoption.

**Decision Method:**
1.  **Fit:** Aligns with the north star of maintaining game integrity and recurring revenue.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" fails on anti-cheat. Patching via retail is impossible. A voluntary client fails to capture the whole user base for anti-cheat.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the client is too buggy at launch, causing a PR backlash that outweighs the game's value.
4.  **Kill Criteria:** If the client crashes >5% of sessions at launch, or if top 3 retail partners drop the game within 30 days of announcement, pause the mandatory rollout and revert to voluntary updates while fixing stability.
5.  **Leading Metric:** Percentage of active users successfully connecting to the client within 7 days of release. Review date: 30 days post-launch.

The risks are real but manageable compared to the certainty of product degradation without this system. The edge (popular IP) mitigates the adoption risk.

DECISION: GO
