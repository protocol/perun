**Steelman:** A full rewrite with publisher-favored surfacing and immediate migration is the only way to reverse traffic decline and compete with a growing rival.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the blocker test on three counts. First, it explicitly contradicts verified community values regarding voting mechanics; alienating the loyal core to chase publishers removes the network effect that creates value, likely accelerating traffic loss rather than fixing it. Second, the "all users at once" migration creates a one-way door with no rollback; if the new mechanics fail, the site cannot revert, risking total community collapse. Third, the engineering risk of a scratch rewrite delays any market response while the rival grows, ignoring cheaper, reversible alternatives like A/B testing specific surfacing changes on the current stack.

**Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves the core but accepts slow decline. A "staged rollout" or "feature flag" approach allows testing publisher favoring without forcing a full rewrite or immediate total migration, offering reversibility. These alternatives reach the potential upside (testing new mechanics) with significantly lower risk of community exodus.

**Edge:** The proposal identifies no unique asset or advantage that guarantees this specific high-risk pivot will succeed where others might fail. "Strategic necessity" to beat a rival is not an edge; it is a pressure that often leads to fatal overcorrection.

**Blockers:** 
1. Direct conflict with core community values on voting mechanics (stated risk).
2. No rollback path for immediate total migration (stated risk).
3. High engineering delay vs. competitor growth (stated risk).

**Risks:** Community exodus, technical failure of rewrite, inability to revert.

**Edge:** NONE. Fear of falling behind is not an edge.

**Decision:** No-go. The plan lacks a reversible path, ignores core user constraints, and offers no unique advantage to justify the existential risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
