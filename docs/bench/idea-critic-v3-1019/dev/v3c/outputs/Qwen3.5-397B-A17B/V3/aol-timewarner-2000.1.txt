**Steelman:** Merging dominant content with the fastest-growing distribution channel creates an unbeatable ecosystem, monetizing legacy assets through new technology before competitors adapt.

**Attack:** The proposal rests on a fatal asymmetry: valuing a volatile, declining dial-up monopoly at a peak bubble premium to acquire stable, tangible media assets. The "broadband" synergy is illusory; cable partners will block access to protect their own emerging internet ventures, and dial-up subscribers will churn rapidly as broadband spreads, leaving the combined entity with inflated equity but eroding cash flow. The cultural clash between agile tech and slow media will paralyze integration exactly when speed is required. The "premium" for the media board is a short-term stock pop exchanged for long-term control by a partner whose core business is obsolescent.

**Alternatives:** 
1. **Do nothing:** Retain stable cash flows and wait to buy tech assets when valuations correct.
2. **Partnership:** License content to the internet provider without merging balance sheets.
3. **Internal Build:** Accelerate the conglomerate's own broadband initiatives.

**Pre-mortem:** In three years, the tech stock collapses 90%, dial-up revenue halves, cable partners launch rival portals, and the merged company faces massive debt with no synergy realized, forcing asset fire sales.

**Blockers:** 
- **Valuation Mismatch:** Trading permanent equity for a transient, overvalued asset class with no hard collateral.
- **Dependency Risk:** Reliance on cable partners who are incentivized to compete, not cooperate.
- **Obsolescence:** The acquirer's primary revenue stream (dial-up) is the specific technology being displaced by the synergy target (broadband).

**Edge:** None. Being "first" is irrelevant if the foundation is sand. Size and brand do not offset a broken economic model.

**Decision:** No edge exists; multiple blockers confirm the plan fails as proposed. The valuation arithmetic alone dictates failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
