**Steelman:** A premium, gap-filling network for global travelers where ground cellular fails, monetizing unique coverage before competitors catch up.

**Attack:** The economics rely on a shrinking window of exclusivity. Ground cellular is expanding rapidly, eroding the "no signal" use case. At $3,000 per handset and high per-minute rates, the addressable market is tiny, yet the $5 billion debt load demands massive, immediate subscriber growth. The product is bulky and fails indoors—precisely where business travelers often operate. Sales channels are unready, delaying revenue while interest accrues.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid sinking further capital into a likely default; let ground networks mature.
2.  **Pivot to Niche:** Target only maritime, aviation, or government emergency services where bulk/price matter less, reducing the required subscriber base.
3.  **Delay Launch:** Wait for handset miniaturization and cost reduction, though debt covenants may forbid this.

**Fit:** Misaligned with a mass-market trajectory; fits only a luxury/niche strategy which the current debt structure cannot support.

**Pre-mortem:** In 12 months, ground roaming covers major cities. Subscribers remain in the thousands, not millions. Cash flow cannot service debt. Bankruptcy ensues, assets sold for pennies.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits high price, poor indoor performance, and unready channels against a backdrop of fast-spreading terrestrial alternatives. The math ($5B debt vs. limited high-price users) does not close.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unit Economics:** Price point restricts volume below the threshold needed to service $5B debt.
*   **Product-Market Fit:** Bulky hardware and indoor failure contradict the needs of the target demographic.
*   **Timing:** Terrestrial networks are closing the coverage gap faster than subscriber acquisition can occur.

**Edge:** None. Being first with an expensive, inferior product is not an advantage when a cheaper, better alternative is scaling globally.

**Kill Criteria:** If subscriber sign-ups do not reach 500k within 6 months, halt expansion. (However, the structural blockers suggest this target is unreachable).

**Verdict:** The plan requires capturing a mass market with a luxury product to pay off industrial-scale debt, while a superior, cheaper substitute is rapidly deploying. This is a financial trap.

DECISION: NO-GO
