**Steelman:** Acquiring the category-defining mobile photo leader eliminates an existential threat to the buyer's core business during a critical transition to mobile, securing user growth and talent for $1B before the target's IPO increases its leverage or value further.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker is the valuation: $1B for zero revenue and ~13 employees implies a massive premium based entirely on future potential and strategic necessity. However, the "do nothing" alternative carries higher risk. If the target IPOs successfully, it solidifies itself as a standalone competitor with public capital to aggressively erode the buyer's photo engagement. Given the buyer's admitted weakness in mobile, building a competing product internally is slower and less certain than acquiring the market leader.

The "culture destruction" risk is real but manageable via operational independence, a common post-acquisition strategy for talent retention. Regulatory review is a delay risk, not a failure mode, given the 2012 antitrust climate. The lack of a business model is irrelevant if the asset's value is defensive (protecting the core ad business) rather than direct monetization.

**Edge:** The buyer possesses the cash reserves to close immediately and the distribution network to scale the app's features globally, which the startup cannot do alone. The timing (pre-IPO) offers a unique window to neutralize the threat before it becomes a capitalized public rival.

**Kill Criteria & Decision:**
A blocker would be evidence that the user growth is fraudulent or that the technology cannot integrate. Absent such evidence, the strategic cost of *not* buying (losing the mobile photo layer to a competitor) outweighs the financial overpayment. The $1B price is a premium for certainty and speed in a winner-take-all mobile shift.

Blockers: NONE (Strategic necessity overrides valuation metrics in platform shifts).
Risks: Cultural attrition, regulatory delay, integration failure.
Edge: Unique cash position to neutralize the primary mobile threat pre-IPO.

DECISION: GO
