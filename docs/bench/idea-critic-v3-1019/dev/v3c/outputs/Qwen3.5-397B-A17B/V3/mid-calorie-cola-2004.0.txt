**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men avoiding both full-sugar guilt and diet stigma, leveraging a premium price to signal innovation before the low-carb trend fades.

**Attack:** The core claim fails the "satisfy neither group" test. Dieters seek near-zero calories; a 50% reduction leaves too many carbs for strict adherence, while taste-compromised products alienate regular drinkers who want full flavor. The "young men" segment is not a monolith; without a clear functional benefit (e.g., energy) or distinct taste profile, the product occupies a "no-man's-land" of value.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Protect margins on existing lines; wait to see if the rival's similar drink succeeds or fails (lowering R&D and campaign risk).
2.  **Flavor Innovation:** Launch a new full-sugar flavor with "natural" branding to attract young men without alienating the core base or confusing dieters.
3.  **Diet Extension:** Improve the diet formula specifically for male palates rather than creating a confusing hybrid category.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If the low-carb trend is a fad (as noted in risks), launching a permanent SKUs based on it creates stranded inventory. Assuming the trend lasts 2 years, the ROI must cover the massive campaign spend within 18 months. A pre-mortem reveals failure via channel confusion: retailers refuse shelf space for a niche product that cannibalizes two cash cows without growing the total category.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal admits a rival is launching a similar drink; being second with a "me-too" product at a *premium* price is a fatal blocker. Consumers rarely pay more for a follower in a speculative category. The risk that "half-sugar satisfies neither group" is a validated blocker based on historical binary choices in diet vs. regular segments. There is no stated "edge" (unique asset or distribution advantage) to overcome the first-mover rival and the binary consumer preference.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** Premium pricing for a "me-too" product against a first-moving rival; high probability of failing to satisfy either core demographic.
*   **Risks:** Trend volatility, cannibalization, shelf-space rejection.
*   **Edge:** None identified.

Without a proven unique selling proposition or a cost advantage to undercut the rival, the plan relies on hope rather than strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
