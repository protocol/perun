**Steelman:** A low-cost, portable cassette player using existing parts can create a new mass market among youth by prioritizing mobility and privacy over recording capability and shared listening.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Dealer Doubt:** Staff skepticism is a distribution friction, not a product flaw. If the price point is aggressive enough, consumer pull will override dealer hesitation. The "antisocial" critique misunderstands the value proposition: privacy *is* the feature for this demographic.
2.  **No Market Research:** Absence of data is not absence of demand. Existing tape decks are bulky and expensive; the gap for "personal" audio is logical even if unproven. Waiting for research cedes first-mover advantage.
3.  **Copycat Risk:** Competitors can copy the technology, but not the head start or brand momentum if executed quickly. Being first with the "personal" narrative creates a category definition that followers must react to.
4.  **Large Production Run:** This is the only genuine financial blocker. However, the low unit cost (no speaker, no recording head, lightweight materials) mitigates the absolute capital exposure compared to full deck manufacturing. The risk is necessary to achieve the target price; a smaller run would fail the pricing strategy entirely.

**Better Way Check:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Competitors eventually fill the gap; brand stagnates as "legacy hi-fi."
*   *Add Recording/Speaker:* Increases cost, destroys the "lightweight/cheap" edge, and dilutes the specific use case.
*   *Wait for Research:* Loses the window of opportunity.

**Kill Criteria & Edge:**
*   *Edge:* First to market with a dedicated "personal" audio identity; cost structure allows a price point competitors cannot match without redesigning their existing bulky lines.
*   *Risk:* Inventory overhang if the cultural shift to "private listening" fails to materialize.
*   *Verdict Logic:* No factual blocker exists. The risks are executional and market-adoption based, which are inherent to creating a new category. The proposal correctly identifies that standard metrics (dealer opinion, existing research) are lagging indicators for a disruptive product. The large run is a calculated bet required for the strategy, not a flaw.

DECISION: GO
