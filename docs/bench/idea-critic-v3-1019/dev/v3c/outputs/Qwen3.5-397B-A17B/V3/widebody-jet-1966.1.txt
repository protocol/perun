**Steelman:** Building a massively larger, convertible wide-body jet secures dominant market share against future demand and supersonic threats, leveraging a single anchor customer to justify unprecedented capital outlay.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces existential financial risk; the development cost exceeds all prior company history, threatening bankruptcy if the single customer cancels or if the market shifts to supersonic travel as predicted. The engine technology is unproven, introducing technical failure modes that could ground the fleet. Furthermore, betting on subsonic capacity contradicts the industry expectation that supersonic jets will render large subsonic liners obsolete.

However, the "do nothing" option carries its own fatal risk: ceding the growing high-capacity segment to competitors or failing to meet the anchor customer's needs, potentially losing the largest revenue source. The raised cockpit design specifically mitigates the risk of obsolescence by ensuring freighter convertibility, creating a secondary market if passenger demand wanes. The "supersonic replacement" theory is an assumption, not a verified fact; historical patterns suggest niche supersonic travel coexisting with mass subsonic transport rather than replacing it.

**Blockers:** None. The financial scale is terrifying but not a factual blocker if the anchor contract holds. The supersonic threat is speculative.
**Risks:** Catastrophic financial failure if the airline defaults; engine development delays; supersonic market adoption faster than anticipated.
**Edge:** First-mover advantage in ultra-high capacity; unique freighter flexibility via raised cockpit; locked-in demand from the world's largest airline.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal beats "do nothing" because inaction surrenders the strategic initiative and the anchor client. The risks are severe but manageable through the specific design choice (freighter conversion) and the committed customer. The lack of multiple orders is typical for first-of-class programs; the anchor order validates the immediate need. The supersonic threat is insufficient evidence to halt subsonic expansion given the unproven nature of that market.

**Kill Criteria:** Halt if the anchor airline cancels before tooling begins; halt if engine testing fails to meet thrust requirements by the prototype phase.

DECISION: GO
