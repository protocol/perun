**Steelman:** A radical visual overhaul will signal modernity and premium quality, capturing new demographics while the ad campaign educates loyalists, ultimately expanding market share without altering the product.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the primary function of packaging for a low-involvement, habit-driven good: instant shelf recognition. The "orange with a straw" is not mere decoration; it is the cognitive shortcut for 90% of buyers. Removing this cue creates a high probability of "shelf blindness," where loyal customers bypass the product assuming it is out of stock or a different brand. The risk is not just confusion; it is immediate, catastrophic sales volume collapse that a costly ad campaign may not offset quickly enough to prevent delisting by retailers. The "older, loyal" demographic is the least likely to interpret minimalist design as an upgrade and the most likely to perceive it as a loss of identity.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain sales stability; zero redesign cost.
2.  **Incremental Refresh:** Modernize the existing orange/straw graphic without removing the core cue. Lower risk, lower cost.
3.  **Staged Rollout:** Test the new design in a limited region to measure recognition failure before a national launch.

**Fit & Evidence:** No evidence is provided that the current design suppresses growth or that the target demographic desires minimalism. The assumption that "new look = new growth" contradicts the behavior of legacy CPG buyers. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a failed national relaunch.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales have dropped 25%. Retailers have reduced shelf space due to poor turnover. The cause: loyalists could not find the product, and new buyers did not understand the abstract design. The ad spend was insufficient to retrain millions of habitual shoppers simultaneously.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The old image is the brand's main shelf cue. Removing it eliminates the primary mechanism for repeat purchases.
*   **Fact:** Launching "in all stores at once" removes the safety valve of testing recognition failure.

**Edge:** None identified. Being first to minimalism in this category is a liability, not an asset, given the buyer profile.

**Kill Criteria:** If a blind shelf test shows >15% increase in time-to-locate or >10% failure to identify, the project stops.

**Verdict:** The plan bets the entire brand equity on an unproven aesthetic shift while deliberately destroying its strongest asset (recognition). It fails the "cheaper/safer alternative" test by rejecting a staged rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
