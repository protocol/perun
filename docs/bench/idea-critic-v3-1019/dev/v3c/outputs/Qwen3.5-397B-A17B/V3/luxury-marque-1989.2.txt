**Steelman:** Creating a distinct luxury brand with a dedicated dealer network and an all-new flagship sedan allows the mass-market manufacturer to bypass its "economy" reputation, directly challenging European incumbents on quality while undercutting them on price to capture value-seeking luxury buyers.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker is brand perception: history suggests economy buyers rarely transition to luxury within the same corporate family without severe brand dilution or rejection. However, the proposal explicitly mitigates this by establishing a **separate brand and dealer network**, effectively creating a "firewall" against the parent company's economy image. This structural separation addresses the core skepticism that the market won't pay luxury prices for an "econobox" maker.

Regarding the thin lineup and high development costs: entering the luxury segment with a single, highly engineered flagship is a standard "halo" strategy to establish credibility before expanding the portfolio. While the upfront capital expenditure for a new network and clean-sheet engineering is massive, the alternative—trying to push luxury vehicles through existing economy channels—has a near-certain failure rate given the stated market dynamics. The risk of incumbents' prestige is countered by the "price well below" positioning, targeting a specific gap in the market rather than a head-on feature war.

The "sunk cost" of development is not a valid reason to stop if the forward-looking ROI justifies the remaining investment; abandoning now guarantees a total loss, whereas launching offers a path to recoupment. The separate network is a cost, but it is the *only* viable mechanism to sell at luxury margins without cannibalizing the core brand's value proposition.

**Edge:** The ability to leverage the parent company's known reliability and manufacturing efficiency to produce a high-quality vehicle at a lower cost base than European rivals, passed on as aggressive pricing within a completely insulated luxury ecosystem.

**Verdict:** The structural separation solves the reputation barrier. The single-model launch is a focused, albeit risky, entry point that is superior to a compromised hybrid approach. The plan addresses the identified risks through architecture rather than hope.

DECISION: GO
