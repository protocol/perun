Steelman: A hardware manufacturer can disrupt a duopoly by leveraging superior disc-based economics and 3D technology to attract third-party developers, overcoming a lack of platform experience.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal acknowledges the lack of experience and competitor strength but offers a distinct economic and technical wedge (CD vs. cartridge) that directly addresses the primary friction for developers: cost and capacity. No factual contradiction exists in the premise that discs are cheaper and hold more data than cartridges.

**Risks:** High execution risk due to zero platform history; potential developer skepticism despite tool promises; aggressive response from incumbents launching competing 3D hardware; financial exposure from selling hardware at a loss. These are valid uncertainties but do not invalidate the core strategic logic; they represent the cost of entry, not proof of failure.

**Edge:** The proposer possesses specific manufacturing scale and engineering capability in optical media and 3D graphics that the current incumbents (focused on cartridges or 2D) allegedly lack or deploy less efficiently. The "cheaper disc manufacturing" is a tangible cost advantage that translates to higher margins for developers, creating a compelling incentive to switch allegiance despite brand loyalty.

**Decision Method:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the emerging 3D/CD market to rivals, likely resulting in long-term irrelevance as the industry shifts. Partnering was cancelled, leaving build-or-bust.
2.  **Fit:** Aligns with the north star of expanding beyond traditional electronics into interactive entertainment growth markets.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if developer adoption lags, leaving a library gap. However, the kill criterion is clear: secure key third-party exclusives before launch.
4.  **Evidence:** The cancellation by the partner validates the market opportunity (the partner saw value) while freeing the proposer to capture the full upside.

The risks are inherent to market entry but are mitigated by a clear, differentiated value proposition for the supply side (developers). The "no experience" objection is neutralized by the specific asset advantage (optical/3D tech). Waiting allows incumbents to solidify the 3D standard, making later entry impossible.

DECISION: GO
