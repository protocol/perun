**Steelman:** A premium, high-capacity device restricted to a niche ecosystem can succeed if it offers a user experience so superior that it justifies the price and creates a halo effect for the core computer business, eventually expanding beyond the initial walled garden.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" and "addressable base" tests in its current form. In early 2001, the market for digital music players is nascent and price-sensitive; existing cheaper units have failed due to poor usability and low storage, not just price. However, this proposal doubles down on the highest friction points: a $400 price tag (significantly above competitors) and a restriction to a small-share computer platform.

The critical blocker is the **distribution constraint**. Limiting usage to owners of a small-market-share computer initially caps the total addressable market to a fraction of a niche. While the 5GB drive is a technical advantage, the inability to serve the dominant Windows user base immediately removes the volume required to justify the supply chain risk and component sourcing costs within a one-year timeline. The "halo effect" argument assumes the device will be a hit despite these constraints; history suggests walled gardens in hardware accessories fail without immediate mass compatibility or a killer exclusive content deal (which the proposal admits is missing, as legal downloads are not yet licensed).

The "do nothing" or "wait" option is stronger: wait for legal download infrastructure to mature and build a cross-platform solution. Launching a high-cost, low-compatibility device now risks brand damage and inventory write-downs. The proposal lacks a verified path to profitability given the tiny initial user base and high unit cost. There is no stated "edge" that overcomes the lack of market fit other than the hope of superior design, which is insufficient to clear the blocker of market size.

**Blockers:** The initial addressable market (own-computer owners only) is too small to sustain the $400 price point and supply chain risk.
**Risks:** Component sourcing delays, continued industry resistance to legal downloads.
**Edge:** None verified; superior storage is negated by compatibility limits.

DECISION: NO-GO
