**Steelman:** Leverage existing music ecosystem and design control to create a premium, integrated communication device, disrupting carrier-dominated norms.

**Analysis:**
The proposal faces severe blockers. First, **no phone-industry experience** combined with **no third-party applications** creates a hollow value proposition against established business phones with keyboards and vast software libraries. A premium price for a feature-incomplete device is historically fatal.

Second, the **exclusivity deal** limits initial reach to a single carrier's subscriber base. While revenue sharing is attractive, it caps volume during the critical launch window where network effects matter most.

Third, the **lack of a physical keyboard** contradicts the dominant user preference for business communication in 2007. Betting on a touchscreen-only interface without a proven track record in telephony is a massive unverified assumption.

**Better Way:** License the music software to existing handset makers to capture immediate revenue and learn the market without hardware risk, or launch a lower-priced device with keyboard options to test demand before committing to a premium, exclusive, keyboard-less strategy.

**Kill Criteria:** The plan fails if early adopters (music fans) do not tolerate the lack of apps and keyboard for the music integration alone. There is no evidence provided that music loyalty overrides business utility or price sensitivity.

**Edge:** The only stated edge is the "leading digital music player." However, competitors are already adding music players to phones. The proposal assumes the integrated experience is superior enough to overcome the lack of apps, keyboard, and high price, but offers no data to support this leap. The "control over design" is a means, not a market advantage in itself.

**Verdict:** The risks (no experience, no apps, wrong form factor, limited distribution) outweigh the unproven edge. The plan relies on multiple unverified assumptions about user behavior and market readiness. A cheaper, staged approach or licensing strategy reduces downside while testing the core hypothesis.

DECISION: NO-GO
