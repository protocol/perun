**Steelman:** Aggressive capital deployment via IPO will secure dominant market share and eliminate competition through acquisition and predatory pricing, creating a monopoly that justifies current losses.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the most basic unit economics test. Selling products below cost ("negative gross margins") while simultaneously increasing fixed costs (warehouses) and variable costs (shipping heavy items, national TV ads) creates a feedback loop where every additional customer accelerates cash burn. The plan assumes capital markets will remain open indefinitely to fund this deficit, despite explicit risks of market closure. If markets tighten, the company cannot pivot to profitability because its core mechanism—selling at a loss—is designed to lose money on every transaction.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing/Status Quo:** Continue organic growth; preserves cash but risks being outspent.
2.  **Profitability First:** Raise prices to cover margins, cut ad spend, delay warehouses. This builds a sustainable business model before scaling, though growth slows.
3.  **Targeted Acquisition:** Buy the competitor using stock rather than cash, delay IPO until unit economics are positive.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** In a dot-com bubble burst scenario (highly probable given the era and "heavy spending" by all competitors), this strategy guarantees insolvency. The "edge" claimed is scale, but scale without positive unit economics is merely faster bankruptcy. The proposal relies on the "greater fool" theory of infinite capital availability, which contradicts the stated risk of capital markets closing.

**Blockers:**
*   **Negative Unit Economics:** Selling below cost with high shipping/logistics costs means revenue does not cover variable costs, let alone fixed overhead.
*   **Capital Dependency:** The entire plan collapses if the IPO fails or follow-on funding dries up, which is a stated high-probability risk.
*   **No Path to Profit:** The plan adds cost centers (TV, warehouses) to a loss-making core operation without a mechanism to flip margins positive.

**Edge:** None identified. Being first to lose the most money is not a competitive advantage.

**Decision:** The plan violates the fundamental constraint that a business must eventually generate more cash than it consumes. The risks are not hypothetical; they are mathematical certainties under the proposed operating model.

DECISION: NO-GO
