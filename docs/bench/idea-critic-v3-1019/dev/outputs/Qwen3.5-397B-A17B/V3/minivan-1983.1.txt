**Steelman:** Leveraging existing compact-car assets to create a new "garage-fit" family vehicle category offers a capital-efficient path to recovery, differentiating from commercial vans and large competitors.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker is the claim that "no such vehicle category exists." Historically, the absence of a category often indicates unmet demand rather than lack of interest, provided the product solves a specific pain point (garage storage, car-like handling). The risk of buyers perceiving it as a commercial van is mitigated by the explicit design goal: "drive like a car" and fit in a home garage. This positioning directly targets the family demographic described, not fleet buyers.

Financial constraints are severe ("little cash"), making the proposed strategy of reusing an existing platform and converting a factory the *only* viable option. Building a new platform or factory is impossible. While rivals could copy the concept, first-mover advantage in a newly defined category, combined with the specific brand trust of a "saved" carmaker, provides a temporary moat. The risk of limited space due to the compact platform is a necessary trade-off for the "garage-fit" requirement; if the vehicle fits in a garage, it inherently sacrifices the volume of a full-size van, which is the intended value proposition, not a flaw.

The "do nothing" alternative leads to continued reliance on government aid and potential eventual failure as family needs shift away from cumbersome full-size vans. The "cheaper" alternative is already embedded in the proposal (reuse platform). There is no cheaper way to enter the market without a vehicle.

**Edge:** The company possesses the specific compact-car platform and a converted factory ready for low-capital deployment. Competitors with full-size van dominance face higher inertia and cost structures to downscale effectively.

**Verdict:** The proposal turns constraints (low cash, existing small platform) into the defining features of a new market segment. The risks are manageable through positioning, and the alternative is stagnation.

DECISION: GO
