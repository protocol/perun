**Steelman:** Rapidly acquiring 130–150 sites leverages immediate scale to preempt the entrenched competitor, accepting high execution risk to secure market presence before local prices adjust or the rival reacts.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "one-way door" test. Committing to ~150 leases within two years creates massive fixed costs before the new supply chain and inventory system are proven in a cross-border, bilingual environment. The "pace" risk is not merely operational; it is existential. If the new inventory system glitches (highly probable for unproven tech in complex new regimes), the retailer faces stockouts or overstock across 150 locations simultaneously, with no off-ramp. Leases are liabilities, not options.

**Better Way:** A staged approach (e.g., 10–20 pilot stores) allows the supply chain and inventory system to debug in live fire with limited exposure. This sacrifices some speed but preserves capital and reputation if the model breaks. The current plan bets the entire international thesis on a "big bang" launch where failure means immediate, catastrophic loss rather than a learnable setback.

**Fit & Edge:** The retailer's edge is "loyal domestic following" and "discount" positioning. However, in a market with higher prices and an entrenched rival, this edge is unproven. "Discount" requires flawless logistics to maintain margins; the proposed unproven supply chain directly threatens the core value proposition. There is no evidence that speed alone overrides the incumbent's advantage or that customers will switch based on a brand they don't know yet, especially if execution stumbles cause empty shelves.

**Blocker:** The commitment to 150 sites before validating the new supply chain and inventory system in the target environment. This violates the principle of reversible decisions for high-uncertainty ventures. The cost of delay (waiting for pilots) is far lower than the cost of failure (150 bad leases).

**Edge:** NONE. Speed without proven operations is a liability, not an advantage.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed without a pilot phase proving the inventory system and cross-border logistics at a smaller scale (e.g., <20 stores) first.

DECISION: NO-GO
