**Steelman:** Combining German engineering prestige with US mass-market profitability creates a dominant global entity through platform synergies and balanced governance.

**Attack:** The proposal collapses under the weight of unaddressed cultural incompatibility and cyclical timing risks. The "merger of equals" structure is historically fragile when corporate governance models (two-tier German vs. single-tier US) and labor relations (co-determination vs. at-will) are diametrically opposed. The US partner's current profitability relies entirely on a light-truck boom; acquiring this asset at its cyclical peak exposes the combined entity to massive downside when the cycle turns, precisely when integration costs are highest. Platform sharing between premium and mass-market segments often dilutes brand equity rather than saving costs, risking the German firm's core pricing power. The lack of a clear integration lead in an "equals" structure invites decision paralysis during the critical post-merger window.

**Better Way:** A strategic alliance for specific platform sharing without equity entanglement avoids cultural collision while capturing cost benefits. Alternatively, a staged acquisition with a clear controlling partner prevents governance deadlock.

**Kill Criteria:** If the combined board cannot agree on a single decision-making protocol for labor disputes within 90 days, or if the US truck market share drops by >5% within 18 months, the merger thesis fails.

**Edge:** NONE. The proposal cites "scale" and "complementary brands," which are generic hopes, not verified advantages. It ignores the specific, stated fact of "very different corporate and labour cultures" as a solvable variable, treating a fundamental blocker as a mere concern. The "merger of equals" framing obscures the lack of a decisive leadership structure required to navigate these specific differences.

**Blockers:** 
1. Unresolved governance conflict between German co-determination and US management structure.
2. Acquisition of US assets at peak cyclical valuation without downside protection.
3. "Merger of equals" ambiguity creating immediate leadership vacuum.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, labor strikes, integration paralysis, cyclical downturn.

**Decision:** The proposal presents a high-probability failure scenario due to structural governance flaws and poor timing, with no concrete mechanism to resolve the stated cultural chasm.

DECISION: NO-GO
