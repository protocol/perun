**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant independent mobile messaging utility at scale prevents existential displacement of the buyer's core social graph by a competitor-controlled protocol, securing the mobile future regardless of immediate revenue.

**Attack:** The $19B price tag represents a >12x increase over the prior $1.5B valuation in just 12 months, with no monetization path, minimal staff (50), and an explicit anti-ad stance. Paying a historic premium for a free/low-cost utility with regulatory headwinds and retention risk violates standard capital discipline. If the founders leave or regulators block data integration, the asset becomes a costly, isolated silo that fails to solve the displacement fear.

**Better Way:** "Do nothing" and accelerate internal mobile messaging features using existing capital; or attempt a minority stake/partnership to monitor the threat without assuming full balance-sheet risk. However, if the target is already becoming the default OS-level communication layer, these alternatives may fail to stop the network effect shift.

**Fit & Edge:** The buyer's fear confirms the strategic fit: mobile messaging is displacing core services. The only defensible "Edge" is the target's massive, engaged user base (hundreds of millions) which cannot be quickly replicated organically. Size alone is not an edge, but *ownership* of this specific distribution channel is the unique asset required to survive the platform shift.

**Blockers:** None. The financial metrics (revenue, headcount) are irrelevant if the strategic premise (displacement) is true; the cost is the price of survival, not a traditional ROI play. The lack of a monetization plan is a post-acquisition integration challenge, not a pre-deal blocker, provided the primary goal is defensive retention of the user graph.

**Risks:** Massive overpayment if the trend reverses; regulatory breakup; founder departure; cultural clash.

**Kill Criteria:** If monthly active users decline by >10% within 18 months post-close, or if the founder team departs en masse within 12 months, initiate immediate strategic review for divestiture or shutdown.

**Decision:** The strategic necessity of owning the primary mobile communication layer outweighs the absurd financial multiples. This is a one-way door to prevent obsolescence.

DECISION: GO
