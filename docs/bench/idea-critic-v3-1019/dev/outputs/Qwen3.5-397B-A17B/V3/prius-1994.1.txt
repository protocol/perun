**Steelman:** Committing now secures a decade-long lead in hybrid technology, forcing competitors to play catch-up while defining the segment before regulators intervene.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "edge" test. With cheap fuel and no regulatory mandate, the customer value proposition is weak; the premium cost cannot be justified by fuel savings alone. The "novel" transmission and unproven battery durability create massive warranty exposure that could bankrupt the program post-launch. Being first is not an edge when the market has not asked for the product and the technology risks are existential. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a failed launch.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital, wait for fuel prices to rise or regulations to force the issue, then enter with proven tech.
2.  **Limited Pilot:** Release a small fleet to taxi companies to validate battery durability before mass production commitment.
3.  **Current Proposal:** High cost, high risk, zero near-term demand.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Edge:** Cheap fuel removes the primary economic incentive for buyers; "being first" is a vanity metric, not a business advantage.
*   **Unproven Core Tech:** Nickel-metal-hydride durability is unknown at scale; a mass failure triggers catastrophic warranty costs.
*   **Market Mismatch:** No regulatory or consumer pull exists to absorb the price premium.

**Risks:** Technical failure of the power-split unit, battery degradation leading to brand damage, financial bleed from low volume/high cost.

**Verdict:** The plan relies on creating a market that does not exist while betting the company on unproven components. The lack of a tangible customer benefit (due to cheap fuel) means the product cannot command the necessary price to cover its novel engineering costs. This is a solution searching for a problem.

DECISION: NO-GO
