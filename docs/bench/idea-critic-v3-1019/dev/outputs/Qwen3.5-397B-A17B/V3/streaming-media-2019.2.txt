**Steelman:** Sacrificing guaranteed licensing revenue to build a direct-to-consumer asset captures long-term value and data, betting that subscriber growth will outpace the cash burn and lost fees.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on a fatal arithmetic gap. The company earns "billions" in high-margin licensing fees today. Replacing this immediate cash flow requires tens of millions of subscribers paying a "low price." Even at 30 million subscribers, if the price is below the market leader, the gross revenue may not cover the lost licensing billions plus the massive costs of content production, marketing, and building a tech platform from scratch. The plan explicitly funds this with "multi-year operating losses," but ignores the liquidity crisis: can the balance sheet sustain billions in annual burns while theatrical/cable revenue potentially cannibalizes?

**Better Way:** A hybrid model. Keep licensing high-value legacy content to rivals for immediate cash while launching a premium-tier service with exclusive new releases only. This reduces the subscriber count needed for profitability and lowers the required cash burn.

**Fit & Edge:** The "edge" cited is merely content ownership, which rivals also possess or can license. Being a late entrant with a lower price is a commodity strategy, not a moat. The proposal admits no technical capability exists internally, introducing high execution risk.

**Pre-mortem:** In five years, the service has 20 million subscribers but burns $5B annually. Licensing revenue is gone. The stock collapses as investors realize the lifetime value of a low-price subscriber does not cover the acquisition cost and content spend. The company is forced to raise prices, causing churn, or sell the platform at a loss.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Financial Mismatch:** No evidence provided that low-price subscription revenue can mathematically replace "billions" in licensing fees within a reasonable timeframe given the stated "multi-year losses."
2. **Capability Gap:** Explicit admission of zero experience running the required technology platform.

**Verdict:** The plan trades certain, high-margin current revenue for speculative, low-margin future revenue without a credible bridge for the cash flow valley. It fails the "beat do nothing" test on current facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
