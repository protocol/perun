**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can overwrite the "kids' brand" perception, forcing adult trial of a premium burger that generates higher margins and diversifies revenue.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests. The brand's core equity is value and family fun; a "premium" price point directly contradicts the established value proposition that drives volume. The risks identified are not merely potential; they are structural contradictions. Adults seeking premium burgers avoid chains defined by toys and low prices, regardless of ad spend. The proposal relies on brute-force marketing to solve a product-market fit problem, which history shows is the most expensive way to fail. There is no "edge" here—no unique asset makes this chain better at premium burgers than existing competitors; in fact, the brand image is a liability. The "largest budget" is a blocker, not an enabler, as it magnifies the mismatch between brand identity and product offering. A cheaper alternative exists: test a limited premium item in adult-heavy markets with targeted, low-cost media before committing industry-record spending.

**Blockers:** 
1. Brand identity (value/kids) contradicts the premium price point.
2. No evidence that ad spend can overcome deep-seated consumer perception of "fast/cheap."
3. The proposed solution (spend more) ignores the root cause (product/brand mismatch).

**Risks:** 
1. Alienating the core customer base.
2. Wasting capital on unproven demand.
3. Diluting brand clarity.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first to spend heavily is not an edge; it is a vulnerability when the product does not fit the brand.

**Decision:** The plan commits real money to a strategy where the core premise (adults will pay premium prices at a kids' chain) is unproven and likely false based on the stated brand image. The lack of a pilot or staged approach makes this a one-way door with high downside.

DECISION: NO-GO
