**Steelman:** Leveraging existing, spiky infrastructure to sell unused compute capacity creates a high-margin revenue stream that amortizes fixed costs, turning a liability into a defensible moat against retail margin pressure.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "edge" test. The retailer has zero experience running a developer platform, while incumbents dominate trust and reliability. Entering a capital-intensive, unproven market while investors already question heavy spending invites catastrophic distraction. The "spiky" internal load suggests surplus is intermittent; selling unreliable, interruptible capacity to enterprises violates the core promise of cloud computing. Without a verified technical mechanism to guarantee uptime distinct from retail traffic, the brand risks reputational collapse. The "cheap alternative" is doing nothing: optimize internal efficiency rather than betting the company on an unproven external product with no distribution channel to developers.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-launch, the platform suffers major outages during retail peak seasons (e.g., holidays), causing enterprise customers to flee and sue for SLA breaches. The core retail business suffers performance degradation due to resource contention. Investors pull funding as margins dilute further, forcing a fire sale of the infrastructure division.

**Blockers:** 
1. **No Edge:** Being a retailer confers no advantage in selling compute; it is a distraction.
2. **Contradictory Evidence:** "Spiky" internal usage implies inability to guarantee the consistent availability required for paid external contracts.
3. **Capital Conflict:** Heavy capex is already under investor scrutiny; adding more risk without proven demand violates fiduciary duty.

**Risks:** Total brand erosion, core site instability, massive capital burn on an unproven model.

**Edge:** NONE. Cost advantage from spare capacity is negated by the cost of building enterprise-grade reliability and support from scratch.

**Decision Logic:** A plan with no competitive edge, high capital requirements, direct conflict with investor concerns, and a high probability of destroying core business value cannot proceed. The risks are existential; the upside is speculative.

DECISION: NO-GO
