The claim is that a $1,500 head-worn camera device, positioned as an everyday consumer product, will succeed via an invitation-only early adopter program followed by a general launch.

**Steelman:** An exclusive, high-price launch creates scarcity and validates the category among tech enthusiasts, generating enough revenue and feedback to refine the product for mass market before privacy or battery issues become fatal at scale.

**Attack:**
1.  **Value Mismatch:** The proposal positions this as an "everyday consumer product," yet the $1,500 price point and "invitation-only" access explicitly exclude the everyday consumer. The core value proposition (utility for most people) contradicts the go-to-market strategy (elite exclusivity).
2.  **Unresolved Blockers:** The raised risks are not mere uncertainties; they are fundamental flaws for the stated positioning. "No clear everyday use for most people" directly negates the "everyday consumer product" claim. If the use case isn't clear at $1,500 with early adopters (who tolerate flaws for novelty), it will not emerge at scale.
3.  **Privacy & Social Friction:** A always-on camera in public spaces invites immediate regulatory and social backlash. Without a proven technical or policy mitigation (e.g., hardware indicators, strict data limits) mentioned in the plan, the product faces boycott or bans before reaching the "general public" phase.
4.  **Battery Reality:** Short battery life renders an "everyday" device unusable. This is a functional failure, not a risk to be managed later.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids reputational damage from a failed high-profile launch.
*   **Enterprise Pivot:** Target specific industrial uses (logistics, surgery) where $1,500 is justified, privacy is controlled, and utility is clear. This bypasses the social/privacy blockers.

**Verdict:**
The plan lacks an **edge**. Being first, having a brand, or creating hype is not an edge when the core utility is undefined for the target mass market and the price prohibits that market from participating. The proposal admits the primary target ("most people") sees no use for it. Launching as an "everyday product" when it is neither affordable nor useful for everyday people is a strategic contradiction. The risks cited are actually known blockers that the proposal fails to solve.

DECISION: NO-GO
