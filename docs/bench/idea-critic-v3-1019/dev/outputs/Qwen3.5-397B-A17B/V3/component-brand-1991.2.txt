**Steelman:** Transform an invisible component into a consumer demand driver by subsidizing OEM ads featuring a new brand logo, forcing buyers to specify the chip by name.

**Analysis:**
The proposal addresses a critical vulnerability: commodity pricing driven by buyer ignorance. If buyers cannot distinguish the product, they will not pay a premium, and clone makers will erode margins. The "unprecedented" nature of consumer advertising for a component is not a blocker but the specific edge required; if it were common, the opportunity would already be exploited. The risk of OEM resentment is mitigated by the financial incentive (subsidized advertising), aligning interests rather than dictating creative control.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Continue B2B-only sales. Result: Buyers remain indifferent, clone makers capture share via part-number confusion, and pricing power collapses.
2.  **Price Cuts:** Lower processor costs to compete with clones. Result: Immediate margin destruction without solving the brand ambiguity; trains buyers to expect lower prices.
3.  **Legal Action:** Sue clone makers on part numbers. Result: Slow, costly, and fails to address the root cause (buyer ignorance).

**Pre-mortem & Kill Criteria:**
Assume failure: The campaign burns budget while OEMs refuse to place the logo prominently, or buyers ignore the logo.
*   **Door Type:** Two-way door. If the campaign fails to move demand within two quarters, the subsidy can be halted immediately without irreversible damage.
*   **Cost of Delay:** High. Every month delayed allows clone makers to solidify market perception that "all chips are the same."
*   **Edge:** First-mover advantage in creating a consumer brand for a hidden component. No rival has this asset.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal contains its own funding mechanism (marketing budget reallocation) and addresses the core strategic threat.
**Risks:** OEM cooperation levels, consumer apathy. These are execution risks, not strategic blockers.
**Edge:** Unique ability to decouple price from commodity comparison by injecting emotional brand value into a technical purchase.

The plan beats "do nothing" because the status quo guarantees commoditization. The risks are manageable via the proposed pilot structure (paying only when ads carry the logo).

DECISION: GO
