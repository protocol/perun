Steelman: A $180 tabletop stereoscopic 3D console with red monochrome displays fills the gap between handheld and home generations, leveraging existing IP before the next home console arrives in two years.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Product-Market Fit Failure:** The device is neither portable (defeating the "handheld gap" logic) nor comfortable (eyepiece requirement), creating a useless middle child.
2.  **Health Liability:** Known risk of eye strain and headaches directly contradicts the core value proposition of immersive play, inviting returns and reputational damage.
3.  **Content Void:** "Few games at launch" guarantees poor software attach rates, the primary driver of console profitability.
4.  **Obsolescence:** With a true next-gen home console due in 24 months, this stopgap risks cannibalizing marketing focus and developer mindshare for a superior, imminent product.

**Risks:**
Red-on-black visuals limit genre appeal; high price point ($180 in 1995) for a single-feature device; potential brand dilution if perceived as a gimmick.

**Edge:**
NONE. Being first to market with uncomfortable, headache-inducing technology is not an advantage. The proposal relies on "strategic necessity" to fill a timeline gap rather than a genuine consumer demand or technical breakthrough. The stated facts confirm the target users likely do not want a stationary device that causes physical pain.

**Pre-mortem:**
Launch occurs; reviewers pan the ergonomics and visuals; parents return units due to children's headaches; developers ignore the platform for the upcoming home console; inventory piles up within six months, forcing a fire sale that devalues the brand.

**Decision Method:**
The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test. Doing nothing preserves capital and brand equity for the upcoming home console. The cheaper alternative is to accelerate software for the existing handheld or wait for the home console. The cost of delay (waiting 2 years) is lower than the cost of a failed product launch that damages the brand. No kill criteria can save a product fundamentally flawed in ergonomics and value proposition.

DECISION: NO-GO
