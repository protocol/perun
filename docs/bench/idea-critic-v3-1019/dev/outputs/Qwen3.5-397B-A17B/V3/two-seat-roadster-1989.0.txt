The claim rests on reviving a "dead" niche via affordability and lightweight design. The steelman: a low-capital, front-engine/rear-drive convertible can profit by targeting underserved enthusiasts ignored by mass-market safety bloat, leveraging existing components to bypass heritage deficits.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The stated facts confirm the segment has shrunk for years and sales rely on a finite pool of "early enthusiasts" before fading. With no heritage, no proven demand beyond initial nostalgia, and high regulatory costs for a low-volume body style, the unit economics likely cannot sustain production after the initial launch spike. The "affordable" price point conflicts directly with the "costly" safety rules for convertibles; without a verified bill of materials showing profitability at the target price, the core financial premise is unsupported.

Alternatives:
1. **Do nothing:** Avoids sunk costs in a shrinking market.
2. **Limited edition:** A fixed-run model caps exposure but sacrifices long-term viability.
3. **Shared platform:** Use an existing open-top chassis to reduce tooling, though this compromises the "lightweight" edge.

The pre-mortem reveals failure occurs when initial enthusiast orders exhaust, leaving high fixed costs against a too-small total addressable market. The cost of delay is low; the niche is not expanding. The proposal lacks an "edge": being first to a shrinking market is not an advantage, nor is "affordability" if safety regulations erase margins. There is no stated asset (e.g., unused engine capacity, specific supplier deal) that rivals lack.

Blockers: The conflict between "affordable" pricing and "costly" safety compliance is unresolved; no evidence shows positive margins post-launch spike.
Risks: Rapid sales fade after early adopters; regulatory cost overruns.
Edge: NONE (nostalgia is not a sustainable moat against structural market shrinkage).

Without a capped, time-boxed pilot or verified margin data, the plan assumes demand that the proposal itself says is disappearing.

DECISION: NO-GO
