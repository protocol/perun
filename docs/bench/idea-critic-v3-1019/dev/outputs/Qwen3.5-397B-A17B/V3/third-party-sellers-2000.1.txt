The claim is that opening the platform to third-party sellers, including competitors, will drive profitability despite risks of cannibalization and brand damage. The steelman: this transforms fixed inventory risk into variable fee revenue, creates a long-tail selection impossible to stock alone, and locks in customers by making the site the universal starting point for commerce, regardless of who fulfills the order.

Alternatives like strict inventory expansion or waiting for organic profitability fail to address the core constraint: capital inefficiency. Doing nothing preserves margin per unit but caps total addressable market and leaves fixed costs uncovered. The "cheaper" path is leveraging existing traffic to monetize demand the retailer cannot physically satisfy.

Risks of undercutting and used-item cannibalization are real but secondary to the survival imperative of reaching profitability. If a used copy sells, the retailer earns a fee without holding the asset; if a competitor sells, the retailer captures rent on infrastructure it already built. Supplier objection is a negotiation problem, not a business model flaw. Trust risks are mitigated by the retailer's curation role; failure here is an execution gap, not a strategic veto.

The pre-mortem reveals failure only if the marketplace dilutes the brand so severely that core customers flee before fee revenue scales. However, the proposal's edge is unique: the retailer owns the customer relationship and the product page real estate. Competitors lack this aggregated demand; pure marketplaces lack the retailer's trusted inventory baseline. This asymmetry allows the retailer to set the terms of coexistence.

The blocker test finds no factual contradiction: the math works if fee volume offsets margin erosion, which is likely given the infinite shelf-space advantage. The risks are manageable via kill criteria (e.g., churn rates, defect rates), not reasons to halt. The edge is the owned traffic and trust, which competitors cannot replicate instantly.

Blockers: NONE.
Risks: Brand dilution, supplier friction (managed via metrics).
Edge: Owned demand aggregation and trusted infrastructure.

DECISION: GO
