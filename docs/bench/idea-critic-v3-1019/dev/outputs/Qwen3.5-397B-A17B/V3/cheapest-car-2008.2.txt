The claim is that launching the world's cheapest car at $2,500 from a new factory will successfully transition scooter families to cars. The steelman: an ultra-low price point unlocks a massive, unserved demographic, creating a new market segment where volume offsets razor-thin margins.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test on three critical fronts derived directly from the provided facts. First, the "cheapest" positioning is a strategic liability, not an edge. The facts state buyers may not want a car *known* as the cheapest, and scooters/used cars are cheaper alternatives. If the primary differentiator is price, but the product is still more expensive than the incumbent (scooters) and carries a stigma of inferiority, the value proposition collapses. There is no stated asset (e.g., superior safety, weather protection) that outweighs the cost delta against used cars.

Second, the operational foundation is unstable. The factory site faces active farmer protests. This is not a future risk; it is a current fact threatening the very existence of the production line. A plan relying on a dedicated high-volume factory cannot proceed when the land access is contested.

Third, the cost-cutting required to hit $2,500 explicitly raises doubts about safety and quality. For a family vehicle, safety is a baseline requirement, not a luxury. If the market perceives the car as unsafe due to aggressive cost cutting, the "family" target audience will reject it regardless of price.

The proposal lacks an edge. Being first to a "cheapest" label is not an advantage when the label itself drives rejection. The risks (land protests, safety perception, price stigma) are not merely possibilities; they are stated conditions that directly contradict the path to success. The alternative of "do nothing" or waiting to resolve the land dispute and reposition the brand away from "cheapest" is superior to launching a stigmatized product on contested land.

Blockers: Contested land prevents production; "cheapest" branding repels the target family demographic; safety doubts undermine the core use case.
Edge: NONE. Price alone is insufficient against cheaper, proven alternatives.

DECISION: NO-GO
