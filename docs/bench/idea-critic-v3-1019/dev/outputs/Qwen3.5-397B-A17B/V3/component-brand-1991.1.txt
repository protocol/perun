**Steelman:** Transform an invisible component into a consumer demand driver by subsidizing OEM advertising, forcing buyers to specify the brand and compelling manufacturers to comply to access marketing funds.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces four explicit risks: unprecedented category strategy, high budget consumption, channel resentment, and unproven sales linkage. However, the "invisible component" status is the primary blocker to premium pricing; without consumer pull, the company remains a commodity supplier at the mercy of computer makers.

*   **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes branding power to clone makers using similar part numbers, accelerating commoditization. Direct-to-consumer education without OEM partnership is impossible if buyers cannot identify the product inside the machine. The proposed co-op model is the only mechanism that aligns OEM reach with brand visibility.
*   **Fit:** This directly attacks the "clone" threat by making the specific part number a consumer requirement, differentiating the product beyond technical specs.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if OEMs refuse the logo conditions or if consumers ignore the mark. Yet, the financial lever (paying part of their ad costs) creates a strong incentive for compliance; refusing free marketing budget is irrational for cost-sensitive manufacturers.
*   **Evidence:** The proposal acknowledges the risks but offers a structural solution: financial subsidy in exchange for brand placement. The "unprecedented" nature is the source of the competitive edge, not a flaw; if it were common, clones would already be doing it.
*   **Blockers:** None. The risks are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The lack of prior art in component branding is an opportunity for first-mover advantage, not a prohibition.
*   **Edge:** The ability to fund OEM ads creates a dependency that turns manufacturers into brand distributors. No rival can replicate this without matching the subsidy commitment.

**Kill Criteria:** If, after one year, consumer awareness of the logo remains below 20% or OEM participation drops below 50%, the program must be terminated.

**Decision:** The potential to shift power from manufacturers to the component supplier outweighs the budget risk. The co-op model effectively buys distribution and visibility simultaneously.

DECISION: GO
