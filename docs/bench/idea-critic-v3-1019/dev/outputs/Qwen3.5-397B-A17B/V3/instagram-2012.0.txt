**Steelman:** Acquire the dominant mobile-native photo network immediately to neutralize the primary threat to the buyer's mobile relevance and secure the user base before an IPO makes the target untouchable or acquired by a rival.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The valuation ($1B for ~12 staff, $0 revenue) is mathematically indefensible on current fundamentals. However, the strategic cost of *not* acting exceeds the financial loss if the target becomes the default mobile layer for social sharing, rendering the buyer's existing photo product obsolete. The "no business model" risk is irrelevant if the asset's value is purely defensive (blocking a competitor) and distributive (access to tens of millions of high-engagement mobile users).

The "culture destruction" risk is the most probable failure mode. History shows acquirers often suffocate small, agile teams with bureaucracy, causing the very talent and innovation being purchased to walk out the door. Yet, the alternative—allowing a rival to own this network or letting the target IPO and compete independently—poses an existential threat to the buyer's mobile strategy. The regulatory review is a delay, not a blocker, given the market dynamics of 2012.

**Kill Criteria & Edge:**
*   **Blocker:** None. The threat is verified (leading mobile threat), and the window is closing (impending IPO).
*   **Edge:** Only the buyer has the cash reserves and strategic desperation to pay this premium *now*. No other entity can close this specific defensive gap immediately.
*   **Risk:** Overpayment and cultural integration failure.
*   **Mitigation:** The deal must include strict autonomy guarantees for the target team to preserve the "edge" (their culture/product sense).

**Decision Logic:**
While the price is exorbitant and the revenue non-existent, the strategic necessity of controlling the mobile photo graph outweighs the financial absurdity. Waiting for a "better price" or a "proven business model" allows the threat to mature beyond containment. The cost of delay is the loss of mobile relevance. The only viable path is to absorb the financial hit to secure the strategic position, provided the integration preserves the target's independence.

DECISION: GO
