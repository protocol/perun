The claim is that a clear cola, validated by test markets, should launch nationwide immediately with a major campaign to capture growth. The steelman: clarity signals purity to a specific segment, creating a new category where the brand leads, offsetting soft-drink stagnation before rivals react.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The "edge" relies entirely on test market data that the proposal itself admits "can overstate demand." There is no verified evidence that the positive response translates to repeat purchase once novelty fades, a critical failure mode explicitly noted as a risk. Furthermore, the plan lacks a defined kill criterion or a staged rollout; it demands a full nationwide launch and large marketing spend despite unproven long-term viability. This ignores the "do nothing" or "limited regional expansion" alternatives which would validate repeat rates at a fraction of the cost and cannibalization risk.

The pre-mortem is clear: the product launches as a fad, novelty buyers do not return, and the massive marketing spend accelerates cannibalization of the flagship without securing a permanent new customer base. The proposal offers no mechanism to stop or pivot if early sales are driven solely by curiosity. A sound plan must prove retention, not just trial. Since the proposal commits to a high-cost, low-reversibility national launch without first verifying the core assumption of repeat purchase or establishing a safety valve, it cannot be approved as written. The risk of damaging the flagship brand's equity and wasting capital on a fleeting trend outweighs the unverified upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
