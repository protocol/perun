**Steelman:** Migrating to the dominant architecture solves the thermal/performance bottleneck preventing thin laptop competitiveness, using a translation layer to preserve the existing software ecosystem during a managed two-year transition.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces severe execution risks. Announcing early risks immediate revenue collapse as customers freeze purchases awaiting the new line. The "distinctiveness" risk is existential; if the hardware becomes identical to generic PCs, the brand loses its premium justification. Furthermore, relying on a translation layer admits a period of sub-par performance, potentially damaging the reputation for quality. The memory of a painful prior transition suggests organizational fatigue and potential operational fragility.

However, the **blocker test** reveals a fatal flaw in the status quo: the current supplier *cannot* deliver the required physics (cool, efficient chips for thin form factors). Staying on the current roadmap guarantees product irrelevance in the emerging thin-and-light category. The "do nothing" option leads to market exit.

The risks cited (sales freeze, developer friction, performance hit) are execution challenges, not blockers, provided the transition is managed tightly. The translation layer is a necessary bridge, not a permanent crutch. The loss of distinctiveness is mitigated if the new architecture enables industrial design innovations (thinness, battery life) that generic competitors cannot immediately match due to integration expertise. The painful history is a warning to over-communicate and stage the rollout, not a reason to abort.

**Edge:** The company controls both hardware and OS integration, allowing it to optimize the translation layer and thermal management better than fragmented competitors. This vertical integration is the only path to achieving the "thin and cool" requirement that the current supplier fails to meet.

**Decision Logic:**
- **Blockers:** None. The current path is a guaranteed failure; the proposed path is the only viable route to product viability.
- **Risks:** High, but manageable with strict secrecy until launch and aggressive developer support.
- **Edge:** Vertical integration enables performance/efficiency unattainable by the current supply chain.

The cost of delay is total market obsolescence in the laptop segment. The proposal must proceed, but with extreme operational discipline to mitigate the transition pain.

DECISION: GO
