**Steelman:** A controlled native app store creates a defensible ecosystem that balances developer innovation with product quality and security, turning a "web-only" device into a platform while retaining brand control.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker is the contradiction between the CEO's public stance ("web apps are sufficient") and this pivot. However, the existence of rival ecosystems with open distribution creates an existential threat: without native apps, the phone risks irrelevance regardless of current polish. The "web-only" strategy fails the north star of long-term platform viability.

Security and stability risks are real but mitigated by the proposed central review process. While this creates a workload bottleneck, it is the necessary cost of curating quality against "open" rivals that may suffer from fragmentation or malware. Carrier objections are a negotiation hurdle, not a technical blocker; the 30% revenue share and controlled environment offer carriers a safer alternative to unmanaged bypasses.

The "do nothing" option preserves short-term polish but guarantees long-term decline as user expectations shift toward rich native experiences. The proposed model beats "do nothing" because it captures the upside of an ecosystem (developer lock-in, new revenue) while minimizing the downside of open distribution (instability, security breaches).

**Edge:** The single company-run store allows for a curated, high-quality user experience that fragmented open platforms cannot match, leveraging the phone's existing reputation for polish. The 30% cut aligns incentives without stifling development.

**Risks:** Review bottlenecks could slow innovation; carrier pushback could delay launch. These are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The kill criterion is clear: if the review process cannot scale to meet developer demand within 6 months, the model must pivot to tiered approval.

**Verdict:** The proposal addresses the critical gap in the product strategy. The risks are manageable via the proposed controls, and the edge (curated quality) is distinct. The CEO's prior stance is a communication challenge, not a strategic barrier.

DECISION: GO
