**Steelman:** A full rewrite with a new surfacing algorithm and publisher focus, launched via big-bang migration, is the only way to reverse traffic decline and compete with a growing rival.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Irreversibility):** Moving all users at once eliminates the rollback path. If the new algorithm alienates the core community (who value existing mechanics), traffic could collapse permanently before fixes are deployed. This contradicts the need to preserve the loyal base while experimenting.
2.  **Edge Analysis:** The proposal cites a rival's growth and "strategic necessity" but offers no unique asset (cost, distribution, or product advantage) to win. Favoring publishers may further erode the community-driven value proposition that defined the site's initial success.
3.  **Better Way:** A phased rollout (canary release) to a small user segment would validate the new mechanics and publisher focus without risking the entire community. This allows for data-driven iteration rather than a binary success/failure launch.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Launch fails because the core community revolts against the altered voting mechanics, perceiving the shift to publisher content as a betrayal of the platform's ethos, driving them to the rival or alternative niches. The "big-bang" approach ensures this failure is total and immediate.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** The "no rollback" condition of a big-bang launch is a factual blocker given the high risk of community alienation.
*   **Edge:** NONE. The proposal relies on fear of falling behind and a hope that "new is better," without a demonstrated unique advantage.
*   **Risks:** High engineering risk, community churn, total service disruption.

The plan fails the blocker test: it proposes an irreversible action without a safety net for a high-probability failure mode (community rejection). It lacks a defined edge beyond catching up to a rival. The correct move is a staged rollout with clear kill criteria, not a total rewrite launched to everyone simultaneously.

DECISION: NO-GO
