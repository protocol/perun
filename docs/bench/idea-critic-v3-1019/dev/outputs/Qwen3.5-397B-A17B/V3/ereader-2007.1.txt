**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled e-reader removes friction for book buyers and solves publisher piracy fears, creating a new high-margin digital revenue stream that outweighs hardware risks and print cannibalization.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces four critical objections: lack of hardware expertise, publisher resistance to low prices, historical failure of e-readers, and internal cannibalization.

1.  **Hardware Inexperience:** While valid, this is a "build vs. buy" execution risk, not a strategic blocker. The core value is the ecosystem, not the silicon. Partnering or outsourcing manufacturing mitigates this.
2.  **Publisher Margins:** Publishers fear devaluation. However, the "single low price" model expands the total addressable market by enabling impulse buys. The alternative—piracy or stagnation—offers zero margin. The cellular connection acts as a distribution moat, ensuring sales happen exclusively through the store, appeasing publishers with volume and security.
3.  **Past Failures:** Previous e-readers failed due to poor screens, high cost, and lack of content. This proposal specifically addresses all three: paper-like screen (usability), subsidized connectivity (convenience), and a massive existing catalog (content). The "do nothing" option guarantees ceding the digital future to competitors who solve these specific gaps.
4.  **Cannibalization:** Cannibalization is inevitable; the choice is whether to cannibalize your own print sales or let a competitor do it. Digital offers near-zero marginal cost per unit compared to print logistics, ultimately improving blended margins despite lower per-title revenue.

**Edge:** The company possesses the only asset that mattered in 2007: a massive, curated catalog and an existing customer base of readers. Competitors have hardware; this company has the content demand. The cellular subsidy is the "loss leader" strategy applied to distribution, locking users into the store.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks are executional, not fundamental flaws in the thesis.
**Risks:** Hardware supply chain delays; publisher holdouts.
**Kill Criteria:** If device adoption does not reach 10% of active buyers within 18 months, or if publisher participation remains below 50% of top titles, halt hardware expansion and pivot to software-only apps.

The "do nothing" path yields certain long-term decline as the market digitizes. The proposed path trades short-term hardware complexity for long-term platform dominance.

DECISION: GO
