**Steelman:** Direct-to-consumer streaming captures full lifetime value and customer data, securing long-term survival as licensing windows shrink and rivals build competing walled gardens.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Continue earning billions in high-margin licensing fees while rivals use that capital to dominate the direct-to-consumer market, eventually squeezing the conglomerate out entirely.
2.  **Hybrid/Windowing:** License content with delays to preserve revenue while testing a smaller service. *Failure:* Rivals gain exclusive mindshare; delayed content loses cultural relevance.
3.  **The Proposal:** Sacrifice immediate cash flow for market position.

**Blockers:** None found in the text. The loss of licensing revenue and cash burn are explicit costs, not hidden contradictions. The proposal acknowledges the lack of tech experience as a risk to be managed, not a fact proving impossibility.

**Risks:** Massive cash burn requiring deep pockets; execution failure on unproven tech stack; cannibalization of existing profitable cable/theatrical units; subscriber targets may be overly optimistic against an entrenched leader.

**Edge:** Ownership of the IP library is a unique asset competitors cannot replicate. Unlike pure-tech entrants, this entity controls the supply chain. Being late is a greater existential threat than the cost of entry. The "low price" strategy leverages existing brand recognition to undercut the leader.

**Decision Logic:**
The "do nothing" option leads to eventual irrelevance as the market shifts decisively to streaming. While the financial risks are severe, they are known quantities funded by current billions in revenue. The lack of tech experience is a hurdle, not a blocker, solvable via hiring or acquisition. The proposal explicitly accepts the loss of licensing revenue as the price of admission. Since the core claim (survival requires ownership of the customer relationship) holds against the alternative (dependency on rivals), and the risks are acknowledged rather than ignored, the plan survives the pre-mortem. The edge (IP ownership) is real and defensible.

DECISION: GO
