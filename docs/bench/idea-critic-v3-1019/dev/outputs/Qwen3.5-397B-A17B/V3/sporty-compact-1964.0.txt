**Claim:** Launch a sporty, customizable coupe/convertible on existing compact mechanics with a low base price and massive ad spend to capture young postwar buyers.

**Steelman:** Reusing proven mechanicals minimizes development risk and cost, enabling a low entry price that hooks volume buyers, while high-margin options and distinctive styling create a profitable "affordable luxury" segment before competitors react.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly mitigates the "cheap look" risk via styling cues (long hood/short deck) and separates the product perception from the economy donor through customization and marketing. The unproven demand is the very gap this product intends to fill; waiting for proof cedes the segment.

**Risks:** If the styling fails to distance the car from its economy roots, sales will stall despite ad spend. If the options list is too complex, it may confuse rather than entice. However, the low base price limits downside exposure per unit, and the shared parts keep fixed costs low.

**Edge:** First-mover advantage in a nascent "pony car" category by leveraging existing manufacturing capacity to undercut potential rivals on price while offering superior stylistic emotional appeal. The donor platform's reliability is an asset, not a liability, if marketed correctly.

**Decision Method:**
1.  **Alternatives:** Doing nothing yields zero growth in this demographic. Building a new platform from scratch is too slow and expensive. A cheaper path (facelift the compact) lacks the necessary sporty image.
2.  **Fit:** Directly targets the identified demographic shift (young, style-conscious, budget-limited).
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs only if the styling looks too much like the economy car or the price creeps up. The proposed long options list allows the base price to remain a headline grabber.
4.  **Kill Criteria:** If first-year sales do not cover the marginal marketing spend, pivot to fleet sales or discontinue the convertible.

The plan converts a commodity platform into a high-margin lifestyle product. The risks are manageable marketing challenges, not structural flaws. The edge is clear: speed to market with emotional design at an unbeatable price point.

DECISION: GO
