**Steelman:** Replacing the flagship with a demonstrably superior-tasting formula will halt share loss by satisfying the primary driver of consumption: taste preference.

**Attack:** The proposal conflates "preferred in a sip test" with "preferred as a daily beverage." Blind tests isolate flavor but strip away brand identity, heritage, and the psychological ownership consumers feel toward a century-old institution. By withdrawing the original entirely, the plan transforms a product upgrade into a cultural dispossession. The testing methodology is fatally flawed: it failed to disclose the removal of the original, meaning participants evaluated the new drink as an *addition* or *substitute* in a vacuum, not as a *replacement* of something they love.

**Better Way:** A line extension (launching the sweeter formula alongside the original) captures the taste-preference segment without alienating the heritage base. This preserves the "do nothing" safety net while testing real-world sales impact.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch triggers a backlash where loyalists perceive the removal as corporate arrogance. The narrative shifts from "better taste" to "they took away our history." Sales plummet not because the drink tastes bad, but because the act of removal validates negative emotions. Without a fallback, the company cannot de-escalate; they are forced to defend an unpopular decision until financial damage forces a humiliating reversal.

**Blocker:** The testing data explicitly excludes the variable of "removal." Therefore, the core claim—that the new formula will succeed *as a replacement*—is unsupported by the cited evidence. The risk of emotional attachment is not a mere "risk"; it is a certainty given the brand's tenure, and the plan offers no mitigation other than hope.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first to remove a heritage product is a liability, not an advantage. The "sweeter" edge is negated by the loss of trust.

**Verdict:** The plan relies on data that actively hides the primary failure mode (removal anxiety). Proceeding without a fallback or a test that includes the removal variable is reckless.

DECISION: NO-GO
