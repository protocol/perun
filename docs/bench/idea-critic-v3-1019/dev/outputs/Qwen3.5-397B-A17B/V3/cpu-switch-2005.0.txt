**Steelman:** Migrating to the dominant architecture solves the thermal/performance bottleneck preventing competitive thin laptops, using a translation layer to preserve the existing software ecosystem during a two-year transition.

**Attack & Options:**
The proposal faces severe execution risks: sales freezes upon announcement, performance penalties for translated apps, and brand dilution. However, the *status quo* is a fatal blocker: the current supplier cannot deliver chips for thin, efficient laptops, and their roadmap lags the market. Without this move, the manufacturer cannot compete in the fastest-growing PC segment. "Do nothing" guarantees irrelevance in portable computing.

Alternative options like dual-sourcing are impossible given the supplier's roadmap failure. A slower transition extends the period of uncompetitive products. The only viable alternative is abandoning the laptop market entirely, which contradicts the company's core identity.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the north star of delivering superior user experiences in portable forms. A pre-mortem suggests failure would stem from a buggy translation layer or mismanaged timing causing a sales cliff. Yet, the company has survived a painful transition before; institutional knowledge exists to mitigate this. The risk of announcing early is real but manageable via controlled leaks or tight launch windows; the risk of *not* announcing is shipping uncompetitive hardware.

**Evidence & Edge:**
The factual premise is the supplier's inability to provide cool, efficient chips. This is a hard constraint, not a projection. The company's edge is its loyal user base and the translation layer, which lowers the switching cost for users compared to a hard break. While translated software is slower, native performance on the new architecture offers a massive upside that current chips cannot match.

*Blockers:* NONE. The current path is unsustainable.
