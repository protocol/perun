Steelman: Acquiring the agency and platform instantly grants the technology and scale needed to challenge the search leader's recent move, preventing permanent market exclusion.

**Skeptic:** The $6 billion cash price is a massive premium in a fast-changing market, risking severe value destruction if display technology shifts again. The proposal admits conflicts with existing agency customers; alienating current revenue streams to chase a volatile new segment contradicts fiduciary caution. The "fast-changing" nature of the target market makes the asset's longevity highly uncertain, turning a strategic buy into a speculative gamble.

**Better-way:** The "do nothing" option avoids immediate cash depletion and customer conflict while allowing observation of the competitor's integration struggles. A cheaper, reversible alternative exists: build proprietary technology organically or form exclusive licensing partnerships, avoiding the integration nightmare and cultural clash of buying a direct competitor to one's own clients.

**Kill-criteria:** If the acquisition does not secure a top-three market share within 18 months, or if churn among existing agency customers exceeds 15% post-announcement, the strategic thesis fails. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a botched $6 billion integration.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Financial Overreach:** The cash outlay represents an unjustified premium for an asset in a volatile sector without guaranteed retention of the target's talent or clients.
2. **Channel Conflict:** The plan explicitly acknowledges damaging relationships with existing agency customers, creating an immediate revenue risk that outweighs the hypothetical future gain.
3. **Market Timing:** Entering via acquisition during a "fast-changing" technological shift often results in buying peak assets just before obsolescence.

**Edge:** NONE. Being second to market with a more expensive, conflicted solution is not an edge; it is a defensive panic move. The competitor's move does not mandate an identical, overpriced response.

**Decision:** The combination of excessive price, admitted channel conflict, and market volatility creates a high probability of capital destruction. The plan fails to demonstrate how it preserves existing value while capturing new value.

DECISION: NO-GO
