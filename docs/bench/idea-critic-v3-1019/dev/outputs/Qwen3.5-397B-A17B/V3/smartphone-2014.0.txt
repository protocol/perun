Steelman: A differentiated hardware feature (3D display) and unique utility (one-touch shopping) can overcome app ecosystem deficits and carrier exclusivity to capture high-value shoppers.

**Skeptic:** The proposal assumes users value 3D visuals and instant product identification more than access to mainstream apps. In a two-platform duopoly, the "app gap" is a primary driver of churn, not a secondary concern. The "unsubsidised-equivalent price" places a niche device in direct competition with established flagships that offer full ecosystem support. Without verified data showing existing customers prioritize 3D over app availability, this is an unsupported claim. The head-tracking 3D feature is a novelty, not a sustained utility, likely failing to retain users after initial curiosity.

**Better-way:** The "do nothing" option preserves capital and avoids entering a saturated market with a structural disadvantage (forked OS). A cheaper alternative is integrating the "one-touch product identification" software feature into the existing successful e-reader and tablet apps, leveraging the current user base and full app stores of iOS/Android. This captures the unique value proposition without the massive overhead of hardware manufacturing, carrier negotiations, and OS maintenance.

**Kill-criteria:** If the device cannot secure a critical mass of daily active users within six months due to app incompatibility, the project fails. The "one-way door" of building a forked OS creates long-term technical debt with limited exit options. The cost of delay is low; waiting for the market to mature or for AR technology to become standard on flagship devices reduces risk.

**Edge:** NONE. Being an online retailer does not confer a hardware or OS advantage. "Strategic necessity" or fear of missing out is not an edge. The proposed differentiators are gimmicks compared to the utility of the dominant platforms.

Blockers: The app gap contradicts the requirement for a daily-use device; price matches flagships without matching value; carrier exclusivity limits reach in a saturated market.
Risks: High churn due to missing apps; novelty wear-off on 3D feature; massive sunk cost in OS fork.

DECISION: NO-GO
