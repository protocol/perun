**Steelman:** A mid-size manufacturer can profitably revive the affordable two-seat roadster by leveraging lightweight, front-engine, rear-drive simplicity to serve an underserved niche that mass-market players have abandoned due to complexity, not lack of demand.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces immediate structural blockers. First, the "affordable" claim conflicts with the reality of 1989 safety regulations; complying with crash standards for a low-volume convertible typically destroys the cost advantage required to be "affordable." Second, the company lacks sports-car heritage, meaning it possesses neither the supply chain for specialized components nor the brand equity to command the premium necessary to offset low volumes. Third, the admission that sales may fade after early enthusiasts buy confirms a lack of sustainable total addressable market; without a recurring buyer base or fleet potential, the tooling investment cannot be amortized over a viable product lifecycle.

**Alternatives:**
The "do nothing" option preserves capital for core segments with growing demand. A "better way" would be a limited edition trim on an existing chassis rather than a dedicated new launch, reducing risk and tooling costs. However, even this fails to solve the heritage deficit.

**Edge Assessment:**
The proposal claims an edge based on "market disappearance," but this is a trap; the market disappeared because the economics turned negative, not because of a temporary oversight. Being first to re-enter a graveyard is not an edge. The plan relies on the hope that "lightweight" offsets regulatory costs, but offers no evidence of a technical breakthrough to achieve this. There is no stated asset, cost advantage, or proven demand beyond a fleeting enthusiast wave.

**Verdict:**
This is a classic "nostalgia trap." The risks are confirmed by the proposer's own admissions (shrinking segment, fading sales). There is no blocker that can be removed by revision because the core unit economics appear fundamentally broken given the constraints. The lack of heritage and the high cost of regulatory compliance for low-volume convertibles create a fatal gap between "affordable" and "compliant."

DECISION: NO-GO
