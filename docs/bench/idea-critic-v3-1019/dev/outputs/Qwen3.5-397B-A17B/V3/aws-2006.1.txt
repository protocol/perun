**Steelman:** Leveraging existing, spiky infrastructure to sell unused compute capacity creates a high-margin revenue stream that amortizes fixed costs, turning a liability into a competitive advantage despite lack of platform experience.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "Edge" test. The retailer's core competency is retail logistics, not developer platform reliability or ecosystem management. The "spiky" infrastructure designed for internal retail peaks is architecturally mismatched for the steady, multi-tenant reliability required by external enterprise developers. 

**Blockers:**
1.  **Capability Gap:** No internal experience running a developer platform at scale creates an unbridgeable execution risk against incumbents who live in this domain.
2.  **Strategic Conflict:** Investors already question heavy capital spending; adding a capital-intensive, low-trust business line dilutes focus and exacerbates margin concerns without a proven path to profitability.
3.  **Market Validation:** The market for hourly server rental is explicitly "unproven." Entering a nascent market without a unique technological edge or existing developer relationships is speculation, not strategy.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Avoids distraction and capital risk; preserves focus on core retail margins.
*   *Partner:* License excess capacity to an established vendor rather than building a competing platform.

**Pre-mortem:**
One year post-launch, the platform suffers frequent outages because retail-traffic optimization conflicts with multi-tenant isolation. Major developer clients churn due to unreliability. The core retail business suffers from under-investment as management attention fractures. The initiative is shut down, having consumed significant capital with zero ROI.

**Verdict:**
There is no defensible edge. Being a retailer does not confer advantage in cloud infrastructure; it introduces disadvantage in culture and architecture. The risks (distraction, margin dilution, unproven market) are immediate and concrete, while the upside relies on executing a completely new business model better than entrenched specialists with no evidence of demand. This violates the principle that a new venture must beat "do nothing" on stated facts; here, the facts suggest failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
