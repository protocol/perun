**Steelman:** Separating the declining DVD asset from the high-growth streaming brand allows distinct strategies, prevents the legacy cost structure from dragging down the streaming valuation, and isolates customer anger over the recent 60% price hike to the legacy brand.

**Attack:** The proposal catastrophically misreads the timing. Executing a brand split, separate billing, and dual logins immediately after a 60% price increase is not strategic differentiation; it is perceived as a "double penalty" designed to extract maximum friction and cost from loyal users. The rationale claims this frees streaming, but the immediate effect is to force customers who want both services to manage two accounts and pay two bills, directly amplifying the "customer confusion" and "reaction" risks already identified.

The loss of shared ratings and queues degrades the user experience for the hybrid customer without offering a compensating benefit. While the DVD business is declining, it still generates cash flow; fracturing the relationship now risks accelerating churn faster than the streaming unit can grow, destroying total enterprise value. The "different management" goal can be achieved internally via separate P&Ls without inflicting operational friction on the customer base during a period of extreme price sensitivity.

**Kill Criteria:** If the separation increases support ticket volume regarding billing confusion by >20% in the first month or causes combined-subscriber churn to exceed 15% in Q4, the strategy fails. The current plan lacks a mitigation bridge for the price-hike backlash.

**Edge:** NONE. The proposed edge (strategic freedom) is outweighed by the execution risk of alienating the entire existing customer base simultaneously. There is no asset or market condition cited that makes *this specific moment* the right time to add friction. The plan solves an internal accounting desire while ignoring the external perception of a cash grab.

**Verdict:** The proposal fails the "fit" test against customer sentiment. It treats a pricing crisis as a branding opportunity. The correct move is to stabilize the relationship post-price-hike before introducing structural fragmentation.

DECISION: NO-GO
