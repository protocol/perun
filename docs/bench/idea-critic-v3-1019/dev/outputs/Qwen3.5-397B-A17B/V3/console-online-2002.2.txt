**Steelman:** A unified, paid online service creates a defensible moat through network effects and recurring revenue, differentiating the console via superior user experience (identity, voice, matchmaking) that fragmented, free alternatives cannot match.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces severe headwinds: low broadband penetration in 2002, consumer expectation of free online play, and the company's lack of operational experience in running a global service. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental blockers.

*   **Broadband:** While adoption is low, it is the inevitable future. Early adoption targets the most valuable demographic (hardcore gamers) who drive software attachment rates. Being first to standardize this captures the high-end market before rivals react.
*   **Free Expectation:** Competitors offering "free" services provide fragmented, inferior experiences without central identity or voice chat. The value proposition shifts from "access to servers" to "premium social infrastructure." Users pay for quality and convenience, not just connectivity.
*   **Operational Risk:** The lack of internal expertise is a valid concern but solvable via hiring or acquisition. It is a hurdle, not a stopper. The strategic advantage of owning the customer relationship and data outweighs the initial operational friction.
*   **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the premium online segment to future competitors. "Publisher-led" models fail to create a unified ecosystem, fragmenting the user base and weakening the console's brand value.

**Edge:** The company controls the hardware and OS, allowing deep integration of the service (single sign-on, system-level voice chat) that third-party publishers or rival consoles cannot easily replicate without similar control. This creates a "walled garden" advantage.

**Kill Criteria:** If subscriber adoption fails to reach 15% of the installed base within 18 months of launch, or if operational costs exceed 40% of subscription revenue in Year 1, the service must be restructured or subsidized differently.

**Verdict:** The risks are real but manageable compared to the strategic necessity of defining the online standard. The potential to lock in users and create a recurring revenue stream justifies the investment.

DECISION: GO
