The proposal fails the "edge" test and introduces a confirmed blocker. The plan attempts to solve a pricing backlash (a 60% increase) by fracturing the user experience into two brands, two logins, and two bills. This treats the symptom (brand association with the hike) while ignoring the disease (the value proposition no longer matches the cost).

**Blocker:** The proposal explicitly admits "customer confusion" and "two logins and bills" as risks. In a consumer subscription model, adding friction immediately after a massive price hike is a factual route to churn, not retention. Separating the services destroys the "combined plan" value entirely, making the 60% hike look like two separate price increases rather than one bundled adjustment. There is no asset or advantage here; splitting the brand does not lower costs or improve the product, it only hides the parent company's name from the DVD service.

**Edge:** NONE. The rationale that the DVD business is "declining" is a reason to manage it differently, not to alienate its remaining paying customers by forcing a re-registration process. A declining cash cow should be milked with maximum convenience, not friction. The "strategic necessity" for streaming to have its own strategy does not require separate billing or accounts; internal management can be split while keeping a unified customer front-end.

**Better Way:** Keep one brand, one login, and one bill. Manage the two divisions internally with separate P&Ls and roadmaps. If the DVD service must be spun down, do it via a gradual sunset or a clear, single-bill tiered option, not a confusing re-branding that feels like a punishment for loyal users.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-split, the DVD brand sees accelerated churn due to login fatigue and perceived abandonment, while the streaming brand inherits the goodwill hit without the buffer of the physical service. The market perceives the move as a desperate attempt to hide the price hike, damaging both entities.

The proposal creates operational complexity and customer hostility without solving the core pricing mismatch. It blocks its own success by removing the convenience that justifies any premium price.

DECISION: NO-GO
