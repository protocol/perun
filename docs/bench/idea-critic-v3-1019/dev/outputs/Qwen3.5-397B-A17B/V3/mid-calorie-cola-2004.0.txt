**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men avoiding full-sugar drinks but rejecting the taste of diet cola, leveraging a premium price to signal innovation before the low-carb trend fades.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "better-way" and "fit" tests against stated facts. The primary risk—that half-sugar satisfies neither group—is a fundamental product-market fit flaw, not a mere execution risk. Young men, the target demographic, typically prefer either full-sugar taste or zero-calorie utility; a compromised middle ground often results in low repeat purchase rates.

The "premium price" exacerbates this by deterring trial for an unproven taste profile. If the product fails to deliver distinct value, the high price accelerates rejection. Furthermore, the low-carb trend is explicitly identified as a potential "fad." Investing in a large campaign and new SKU for a fleeting trend violates sound capital allocation, especially when a rival is already testing similar waters, allowing them to bear the initial market-education cost.

The "do nothing" alternative remains stronger: continue growing the diet segment (which is already expanding) and maintain full-sugar loyalty. A mid-calorie option cannibalizes full-sugar sales without guaranteeing retention of diet users who might simply switch to the rival's established brand or stick to zero-calorie options if the taste compromise is unacceptable.

The proposal lacks a specific "edge." Being a large cola maker is not an edge in itself; it does not prove this specific formulation will succeed where the taste compromise is the core hurdle. The risks (cannibalization, fad dependency, ambiguous value proposition) act as blockers because the proposal offers no evidence or mechanism to overcome the likelihood that the product serves no distinct need better than existing options.

Without a pilot to validate the "satisfaction" hypothesis or a strategy to mitigate the fad risk, launching now is premature. The plan commits significant resources to a hypothesis that contradicts basic consumer segmentation logic (the "neither group" risk).

DECISION: NO-GO
