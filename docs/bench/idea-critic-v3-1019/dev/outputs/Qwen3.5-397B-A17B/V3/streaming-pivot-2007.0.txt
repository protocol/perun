The claim is that a dominant DVD rental leader should immediately launch a free, limited streaming tier in 2007 despite high costs, poor quality, and cannibalization risks. The steelman: this is a defensive necessity to retain subscribers and gather data before giants like Amazon or Apple lock up the market, leveraging existing brand trust to transition users gradually.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The "edge" is nonexistent; the offering is inferior (small catalog, poor quality) and priced at zero, offering no competitive advantage against deep-pocketed tech entrants who can subsidize losses. The primary risk is not just cannibalization, but active value destruction: paying high licensing fees for content that drives users away from the high-margin DVD core without generating new revenue. The math does not work; the unit economics are negative on day one with no clear path to profitability given the broadband limitations of 2007.

A better way exists: wait. Let competitors burn capital educating the market and fixing technology standards. Enter only when broadband penetration is higher, licensing costs stabilize, and the company can negotiate from a position of strength rather than desperation. Alternatively, launch a separate, paid pilot to test willingness-to-pay before giving it away for free to seven million users.

The pre-mortem is clear: the company bleeds cash on licensing, accelerates DVD churn faster than streaming revenue can replace it, and lacks the technical infrastructure to compete with Silicon Valley, leading to a collapsed stock price and forced sale. The cost of delay is minimal compared to the cost of executing a flawed, loss-leading model prematurely. There is no observable metric in this proposal that guarantees success; instead, it guarantees immediate margin compression.

Blockers: Negative unit economics (high license cost vs. zero price), inferior product quality, and lack of unique distribution advantage.
Risks: Accelerated cannibalization, cash burn, failure against tech giants.
Edge: NONE. Being first with a bad product is not an edge.

DECISION: NO-GO
