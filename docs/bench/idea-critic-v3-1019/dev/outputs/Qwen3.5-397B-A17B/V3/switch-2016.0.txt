**Steelman:** A hybrid device leverages the maker's unique handheld heritage to create a new category, escaping direct spec-warfare with rivals while offering home-console quality portability that phones cannot match.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal admits weak hardware, confusing history, and thin software. However, the "blocker" test reveals no fatal factual contradiction. The weak specs are a feature, not a bug: they enable the hybrid form factor and lower price point, targeting a different use case (portability) than the "far more powerful" rivals. The financial loss from the previous home console necessitates a radical departure, making "do nothing" or "iterate on home console" riskier than a pivot.

The "confusing positioning" risk is mitigated by the clarity of the hybrid value proposition itself (TV + Handheld), which is simpler to demonstrate than abstract specs. While third-party support is a risk, the proposal relies on the maker's own studios for launch, a controllable variable. Supply constraints and timing are execution risks, not strategic blockers; every launch faces them.

**Edge:** The maker possesses a specific asset rivals lack: a beloved handheld IP and expertise in portable hardware. Rivals are locked into the high-power home box or non-gaming phones. No other entity can credibly launch a "home console you can take anywhere" at this price point immediately.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders do not exceed the maker's last handheld launch by 20% within 3 months, or if attach rate of first-party software drops below 1.5 units per console in Q1, the strategy must pivot to aggressive price cuts or software bundling.

**Verdict:** The proposal survives the pre-mortem. The risks are known execution hurdles, not strategic impossibilities. The alternative (continuing to lose money on home consoles while phones erode handhelds) guarantees decline. The hybrid approach is the only path to a distinct market position.
