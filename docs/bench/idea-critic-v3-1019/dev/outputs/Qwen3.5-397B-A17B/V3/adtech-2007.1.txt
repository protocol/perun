**Claim:** Acquire a digital advertising agency and ad-serving platform for $6B cash to compete with the search leader following their similar move.

**Steelman:** Immediate ownership of serving technology and talent is the only viable path to close the gap before the competitor's integration creates an insurmountable network effect, justifying a premium price to secure market relevance.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Blocker:** The $6B cash price represents a massive premium in a "fast-changing" market. Paying peak valuation for technology prone to rapid obsolescence risks severe capital destruction if the market shifts again within 24 months.
2.  **Strategic Contradiction:** Buying an agency directly conflicts with existing agency customers who view the buyer as a partner, not a competitor. This threatens the core search revenue stream to chase a speculative display future.
3.  **Reactive Motive:** The primary driver is matching a competitor's move ("they just agreed"), not a verified internal capability gap. This is fear-based strategy, not edge-based.
4.  **No Named Edge:** The proposal cites size and cash as advantages. However, being first or biggest is not an edge in a technology pivot; the proposal admits no unique asset that guarantees winning against the incumbent ad-leader or the search rival.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve $6B cash; monitor if the competitor's integration fails (high risk in fast markets).
*   **Partner/License:** Access technology without the cultural clash of owning an agency or the balance sheet risk of a $6B outlay.
*   **Build:** Develop serving tech internally, avoiding customer conflicts, though slower.

**Pre-mortem:** Integration fails due to culture clashes with existing clients; technology becomes outdated before ROI is realized; core search partners defect to competitors due to the conflict of interest. The company is left with depleted cash reserves and a fractured ecosystem.

**Verdict Logic:** The proposal lacks a specific "edge" beyond reactive fear and financial muscle. It introduces a direct conflict with the current revenue base (agencies) and assumes a static target in a dynamic market. The financial risk ($6B cash) outweighs the unproven strategic benefit. A "go-if" condition cannot be met instantly; the conflicts and valuation require fundamental revision before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
