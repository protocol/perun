**Steelman:** A low-cost, angular crossover on an existing minivan platform targets youth with camping utility, aiming for 30k annual sales at ~$20k to break even.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts provided in the text. First, **demand is negated**: early customer clinics explicitly rated the styling poorly. Since the "distinctive angular body" is the primary differentiator for the target youth demographic, negative validation on this core attribute removes the route to the projected 30,000 sales. Second, the **platform mismatch** is fatal: an aging minivan chassis cannot support the "crossover" positioning if it lacks real off-road ability, especially when competing against emerging SUV options. Third, **execution risk** is confirmed: cost cuts have already compromised the design, suggesting the final product will perform worse than the already-rejected clinic models.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Avoids launching a product with proven styling rejection and platform limitations.
*   *Revise Platform:* Move to a capable chassis, but this violates the mid-$20k price point and breaks the break-even model.
*   *Restyle:* Requires re-opening clinics, delaying launch and increasing costs beyond the current compromised budget.

**Edge:** NONE. The proposal relies on an aging asset with known defects (poor styling, no off-road capability) and no stated advantage over competitors. "Strategic necessity" to fill a segment gap does not constitute an edge when the specific execution has failed validation.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Fact:** Customer clinics rated styling poorly. **Impact:** Destroys demand hypothesis for the target demographic.
2.  **Fact:** No real off-road ability on an aging platform. **Impact:** Contradicts the "crossover" value proposition.
3.  **Fact:** Cost cuts compromised design. **Impact:** Guarantees the final product is inferior to the tested prototype.

**Risks:** High probability of market failure; brand damage in the mid-market segment; inventory write-downs if sales miss the 30k break-even.

**Conclusion:** The proposal presents a product that has already failed validation (styling), lacks functional credibility (off-road), and suffers from degraded quality (cost cuts). There is no edge to justify launching a compromised vehicle into a competitive segment. The break-even target of 30,000 units is unreachable given the negative clinic data.

DECISION: NO-GO
