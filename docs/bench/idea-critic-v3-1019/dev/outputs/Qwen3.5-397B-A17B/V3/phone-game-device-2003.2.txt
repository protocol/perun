**Steelman:** Leveraging mobile dominance to disrupt handheld gaming via a hybrid device at $300, using removable media for flexibility.

**Attack & Options:**
The proposal fails the "better-way" and "fit" tests against current facts.
1.  **Fatal Usability Flaw:** The requirement to remove the battery to change games is a catastrophic user experience error. It renders the device unusable for its secondary function without killing its primary function (communication). No competitor forces this trade-off.
2.  **Price Disadvantage:** At $300, the device is significantly more expensive than the market leader's dedicated handheld. Without a committed library of exclusive games (publishers are "uncommitted"), there is no value proposition to justify the premium over the cheaper, library-rich incumbent.
3.  **Form Factor Compromise:** An "awkward" phone makes daily communication frustrating. Users will not tolerate a degraded primary tool for a speculative secondary feature.
4.  **Do Nothing/Cheaper Alternative:** The company should license games to existing popular handhelds or wait until battery/memory technology allows non-intrusive game swapping. Entering now with a flawed prototype guarantees brand damage in both sectors.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, reviewers mock the battery-swap mechanic, gamers reject the high price for a sparse library, and the awkward ergonomics lead to poor phone sales. Inventory piles up; the project is canceled within a year.

**Blockers:**
*   **UX Blocker:** Battery removal for game swapping is a functional dealbreaker.
*   **Economic Blocker:** Higher price point with no content advantage over the incumbent.
*   **Supply Blocker:** Lack of publisher commitment ensures a weak launch library.

**Edge:** NONE. Being a large phone maker provides no advantage if the resulting product is inferior in ergonomics, cost, and content.

**Verdict:** The proposal relies on unproven publisher interest while introducing confirmed dealbreaking flaws in usability and pricing. It cannot beat "do nothing" or a dedicated handheld on any metric.

DECISION: NO-GO
