The claim is that launching a 7-inch tablet on an untested OS without native email will succeed against a rival's established large-screen tablet. The steelman posits that portability (7-inch) creates a distinct "phablet" category where the device acts as a companion to the dominant business phone, leveraging the existing email ecosystem via pairing rather than rebuilding it natively.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. First, the core value proposition for a business-leading manufacturer is seamless communication. Shipping a device explicitly *without* built-in email, calendar, or contacts unless paired contradicts the brand's primary equity and the stated goal of competing in the business sector. A business user cannot rely on a secondary device being present or paired for core functionality; this is a functional deficit, not a feature constraint. Second, pricing a smaller, unproven device with fewer capabilities equal to a rival's larger, established tablet with a massive app store offers no economic or utility edge. The "edge" cited relies on the hope of future developer commitment and category creation, which are risks, not assets. The proposal admits the OS is untested and developers may not commit; these are not unknowns to be solved post-launch but immediate barriers to adoption.

The "better way" is clear: either delay launch to integrate native core apps (validating the business pitch) or price significantly lower to offset the lack of an app store and native features. As proposed, the product undercuts its own marketing pitch, matches the competitor's price despite inferior specs/ecosystem, and relies on unproven developer interest. The risk of brand dilution in the business segment is a concrete blocker. There is no named edge that overcomes the lack of native email at a parity price point.

Blockers: Native email absence contradicts business brand; Price parity with superior rival; Untested OS with no developer commitment.
Risks: Category failure; App store gap.
Edge: NONE.

DECISION: NO-GO
