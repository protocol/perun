**Steelman:** A search company can disrupt mature email by offering 1,000x more storage than incumbents, using superior search technology to make large mailboxes usable, and funding costs via contextual ads, despite privacy concerns and high initial infrastructure investment.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's core premise—that storage costs are prohibitive—is challenged by the proposer's unique position as a search company with likely economies of scale or efficiency advantages in data management. The "mature market" argument ignores that incumbents are constrained by their own low-storage business models, creating a clear opening for a differentiator.

**Risks:** 
1. Privacy backlash from scanning email content for ads could damage brand trust.
2. High per-user storage costs could bleed capital if ad revenue lags.
3. Invitation-only growth might be too slow to achieve network effects before competitors react.

**Edge:** The proposer's core competency is search and large-scale data handling, giving them a structural cost and performance advantage in delivering "fast search across all mail" and managing gigabyte-scale storage that incumbents lacking this DNA cannot easily match. The 1,000x storage leap is a defensible wedge that forces competitors into a costly arms race they are ill-equipped to fight.

**Kill Criteria:** If, after 12 months, the cost per user exceeds ad revenue per user by more than 20% without a clear path to profitability via scale, or if a major privacy scandal causes user growth to stall for two consecutive quarters, the project must pivot to a paid tier or shut down.

**Leading Metric:** Daily active users sending/receiving mail (not just signups) vs. infrastructure cost per active user, reviewed quarterly.

The risks are real but manageable; the edge is distinct and leverages existing strengths. The "no direct revenue" at launch is acceptable given the invitation-only constraint controls burn rate while validating the model. The market is mature only because no one has broken the storage constraint yet.

DECISION: GO
