The claim rests on the assumption that premium production value translates to mobile short-form demand, despite abundant free alternatives and rising subscription fatigue. The steelman argues that a vertical/horizontal adaptive technology and A-list talent create a unique wedge to overcome fatigue. However, the proposal fails the "edge" test: being first, having major studio backers, and high budgets are not competitive edges in a market defined by attention scarcity, not content scarcity. The "technology feature" (adaptive orientation) is a baseline expectation, not a differentiator.

The pre-mortem reveals a likely failure mode: high burn rate from A-list talent and production costs meets low retention because the core behavior (watching short video) is already satisfied by free, algorithmic feeds. The cost of delay is low; waiting allows the market to further clarify if premium short-form is viable without risking $1 billion upfront. A cheaper alternative exists: license existing premium shorts or produce a slates of lower-budget pilots to test willingness-to-pay before committing to a full platform launch and billion-dollar raise. The proposal ignores the "do nothing" option, which preserves capital while the subscription fatigue metric evolves.

Blockers: The proposal cites "no proven demand" as a risk but proceeds with a full-scale launch anyway, contradicting its own evidence. There is no kill criterion or pilot phase to validate the subscription model before the massive capital deployment.
Risks: Catastrophic capital loss due to misaligned product-market fit; inability to differentiate from free, user-generated competitors.
Edge: NONE. High budget and studio backing are inputs, not advantages in a consumer attention market.

Without a validated demand signal or a staged approach to mitigate the $1 billion exposure, the plan fails the blocker test. The lack of a proven edge combined with acknowledged market headwinds necessitates a rejection of the current scope.

DECISION: NO-GO
