**Steelman:** Acquiring the publisher instantly solves the boy-demographic gap and secures IP ownership for a self-sustaining film engine, leveraging the target's recent box-office proof.

**Attack & Options:**
The $4 billion price tag assumes the target's film success is repeatable, yet their slate is short, unproven, and their best assets are locked in rival licenses. "Do nothing" costs less; "License specific characters" targets the demographic without assuming film production risk or creative-control disputes. The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" test: buying the whole studio to access encumbered characters is inefficient capital allocation.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
While fitting the expansion vision, the cyclical nature of comics films creates a high-risk entry point. If the next self-financed film flops, the $4 billion investment becomes a massive write-down, and creative clashes with the target's leadership could stall integration. The cost of delay is low; waiting for long-term rival licenses to expire or for a larger proven slate reduces premium exposure.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The core claim relies on immediate access to top characters for boy-focused franchises. However, the proposal admits these characters are under long-term deals with rivals. This is a **blocker**: the primary strategic asset (famous characters) yields no immediate upside for the acquirer's specific goal (fixing weak boy reach) because they cannot be used now. The film division, the only immediate unencumbered asset, is too unproven to justify the full premium alone.

**Edge:**
The only edge is owning the IP long-term, but this is a financial bet, not a strategic inevitability. Being first to buy is not an edge if the assets are unusable. Fear of falling behind in boys' content does not validate overpaying for inaccessible tools.

**Verdict:**
Blockers exist (encumbered core IP, unproven film slate at premium price). A cheaper, staged alternative (targeted licensing or waiting for contract expiries) reaches the same upside with less risk. No immediate strategic edge justifies the $4 billion outlay given the constraints.

DECISION: NO-GO
