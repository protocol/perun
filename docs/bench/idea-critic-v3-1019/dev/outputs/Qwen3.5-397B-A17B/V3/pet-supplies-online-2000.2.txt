**Steelman**: Aggressive capital deployment via IPO will secure dominant market share and eliminate competition before rivals can react, accepting short-term losses for long-term monopoly pricing power.

**Skeptic**: The proposal relies on perpetual capital market openness and investor tolerance for infinite cash burn. Selling products below cost (negative gross margin) while expanding physical infrastructure (warehouses) and buying competitors creates a "triple burn" scenario. If capital markets close—as explicitly warned—the company faces immediate insolvency with no path to profitability. The unit economics are fundamentally broken; scaling a model that loses money on every transaction accelerates failure, not success.

**Better-Way**: The "do nothing" or "optimize" alternative is superior. Instead of an IPO under distressed unit economics, the company should pause expansion, raise prices to achieve positive contribution margins, and focus on retaining existing customers rather than buying unprofitable new ones via TV ads. Acquiring a competitor while bleeding cash adds integration risk without solving the core margin defect. A staged approach proving unit profitability in one region before national TV spend reduces the blast radius of failure.

**Kill-Criteria**: 
1. Stop if capital markets show signs of tightening (dot-com bubble context).
2. Halt expansion if gross margins remain negative after 90 days.
3. Abort acquisition if cash runway drops below 12 months post-IPO.

**Pre-mortem**: The IPO fails due to poor market timing; the company cannot raise the needed funds. Burdened by debt from warehouse construction and competitor acquisition, and unable to cover shipping costs on below-margin sales, liquidity evaporates within six months. The sports broadcast ad spend yields customers who immediately churn when prices normalize.

**Edge**: NONE. The proposal cites no unique asset, technology, or distribution advantage that allows this firm to succeed where others with similar strategies are failing. It relies entirely on "strategic necessity" and fear of falling behind, which are not edges. The plan attempts to buy scale before proving viability.

**Blockers**: 
1. Negative gross margins on core items make scaling mathematically fatal without infinite capital.
2. High risk of capital market closure contradicts the reliance on IPO proceeds for operations.
3. No evidence that TV advertising yields sustainable LTV given high acquisition costs.

DECISION: NO-GO
