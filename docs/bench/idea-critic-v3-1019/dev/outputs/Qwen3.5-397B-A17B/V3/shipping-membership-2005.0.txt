**Steelman:** An upfront $79 fee locks in loyalty and increases purchase frequency, offsetting shipping losses through higher lifetime value and volume efficiencies.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the **unit economics mismatch**. Heavy buyers (the target) will generate shipping costs exceeding $79 quickly. Without verified data showing that increased order frequency yields sufficient *incremental* gross margin to cover the shipping subsidy, the plan guarantees a loss on every active member. The proposal admits economics are "unproven," which is a fatal flaw for a capital-intensive commitment.

*Alternative 1 (Do Nothing):* Maintain per-order fees. Risk: slower growth, but zero guaranteed margin erosion.
*Alternative 2 (Threshold-Based Free Shipping):* Offer free shipping only on orders over a high monetary threshold. This protects margins by ensuring every "free" shipment includes enough profit to cover the cost, unlike the blanket membership.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, membership uptake is high among existing heavy spenders who simply stop paying per-order fees without increasing their total spend. The company burns cash on shipping subsidies it cannot recoup, forcing a chaotic cancellation of the program and massive customer backlash.

**Edge Analysis:** The proposal claims no specific asset (like exclusive logistics capacity or unique product leverage) that allows this retailer to absorb shipping costs better than competitors. "Strategic necessity" or fear of falling behind is not an edge. Without a proven mechanism to ensure incremental profit > shipping cost, the edge is **NONE**.

**Verdict Logic:**
1.  **Blocker:** The core financial premise (fee revenue > shipping cost + margin hit) is explicitly stated as "unproven." Proceeding without this validation violates basic fiscal hygiene.
2.  **Risk:** The "hard to reverse" nature creates a one-way door with high downside.
3.  **Requirement:** A pilot with strict kill criteria (e.g., cap members at 1,000, measure incremental margin weekly) is required before a full launch.

As proposed, this is a bet on hope rather than data. It fails the blocker test because the economics are unknown, and it lacks an edge to justify the risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
