The claim is that launching two social-networking phones on a new OS, via a single carrier with a mandatory expensive data plan and no app store, will successfully capture the teenage market in 2010.

Steelman: Leveraging an acquired messaging brand and sunk development costs to create a closed, carrier-subsidized ecosystem specifically for social teens could bypass the need for a broad app catalog initially.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts provided:
1.  **Market Mismatch:** The target demographic (teenagers) is highly price-sensitive. A mandatory data plan costing "as much as a full smartphone plan" creates an immediate financial barrier that rivals with flexible pricing do not have.
2.  **Competitive Disadvantage:** Rivals already possess "large app catalogs." Launching with "no application store" removes the primary utility drivers for smartphones in 2010. Social networking alone is insufficient when competitors offer social plus games, tools, and media.
3.  **Strategic Fragmentation:** Building a "new in-house operating system" instead of using the company's "own mobile platform" fractures engineering resources and ignores existing ecosystem advantages, increasing long-term maintenance costs without offering a unique user benefit that justifies the switch.

The "two years of sunk development" is a fallacy; it cannot justify launching a product that is objectively more expensive and less functional than current market alternatives. The "edge" is nonexistent; being first with a restricted, expensive device is not an advantage. The risks (high cost, no apps, new OS) are not merely potential; they are inherent features of the proposed plan that directly contradict the needs of the target audience and the realities of the competitive landscape.

There is no cheaper, safer alternative presented here that reaches the same upside because the core premise (expensive, app-less phone for teens) is flawed. The plan does not beat "do nothing" or simply porting the messaging features to the company's existing mobile platform.

Blockers: High cost for teens vs. rivals; No app store vs. rival catalogs; New OS vs. existing platform.
Edge: NONE.
Risks: Total market rejection due to price and feature gap.

DECISION: NO-GO
