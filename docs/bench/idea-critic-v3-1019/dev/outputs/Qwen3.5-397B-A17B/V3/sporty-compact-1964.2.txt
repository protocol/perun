**Claim:** Launch a sporty, customizable coupe/convertible on existing compact mechanics with aggressive advertising to capture young postwar buyers.

**Steelman:** Reusing proven mechanicals minimizes development risk and cost, enabling a low base price. The "long hood, short deck" styling creates a premium sports car illusion, while an extensive options list allows margin expansion and personalization. Massive advertising educates the market on this new category before competitors react.

**Attack & Analysis:**
*   **Assumptions:** Young buyers prioritize style over practicality; "cheap" parts can be styled to look premium; customization drives attachment more than standard features.
*   **Better Ways:** A low-volume niche import conversion avoids heavy ad spend but lacks scale. Waiting for data avoids risk but cedes the segment. The proposed path beats "do nothing" because the demographic wave is imminent and unaddressed.
*   **Fit:** Directly targets the exploding postwar boom demographic with a product distinct from the parent brand's "dull" reputation.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the styling fails to mask the economy underpinnings, making the car look "cheap" rather than "affordable sporty," or if ad spend burns cash without converting unproven demand.
*   **Blockers:** None. The risks (unproven demand, image dilution, high upfront cost) are inherent to creating a new category, not fatal flaws. The mechanical reuse is a strategic advantage (speed/cost), not a blocker, provided styling succeeds.
*   **Edge:** First-mover advantage in a mass-market "affordable sporty" segment using existing infrastructure to undercut potential rivals on price and time-to-market.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal addresses a clear demographic shift with a differentiated product. The risks are manageable via the options list (margin buffer) and styling focus (image buffer). The "unproven demand" is precisely why a large launch campaign is necessary to create the category. No factual blocker exists; the plan leverages existing assets to seize a timely opportunity.

**Kill Criteria:** If first-year sales do not cover the launch ad spend by 18 months, halt further variant development. Leading metric: Options attachment rate per unit (validates customization thesis).

DECISION: GO
