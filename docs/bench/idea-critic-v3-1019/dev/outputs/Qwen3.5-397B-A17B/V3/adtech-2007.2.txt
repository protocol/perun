**Steelman:** Acquiring the agency and platform instantly grants the scale and technology required to challenge the search leader's nascent display dominance, preventing permanent market exclusion.

**Attack:** The $6 billion cash price represents a massive premium in a fast-changing market, creating severe integration risk with existing agency customers who may view the buyer as a competitor. The "fast-changing" nature of display technology suggests the acquired assets could become obsolete quickly, turning a large premium into a total loss. Unlike search, display relies on creative and publisher relationships that do not guarantee transferable loyalty. The proposal assumes ownership is the only path to compete, ignoring potential partnerships or organic build-outs that avoid cultural clash and customer conflict.

**Better Way:** Instead of a full buyout, pursue a strategic minority stake or technology licensing deal to test the waters without alienating current agency clients. This reduces capital exposure and allows time to resolve channel conflicts before committing $6 billion.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If the integration fails due to client churn or tech shifts, the company loses liquidity needed for core search innovation. The cost of delay is low; the market is not winner-take-all yet. Waiting six months could reveal clearer winners or lower valuations.

**Edge:** NONE. Being second to market with a costly, conflicted acquisition is not a defensible advantage. The rival's move does not force a panicked, overpriced reaction.

**Blockers:** 
1. The proposal admits "conflicts with existing agency customers" but offers no mitigation; this directly threatens current revenue streams.
2. Paying a "large premium" for "fast-changing" technology violates basic capital allocation principles without a clear moat.

**Risks:** Total asset obsolescence, mass client defection, cultural integration failure.

**Kill Criteria:** If the acquired platform's revenue drops >10% in Year 1 due to client conflicts, divest immediately.

**Decision:** The plan relies on panic rather than strategy, risking core stability for uncertain gains in a volatile sector.

DECISION: NO-GO
