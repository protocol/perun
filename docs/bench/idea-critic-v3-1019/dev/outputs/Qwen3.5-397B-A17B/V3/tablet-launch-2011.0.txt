**Steelman:** Leverage the computer maker's brand and the acquired OS to enter the tablet market immediately, betting that heavy marketing and developer incentives can overcome a small app catalog and unproven status before the window closes.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "edge" test. In 2011, the market leader dominates with a massive ecosystem; shoppers prioritize app availability over hardware branding. A "small app catalog" is a fatal defect, not a temporary hurdle, because network effects favor the incumbent exponentially. Heavy advertising cannot fix a product deficiency that users experience immediately upon purchase. The "leadership transition" adds execution risk to an already fragile go-to-market strategy.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "delay" option is superior. Wait until the app catalog reaches a critical mass or until the OS gains traction in smartphones (if applicable) to create pull-demand. Launching now burns capital on advertising for a product likely to receive poor reviews due to app scarcity, damaging the computer maker's brand equity. A cheaper alternative is to license the OS to multiple hardware partners to build market share first, rather than bearing the full cost of a standalone hardware launch with a weak value proposition.

**Pre-mortem:** The tablet launches, reviewers praise the hardware but pan the lack of apps. Sales flatline despite ad spend. Developers ignore the outreach because the user base is tiny. The project is cancelled in 18 months, writing off the acquisition's potential.

**Blockers:** 
1. No named edge: Brand and ad spend do not overcome the ecosystem gap (a stated fact).
2. The core value proposition (apps) is explicitly admitted as weak ("small fraction").
3. Leadership transition creates uncertainty during a critical launch phase.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first with this specific configuration offers no advantage; being late with a better ecosystem would be stronger.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not launch until the app catalog matches at least 40% of the leader's key categories, or until the OS proves market fit in another segment.

DECISION: NO-GO
