**Steelman:** A premium, high-capacity device restricted to a niche ecosystem can succeed if it offers a vastly superior user experience that justifies the price, creating a halo effect for the parent brand despite limited initial compatibility.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" and "addressable base" tests. Competitors are cheaper; the music industry lacks legal downloads, limiting content; and the hardware constraint (5GB HDD in <1 year) is unproven for this maker. The "edge" relies entirely on a non-existent software ecosystem and a price premium consumers have already rejected in broader tests. The addressable market is too small to sustain the R&D and sourcing risks within the timeline.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches late due to sourcing failures, sells few units because non-owners cannot use it, and is undercut by flash-based players that are smaller and cheaper. The brand dilutes its core computer focus without gaining new users.

**Blockers:** 
1. **No Edge:** The proposal cites no unique asset (e.g., existing supply chain, licensed content) that rivals lack. "Strategic necessity" and fear of falling behind are not edges.
2. **Market Contradiction:** Cheaper players have already failed to sell well; raising the price while shrinking compatibility contradicts observed demand.
3. **Execution Risk:** A computer maker with no consumer electronics experience cannot reliably source components for a pocket device in under a year.

**Risks:** High price, tiny compatible base, no legal content ecosystem, aggressive timeline.

**Edge:** NONE. The proposal leans on "being first" in a category where others have already struggled, and "strategic necessity" rather than a demonstrated advantage.

**Decision Method:** The plan does not beat "do nothing" or a cheaper, staged alternative (e.g., software-only partnership). It commits real money without a capped, time-boxed pilot. The lack of a verified edge and the presence of concrete blockers (market rejection of higher prices, no content, inexperience) mandate a no-go.

DECISION: NO-GO
