**Steelman:** Vertical integration secures the primary hardware partner, preventing defection to the rival platform and allowing tighter software-hardware optimization to improve user experience, potentially slowing market share erosion.

**Attack:** The core failure mode is an ecosystem deficit, not hardware alignment. Developers prioritize the market leader due to existing user volume; owning a declining handset manufacturer does not instantly create app demand. The proposal attempts to solve a software/network-effect problem with a hardware acquisition. With smartphone sales already falling, the company is purchasing a depreciating asset and a large, difficult-to-integrate workforce. Competitors may react by accelerating their own vertical moves or forming stronger alliances against this hybrid model. The €5.4 billion cost diverts capital from the actual bottleneck: incentivizing developers and improving the OS directly. History suggests that without a dominant app ecosystem, hardware ownership alone cannot reverse a platform's decline. The risk of integration distraction outweighs the benefit of securing a shrinking distribution channel.

**Better Way:** Invest the capital directly into developer funds, API improvements, and marketing to boost the OS on *all* existing partner devices, rather than betting on a single failing manufacturer.

**Kill Criteria:** If the acquired division's sales continue to fall post-integration without a corresponding spike in OS adoption across other partners, the thesis fails.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first to integrate with a declining partner is not a competitive advantage when the platform itself lacks critical mass. The "strategic necessity" of stopping defection is fear-based, not value-creating.

**Verdict:** The plan addresses the symptom (potential defection) rather than the disease (lack of apps and users). It locks the company into a hardware business it may not excel at while draining resources needed to fix the software ecosystem. The falling sales trend indicates the asset is a liability.

DECISION: NO-GO
