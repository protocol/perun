**Steelman:** Win by prioritizing portability, battery life, and software depth over graphical fidelity, leveraging a puzzle game bundle to drive mass adoption at an accessible price point.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary objection is the monochrome screen against a color rival. However, color hardware drains batteries rapidly and increases unit cost. The proposal's "modest" specs are a strategic constraint enabling 10+ hour battery life, a critical differentiator for a truly portable device. The $89.95 price point undercuts competitors significantly, reducing purchase friction.

The risk of "thin margins" is mitigated by the "razor-and-blades" model: the hardware serves as a loss-leader or low-margin entry to sell high-margin software cartridges. The company's history of single-game devices is a legacy constraint, not a technical one; bundling a sticky puzzle game creates immediate value and demonstrates a library strategy.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it is because the market perceives "monochrome" as "obsolete" rather than "practical," or if third-party developers ignore the platform. The $20M marketing spend must aggressively reframe the lack of color as a feature (battery life) rather than a defect.

**Kill Criteria & Edge:**
*   **Blocker:** None. The technical trade-offs are intentional and align with a specific user need (portability) unmet by the color rival.
*   **Edge:** Superior battery life, lower price point, and a killer app bundle that competitors cannot match at that price.
*   **Metric:** Sell-through rate of the bundled puzzle game vs. hardware units in Q1.
*   **Decision:** The plan beats "do nothing" and the "color rival" on the specific axis of true portability and mass-market accessibility. The risks are executional, not fundamental flaws in the thesis.

DECISION: GO
