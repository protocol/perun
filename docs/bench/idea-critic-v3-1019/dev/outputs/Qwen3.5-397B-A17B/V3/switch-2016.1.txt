**Steelman:** A hybrid device leverages existing IP to unify fragmented audiences, sacrificing raw power for unique utility (portability + home) to escape direct specification wars with rivals.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "Edge" test. Pricing a modest-power machine equally to far more powerful rivals removes the only logical purchase driver for non-loyalists: value. In late 2016, the market is bifurcated between high-fidelity home experiences and ubiquitous mobile gaming. A "modest" chip cannot compete on fidelity, and "detachable controllers" do not justify a premium price point against established 4K-capable competitors when the software lineup is explicitly "thin."

The "hybrid" concept introduces significant execution risk (supply constraints, confusing positioning) without a clear unique selling proposition that survives scrutiny. If the hardware is weaker, the price must be lower to penetrate the market, or the software must be system-selling day one. Neither condition is met. The "edge" cited relies on brand hope rather than a tangible asset (cost advantage, exclusive must-have software available at launch, or superior performance). Fear of falling behind or "strategic necessity" is not an edge.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to confusion; core gamers reject the weak specs at a premium price, while casual users stick to phones. Inventory piles up as third parties ignore the weak install base. Financial losses compound because the unit economics rely on volume that the price/spec mismatch prevents.

**Blockers:**
1. **Price/Performance Mismatch:** Charging parity prices for inferior hardware contradicts basic market mechanics unless software dominance is proven (which is denied by the "thin line-up" admission).
2. **No Named Edge:** The proposal offers no concrete reason why this specific configuration wins against superior alternatives or cheaper mobile options.

**Risks:** Supply chain failure, brand erosion from another confusing launch, developer abandonment.

**Verdict:** The plan commits real money without a viable route to the upside. It bets on a "hope" strategy rather than a demonstrated advantage. Without a price cut or a stronger launch window of software, the math does not work.

DECISION: NO-GO
