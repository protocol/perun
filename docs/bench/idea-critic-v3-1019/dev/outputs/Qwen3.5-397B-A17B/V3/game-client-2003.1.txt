**Steelman:** A mandatory client solves critical update and cheating failures while creating a future distribution monopoly, outweighing short-term friction.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal bets on a shift from physical to digital distribution before infrastructure is ready. In 2003, universal broadband is a false assumption; many core gamers rely on dial-up, making large mandatory downloads a hard blocker for adoption. Retail partners, currently the primary revenue channel, will likely retaliate by delisting boxed games if forced to adopt a client-dependent model, causing immediate revenue collapse.

While anti-cheat and updates are genuine pains, the "mandatory" nature for *new* games alienates the existing user base before the network effect of the store can materialize. Competitors copying the idea is irrelevant if the first mover fails due to infrastructure constraints and channel conflict. The risk of early bugs in a monolithic client could permanently brand the developer as unreliable.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Continue losing to cheaters and struggling with patches, but retain retail relationships and dial-up users.
2.  **Optional Client:** Offer the client for updates/anti-cheat but allow standalone play/patches via traditional means. This mitigates churn but slows adoption.
3.  **Phased Rollout:** Launch the client only for multiplayer components, keeping single-player accessible offline.

**Verdict Logic:**
The proposal fails the "fit" test against current reality: home broadband is not universal, and the plan alienates the installed base required to fund the transition. The "edge" is purely speculative (future store dominance) while the blockers (infrastructure limits, retail channel destruction) are immediate and factual based on the 2003 context provided. The plan attempts to force a 2010 business model onto a 2003 infrastructure. A "go-if" condition requiring universal broadband cannot be met today. The risk of catastrophic channel conflict and user abandonment exceeds the potential upside of solving updates prematurely.

DECISION: NO-GO
