**Steelman:** Offering 1,000x more storage than competitors, funded by contextual ads, disrupts a mature market by solving the user pain point of deletion, turning email into a searchable archive.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Economic Blocker:** In 2004, storage costs are non-trivial. Giving 1GB free where others give MBs risks catastrophic burn if adoption scales before ad revenue matures. However, hardware costs are falling exponentially (Moore's Law); the "blocker" is a timing bet, not a physical impossibility.
2.  **Privacy Objection:** Scanning private mail for ads is a profound trust violation. Competitors don't do this. If users perceive this as surveillance, adoption fails regardless of storage size. This is a *risk*, not a proven blocker, contingent on transparent framing ("automated matching" vs. "human reading").
3.  **Market Entry:** Entrenched incumbents (Yahoo, Hotmail) are complacent, offering low storage. They are vulnerable to a "10x better" metric. The "mature market" argument ignores that maturity often signals stagnation ripe for disruption.
4.  **Revenue Model:** No direct revenue at launch is standard for platform plays. The risk is the gap between user acquisition cost and ad yield. Text ads have high margins; if engagement is high, the unit economics likely work at scale.
5.  **Strategy:** Invitation-only creates scarcity and manages server load, allowing cost validation before full exposure.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if privacy backlash becomes a PR firestorm before the utility value creates user lock-in, or if storage costs remain higher than projected for longer than expected.

**Edge:** The proposer is a *search* company. They possess the unique infrastructure to index massive data quickly and the existing ad-tech to monetize it. Incumbents are portal companies, not search-native; they cannot replicate the "fast search across all mail" experience efficiently. This is an asymmetric advantage.

**Verdict:** The storage cost is a calculated bet on hardware trends. The privacy risk is real but manageable through product design and messaging. The market is stagnant. The proposer has the specific technical edge (search + ads) to execute this where others cannot. The potential to redefine email from "communication" to "knowledge management" justifies the risk.

DECISION: GO
