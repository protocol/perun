Steelman: Launching in May 1995 secures a four-month head start to establish market presence before the competitor arrives, capturing early adopters despite limited inventory.

**Skeptic:** The proposal admits the machine is hard to program for 3D and developers were not planned for this date. Launching with "few games" for a 32-bit console in 1995 is fatal; hardware without software is a demo, not a product. The $399 price point ($100 above the rival) combined with a weak library gives consumers a clear reason to wait for the cheaper, better-supported competitor. The "early" advantage is negated if the initial user experience is poor, creating negative word-of-mouth that persists until the competitor launches.

**Better-way:** The alternative is to maintain the September date. This allows time to lower the price to match the rival, secure a robust launch lineup by honoring developer schedules, and avoid alienating major retailers excluded from the May shipment. A synchronized launch with strong software and competitive pricing beats a hollow early arrival. "Do nothing" (stick to the plan) avoids the specific risk of brand damage from a botched early release.

**Kill-criteria:** If the launch library contains fewer than five compelling titles, or if major retail partners threaten delisting due to exclusion, the early launch fails its own goal of market establishment. The pre-mortem shows a scenario where the console is remembered as the "expensive, empty machine" that launched too soon.

**Decision:** The plan lacks an edge. Being first with an undercooked product and higher price is not an advantage; it is a liability. The stated facts (hard to program, few games, high price, angry retailers) constitute a blocker: the product cannot compete on value or experience at the proposed time. The edge is NONE; the risks are concrete and immediate.

DECISION: NO-GO
