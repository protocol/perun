The claim is that switching to the dominant processor architecture with a translation layer will solve thermal inefficiency and roadmap lag within two years.

**Steelman:** Migrating now secures the technical future for thin laptops, using a translation bridge to protect the existing user base during the transition, leveraging the supplier's superior volume and efficiency.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the "announcement freeze": revealing the switch before new units ship could crater current revenue, funding the very transition required. The proposal acknowledges this risk but offers no mitigation (e.g., silent development until launch readiness).
*Alternative 1 (Do Nothing):* Continue with current chips. Result: Product stagnation and eventual irrelevance in the thin laptop market.
*Alternative 2 (Dual Source):* Introduce the new architecture only in premium thin models while maintaining the old line for legacy users. This reduces shock but splits engineering focus.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
If executed poorly, the brand loses its "distinctiveness," becoming just another PC clone, while the translation layer frustrates power users with slow performance. The "painful earlier transition" suggests organizational trauma that could stall execution. However, the cost of delay is existential; without efficient chips, the core product (thin laptops) cannot compete physically.

**Evidence & Edge:**
The facts state the current supplier *has not* delivered and *lags* the competitor. This is a verified capability gap. The "Edge" is the translation layer, which theoretically solves the software compatibility hurdle that usually blocks architecture swaps. While risks like developer rebuilds and sales freezes are real, they are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws in the strategic direction. The alternative (staying on a dead-end roadmap) guarantees failure. The proposal's two-year timeline allows for a managed transition if the announcement freeze is handled via strict secrecy until product availability.

**Decision:**
No factual blocker proves the plan *cannot* work; the risks are manageable execution hurdles compared to the certainty of failure by staying put. The edge (access to superior silicon + compatibility layer) outweighs the transition pain.
