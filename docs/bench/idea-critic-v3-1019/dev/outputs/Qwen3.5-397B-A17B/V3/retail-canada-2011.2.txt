**Steelman:** Rapidly acquiring 130–150 sites leverages existing lease opportunities to establish immediate scale, deterring the entrenched competitor before they can react, despite operational complexity.

**Attack:** The proposal conflates "speed" with "advantage." Entering a foreign market with unproven cross-border logistics, a new inventory system, and bilingual regulatory hurdles simultaneously creates a single point of catastrophic failure. The "entrenched competitor" likely possesses superior local supply chains and brand loyalty that cannot be overturned merely by opening doors. If the new inventory system glitches or customs delays occur across 130 locations simultaneously, the brand's "loyal domestic following" offers no buffer against empty shelves and negative local press. The cost of reversing 150 leased sites is massive compared to a pilot.

**Better Way:** A staged entry (5–10 stores) validates the supply chain, inventory system, and price perception with capped exposure. This allows for iterative fixes before scaling. The current plan bets the entire international thesis on a "big bang" launch without a proven operational backbone.

**Fit & Edge:** The retailer's edge is "low price," but the proposal admits local prices are already higher on some goods and the competitor is dominant. There is no stated asset (e.g., exclusive supplier contracts, significantly lower cost basis) that guarantees profitability against an incumbent in a new regulatory environment. Being first to open 150 stores is not an edge if the stores are operationally broken.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Unproven Systems:** Launching 130+ stores on a new, untested inventory system and supply chain creates a high probability of systemic stockouts or compliance failures.
2. **No Pilot Data:** Zero evidence exists that the price perception or logistics model works in this specific country.
3. **Irreversibility:** Leasing 150 sites creates a massive fixed-cost burden before revenue is proven.

**Edge:** NONE. Speed without operational validation is a liability, not an advantage. The plan lacks a kill criterion or a staged approach to mitigate the high risk of simultaneous failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
