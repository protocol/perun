**Steelman:** Leveraging existing compact-car assets to create a new "garage-fit" family vehicle category offers a high-upside, capital-efficient path to recovery, distinct from commercial vans.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker is the claim that "no such category exists." Historically, the absence of a category often signals unmet demand rather than impossibility, provided the product solves a specific friction (garage storage vs. wagon capacity). The risk of buyers perceiving it as a commercial van is mitigated by the "drives like a car" positioning and station wagon heritage.

The financial constraint ("little cash") is directly addressed by reusing the compact-car platform and converting an existing factory. This avoids the prohibitive costs of a clean-sheet design and new tooling, making the "cheaper alternative" (doing nothing or waiting) riskier given the recent near-collapse. The government loan guarantee provides the necessary runway to execute this specific, low-capital pivot.

Regarding competition: while rivals could copy the concept, first-mover advantage in defining a new segment (the minivan) creates brand ownership before clones arrive. The platform limitation on space is a feature, not a bug, ensuring the "home garage" fit that larger commercial vans cannot achieve.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure would occur only if the marketing fails to distinguish the vehicle from work vans or if the ride quality feels too utilitarian. However, the proposal explicitly targets the "car-like" experience, directly countering this.

**Edge:** The company possesses the unique combination of a recent government lifeline, an idle factory suitable for conversion, and a compact platform ready for adaptation. Competitors lack this specific alignment of distressed assets and urgent necessity to pivot quickly. The "no category" risk is the very opportunity; being the first to define "family hauler that fits in a garage" creates the market.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks are manageable execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The financial strategy aligns with available resources.
**Risks:** Marketing misclassification; slow competitor response.
**Edge:** Unique asset alignment (loan + factory + platform) allowing rapid, low-cost category creation.

DECISION: GO
