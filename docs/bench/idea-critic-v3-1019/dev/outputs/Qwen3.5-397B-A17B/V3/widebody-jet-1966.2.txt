Steelman: Building a massively larger, convertible wide-body jet now secures market dominance against supersonic threats and meets urgent airline demand for lower seat costs.

**Skeptic Analysis**: The financial exposure is existential; the development and factory costs dwarf all prior company outlays. Relying on an unproven high-bypass engine introduces critical technical failure modes. The market assumption that subsonic jets will persist is directly threatened by the industry expectation that supersonic airliners will replace them. Currently, only one customer has committed, leaving the company exposed if that single order collapses.

**Better-Way**: The "do nothing" option avoids catastrophic bankruptcy risk if the supersonic transition occurs as predicted. A smaller, incremental upgrade to existing narrow-body fleets could achieve cost reductions without the massive capital expenditure of a new factory and clean-sheet design. Waiting for the engine technology to mature reduces technical risk.

**Kill-Criteria & Pre-mortem**: If supersonic travel becomes the standard within the decade, this subsonic behemoth becomes obsolete inventory. If the sole airline customer cancels, the specialized factory becomes a stranded asset. The cost of delay is losing the current customer's loyalty, but the cost of failure is company insolvency.

**Decision Logic**:
*   **Blockers**: None definitively proven yet; the supersonic threat is an expectation, not a realized fact. The engine is unproven but potentially viable.
*   **Edge**: The specific design choice (raised cockpit for freighter conversion) provides a unique second-life value proposition that competitors lack, hedging against passenger market shifts. The "first mover" advantage in high-capacity subsonic travel creates a monopoly on low-cost seats if supersonic adoption lags.
*   **Risk vs. Reward**: While the risks are extreme, the "do nothing" approach cedes the growing high-density market to potential future competitors and ignores the immediate, validated demand from the largest customer. The convertible design specifically mitigates the risk of passenger obsolescence.

The proposal addresses the identified risks through unique design features (freighter conversion) and targets a verified immediate need. The supersonic threat remains speculative enough to warrant the bet, given the massive upside of dominating the subsonic market for the interim decades.

DECISION: GO
