**Steelman:** A complete rewrite with a publisher-focused algorithm and forced migration is the only way to reverse traffic decline and modernize the platform before a rival captures the market.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the blocker test on three fronts. First, it explicitly alienates the "loyal community" by devaluing their preferred voting mechanics in favor of publishers; if the core user base leaves, the network effect collapses, rendering new publisher content worthless. Second, a "big bang" migration of a rewrite offers zero rollback capability; if the new algorithm fails to engage users, the site dies instantly with no path to recovery. Third, the engineering risk of a full rewrite delays any market response, allowing the growing rival to solidify their lead while resources are tied up in construction rather than iteration.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" baseline is inferior, but a staged approach beats the proposal. A/B testing the new surfacing logic on a small traffic segment allows validation without endangering the core community. Incremental refactoring reduces engineering risk compared to a scratch rewrite.

**Fit & Edge:** The proposal lacks a defined edge. "Favoring publishers" is a strategic guess, not a proven asset, and being first is irrelevant if the execution destroys the existing user base. The plan relies on the hope that new features will fix retention, which is a post-launch fix, not a pre-launch advantage.

**Verdict:** This is a one-way door with a high probability of catastrophic failure. The plan ignores the primary asset (the community) to chase an unproven secondary market (publishers) using a deployment strategy (big bang) that eliminates safety nets. The risk of total community churn is a concrete blocker that the proposal does not address.

**Kill Criteria:** Any launch must preserve a rollback path and validate algorithm changes on a subset of users before full migration.

**Decision:** No-go. The proposal must be revised to include incremental rollout, A/B testing of the new mechanics, and a preservation strategy for the core community before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
