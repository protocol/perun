Steelman: A low-cost ($180) interim device leveraging existing IP to bridge the hardware gap between handheld and home generations using novel stereoscopic 3D.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Physiological Failure:** The proposal admits "eye strain or headaches" for users. A core entertainment device causing physical pain is a fundamental product failure, not a manageable risk.
2.  **Value Proposition Collapse:** "Red-on-black visuals only" combined with "neither portable nor comfortable" removes the advantages of both handhelds (mobility) and home consoles (immersion/comfort). It is the worst of both categories.
3.  **Ecosystem Void:** "Few games at launch" guarantees poor software attachment rates, ensuring financial loss regardless of hardware margins.
4.  **Cannibalization/Obsolescence:** With the next home console due in two years, this device faces immediate irrelevance, confusing the market and diluting brand equity right before a major flagship launch.

**Risks:**
High probability of brand damage associated with discomfort; inventory write-downs due to rapid obsolescence; developer abandonment due to limited install base and technical constraints.

**Edge:**
NONE. Being "first" to market with a painful, visually limited experience is not an advantage; it is a liability. Cost savings ($180) do not offset the lack of utility. The "strategic necessity" to fill a timeline gap does not justify shipping a defective user experience.

**Pre-mortem:**
Launched in 1995, reviews cite nausea and poor graphics within weeks. Sales stall immediately as consumers wait for the true next-gen home console. The project is discontinued within a year, becoming a historical footnote of failure rather than a successful bridge. The company loses money on every unit sold when accounting for R&D and marketing, and distracts internal teams from the upcoming critical home console launch.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal contains verified internal contradictions: it claims to be a "new product" but offers reduced functionality compared to existing generations. The admitted physical side effects constitute a blocker that cannot be mitigated by price. No kill criteria can save a product that fundamentally fails the primary test of user comfort and visual fidelity.

DECISION: NO-GO
