The claim is that launching the world's cheapest car at $2,500 from a new factory will successfully transition scooter families to cars. The steelman: this price point unlocks a massive, untapped market segment by undercutting all four-wheel alternatives, relying on volume to offset razor-thin margins.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The "cheapest" positioning is a double-edged sword; in automotive markets, low price often signals unsafe or inferior quality, deterring families whose primary concern is safety. If buyers perceive the car as a "death trap," the volume model collapses. Furthermore, the factory site protests represent a critical execution risk. A delayed or halted factory destroys the high-volume economics required to sustain the $2,500 price point. Without the dedicated factory running at capacity, the unit cost rises, breaking the core value proposition.

Alternatives exist: launching a slightly higher-priced model using existing contract manufacturing avoids the land protest risk and allows for better safety features to combat the "cheap" stigma. Used cars and scooters remain cheaper; if the new car's quality is doubted, these established options win. The proposal assumes demand is purely price-elastic, ignoring that trust and perceived safety are prerequisites for family vehicles.

The strongest attack is the brand perception trap: marketing it explicitly as the "cheapest" may alienate the very aspirational buyers who want to leave scooters behind. They may prefer a used car with status over a new car with stigma. The land protests threaten the only way the math works (high volume). Since the proposal offers no mitigation for the reputation risk or the supply chain halt, and the "cheapest" label actively undermines the safety requirement for families, the plan as stated is too fragile.

Blockers: Land protests threaten factory completion; "cheapest" branding may repel safety-conscious families.
Risks: Safety perception, used car competition, margin erosion if volume misses.
Edge: None clearly defined beyond price, which is vulnerable to perception.

DECISION: NO-GO
