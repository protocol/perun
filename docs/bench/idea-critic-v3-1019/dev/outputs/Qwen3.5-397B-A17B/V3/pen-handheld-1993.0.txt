The claim is that a $700 pen-based handheld with unreliable handwriting recognition can successfully launch a new category via CEO publicity. The steelman: First-mover advantage in an unproven market justifies the premium price and technical risk, betting that executive visibility will overcome early product flaws and sparse software.

**Skeptic:** The core value proposition (handwriting input) is explicitly stated as unreliable in testing. Releasing a primary feature that does not work as advertised creates immediate reputational damage that publicity amplifies rather than fixes. The high price ($700 in 1993 is prohibitive) combined with a large form factor (not pocketable) removes the two key drivers for mobile adoption: utility and portability.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" option avoids sinking capital into an unproven category with known technical blockers. A cheaper, staged alternative would be to license the technology to a third party or launch a keyboard-based companion device first to build the software ecosystem without the handwriting risk. Waiting for the technology to mature reduces the chance of a catastrophic public failure.

**Kill-Criteria:** The proposal fails the basic viability test: the primary mechanism (recognition) is broken, and the price point excludes the mass market required to validate an "unproven category." There is no stated edge other than "first," which is not an edge when the product is fundamentally flawed. The risks (unreliable tech, high cost, poor form factor, no software) are not hypothetical; they are confirmed facts in the prompt. No kill criteria can save a product that fails its primary function out of the box.

**Verdict:** This is a classic case of solution-looking-for-a-problem with insurmountable execution risks. The lack of a working core feature and the absence of a software ecosystem make success highly improbable regardless of marketing spend. The "edge" is non-existent; being first with a broken product cedes the market to a later, functional entrant.

DECISION: NO-GO
