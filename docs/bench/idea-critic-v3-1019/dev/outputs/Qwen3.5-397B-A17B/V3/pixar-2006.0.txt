**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio secures a proven hit-making engine, prevents a rival from gaining it, and revitalizes the buyer's struggling hand-drawn unit by installing superior creative leadership.

**Attack:** The $7.4 billion price tag relies entirely on the continuation of a "string of hits" from a team producing only one film annually. This creates extreme concentration risk: a single box-office failure could crater the stock value used to pay for the deal. Furthermore, integrating a distinct, successful culture into a conglomerate with recent underperformers often leads to talent exodus rather than synergy. If the creative leaders leave post-acquisition, the buyer owns an expensive empty shell. The "all-stock" structure dilutes existing shareholders significantly if the studio's performance merely normalizes rather than accelerates.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Risk the studio partnering with a rival, but avoid $7.4B debt/dilution and integration failure.
2. *Distribution-Only Renewal:* Negotiate a high-revenue share deal without ownership, capping downside while maintaining access to content.

**Fit & Edge:** The buyer lacks an internal animation edge, evidenced by recent underperformers. The studio possesses the specific asset (creative talent/brand) the buyer needs. However, the proposal lacks a "go-if" condition regarding the retention of key creative staff or a mechanism to validate the valuation beyond past performance. The "edge" is purely the studio's existing output, which is already priced in at a premium.

**Blockers:** None explicitly fatal based *only* on provided text, as the strategic necessity to stop a rival and fix a broken unit is clear. However, the lack of defined kill criteria for talent retention is a massive unmitigated risk.

**Risks:** Culture clash, talent departure, overpayment for a single-asset pipeline, shareholder dilution.

**Decision Logic:** While the price is high and risks are severe, the alternative (a rival gaining the studio while the buyer's own unit fails) represents an existential strategic threat. The acquisition is the only path to immediately secure the necessary creative capital. The risks must be managed post-decision, but the strategic imperative forces the move.

DECISION: GO
