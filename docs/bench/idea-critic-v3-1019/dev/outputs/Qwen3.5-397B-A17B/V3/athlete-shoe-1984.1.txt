**Steelman:** Secure a generational talent early to dominate basketball culture, using controversy and exclusivity to drive demand that transcends on-court performance.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly accepts the risks of injury, fines, and budget concentration as the cost of entry. No factual contradiction exists in the premise; the "unproven" status is the very asset being purchased at a discount relative to future potential.

**Risks:** Career-ending injury, league bans, consumer rejection of non-conformist aesthetics, budget cannibalization. These are valid but are inherent to the strategy of high-reward disruption. They do not block the decision; they define the kill criteria.

**Edge:** First-mover advantage on a specific, high-charisma asset before market pricing reflects his value. The willingness to violate uniform norms creates a "forbidden fruit" narrative that competitors playing by the rules cannot replicate. This is not just an endorsement; it is a cultural wedge.

**Alternatives:** Signing established veterans costs more for less upside. "Do nothing" cedes the category to rivals. Incremental marketing fails to break the noise.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal passes the "beat do nothing" test. The risks are known and priced into the deal structure (five-year term). The potential upside (category leadership, cultural iconography) dwarfs the cost of the marketing budget share. The controversy regarding uniform rules is a feature, not a bug, generating free publicity.

**Kill Criteria:** If the player suffers a career-ending injury in Year 1, terminate active marketing spend immediately. If league fines exceed 20% of the deal value in Year 1, renegotiate or pivot messaging. If sales of the signature line do not exceed the cost of the deal plus fines by end of Year 2, discontinue the line.

**Leading Metric:** Pre-order volume for the first colorway vs. production cost; media sentiment share regarding the "banned" narrative.

DECISION: GO
