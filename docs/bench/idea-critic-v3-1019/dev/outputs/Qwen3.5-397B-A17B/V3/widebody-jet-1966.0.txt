**Steelman:** Building a massively larger, efficient wide-body jet with freighter conversion capability secures long-term market dominance and meets urgent customer demand despite high upfront costs.

**Analysis:**
The proposal faces existential financial risk; the development and factory outlay exceeds all prior company history. Relying on a single customer and an unproven engine supplier creates a fragile foundation. The threat of supersonic replacement adds strategic uncertainty. However, the "do nothing" option cedes the growing high-capacity market to competitors and alienates the largest customer. The raised cockpit design provides a crucial, low-cost insurance policy: if passenger demand shifts, the asset retains value as a freighter, mitigating the supersonic risk.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Lose the key customer and fail to capture economies of scale as travel grows.
2.  **Supersonic Focus:** Bet entirely on unproven, likely expensive-to-operate technology while ignoring immediate capacity crunches.
3.  **Incremental Stretch:** Modifying existing jets fails to achieve the required "two and a half times" efficiency gain.

**Verdict Logic:**
While the risks are catastrophic if failed, the "edge" is clear: only this specific manufacturer has the customer mandate and technical baseline to attempt such a leap. The freighter conversion feature directly addresses the primary obsolescence risk (supersonic shift). The lack of multiple orders is typical for first-of-class mega-projects; the lead customer's commitment validates the immediate need. Waiting for more proof allows competitors to establish the standard. The cost of delay is the loss of market leadership. The plan beats "do nothing" because the status quo cannot support the projected growth in travel volume.

**Blockers:** NONE. The financial scale is a risk, not a factual blocker, as the customer demand justifies the investment thesis.
**Risks:** Engine failure, single-customer dependency, supersonic disruption.
**Edge:** Unique customer mandate combined with a dual-use (passenger/freighter) design that hedges against technological shifts.

DECISION: GO
