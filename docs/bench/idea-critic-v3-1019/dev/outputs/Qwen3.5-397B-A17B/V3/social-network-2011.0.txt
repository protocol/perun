**Steelman:** Leveraging massive existing distribution (search, mail, video) to solve the cold-start problem that killed previous attempts, using unique video technology to carve a niche before expanding.

**Attack:**
1.  **History as Data:** Three prior failures indicate a structural inability to crack social engagement, not just bad timing. Repeating the pattern with "forced integration" validates the risk of user annoyance rather than mitigating it.
2.  **Incentive Misalignment:** Tying executive bonuses to *adoption* (vanity metrics) rather than *retention* or *engagement depth* encourages spammy integration tactics that degrade core product utility (search/mail), accelerating user churn.
3.  **Network Effect Reality:** "Circles" and "Hangouts" are features, not platforms. The incumbent can copy these instantly. Without a fundamentally new value proposition beyond "we are everywhere," users have zero incentive to fragment their social graph.
4.  **Forced Integration Risk:** Blending social layers into utility products (search, mail) risks contaminating the primary value proposition (efficiency/trust) with social noise, potentially damaging the core revenue engine to chase a low-probability win.

**Better Way:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Protect core utility dominance; acquire a social player if the market shifts.
*   **Standalone Beta:** Test "Hangouts" as a distinct product without poisoning the trust of search/mail users.
*   **Pivot Incentives:** Tie bonuses to daily active usage duration, not sign-up counts.

**Pre-mortem:** Launch occurs; users resent mail/search clutter; early adopters try "Hangouts" but find no one else there because the network effect hasn't tipped; executives game the bonus system by forcing sign-ups; core search satisfaction dips; project killed in 18 months, branded as another failure.

**Edge:** NONE. Distribution is not an edge if the product lacks organic pull; being first with video chat is transient against a monopolist.

**Blockers:**
1.  Incentive structure guarantees quality degradation (bonuses on adoption vs. retention).
2.  History of three failures suggests a missing core competency not addressed by "integration."

**Kill Criteria:** If Day-30 retention < 20% or core product (search) satisfaction drops >5%, terminate immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
