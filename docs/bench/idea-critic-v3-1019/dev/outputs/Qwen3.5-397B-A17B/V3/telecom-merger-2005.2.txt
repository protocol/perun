**Steelman:** Acquiring the target secures a sticky, high-margin enterprise segment (construction/business) via its unique push-to-talk service, creating immediate revenue synergies that offset the $36B price tag before network integration completes.

**Attack & Options:**
The core claim fails on the "incompatible technologies" constraint. Running dual networks indefinitely destroys the primary merger value driver: cost synergy. The proposal admits the target's tech is "limited for data," yet the buyer pays a premium for it.
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids $36B debt and integration chaos; preserves capital for organic data network builds.
*   **Alternative:** License the push-to-talk application layer to run on the buyer's existing infrastructure, acquiring only the customer contracts, not the incompatible physical network. This captures the "edge" (customers) without the "blocker" (legacy hardware).

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
In 2005, the industry north star is migrating to high-speed data (3G/EVDO). Doubling down on a legacy, voice-centric, incompatible network contradicts this trajectory.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Integration stalls due to technical debt; the legacy network becomes a cash drain rather than an asset. Goodwill impairment hits earnings when the promised migration timeline slips.
*   **Cost of Delay:** Minimal. The target's niche status means they are not an imminent threat if left independent for 12 months.

**Blockers, Risks, Edge:**
*   **Blockers:** The "incompatible technologies" fact directly negates the operational feasibility of a standard merger. The $36B valuation likely prices in synergies that the technical incompatibility makes impossible to realize quickly.
*   **Risks:** Massive integration cost, culture clash, goodwill write-down, distraction from data rollout.
*   **Edge:** NONE. Being first to merge incompatible networks is not an advantage; it is a liability. The customer base is an asset, but the proposal demands buying the *network* too.

**Decision:**
The proposal forces the buyer to inherit a technological dead end to get a customer list. The blocker (incompatibility) is structural, not solvable by management will. The deal structure must change to an asset-light acquisition of customers only. As proposed—buying the whole incompatible network—the math does not work.

DECISION: NO-GO
