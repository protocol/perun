**Verdict: NO-GO**

The proposal fails the "Edge" and "Blocker" tests required for a go decision. The core claim relies on acquiring a franchise to feed parks, products, and films. However, the facts provided indicate the franchise's most recent entries received mixed reviews, signaling declining audience demand. Furthermore, the creative lead is retiring, removing the primary asset driving past success. 

The "Edge" is nonexistent. The proposal cites no unique asset, cost advantage, or proven demand that rivals lack. "Strategic necessity" or fear of falling behind are not edges. The high price ($4 billion) against current film output creates a financial blocker; the ROI cannot be validated when the core product (films) is showing signs of fatigue and the key creator is exiting. 

The risks are not merely hypothetical; they are stated facts. Mixed reviews and the loss of the founder are concrete indicators that the franchise may not sustain the required cross-business synergy. The fact that much merchandise value is already licensed out further caps the immediate upside for the consumer products division. 

A "do nothing" or "wait and see" alternative is superior. Waiting allows the market to clarify the franchise's viability without the founder and with recent mixed performance. A cheaper, staged alternative would be to license specific elements for a single test film rather than a $4 billion outright purchase of the entire company and its liabilities. 

The proposal lacks a kill criterion or a capped, time-boxed first step. It commits massive capital based on past glory while ignoring current negative indicators. Without a named edge that survives the loss of the founder and mixed recent performance, this is a speculative bet, not a strategic acquisition. The blocker (high price + declining quality signals + loss of creative lead) outweighs the unproven upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
