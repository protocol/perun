**Steelman:** Leverage the existing seven-million-subscriber base and cash flow from the profitable DVD unit to secure early streaming rights, establishing a foothold before broadband saturation and incumbent tech giants make entry impossible, accepting short-term margin pressure and cannibalization as the cost of long-term survival.

**Attack:** The proposal faces immediate structural headwinds: high licensing costs for a small, older catalogue; poor video quality that may alienate users accustomed to DVD fidelity; and limited broadband penetration in 2007 that caps the addressable market. The risk of cannibalizing the highly profitable core DVD business is acute, potentially eroding the very cash engine needed to fund the transition. Furthermore, entering a capital-intensive war against future tech and retail giants without a content advantage or superior technology suggests a high probability of burning cash for negligible market share.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Protect current DVD margins; risk obsolescence if streaming adoption accelerates faster than broadband infrastructure.
2.  **Paid Tier:** Launch streaming as a premium add-on to test demand without subsidizing it via DVD profits, reducing cannibalization risk.
3.  **Hybrid Delay:** Wait for broadband penetration to reach a critical threshold and video quality standards to improve before committing significant capital.

**Pre-mortem:** The initiative fails because licensing costs drain DVD profits while the poor catalogue and quality fail to retain users, leaving the company weakened just as deep-pocketed competitors launch superior services.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** None explicitly proven fatal yet, but the "no extra charge" model removes the ability to validate unit economics before scaling costs.
*   **Edge:** The only edge is the installed base, but offering the service for free devalues this asset by inviting low-engagement users who strain bandwidth without generating incremental revenue.
*   **Verdict:** The proposal lacks a mechanism to validate demand or manage cannibalization. Giving away a costly, inferior product to protect a profitable one is strategically unsound without a paid tier or a clearer path to content parity. The "free" element is the critical flaw; it accelerates cash burn without proving retention value against future competitors.

DECISION: NO-GO
