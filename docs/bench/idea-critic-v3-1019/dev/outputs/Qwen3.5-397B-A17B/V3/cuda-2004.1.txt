**Steelman:** Embedding general-purpose compute capability into mass-market GPUs now creates a ubiquitous, low-cost parallel supercomputer, positioning the company to own the emerging era of accelerated computing before rivals or CPU vendors can react.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the blocker test on three critical fronts derived strictly from the provided facts.

First, **financial viability**: The plan raises unit costs for a product sold in a competitive consumer market ("mass-market") where revenue relies on gaming. With "no large paying market" and "no committed customers," there is no immediate revenue stream to offset the increased manufacturing cost or the heavy R&D spend on compilers and toolchains. In a "core race with its main rival," voluntarily increasing costs without a corresponding increase in perceived consumer value (gamers do not need scientific programmability) is a direct path to margin collapse and market share loss.

Second, **strategic focus**: The company faces an existential "core race" in its primary sector. Diverting engineering resources to build a compiler and toolchain for a "niche demand" constitutes a dangerous distraction. The "better way" is to wait for the academic experiments to coalesce into a defined market requirement before committing silicon area, rather than betting the company on a hypothetical future.

Third, **lack of edge**: The proposal admits the market is currently limited to academics using "hacked graphics APIs." There is no evidence provided that this specific company has a unique asset to win this future market over CPU manufacturers or other chipmakers who could enter later with less risk. Being first into a non-existent market is not an edge; it is a burn rate.

**Verdict:**
This is a one-way door with massive irreversible costs (silicon area, toolchain development) based entirely on assumptions. The "cost on every unit" is a confirmed blocker because the value proposition for the actual payer (the gamer) is zero. The plan must be rejected in its current form until a paying customer segment is identified or the cost impact is neutralized.

DECISION: NO-GO
