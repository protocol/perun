The claim is that replacing a coupon-driven model with everyday low pricing (EDLP) and branded shops will reverse falling sales for a mid-market department store whose customers are historically conditioned to wait for discounts.

**Skeptic:** The core assumption—that customers value "fair" everyday prices over perceived savings from coupons—contradicts the stated fact that most sales historically come from discounted items. The customer base is explicitly described as "coupon-driven." Removing the mechanism that triggers their purchasing behavior (the coupon) without evidence they want to stop using it assumes a change in consumer psychology that has not been demonstrated. The proposal treats the discount model as a flaw to be fixed rather than the primary value proposition for the existing base.

**Better-way:** The "do nothing" option or a hybrid approach maintains the known revenue engine while testing EDLP in a subset of locations. A full chain-wide reboot ignores Chesterton's Fence: the constant sales model exists because it currently drives the majority of volume. A cheaper, safer alternative is a pilot program to measure if the "older" demographic actually converts to non-promotional pricing before risking the entire chain's cash flow.

**Kill-criteria:** The pre-mortem is clear: if the core customer base refuses to pay full price, revenue will collapse immediately. The "one-way door" nature of announcing this chain-wide without a test removes the ability to retreat without massive brand confusion and financial loss. The cost of delay (waiting for pilot results) is negligible compared to the cost of executing a failed strategy across all stores simultaneously. There is no stated "edge" (e.g., exclusive products, superior convenience) that would compel coupon-trained shoppers to accept higher effective prices. The proposal relies on the CEO's past success in electronics, a different category with different buying cycles, which is not a valid edge here.

**Blockers:** The plan fails the basic math of its own premise: removing the primary driver of historical sales (discounts) for a customer base trained to seek them, with no evidence of demand for the new model.
**Risks:** Total alienation of the core base; immediate revenue cliff.
**Edge:** NONE.

DECISION: NO-GO
