**Steelman:** Leverage existing brand loyalty and a superior hardware interface (larger screen) to disrupt a complacent market leader by offering seamless wireless sharing and a unified store, betting that ecosystem friction outweighs first-mover advantage.

**Skeptic:** The proposal admits being "late to market" against a dominant player with high switching costs. Dropping the industry-standard copy-protection (DRM) alienates current partners (labels and device makers) whose content is essential for the store's viability. Without label support, the store fails; without the store, the hardware is a commodity. The "large marketing budget" cannot fix a broken supply chain or convince labels to abandon the incumbent's proven model.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" option avoids burning capital on a likely failure. A cheaper alternative is licensing the software to existing hardware makers rather than building new devices, or focusing solely on the desktop client where the company already has traction, avoiding the hardware risk entirely. The proposed path requires winning on two fronts (hardware and content) simultaneously, which is historically fatal for late entrants.

**Kill-Criteria:** If major labels refuse to license content without the established DRM standard, the store cannot launch. If wireless sharing is restricted by labels as feared, a core differentiator vanishes. The proposal offers no contingency for label refusal, treating their participation as assumed rather than negotiated.

**Edge:** NONE. Being "first" to drop DRM is a liability, not an asset, when content owners control the inventory. "Larger screen" and "marketing budget" are not structural advantages against a locked ecosystem. The plan relies on rivals behaving irrationally (abandoning their standard) and customers ignoring network effects.

**Blockers:** 
1. Content availability: Labels are likely to restrict sharing and refuse non-DRM content, starving the store.
2. Ecosystem lock-in: Users will not switch to a device with a smaller library despite a better screen.

**Risks:** Massive capital expenditure on hardware with no guaranteed content pipeline; partner retaliation.

**Decision:** The plan fails because it assumes content providers will cooperate while the proposer actively undermines their preferred security model. Without a signed commitment from major labels, the store is empty.

DECISION: NO-GO
