**Steelman:** Launching a premium, keyboard-less touchscreen phone with full web and music capabilities, sold via an exclusive carrier partnership that cedes design control and shares service revenue, creates a new category to defend the music player franchise.

**Attack:** The claim fails on three verified premises from the text. First, the proposer has **no phone-industry experience**, yet the plan requires dominating hardware/software design against carriers who "normally dictate" these terms; expecting a novice to invert this power dynamic immediately is unsupported. Second, the market is explicitly "led by keyboard business phones," yet the proposal offers a premium device without a keyboard, relying on unproven consumer willingness to abandon physical keys for touch. Third, "exclusivity limits initial reach," which directly contradicts the need for rapid scale to offset the high risk of entering a new category with no third-party applications to drive utility.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" option preserves the leading music player position without risking capital on an unproven handset in a hostile carrier environment. A safer alternative would be licensing the music software to existing keyboard-phone manufacturers, leveraging their carrier relationships and keyboard expertise while maintaining revenue streams without hardware risk.

**Fit & Edge:** The proposal moves the north star of defending music sales but lacks a stated **edge**. Being first to market with a touchscreen is not an edge without proven demand; "strategic necessity" to stop erosion is fear, not advantage. The proposer's brand strength in music does not automatically translate to phone hardware dominance, especially given the lack of apps and keyboard.

**Blockers:**
1. No evidence supports that a carrier will grant design control to a company with zero phone experience.
2. No evidence shows consumers will pay a premium for a keyboard-less phone when the market leader uses keyboards.
3. The lack of third-party apps removes a key driver for smartphone adoption, making the premium price unjustified by current facts.

**Risks:** Total failure due to carrier friction, consumer rejection of touch-only input, and limited reach from exclusivity.

**Edge:** NONE. The proposal relies on hoped-for future advantages (design control, touch adoption) rather than current assets that guarantee success.

DECISION: NO-GO
