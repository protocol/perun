**Steelman:** Acquire the dominant video platform to secure market leadership, user base, and content library, betting that scale and legal resolution will unlock massive ad revenue despite current losses and litigation risks.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the blocker test on three critical facts provided:
1.  **Financial Blocker:** The target has "no meaningful revenue" yet commands a $1.65 billion price tag. This valuation relies entirely on speculative future monetization of an unproven ad model. Paying a premium for zero current cash flow violates basic acquisition logic without a guaranteed path to profitability.
2.  **Legal Blocker:** The core asset (user uploads) consists largely of "copyrighted TV and film clips" with media companies already threatening lawsuits. This is not a manageable risk; it is an existential threat. The acquisition transfers massive, undefined liability directly to the buyer, potentially resulting in judgments exceeding the purchase price or an injunction shutting down the service.
3.  **Operational Blocker:** The target suffers from "high bandwidth bills" with no revenue to offset them. Scaling this model immediately deepens losses. The buyer's own smaller service suggests organic growth is possible without inheriting these toxic unit economics.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid transferring liability and burning cash on an unproven model. Continue growing the existing smaller service organically.
*   **Wait for Legal Clarity:** Delay acquisition until copyright frameworks are established or the target proves monetization viability, drastically reducing the risk premium.

**Edge Assessment:**
The only stated edge is "largest online video destination." However, size without revenue or legal safety is a liability, not an asset. Being first to lose money at scale is not a competitive advantage. The "strategic necessity" of video does not justify buying a lawsuit factory at a peak valuation before the business model is proven.

**Verdict:**
The combination of zero revenue, massive unquantified legal liability, and negative unit economics constitutes a hard blocker. The plan fails to demonstrate how these specific, stated facts will be resolved post-acquisition.

DECISION: NO-GO
