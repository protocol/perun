Steelman: Acquiring the dominant code-hosting platform neutralizes the industry's central open-source hub as a competitive threat, secures the primary pipeline for developer talent, and converts a hostile ecosystem into a controlled asset before a rival does.

However, the claim fails the blocker test. The proposal admits the target is loss-making, lacks permanent leadership, and faces active community hostility toward the buyer. Crucially, viable free alternatives exist and are being actively touted by users as an exit ramp. In a platform business, the asset is the community, not the code; if the acquisition triggers a mass migration to these free alternatives due to distrust, the $7.5 billion purchase price buys a hollow shell. The "run independently" mitigation is unproven against a community that explicitly distrusts the owner's historical hostility.

The "Edge" is undefined. Being first, having money, or fearing a rival's move are not edges. The proposal offers no specific asset (technical or relational) that prevents the predicted defection. Without a verified mechanism to retain the developer base despite the ownership change, the core value driver (network effects) is at immediate risk of collapse. The loss-making status combined with the high probability of user churn means the financial upside relies entirely on a hope that distrust will vanish post-signing, which contradicts the stated facts.

A cheaper, safer alternative is to integrate deeply with the platform as a partner or build a competing niche service without the baggage of a $7.5 billion hostile takeover premium. The current plan bets the company's reputation and cash on a fragile asset that the market is already signaling it may abandon. Until there is concrete evidence (not assumption) that the community will stay despite the acquisition, the deal destroys value.

Blockers: High probability of community exodus to free alternatives nullifies network effects; no retention mechanism provided.
Risks: Regulatory scrutiny, integration failure, continued losses.
Edge: NONE (fear of falling behind is not an edge).

DECISION: NO-GO
