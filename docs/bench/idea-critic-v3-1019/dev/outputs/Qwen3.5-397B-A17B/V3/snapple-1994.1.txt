**Steelman:** Leveraging scale to transform a niche, high-growth tea brand into a mass-market powerhouse, replicating the buyer's historical sports drink success.

**Attack:** The core value of the target lies in its "cult image" and "quirky" independent distributor loyalty. The proposal explicitly plans to replace these specific assets with mass-market advertising and supermarket distribution. This is not synergy; it is cannibalization. History shows that "cult" brands often collapse when stripped of their scarcity and community ethos to chase volume. The buyer's past success with a sports drink (a functional, performance-based category) does not guarantee success in premium tea (an identity/lifestyle category). The risks raised are not merely potential; they are direct consequences of the proposed execution strategy. The plan assumes the brand's equity is transferable to a mass channel, ignoring that the equity *is* the channel and the mystique.

**Better Way:** Maintain the independent distribution and niche marketing to preserve growth velocity and margin, using the buyer's capital only for production scaling, not brand repositioning.

**Kill Criteria:** If the brand's price premium erodes by >10% within 12 months of distribution change, or if distributor churn exceeds 20%, the strategy has destroyed the asset.

**Edge:** NONE. The buyer's size and cash are not edges here; they are liabilities that threaten the target's unique market position. The "strategic necessity" of growth does not justify a strategy that likely eliminates the very thing being bought.

**Blockers:** The plan's central mechanism (mass distribution/advertising) directly contradicts the source of the target's value (cult status/indie distribution). The evidence of "strong consumer loyalty" is tied to the current model, which the proposal intends to dismantle.

**Risks:** Total brand dilution, distributor revolt, failure to compete with soft-drink giants who can out-spend on mass media anyway.

DECISION: NO-GO
