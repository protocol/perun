The claim is that withdrawing licensed content to launch a subsidized, low-price streaming service will generate sufficient long-term value to offset immediate billions in lost licensing revenue and massive operating losses.

**Skeptic:** The proposal assumes the company can successfully build and scale a global technology platform it has never operated, while simultaneously absorbing the loss of guaranteed licensing income. The "low price" strategy relies on a multi-year cash burn with no guarantee of retention once prices rise. The math requires capturing a massive share of a market where a competitor already holds over 100 million subscribers, despite this company having no existing direct-to-consumer infrastructure.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" or "hybrid" alternative retains billions in high-margin licensing revenue while developing a smaller, niche service or partnering with an existing tech giant. This avoids the binary risk of burning cash on unproven technology and alienating current distribution partners. A staged rollout or white-label solution reduces the execution risk of building a proprietary platform from scratch.

**Kill-Criteria:** If the company cannot demonstrate a viable path to profitability within the stated five-year window without relying on optimistic subscriber growth that exceeds the market leader's early trajectory, the plan fails. The loss of licensing revenue is a guaranteed, immediate negative; the subscriber gain is speculative. Without a proprietary tech advantage or exclusive content that cannot be licensed, the "edge" is merely spending more money than rivals to acquire customers who may churn when subsidies end.

**Verdict:** The proposal lacks a defined edge beyond capital expenditure and hopes for audience migration. It trades certain, high-margin revenue for uncertain, low-margin subscription fees while introducing massive execution risk in technology and operations. The "multi-year operating loss" is a blocker given the lack of proven capability to run such a platform. The plan bets the company on a "first mover" disadvantage in a crowded field without a clear differentiation strategy other than price, which is unsustainable.

DECISION: NO-GO
