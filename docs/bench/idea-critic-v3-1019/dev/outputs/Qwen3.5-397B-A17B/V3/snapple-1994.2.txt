**Steelman:** Leveraging massive distribution and marketing spend will accelerate the tea brand's growth beyond what its niche network can achieve, justifying the $1.7B premium.

**Attack:** The proposal assumes the brand's value lies in volume potential rather than its current "cult" status. History shows that moving quirky, premium brands into mass-market channels often destroys the exclusivity that drives their loyalty. The "small, quirky" distributor network is likely a feature, not a bug, maintaining tight shelf control and brand narrative. Integrating them into a supermarket-focused mass system risks alienating core consumers who value the brand's independence. Furthermore, $1.7B in cash is a massive outlay for a brand facing imminent competition from soft-drink giants who can instantly replicate the product with superior existing distribution, turning this into a price war the acquired brand cannot win on its own terms.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Avoids $1.7B cash drain and brand dilution risk; competitor tea launches still happen but without the integration chaos.
2. *Partnership:* License the buyer's distribution for specific regions while keeping the original network intact for core markets, testing the "mass" hypothesis without full acquisition risk.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If the goal is long-term brand equity, this fails. In a pre-mortem scenario (1996), the brand is delisted from specialty stores, hated by original fans for "selling out," and lost in the supermarket aisle against Coke/Pepsi entries, rendering the $1.7B investment a write-down. The "edge" claimed (distribution scale) is actually a liability for this specific asset class. The proposal lacks a mechanism to preserve the brand's unique identity post-acquisition.

**Blockers:** The plan explicitly intends to dismantle the very distribution model that created the brand's loyalty, with no evidence that mass-market consumers will adopt a "premium cult" product once it loses its exclusivity. The cash price assumes continued high growth despite these structural changes.

**Edge:** NONE. Scale is not an edge when the product's value proposition relies on scarcity and niche appeal.

**Kill Criteria:** If specialty store sales drop >15% within 12 months of integration, halt mass rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
