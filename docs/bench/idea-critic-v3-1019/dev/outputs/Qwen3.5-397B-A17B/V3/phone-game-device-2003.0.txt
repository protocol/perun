**Steelman:** Leveraging dominant mobile distribution to disrupt the handheld market by converging communication and gaming in a single $300 device.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on basic utility and economics.
1.  **Usability Blocker:** Removing the battery to swap games is a catastrophic design flaw. It interrupts gameplay instantly and risks data loss, making the device inferior to dedicated competitors where swapping takes seconds.
2.  **Ergonomics:** A device "awkward to hold for calls" fails its primary function as a phone. A converged device must excel at both; failing the core use case destroys adoption.
3.  **Price & Library:** At $300, it is significantly more expensive than the market leader, yet offers a fraction of the game library. Without publisher commitment, the hardware is a brick. Consumers will not pay a premium for a worse experience and fewer games.
4.  **Strategic Fit:** Entering a specialized market without an asset advantage (exclusive games, superior ergonomics, or lower cost) is speculative. "Being a large phone maker" is not an edge in gaming software or hardware design.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Avoid brand damage from a flawed product.
*   *Software Only:* Partner with the existing market leader to pre-install a store or games on their standard phones, leveraging distribution without hardware risk.
*   *Accessory:* Release a clip-on game controller for existing phones, solving the ergonomics issue without reinventing the battery architecture.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, reviewers mock the battery-swap mechanic, gamers reject the high price for a small library, and the phone's awkward shape drives away business users. The project is canceled within a year, wasting R&D capital.

**Blockers:**
*   Battery removal required for game swaps (Fact: proposed mechanism).
*   Ergonomic failure for primary use case (Fact: "awkward to hold").
*   Price premium without content parity (Fact: $300 vs leader, uncommitted publishers).

**Edge:** NONE. Distribution strength cannot overcome a fundamentally broken user experience and lack of content.

**Kill Criteria:** If a prototype cannot swap games in under 10 seconds without power loss, or if three major publishers do not sign exclusives before tooling begins, the project stops.

DECISION: NO-GO
