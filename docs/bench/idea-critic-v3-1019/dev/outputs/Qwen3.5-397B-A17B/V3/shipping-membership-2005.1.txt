**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee ($79) eliminates per-order friction, locking in loyalty and increasing purchase frequency enough to offset shipping losses through volume and working capital float.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is unit economics: if heavy users ship >15 times annually at a net loss per shipment, the program bleeds cash. However, the "do nothing" alternative guarantees continued friction and lost share to emerging competitors. A "pay-per-use" discount fails to solve the psychological friction of recurring fees. The "hard to reverse" risk is mitigated by the annual cycle; the program can be closed to new members while honoring existing terms, limiting liability to one year of exposure.

**Pre-mortem & Door:**
Failure mode: Adoption is too low to negotiate better carrier rates, keeping costs high while fixed revenue remains flat. This is a two-way door regarding future pricing tiers but a one-way door for the current cohort's expectations. Cost of delay is high; waiting for "proven" economics in a nascent e-commerce landscape cedes first-mover advantage.

**Evidence & Edge:**
No external data exists to verify margins, but the proposal correctly identifies shipping fees as the dominant friction. The edge is not distribution or brand, but a structural arbitrage: converting variable, high-friction transactions into fixed, predictable revenue while psychologically decoupling shipping cost from purchase decision. This drives the "long tail" of purchases that per-order fees suppress.

**Blockers, Risks, Edge:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The financial risk is a function of execution (volume/cost control), not a factual contradiction in the plan.
*   **Risks:** Short-term margin compression; adverse selection (only heavy buyers join).
*   **Edge:** Psychological removal of transaction friction combined with upfront cash flow.

**Decision:**
The proposal addresses the single biggest barrier to repeat behavior. While financial risks are real, they are manageable through caps on eligible items and dynamic carrier negotiations triggered by volume. The strategic necessity of owning the customer relationship outweighs the short-term margin hit.

**Kill Criteria:** Halt expansion if membership-driven margin erosion exceeds 15% after two quarters without a corresponding 20% lift in order frequency.

DECISION: GO
