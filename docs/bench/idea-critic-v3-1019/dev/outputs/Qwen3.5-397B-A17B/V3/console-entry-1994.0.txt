**Steelman:** A hardware incumbent can leverage existing manufacturing scale and developer tooling expertise to disrupt a duopoly by offering a superior, lower-cost 3D platform when the market shifts from 2D cartridges to CD media.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the "chicken-and-egg" platform trap: gamers buy consoles for exclusive hits, but developers only build hits for installed bases. With zero first-party studios and two entrenched rivals owning the most valuable IP, the device launches with no compelling software. The "easier tools" argument fails if the audience is zero. Furthermore, entering a loss-leading hardware business without a proven content pipeline risks catastrophic capital burn. The timing coincides with a rival's 3D launch, guaranteeing a direct feature comparison against an experienced platform holder.

**Better Way:** Do not launch a standalone console. License the CD drive technology to the existing incumbents as a peripheral (validating the format without platform risk) or acquire a mid-tier game studio immediately to secure a launch-title pipeline before committing to hardware production.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** The strategy assumes "better tech" equals market share, ignoring network effects. In a pre-mortem, the console fails because third-party developers wait to see sales numbers before committing resources, resulting in a barren library at launch. The "edge" cited is manufacturing cost, which is irrelevant if the unit does not sell. Being first to market with CD-ROM is not an edge if the competitor launches with stronger games.

**Blockers:** 
1. No first-party content or exclusive partnerships to drive initial hardware adoption.
2. Zero proven track record in platform ecosystem management or developer relations.
3. Immediate head-to-head competition with an incumbent's 3D launch.

**Edge:** NONE. Cost advantages and tooling ease do not overcome the lack of software content in a platform business.

**Kill Criteria:** If no major third-party publisher commits to two exclusive launch titles within 90 days, the project must be pivoted to a peripheral licensing model or cancelled.

DECISION: NO-GO
