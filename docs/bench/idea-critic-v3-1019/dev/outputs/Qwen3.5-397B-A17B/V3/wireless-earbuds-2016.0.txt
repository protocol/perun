**Steelman:** Removing the headphone jack forces a transition to wireless audio; launching proprietary earbuds at $159 captures immediate revenue, locks users into the ecosystem via custom silicon, and offsets the loss of bundled wired headphones with a superior, albeit expensive, convenience product.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker is customer backlash against removing a standard feature without a seamless, low-friction replacement. However, the proposal mitigates this by offering an integrated solution (automatic pairing) that competitors lack. The $159 price point is a risk, but it positions the product as premium rather than a commodity replacement, protecting brand margin. The "odd look" and "easy to lose" risks are subjective and operational, respectively; they do not structurally prevent success if the user experience (pairing, case charging) is flawless.

The "do nothing" alternative (keeping the jack) delays inevitable industry migration to wireless and water resistance but forfeits the opportunity to define the new standard. A cheaper alternative (selling third-party buds) fails to capture the ecosystem lock-in value of the custom chip.

**Edge:** The proprietary chip enabling automatic pairing creates a frictionless user experience that third-party $159 competitors cannot match at launch. This specific technical advantage justifies the premium over free wired options for a segment of users valuing convenience.

**Risks:** High price sensitivity may limit initial adoption volume; losing individual earbuds could generate negative PR. These are execution risks, not blockers, provided the core pairing promise is delivered.

**Kill Criteria:** If initial pre-orders indicate less than 15% attach rate within the first quarter, or if return rates due to fit/connectivity exceed 5%, the product strategy must pivot to a lower-cost or revised form factor immediately.

**Verdict:** The plan has a defined edge (custom silicon integration) and addresses the forced transition proactively. No factual blocker exists in the text provided; the risks are manageable trade-offs for strategic positioning.

DECISION: GO
