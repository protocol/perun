Steelman: A distinct brand identity, decoupled from the parent's economy reputation and supported by dedicated retail experiences, can overcome heritage deficits if product quality matches incumbents at a superior price point.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly addresses the primary brand risk by mandating a separate brand and dealer network, preventing the "economy car" stigma from contaminating the luxury perception. The heavy development cost is sunk; halting now guarantees total loss, whereas launching offers a path to recovery. The thin lineup is a standard risk for market entry, not a factual failure of the plan itself.

**Risks:** High capital burn for the dealer network; potential consumer skepticism despite branding; intense competition from entrenched European rivals with deeper heritage. These are execution risks inherent to any greenfield luxury launch, not unique flaws invalidating this specific approach.

**Edge:** The proposal leverages the parent company's core competency in reliable, economical engineering to deliver equivalent quality at a significantly lower price point than European incumbents. This cost-structure advantage allows undercutting established competitors while maintaining margins, offering a tangible value proposition that heritage brands cannot easily match without diluting their own pricing power.

**Pre-mortem & Door:** If this fails, it will be due to an inability to convince buyers that "cheap luxury" is not an oxymoron, or if the single model cannot sustain dealer profitability. This is a one-way door regarding the sunk investment in the new network and tooling, but a two-way door regarding future model expansion or brand positioning adjustments post-launch. The cost of delay is the continued erosion of the sunk development investment and ceding the growing premium segment to competitors.

**Kill Criteria:** Halt expansion if the flagship sedan fails to achieve a defined market share threshold (e.g., top 5 in segment) within 24 months of launch, or if dealer profitability metrics remain negative after 18 months. Leading metric: Initial order book conversion rate and customer perception scores on "quality vs. price" relative to key European rivals.

The plan survives the attack because it isolates the brand liability and exploits a clear cost-quality arbitrage.

DECISION: GO
