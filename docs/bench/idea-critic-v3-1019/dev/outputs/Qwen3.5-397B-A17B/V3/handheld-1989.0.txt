**Steelman:** A focused, affordable device leveraging battery life and a killer app can capture a mass market underserved by expensive, power-hungry color rivals.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal intentionally accepts inferior hardware (monochrome screen, modest processor) to solve the critical failure mode of competitors: poor battery life and high cost. The "risk" of a single-game history is mitigated by bundling a puzzle game with high replayability, shifting the value proposition from hardware specs to software engagement.

*   **Better Way:** Doing nothing cedes the portable market to the color rival. A cheaper color device is likely technically infeasible at the $89.95 price point while maintaining acceptable battery life. A higher-priced device limits the install base, harming long-term software sales.
*   **Fit:** This moves the north star of "play everywhere" by removing the friction of charging and cost, targeting a broader demographic than the core console audience.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if third-party developers ignore the platform due to the monochrome screen. However, the low hardware cost reduces the barrier for users, creating a large install base that forces developer support regardless of screen type.
*   **Evidence:** The $20M marketing spend is significant but necessary to rebrand the company from "single-game toys" to a serious portable platform. The price point undercuts the competition by a wide margin, appealing to parents and casual gamers.
*   **Blockers:** None. The stated risks (monochrome, modest specs) are actually the strategic differentiators enabling the price and battery advantages.
*   **Edge:** The edge is a superior cost-to-play ratio and battery endurance, creating a "must-have" bundle that the stronger hardware rival cannot match at this price.

**Decision:**
The proposal correctly identifies that "better" hardware is not the winning vector for this specific market segment. The constraints are features, not bugs. The kill criterion is clear: if the bundled game does not drive attachment rates above 80% in Q1, the strategy fails. However, based on current facts, the plan is sound.

DECISION: GO
