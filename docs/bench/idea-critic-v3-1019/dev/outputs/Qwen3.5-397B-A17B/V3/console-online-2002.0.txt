**Steelman:** A unified, paid online service creates a defensible ecosystem with superior user experience (identity, voice, matchmaking) that rivals cannot match with fragmented, free alternatives, justifying the operational lift.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Readiness:** In 2002, broadband penetration is critically low. Mandating it excludes the vast majority of the installed base, artificially capping the addressable market for both the service and games utilizing it.
2.  **Pricing Friction:** Consumers accustomed to free online play (or publisher-hosted models) will resist a double-paywall (console + subscription). This creates a high barrier to entry, risking a "ghost town" network effect where few users deter developers, and few games deter users.
3.  **Operational Overreach:** The company lacks history in running global hosting and support operations. Failure here damages the core hardware brand, not just a software vertical.
4.  **No Clear Edge:** Being first to a *paid* wall is not an edge; it is a handicap. The proposal offers no evidence that the unified feature set is valuable enough to overcome the price and connectivity hurdles. The "edge" relies on future adoption, not current assets.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Broadband adoption is too low to sustain a mandatory service model in 2002.
*   **Fact:** No proven demand exists for a premium subscription when free alternatives are the norm.
*   **Fact:** The company lacks the operational capability to deliver the promise, creating high execution risk.

**Risks:** Low adoption leads to developer abandonment; operational failures tarnish the console launch; high price point stalls user growth.

**Edge:** NONE. The proposal relies on "strategic necessity" and fear of falling behind rather than a verified asset or demonstrated demand that outweighs the friction. A cheaper, staged alternative (optional service, dial-up support, or publisher-partnered free tier) reaches more users with less risk.

The plan fails the blocker test: the stated facts (low broadband, free expectations, no ops history) directly contradict the requirements for success (mandatory broadband, paid subscription, complex ops).

DECISION: NO-GO
