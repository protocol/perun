**Steelman:** Leverage the dominant portable player to monetize music via a legal, à la carte store, securing label partnerships to combat piracy.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces severe headwinds: ubiquitous free alternatives, thin margins, platform lock-in, and industry skepticism favoring subscriptions. However, the "free" alternative carries legal risk and poor user experience (searching, viruses, incomplete albums). Subscription models in 2003 suffer from poor connectivity and ownership ambiguity.

**Fit & Edge:**
The critical edge is the existing installed base of the successful portable player. Competitors lack a dedicated, high-volume hardware vehicle to drive software adoption. While the store is initially restricted to company computers, this friction ensures a seamless sync experience that generic file-sharing cannot match. The 99-cent price point is a psychological anchor; even with low margins, volume drives hardware loyalty and ecosystem lock-in.

**Pre-mortem & Kill Criteria:**
Failure mode: Labels withdraw licenses due to internal politics or demand higher rates, starving the catalog. Another failure is rivals successfully pivoting consumers to subscriptions before this model gains traction.
Cost of delay: High. Piracy habits are cementing; waiting allows illegal networks to become the default infrastructure.

**Blockers:** None. The risks (margin, licensing, platform restriction) are known constraints, not factual contradictions. The margin issue is offset by the strategic goal of hardware differentiation. The platform restriction is a temporary launch constraint, not a permanent flaw.
**Risks:** Label instability, slow adoption due to price vs. free.
**Edge:** Unique hardware/software integration that rivals backing only subscriptions or generic players cannot immediately replicate.

**Decision:**
The plan beats "do nothing" (losing relevance to pirates) and "subscription only" (premature for 2003 bandwidth/consumer mindset). It converts a hardware advantage into a sustainable ecosystem. The risks are manageable execution challenges, not fundamental blockers.

DECISION: GO
