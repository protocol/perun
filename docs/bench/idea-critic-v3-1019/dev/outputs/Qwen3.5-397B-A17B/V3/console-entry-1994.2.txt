Steelman: A consumer electronics firm can disrupt the console market by leveraging its manufacturing scale to offer cheaper CD-based media and superior 3D tools, enticing developers away from expensive cartridge incumbents despite having no prior platform experience.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal acknowledges the lack of experience and incumbent loyalty but posits that cost structures and developer tooling are sufficient wedges. No factual contradiction exists within the text preventing an attempt; the risks are executional, not structural impossibilities.

**Risks:** High. Failure to secure key third-party titles could render the hardware irrelevant. Selling hardware at a loss requires deep pockets and precise volume forecasting. An incumbent launching a competing 3D system simultaneously creates a direct feature war against a brand with established gamer trust. The "no experience" factor increases the probability of strategic missteps in platform governance.

**Edge:** Cost and Medium. Cartridges are explicitly cited as more expensive and technically limiting compared to discs. If the proposer can translate its consumer electronics manufacturing efficiency into a significantly lower price point for both the console and games, it creates a tangible value proposition that incumbents tied to legacy cartridge supply chains may struggle to match immediately. The shift to 3D is a market reset moment where past software libraries matter less than future capability.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal identifies a clear market inefficiency (cartridge cost/limits) and offers a specific solution (CD/3D) with a defined target (outside developers). While the risks of execution are severe, they do not constitute a logical blocker to launching the initiative. The "do nothing" alternative guarantees zero market share in a shifting landscape. The potential upside of defining the next generation of gaming outweighs the verified risks, provided the company accepts the possibility of failure inherent in entering a new vertical. The edge in manufacturing cost provides a plausible path to victory.

DECISION: GO
