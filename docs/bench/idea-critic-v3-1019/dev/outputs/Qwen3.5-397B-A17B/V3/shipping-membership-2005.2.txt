**Steelman:** An upfront $79 fee buys loyalty and increases order frequency, offsetting shipping losses through higher gross merchandise volume and locking out competitors.

**Attack:** The core failure mode is adverse selection: only heavy buyers will join, guaranteeing that shipping costs exceed revenue on every membership sold. Unlike digital goods, physical shipping has high marginal costs; if a member orders four times a year with average shipping of $20, the retailer loses $1 immediately, ignoring the cost of the goods themselves. The proposal admits economics are "unproven" yet seeks approval for a full launch. This is a one-way door; once sold, memberships cannot be revoked without destroying brand trust, making the "hard to reverse" risk a potential existential threat if the unit economics are negative.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" option preserves current margins. A superior alternative is a limited pilot: cap membership to 5,000 users in a single geographic zone to measure actual lift in frequency versus shipping drag before a national rollout. Another alternative is a threshold-based free shipping model (e.g., free shipping over $50) which encourages larger basket sizes without uncapping delivery liability.

**Fit & Edge:** While customer friction is real, the proposal offers no evidence that $79 covers the variable cost of shipping for the specific demographic likely to join. Being first is not an edge if the unit economics are negative. The "edge" relies entirely on the assumption that increased volume will outpace shipping losses, a claim explicitly flagged as unproven in the text.

**Blockers:** The proposal explicitly states "economics are unproven." Launching a financial product with known potential for negative unit margins and high reversibility friction violates basic fiscal governance. There is no data showing the break-even order frequency.

**Risks:** Massive short-term margin hit; inability to cancel contracts; competitor undercutting if the model proves profitable later but fails now due to over-extension.

**Edge:** NONE. The proposal relies on hope rather than verified unit economics.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed without a pilot proving that average member shipping costs < $79 + (incremental margin from increased frequency).

DECISION: NO-GO
