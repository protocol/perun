**Steelman:** Transforming from a pure retailer into a hybrid marketplace creates immediate inventory depth and variety without capital expenditure, turning fixed inventory risk into variable fee income while capturing transaction value even when the retailer cannot win the sale.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary fear is cannibalization: used or competitor items undercutting the retailer's own stock. However, for an unprofitable company, holding exclusive inventory is a cash trap. The alternative—"do nothing" and grow inventory organically—burns cash on slow-moving SKUs and limits selection, capping growth. A "curated partners only" model reduces risk but fails to solve the long-tail inventory problem or generate sufficient fee volume quickly.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a survival-stage strategy where cash flow and selection breadth trump margin per unit. If this fails, it will be because customer service fractures due to bad actors, destroying trust. The "one-way door" risk is low; bad sellers can be removed, and the retailer can stop listing its own items on specific pages if margins collapse. The cost of delay is continued cash burn on inventory that could be replaced by fee-generating third-party stock.

**Evidence & Edge:**
The proposal relies on the fact that customers prioritize price and availability over the seller's identity, provided the platform guarantees the transaction. The retailer's edge is its existing traffic and product pages; competitors lack this specific aggregation of demand. The "blocker" of supplier objection is a negotiation risk, not a functional failure; suppliers already sell to distributors who compete.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The risks (cannibalization, quality control) are manageable operational challenges, not fatal flaws. Cannibalization is acceptable if the fee income exceeds the lost margin on the displaced sale.
*   **Edge:** Existing traffic and product data create an instant marketplace without user acquisition costs.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If third-party defect rates exceed 2% within six months, pause new seller onboarding.

The proposal converts a capital-intensive bottleneck into a scalable platform. The risk of staying unprofitable outweighs the risk of channel conflict.

DECISION: GO
