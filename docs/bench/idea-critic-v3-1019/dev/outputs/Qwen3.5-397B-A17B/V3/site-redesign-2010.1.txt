**Steelman:** A full rewrite with a publisher-focused algorithm and immediate cutover is the only way to reverse traffic decline and compete with a growing rival.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Community Fit):** The core community values existing voting mechanics. Shifting focus to "publishers' content" directly contradicts the user base's primary value proposition. Alienating the loyal community to chase publishers risks collapsing the network effect before new traffic arrives.
2.  **Blocker (Execution Risk):** A "scratch rewrite" in 2010 implies massive engineering overhead with high defect rates. Combined with a "move all users at once" strategy, this creates a single point of failure. If the new algorithm fails or bugs emerge, there is no rollback path, potentially causing irreversible traffic loss.
3.  **Better Way:** The proposal ignores staged rollout or A/B testing. A cheaper, safer alternative is to tweak the existing ranking algorithm to favor publishers slightly while monitoring engagement, or to launch the new design to a small subset of users first. This validates the "publisher focus" hypothesis without betting the entire platform.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. The loyal users revolt against the changed mechanics and leave; the new publisher content fails to attract sufficient new traffic to replace them; a critical bug in the untested rewrite takes the site down for days with no quick revert. The rival site absorbs the displaced community.

**Edge Analysis:** The proposal claims "strategic necessity" due to a rival's growth, but offers no unique asset or proven demand for publisher-focused content from *this* specific community. Being first to rewrite is not an edge; it is a gamble. The plan lacks a specific metric to validate the shift before total commitment.

**Verdict:** The plan contains two factual blockers: it antagonizes the stated core value of the existing user base and eliminates the safety net of rollback during a high-risk engineering event. The "better way" of iterative testing reaches the same upside (validating publisher content) with significantly lower risk of total collapse.

DECISION: NO-GO
