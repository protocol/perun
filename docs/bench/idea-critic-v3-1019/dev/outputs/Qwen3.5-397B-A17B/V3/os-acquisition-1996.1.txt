**Steelman:** Acquiring a modern, object-oriented OS core from a small vendor is the only viable path to rapidly replace a failing, dated system after two internal failures, leveraging external innovation to halt market share decline.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces severe headwinds: the target has negligible revenue, migration friction is high, the founder's history is toxic, and the price appears unjustifiable by traditional multiples. Licensing or continuing internal development are the primary alternatives.

However, the "do nothing" or "internal rebuild" paths have already failed twice, correlating directly with falling market share. The cost of delay is existential; a slower, cheaper internal attempt risks total irrelevance before completion. Licensing a competitor's system surrenders strategic control and differentiation, effectively admitting defeat in the platform war.

While the target's low sales are a financial red flag, they represent an opportunity to acquire deep technology and talent at a discount relative to the strategic value of survival. The "high price" is only high if measured against current revenue, not against the cost of company failure. The founder's difficult history is a manageable personnel risk compared to the technical debt of the current OS. The migration risk is inherent to any OS replacement but is mitigated by acquiring the original architects who understand the object-oriented core best.

The edge is clear: this is the only available asset that provides a modern, shippable core immediately, breaking the cycle of internal failure. No other option offers a path to a competitive product within the necessary timeframe. The risks are executional, not fatal; the risk of inaction is fatal.

**Blockers:** NONE. The financial and personnel concerns are risks, not blockers, as they do not make the plan impossible, only difficult.
**Risks:** Integration complexity, founder friction, developer adoption.
**Edge:** Immediate access to a modern core after repeated internal failures; avoidance of platform dependency via licensing.

**Kill Criteria:** Halt integration if the core cannot boot existing critical apps within 12 months, or if the founder refuses to engage within 30 days. Leading metric: Developer sign-ups for the new SDK within 6 months.

DECISION: GO
