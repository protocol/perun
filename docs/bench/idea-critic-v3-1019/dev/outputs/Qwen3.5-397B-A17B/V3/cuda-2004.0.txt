The claim is that a graphics-chip maker should pivot its mass-market architecture toward general-purpose scientific computing between 2004 and 2006, despite zero existing revenue in that sector.

Steelman: Embedding programmable features now creates a foundational monopoly in parallel computing before rivals react, turning every gaming unit into a potential supercomputer node.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The plan requires raising unit costs and diverting engineering resources from the core gaming race against a primary rival to serve a market with **no committed customers** and **no large paying demand**. The proposal explicitly admits the only current users are academics "experimenting with hacked APIs," which is not a viable business case for a hardware redesign affecting every consumer unit.

The "better way" is to wait for a specific, high-value vertical (like oil/gas or finance) to commit to funding a specialized accelerator or to introduce these features only in high-margin workstation cards where unit cost impact is negligible. Betting the entire mass-market volume on a hypothetical future market while fighting a fierce war in the core gaming segment violates basic capital allocation principles. The risk of losing the core gaming war due to engineering distraction outweighs the uncertain upside of a market that does not yet exist.

There is no "edge" here; being first to build a solution for a non-existent paying customer base is not an advantage, it is a sunk cost trap. The proposal lacks a kill criterion or a phased approach; it demands a full multi-year commitment across all chips immediately. Without a named customer or a protected revenue stream to offset the increased unit cost, this is speculative gambling with the company's primary revenue engine.

Blockers: No paying market exists; increased unit cost on all volume; direct threat to core gaming competitiveness.
Risks: Total loss of R&D spend; losing the gaming war to rivals.
Edge: NONE (speculative first-mover advantage in a non-market).

DECISION: NO-GO
