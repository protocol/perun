**Steelman:** A controlled native app store balances developer demand with product integrity, creating a revenue share model while mitigating security risks through mandatory review, despite the CEO's prior web-app stance.

**Skeptic:** The proposal contradicts the CEO's public position that web apps are sufficient, risking strategic incoherence. Security reviews create a bottleneck; if approval lags, developer enthusiasm dies before launch. Carrier objections regarding bypassed services could jeopardize essential network distribution deals, killing the hardware business to save the software ecosystem.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" option (web apps only) preserves stability and carrier relationships immediately, avoiding the massive operational overhead of building a review infrastructure from scratch. A hybrid approach (web first, native later) reduces initial risk. However, rivals already possess open ecosystems; delaying native support cedes the developer mindshare permanently. Web apps in 2008 cannot match native performance or hardware access, making "do nothing" a slow death for platform relevance.

**Kill-Criteria & Pre-mortem:** If the review process takes longer than 48 hours per app, the store becomes a graveyard of abandoned submissions. If carriers block the device due to service bypass, the product fails regardless of app quality. The one-way door is alienating carriers; the two-way door is rejecting specific apps.

**Edge:** The company controls the hardware and user experience, allowing a curated "premium" store that rivals' open chaos cannot match. The 30% cut funds the review infrastructure.

**Blockers:** NONE. The contradiction with the CEO's past statements is a strategic pivot, not a factual blocker. Carrier risk is real but manageable through negotiation, not avoidance.
**Risks:** Review backlog slowing adoption; carrier retaliation.
**Edge:** Curated quality and integrated hardware/software control.

**Decision:** The risk of irrelevance from lacking native apps outweighs the operational friction of a walled garden. The pivot from web-only is necessary to survive the emerging platform war.
