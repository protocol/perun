**Steelman:** Embedding general-purpose compute capabilities into mass-market GPUs now creates a ubiquitous, low-cost supercomputing fabric, positioning the company as the foundational hardware layer for future scientific and AI workloads before competitors realize the shift.

**Attack:** The proposal demands raising unit costs and diverting critical engineering resources from the core gaming rivalry during a hyper-competitive window, targeting a market with zero current revenue and no committed customers. The "niche" academic demand does not justify the massive capital expenditure of a full compiler stack and silicon area increase on every consumer card. If the anticipated general-purpose market fails to materialize quickly, the company loses the gaming war due to higher costs/lower performance relative to rivals, leading to total collapse. The risk is existential; the reward is speculative.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain dominance in gaming/workstation graphics; let others experiment with hacked APIs.
2.  **Wait-and-See:** Monitor academic progress; add programmability only once a clear commercial use case emerges.
3.  **Specialized Card:** Build a separate, expensive professional line for computing, keeping consumer chips lean.

**Fit:** This contradicts the immediate survival need to win the gaming race. It assumes a future market that currently has no economic validation.

**Pre-mortem:** By 2007, the gaming division loses significant share to the rival because their chips are cheaper and faster for games. The scientific software stack is unfinished and unused by mainstream industry. The company stock plummets, and the program is canceled as a costly distraction.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Edge:** The proposal cites no unique asset (other than existing manufacturing) that guarantees winning a non-existent market against potential entrants who don't have the "gaming distraction."
*   **Negative Cash Flow:** Raising unit costs without a paying customer base directly undermines the core revenue engine.
*   **Strategic Divergence:** Engineering focus is split during a "race" condition, which is typically fatal.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first in a non-market is not an edge; it is a burn rate.

**Verdict:** The plan bets the entire company on a hypothetical future while ignoring the immediate, verified threat of the gaming rival. Without a paying customer or a guaranteed path to monetization that outweighs the immediate competitive disadvantage in gaming, this is financially reckless.

DECISION: NO-GO
