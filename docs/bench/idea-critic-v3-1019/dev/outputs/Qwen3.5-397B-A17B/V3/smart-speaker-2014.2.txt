**Steelman:** A dedicated, always-listening home speaker creates a new convenience category that drives membership loyalty and recurring commerce, leveraging existing retail distribution to overcome hardware inexperience.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The proposal fails the "Edge" test. The company's last hardware launch failed, indicating unresolved operational or quality deficits. Entering a non-existent category with a complex, privacy-invasive device (always-on mic) immediately after a public failure compounds reputational risk. Voice recognition in 2014 is unreliable; a core mechanism that "often fails" destroys trust in a home device.

*   **Do Nothing:** Preserves capital and brand equity while monitoring the nascent smart home market.
*   **Software-First:** License the assistant to existing hardware manufacturers to validate demand without supply chain risk.
*   **Limited Pilot:** Release a non-audio smart home device (e.g., button or hub) to rebuild hardware credibility before attempting a complex speaker.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, voice commands fail frequently in noisy environments, and privacy backlash mounts. Sales stall at the invitation-only stage due to lack of category understanding. The $199 price point is rejected for an unproven utility. The project is cancelled, writing off R&D and further damaging the brand's hardware reputation.

**Blockers:**
1.  **No Edge:** The proposal cites no unique asset (distribution is generic; brand is currently damaged by the phone failure). "Strategic necessity" and fear of falling behind are not edges.
2.  **Contradictory Evidence:** The company's recent hardware failure directly contradicts the readiness to execute a more complex hardware product.
3.  **Core Mechanism Risk:** Unreliable voice recognition is a functional blocker for a voice-first product.

**Risks:** Privacy rejection, category education costs, high return rates due to recognition failure.

**Verdict:** The plan relies on executing a difficult, novel hardware category immediately after a demonstrated failure in a simpler one, with no unique advantage to offset these risks. The "always-on" feature is a liability without proven trust.

DECISION: NO-GO
