The claim is that a $700 pen-based handheld with unreliable handwriting recognition can successfully launch a new category via CEO publicity. The steelman: First-mover advantage in an unproven market justifies the premium price and technical risk, betting that CEO-driven hype will overcome current product flaws to establish a dominant standard.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The core value proposition (handwriting recognition) is explicitly stated as "unreliable in testing." Releasing a primary input mechanism that does not work reliably creates a fundamental product failure that marketing cannot fix post-launch; it guarantees negative word-of-mouth that kills the category before it matures. The high price ($700 in 1993 is a significant luxury spend) compounds this, as early adopters paying a premium expect functionality, not a promise of future fixes. The "too big for a pocket" constraint violates the essential utility of a "handheld" device, while the lack of software ensures low utility even if the hardware worked.

The "do nothing" alternative is superior: wait for the technology to mature (reliable recognition, miniaturization) and for the ecosystem to develop, then enter with a functional product. The proposed plan relies on "publicity" to mask a broken core feature, which is a strategy for reputation damage, not category leadership. There is no named edge here; being first with a flawed, expensive, oversized device is not an asset. The risks (unproven category, high price, technical failure) are not merely potential; the technical failure is a confirmed fact in the proposal's own evidence.

No concrete kill criteria can save a product launched with a known, broken primary function. The plan commits real money to a high-blast, low-reversibility brand failure.

Blockers: Core input mechanism (handwriting) is unreliable; device violates "pocket" form factor; price too high for unproven utility.
Risks: Category failure, brand damage, inventory write-down.
Edge: NONE (Being first with a broken product is not an edge).

DECISION: NO-GO
