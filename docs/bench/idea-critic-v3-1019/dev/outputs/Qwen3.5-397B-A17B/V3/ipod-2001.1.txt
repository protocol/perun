**Steelman:** A premium, integrated device leverages a loyal, albeit small, user base to establish a foothold in digital music, betting that superior usability and design will overcome high price and limited compatibility, eventually expanding the ecosystem.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "edge" test. The manufacturer has no consumer electronics experience, facing steep learning curves in miniaturization and supply chain management within an aggressive one-year timeline. The addressable market is critically constrained: only owners of a niche computer platform can use the device, while the broader market already rejects existing, cheaper players. Without legal digital downloads, the value proposition relies on manual CD ripping, a friction point that has stalled category adoption. A $400 price point for an unproven brand in a stagnant category, restricted to a tiny install base, presents a fatal unit-economic mismatch. The "integration" advantage is nullified when the platform itself lacks market penetration.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Avoids catastrophic inventory risk and brand dilution from a likely failed hardware entry.
2. *Software First:* License the management software to Windows/Mac to build the ecosystem before committing to expensive hardware.
3. *Partner:* License the design to an experienced electronics manufacturer to mitigate execution risk.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches late due to sourcing failures, sells fewer than 50,000 units due to the high price and platform restriction, and is discontinued within 18 months, draining cash reserves needed for the core computer business.

**Blockers:** 
1. No verified path to profitability given the tiny compatible install base and high BOM costs of a 5GB drive.
2. Execution risk is extreme due to zero prior consumer hardware experience.
3. Market demand is unproven and currently negative for high-priced players.

**Edge:** NONE. Being "first" with a hard drive in a niche ecosystem is not an edge when the ecosystem cannot sustain the volume required to lower costs.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders from the existing computer base do not cover 40% of the tooling costs within 60 days of announcement, cancel immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
