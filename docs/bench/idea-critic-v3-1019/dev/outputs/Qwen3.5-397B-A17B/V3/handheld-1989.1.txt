**Steelman:** Win by prioritizing battery life, durability, and a killer app library over raw graphical fidelity, targeting a mass market at an accessible price point rather than competing directly on specs with the incumbent color rival.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly accepts the "monochrome" and "modest hardware" constraints as strategic trade-offs for battery life and price, not as oversights. The margin risk is mitigated by the projected volume from the low $89.95 price and the high attach rate of the bundled puzzle game.

**Risks:** The monochrome screen may be perceived as outdated by early adopters fixated on the rival's color display. Third-party developer support is unproven given the company's history of single-game devices.

**Edge:** The specific combination of long battery life (portability), ruggedness, and a sub-$90 price point creates a distinct value proposition the color rival cannot match without sacrificing performance or raising costs. The bundled puzzle game provides immediate, addictive value that demonstrates the device's utility beyond technical specs.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the marketing campaign fails to shift the narrative from "inferior tech" to "superior play experience," or if the supply chain cannot sustain margins at $89.95 while funding the $20M campaign.

**Kill Criteria:** If initial sell-through does not reach 1 million units within the first quarter, or if the attach rate for additional software titles remains below 1.5 per unit by month six, the project must pivot to a cost-reduced model or discontinue hardware subsidies.

**Decision Method:** The proposal beats "do nothing" by entering a growing market. It beats "matching the rival" by avoiding a spec war the company might lose on cost or battery. The "cheaper alternative" is essentially what is proposed: a focused, lower-spec device. The plan addresses the "single-game" stigma via the bundled hit and implied ecosystem shift.

DECISION: GO
