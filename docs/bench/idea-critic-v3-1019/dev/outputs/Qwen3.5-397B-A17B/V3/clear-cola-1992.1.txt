**Steelman:** A clear cola leverages positive test data to capture demand for a "purer" alternative, reversing growth slowdowns before competitors act.

**Attack:** The core claim relies on test markets overstating demand for a novelty that contradicts deep-seated consumer associations between cola flavor and dark color. The risk of cannibalization is high without a distinct functional benefit beyond appearance. If the "purer" positioning fails to drive repeat purchases after the initial novelty wears off, the large marketing spend becomes a sunk cost while eroding flagship sales. The proposal lacks a specific kill criterion for repeat purchase rates post-launch, treating a potential fad as a permanent brand extension.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Preserve flagship margins; risk appearing stagnant.
2. *Regional Rollout:* Limit exposure while testing repeat rates; slower revenue impact.
3. *Co-Branding:* Launch as a sub-line to minimize flagship cannibalization.

**Fit:** Aligns with growth goals but assumes color is a primary barrier to entry, which is unproven at scale.

**Pre-mortem:** Launch fails because consumers perceive the clear liquid as "watered down" or lacking full flavor, leading to high trial but near-zero repeat. Rivals copy the gimmick quickly, fragmenting the category without expanding it.

**Evidence:** Test market positivity is the only data point; it is marked as potentially inflated. No long-term retention data exists.

**Decision Logic:**
*Blockers:* None definitively proven, but the risk of fundamental consumer rejection (color/flavor link) is a severe structural weakness not addressed by the plan.
*Risks:* High cannibalization, novelty decay, competitor copying.
*Edge:* First-mover advantage on "purity," but this is fragile if the product experience doesn't match the visual promise.

The proposal commits to a nationwide launch with large spend based on short-term trial data that explicitly may overstate demand. It lacks a staged approach to verify the critical "repeat purchase" hypothesis before full commitment. A cheaper, safer alternative (regional rollout) reaches the same learning upside without the catastrophic downside of a failed national launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
