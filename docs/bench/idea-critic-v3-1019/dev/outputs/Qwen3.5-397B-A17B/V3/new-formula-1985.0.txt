The claim is that replacing the flagship formula with a sweeter alternative, validated by blind taste tests, will reverse market share loss. The steelman: sensory preference drives purchase, and removing the inferior old product forces migration to the superior new one, maximizing efficiency and sales.

However, the attack reveals a fatal blocker. The evidence cited (blind taste tests) explicitly isolates flavor while stripping away brand context. The proposal ignores the verified fact that the product's value is not solely sensory but deeply emotional and heritage-based. By withdrawing the original entirely, the plan destroys the very asset (heritage) that sustains the brand's premium positioning. The testing methodology failed to simulate the actual market condition: a choice between a familiar legacy and an unknown replacement, not just liquid A vs. liquid B.

The "better way" is a line extension: launch the sweeter formula as a new variant while retaining the original. This captures the taste-preference segment without alienating the heritage core. The cost of delay in launching the sweeter taste is lower than the catastrophic risk of severing the emotional contract with loyalists.

The pre-mortem is clear: launch day brings immediate backlash, not from taste, but from the perception of loss and corporate arrogance. The "one-way door" of withdrawing the original creates a crisis with no immediate reversal mechanism other than a humiliating re-launch later. The proposal's edge relies on a false equivalence between sip-testing and brand loyalty. Since the testing did not inform participants of the removal, the data is invalid for this specific strategic move. The risk is not theoretical; it is a direct contradiction of the brand's established equity.

Blockers: The evidence (blind tests) contradicts the strategic action (total replacement) because it omits the variable of brand removal, which is the primary driver of consumer reaction.
Risks: Mass boycott, bottler revolt, permanent brand damage.
Edge: NONE (taste advantage is negated by heritage loss).

DECISION: NO-GO
