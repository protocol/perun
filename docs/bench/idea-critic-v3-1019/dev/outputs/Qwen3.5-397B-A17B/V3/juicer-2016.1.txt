**Steelman**: A premium, connected juicer creates a recurring revenue stream by solving convenience and freshness verification for affluent consumers, justifying a high upfront hardware cost through a locked-in subscription model.

**Attack**:
1.  **Unit Economics & Value Prop**: At $699, the device costs ~7x a high-end traditional juicer. The proprietary bag subscription likely carries a massive premium over grocery produce. Consumers rarely pay a 500%+ markup for convenience in commoditized categories like juicing, especially when the core action (chopping) is eliminated but the cleanup (disposing of bags) remains.
2.  **Connectivity Friction**: Requiring Wi-Fi to verify "freshness" of a physical bag is a solution in search of a problem. It introduces failure points (offline usage, network setup) for a mechanical process. If the internet is down, can the user not juice? This creates artificial scarcity and frustration.
3.  **Market Size & Timing**: In 2016, the "smart appliance" market was nascent and fraught with failures (e.g., connected forks, smart scales). The addressable market for a $699 single-function appliance is vanishingly small. The "well-funded" status suggests burn rate pressure; this product requires massive education spend to convert users from $50 blenders or $300 juicers.
4.  **Better Way**: A non-connected premium juicer with a optional subscription for *pre-chopped* (not proprietary bag-locked) produce delivers 90% of the value at half the hardware cost and zero connectivity friction.

**Pre-mortem**: The company burns cash on inventory and marketing, fails to achieve subscription retention due to price fatigue, and is left with warehouses of obsolete, Wi-Fi-dependent bricks that cannot function without server support. The "freshness check" is perceived as a gimmick to enforce DRM on fruit.

**Edge**: None. Being first to "smart juice" is not an edge; it is a liability. No distribution or brand advantage offsets the fundamental friction of proprietary consumables and connectivity requirements for a low-frequency task.

**Blockers**: The business model relies on consumers accepting a locked ecosystem for a commodity product, which historical data in kitchen appliances suggests is a fatal flaw. The connectivity requirement actively degrades the user experience without adding tangible safety value.

DECISION: NO-GO
