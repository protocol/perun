**Claim:** Launch a 99¢ per-song store with light DRM, licensed from major labels, initially exclusive to our computers, despite free sharing and rival subscription models.

**Steelman:** This creates the first legal, convenient, high-fidelity alternative to piracy, leveraging our hardware dominance to jumpstart a new digital ecosystem before rivals lock users into subscriptions.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The "free everywhere" risk is real but misdiagnosed; users pay for reliability and safety if the friction is low enough. The margin concern is valid but secondary to the strategic goal: selling more portable players. The store is a loss leader for hardware, not a standalone profit center. Limiting the store to our computers is a feature, not a bug, in 2003; it creates a "walled garden" that drives hardware differentiation against generic PC rivals.

The subscription rival model assumes users want access over ownership, an unproven hypothesis in 2003 given bandwidth limits and device storage. Per-song sales match existing consumer mental models (buying albums/tracks) better than renting. Label withdrawal is a risk, but they are currently earning zero from file-sharing; any revenue is an upgrade.

**Blockers:** NONE. The math works if hardware uplift offsets thin software margins.
**Risks:** Labels demand higher rates later; competitors clone the model on Windows.
**Edge:** Vertical integration. We control the player, the software, and the store. Rivals must negotiate with us or fragment the experience. No other entity can guarantee the "it just works" experience required to convert pirates.

**Kill Criteria:** If label licensing deals cannot be signed within 6 months, or if attach rate (songs per device) remains below 5 after one year, pivot to a subscription model or license the store to Windows.

**Decision:** The proposal turns a threat (piracy) into a hardware moat. The exclusivity forces hardware adoption, and the price point undercuts the psychological barrier of album purchases while offering what piracy cannot: simplicity and safety.

DECISION: GO
