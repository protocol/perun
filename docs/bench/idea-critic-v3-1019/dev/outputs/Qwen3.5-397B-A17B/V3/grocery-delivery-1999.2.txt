**Steelman:** Secure dominant national footprint immediately to create an insurmountable barrier to entry, betting that speed outweighs the cost of inevitable early operational failures.

**Attack:** The proposal commits irreversible capital ($1B+) before validating the core hypothesis: that unit economics can ever be positive in a low-margin business with high density requirements. The plan explicitly rejects learning from the initial market before scaling. In 1999, internet penetration and logistics automation were insufficient to support the required throughput for profitability. The "first-mover" edge is illusory if the underlying model burns cash faster than it can raise it; being first to bankruptcy is not a strategy. The risks cited (capital intensity, thin margins, no track record) are not mitigated by the plan; they are accelerated by it.

**Better Way:** Operate in the single current metro area until unit economics are proven positive for three consecutive months. Use third-party logistics or smaller, cheaper fulfillment centers to test density requirements without billion-dollar fixed costs. Scale to the second city only after the first is self-sustaining. This preserves capital and allows the model to adapt to real-world constraints.

**Pre-mortem:** By 2001, the company has constructed a dozen empty, highly automated warehouses in cities where customer adoption is too low to cover operating costs. The debt service on the construction loans consumes all available venture capital. The inability to pivot because assets are specialized and geographically fixed forces liquidation.

**Blockers:** 
1. **No validated unit economics:** Scaling an unproven, low-margin model with high fixed costs guarantees catastrophic loss.
2. **Irreversible commitment:** Building before learning removes the ability to pivot based on the first market's data.
3. **Technological mismatch:** 1999 infrastructure likely cannot support the efficiency required for 30-minute slots at scale.

**Edge:** NONE. Speed without a working model is a liability, not an advantage.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed without a pilot showing positive contribution margin per order in the first city.

DECISION: NO-GO
