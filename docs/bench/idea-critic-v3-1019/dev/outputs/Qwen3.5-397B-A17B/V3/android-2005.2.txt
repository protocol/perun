**Steelman:** Acquiring the startup secures a free, open mobile OS to commoditize handset software, forcing widespread adoption of mobile web browsing and locking in future search-ad dominance before competitors control the platform layer.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the lack of a direct revenue model and the extreme difficulty of penetrating a carrier-controlled distribution channel with no hardware leverage. Established vendors (Symbian, Microsoft) and handset makers have little incentive to adopt an unproven, free alternative that disrupts their existing licensing or differentiation strategies. The "do nothing" alternative preserves capital but cedes the emerging mobile web to incumbents. A "partner-only" approach fails because carriers demand control, not open chaos.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if handset makers ignore the OS due to lack of carrier support, or if the open-source community fragments the codebase, delaying a coherent user experience. The cost of delay is high: if a rival secures the mobile gateway first, search ad inventory becomes inaccessible.

**Edge:** The proposer's unique edge is the massive existing search ad engine. Unlike pure OS vendors, they do not need to monetize the phone itself; they can afford to give the OS away to fuel the core business. This asymmetry allows them to out-spend and out-last competitors who need immediate handset margins.

**Verdict:**
*   **Blockers:** None. The lack of a direct OS revenue model is a feature, not a bug, given the search-ad strategy.
*   **Risks:** Carrier resistance and slow adoption are real but mitigated by the "free" value proposition to handset makers squeezed by licensing fees.
*   **Edge:** Monetization via search ads creates a sustainable moat that pure-play OS vendors cannot match.

The $50M entry price is negligible compared to the strategic value of owning the mobile gateway. While risks are high, the cost of being locked out of the mobile web era is existential. The proposal correctly identifies that open source is the only wedge strong enough to break carrier and vendor lock-in.

DECISION: GO
