**Steelman:** A unified, paid online service creates a premium, secure ecosystem that differentiates the console from fragmented, free competitor offerings, turning online play into a recurring revenue stream rather than a cost center.

**Attack:** The proposal assumes users will pay for a utility competitors give away, despite low broadband adoption in 2002. The company lacks operational experience in running 24/7 hosting and support, creating high execution risk. A mandatory subscription creates a hard barrier to entry, potentially shrinking the install base below the critical mass required for matchmaking viability. If the "shared identity" and "friends list" do not offer significantly superior social stickiness than free alternatives, the value proposition collapses.

**Better Way:** A hybrid model: keep basic matchmaking free to build density, charging only for premium features (voice, large tournaments). This lowers the adoption barrier while testing willingness to pay. Alternatively, delay the mandatory requirement until broadband penetration crosses a verified threshold (e.g., 15% of target households).

**Fit & Edge:** The edge is not "being first" or "strategic necessity," but the specific integration of a single identity and friends list across *all* games, which fragmented publisher-led models cannot easily replicate. However, the proposal admits the company has *never* run such operations. This is a concrete capability gap, not just a risk. Without a partner or acquired talent to bridge this, the service likely fails on reliability, killing the premium pitch.

**Blockers:** The lack of existing operational capability for hosting/support is a factual blocker for a launch-ready service. The assumption that users will pay upfront without a proven free tier to demonstrate superiority is unsupported by the stated market expectation of "free."

**Decision:** The plan fails the capability test and ignores the price sensitivity barrier. It requires a pivot to a hybrid pricing model and a verified operational plan before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
