The claim is that rebranding a Japanese cartridge console as an "Entertainment System" with non-game bundles and strict publisher controls will revive the collapsed 1983 North American market.

**Steelman:** By distancing the product from the "video game" label associated with the crash, bundling it with toys to justify a higher price point, and guaranteeing quality via a lockout chip while removing retailer risk via buy-backs, the company can bypass retailer refusal and rebuild consumer trust.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the retailer mindset; however, the proposal directly addresses this by removing the "video game" stigma and financial risk. Doing nothing cedes the market to recovering home computers. Selling it as a standard console guarantees immediate rejection due to the 1983 stigma. Cheap home computers lack the specific arcade-quality experience and toy integration this bundle offers.

**Pre-mortem & Fit:**
If this fails, it will be because the lockout chip stifles software variety, making the system barren despite the marketing. However, the "take-back" promise is a powerful two-way door for retailers, incentivizing trial. The delay cost is high; waiting allows home computers to entrench further as the default gaming platform.

**Evidence & Edge:**
The proposal relies on the fact that the crash was caused by market saturation of low-quality software and retailer overstock. The edge is the unique combination of perceived value (robot/light gun) and risk reversal (buy-back), which no home computer competitor offers. The lockout chip, while risky for developers, ensures the "quality" narrative holds, preventing a second crash.

**Blockers:** NONE. The specific objections (retailer refusal, no track record) are mitigated by the bundling and buy-back strategy.
**Risks:** Developer backlash from lockout; high cost of returns if sales flop. These are managed risks, not blockers.
**Edge:** First-mover advantage in a "post-crash" vacuum with a risk-free proposition for retailers.

DECISION: GO
