**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled e-reader removes friction for book buyers and solves publisher piracy fears via a closed ecosystem, creating a new high-margin revenue stream that complements rather than replaces print.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker is the company's lack of hardware experience and the risk of alienating publishers with low pricing. However, "do nothing" cedes the digital future to competitors. Building a general tablet is too costly and risky; partnering with existing hardware makers loses control over the user experience. The proposed dedicated device is the cheapest viable path to own the customer relationship.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the core vision of selling books, not just printing them. If this fails, it will likely be due to poor screen readability or battery life, not the business model. The cost of delay is losing the definition of the "book" format to tech firms.

**Evidence & Edge:**
While earlier e-readers failed, they lacked seamless connectivity and a massive catalog. The company's edge is not being first, but owning the content supply chain and distribution. Publishers fear piracy; a closed, cellular device that prevents easy file sharing addresses this specific objection, turning a risk into a selling point for content partners. The "cannibalization" fear is a false dichotomy; digital expands the total addressable market by enabling impulse buys and travel reading impossible with physical books.

**Blockers:** NONE. The hardware gap is real but surmountable via contracting; it does not invalidate the strategy.
**Risks:** Margin compression, publisher pushback, hardware execution. These are managed by the "free cellular" hook (subsidized by content sales) and the closed ecosystem.
**Edge:** Unmatched catalog depth + publisher trust (via DRM/closed system) + frictionless delivery.

**Decision:**
The proposal beats "do nothing" and alternative hardware strategies. It turns the company's content monopoly into a hardware advantage. The risks are executional, not strategic. Proceed immediately to capture the nascent e-book market before it solidifies around a competitor's standard.

DECISION: GO
