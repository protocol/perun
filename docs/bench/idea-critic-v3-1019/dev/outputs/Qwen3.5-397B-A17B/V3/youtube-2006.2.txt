**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant video platform eliminates the primary competitor, secures the category leader despite current losses, and bets on future ad monetization outweighing legal and infrastructure costs.

**Blockers:** NONE. The price is astronomical relative to current revenue, and copyright liability is severe, but neither factually prevents the transaction from closing or achieving strategic dominance. The "blocker" test requires a fact showing the plan fails *as proposed*; high risk is not a blocker if the buyer accepts the bet.

**Risks:** Massive copyright litigation could result in injunctive shutdowns or damages exceeding the purchase price. Bandwidth costs may remain unsustainable if ad yields do not scale linearly with views. The "unproven ad model" might never materialize, leaving a money-losing asset. Integration of distinct cultures and technologies could fail.

**Edge:** The target is already the clear market leader with network effects that the buyer's internal service cannot match organically. Waiting allows a competitor to buy them or for the target to monetize independently, locking the buyer out of the category. The buyer has the stock currency to pay without depleting cash reserves.

**Analysis:** The proposal is a classic "one-way door" with high uncertainty. However, in platform markets, being #2 is often worthless. The buyer's existing service is trailing; building a competitor organically against the target's head start is likely more expensive and less certain than paying the premium now. The copyright risk is real but manageable through indemnities, settlements, or building content ID systems post-acquisition (a known capability of the buyer). The lack of revenue is typical for hyper-growth platforms pre-monetization. The alternative ("do nothing") cedes the video vertical to a rival or leaves the buyer with a stagnant internal product. The premium price is the cost of instant category ownership.

**Kill Criteria:** If within 24 months the platform cannot serve ads on >50% of views without triggering mass user exodus, or if total legal settlements exceed $500M, the thesis fails.

**Decision:** The strategic necessity of owning the category leader outweighs the financial and legal risks, provided the buyer moves quickly to implement monetization and legal safeguards.

DECISION: GO
