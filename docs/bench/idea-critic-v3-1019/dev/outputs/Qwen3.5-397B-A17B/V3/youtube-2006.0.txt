**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant video platform eliminates the buyer's failed organic attempt, secures the category leader instantly, and bets that user growth will outpace legal and infrastructure costs, forcing media companies to partner rather than litigate.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal; the price is high but payable in stock, and the risks are known variables, not disqualifiers. The "no revenue" fact is typical for this growth stage and does not prevent the transaction.

**Risks:** Massive copyright liability could bankrupt the entity; bandwidth costs may exceed ad yield indefinitely; the ad model for short-form video remains unproven; integration could stall innovation. These are severe execution and legal risks, but they are uncertainties, not proven failures.

**Edge:** The target possesses a network effect and brand dominance the buyer cannot replicate organically. Competitors cannot easily dislodge the incumbent user base. The buyer's stock currency allows paying a premium without immediate cash drain. The "first-mover" advantage in video search data is unique to this asset.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if courts order a shutdown of core functionality or if bandwidth costs scale linearly without monetization improvement. However, the proposal implies a bet on fair use evolution and ad-tech maturation.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a one-way door with high irreversibility. The kill criterion for the *future* would be: if, within 24 months, copyright settlements exceed $500M annually or ad RPM (revenue per mille) remains below $2.00 despite traffic growth, the division should be spun off or shut down.

**Decision Logic:** The alternative ("do nothing") cedes the video future to a rival who might buy this same asset. Given the buyer's resources and the target's unrivaled traction, the strategic necessity of owning the category leader outweighs the quantifiable financial risks. The lack of current revenue is a feature of the acquisition timing (buying growth), not a bug. The copyright threat is a negotiation lever, not necessarily a death sentence.

DECISION: GO
