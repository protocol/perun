Steelman: A clear cola, validated by positive test markets, can capture demand for purity and extend the brand before competitors act.

**Assumptions:** Test market positivity translates to national repeat purchase; "purity" is a sufficient driver to overcome the cognitive dissonance of colorless cola; marketing spend can inoculate against immediate copycats.

**Better Ways:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Protect flagship margins; avoid cannibalization risk entirely.
2.  **Regional Rollout:** Launch in only one-third of markets to verify repeat rates without national media spend.
3.  **Line Extension Lite:** Release as a limited-time offer to gauge novelty decay without permanent shelf commitment.

**Fit:** Aligns with growth needs but conflicts with the core asset (cola = brown). If the category is shrinking, extending it may accelerate decline rather than reverse it.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches nationally. Initial trial is high due to curiosity, but repeat purchase collapses because consumers psychologically associate "clear" with "lack of cola taste." The flagship brand suffers margin erosion from cannibalization. Competitors launch cheaper copies within months, diluting the novelty. The massive marketing spend yields no long-term equity.

**Evidence:** Only data point provided is "positive response" in test markets. No data on repeat rates, price elasticity, or specific cannibalization modeling is cited. Test markets are notorious for overstating demand due to the "novelty effect" and heightened awareness that cannot be sustained nationally.

**Blockers:** NONE explicitly stated in the text that makes the plan impossible, but the evidence is thin.
**Risks:** High probability of novelty fade; unquantified cannibalization; lack of defensible moat against copies.
**Edge:** First-mover advantage in the "clear" sub-segment; existing distribution network.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal asks for a **national launch** with a **large marketing campaign** based solely on **test market trial**. This ignores the critical gap between trial and repeat. The risks (cannibalization, novelty fade) are acknowledged but not mitigated by the plan, which goes "all in" immediately. A cheaper, safer alternative (regional rollout) exists to verify the "repeat" assumption before committing national capital. The plan fails to beat "do nothing" or "regional rollout" on risk-adjusted return because it treats unproven repeat behavior as fact.

DECISION: NO-GO
