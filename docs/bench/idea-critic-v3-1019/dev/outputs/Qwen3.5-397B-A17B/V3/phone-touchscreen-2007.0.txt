The claim is that a computer and music player maker can disrupt the mobile market by launching a premium, keyboard-less touchscreen phone with exclusive carrier control and revenue sharing, despite lacking phone industry experience.

**Steelman:** Leveraging deep integration of hardware, software, and media creates a superior user experience that justifies a premium price, while carrier revenue sharing offsets the risk of exclusivity and funds the entry of an inexperienced player.

**Attack:**
1.  **Experience Gap:** The proposer has zero history in telephony, radios, or carrier negotiations. Failure in basic call reliability or network compatibility would destroy the brand's premium status immediately.
2.  **Market Fit:** The business market, the most lucrative segment, demands physical keyboards for email. A glass-only interface is unproven and risky for core productivity tasks.
3.  **Ecosystem Void:** Launching without third-party applications limits utility to built-in features, making the device a "walled garden" appliance rather than a platform, potentially stifling long-term adoption.
4.  **Distribution Bottleneck:** Exclusivity with one carrier severely caps initial market reach. If that carrier's network quality is poor, the device fails regardless of its design merits.
5.  **Price Barrier:** A premium price point for an unproven form factor in a competitive market invites immediate undercutting by established rivals with keyboards and broader distribution.

**Better Way:** License the software to multiple handset manufacturers (like the PC model) to gain instant scale and diverse form factors without bearing hardware risk, or launch a lower-cost device with a hybrid keyboard to mitigate input risk.

**Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks a verified path to overcome the "no experience" blocker in radio hardware and carrier relations. The assumption that users will abandon physical keyboards for touch is unproven in 2007. The "Edge" relies entirely on the music player brand, which may not translate to telephony trust. The risk of a flawed first impression due to inexperience is a fatal blocker that cannot be mitigated by revenue sharing alone.

DECISION: NO-GO
