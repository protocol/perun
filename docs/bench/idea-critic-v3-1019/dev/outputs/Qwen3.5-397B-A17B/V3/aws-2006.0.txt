**Steelman:** Leveraging existing, spiky infrastructure to sell unused compute capacity creates a high-margin revenue stream that amortizes fixed costs, turning a cost center into a profit center despite core business differences.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "edge" test. The retailer possesses capital and hardware, but lacks the critical assets for a developer platform: brand trust among engineers, enterprise support workflows, and software abstraction layers. Incumbents own the developer relationship. Entering this market requires a distinct operating model the company explicitly lacks experience in.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids distraction and margin dilution; investors remain wary of capex, but core retail focus is preserved.
2.  **Internal Optimization:** Use spiky capacity to improve retail reliability or lower internal costs, avoiding external SLA risks.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** High risk of failure due to cultural mismatch, unproven market, and lack of technical moat beyond raw hardware.

**Fit:** Contradicts the stated constraint of "thin margins" by introducing a capital-intensive, low-trust venture during investor skepticism regarding heavy spending. It does not move the retail north star; it diverts from it.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-launch, the platform suffers outages unacceptable to enterprise clients, support costs overwhelm retail operations, and the brand is diluted. The project is shut down, having consumed capital that could have strengthened the core retail logistics.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Edge:** Raw hardware is a commodity; the proposer has no distribution advantage to developers, no software differentiator, and explicitly lacks the required operational experience. "Having servers" is not a business model.
*   **Strategic Mismatch:** Investors are already questioning capex; this doubles down on the exact concern without a proven path to ROI in a new, unproven category.

**Risks:** Distraction from core retail, margin dilution, reliability failures damaging the retail brand, direct competition with entrenched vendors.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first to market with a commodity offering without a developer ecosystem or software layer is not an edge.

**Verdict:** The proposal bets the company's reputation and capital on a capability it does not possess, in a market it does not understand, against competitors who do, with no unique advantage other than excess inventory. This is speculation, not strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
