**Steelman:** Acquiring the target secures a sticky, high-margin enterprise segment (construction/business) via its unique push-to-talk service, creating immediate revenue synergies that offset the $36B price tag before network integration completes.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker is the **technological incompatibility**. Running two distinct networks indefinitely destroys the operational cost synergies usually required to justify a $36B merger. The "integration cost" is not merely an expense; it is a binary risk of service degradation during migration, likely triggering churn in the very high-value base being purchased. The "limited data" capability of the target suggests the asset is a legacy cash cow, not a growth engine, making the massive goodwill exposure dangerous if the niche erodes.

*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids $36B debt and integration chaos; foregoes the niche enterprise base.
*   **Alternative:** Build or license a push-to-talk application on the buyer's existing data network. This captures the same customer need without the burden of maintaining incompatible legacy infrastructure or absorbing massive goodwill.
*   **Proposed Deal:** High cost, high technical debt, high integration risk.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
In 2005, the industry trajectory is unequivocally toward unified IP-based data networks. Buying a "limited data" network contradicts the strategic north star of data convergence.
*Pre-mortem:* Two years post-merger, integration stalls due to technical debt. The legacy network drains capital, the enterprise customers defect due to service instability during migration, and the company is forced to write down the $36B goodwill, crashing stock value.

**Edge Analysis:**
The only stated edge is the target's existing customer base. However, size and current revenue are not structural edges if the delivery mechanism is obsolete. There is no stated advantage proving this specific buyer can migrate these users better than a competitor or than the users could switch to emerging app-based solutions. The "strategic necessity" of scale does not overcome the flaw of buying incompatible tech.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Incompatible Technology:** The core asset requires maintaining a separate, legacy network, negating operational synergies essential for ROI on a $36B spend.
2.  **Strategic Mismatch:** Acquiring "limited data" tech in 2005 opposes the industry shift to data convergence.
3.  **Financial Risk:** Massive goodwill on a non-growth, legacy asset creates a severe downside if integration falters.

**Risks:** Service degradation during migration, capital drain from dual networks, goodwill impairment.

**Edge:** NONE (Revenue alone is not an edge against obsolescence).

DECISION: NO-GO
