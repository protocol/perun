Steelman: Combining two distressed media assets creates sufficient scale and data to disrupt the ad duopoly, leveraging the carrier's distribution to reverse declining traffic.

**Skeptic**: The proposal assumes scale alone solves relevance. However, both assets suffer from structural decline, not just size issues. The carrier lacks consumer media DNA, making integration a high-probability failure point. The "declining traffic" trend is treated as reversible without evidence of a product mechanism to stop it. Buying a falling knife to complement another falling knife does not create a helicopter; it creates a larger debris field.

**Better-Way**: The "do nothing" alternative preserves $4.8B capital. A cheaper path exists: license specific data patents or acquire smaller, growing niche players rather than a bloated, declining legacy portal. The cost of delay is low because the ad market is consolidating around tech platforms, not content aggregators. Waiting avoids paying a premium for a business model currently being rendered obsolete.

**Kill-Criteria**: Pre-mortem reveals failure via inability to integrate cultures and halt revenue bleed within 12 months. This is a one-way door; once spent, the capital and management focus are gone. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the risk of immediate value destruction.

**Blockers**: 
1. No evidence provided that combining two declining revenue streams yields growth; historically, it accelerates decline due to distraction.
2. The acquirer has zero track record in this domain, increasing integration risk to near certainty.
3. The target's core value proposition is eroding; no "edge" is identified other than size, which is not a moat in ad-tech.

**Edge**: NONE. Size, fear of falling behind, and "strategic necessity" are not edges. There is no unique asset, cost advantage, or proven demand shift that rivals lack.

**Risks**: Continued ad-revenue decline, culture clash, management distraction, overpayment for a depreciating asset.

**Verdict**: The plan fails the blocker test. It relies on the false premise that scale fixes broken product-market fit in a shifting landscape. Without a named edge or a mechanism to reverse the target's decline, this is value destruction.

DECISION: NO-GO
