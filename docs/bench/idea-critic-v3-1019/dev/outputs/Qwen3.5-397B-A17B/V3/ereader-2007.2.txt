**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled e-reader removes friction for book buyers and solves publisher piracy fears via a closed ecosystem, creating a new high-margin revenue stream that complements rather than replaces print.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces four critical objections: lack of hardware expertise, publisher resistance to low pricing, historical market failure of e-readers, and internal cannibalization of print sales.

1.  **Hardware Inexperience:** While valid, this is an execution risk, not a strategic blocker. The core value lies in the integrated service (store + device), not just the silicon. Partnering or acquiring talent mitigates the build risk.
2.  **Publisher Margins:** The "low price" fear assumes a zero-sum game. However, a frictionless device expands the total addressable market by enabling impulse buys and reading in new contexts (travel, night), potentially increasing volume enough to offset lower per-unit margins. Piracy concerns are addressed by the closed system.
3.  **Past Failures:** Previous devices failed due to poor screens, lack of content, or required PC connections. This proposal specifically solves these with "paper-like" technology, a "very large catalog," and "free cellular." It attacks the reasons for past failures, not just the concept.
4.  **Cannibalization:** This is a feature, not a bug. Customers who stop buying print due to convenience would otherwise leave the ecosystem entirely (or go to competitors). Better to capture the digital sale than lose the customer. Print and digital often coexist as different use cases.

**Verdict Logic:**
The "do nothing" option cedes the emerging digital market to competitors who will eventually erode print sales anyway. The proposed solution directly addresses the specific failure modes of prior attempts. The risks are manageable execution hurdles, not fundamental flaws in the business logic. The edge is the existing massive catalog and brand trust, which no new entrant possesses.

**Kill Criteria Check:**
No factual blocker exists in the text. The risks are hypothetical without evidence that they cannot be overcome given the specific differentiators (cellular, screen, catalog). The potential upside (owning the digital transition) vastly outweighs the status quo risk.

DECISION: GO
