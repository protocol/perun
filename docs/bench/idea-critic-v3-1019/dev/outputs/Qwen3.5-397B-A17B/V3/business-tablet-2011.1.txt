**Steelman:** A focused, phone-tethered 7-inch tablet leverages existing business dominance to validate a new operating system with minimal upfront investment, accepting short-term feature gaps for long-term ecosystem control.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "fit" and "edge" tests against stated facts. The company leads in *business email*, yet the device ships without built-in email unless paired with their phone. This directly contradicts the core value proposition for the target demographic. A business user will not accept a workflow dependency on a secondary device for primary functions, especially when the rival offers a standalone large-screen alternative with a mature app store.

The price parity with the rival's *larger* tablet destroys the value argument. Paying the same amount for a smaller screen, an untested OS, no native apps, and a restricted ecosystem is not a "cheaper path"; it is a premium price for an inferior product. The "edge" is nonexistent; being first with a broken experience is not an advantage. The risk of developer abandonment is high because the installed base is artificially capped by the phone-pairing requirement.

This is not a "two-way door" experiment; launching a flawed flagship product damages the brand's reputation for business reliability. The "do nothing" or "wait" option preserves the brand while the new OS matures. The proposed plan asks the market to accept a downgrade in exchange for a future promise, which historically fails in hardware launches.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Contradictory Value:** Selling a "business" device without native email/calendar breaks the core promise.
2.  **Price/Value Mismatch:** Matching the price of a superior (larger, mature OS) competitor ensures zero adoption.
3.  **No Edge:** No asset, cost advantage, or proven demand exists for this specific constrained configuration.

**Risks:** Brand erosion, developer rejection, inventory write-offs.
**Edge:** NONE.

DECISION: NO-GO
