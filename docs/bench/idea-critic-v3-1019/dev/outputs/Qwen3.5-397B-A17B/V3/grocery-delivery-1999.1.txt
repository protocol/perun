**Steelman:** Secure dominant national infrastructure immediately to create an insurmountable barrier to entry, betting that speed of scale outweighs the cost of premature optimization in a winner-take-all market.

**Attack:** The proposal violates the fundamental logic of startup validation. It commits $1 billion to fixed, irreversible assets (warehouses, fleets) across a dozen markets before proving unit economics in a single pilot. Groceries operate on razor-thin margins where density dictates viability; building capacity before verifying demand guarantees massive idle costs. The "first-mover" edge is illusory if the capital structure collapses before product-market fit is achieved. This is not a calculated risk; it is a refusal to learn.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing/Slow Down:** Validate the first city, achieve positive unit economics, then expand. (Lowest risk, preserves capital).
2.  **Leased/Third-Party Logistics:** Use existing infrastructure to test density without $1B CAPEX. (Reversible, variable cost).
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Maximum rigidity, maximum burn, zero feedback loop integration.

**Pre-mortem:** By Q4 2000, cash is exhausted. Warehouses sit half-empty in cities where customer density never materialized. The fixed cost burden prevents pricing flexibility against competitors who started small. The company enters insolvency because it scaled a broken or unproven model.

**Door Type:** One-way door. Once $1 billion is spent on specific real estate and hardware, it cannot be recovered or easily repurposed if the hypothesis fails.

**Edge Analysis:** The proposal cites no unique asset, proprietary technology, or existing demand that justifies bypassing the learning phase. "Speed" and "VC money" are not edges; they are fuel. Without a proven engine (unit economics), adding fuel only causes a larger explosion. The plan relies on the hope that the market will conform to the infrastructure, rather than building infrastructure to match the market.

**Blockers:**
1.  **No Validated Learning:** Expanding to 12+ cities before learning unit economics in the first violates basic operational hygiene.
2.  **Irreversibility:** Committing $1B before proving the model removes the ability to pivot.
3.  **Margin Mismatch:** Low-margin business cannot support high fixed-cost utilization risks.

**Verdict:** The plan lacks a defined edge to justify the extreme risk and fails the "must beat do nothing" test. It bets the company on an unproven assumption.

DECISION: NO-GO
