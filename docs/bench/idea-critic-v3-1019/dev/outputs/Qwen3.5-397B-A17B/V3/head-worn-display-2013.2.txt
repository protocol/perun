**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to validate demand, refine the product based on early adopter feedback, and establish a premium brand presence before a general consumer release.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "everyday consumer product" positioning test against the stated facts. The risks identified are not merely hurdles; they are fundamental blockers to the core value proposition for the mass market.
1.  **Value Mismatch:** There is "no clear everyday use for most people." A product without a defined daily utility cannot succeed as an "everyday" device, regardless of price reductions later.
2.  **Social & Privacy Friction:** "Privacy objections" and "social acceptance" issues are structural. Unlike battery life (a technical fix), social rejection of a visible camera is a cultural barrier that an invitation-only program does not solve; it may exacerbate the "creepy" perception by creating an exclusive class of users exempt from social norms.
3.  **Price vs. Utility:** At $1,500, the device is priced as a luxury gadget, yet the lack of clear use cases means the value equation is negative. Early adopters may buy out of curiosity, but this does not validate a path to the "general public."

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids reputational damage from a high-profile failure and saves R&D spend.
*   **Pivot to Enterprise:** Target specific industrial use cases (manufacturing, logistics) where the camera adds clear value, privacy is managed by policy, and the $1,500 price is justifiable by productivity gains. This removes the "everyday consumer" friction.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches to media backlash regarding privacy. Sales stall after the initial curiosity of early adopters fades because no habitual use case emerges. The brand becomes synonymous with social awkwardness, killing future wearable attempts. The "invitation-only" phase merely delays the inevitable failure while consuming marketing budget.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first to market with a socially rejected product is a liability, not an asset. The proposal relies on the hope that usage patterns will emerge, which contradicts the stated fact that no clear use exists.

**Blockers:** The absence of a clear everyday use case for the target mass market prevents the transition from "early adopter" to "general public."
**Risks:** Cultural rejection, privacy regulation, battery limitations.
**Kill Criteria:** If pilot users do not report a specific, repeated daily utility within 3 months, terminate the consumer path.

DECISION: NO-GO
