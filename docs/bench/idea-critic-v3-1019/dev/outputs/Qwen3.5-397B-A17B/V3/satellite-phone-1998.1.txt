**Steelman:** A global, gap-free mobile network captures high-value travelers ignored by fragmented ground cellular, justifying premium pricing to service debt before competitors expand.

**Attack:** The economics rely on a shrinking window of exclusivity. Ground cellular is spreading rapidly, eroding the unique value proposition of "global coverage" precisely where business travelers congregate (cities, airports). At $3,000 per handset and several dollars per minute, the service addresses a niche too small to support $5 billion in debt. The bulky hardware and poor indoor performance further restrict utility, making the high price unjustifiable against improving, cheaper local alternatives. The "first mover" advantage is actually a "first burden" risk: massive fixed costs with a declining total addressable market.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid $5B debt exposure; let ground networks mature.
2.  **Niche Pivot:** Target only maritime/aero/government users (lower volume, higher willingness to pay) without mass-market handset costs.
3.  **Delay/Lease:** Wait for handset miniaturization and lease capacity rather than owning the constellation.

**Fit:** The proposal assumes demand is inelastic to price and that ground networks will remain patchy. Evidence suggests the opposite: rapid cellular rollout and price sensitivity.

**Pre-mortem:** In two years, ground coverage reaches 80% of business hubs. Subscribers remain in the thousands, not millions. Revenue cannot cover interest. The company defaults, satellites become space debris.

**Blockers:**
*   **Math Failure:** Debt service requires massive scale; price point restricts scale to a tiny fraction of what is needed.
*   **Obsolescence:** The core differentiator (coverage) is being commoditized by ground infrastructure faster than the subscriber base can grow.
*   **Product-Market Mismatch:** Bulkiness and indoor failure contradict the needs of premium business users.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first with an expensive, inferior product in a rapidly solving market is a liability, not an asset.

**Kill Criteria:** If subscriber growth does not hit 500k in Year 1, stop launches. Leading metric: Monthly churn vs. local cellular availability.

DECISION: NO-GO
