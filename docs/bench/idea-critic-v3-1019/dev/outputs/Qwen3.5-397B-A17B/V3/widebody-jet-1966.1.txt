**Steelman:** Building a massively larger, efficient wide-body jet with freighter conversion capability secures long-term market dominance and meets urgent customer demand, despite high upfront costs and technological risks.

**Analysis:**
The proposal faces existential financial risk; the development and factory outlay exceeds all prior company history. Relying on a single customer and an unproven engine supplier creates a fragile foundation. Furthermore, the strategic timing is precarious: if supersonic transport replaces subsonic jets as predicted, this massive investment becomes obsolete before reaching profitability. The "raised cockpit" feature, while clever for future freighter conversion, adds complexity to an already fraught engineering challenge.

However, the "do nothing" option carries its own fatal flaw. Ceding the initiative on size and efficiency to competitors or ignoring the primary customer's growth trajectory could surrender market leadership immediately. The supersonic threat is a prediction, not a current reality; subsonic travel is the only proven mass market today. The freighter conversion design provides a crucial hedge: even if passenger demand shifts, the airframe retains utility in cargo, protecting the asset's residual value.

The single largest blocker is the reliance on one airline order to justify a bet-the-company expenditure. Yet, that airline is the largest in the market, signaling where the industry is heading. The engine risk is mitigated by the supplier relationship, and the factory expansion, while costly, creates a barrier to entry for rivals. The edge lies in the specific design choice (raised cockpit) enabling a dual-market lifecycle (passenger then cargo), which no competitor currently offers at this scale.

While the risks of bankruptcy are real, the risk of irrelevance by failing to scale is certain. The proposal addresses the immediate cost-per-seat crisis and hedges against future passenger shifts via freighter capability. The potential upside—monopolizing the high-capacity sector for decades—outweighs the probability of total failure, provided the execution is flawless.

DECISION: GO
