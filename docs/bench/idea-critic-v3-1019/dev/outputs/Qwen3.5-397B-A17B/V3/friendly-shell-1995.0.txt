**Steelman:** A $100 cartoon-house shell makes PCs accessible for novices on high-end hardware, capturing the home market before a major OS update arrives.

**Skeptic:** The proposal fails on three verified facts. First, it requires a "high-end PC," yet the primary risk states most home PCs lack sufficient memory; the target audience cannot run the product. Second, a major OS release with a new interface is imminent; this standalone shell will be rendered obsolete or redundant by the platform holder's own update within months. Third, the $100 price point is prohibitive for a niche add-on with "few third-party add-ons," offering poor value compared to the impending free/included OS upgrade.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" option is superior. Waiting for the major OS release provides a native, optimized interface without extra cost or hardware requirements. Alternatively, integrating accessibility features directly into the existing OS (rather than a separate $100 product) addresses the "hard to use" problem without fragmenting the user base or requiring hardware upgrades.

**Kill-Criteria:** The plan lacks a path to viability if the hardware requirement excludes the majority of the target "home user" market. The imminent OS release acts as a hard blocker; launching a competing interface months before a platform-shift is financially suicidal. The "patronizing" risk for experienced users further shrinks the total addressable market to a tiny fraction of novices with expensive computers.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first is irrelevant when the platform owner is about to release a superior, integrated version. High price and high hardware barriers are not edges; they are exclusionary.

**Blockers:** 
1. Target hardware (high-end) contradicts market reality (most have low memory).
2. Imminent major OS release makes the product obsolete before launch.
3. No distribution advantage against the OS vendor.

**Risks:** Low adoption due to price/hardware; user resentment; rapid obsolescence.

**Decision:** The proposal targets a non-existent market segment (novices with high-end PCs) and ignores an immediate existential threat (the new OS release).

DECISION: NO-GO
