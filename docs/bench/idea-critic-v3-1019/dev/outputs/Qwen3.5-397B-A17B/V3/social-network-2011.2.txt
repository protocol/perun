**Steelman:** Leveraging massive existing distribution (search, mail) to solve the cold-start problem that killed prior attempts, using unique video tech to carve a niche before scaling.

**Attack:**
1.  **History vs. Hype:** Three previous failures indicate a fundamental mismatch between this company's core utility (finding information) and social networking (sharing life). Past failure is a stronger predictor than executive optimism.
2.  **Forced Integration Risk:** Integrating into essential tools (mail, search) risks degrading the user experience of profitable, dominant products to subsidize a speculative venture. If users feel forced, backlash could damage the core brand.
3.  **Network Effects:** The incumbent's hundreds of millions of users create a moat that "circles" and "hangouts" may not breach. Features are easily copied; established graphs are not.
4.  **Incentive Distortion:** Tying executive bonuses to adoption encourages short-term manipulation (spammy invites, dark patterns) over sustainable product-market fit, potentially accelerating user churn.
5.  **Opportunity Cost:** A company-wide push diverts engineering talent from the core search business, which faces its own competitive threats.

**Better Way:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve core product integrity; let social happen organically if users demand it.
*   **Acquire:** Buy an existing, thriving niche network rather than building from scratch again.
*   **Feature, Not Platform:** Integrate specific social features (like hangouts) into existing products without attempting a full-profile network replacement.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   If core product engagement (search/mail) drops by >1% post-integration.
*   If beta retention after 30 days is <20% of invited users.
*   If the cost per active user exceeds 5x the industry average.

**Verdict:** The proposal ignores the empirical evidence of three prior failures and bets the company's core utility on a low-probability social breakthrough. The risks of brand damage and resource diversion outweigh the speculative upside of challenging an entrenched monopoly with a "me-too" plus video strategy. The incentive structure invites gaming rather than genuine value creation.

DECISION: NO-GO
