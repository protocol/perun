**Steelman:** A mandatory client solves critical update/cheat failures and creates a defensible distribution moat, transforming a product seller into a platform owner before competitors react.

**Analysis:**
The status quo (boxed discs) is failing core functionality: updates and anti-cheat. Physical media cannot patch quickly enough to stop cheaters or fix bugs, directly threatening the lifespan of "very popular" titles. While risks regarding broadband adoption (2003 context) and retail friction are real, they are execution hurdles, not fundamental flaws. The "do nothing" alternative guarantees continued erosion of game integrity and missed revenue from digital sales.

The "better way" of optional patchers fails because cheaters will simply bypass them; mandatory enforcement is the only viable mechanism for integrity. The risk of retail partners dropping the product is mitigated by the leverage of "very popular" games; retailers need the hit titles more than the developer needs every shelf slot. The client-as-store optionality provides massive upside leverage with minimal marginal cost once the infrastructure is built for the primary goal.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal addresses an existential threat (cheating/obsolescence) that the current model cannot solve.
**Risks:** Adoption friction and channel conflict. These are managed by the high value of the underlying IP; players will tolerate friction to play popular games securely.
**Edge:** First-mover advantage in integrating anti-cheat, updates, and commerce into a single mandatory layer for major PC titles, creating a sticky ecosystem before competitors establish their own.

The pre-mortem suggests failure only if the client is technically unusable at launch. However, the proposal sequences the store feature for "later," focusing initial scope on the non-negotiable utility of updates and security. The cost of delay is high: every month without this system is a month of unchecked cheating and lost digital revenue. The strategic necessity of owning the customer relationship outweighs the temporary discomfort of transition.

DECISION: GO
