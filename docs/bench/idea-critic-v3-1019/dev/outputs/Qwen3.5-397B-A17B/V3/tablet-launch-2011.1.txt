**Steelman:** Leverage existing mobile OS acquisition to enter the tablet market immediately, matching the leader's price while aggressively subsidizing developer growth and consumer awareness to overcome the "unproven" stigma during leadership transition.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "edge" test. Entering a market dominated by a single competitor with a superior app ecosystem, while charging a premium price for an unproven device, is a classic "me-too" failure mode. In 2011, tablet buyers prioritized app availability over hardware specs; a "small catalog" is a fatal defect, not a temporary hurdle. Heavy ad spend cannot fix a product deficit; it only accelerates the realization of that deficit. The "leadership transition" introduces execution risk precisely when long-term developer commitment is required. There is no stated asset (e.g., exclusive content, unique hardware feature, existing user base migration path) that compels a switch.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "wait" option is stronger. Wait until the app catalog reaches a critical threshold or differentiate via a significantly lower price point to target a budget segment the leader ignores. Launching now at parity invites direct comparison where the proposal admits defeat (apps, market share).

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales are negligible. Developers ignored the outreach because the installed base is too small to justify porting costs. Marketing dollars burned with no ROI because the value proposition ("same price, fewer apps") is irrational for consumers. The leadership transition causes strategy drift, killing the developer program before it starts.

**Blockers:**
1. **No Edge:** The proposal explicitly admits inferiority in the primary purchase driver (apps) while demanding equal price.
2. **Market Reality:** Consumers will not pay a premium for an unproven ecosystem when a dominant alternative exists.

**Risks:** Wasted capital on ads; reputation damage from a failed launch; developer churn.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first with *this* specific configuration is a disadvantage.

**Kill Criteria:** If app count does not triple in Q1, halt ad spend.

**Verdict:** The plan relies on hope (developer outreach) to overcome a structural deficit (app gap) without a pricing or feature lever. It violates the rule that a new entrant must offer superior value or lower cost.

DECISION: NO-GO
