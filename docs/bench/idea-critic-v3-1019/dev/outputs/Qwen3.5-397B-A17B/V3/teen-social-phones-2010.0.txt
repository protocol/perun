**Blockers:**
1. **Value/Cost Mismatch:** The target demographic is price-sensitive, yet the proposal mandates a data plan costing as much as a smartphone while explicitly removing high-value drivers like games and third-party apps. The math fails: high fixed cost + zero entertainment utility = no purchase intent.
2. **Strategic Cannibalization:** Launching this limited device immediately before the company's main new platform creates internal competition. It risks confusing the market and diluting the impact of the primary launch without offering a distinct, viable niche.
3. **Operational Friction:** The history of internal conflict between the teams, combined with a delayed underlying software switch, introduces a high probability of execution failure or quality issues at launch.

**Risks:**
- Carrier reliance limits distribution agility.
- "Social-only" positioning may be too narrow if core social features can be replicated on cheaper existing devices.
- No app store prevents future monetization or feature expansion if the initial hook fails.

**Edge:**
NONE. The proposal relies on a carrier partnership and an acquired team, but offers no unique asset, cost advantage, or proven demand that rivals (or the company's own upcoming flagship) cannot easily match or undercut. Being "first" in a restricted, expensive category is not an edge.

**Analysis:**
The steelman of this claim is that a dedicated, carrier-subsidized social device captures youth market share before competitors. However, the pre-mortem reveals immediate failure: young users will reject an expensive data plan for a device that cannot play games or run popular third-party apps. The "better way" is to integrate these social features into the upcoming main platform or release a lower-cost, Wi-Fi-first variant. The current plan demands high expenditure for a product that solves no problem the target audience has at a price they can afford. The internal conflict further jeopardizes delivery. There is no factual basis to believe this specific configuration will succeed where a broader strategy might.

DECISION: NO-GO
