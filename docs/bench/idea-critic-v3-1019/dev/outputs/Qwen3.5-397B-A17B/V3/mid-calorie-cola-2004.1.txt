**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men avoiding both full-sugar guilt and the "diet" stigma, leveraging a premium price to signal innovation before the low-carb trend fades.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "fit" and "edge" tests. First, the core assumption that young men want a "half-sugar" drink is unproven; history suggests binary preferences (full taste vs. zero calorie). A hybrid often satisfies neither, creating a "uncanny valley" of flavor that alienates the core base without attracting dieters. Second, the "premium price" directly contradicts the need for mass trial in a crowded market, especially when a rival is launching a similar product; price sensitivity usually kills me-too products unless differentiation is stark, which "half-sugar" is not. Third, the low-carb trend is flagged as a potential fad; betting a major campaign on a fleeting macro-nutrient fear for a specific demographic is a high-risk, one-way door if the trend collapses. Finally, the proposal admits the target audience already has a solution (diet cola) or the original; it offers no distinct "edge" other than a numerical compromise that may taste inferior. The risk of cannibalizing high-margin regular sales with a confusing, premium-priced alternative outweighs the speculative gain from a trend that may vanish.

**Blockers:** 
1. No evidence that "half-sugar" delivers acceptable taste to young men (the primary driver of soda consumption).
2. Premium pricing creates a barrier to trial for a "me-too" product against a rival launch.
3. The strategic edge relies entirely on a trend identified as a potential fad.

**Risks:** Brand confusion, cannibalization of core SKUs, failure to gain distribution against the rival's similar product.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first to "half-sugar" is not an edge if the consumer value proposition is weak.

**Decision:** The plan lacks a verified consumer desire for the specific hybrid profile and introduces pricing friction without a defensible competitive moat.

DECISION: NO-GO
