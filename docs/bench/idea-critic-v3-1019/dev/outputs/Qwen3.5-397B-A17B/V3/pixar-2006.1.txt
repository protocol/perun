**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio secures the industry's most valuable animation pipeline, instantly reversing the conglomerate's hand-drawn decline and preventing a rival from gaining the asset, justifying the $7.4B premium as the cost of category dominance.

**Attack:** The valuation relies entirely on the continuation of a specific creative streak with no guarantee of future hits, creating massive exposure if the key leaders depart or the next film underperforms. The all-stock structure significantly dilutes existing shareholders while handing substantial equity and influence to the studio's founder, potentially creating a conflicted power center within the board. Furthermore, integrating a distinct, hit-driven culture into a bureaucracy that recently produced flops carries a high probability of talent exodus, destroying the very value being purchased.

**Better Way:** Negotiate a profit-sharing distribution deal without equity transfer to maintain the relationship at lower capital risk, or attempt to hire the creative leaders directly without acquiring the studio's overhead and legacy contracts.

**Fit & Edge:** The move aligns with the necessity to pivot from failing hand-drawn units to computer animation. However, the proposal lacks a defined "edge" beyond paying a premium; it assumes the conglomerate can successfully manage the founders better than they managed themselves, which is unproven. The "edge" is purely financial engineering (stock swap) rather than operational superiority.

**Kill Criteria:** Without a binding retention agreement for the creative leadership team and a clear governance structure limiting the founder's shareholder influence, the risk of culture clash and talent flight is a blocker. The proposal as stated offers no mitigation for the dependence on a few individuals while granting them major equity status.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the blocker test regarding governance and retention. Paying $7.4B without locked-in human capital protections and a defined path to cultural integration is speculation, not strategy. The risk of paying for assets that walk out the door upon closing is too high.

DECISION: NO-GO
