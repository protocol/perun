The claim is that a premium burger, priced above the core menu and backed by record industry advertising, will successfully pivot a child-centric fast-food chain to an adult demographic. The steelman posits that massive visibility can overwrite brand perception faster than organic evolution, forcing adults to reconsider the chain as a viable dining option.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The primary blocker is a fundamental contradiction between the brand's entrenched identity ("child-centered," "value") and the proposed product positioning ("premium," "high price"). Brand equity in this sector is sticky; adults do not currently associate the chain with quality food, and a price increase directly attacks the "value" expectation that drives their existing traffic. The proposal admits customers come for kids' meals, yet offers no mechanism to decouple the adult purchase from the family context.

The "better way" analysis reveals a superior alternative: introducing a higher-margin adult item at the *current* price point to test demand without the friction of a price hike, or launching a sub-brand to avoid contaminating the core value proposition. The proposed strategy bets the entire margin on unproven demand before validation.

The pre-mortem is clear: the campaign launches, adults perceive the high price as unjustified for a "kids' place," regular value-conscious customers feel alienated by the price creep, and the massive ad spend yields low conversion because the product-market fit was assumed, not proven. The "edge" cited is merely spending power, which is not a strategic advantage when the underlying value proposition is misaligned with customer perception.

There is no evidence provided that the target demographic wants premium food from this specific chain, nor that advertising alone can bridge the trust gap. The risks listed in the proposal (price deterrence, brand mismatch) are not merely potential issues; they are structural flaws that the current plan ignores. Without a pilot to validate adult demand at a lower risk profile, proceeding constitutes a reckless allocation of capital based on hope rather than data.

DECISION: NO-GO
