The claim is that a differentiated display and shopping feature justify a flagship-priced, carrier-exclusive smartphone on a forked OS lacking mainstream apps in a saturated duopoly.

**Steelman:** The device leverages unique hardware to capture high-value shoppers, making app gaps irrelevant for its specific use case.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "edge" test. Being first with 3D or shopping tools is not an edge if the core utility (mainstream apps) is missing; history shows users reject fragmented ecosystems regardless of niche features. The "unsubsidised-equivalent price" of flagships is a fatal blocker when the product offers *less* utility (no app store) than competitors at the same price point. Carrier exclusivity caps the addressable market precisely when network effects are needed to overcome the app gap. The "one-touch" feature is a nice-to-have, not a primary driver for a $600+ purchase decision.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve capital; the tablet/e-reader success does not translate to phones without ecosystem parity.
2.  **Low-cost entry:** Match the low-priced tablet strategy (sub-$200) to reduce risk, rather than competing on price with flagships while offering fewer features.
3.  **Software-first:** License the shopping tech to existing platforms instead of building a hardware silo.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to poor reviews citing the app gap, sales miss targets due to carrier limits, and the project is written off within 18 months as a costly distraction. The "3D" feature is viewed as a gimmick that drains battery without solving a user need.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Price/Value Mismatch:** Charging flagship prices for a device with inferior software utility contradicts basic market logic.
2.  **Ecosystem Deficit:** A forked OS without mainstream apps is a proven non-starter for mass-market smartphones in a duopoly.

**Edge:** NONE. Hardware novelty cannot overcome software ecosystem deficits at a premium price point.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot cannot prove users will pay full price for a phone missing top 20 apps, the project must stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
