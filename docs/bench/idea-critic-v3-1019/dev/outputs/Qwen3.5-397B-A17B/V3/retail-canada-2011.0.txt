The proposal attempts a massive, irreversible "big bang" entry into a foreign market dominated by an entrenched rival, relying on unproven logistics and inventory systems. This violates the core principle of testing assumptions before scaling.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Unproven Core Systems:** The plan hinges on a new supply chain and inventory system that are "not yet proven." Deploying these simultaneously with a complex cross-border expansion creates a single point of catastrophic failure. If the system fails, 150 stores cannot operate.
2.  **Pace vs. Learning:** Opening 130–150 stores in two years leaves no room to learn from early mistakes. The retailer admits it must "learn" logistics, regulation, and labeling, yet the schedule assumes immediate mastery across the entire footprint.
3.  **Lack of Edge:** The proposal cites "higher prices on some goods" by competitors but offers no specific evidence of a sustainable cost advantage or unique value proposition that overcomes the incumbent's dominance and the retailer's lack of local brand recognition. "Strategic necessity" or fear of missing out is not an edge.

**Risks:**
*   Regulatory non-compliance due to bilingual labeling errors across hundreds of locations.
*   Inventory mismanagement leading to stockouts or overstock in a new market.
*   Price perception mismatch despite the discount model.

**Better Way:**
A staged approach (pilot 5–10 stores) would validate the supply chain, inventory system, and local price sensitivity with minimal capital exposure. This allows for iterative learning before committing to 150 leases. The current plan skips this essential validation step.

**Kill Criteria:**
The proposal lacks any pre-committed metric to stop expansion if the new systems fail or initial stores underperform. Without a "stop" condition, the company risks doubling down on a failing strategy.

**Verdict:**
The combination of unproven critical infrastructure, an aggressive timeline that precludes learning, and the absence of a defined competitive edge against a dominant local player constitutes a clear blocker. The risk of total systemic failure outweighs the potential upside. The plan must be revised to include a pilot phase and proven systems before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
