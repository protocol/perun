The claim is that replacing the flagship formula with a sweeter variant, validated by blind taste tests, will reverse market share loss. The steelman: sensory superiority drives purchase, and removing the old formula forces migration to the superior product, maximizing efficiency.

However, the evidence contains a fatal flaw. The taste tests measured liquid preference in isolation, not the value of the brand ecosystem. The proposal explicitly notes testing did not inform participants the original would be removed. This omits the primary variable: consumer reaction to loss of heritage. The "sweeter wins" data is true only under the condition of choice abundance, not forced substitution.

Alternatives were inadequately considered. A "line extension" (selling both) captures the sweeter preference without triggering the backlash of removal. The "do nothing" option retains the loyal base while seeking other growth vectors. The proposed path bets the entire century-old equity on a single sensory metric that ignores emotional attachment.

Pre-mortem: Launch occurs, loyalists perceive the removal as a corporate betrayal rather than an improvement. The narrative shifts from "better taste" to "they took away my Cola." Sales plummet not because the new drink tastes bad, but because the act of removal destroys trust. Bottlers face riots; competitors capitalize on the void by re-marketing the original formula as the "real" choice. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of repairing a fractured brand identity.

Blocker: The testing methodology explicitly excluded the core risk (removal of the original), rendering the data insufficient to support a total replacement. The plan relies on a "strategic necessity" to fix share loss but lacks an edge; being first to remove your own heritage is a disadvantage, not an asset. There is no proof that the users who prefer the sweeter taste in a blind sip will accept the loss of the original in a real-world purchase.

Edge: NONE. The plan creates a monopoly on a specific flavor profile but sacrifices the brand's foundational promise.

DECISION: NO-GO
