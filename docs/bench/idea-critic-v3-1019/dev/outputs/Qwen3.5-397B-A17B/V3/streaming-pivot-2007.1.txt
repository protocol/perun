The claim is that a profitable DVD leader should add free streaming despite a weak catalogue, poor quality, high licensing costs, and imminent giant competitors.

**Steelman:** Offering free streaming now locks in subscriber loyalty and gathers essential data before the market consolidates, using existing cash flow to fund a defensive moat that pure-play tech rivals cannot match immediately.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cannibalization without Upside:** The streaming library is "small and mostly older," while video quality is "poor." Subscribers paying for premium DVD service gain little value, yet the company incurs high licensing fees. This destroys margin without securing retention; users will still defect to higher-quality entrants (Netflix's actual history shows this transition took years of library building).
2.  **Capital Misallocation:** Licensing rights at "high prices" for a subpar product burns the cash cow's profits on a losing battle. With broadband "not universal," the addressable market is limited, while the cost base expands permanently.
3.  **Strategic Exposure:** Entering now with an inferior product signals weakness, not strength. It invites larger tech/retail entrants to crush the offering immediately, potentially accelerating the decline of the DVD brand by associating it with poor streaming quality.
4.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "pilot only" option is superior. Wait for broadband penetration to rise and licensing costs to normalize. Use the current monopoly profits to acquire content libraries later or buy a struggling streamer, rather than building a costly, low-quality infrastructure today. The proposal fails to prove that *this specific* weak offering prevents churn better than simply maintaining the highly profitable DVD core.

**Blocker:** The proposal admits the product is inferior (poor quality, small catalog) and costly (high licenses), yet offers it for free. This guarantees margin erosion with no proven retention benefit, violating the basic unit economics required to survive the incoming competition. There is no "edge" listed other than fear of falling behind.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first with a bad product is a liability, not an asset.

**Kill Criteria:** If the company cannot secure a licensing cost structure that allows profitability at current subscriber scales within 12 months, or if churn does not drop measurably among streaming users compared to DVD-only users in a limited pilot, the initiative must stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
