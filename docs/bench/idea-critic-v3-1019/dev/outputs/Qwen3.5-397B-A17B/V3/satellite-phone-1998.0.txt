The claim is that a $5 billion satellite network can succeed in 1998 by targeting business travelers with $3,000 handsets and high per-minute rates. The steelman: this is the only solution for true global coverage where terrestrial cells cannot reach, creating a monopoly niche for high-value users.

However, the alternatives crush this claim. "Do nothing" (waiting 2–3 years) allows terrestrial networks to cover 90% of business travel routes at 1% of the cost, with pocket-sized phones. The "cheaper path" is roaming agreements between emerging global carriers, not building a space-based infrastructure. The cost of delay is negative; waiting reduces competition and hardware costs while the market matures.

The pre-mortem is clear: the service launches, early adopters buy the bulky phones, but they fail indoors and in cars without external antennas. Users realize coverage gaps align perfectly with where they actually make calls (offices, hotels, urban centers), which terrestrial networks already cover. The $5 billion debt burden forces aggressive sales targets that the tiny niche of "ocean/air/desert" users cannot meet. Bankruptcy follows within two years as the addressable market proves to be a fraction of projections.

The evidence provided highlights fatal blockers:
1.  **Price vs. Value:** $3,000 handsets plus high call rates limit the market to a sliver too small to service massive debt.
2.  **Product Deficit:** Bulky, poor indoor performance makes the product inferior to the rapidly spreading, cheaper, pocket-sized alternative.
3.  **Timing:** Launching just as the competitor (ground cellular) achieves critical mass is a strategic error.
4.  **Operational Readiness:** Sales channels are not ready, delaying revenue while interest accrues.

There is no edge. Being first to a shrinking niche is not an advantage. "Strategic necessity" of global coverage does not overcome the math of debt service against a collapsing total addressable market. The plan relies on users paying a massive premium for a worse user experience (bulky, indoor failures) for a feature (coverage in uninhabited areas) they rarely need.

Blockers: Debt payments require growth rates impossible in the defined niche; product performance (indoor/bulk) contradicts business traveler needs; competitor trajectory eliminates the unique value proposition before launch.
Edge: NONE.

DECISION: NO-GO
