**Steelman:** Acquiring the distressed department chain provides immediate scale and real estate to pivot the discounter into a broader format, betting that combined operations can cut costs faster than sales decline.

**Attack:** This is a "merger of equals" in distress, not a strength-acquiring-weakness play. Both entities have declining sales, aging infrastructure, and underinvested stores. The proposal admits cost savings are "uncertain" and customer bases differ, undermining the core synergy thesis. Converting old, underperforming stores into a new format requires significant capital exactly when the restructured entity should be preserving cash.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve cash, fix the core discount model, and wait for cheaper assets.
2.  **Asset Strip:** Buy only the best real estate/brands without assuming the operational burden of the whole chain.
3.  **Organic Growth:** Invest in remodeling existing stores rather than integrating a incompatible culture and inventory system.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** In 2004, big-box rivals are growing efficiently. This deal distracts management with complex integration while competitors expand. A pre-mortem reveals failure via: inability to harmonize distinct customer expectations, hidden costs in renovating old stores, and cash burn exceeding uncertain savings. The "edge" is nonexistent; there is no unique asset, only shared liabilities. Being first to combine two declining models is a disadvantage, not a strategy.

**Blockers:**
*   **Uncertain Synergies:** The proposal explicitly states cost savings are uncertain, removing the primary financial justification.
*   **Capital Mismatch:** Converting old stores requires heavy CapEx, which a recently restructured company with weak sales cannot safely absorb.
*   **Strategic Drift:** Merging distinct customer bases without a proven conversion model invites revenue collapse in both segments.

**Risks:** Integration failure, accelerated sales decline due to distraction, liquidity crisis.

**Edge:** NONE. The proposal relies on hope that "combined format" works, without evidence that the specific customers overlap or that the physical stores are convertible at a reasonable cost. Fear of falling behind or "strategic necessity" does not constitute an edge.

DECISION: NO-GO
