**Steelman:** Acquire the leading mobile-native photo network immediately to neutralize a existential threat to the core business, secure the mobile shift, and absorb high-growth user engagement that the internal team cannot replicate in time.

**Attack:** The $1B price tag lacks revenue justification, creating massive goodwill risk and potential shareholder backlash. Integrating a dozen-person culture into a large corporation often destroys the very agility that created the value. Regulatory bodies may block the deal as anti-competitive. If the mobile trend proves fleeting, the company burns cash on a fad.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Risk the competitor defining the mobile photo standard, potentially cannibalizing the core desktop business.
2. *Build Internally:* Too slow; the competitor already has tens of millions of users and momentum.
3. *Wait for IPO:* Far more expensive post-IPO, with no guarantee of acquisition success.

**Fit:** Aligns with the strategic necessity to dominate mobile, the emerging "north star" for 2012.

**Pre-mortem:** Deal closes, culture clashes cause key founders/engineers to quit within six months, product stagnates, and the $1B is written down. However, the cost of *not* buying allows a rival to entrench themselves as the primary mobile interface, threatening the core ad business long-term.

**Evidence & Edge:** The "edge" is not size or money, but the unique, verified fact that this specific team has already achieved network effects (tens of millions of users) that the buyer cannot organically reach quickly enough. The threat is immediate and verified by growth metrics. The valuation is high, but the cost of a failed organic attempt or lost market dominance is higher.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of revenue is a known variable priced into the strategic premium, not a hidden flaw.
**Risks:** Culture clash, regulatory delay, integration failure. These are managed via kill criteria: retain key staff for 24 months, maintain brand independence initially.
**Edge:** Unique, verified mobile network effects that cannot be built internally in the required timeframe.

This is a two-way door in strategy (can pivot post-acquisition) but a one-way door in opportunity (cannot buy later if competitor wins). The strategic necessity outweighs the financial opacity.

DECISION: GO
