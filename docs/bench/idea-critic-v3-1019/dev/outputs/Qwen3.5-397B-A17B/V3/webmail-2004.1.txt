**Steelman:** A search company can disrupt mature email by offering 1,000x more storage than incumbents, using superior search to make deletion obsolete, and funding costs via contextual ads, despite privacy concerns and high initial infrastructure outlay.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's core risk (storage cost) is its primary differentiator; if the company cannot solve this, the product fails, but the premise assumes they can leverage their specific infrastructure expertise. No factual contradiction exists in the prompt preventing launch.

**Risks:** 
1. **Privacy Backlash:** Scanning content for ads may trigger regulatory or user trust issues.
2. **Unit Economics:** If ad yield per user < storage/serve cost, the model bleeds cash indefinitely.
3. **Adoption Friction:** Invitation-only limits viral growth speed; users may resist switching from entrenched providers.

**Edge:** The proposer is a *search company*. Incumbents offer megabytes because they view email as a cost center with limited utility. This proposer views email as a search problem and data asset. Their existing ad infrastructure and storage optimization (required for web indexing) provide a marginal cost advantage competitors lack. The "gigabyte" claim is only viable if their marginal cost of storage is drastically lower than 2004 norms, which fits their profile.

**Pre-mortem:** The service launches, users hoard data, ad relevance is low due to short message context, and costs spiral. The company shuts it down as a money pit. 
**Door:** Two-way (can shut down service, though reputation damage is sticky).
**Delay Cost:** High. Competitors (Microsoft, Yahoo) could replicate the storage jump if given time, neutralizing the first-mover advantage in "searchable" mail.

**Kill Criteria:** 
1. **Metric:** Ad revenue per user must exceed storage + compute cost within 18 months.
2. **Threshold:** If privacy complaints exceed 0.5% of user base, halt ad-scanning feature.
3. **Pivot:** If invitation waitlist < 1M in 3 months, open registration immediately.

**Decision:** The asymmetry is clear: the cost of storage is dropping faster than ad value is rising. The only entity that can make the math work is a search giant. The privacy risk is real but manageable via transparency. The "mature market" argument is invalid because the incumbents are solving the wrong problem (storage limits vs. retrieval).

DECISION: GO
