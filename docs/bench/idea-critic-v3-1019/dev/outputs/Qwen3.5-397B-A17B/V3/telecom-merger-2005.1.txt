**Steelman:** Acquiring the target secures a sticky, high-margin enterprise segment (construction/business) that the buyer cannot organically replicate due to incompatible technology, justifying the $36B premium as a defensive moat against churn and a bridge to data services.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The core flaw is the "integration cost" understatement. Merging incompatible networks in 2005 requires maintaining parallel infrastructures indefinitely or forcing a costly, churn-inducing migration of the very customers being bought. The "limited for data" attribute of the target suggests the asset is a sunset technology, not a growth engine.
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids $36B debt and integration chaos; allows organic build-out of data-native enterprise solutions.
*   **Partner/Resell:** Contract for push-to-talk services without the balance sheet burden and technical debt.
*   **Acquire Tech Only:** Buy the customer list and migrate them to the buyer's native stack immediately, avoiding legacy network ops.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-merger, the legacy network drains CapEx, delaying 3G/4G rollout. Enterprise customers, frustrated by stagnant features on old handsets, defect to emerging smartphones with native VoIP/data capabilities. The goodwill is impaired, triggering a massive write-down. The "synergy" was negative; the buyer paid for a shrinking user base with a technology ceiling.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Technological Obsolescence:** The target's core value (push-to-talk on legacy tech) is incompatible with the industry's rapid shift toward data-centric smartphones. Buying a "walled garden" of incompatible tech locks the buyer into maintaining a dying standard.
2.  **Valuation Mismatch:** $36B for a network described as "limited for data" and "incompatible" implies paying a growth multiple for a sunset asset. No edge is proven that justifies this premium over organic development or partnership.

**Edge:** NONE. Being first to merge incompatible networks is not an advantage; it is an integration hazard. The "high-value" base is held hostage by technology, not loyalty; once a viable data-native alternative appears, they will leave.

**Decision:** The proposal fails the blocker test. It acquires technical debt and obsolescence at a premium price, betting on a legacy standard in a data-driven future.

DECISION: NO-GO
