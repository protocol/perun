**Steelman:** Acquiring a modern, object-oriented OS core from a small vendor is the only viable path to rapidly replace a failing, dated system after two internal failures, leveraging the founder's expertise despite historical friction.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces severe headwinds: the target has negligible revenue, migration is technically perilous, and the founder's return invites cultural conflict. Licensing appears safer and cheaper. However, the "do nothing" scenario guarantees continued market share erosion and eventual irrelevance given two prior internal failures. Licensing a competitor's system surrenders strategic control and differentiation, effectively admitting defeat in the platform war.

The "high price" relative to current revenue is a category error; the purchase is for technology and talent acceleration, not current cash flow. The risk of migration is real but is shared by any replacement strategy; delaying increases the technical debt gap. The founder's difficult history is a manageable personnel challenge compared to the existential threat of a dated OS. The target's low sales volume is irrelevant if the technology is superior and intended for internal deployment rather than immediate standalone resale.

**Blockers:** None. The facts state internal replacement failed twice; licensing sacrifices the platform; inaction leads to decline.
**Risks:** Migration complexity, cultural friction, integration cost. These are execution challenges, not strategic blockers.
**Edge:** Unique access to a modern, object-oriented architecture that internally cannot be built quickly enough, combined with the specific visionary talent required to execute the pivot.

**Decision:**
The proposal beats "do nothing" (death spiral) and "license" (loss of sovereignty). The cost is justified as a rescue premium for speed and control. The risks are inherent to any turnaround but are preferable to the certainty of failure without this asset.

**Kill Criteria:** If the acquired technology cannot run a prototype of the legacy app suite within 6 months, or if the founder refuses the advisor role, the deal thesis fails.

**Leading Metric:** Developer adoption rate of the new core APIs within 12 months post-acquisition.

DECISION: GO
