The claim is that launching the world's cheapest car at $2,500 from a new dedicated factory will successfully transition scooter families to cars. The steelman is that extreme affordability unlocks a massive, untapped market segment where no viable four-wheel alternative exists, creating volume sufficient to offset razor-thin margins.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The "cheapest" positioning is a double-edged sword; in automotive markets, low price often signals unsafe or inferior quality, directly deterring families whose primary motivation is safety. If the brand becomes synonymous with "dangerous tin can," volume targets will not materialize regardless of price. Furthermore, the dedicated factory faces active land protests. Building a high-volume plant on contested land introduces severe execution risk: delays, reputational damage, and potential work stoppages that contradict the "high volume" requirement essential for this cost structure.

Alternatives exist. A "better way" would be to launch a slightly higher-priced model using an existing facility to bypass land protests and position the vehicle as "value-safe" rather than "cheapest." Another alternative is targeting the used car market upgrade path rather than competing directly with scooters on absolute price floor.

The pre-mortem reveals a likely failure mode: the factory is delayed by protests, forcing cost-cutting measures that compromise safety features to hit the $2,500 price point. Media highlights a safety defect, cementing the "death trap" narrative. Families stick to scooters or buy used cars, leaving the dedicated factory underutilized and financially ruinous.

The proposal lacks a verified edge. Being first to a low price point is not an edge if the product perception is negative. The risks (safety perception, land protests, alternative transport) are not merely uncertainties; they are active threats that the current plan does not mitigate. The plan relies on assumptions about buyer psychology (price over safety) and operational stability (protest resolution) that are unsupported by the provided facts. Without a strategy to overcome the "cheapest = unsafe" stigma or secure the factory site, the upside is unreachable.

DECISION: NO-GO
