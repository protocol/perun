Steelman: Launching in May 1995 secures a four-month head start to establish market presence and lock in early adopters before the competitor arrives in September.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test on multiple fronts. First, the "edge" is illusory. A console without games is a toy; shipping with "few games ready" while the machine is known to be "hard to program for 3D" means the head start yields no compelling software library. Early adopters will buy a hollow box, generate negative word-of-mouth, and wait for the rival's September launch which promises a robust lineup.

Second, the pricing strategy is a fatal flaw. At $399, the unit is $100 (25%) more expensive than the rival. In the mid-90s console market, price sensitivity was extreme. Paying a premium for inferior hardware with no software is commercially irrational.

Third, the channel risk is immediate and severe. Shipping only to "a few retailers" alienates the majority of the distribution network. Unlike digital goods, physical consoles require shelf space and retailer advocacy. Retaliatory exclusion from major chains during the critical holiday season would cripple volume, rendering the early launch a costly footnote rather than a strategic victory.

The pre-mortem is clear: By December 1995, the company holds high inventory, angry retail partners, and a reputation for an overpriced, game-less system, while the competitor captures the holiday season with a full library and lower price point. The "head start" becomes a "head fake" that burns cash and brand equity.

There is no verified evidence that demand exists for an expensive, under-powered device without software. The stated facts (hard to program, few games, high price, angry retailers) directly contradict the core claim that an early launch secures an advantage. The only path to upside requires a full library and broad distribution, neither of which are possible in May under current constraints.

Blockers: High price vs rival, lack of software library, alienated distribution network.
Risks: Brand damage, inventory glut, developer churn.
Edge: NONE (Early availability of an incomplete product is not an advantage).

DECISION: NO-GO
