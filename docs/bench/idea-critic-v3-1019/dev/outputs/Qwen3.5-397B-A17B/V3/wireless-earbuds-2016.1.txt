**Steelman:** Removing the jack forces a transition to wireless audio; launching a premium, seamlessly integrated ecosystem product ($159) captures immediate high-margin revenue and defines the category before competitors, offsetting the loss of bundled wired earphones.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Keep the jack. *Result:* Delays industry shift, maintains low-margin accessory bundle, cedes wireless innovation to rivals.
2.  **Cheaper Alternative:** Launch a basic Bluetooth headset without a case or custom chip. *Result:* Fails to solve "easy to lose" or pairing friction; commoditizes the experience; unlikely to justify $159 or drive brand prestige.
3.  **The Blocker Test:** The proposal cites risks (ridicule, loss, price, battery) but offers no *factual blocker*. These are execution risks, not structural failures.
    *   *Price:* $159 is high vs. free, but free wired earphones have near-zero perceived value. The value prop is convenience, not just audio.
    *   *Battery:* 5 hours is sufficient for most commutes; the case extends total utility.
    *   *Loss/Ridicule:* Design risks are real but manageable via marketing; they do not prevent launch.
    *   *Anger:* Customer backlash is transient if the ecosystem benefit (auto-pairing) is superior.

**Edge:** The manufacturer controls both the phone and the chip. This vertical integration enables "automatic pairing," a user experience competitor accessories cannot match without licensing or complex setup. This is a defensible product advantage, not just "being first."

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it is because the battery life proves unusable for power users or the price point creates a perception of greed without delivering noticeable convenience. However, the proposal includes the charging case to mitigate battery anxiety and the custom chip to ensure convenience.

**Decision Logic:** No factual blocker exists. The risks are known variables, not show-stoppers. The "do nothing" option yields lower strategic upside. The "cheaper" option fails to capture the premium edge provided by the custom chip. The edge (vertical integration/pairing) is specific and defensible.

**Kill Criteria:** If adoption of the wireless buds is <20% of new phone buyers within 6 months, or if return rates due to connectivity issues exceed 5%, pivot to a lower-price tier or include a dongle in the box.

DECISION: GO
