**Steelman:** Acquiring the struggling handset maker secures the primary hardware reference for the niche OS, preventing its defection to the dominant rival and buying time to solve the app ecosystem deficit through vertical integration.

**Attack:** The core failure mode is misdiagnosis. The proposal assumes hardware ownership fixes an app-ecosystem problem. However, developers follow users and revenue, not hardware ownership. With smartphone sales already falling and the rival holding a near-monopoly on apps, buying a declining asset for €5.4 billion burns cash on a shrinking user base without addressing the root cause: lack of developer incentive. The "edge" is negative; the company inherits a losing cost structure and integration drag while the market tip accelerates toward the rival.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids €5.4B cash outflow and integration chaos. The handset maker might still defect, but the acquirer preserves capital to pivot or license the OS elsewhere.
2.  **Licensing/Partnership:** Subsidize other manufacturers to build devices for this OS, spreading risk rather than concentrating it in one failing division.
3.  **The Proposal:** High cost, high integration risk, low probability of reversing the app gap.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-acquisition, the division remains unprofitable, the workforce is bloated, other manufacturers abandon the OS feeling threatened or neglected, and the app gap widens because the total installed base continues to shrink relative to the rival. The €5.4B is written off.

**Blockers:** The proposal claims hardware control stops the manufacturer from switching, but if the platform lacks apps, the hardware cannot sell regardless of ownership. The falling sales trajectory contradicts the assumption that this asset can serve as a viable anchor. There is no evidence that owning the hardware changes the developer calculus if user volume declines.

**Edge:** NONE. Being the owner of a failing handset division is not a competitive advantage against a platform with network effects. Fear of the rival switching is a defensive panic, not a strategic edge.

**Kill Criteria:** If the combined entity does not achieve positive cash flow within 18 months or gain >5% app share growth year-over-year, the division must be spun off or closed.

DECISION: NO-GO
