**Steelman:** A mid-size maker can profitably revive the affordable two-seat roadster by leveraging lightweight simplicity against a market void left by mass-market exit, capturing enthusiast demand before it fully dissipates.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces immediate structural blockers. First, the "affordable" claim contradicts the reality of 1989 safety regulations; complying with rollover and crash standards for a low-volume convertible typically destroys the cost advantage required for an "affordable" price point. Second, the admission that the segment has "shrunk for years" and relies on a finite pool of "early enthusiasts" suggests a rapid sales cliff once initial demand is exhausted, with no mass-market fallback. Third, lacking sports-car heritage means the company cannot command the premium pricing necessary to offset low-volume manufacturing costs, nor does it possess the specific engineering supply chain for rear-drive lightweight chassis without cannibalizing existing profitable lines.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital and avoid the high fixed costs of a niche platform that conflicts with the company's mass-market core competencies.
2.  **Badge Engineering:** Partner with an existing specialist manufacturer to sell a rebadged unit, eliminating R&D risk while testing the "affordable" hypothesis.
3.  **Targa/Hardtop Convertible:** Mitigate safety compliance costs and structural weakening by retaining a fixed roof structure, though this dilutes the "pure roadster" appeal.

**Pre-mortem:**
The car launches to strong press but weak retail conversion. Early enthusiasts buy the first run, but the lack of brand heritage prevents broader adoption. Safety compliance drives the unit price 20% above the "affordable" target, alienating the core demographic. The project bleeds margin on every unit sold, becoming a stranded asset within three years as safety norms tighten further.

**Verdict:**
The proposal fails the "affordable" constraint due to unaddressed regulatory costs and lacks a defensible edge beyond a market gap that exists precisely because it is unprofitable for generalists. The risk of a rapid demand fade combined with no heritage premium creates a negative ROI scenario.

DECISION: NO-GO
