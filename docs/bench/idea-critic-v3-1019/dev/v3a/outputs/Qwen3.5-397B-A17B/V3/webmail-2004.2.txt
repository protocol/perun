Steelman: Offering 1,000x more storage than incumbents with superior search creates a defensible moat that justifies ad-scanning costs through massive user acquisition and engagement.

**Skeptic**: The proposal assumes storage costs will drop fast enough to sustain 1GB free tiers without immediate bankruptcy. It also assumes users will tolerate content scanning despite privacy norms of 2004. However, the "mature market" argument is weak if the product fundamentally changes the utility (search vs. delete). The risk of high server costs is real but mitigated by the invitation-only rollout, allowing controlled scaling.

**Better-Way**: Doing nothing cedes the data advantage to competitors. Alternatives like charging for storage fail to achieve the scale needed for the ad model. The proposed "invitation-only" launch is already the cheapest, safest alternative to a full public beta, acting as a natural throttle on costs while generating demand.

**Kill-Criteria**: The plan fails if the cost per user exceeds the lifetime value of ads before scale is reached. However, the pre-mortem suggests the biggest risk is not cost, but rejection of the privacy model. Yet, if the utility gain (never deleting email, instant search) is high enough, users may trade privacy for convenience. The "no direct revenue" is a feature of the growth stage, not a blocker, provided the ad technology works.

**Blockers**: NONE. The stated risks (cost, privacy, competition) are challenges to be managed, not facts that make the plan impossible. The math works if storage costs follow historical deflation trends and ad relevance is high. The invitation system controls the burn rate.

**Risks**: Privacy backlash could slow adoption; storage costs might not fall as predicted. These are monitored via churn rates and marginal cost per user.

**Decision**: The upside of owning the world's email graph and search index outweighs the operational risks, which are contained by the phased rollout. The proposal beats "do nothing" by creating a new category rather than fighting for scraps in the old one.

DECISION: GO
