**Steelman:** A premium, integrated device leverages a loyal niche to establish a beachhead in digital music, betting that superior user experience outweighs high price and limited initial compatibility, with the hard drive capacity offering a decisive advantage over flash-based competitors.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "addressable base" test. With a small computer market share and no consumer electronics channel, restricting the device to only those computers creates a ceiling too low to justify the $400 price point or the supply chain risk. Cheaper competitors exist; while they sold poorly, this proposal adds friction (compatibility) and cost without solving the core content problem (no legal downloads). The 5GB drive is a technical win, but without a broad software ecosystem or retail presence, it remains a peripheral for a shrinking user base. The "walled garden" strategy requires an existing massive install base to work; here, it compounds the obscurity.

**Better Way:** License the software to Windows immediately to expand the addressable market, or delay launch until a content licensing deal is secured to justify the premium price. Alternatively, reduce capacity and price to compete directly with existing flash players rather than creating a new, expensive category.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to critical acclaim for design but sells fewer than 50,000 units because the total addressable market of compatible computer owners willing to pay $400 is insufficient to cover tooling costs. The company abandons the project within 18 months, writing off the hardware division.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Market Size:** The compatible install base is too small to support the economics of a custom hard-drive supply chain at this price point.
2. **Content Void:** Without legal downloads, the value proposition relies entirely on CD ripping, which cheaper devices already do for less money.

**Risks:** Supply chain delays, brand dilution if the product fails, opportunity cost of diverting engineering resources.

**Verdict:** The constraints (small share, no consumer experience, high price, restricted compatibility) create a fatal mismatch between cost structure and potential revenue. The "integrated experience" argument collapses without a critical mass of users.

DECISION: NO-GO
