Steelman: Acquiring a dormant, high-potential space-fantasy franchise for $4B enables a content engine (films) to drive high-margin downstream revenue (parks, merchandise) across a global entertainment ecosystem, offsetting the loss of the original creator with fresh storytelling.

**Attack & Analysis**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that the $4B price tag cannot be justified if the franchise is creatively dead without its founder. However, the "mixed reviews" of recent entries suggest the brand is damaged but not forgotten; a reset via a new trilogy addresses the creative void directly. The risk of fan rejection is inherent to any revival but is mitigated by the proposal's commitment to a "main trilogy" rather than a one-off, signaling long-term stewardship.

Regarding the financials: while $4B is steep against current film output, the valuation must be weighed against the *total* addressable market of parks and consumer products, not just box office. The fact that much merchandise is already licensed out is actually an asset; it proves existing demand and provides immediate cash flow upon acquisition, rather than requiring new infrastructure build-out. The "do nothing" alternative guarantees the franchise fades into obscurity, yielding zero synergistic value. The "cheaper alternative" of licensing the IP is impossible as the owner is retiring and selling the whole entity.

**Pre-mortem & Kill Criteria**
Failure mode: The new trilogy flops, confirming the franchise was solely dependent on the founder.
Door type: One-way (acquisition is irreversible).
Cost of delay: High. Waiting allows the brand equity to decay further or a competitor to snap up the IP.
The proposal lacks specific kill criteria for the production phase, but as an acquisition decision, the "go" condition rests on the successful closing of the deal and greenlighting the trilogy. The risks (creative lead loss, fan rejection) are managed by the strategy itself (new trilogy), not by avoiding the deal. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal's logic; the high price is the cost of entry for a category-defining asset that feeds the broader business machine.

**Verdict**
No blocker prevents the plan from achieving its upside. The risks are executional, not structural flaws in the acquisition thesis.

DECISION: GO
