The claim assumes positive test market response and "purer" positioning guarantee nationwide success, ignoring that clear cola contradicts the deep-rooted consumer heuristic linking dark color to authentic cola flavor. The strongest attack is that this is a novelty product with high initial trial but near-zero repeat purchase, effectively subsidizing competitors who wait to see if the category sticks.

Alternatives analysis:
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserves flagship margin and avoids brand dilution; cost is zero, risk is missing a trend (reversible if trend proves real later).
2.  **Limited regional rollout:** Tests repeat rates and cannibalization without national marketing spend; lower cost, lower risk, same learning upside.
3.  **Flank brand launch:** Uses a sub-brand to isolate failure from the flagship; higher complexity but protects core equity.

The proposal fails the "beat do nothing" test because it commits massive irreversible marketing spend before validating the core behavioral assumption: that consumers will drink clear liquid expecting cola taste repeatedly. Test markets often overstate demand for novel packaging while understating flavor rejection over time. If shoppers reject the taste after the first buy, the national campaign becomes a sunk cost accelerating brand erosion.

Cannibalization is a confirmed blocker: if the clear cola draws sales from the flagship without expanding the total category, the company incurs higher production/marketing costs for flat revenue. Rivals copying the idea is a risk, but launching first with a flawed product makes the company the lesson rather than the leader.

The pre-mortem reveals a scenario where the launch succeeds in week one (novelty), crashes in week four (taste rejection), and leaves the flagship weakened by association. The cost of delay (waiting for repeat data) is negligible compared to the cost of a failed national launch.

Blockers:
1.  No evidence of repeat purchase rates in test markets; only initial trial is cited.
2.  High probability of net-negative ROI due to cannibalization without category expansion.
3.  Irreversible brand damage if "purer" positioning fails to resonate with taste reality.

Risks: Competitor copies, novelty fade, supply chain complexity.

DECISION: NO-GO
