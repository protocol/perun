Steelman: Leveraging existing search, mail, and video dominance to force-distribute a superior social layer (circles, hangouts) can overcome network effects where standalone attempts failed.

**Skeptic**: The proposal assumes integration equals adoption. History shows three failed standalone attempts; users may perceive forced integration as privacy intrusion or clutter, accelerating churn in core products (search/mail). Tying executive bonuses to adoption creates perverse incentives to spam users or manipulate metrics rather than build genuine value. The "invite-only" beta contradicts the "company-wide push" scale required to challenge a hundreds-of-millions user incumbent immediately.

**Better-Way**: The "do nothing" option preserves core product sentiment. A cheaper alternative is acquiring a niche social player or licensing features rather than rebuilding the entire stack across four massive products simultaneously. A staged rollout (one product first) reduces blast radius compared to integrating search, mail, video, and photos at once.

**Kill-Criteria**: This is a one-way door for brand reputation; if core utility (search/email) degrades, recovery is difficult. The cost of delay is low; the incumbent is not threatening the core search business today. Pre-mortem: The launch fails because users create accounts solely to access hangouts but do not migrate their social graph, leaving a "ghost town" feed. The proposal lacks a specific metric to define success beyond "adoption," which is vague.

**Blockers**: 
1. **Contradiction**: The plan relies on "forced integration" to solve network effects, yet admits users "may not switch" and have previously rejected three social attempts. If the core value proposition failed standalone, bundling it with essential tools risks contaminating those tools without guaranteeing social retention.
2. **Incentive Misalignment**: Tying bonuses to adoption guarantees short-term artificial growth (spammy invites) that destroys long-term engagement, directly undermining the goal of challenging the market leader.
3. **Scope Risk**: Launching across four major products simultaneously creates a single point of failure for the entire company's user experience.

The plan prioritizes executive compensation structures over product-market fit verification and ignores the specific reason previous attempts failed.

DECISION: NO-GO
