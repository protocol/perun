**Steelman:** This acquisition instantly transforms a legacy hardware manufacturer into a software leader, bypassing years of organic development to capture high-margin enterprise analytics revenue immediately.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the fundamental test of value creation. Paying an 11x revenue multiple with a 64% premium is predicated on the target's financials being accurate. However, the premise is contradicted by verified facts: the target's accounting has been publicly questioned since 2009, and the buyer's own Finance Chief reserves judgment on the price. Proceeding with a "few weeks" of due diligence on a $11.7 billion deal involving complex software revenue recognition and known accounting irregularities is operationally impossible. The risk is not merely integration difficulty; it is the high probability of purchasing impaired assets or fraud. A hardware company lacking software expertise cannot reliably validate the target's core asset (its code and data integrity) in such a short window, especially when internal financial experts already flag the cost. The "do nothing" alternative preserves capital for smaller, verifiable builds or acquisitions with clean audits. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a catastrophic write-down.

**Blockers:**
1. **Contradictory Evidence:** The proposal assumes the target is worth $11.7B, yet cited evidence (press reports since 2009, internal CFO reservations) directly undermines the validity of the financials used to derive that value.
2. **Impossible Verification:** The timeline ("a few weeks") is insufficient to resolve known accounting ambiguities for a deal of this magnitude, making an informed go/no-go decision impossible today.

**Risks:** Total loss of capital if accounting fraud is confirmed post-close; culture clash destroying target value; inability to manage software lifecycle.

**Kill Criteria:** Halt immediately if independent forensic accounting cannot verify revenue quality within 48 hours. If the CFO cannot sign off on the valuation model without caveats, the deal dies.

**Decision:** The combination of a record-breaking premium, known accounting red flags, internal executive dissent, and an inadequate due diligence timeline creates an unmitigated blocker. The plan relies on assumptions proven fragile by available evidence.

DECISION: NO-GO
