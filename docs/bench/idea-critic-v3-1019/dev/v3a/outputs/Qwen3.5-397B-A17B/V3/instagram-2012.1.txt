**Steelman:** Acquire the leading mobile-native photo network immediately to neutralize a existential threat to the core business and secure the mobile transition before the target's IPO makes it unattainable or strengthens a competitor.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the valuation: $1 billion for a dozen employees with zero revenue defies traditional financial logic. However, the "revenue" here is strategic defense. The alternative—building a competing mobile product internally—is rejected by the proposal's own admission that the buyer's current mobile product is weak and the target is already the "leading mobile threat." History shows internal attempts to replicate network effects often fail due to speed and culture mismatches.

"Do nothing" carries the highest risk: the target goes public, raises capital, and potentially partners with or is acquired by a direct rival, cementing the buyer's irrelevance in mobile. The cost of delay is the loss of the entire mobile future.

**Pre-mortem & Kill Criteria:**
If this fails, it will be because the buyer's corporate culture suffocated the startup's agility, causing key talent to flee and users to disengage. The integration must remain arm's length.
*   **Kill Criterion:** If more than 3 of the 12 key employees resign within 6 months post-close, the acquisition has failed its primary purpose (talent/culture acquisition) and requires immediate strategic pivot.
*   **Leading Metric:** Daily Active Users (DAU) growth rate of the acquired app must remain ≥90% of pre-acquisition trends for 12 months.

**Verification of Objections:**
The lack of a business model is irrelevant if the asset prevents a competitor from monopolizing the mobile ad market. The regulatory risk is real but manageable compared to the competitive extinction risk. The valuation is high, but the cost of *not* buying (losing the mobile war) is infinite. The facts provided indicate no other viable path to mobile leadership exists at this speed.

**Decision:**
The proposal identifies a clear, immediate existential threat where the cost of inaction exceeds the massive financial outlay. The only viable alternative (build) is explicitly stated as non-competitive. While the cultural risk is severe, it is a management execution issue, not a deal-breaker. The strategic necessity overrides the financial absurdity.

DECISION: GO
