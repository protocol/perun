**Steelman:** Paying $19B secures the dominant mobile messaging layer, neutralizing an existential threat to the buyer's core business and capturing hundreds of millions of engaged users before a competitor does.

**Skeptic Attack:** The price represents a >12x increase over the prior $1.5B valuation in just one year, with no revenue model, no advertising history, and a tiny staff of 50 to integrate. The unit economics are indefensible by traditional standards; the bet relies entirely on the assumption that messaging is the future of social interaction and that the target will not lose users post-acquisition.

**Better Way:** Building a competing messenger internally or acquiring a smaller, cheaper rival would cost a fraction of the price. However, given the target's massive, fast-growing user base, organic growth or smaller acquisitions likely cannot match the defensive speed required. "Do nothing" risks the buyer becoming irrelevant on mobile.

**Kill Criteria & Pre-mortem:** If user growth stalls or founders leave within 12 months, the asset becomes a costly ghost town. The one-way door nature of this spend means failure cannot be undone. The lack of a monetization plan is a critical gap; without a clear path to extract value beyond raw user count, the ROI is purely speculative defensive insurance.

**Decision Logic:** While the financials appear absurd in isolation, the strategic context (fear of displacement) frames this as a survival cost rather than an investment. The "blocker" of no revenue is mitigated if the acquisition prevents the core business from collapsing. However, the sheer scale of the premium ($19B vs $1.5B) without a binding retention agreement for founders or a concrete integration plan presents an unmanaged risk of total capital loss. The proposal lacks specific kill criteria for founder departure or user churn thresholds that would trigger a stop.

**Verdict:** The strategic necessity outweighs the financial insanity, but only if founder retention is guaranteed. As proposed, with "founder-retention" listed merely as a risk rather than a pre-conditioned deal term, the exposure is too high.

DECISION: NO-GO
