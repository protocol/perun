**Verdict: PASS_TO_USER**

**Steelman:** Acquiring a small, open-source mobile OS team for $50M is a strategic hedge to secure future search distribution as mobile usage grows, bypassing carrier and handset maker gatekeepers by offering a free, neutral platform.

**Analysis:**
The proposal faces immediate skepticism: a search company lacks hardware DNA, the $50M price tag is high for a six-person team with no revenue, and the mobile market is hostile to new entrants. However, the "do nothing" alternative carries existential risk. If mobile web usage explodes (a high-probability trend given 2005 trajectories) and this company does not control the interface, its core search ad revenue will be throttled by carriers or incumbent OS vendors.

The "better way" of licensing an existing OS fails because current leaders (Symbian, Windows Mobile) are entrenched and unlikely to prioritize a search competitor's interests. Building internally is too slow; the window to define the mobile web is closing. The open-source model directly addresses the adoption barrier: handset makers get a free, customizable OS, solving their cost problem while giving the acquirer the distribution needed for search.

**Pre-mortem & Kill Criteria:**
If this fails, it will be because handset makers still refuse to adopt despite the price, or the team cannot scale the OS to consumer standards. The kill criterion is clear: if no major handset manufacturer commits to a device using this OS within 18 months of acquisition, the project must be pivoted or shuttered. The leading metric is the number of committed device SKUs from top-10 manufacturers.

**Strongest Attack Survived:**
The sharpest objection is that $50M is a massive overpayment for "vaporware" from six people. This survives because the value isn't the current code, but the *option value* of controlling the mobile gateway before the market consolidates. Waiting for proof of adoption would mean paying billions later or being locked out entirely. The cost of delay (losing the mobile search market) exceeds the cost of the acquisition.

**Dissent Ledger:**
Risk of culture clash between ad-tech and OS engineers; risk that carriers block non-approved OSs.

**Remaining Risk:**
Execution risk in scaling a tiny team to ship a competitive product.

DECISION: GO
