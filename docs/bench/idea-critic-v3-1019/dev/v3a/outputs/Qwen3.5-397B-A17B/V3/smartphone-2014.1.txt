The claim is that a differentiated display and shopping feature can overcome platform dominance, high price, and app scarcity to succeed in a saturated 2014 market.

**Skeptic:** The proposal assumes users value 3D navigation and visual search enough to abandon the "leading platform's own mainstream apps." In 2014, mobile utility is defined by app ecosystem completeness (social, banking, messaging). A forked OS with a smaller store creates an immediate utility deficit that a novelty display cannot offset for mass-market users. The "unsubsidised-equivalent price" places this device in direct competition with established flagships that offer full app support, making the value proposition negative.

**Better-way:** The "do nothing" or "stick to tablets/e-readers" option preserves capital and avoids the massive sunk cost of building a phone ecosystem from scratch. A cheaper alternative is licensing the dominant OS to ensure app compatibility, though this removes the "fork" control. Another path is integrating the "one-touch shopping" feature into existing successful hardware (tablets/e-readers) rather than risking it on a new, unproven phone form factor.

**Kill-criteria:** The market is saturated with two dominant platforms; entering as a third, exclusive, app-poor, high-priced option violates the basic adoption curve. The pre-mortem is clear: carriers will not subsidize a phone users reject due to missing apps, and early adopters will return it once the novelty of 3D wears off without daily app utility. The contradiction is explicit: the proposal targets a mass market ("leading flagship" price point) while removing the primary driver of mass-market smartphone adoption (the mainstream app ecosystem).

**Blockers:** 
1. **App Gap:** Missing mainstream apps at a flagship price point is a factual blocker to adoption in 2014.
2. **Carrier Exclusivity:** Limits reach precisely when network effects are needed to overcome the app deficit.
3. **Price vs. Value:** Unsubsidized pricing for an inferior ecosystem offers no rational purchase trigger.

**Risks:** High return rates, brand damage to the retailer, and stranded inventory.

DECISION: NO-GO
