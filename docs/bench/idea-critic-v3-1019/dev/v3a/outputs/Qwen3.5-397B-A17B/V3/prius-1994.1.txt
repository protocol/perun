**Steelman:** Committing now secures a decade-long lead in hybrid technology, forcing competitors to chase while defining the segment before regulations mandate it.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on three verified facts. First, with cheap fuel and no regulatory mandate, the economic value proposition for the customer is negative; the premium cost cannot be recouped in fuel savings, killing near-term demand. Second, the core technologies (power-split transmission, NiMH packs) are unproven at volume; a premature public schedule invites catastrophic warranty exposure and brand damage if reliability falters. Third, being first offers no advantage if the market does not exist; "no competitor" signals a lack of viable economics, not an open field.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Preserve capital, avoid warranty risk, wait for fuel prices or regulations to create demand.
2. *Limited Pilot:* Develop the technology quietly for fleet/government use to prove durability without a bold public consumer launch.
3. *Proposed Plan:* High cost, high risk, zero near-term revenue.

**Pre-mortem:** By 1998, the car launches to tepid sales due to high price, suffers early battery failures requiring costly recalls, and the program is canceled as a financial drain, setting back hybrid development by years.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Economic Mismatch:** Cheap fuel removes the primary user incentive; the math does not work for mass adoption today.
2. **Technology Readiness:** Unproven durability at volume combined with a fixed public deadline creates an unacceptable risk of failure.
3. **Demand Signal:** Explicitly stated as absent; launching without demand is speculation, not strategy.

**Risks:** Warranty insolvency, brand reputation collapse, capital misallocation.

**Conclusion:** This is a one-way door with a high probability of irreversible financial and reputational damage. The plan relies on creating a market that currently has no economic reason to exist, using technology that has not earned the right to a public deadline. The "bold schedule" is the specific mechanism that turns technical risk into corporate failure. Wait for external drivers (regulation/fuel price) or de-risk via non-public pilots.

DECISION: NO-GO
