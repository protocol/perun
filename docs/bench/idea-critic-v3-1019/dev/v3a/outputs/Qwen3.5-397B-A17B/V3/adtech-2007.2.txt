**Steelman:** Acquiring the agency and platform instantly grants scale to challenge the search leader's emerging dominance, preventing permanent exclusion from the display market.

**Attack:** The $6 billion cash price represents a massive premium in a fast-changing sector, risking severe value destruction if technology shifts or integration fails. Conflicts with existing agency customers could erode the buyer's core B2B trust, while the "fast-changing" nature of display ads suggests the purchased tech may become obsolete quickly, leaving the buyer with an expensive, stranded asset. The reactive motive ("competitor just agreed") indicates strategic panic rather than calculated growth.

**Better Way:** Organic development or smaller, targeted technology tuck-ins avoid cultural clashes and customer conflicts while allowing the market to mature before committing vast capital. Doing nothing preserves cash for proven search dominance rather than gambling on an unproven display future.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If the acquisition fails, it will be due to customer churn from conflicts and rapid tech obsolescence. This is a one-way door with immense delay costs if the competitor secures the market, but the cost of a failed $6B integration is existential.

**Blockers:** None explicitly proven in the text; the price and conflicts are severe risks, not yet confirmed failures. However, the lack of evidence that conflicts can be managed or that the premium is justified by current cash flows makes the proposal dangerously speculative.

**Risks:** Customer attrition, tech obsolescence, integration failure, overpayment.

**Kill Criteria:** If customer churn exceeds 15% within 12 months or if the platform's market share does not grow by 10% in 18 months, divest immediately.

**Decision:** The risks of customer conflict and tech obsolescence at this premium are too high without a verified mitigation plan. The reactive nature suggests poor strategic discipline.
