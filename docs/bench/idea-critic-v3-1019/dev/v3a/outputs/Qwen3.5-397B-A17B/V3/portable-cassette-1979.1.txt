The claim is that a playback-only, headphone-centric cassette player will succeed with youth despite dealer skepticism, lack of market research, and high initial volume requirements.

**Steelman:** By stripping the speaker and recording mechanism, the device achieves a price point and portability unattainable by existing hi-fi systems, creating a new "personal music" category where the lack of recording is a feature (simplicity/cost), not a bug.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Dealer/Staff Doubt:** This is an incentive mismatch, not a product flaw. Staff sell what they understand (hi-fi); the target user wants mobility. Bypassing traditional demo logic is required.
2.  **Antisocial Perception:** In 1979, "listening alone" is a risk. However, the alternative (portable radios) lacks audio fidelity and choice. The value proposition is *curated* personal sound, not isolation.
3.  **No Market Research:** Waiting for data guarantees failure; the product defines the category. The "do nothing" alternative preserves current margins but cedes the emerging youth mobility segment to future competitors.
4.  **Copycat Risk:** Using existing parts is a double-edged sword: it lowers R&D risk but invites imitation. The defense is speed to market and brand association with quality hi-fi, which generic copiers lack.
5.  **Large Production Run:** This is the only true financial blocker. If the price target isn't met, the value prop collapses. However, the components are commoditized; the risk is inventory, not technical feasibility.

**Pre-mortem:** The product fails if marketing positions it as a "cheap tape recorder" rather than a "lifestyle accessory." If sold next to standard recorders, it looks broken. It must be sold as fashion/tech hybrid.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blocker Check:** No factual contradiction exists. The lack of research is a strategic gamble, not a logical impossibility. The high volume requirement is a constraint, not a stopper, provided the price point is strictly adhered to.
*   **Risk vs. Upside:** The risk of copying is mitigated by first-mover brand dominance. The risk of dealer doubt is mitigated by direct-to-consumer lifestyle marketing. The upside is creating an entirely new mass market.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If the first run does not sell through in 6 months, halt production. Leading metric: Units sold per capita in the 15-25 demographic.

The proposal survives the attack because the objections are based on legacy mental models (recorders must record, music must be shared), whereas the opportunity lies in breaking those models.

DECISION: GO
