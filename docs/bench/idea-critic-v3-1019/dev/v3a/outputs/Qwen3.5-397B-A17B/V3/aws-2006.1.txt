**Steelman:** Leverage existing, underutilized spiky infrastructure to create a high-margin revenue stream that amortizes fixed capital costs, turning a cost center into a profit center while diversifying beyond low-margin retail.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces severe headwinds: no internal expertise in developer platforms, unproven market demand for hourly compute in 2006, and active investor skepticism regarding capital intensity. The "distraction" risk is tangible; failing at a complex B2B service could damage the core retail brand's reliability reputation. Established hardware vendors possess deep enterprise relationships this retailer lacks.

However, the **better-way** analysis reveals that "doing nothing" leaves expensive capacity idle during non-peak retail periods, wasting capital. Building a dedicated new infrastructure would be fatal; the only viable path is leveraging the *existing* build-out. The "unproven market" is a risk, but also a first-mover opportunity if the price point is disruptive. The lack of expertise is a hiring/execution risk, not a fundamental flaw in the unit economics.

**Kill Criteria & Pre-mortem:**
If the company cannot achieve 60% utilization of the new capacity within 18 months, or if customer support costs for developer issues exceed 20% of revenue, the venture dilutes rather than adds value. A pre-mortem suggests failure comes from treating this as a side project rather than a distinct business with its own P&L and talent, leading to reliability outages that anger both retailers and developers.

**Decision Logic:**
There is no factual **blocker**. The capital is already spent (sunk cost context), the infrastructure exists, and the marginal cost of selling excess capacity is low. The risks (execution, market fit) are real but manageable through strict kill criteria and isolated ownership. The potential upside—transforming fixed costs into variable revenue and dominating a nascent category—outweighs the risk of distraction, provided the venture is ring-fenced. The "unproven" nature of the market is the very reason to enter now before incumbents react.

DECISION: GO
