The proposal faces immediate, structural blockers that cannot be resolved by a translation layer or a two-year timeline.

First, the **performance contradiction**: The core driver is the current supplier's failure to deliver "cool and efficient" chips for thin laptops. However, the proposal admits translated software will run slower. If native performance is already marginal for thermal constraints, adding a translation overhead will likely exacerbate heat and battery issues, directly undermining the primary goal of the migration. The math of "slower software" on a new architecture does not automatically equal "better efficiency" in the thin form factor without proof of a massive architectural leap that outweighs the emulation tax.

Second, the **distinctiveness paradox**: The brand's value is tied to its unique processor family. Moving to the "dominant PC processor architecture" explicitly erodes this differentiation, making the hardware commoditized. The proposal offers no strategy to replace this lost brand equity, relying instead on the hope that "efficient" matters more than "unique." This is an unverified assumption that risks alienating the core user base who value the ecosystem's distinctiveness.

Third, the **sales freeze risk** is treated as a manageable risk rather than a blocker. Announcing a full platform shift two years in advance creates a "wait-and-see" market reaction that can crater current revenue streams immediately. Given the company's history of a "painful" earlier transition, the organizational capacity to execute a seamless switch while maintaining sales momentum is unproven. The translation layer is a stopgap, not a solution for developers who must still rebuild software, creating a fragmented ecosystem during the transition.

The claim that this move secures the future relies on the unproven premise that the dominant architecture's efficiency gains will outstrip the performance penalty of translation and the revenue loss from sales freezes. Without evidence that the new chips offer a >50% efficiency gain to absorb the translation overhead, the plan fails its own logic.

DECISION: NO-GO
