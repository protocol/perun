**Steelman:** Leveraging mobile dominance to capture the handheld market by converging devices at a premium price point, betting on brand loyalty over dedicated utility.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "do nothing" and "cheaper alternative" tests immediately. The core value proposition (gaming) is actively degraded by the hardware design: removing the battery to swap games destroys user experience compared to the market leader's seamless cartridge system. The form factor compromises both primary functions (awkward for calls, inferior for gaming). At $300, it is significantly more expensive than the dominant competitor while offering a smaller library and uncommitted publishers. The "convergence" here is a compromise that satisfies neither phone users nor gamers.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: Gamers reject a device that makes their hobby inconvenient and overpriced; phone users ignore the gaming feature. The brand strength in phones does not transfer to gaming when the execution is objectively worse than the specialist incumbent.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Functional Regression:** The requirement to remove the battery to change games is a fundamental usability flaw that no amount of marketing can fix. It contradicts the basic expectation of a handheld console.
2. **Price-Value Mismatch:** Charging a premium ($300) for a device that performs worse than the cheaper market leader in its primary secondary function (gaming) ensures low adoption.
3. **Ecosystem Void:** Without committed publishers, the hardware has no content, rendering the platform dead on arrival regardless of phone sales volume.

**Risks:** High inventory write-downs; brand dilution in the mobile sector due to association with a failed gaming product; opportunity cost of not focusing on core mobile improvements.

**Decision Logic:** This is a "no-go" because the product as described is objectively inferior to the status quo for the target use case (gaming) while being more expensive. It does not beat "do nothing" or "wait for better technology." The flaws are not risks to be managed; they are design failures that prevent the product from achieving its stated goal.

DECISION: NO-GO
