**Verdict: HOLD (Internal Revision Required)**

**Claim:** Launch a $10,000 graphical business computer for office workers in 1983.
**Steelman:** A premium, mouse-driven interface justifies a 3-4x price premium over text-based competitors by drastically improving usability for non-technical staff.

**Attack Summary:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $10,000, the unit cost is prohibitive for widespread office deployment compared to established $2,000–$3,000 text-based terminals. The value proposition (GUI) is unproven in this sector.
2.  **Performance Risk:** The proposal admits the machine "runs slowly." In a business context, slow performance directly reduces employee productivity, negating the efficiency gains promised by the graphical interface.
3.  **Ecosystem Void:** "Little third-party software" means the hardware is a solution without problems. Businesses buy computers to run specific applications (spreadsheets, word processing), not for the interface itself.
4.  **Internal Cannibalization:** A cheaper, similar internal alternative exists. Launching the expensive model first risks market confusion and renders the upcoming cheaper unit obsolete before it launches, or vice versa, wasting R&D on the premium unit.

**Better Way:**
Delay the $10,000 launch. Prioritize the cheaper internal alternative to establish the software ecosystem and user familiarity with the mouse/interface at an accessible price point. Use the premium model only for specialized high-end workstations if performance can be improved.

**Kill Criteria:**
Do not proceed unless: (1) Performance benchmarks match or exceed text-based competitors; (2) At least three critical business applications are ready at launch; (3) A clear migration path from the cheaper internal alternative is defined to prevent cannibalization.

**Strongest Attack Survived:**
The argument that "premium features justify premium pricing" fails because the core utility (speed and software availability) is inferior to cheaper alternatives. Usability cannot compensate for inability to perform basic tasks quickly.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal fails the blocker test on price-to-performance ratio and ecosystem readiness. Launching now guarantees commercial failure and internal conflict.

DECISION: NO-GO
