The claim is that an annual membership fee will offset increased shipping costs and drive loyalty. The steelman is that locking in customer lifetime value through upfront cash flow and increased order frequency creates a defensible moat, even if per-order margins dip.

However, the "better way" analysis reveals a critical flaw in the proposed economics without a pilot. The proposal admits economics are unproven yet suggests a full launch. A cheaper, safer alternative is a limited beta: offer the membership to only 5% of customers or for a single quarter. This validates the "heavy buyer" hypothesis without exposing the entire retailer to catastrophic margin erosion if the actuarial assumptions fail.

The pre-mortem is clear: if heavy buyers increase frequency by 40% but the fee covers only 20% extra shipping, the company bleeds cash on its best customers. The "one-way door" risk is real; reversing a promised annual benefit destroys trust permanently. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed full-scale launch.

Crucially, the proposal lacks a specific kill criterion or a capped experiment. It asks for a binary go/no-go on an unproven financial model with irreversible customer expectations. The skepticism regarding "unproven economics" is a blocker, not just a risk, because the proposal offers no mechanism to validate the core assumption before full commitment. The plan fails the "cheaper alternative" test because it skips the validation step entirely.

To proceed, the plan must be revised to include a time-boxed, capped pilot with a specific metric for success (e.g., "membership fee must cover 110% of incremental shipping costs after 90 days"). Without this, the risk of structural loss outweighs the potential upside. The current proposal is a gamble, not a strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
