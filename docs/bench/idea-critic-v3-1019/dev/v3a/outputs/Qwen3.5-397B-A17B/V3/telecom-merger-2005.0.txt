The claim is that merging with the smaller carrier creates sufficient value to justify a $36 billion expenditure despite severe technological friction. The steelman: acquiring the target's sticky, high-margin business segment (construction push-to-talk) justifies the integration burden because organic replication is impossible or too slow, and the revenue stream offsets the legacy drag.

However, the "better-way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the proposed structure. The networks use incompatible technologies; the target is data-limited. In 2005, the industry trajectory is unequivocally toward data and broadband convergence, not voice-centric legacy systems. Buying a "data-limited" network for $36 billion forces the acquirer to run parallel, incompatible infrastructures indefinitely or undertake a prohibitively expensive, high-churn migration of specialized hardware (ruggedized handsets) that defines the very value being bought. The "do nothing" or "partner" alternative captures the revenue share via MVNO or API integration without assuming $36 billion in goodwill risk or the operational nightmare of maintaining two divergent physical layers.

The pre-mortem is clear: integration costs spiral as the legacy network requires distinct maintenance teams and spectrum refarming delays. The "high-value" customers churn when forced onto the buyer's inferior data platform or when handset replacements become cost-prohibitive. The goodwill becomes an impairment charge within two years. The incompatibility is not a minor hurdle; it is a structural blocker to the synergies usually required to justify such a premium. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a botched integration that alienates the exact niche being purchased.

The strongest attack survived is the uniqueness of the customer base, but this does not justify the *method* of acquisition proposed. Buying the whole incompatible network is a blunt instrument that threatens the buyer's core balance sheet for a segment that could be accessed more cheaply. The risks are not merely "execution difficulty"; they are fundamental economic mismatches between the purchase price, the asset's technological trajectory, and the integration reality.

DECISION: NO-GO
