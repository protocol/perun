**Steelman:** A mid-size manufacturer can profitably capture a neglected niche by launching a low-cost, lightweight, front-engine, rear-drive convertible, leveraging simplified engineering to offset low volume and lack of heritage.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal correctly identifies a market gap but fails to prove the economic viability of filling it. The "affordable" price point directly conflicts with the rising costs of safety compliance for convertibles in the late 1980s. Without heritage, the brand cannot command a premium to absorb these costs, yet "affordable" implies thin margins.

The "shrinking segment" risk is critical. If mass-market makers have exited, it suggests the total addressable market (TAM) has fallen below the break-even volume for a dedicated platform. Relying on "early enthusiasts" creates a one-time sales spike followed by a cliff, failing to sustain production lines. A two-seat limitation further caps the TAM, making the business model fragile against economic downturns.

**Better Way:**
Instead of a clean-sheet launch, the company should explore a low-capital derivation from an existing rear-drive platform to share tooling costs, or license an existing design to test the waters before committing to full production. "Do nothing" remains strong if the TAM cannot support the fixed costs of a new assembly line.

**Kill Criteria & Pre-mortem:**
Assume failure: The car launches, initial orders are filled by enthusiasts, but repeat sales vanish within 18 months because the car lacks daily practicality and the brand lacks prestige. The factory runs at 40% capacity, bleeding cash.
*Blocker:* The proposal offers no evidence that the "affordable" price covers the unit cost given low volumes and safety mandates. It assumes demand exists without proving the specific buyer profile is large enough to sustain a mid-size maker's operations.

**Verdict:**
The plan relies on optimistic assumptions about volume and pricing in a segment abandoned by larger competitors for structural reasons. Without a confirmed path to profitability despite low volumes, or a strategy to extend the product lifecycle beyond the initial enthusiast wave, the financial risk is unacceptable.

DECISION: NO-GO
