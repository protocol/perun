**Steelman:** Selling individual songs at 99 cents with light DRM, backed by major label licenses, creates a legal, convenient alternative to piracy that leverages the company's dominant hardware ecosystem, even if initially restricted to its own computers.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that consumers will not pay for what they can steal for free, especially at a price point with thin margins. However, the "free" alternative carries high friction: search difficulty, poor quality, malware risks, and legal exposure. A curated, instant-gratification store solves these pain points. The restriction to own-brand computers is not a bug but a feature in 2003; it drives hardware differentiation and locks in high-value users who already own the player.

Regarding the subscription rival model: in 2003, broadband penetration is insufficient for reliable streaming, and storage costs make downloading entire albums prohibitive for many. The per-song model matches current user behavior (buying singles) and technical constraints better than an all-you-can-eat subscription.

The margin risk is real but secondary to ecosystem growth. The goal is not immediate software profit but sustaining hardware premiums. If labels withdraw licenses, the store fails, but the proposal secures them *before* launch, mitigating this. The "light" copy protection balances label security demands with user usability, avoiding the backlash of unplayable files.

**Pre-mortem:** The plan fails if the interface is clunky or if labels demand crippling DRM that ruins the user experience. However, the proposal specifies "light" protection and leverages a company known for usability. The risk of rivals winning with subscriptions is low given the infrastructure gap of the era.

**Verdict:**
No factual blocker exists. The claim that users won't pay is contradicted by the value of convenience and safety. The hardware restriction aligns with the strategic north star of selling more players. The economics rely on ecosystem lift, not per-song margin, which is a sound strategic trade.

**Kill Criteria:** If major labels refuse licensing terms within 6 months, or if initial attach rates (songs per user) remain below 1.5 after the first quarter, pivot to negotiation or alternative content models.

DECISION: GO
