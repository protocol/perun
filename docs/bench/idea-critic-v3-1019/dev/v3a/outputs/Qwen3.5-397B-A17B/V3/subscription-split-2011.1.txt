Steelman: Separating the declining DVD asset from the high-growth streaming brand allows distinct strategies and prevents the legacy business from dragging down the valuation and focus of the future platform.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The timing creates a fatal contradiction. The company just imposed a 60% price increase on combined users (July 2011). Immediately following this financial hit with a fracturing of the user experience—forcing two logins, two bills, and a new brand name (September 2011)—compounds the perceived hostility. Customers do not see "strategic focus"; they see a bait-and-switch where they pay significantly more for a fragmented, inferior service.

The stated risks are not merely potential downsides; they are guaranteed outcomes that directly undermine the core claim. The proposal argues streaming needs freedom to pursue its strategy, yet this move alienates the existing subscriber base required to fund that growth. By severing ratings and recommendations, the company destroys the unique value proposition of the combined plan without offering a compensatory benefit, effectively telling loyal users their data and convenience are worthless.

A "better way" exists: maintain the unified brand and account structure while internally managing the businesses separately. This achieves the operational and strategic separation required for management focus without inflicting immediate, compounding friction on the customer. The current proposal prioritizes internal accounting convenience over customer retention during a period of extreme price sensitivity.

The specific blocker is the collision of the recent 60% price hike with the immediate imposition of high-friction separation (dual accounts/billing). This combination creates an unforced error that invites mass churn and reputational damage, contradicting the goal of sustainable growth for the streaming unit. The plan as proposed assumes customers will accept reduced utility for higher costs, which the market conditions of September 2011 suggest is false.

DECISION: NO-GO
