The claim is that a full rewrite with altered voting mechanics and a forced immediate migration will reverse slowing traffic. The steelman is that only a radical break from the current architecture and community-driven curation can modernize the platform enough to compete with a growing rival.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The facts state the community is "loyal" and specifically "values the existing voting mechanics." The proposal explicitly changes these mechanics. In social platforms, changing the core engagement loop for a loyal user base without validation is a primary cause of exodus, not retention. If the community leaves due to mechanic changes, the "publisher-favored" content has no audience, rendering the traffic goal impossible.

Furthermore, the "move all users at once" approach creates a one-way door with no rollback. Given the "large engineering risk" of a full rewrite, combining unproven product changes with a high-risk technical deployment and zero fallback violates basic risk management. The growing rival suggests speed is important, but a catastrophic launch is slower than a staged rollout.

The proposal offers no evidence that the current mechanics are the cause of slowing traffic versus content fatigue or external market shifts. It assumes the solution is a total overhaul rather than iterative testing. A cheaper, safer alternative exists: A/B test the new surfacing logic on a segment of users while keeping the rewrite modular, allowing rollback if engagement drops.

Because the proposal ignores the stated value of the current mechanics, combines high technical risk with a no-rollback deployment, and lacks a validation step before forcing changes on the entire loyal base, it contains a fatal flaw. The risk of alienating the core community is a verified blocker based on the proposal's own facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
