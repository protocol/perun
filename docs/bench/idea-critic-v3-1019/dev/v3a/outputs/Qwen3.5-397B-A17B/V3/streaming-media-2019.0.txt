Steelman: Direct-to-consumer distribution captures full lifetime value and data ownership, escaping dependency on rival platforms that may eventually exclude the library entirely.

However, the claim fails the blocker test on three verified facts within the proposal itself. First, the financial math contradicts the "low price" strategy. Funding a "multi-year operating loss" while targeting "tens of millions" of subscribers against a leader with "over a hundred million" creates a cash burn magnitude that likely exceeds the present value of the guaranteed licensing revenue being sacrificed. The proposal admits to losing billions in immediate, high-margin licensing fees to chase speculative, low-margin subscription revenue in a saturated market.

Second, the execution risk is a factual blocker, not a mere risk. The company has "never run" a technology platform of this scale. Building a global streaming infrastructure capable of competing with established leaders while simultaneously managing a "multi-year loss" introduces a single point of failure that threatens the entire conglomerate's liquidity.

Third, the cannibalization of existing cable and theatrical revenue is acknowledged but unmitigated. Pulling content from rivals directly attacks the company's own profitable distribution windows (cable) and devalues theatrical exclusivity, eroding the core business before the new venture proves viable.

The "do nothing" or "hybrid" alternative (licensing while building a smaller, premium niche service) offers a safer path to digital presence without torching the balance sheet. The proposed "all-in" low-price strategy requires assumptions about subscriber acquisition costs and churn rates that are not supported by the provided facts; indeed, the facts suggest the opposite: a costly war of attrition against a dominant incumbent with no clear path to profitability within the stated timeframe.

The contradiction is stark: the plan relies on sacrificing certain, massive current revenue for uncertain future growth while admitting to a lack of core operational capability (tech platform) and accepting self-cannibalization. This is not a calculated bet; it is a structural threat to the conglomerate's solvency.

DECISION: NO-GO
