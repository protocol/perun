Steelman: Combining German engineering prestige with US mass-market profitability creates a diversified global leader capable of sharing platforms to reduce costs while accessing complementary customer bases.

However, the "merger of equals" structure is a fatal flaw disguised as diplomacy. In practice, it creates a governance vacuum where neither party has decisive authority, leading to strategic paralysis when cultures collide. The proposal explicitly admits to "very different corporate and labour cultures" yet offers no mechanism to resolve them other than a combined board, which will likely fracture along national lines.

The financial logic relies on the US partner's current high profitability in trucks and SUVs. This is a cyclical peak, not a structural guarantee. Basing a permanent structural merger on a transient market boom introduces massive downside risk; when the light-truck cycle turns, the combined entity loses its primary cash engine while retaining the high fixed costs of the German premium operations.

Furthermore, "platform sharing" between a premium brand and a mass-market producer often results in brand dilution for the luxury maker and margin compression for the mass maker, rather than the promised synergies. The proposal fails to address how to prevent the premium brand's value from eroding when associated with volume manufacturing.

The "merger of equals" is historically a precursor to a hostile takeover or a slow-motion divorce, not a stable operating model. Without a clear single decision-maker and a hedge against the US cyclical downturn, this deal structure guarantees conflict. The risks are not merely "concerns"; they are structural blockers that invalidate the core premise of stable global scale. The lack of a defined integration authority means the inevitable cultural clashes will have no resolution path, stalling the entire organization.

This is not a sound basis for immediate approval. The governance structure must be scrapped in favor of a clear acquirer/acquired dynamic, and the valuation must stress-test a severe downturn in the US truck market before proceeding. As proposed, it is a recipe for value destruction.

DECISION: NO-GO
