Steelman: A bold visual refresh will modernize the brand, signaling quality and attracting new buyers without altering the product, leveraging a massive ad campaign to override old habits.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The primary function of the carton's current graphic is immediate shelf recognition. The leading cue—the orange with a straw—is being removed entirely. The risks identified are not merely "maybes"; they are structural flaws in the plan. The buyer base is explicitly described as "loyal and older," a demographic that relies heavily on established visual patterns for routine purchases. Removing their primary navigation cue while simultaneously changing the typography (vertical rotation) and form factor emphasis (glass vs. fruit) creates a high probability of "shelf blindness."

The "better way" analysis reveals a fatal alternative: the proposal assumes a nationwide, all-at-once launch. This maximizes exposure to the recognition failure. If shoppers cannot find the brand, they will switch to competitors immediately. A large advertising campaign cannot guarantee in-store recognition at the critical split-second of decision. The cost of a failed national rollout—unsold inventory, lost shelf space, and eroded loyalty—is irreversible compared to the savings of a minimalist design.

The plan does not beat "do nothing" or a staged alternative because it gambles the entire franchise on a hypothesis that contradicts the known behavior of its core users. The evidence provided (older, loyal base, reliance on the old image) directly contradicts the strategy of removing that image. There is no pilot, no regional test, and no safety net. The contradiction is clear: you cannot remove the very thing that makes the product findable to the people who buy it most, then expect ads to fix the confusion at the point of sale.

Blockers: The removal of the primary shelf cue for an older, loyal demographic guarantees recognition failure; the "all stores at once" approach eliminates the ability to learn before catastrophic loss.

Risks: Total sales collapse due to invisibility on shelf; alienation of core base; inability to recover shelf space if the launch fails.

DECISION: NO-GO
