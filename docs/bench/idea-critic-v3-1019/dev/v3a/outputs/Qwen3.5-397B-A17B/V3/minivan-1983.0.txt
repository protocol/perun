**Steelman:** A cash-strapped carmaker can survive by creating a new "minivan" category using existing compact-car parts and a converted factory, offering car-like driving in a garage-friendly package.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker is the claim that "no such vehicle category exists." Historically, the absence of a category often signals unmet demand rather than impossibility, especially when current options (station wagons, full-size vans) force families to compromise between size and drivability. The risk of buyers perceiving it as a commercial van is mitigated by the explicit design goal: "drive like a car" and fit in a home garage. This positioning directly targets the family segment, differentiating it from utilitarian competitors.

Financial constraints are severe ("little cash"), but the proposal leverages a "converted factory" and an "existing compact-car platform." This reuse of assets drastically lowers capital expenditure compared to designing a new chassis or building a new plant. While reusing a compact platform limits space, it enables the crucial "garage-fit" constraint that full-size vans fail. If the platform were too small to be viable, the "family" use case would collapse, but the proposal assumes the compact platform is sufficient for a "sized to fit" van, which is physically plausible.

Rivals copying the idea is a long-term risk, not an immediate blocker. Being first to define the category offers a temporary monopoly to recoup costs before competition arrives. The government loan guarantee provides the necessary bridge to execute this specific, low-capital pivot.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because the marketing failed to distinguish the vehicle from commercial vans, or the compact platform made the interior too cramped for families. However, the proposal's specific focus on "garage" and "car-like" driving addresses the root cause of the current market gap.

**Decision Logic:**
There is no factual blocker. The financial risk is managed by asset reuse. The market risk is addressed by creating a new category where none existed. The "no category" objection is actually the strongest argument *for* the proposal, as it represents a blue-ocean opportunity for a company that cannot afford to fight in established red oceans.

DECISION: GO
