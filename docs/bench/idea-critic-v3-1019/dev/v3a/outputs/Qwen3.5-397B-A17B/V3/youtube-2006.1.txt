**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant video platform eliminates a future competitor, secures the category leader despite current losses, and leverages the buyer's infrastructure to solve bandwidth and monetization hurdles that currently cripple the target.

**Skeptic Analysis:** The valuation ($1.65B) is astronomical for a company with near-zero revenue and active legal threats. The core asset (user uploads) consists largely of copyrighted material owned by aggressive media conglomerates. If courts rule that the platform is liable for infringement rather than a passive host, the acquisition transfers massive, uncapped liability to the buyer. The "unproven ad model" means the purchase is purely speculative on future behavior change.

**Better-Way:** The alternative is "do nothing" or build organically. The buyer already operates a smaller video service. Continuing to invest internally avoids inheriting immediate lawsuits and allows the market to clarify copyright laws before committing capital. Waiting could allow the target to burn cash and lose users if sued, lowering acquisition costs later, or fail entirely, removing the competitive threat without expenditure.

**Pre-mortem & Door Type:** Assume it is 2008 and the deal is a disaster. Why? Major studios win landmark lawsuits, forcing the removal of all popular content or imposing fines exceeding the purchase price. Advertisers flee due to brand safety concerns around unmoderated content. This is a **one-way door**; once the stock is issued and the company merged, the liability and cultural integration cannot be easily undone. The cost of delay is losing the "network effect" lead, but the cost of error is existential legal and financial damage.

**Blocker Test:** The proposal admits "media companies have threatened lawsuits" and "no meaningful revenue." These are not just risks; they are active blockers. The core value proposition relies on content that may be illegal to host. Without a verified legal shield or a proven revenue stream to justify the multiple, the math does not work. The speculation that ads will eventually pay for massive bandwidth and legal fees is an assumption, not a fact. A cheaper alternative exists: let the target bleed cash while refining the buyer's own compliant platform.

**Verdict:** The combination of uncapped legal liability, lack of revenue, and a price tag based entirely on hypothetical future monetization constitutes a fatal flaw. The safer, cheaper path is to wait for legal clarity or organic market correction.

DECISION: NO-GO
