Steelman: A hybrid device leverages existing IP to escape the losing battle of raw power, creating a unique "play anywhere" category that phones and stationary consoles cannot match.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal admits weak specs, confusing history, and a thin launch lineup. Historically, these are fatal. However, the "modest-power mobile chip" is the critical variable. Rivals compete on fidelity; this proposal competes on form factor. If the chip enables true portability without prohibitive battery drain, the spec deficit becomes irrelevant for the target use case (first-party titles).

The "confusing positioning" risk is valid but mitigated if the hybrid nature is demonstrated instantly (e.g., docking/undocking) rather than explained via marketing jargon. The financial losses from the previous home console create a "do or die" scenario; maintaining the status quo (handheld erosion) guarantees decline. The "thin lineup" is acceptable only if the first-party titles are system-sellers that define the hardware's unique capabilities.

**Blocker Check:**
Is there a fact in the proposal that proves failure?
1. *Weak specs:* Not a blocker if the software strategy relies on art style/gameplay over photorealism (common for this maker).
2. *Confusing history:* A reputational risk, not a functional blocker.
3. *Supply constraints:* Common at launch; manageable if demand is pent-up.
4. *Financial losses:* This necessitates the risk, it does not block it.

No internal contradiction exists where the stated facts make success impossible. The risk is high, but the alternative (continuing to lose handheld share while having no viable home presence) is a slower death. The hybrid approach is the only path to a distinct market position. The "modest" chip is a feature for cost/battery, not a bug, provided expectations are set correctly.

**Kill Criteria:**
If pre-orders do not exceed the previous console's first-month sales by 20%, the positioning has failed. If third-party developers do not commit to at least three major titles within six months, the ecosystem is dead.

The proposal survives the pre-mortem because it attacks a blue ocean (hybrid) rather than fighting a lost war (power).
