The claim is that a mass-market economy carmaker can successfully launch a separate luxury brand with a standalone dealer network and a single flagship sedan to compete with established European leaders. The steelman is that structural separation via a new brand and dealers shields the parent's economy reputation while undercutting incumbents on price to gain entry.

However, critical blockers exist in the proposal as stated. First, the "single flagship model" creates an unsustainable business model for a *separate dealer network*. Luxury dealers require high fixed costs and a broad product lineup to remain profitable; a single model cannot generate sufficient foot traffic or service revenue to sustain independent franchises, leading to immediate dealer insolvency or refusal to join. Second, the proposal admits development has "already cost heavily," implying sunk cost fallacy may be driving the decision rather than forward-looking ROI. If the unit economics of a one-car lineup cannot support the proposed distribution channel, the plan fails regardless of vehicle quality.

The "better way" analysis suggests a staged approach: launch the luxury brand initially within existing upgraded showrooms or via a hybrid digital/dedicated boutique model to validate demand before capitalizing on a full separate network. Alternatively, a multi-model launch is required to justify the dealer investment. The current plan forces a binary choice between a doomed dealer network and a brand that cannot reach customers effectively.

The pre-mortem reveals that within 18 months, dealers will demand bankruptcy protection due to low volume, forcing the manufacturer to buy back franchises at a loss, compounding the heavy development costs. The risk of brand dilution remains if the separation is perceived as cosmetic, but the financial failure of the distribution channel is the immediate killer.

This is a one-way door with massive capital exposure. The mismatch between a single-product lineup and the economics of a standalone dealer network is a factual blocker that invalidates the go-to-market strategy. The plan must be revised to either secure a viable distribution method that fits a single model or delay the network build until a full lineup exists.

DECISION: NO-GO
