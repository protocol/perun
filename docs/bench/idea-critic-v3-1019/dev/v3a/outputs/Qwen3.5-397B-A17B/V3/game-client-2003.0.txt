The claim is that a mandatory online client will solve update/cheating issues and create a new store revenue stream. Steelman: Centralizing delivery via a proprietary client is the only viable path to secure real-time anti-cheat and reliable patching for popular PC titles in 2003, while creating a defensible distribution platform.

**Skeptic:** The proposal assumes home broadband is universal in 2003; it is not. Many target users have dial-up, making "large downloads" a hard blocker for adoption, not just a complaint. If the client requires a persistent connection for play (implied by "mandatory"), it alienates the installed base relying on boxed retail copies. The risk of retail partners dropping games is high if the client undermines the value of the physical box they sell.

**Better-way:** The "do nothing" alternative (continuing boxed sales with manual patches) preserves retail relationships and respects bandwidth limits, though it fails on cheating. A hybrid approach (optional client for updates, separate anti-cheat module) reduces friction but complicates the architecture. However, no cheaper alternative achieves the *same upside* of a unified store and real-time security. The store potential justifies the friction if the user base can be migrated.

**Kill-criteria:** This is a one-way door; once retail channels are burned and the ecosystem shifts online, returning to boxed-only is impossible. The cost of delay is ceding the digital distribution market to emerging competitors.

**Blocker Check:** The stated fact that "home broadband is not universal" contradicts the requirement for a *mandatory* online client for *all* new games if those games are unplayable without it. This creates an immediate market exclusion. However, the proposal targets "new games," allowing a transition period where only new titles require the client, while older boxed games remain supported. The "mandatory" nature applies to the *new* ecosystem, not the legacy one. The risk of retail pushback is real but manageable if the games are popular enough to force retailer compliance. No fact provided proves the plan fails as proposed; the risks are execution challenges, not logical contradictions.

**Decision:** The strategic upside of owning the distribution channel and solving cheating outweighs the transition friction. The "non-universal broadband" is a known constraint to manage via client optimization, not a reason to abort.

DECISION: GO
