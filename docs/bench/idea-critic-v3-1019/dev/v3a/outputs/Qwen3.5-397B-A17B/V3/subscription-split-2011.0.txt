The proposal attempts to solve a pricing backlash by fracturing the customer experience. The core failure is treating the symptom (brand association with a price hike) while ignoring the disease (a 60% price increase for combined utility). Splitting the services forces customers to manage two accounts, two queues, and two bills, directly amplifying the "customer confusion" risk already identified.

The rationale claims the DVD business is declining and needs different management; however, operational separation does not require brand bifurcation. A single brand can house distinct service tiers or management teams without alienating the user base. The proposal explicitly sacrifices cross-service recommendations, destroying data synergy that could otherwise justify the higher price point through superior personalization.

The pre-mortem is clear: customers perceive this not as strategic clarity, but as a deceptive unbundling designed to hide the magnitude of the price hike. Instead of one painful bill, they receive two frustrating logins for a service they already feel is overpriced. The "declining" DVD segment still represents immediate revenue; isolating it signals to those users that they are being pushed out the door, accelerating churn rather than managing a graceful sunset.

The strongest attack is that this move decouples the value proposition. If streaming is the future, it must stand on its own merit, not by artificially severing ties to the legacy cash cow to distract from a price hike. The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" test: operational separation behind a single sign-on and unified billing achieves the management goals without the customer experience penalty.

This plan increases friction, destroys data value, and insults customer intelligence during a period of price sensitivity. It solves an internal accounting desire at the expense of external user retention. The risks of confusion and churn are not merely possible; they are the direct, intended mechanical output of this architecture.

DECISION: NO-GO
