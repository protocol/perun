**Steelman:** Acquiring the leading mobile photo threat pre-IPO neutralizes a existential risk to the buyer's core business and instantly secures mobile dominance for ~$1B, a premium justified by strategic defense rather than current revenue.

**Attack:** The valuation is indefensible by traditional metrics (no revenue, tiny team). The primary failure mode is cultural destruction: integrating a nimble startup into a large corporation often kills the very innovation being purchased. Furthermore, regulatory bodies may block the deal as anti-competitive, wasting time and signaling weakness. If the app's growth is platform-dependent and that platform shifts, the asset becomes worthless.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Risk the target becoming the default mobile layer, eclipsing the buyer's photo product entirely before the IPO.
2. *Build/Buy Competitor:* Too slow; the network effect is already established.
3. *Minority Stake:* Fails to neutralize the competitive threat or secure full control.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of maintaining social graph dominance during the mobile transition.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, but key founders leave within six months due to bureaucracy, user growth stalls as the product is merged into the buyer's weaker app, and regulators force a divestiture two years later. The $1B is wasted, and the mobile threat re-emerges stronger.

**Blockers:** NONE. While the financials are absurd, the strategic necessity of removing the only viable mobile competitor before the buyer's IPO creates a unique "must-pay" scenario. The risk of *not* buying (existential obsolescence) outweighs the risk of overpaying.

**Risks:** Cultural integration failure, regulatory blocks, founder retention. These are managed via kill criteria: autonomous operation guarantees, specific retention bonuses, and a hard deadline for regulatory clearance.

**Decision:** The cost of delay is losing the mobile era. The price is high, but the alternative is irrelevance.

DECISION: GO
