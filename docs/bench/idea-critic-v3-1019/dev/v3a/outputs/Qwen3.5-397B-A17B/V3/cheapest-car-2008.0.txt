**Steelman:** Launching a dedicated, ultra-low-cost car at $2,500 will successfully migrate scooter families to four-wheel safety by leveraging unprecedented volume to offset razor-thin margins.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on three verified contradictions within its own text. First, the "cheapest car" positioning directly triggers the stated risk that buyers may reject a vehicle defined by low status; in emerging markets, aspirational value often outweighs pure utility, making the core marketing hook a potential repellent. Second, the "high volume targets" required to make the economics work are physically threatened by the farmer protests over the factory site; without a resolved land acquisition strategy, the volume assumption is baseless, collapsing the cost structure. Third, the alternatives analysis is incomplete: used cars offer similar price points with perceived higher quality and status, while scooters offer significantly lower operating costs. The proposal asserts volume will save it but provides no evidence that the target demographic values "new and cheap" over "used and respectable" or "scooter and economical."

**Better Way:** A staged pilot using contract manufacturing avoids the immediate land-risk trap and tests the "cheapest" branding hypothesis before committing to a dedicated factory. Alternatively, position the car as "safe family transport" rather than "cheapest," accepting a slightly higher price point to preserve aspirational value and margin for safety features.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot survey shows less than 20% of scooter owners prefer this new car over a comparable used vehicle despite the price advantage, the "migration" thesis is false. If land protests delay construction beyond six months, the volume-based cost model breaks.

**Verdict:** The plan relies on unproven behavioral shifts (rejecting used cars for a stigmatized new one) and an obstructed supply chain (land protests). Proceeding as proposed ignores these blockers. The "cheapest" label is a strategic liability, not an asset, and the factory risk is existential.

DECISION: NO-GO
