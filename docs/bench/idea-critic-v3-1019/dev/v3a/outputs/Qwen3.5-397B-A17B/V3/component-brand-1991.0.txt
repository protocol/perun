Steelman: Transform an invisible component into a consumer demand driver by subsidizing OEM ads that feature the brand, forcing buyers to request the chip by name and locking out clones.

**Skeptic:** The premise relies on consumers caring about internal components they cannot see or understand. Historically, component marketing fails because end-users delegate technical choices to manufacturers. If buyers do not recognize the logo's value, the subsidy becomes a pure cost with zero leverage over OEMs or sales. The risk of OEM resentment is high; they may view this as brand encroachment rather than support, potentially leading to channel conflict or refusal to participate.

**Better-Way:** Before committing a large share of the marketing budget to an unprecedented consumer campaign, test the hypothesis with a localized pilot or a trade-focused initiative. A "certified by" seal for retailers or a warranty extension tied to the chip offers tangible value without the massive spend of mass-media advertising. Doing nothing maintains the status quo where volume is driven by OEM relationships, avoiding the risk of alienating those same partners.

**Kill-Criteria:** This is a one-way door; once the brand promise is made to consumers, failure damages reputation permanently. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed global launch. The proposal lacks a specific metric to validate consumer awareness before full spend. Without a pre-committed stop condition (e.g., "halt if consumer recall <15% in pilot markets within 6 months"), the budget could be exhausted with no sales lift.

**Decision:** The proposal bets the company's marketing budget on an unproven behavioral shift: that consumers will demand a component they cannot evaluate. The risks of OEM backlash and budget waste are concrete and immediate, while the benefits are speculative. The plan lacks a staged approach or clear kill criteria to mitigate these existential risks. It fails the "cheaper alternative" test by ignoring pilot programs that could validate demand at a fraction of the cost.

DECISION: NO-GO
