**Steelman:** Migrating to the dominant architecture solves the thermal/performance bottleneck preventing thin laptop competitiveness, using a translation layer to preserve the existing software ecosystem during a two-year transition.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the "performance cliff" of translated software. If the emulation overhead exceeds 20-30%, core professional workflows will fail, causing immediate churn to competitors before native apps arrive. The proposal acknowledges this risk but offers no specific performance targets or fallback if the translation layer proves inadequate for pro users.

*Alternative 1 (Do Nothing):* Maintain current chips. Result: Continued inability to ship thin, cool laptops; gradual loss of market share to competitors with superior form factors.
*Alternative 2 (Hybrid/Dual-Architecture):* Run both lines simultaneously. Result: Higher complexity and cost, but mitigates the "sales freeze" risk and allows a slower, safer transition for pro users.

**Pre-mortem:** The announcement freezes current sales immediately. Developers, seeing a shrinking installed base, delay native builds. The translation layer is too slow for power users, the brand's "pro" reputation crumbles, and the company is stuck with inferior hardware and no software advantage.

**Fit & Evidence:** The proposal correctly identifies that the current supplier roadmap cannot meet the physical requirements (cool/efficient) for the next generation of hardware. Without this move, the product line hits a physical ceiling. However, the reliance on a translation layer is a single point of failure not sufficiently stress-tested in the proposal.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Unverified Performance Viability:** The proposal assumes the translation layer will be "fast enough" without citing benchmark projections or a threshold for acceptance. If native speed cannot be approached, the value proposition collapses.
2.  **Sales Freeze Mechanism:** Announcing two years out guarantees a sales drought for current models. The proposal lacks a bridge strategy (e.g., immediate hybrid release or strict embargo until shipping) to prevent revenue collapse.

**Risks:** Developer apathy, brand dilution, execution complexity of a second transition.

**Decision Logic:** While the strategic direction is sound due to physical constraints of the current chips, the plan as stated lacks the necessary safeguards for the transition period. Approving a two-year gap with unproven emulation performance is too hazardous. The plan must first define performance kill-criteria and a sales-bridge strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
