**Steelman**: This is the only solution for truly global, seamless mobile coverage, capturing high-value travelers who cannot rely on patchy terrestrial networks.

**Attack**: The economic model collapses under the weight of rapidly improving alternatives. Ground cellular networks are expanding faster than anticipated, eroding the unique value proposition before critical mass is reached. A $3,000 handset with bulky form factors and poor indoor performance creates a friction barrier too high for mass adoption, even among business travelers. The debt structure requires immediate, massive subscriber growth that the niche market cannot support. Sales channels are unprepared, delaying revenue further. The "first mover" advantage is negated by the superior user experience and lower cost of emerging local solutions. The pre-mortem is clear: the company defaults on its $5 billion debt because the addressable market shrinks quarterly while fixed costs remain static.

**Better Way**: Do not launch commercially yet. Pivot to a government/defense contract model to subsidize infrastructure costs while waiting for handset technology to miniaturize and prices to drop. Alternatively, lease capacity to existing carriers rather than building a consumer brand.

**Kill Criteria**: If terrestrial coverage reaches 80% of key business hubs within 12 months, the consumer case is dead. If handset prices do not drop below $1,000 within 18 months, adoption will fail to meet debt service requirements.

**Decision Logic**: The proposal ignores the speed of the "do nothing" alternative (waiting for ground networks to solve the problem organically at zero capital risk to this entity). The financial leverage is fatal; one delay in adoption triggers insolvency. The risks are not hypothetical; they are structural contradictions between the cost base and the market reality. The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test because the gap it fills is closing rapidly without this specific intervention.

DECISION: NO-GO
