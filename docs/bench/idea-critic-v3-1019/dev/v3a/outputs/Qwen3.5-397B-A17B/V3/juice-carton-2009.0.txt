**Steelman:** The brand must modernize its visual identity to signal freshness and premium quality, betting that a bold, unified launch with heavy advertising will overcome temporary recognition friction and attract new buyers without alienating the core base.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the specific function of the existing "orange with straw" graphic as the primary shelf cue for a loyal, older demographic. In a low-involvement category like juice, shoppers often buy on autopilot using visual anchors rather than reading brand names. Removing this anchor creates a high probability of "search failure," where loyalists simply grab a competitor's product thinking the brand is out of stock. The risk is not just confusion; it is immediate, irreversible sales collapse before advertising can retrain behavior.

**Better Way:** A staged rollout in a test market would validate recognition speed and sales impact at a fraction of the nationwide cost. Alternatively, evolving the current design (e.g., refining the straw/orange graphic) retains the cue while refreshing the look. The "all stores at once" approach eliminates the safety valve of learning from early failures.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Assuming the goal is growth, alienating the existing loyal base to chase undefined new buyers contradicts the stability required for a market leader. If the redesign fails, the cost of reverting packaging nationwide is prohibitive, and the brand signal becomes "unstable." The delay cost of testing is negligible compared to the risk of a nationwide flop.

**Blockers:**
1. **Contradiction:** The proposal admits the old image is the "main shelf cue" and buyers are "older/loyal," yet plans to remove that cue entirely in a single step. This directly undermines the mechanism of current sales.
2. **Evidence Gap:** No data is provided showing the target demographic responds to "minimalist" designs or that advertising can overcome ingrained motor-memory shopping habits quickly enough to prevent churn.

**Risks:** High probability of immediate revenue drop; irreversible packaging waste if reverted; competitor exploitation of shelf confusion.

**Kill Criteria:** If a test market shows >5% sales drop in week 1, halt.

The plan fails the blocker test: it removes the primary purchase driver without a verified replacement mechanism or a safe staging plan.

DECISION: NO-GO
