The claim is that a monochrome, modest-spec handheld at $89.95 can succeed against superior color rivals by leveraging battery life, price, and a bundled puzzle game.

**Skeptic:** The primary assumption is that portability and battery life outweigh visual fidelity for the mass market. The "single-game" history of prior devices is a reputational risk, but the bundled puzzle game (implied high replayability) directly counters the "one-and-done" perception. The thin margin risk is real but mitigated by the low entry price driving volume.

**Better-way:** Alternatives include matching the rival's color screen (raising cost to ~$150+, killing the impulse buy), waiting for cheaper color tech (ceding the market), or targeting only core fans (limiting scale). Doing nothing allows the rival to entrench. The proposed path is the only one offering a distinct, affordable value proposition.

**Kill-criteria:** This is a two-way door; failure results in inventory write-downs but does not destroy the core console business. The cost of delay is losing the "affordable portable" category definition to the rival. The pre-mortem suggests failure occurs if the puzzle game lacks depth or if third-party developers ignore the platform. However, the low price creates a massive installed base potential, forcing developer attention.

**Decision:** The proposal survives the attack because it targets a different job-to-be-done (long-session, affordable play) rather than fighting a spec war. The risks are manageable execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The specific constraints (monochrome, low cost) are actually strategic advantages that force focus on gameplay over graphics.

Blockers: NONE. The facts provided support the viability of a differentiated, low-cost strategy.
Risks: Developer adoption and screen perception, mitigated by price and the killer app bundle.

DECISION: GO
