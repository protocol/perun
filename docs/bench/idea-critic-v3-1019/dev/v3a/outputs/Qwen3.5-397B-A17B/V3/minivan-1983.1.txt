The claim is that a front-wheel-drive, car-based minivan will save a cash-poor automaker by creating a new consumer category. The steelman: this vehicle uniquely combines garage fit, car-like handling, and family utility on an existing low-cost platform, unlocking a latent market competitors ignore.

**Skeptic:** The premise that "no such category exists" is a double-edged sword; it implies zero proven demand. With little cash, a misread of buyer perception (fearing "commercial van" stigma) could be fatal. Reusing a compact platform risks insufficient cargo space, alienating the very families targeted. However, the government loan guarantee mitigates the immediate liquidity crisis, allowing room for a single strategic bet.

**Better-way:** "Do nothing" means continuing with station wagons and full-size vans, segments likely saturating or declining given the company's near-collapse. Building a new rear-wheel-drive platform is too capital-intensive. Converting an existing factory is the only viable cost path. There is no cheaper alternative that offers the same differentiation; the compact-car reuse is the feature, not just a cost-saver.

**Kill-criteria:** The plan fails if the vehicle cannot physically fit in a standard garage while carrying three rows of passengers, or if the "commercial" perception cannot be shifted via marketing. The risk of rivals copying is irrelevant if the first-mover captures the category definition. The "no category" risk is actually the opportunity; if the product delivers the promised "car-like" drive in a garage-friendly size, the category creates itself.

**Verdict:** The proposal addresses the core constraints: low cash (reuse platform/factory) and market need (family utility without van penalties). The risks are real but inherent to any turnaround innovation; the alternative is stagnation. The specific combination of front-wheel-drive, garage size, and car dynamics is a defensible wedge that existing large vans and small wagons cannot match. No factual blocker exists in the text; the "no category" objection is the value proposition, not a flaw.

DECISION: GO
