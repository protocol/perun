**Steelman:** Leverage existing compact mechanics to rapidly deliver a stylish, customizable coupe/convertible at a low entry price, targeting young buyers with massive advertising to create a new "pony car" segment.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker concern is that sharing parts with a "dull" economy car will tarnish the sporty image. However, the proposal mitigates this via distinct styling (long hood, short deck) and a "long options list." This allows the base price to remain low while enabling high-margin customization, distancing the final product from its utilitarian roots. The risk of unproven demand is real, but the target demographic (postwar boom) represents a massive, underserved wave that traditional manufacturers are ignoring. Doing nothing cedes this emerging segment to competitors or leaves money on the table.

The "heavy launch spending" risk is a necessary cost of category creation; without it, the car remains just another compact variant. The reversibility is moderate: tooling is shared (lowering sunk costs), but marketing spend is unrecoverable. Yet, the cost of delay is high, as the demographic window is opening now.

**Blockers:** NONE. The fear of "looking cheap" is addressed by the specific design language and customization strategy. The financial risk is contained by the high degree of parts reuse, limiting capital exposure compared to a clean-sheet design.

**Risks:**
1.  **Brand Dilution:** If the styling fails to mask the economy roots, the car may fail to attract the aspirational buyer.
2.  **Option Confusion:** A too-complex options list could paralyze buyers or complicate production.
3.  **Market Miss:** The assumption that young buyers prioritize style over practicality could be partially wrong, though the low base price hedges this.

**Kill Criteria:**
If first-year sales do not exceed 100,000 units, or if the attach rate for high-margin options remains below 40%, the program must be re-evaluated for a pivot or discontinuation. The leading metric to watch is the ratio of sporty-option uptake versus base-model sales in the first quarter.

The proposal effectively balances low development risk with high market potential by repurposing proven mechanics for a new emotional appeal. The risks are manageable and outweighed by the strategic opportunity to define a new segment.

DECISION: GO
