The claim is that a $699 Wi-Fi juicer with proprietary subscriptions will succeed in 2016. Steelman: A premium, hassle-free health appliance creates a recurring revenue moat by eliminating prep and cleaning friction.

However, the unit economics and market fit present immediate blockers. First, the price point is prohibitive; $699 exceeds the cost of high-end traditional juicers that accept any produce, while the subscription model likely carries a significant premium over grocery store prices. Consumers rarely accept locked ecosystems for perishable goods unless the convenience delta is massive, which is unproven here. Second, requiring internet connectivity for a basic mechanical function (checking freshness) introduces unnecessary failure points and privacy concerns without clear user value, increasing support costs and return rates. Third, the market for single-use, internet-connected kitchen appliances in 2016 is nascent and skeptical; history shows high failure rates for "smart" devices that solve non-existent problems while adding complexity.

Alternatives exist: a non-connected version at a lower price point using standard containers, or a partnership with existing grocery delivery services rather than building a proprietary supply chain. The "do nothing" option avoids burning capital on hardware inventory and logistics before validating demand for the subscription model itself.

The pre-mortem reveals a likely failure mode: high customer acquisition costs coupled with low retention once users realize the per-juice cost is 3-4x higher than manual juicing. The cost of delay is low; waiting to validate the subscription willingness-to-pay via a simpler MVP (e.g., manual bags sold online) reduces hardware risk. The proposal assumes users prioritize app-integrated freshness checks over cost and flexibility, a premise contradicted by general consumer behavior in the kitchen appliance sector.

Blockers: The combined friction of high upfront cost, locked proprietary supply chain, and mandatory connectivity creates a value proposition that likely fails against cheaper, flexible alternatives. No evidence is provided that the target market accepts these constraints.
