The proposal relies on a fatal contradiction: it plans to fund massive expansion (IPO, TV ads, acquisitions, warehouses) by selling products below cost. This strategy intentionally deepens negative gross margins while simultaneously increasing fixed costs and cash burn. The premise that capital markets will remain open to fund a company explicitly losing money on every unit sold, while competitors engage in similar spending wars, is unsupported by the stated risks.

The "better way" analysis reveals that "doing nothing" or pursuing a profitability-first path is safer. The proposed path requires continuous access to cheap capital, which the proposal itself admits could "close." If markets close even briefly, the company faces immediate insolvency due to its reliance on external funding to cover operational losses and expansion costs.

The strongest attack is the cash flow mechanics: selling below cost means every new customer acquired via the proposed TV campaign increases the total loss. The IPO proceeds would be consumed almost instantly by the combination of below-cost sales, high shipping costs for heavy items, and the acquisition premium. There is no path to positive unit economics described. The risk of "capital markets closing" is not a mere possibility but a near-certainty trigger for failure given the dependency on infinite future funding rounds to sustain current operations.

The pre-mortem shows the company running out of cash within months of the IPO as the advertising spend accelerates losses faster than revenue growth can justify to public investors. The "kill criterion" here is immediate: a business model requiring perpetual below-cost sales to grow cannot survive a market correction.

Blockers:
1. The plan requires selling below cost to grow, guaranteeing increased losses with scale, while depending on open capital markets that are flagged as at risk of closing.
2. No mechanism exists to achieve profitability; the strategy is purely a cash-burn race with no finish line defined.

Risks:
1. Competitors outspending or undercutting further.
2. Shipping costs eroding any remaining value.
3. Public market rejection of negative margin growth.

DECISION: NO-GO
