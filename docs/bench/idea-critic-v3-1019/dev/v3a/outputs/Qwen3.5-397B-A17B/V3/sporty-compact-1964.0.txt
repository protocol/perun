**Steelman:** Leverage existing compact mechanics to deliver affordable sporty styling for a new demographic, monetizing via customization and aggressive marketing.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Assumptions:** The "dull" reputation of the base car can be overwritten by distinct styling; young buyers prioritize style over brand prestige; parts sharing reduces cost enough to allow a low entry price without destroying margins.
2.  **Better Ways:** Building a clean-sheet platform lowers risk of "cheap" perception but drastically increases cost and time, missing the demographic window. Doing nothing cedes the segment to competitors. The proposed "long hood, short deck" styling specifically targets the psychological desire for performance, differentiating it from the economy donor better than a simple re-badge would.
3.  **Fit:** Aligns with the influx of postwar boom buyers seeking individuality. The long options list turns the car into a revenue engine, offsetting the low base price.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the marketing fails to separate the car's image from the economy donor, or if the "unproven" demand is actually non-existent. However, the low base price limits downside exposure per unit, while the options list captures upside.
5.  **Evidence:** The mechanical reliability is proven (donor car). The demographic shift is a stated fact. The risk of "looking cheap" is mitigated by the specific stylistic proportions and the convertible option, which inherently signals leisure over economy.
6.  **Decision:** The risks are real but manageable. The "unproven demand" is a market opportunity, not a blocker, given the low variable cost of using existing parts. The heavy ad spend is necessary to overcome the donor car's image; without it, the styling changes alone might fail. The potential reward (creating a new mass-market segment) vastly outweighs the risk of a marketing miss, especially since the hardware investment is minimized by parts sharing. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal's logic.

**Blockers:** NONE.
**Risks:** Marketing inefficacy; residual "economy" stigma.
**Kill Criteria:** If first-year sales do not cover launch ad spend by 18 months, pivot to fleet sales or discontinue convertible.

DECISION: GO
