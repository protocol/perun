**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee ($79) eliminates per-order friction, locking in loyalty and increasing purchase frequency enough to offset shipping losses through volume and margin expansion.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the unit economics assumption: that increased frequency yields net profit after subsidizing two-day shipping. In 2005, fuel and logistics costs were volatile; heavy buyers could easily generate shipping costs exceeding $79 annually, creating a direct loss on every "loyal" transaction. The proposal admits economics are "unproven" yet seeks full launch. This violates the requirement to validate the core revenue model before scaling.

Alternatives include:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain status quo; avoid immediate margin hit.
2.  **Pilot Program:** Launch in a single region or for a capped number of users to verify the "heavy buyer" loss ratio before national rollout.
3.  **Threshold-Based Free Shipping:** Offer free shipping only on orders over a specific amount, protecting margins on small baskets while still reducing friction.

**Pre-mortem:** The program launches nationally. Heavy users immediately optimize behavior, splitting large orders into frequent small ones to maximize free shipping. Logistics costs skyrocket, exceeding the $79 revenue per user by 40%. The company cannot cancel memberships without massive reputational damage and refund liabilities. Cash flow collapses under the weight of subsidized logistics, forcing a premature, brand-damaging reversal.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Unproven Economics:** The proposal explicitly states the economics are unproven. Launching a irreversible, high-cost financial product without validated unit economics is a factual failure of the plan's own premise.
2.  **Irreversibility:** Once sold, memberships are hard to revoke, locking the company into potential losses for a full year.

**Risks:** Margin erosion, logistics capacity strain, cannibalization of high-margin express shipping.

**Conclusion:** The plan fails the blocker test. You cannot approve a financial product with admitted unproven economics and high irreversibility. It requires a pilot to establish the break-even frequency and average order value before a full go.

DECISION: NO-GO
