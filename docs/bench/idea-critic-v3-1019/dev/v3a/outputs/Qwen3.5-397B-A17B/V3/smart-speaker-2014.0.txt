The claim is that a cylindrical, always-listening speaker will succeed where the company's smartphone failed, despite market skepticism on privacy and utility. The steelman: leveraging existing member trust and a low-entry price ($99) creates a new, high-frequency home touchpoint that phone assistants cannot physically occupy, turning a hardware failure into a platform win.

However, the "better-way" analysis reveals a critical flaw. The proposal ignores the company's most recent data point: a poorly selling smartphone. This indicates a fundamental weakness in the company's hardware execution, supply chain, or brand trust in devices. Launching a *more* intrusive device (always-on microphone) immediately after a failure, into a *non-existent* category, compounds risk rather than mitigating it. Phone-based assistants already solve the "question answering" and "shopping list" needs without the privacy friction of an always-on home device. The "do nothing" option preserves capital while the company fixes its core hardware reputation.

The pre-mortem is clear: The device launches, privacy concerns dominate headlines due to the "always-listening" nature, voice recognition fails in noisy home environments (a known 2014 limitation), and the lack of a defined category means consumers don't understand why they need it. The result is another high-profile hardware failure that damages the core retail brand.

The strongest attack is the contradiction between the stated goal (expanding reach) and the evidence (recent hardware failure). The proposal assumes the market will forgive a second hardware misstep immediately, which is unsupported. The "invitation only" strategy does not solve the fundamental product-market fit or privacy trust issues; it merely limits the scale of the failure.

Blockers: The company's demonstrated inability to sell hardware (poor smartphone sales) directly contradicts the assumption that they can successfully launch a complex, sensitive new hardware category. The privacy risk is a factual barrier in 2014, not just a perception issue.

Risks: Voice recognition failure rates, undefined category adoption, brand damage from a second failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
