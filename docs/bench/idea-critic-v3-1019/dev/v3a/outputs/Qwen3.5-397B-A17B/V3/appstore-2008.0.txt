The claim is that a closed, curated native app store will succeed despite prior executive skepticism of native apps and existing open rivals. The steelman: a single controlled store balances developer reach with product integrity, turning security and stability from risks into selling points while capturing revenue via a 30% cut.

**Skeptic:** The CEO's public stance against native apps contradicts this pivot; if web apps were truly sufficient, this move dilutes brand consistency. However, the rival open ecosystems likely suffer from fragmentation and malware, creating an opening for a "it just works" alternative. The 30% fee may deter early adopters compared to open sideloading, but developers pay for distribution and trust.

**Better-way:** Doing nothing preserves the current web-app strategy and avoids carrier friction. Yet, without native APIs, the phone cannot compete on performance or hardware integration, ceding the high-end market to rivals. A hybrid model (web first, native later) delays the ecosystem effect needed to lock in users. The proposed store is the cheapest viable path to parity.

**Kill-criteria:** If the review process creates a bottleneck delaying key apps by >3 months, or if carriers block the device due to bypassed services, the plan fails. The pre-mortem suggests a flood of low-quality apps could tarnish the brand, but the "approval gate" directly mitigates this. The cost of delay is total irrelevance in the emerging platform war.

**Verdict:** The contradiction between the CEO's past views and this proposal is a strategic pivot, not a logical flaw, driven by market necessity. The "open" rivals provide a negative example that validates the curated approach. No factual blocker exists in the text; the risks are manageable via the proposed review process. The 30% cut is standard for value provided. The plan beats "do nothing" which guarantees obsolescence.

DECISION: GO
