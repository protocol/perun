**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men avoiding both full-sugar guilt and diet stigma, leveraging a premium price to signal innovation before the low-carb trend fades.

**Attack & Options:**
The core claim fails the "satisfy neither group" test. Dieters seek near-zero calories; a 50% reduction leaves too many carbohydrates for strict low-carb adherence, while taste-compromised dieters won't accept half-sugar. Regular drinkers want full taste, not a diluted premium product. This creates a "valley of death" in positioning.

Comparing alternatives:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain share in two distinct, growing segments (regular and diet) without cannibalization risk.
2.  **Diet Extension:** Improve diet cola taste for young men (cheaper, targets the actual growth driver).
3.  **The Proposal:** High cost (large campaign), high price barrier, direct rivalry, and reliance on a potentially fleeting trend.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, confusion reigns over who it is for, trial is blocked by premium pricing, and the low-carb fad ends within a year, leaving a SKU that satisfies no one. The rival's similar move suggests a "me-too" trap rather than a validated market gap.

**Evidence & Contradiction:** The proposal admits the risk that "people who want fewer calories already buy diet." This is not a risk; it is a behavioral fact that contradicts the target audience's existence. If the primary value proposition is "fewer calories," the target has already optimized to diet. If the value is "better taste," the half-sugar compromise likely fails versus full-sugar. The "premium price" further deters the only logical trial group: curious regular drinkers.

**Blocker:** The stated fact that "a half-sugar drink may satisfy neither group" combined with the existence of a dedicated diet segment for calorie-conscious consumers creates a fundamental product-market fit failure. The proposal offers no evidence that "young men" specifically desire *exactly* half-sugar rather than zero or full.

**Risks:** Trend expiration, cannibalization, price resistance.

**Verdict:** The plan relies on a hypothetical demographic that likely does not exist, ignores the sufficiency of existing diet options for the health-conscious, and enters a price-sensitive trial phase with a premium barrier.

DECISION: NO-GO
