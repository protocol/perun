The claim is that a profitable DVD leader should immediately launch free streaming despite a weak catalog, poor quality, high licensing costs, and imminent giant competitors. The steelman: this is a defensive necessity to retain subscribers and learn the medium before the market shifts entirely away from physical media, using the existing cash cow to fund the transition.

However, the facts provided indicate a fatal mismatch between the proposal's costs and its current value. The streaming catalog is small and old, quality is poor, and broadband penetration is low in 2007. Offering this at no extra charge invites cannibalization of the highly profitable DVD core without providing a compelling alternative that justifies the high licensing fees. The "better way" is to wait for broadband maturity and catalog depth, or to launch a separate, priced tier to test willingness to pay without eroding the core margin.

The strongest attack is the economic contradiction: paying high prices for inferior goods to give away for free, while risking the destruction of the primary revenue engine. The proposal admits the risks (licensing cost, cannibalization, tech cost, competition) but offers no mitigation other than hope. A "go" decision here assumes that being first with a bad product is better than being second with a good one, which contradicts the company's current strength in logistics and curation. The pre-mortem is clear: the company burns cash on licenses, angers studios, confuses users with poor quality, and accelerates DVD churn without gaining enough streaming loyalty to offset the loss.

This fails the blocker test. The stated facts (high cost, low value, high cannibalization risk) directly contradict the path to profitability or strategic advantage as proposed. The plan does not beat "do nothing" or "wait and learn via partnership." It is a premature surrender of margins for an unproven, low-quality channel.

DECISION: NO-GO
