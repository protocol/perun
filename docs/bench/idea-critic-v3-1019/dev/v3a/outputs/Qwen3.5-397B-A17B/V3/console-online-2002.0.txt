The claim is that a mandatory, paid, broadband-only online service will succeed despite market norms of free, publisher-run multiplayer and low broadband penetration in 2002.

**Steelman:** A unified, high-quality service creates a premium ecosystem that differentiates the console, justifying the hardware and subscription through superior reliability, identity, and social features that fragmented free services cannot match.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Fit:** In 2002, broadband adoption is critically low. Mandating it excludes the vast majority of the installed base using dial-up, artificially capping the addressable market for both the service and games utilizing it.
2.  **Pricing Friction:** Consumers accustomed to free online play face a "double dip" (console cost + annual fee). This creates a massive barrier to entry compared to rivals offering free alternatives.
3.  **Operational Risk:** The company lacks experience running large-scale hosting and support operations. Failure here damages the core brand, not just a peripheral software title.
4.  **Publisher Alienation:** Removing control from publishers may reduce third-party enthusiasm if they cannot monetize or manage their own communities.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing/Status Quo:* Allow publishers to handle online play (free or paid individually). Lower risk, but no unified differentiation.
*   *Hybrid Model:* Offer the service as optional or free initially to build the user base, monetizing later once broadband adoption matures and habit is formed.
*   *Dial-up Support:* Include dial-up compatibility to maximize reach, even if performance is inferior.

**Pre-mortem:** The service launches with a tiny user base due to broadband requirements. Gamers refuse to pay an annual fee for a ghost town. Publishers bypass the system. The company burns cash on unused server capacity and support infrastructure, forcing a shutdown within 18 months.

**Blocker:** The combination of mandatory broadband (excluding most users) and mandatory payment (contradicting user expectations) in an era where the company has zero operational track record creates a fatal adoption gap. The "premium" argument fails if the user base is too small to facilitate matchmaking.

**Decision:** The risks outweigh the potential differentiation. The proposal requires revision to address accessibility (dial-up) or pricing (freemium/optional) before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
