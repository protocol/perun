**Steelman:** Building a massively larger, efficient wide-body jet with freighter conversion capability secures long-term market dominance and meets urgent customer demand for lower seat costs, despite high upfront investment.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker claim is that supersonic transport (SST) will render subsonic giants obsolete. However, SSTs inherently suffer from high fuel consumption and sonic boom restrictions, limiting them to specific routes and premium pricing. They cannot achieve the "cost per seat" reduction the customer demands. The risk of a single airline ordering is mitigated by the sheer size of the addressable market if the unit economics hold; the request comes from the *largest* airline, signaling a shift in industry density needs.

The financial risk is extreme, but "do nothing" cedes the high-capacity segment to competitors or leaves the customer unserved, inviting them to seek other solutions. The unproven engine technology is a execution risk, not a strategic blocker, provided the supplier partnership is locked. The raised cockpit design for freighter conversion is a critical differentiator that extends the asset's lifecycle, directly addressing long-term ROI concerns.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Lose the largest customer's future fleet orders; allow competitors to define the wide-body standard.
2.  **Incremental Stretch:** Modifying existing narrow bodies fails to achieve the required 2.5x capacity and efficiency gains.
3.  **Wait for SST:** Betting on supersonic ignores the economic reality of mass market travel where cost-per-seat drives volume.

**Verdict:**
The proposal addresses a fundamental economic driver (cost per seat) that supersonic jets cannot touch. The "single airline" risk is a first-mover advantage if the product delivers on its efficiency promise. The freighter capability ensures residual value. The scale of investment is a barrier to entry that protects future margins. No factual blocker exists; the risks are manageable execution challenges inherent to pioneering a new category.

DECISION: GO
