The claim is that withdrawing licensed content to launch a subsidized, low-price streaming service will generate sufficient long-term value to offset immediate billions in lost licensing revenue, massive operating losses, and execution risks in an unproven technology domain.

**Steelman:** Direct-to-consumer ownership captures full lifetime value per user and data control, which licensing dilutes. If subscriber growth hits tens of millions rapidly, the eventual recurring revenue and equity multiple outweigh the transitional cash burn and lost licensing fees.

**Better-way:** The "do nothing" option preserves billions in guaranteed licensing cash flow with zero execution risk. A hybrid approach (licensing non-core content while building a smaller niche service) reduces burn. The proposed "all-in" strategy bets the company on a crowded market where the leader has a massive head start (100M+ vs. 0).

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This assumes the market will accept a new entrant solely on price, ignoring switching costs and content fragmentation fatigue. If growth stalls at half the target due to competition or technical failures, the company faces billions in sunk costs with no licensing safety net to fall back on. The "multi-year operating loss" creates immediate liquidity pressure without a guaranteed path to profitability.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal relies entirely on future subscriber targets ("tens of millions") which are assumptions, not facts. The leading competitor's scale (100M+) creates a high barrier to entry. The loss of existing licensing revenue is a confirmed, immediate negative cash flow, whereas subscriber revenue is speculative and delayed. The company has no proven track record running a global streaming tech platform, introducing high execution risk.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It trades certain, immediate billions in revenue for speculative future gains while exposing the firm to unlimited downside (cash burn, tech failure, cannibalization). The "do nothing" or hybrid alternatives offer better risk-adjusted returns. The lack of a proven tech platform and the sheer scale of the competitor make the "go" decision irresponsible without a pilot or phased approach, which this proposal explicitly rejects in favor of a full immediate pull.

DECISION: NO-GO
