**Steelman:** A cylindrical, always-listening speaker at $199 ($99 for members) creates a new home category, leveraging invitation-only scarcity to overcome privacy fears and past hardware failures.

**Attack:** The core claim fails on three verified facts from the proposal itself. First, the company's last hardware launch "went badly," indicating broken supply chains, quality control, or market fit processes that remain unaddressed; repeating hardware execution without fixing the root cause is reckless. Second, the primary risk—"customers may not accept an always-on microphone"—is existential. In 2014, no category exists to prove demand, and privacy skepticism is high; an always-listening device from a retailer known for poor hardware is a trust liability, not a convenience. Third, "voice recognition often fails" directly contradicts the value proposition of "answering questions." If the core mechanism is unreliable, the product delivers frustration, not utility.

**Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option avoids compounding a recent failure. A cheaper alternative is integrating voice into existing successful mobile apps where users already accept permissions, rather than building new, intrusive hardware. The proposed pricing ($199) is high for an unproven category from a distrusted hardware vendor; even the $99 member price assumes willingness to try a flawed technology.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: The device ships, voice commands fail frequently due to 2014 tech limits, privacy scandals erupt regarding always-on listening, and returns surge, destroying brand trust further. The "invitation only" strategy creates hype but cannot mask a broken user experience.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Execution Risk:** Recent hardware failure suggests incapable operations; no evidence shows this is fixed.
2. **Product-Market Fit:** No category exists, and the specific risks (privacy, reliability) are untreated showstoppers for mass adoption.
3. **Technical Viability:** Acknowledged high failure rate of voice recognition makes the core feature unusable.

This is a one-way door with high capital cost and reputation risk. The risks are not merely "possible"; they are stated probabilities that invalidate the value proposition. The plan does not beat "do nothing" or a software-first approach.

DECISION: NO-GO
