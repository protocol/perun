The claim is that acquiring the dominant, loss-making code host for $7.5B will secure the buyer's open-source pivot despite community hostility and leadership vacuums.

**Steelman:** Only immediate ownership prevents a competitor from locking up the world's primary developer graph; the premium buys an unassailable network effect that organic growth cannot match before the window closes.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financials:** Paying a massive premium for a loss-making entity with no CEO suggests overpayment. If the target is bleeding cash, the buyer inherits immediate burn without a captain to stop it.
2.  **Community Risk:** The core asset is developer trust. A historically hostile buyer acquiring a beloved tool triggers the exact "defection" risk cited. If developers flee to the free competitor upon announcement, the $7.5B purchases a hollow shell.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows the target to potentially stabilize under new leadership or be acquired by a neutral party. Buying now crystallizes the distrust narrative.
4.  **Regulatory:** Dominance plus a giant acquirer invites antitrust scrutiny that could block the deal or force destructive breakups later, wasting the integration effort.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, developers migrate en masse to the free alternative within 12 months, revenue collapses, and the buyer is left holding a toxic brand and regulatory baggage. The "independent" operation promise fails as cultural friction stalls innovation.

**Blocker:** The proposal lacks a verified mechanism to retain the developer community *before* the deal closes. Without a binding commitment from the target's leadership (who are absent) or concrete retention guarantees for key maintainers, the primary value driver (the network) is at immediate risk of vaporizing upon announcement. The absence of a permanent CEO means there is no authorized counterparty to enforce such guarantees today.

**Decision:** The risk of asset vaporization (developer flight) due to cultural mismatch and leadership void outweighs the strategic necessity. The price is unjustified without a stable captain to guarantee continuity.

DECISION: NO-GO
