**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men avoiding full-sugar drinks but rejecting artificial sweeteners, leveraging a premium price to signal innovation before the low-carb trend fades.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "satisfy neither group" test. Core cola drinkers demand full sugar for taste; diet buyers demand zero calories for health. A hybrid likely tastes inferior to the former and offers insufficient caloric reduction for the latter. The "young men" target is unsubstantiated; no evidence suggests this demographic specifically desires half-sugar over zero-sugar. Furthermore, entering a crowded space with a premium price against a rival's similar launch increases failure risk if the trend is indeed a fad. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed national launch; waiting to see if the rival succeeds or the trend persists is safer.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Protect margins, avoid cannibalizing existing SKUs, and let the rival test the market.
2.  **Diet Variant Extension:** Improve the existing diet cola flavor for men rather than creating a new sugar category.
3.  **Regional Pilot:** Test in a single market to validate the "half-sugar" taste preference before a national campaign.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradiction:** The proposal admits users wanting fewer calories already buy diet. If the product satisfies neither full-sugar nor zero-sugar needs, there is no viable market segment, rendering the revenue model impossible.
*   **Unproven Demand:** The specific desire for "half-sugar" among young men is an assumption with no cited evidence.

**Risks:** Trend expiration, brand confusion, premium price resistance.

**Kill Criteria:** If a blind taste test shows less than 40% preference over diet cola among the target demographic within 3 months, stop.

**Decision:** The fundamental value proposition is contradictory. Creating a product that potentially satisfies no one, at a higher price, during a potentially fleeting trend, without evidence of demand for the specific "mid-calorie" attribute, is a strategic error. The "do nothing" or "pilot" options are superior.

DECISION: NO-GO
