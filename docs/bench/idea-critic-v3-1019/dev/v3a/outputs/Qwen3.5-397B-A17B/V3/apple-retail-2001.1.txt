The claim is that opening high-cost proprietary retail stores will reverse losses for a struggling PC maker with low market share by enabling product demonstration and a music strategy. The steelman: direct control over the customer experience is the only way to communicate the value of a complex ecosystem (hardware + music) that third-party retailers, incentivized to sell cheaper competitors, actively undermine.

However, the "better-way" analysis reveals fatal flaws. The proposal ignores the "do nothing" or "low-capex" alternatives. In a recession, locking into long-term prime mall leases creates massive fixed costs for a company already running losses. The industry track record for PC retail is poor precisely because margins cannot support such overhead. A cheaper, reversible alternative exists: create small "shop-in-shop" demonstration zones within existing retailer locations (paying for staff training or display space rather than full leases) or launch a direct-to-consumer online configuration tool, which was emerging as a viable channel in 2001. These options solve the "demonstration" and "channel conflict" problems without the existential risk of heavy real estate liability.

The pre-mortem is clear: a recession deepens, sales volume in the new stores fails to cover the high lease and staffing costs, and the company burns through remaining cash reserves faster than if it had stayed purely wholesale. The "one-way door" nature of long-term leases makes this irreversible in the short term. The evidence provided (losses, low share, poor industry retail track record) directly contradicts the feasibility of this capital-intensive expansion. The plan bets the company on a high-fixed-cost model in a cyclical downturn when flexibility is paramount. The stated risks (lease costs, inventory, channel conflict) are not just possibilities; they are near-certainties given the premises. No kill criteria or staged pilot is proposed to mitigate the massive upfront commitment.

Blockers: The company's current financial state (losses) cannot support the high fixed costs of prime retail leases in a recession; a lower-cost alternative (shop-in-shop or online) achieves the demonstration goal without the existential risk.
Risks: Total cash burn acceleration, worsened channel conflict leading to retailer retaliation, failure to achieve volume targets.

DECISION: NO-GO
