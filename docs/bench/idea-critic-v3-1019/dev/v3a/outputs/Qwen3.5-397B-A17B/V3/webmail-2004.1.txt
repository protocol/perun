**Steelman:** Offering 1,000x standard storage with superior search disrupts a complacent market by solving the user's primary pain point (deletion anxiety), monetized via high-value contextual ads that justify infrastructure costs.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces four critical hurdles. First, the **economic model** relies on text ads matching message content. In 2004, ad CPMs for email context are unproven; if yield is low, the cost of a gigabyte per user becomes an unsustainable burn rate before scale is reached. Second, **privacy backlash** is a near-certainty. Scanning private correspondence for commercial gain invites regulatory scrutiny and user revolt, potentially destroying brand trust before launch. Third, **infrastructure strain**: serving fast search over massive datasets requires novel architecture; if latency suffers, the "fast interface" promise fails, driving users back to entrenched incumbents. Fourth, **market inertia**: users rarely switch email providers due to high switching costs (network effects, established addresses). An invitation-only launch mitigates server load but slows the viral adoption needed to prove the ad model.

**Better Ways:**
Doing nothing avoids massive capex risk. A cheaper alternative is launching with moderate storage (e.g., 100MB) to validate the ad-tech and search speed without the extreme storage liability, scaling up only as ad revenue confirms unit economics.

**Kill Criteria & Pre-mortem:**
Assume failure: The service launches, privacy groups expose the scanning mechanism, causing a PR crisis that halts user growth. Simultaneously, ad revenue per user falls 50% below projections, making the storage cost insolvent within 18 months. The "invitation-only" gate fails to create sufficient exclusivity demand.

**Verdict:**
While the storage advantage is compelling, the reliance on unproven ad-yield to cover extreme infrastructure costs, combined with the acute privacy liability of scanning content, presents a fundamental flaw in the 2004 context. The risk of brand destruction and financial insolvency before product-market fit is proven is too high. The proposal lacks a validated path to profitability that outweighs the reputational and operational risks.

DECISION: NO-GO
