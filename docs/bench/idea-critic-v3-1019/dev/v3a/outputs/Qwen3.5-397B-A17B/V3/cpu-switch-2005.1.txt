The claim is that switching to the dominant processor architecture within two years, using a translation layer, will solve thermal and roadmap deficits despite significant risks.

**Assumptions:** The translation layer can maintain acceptable performance for most users; the supply chain can meet the two-year deadline; the brand can survive becoming architecturally identical to competitors; sales freeze is temporary and outweighed by long-term gains.

**Better Ways:** 
1. Continue with the current supplier but demand a dedicated low-power variant (avoids transition pain but risks continued roadmap lag).
2. Hybrid approach: introduce the new architecture only in high-end thin models first, reducing initial risk and allowing iterative learning.
3. Do nothing: accept the status quo and risk losing the "thin laptop" segment entirely to competitors with better chips.

**Kill Criteria:** If the translation layer causes >30% performance degradation in key apps during beta; if the supplier cannot guarantee volume production within 18 months; if pre-orders for the new line fall below 50% of the previous generation's launch pace.

**Pre-mortem:** The transition fails because the translation layer is too slow, developers refuse to rebuild apps due to small market share, and the brand loses its "different" appeal without gaining enough performance to compensate. Sales freeze becomes permanent.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The risk of sales freeze is real, but the current roadmap lag guarantees eventual irrelevance in the thin laptop market. The translation layer mitigates the app compatibility risk, and the two-year window allows for marketing to reframe "sameness" as "compatibility." The pain of the previous transition is a sunk cost; avoiding this move guarantees a slower, more painful decline.

**Decision:** The proposal addresses an existential threat (roadmap lag) with a viable, albeit risky, mitigation strategy (translation layer). The cost of delay is market obsolescence. The risks are manageable with strict kill criteria on performance and supply.

DECISION: GO
