**Steelman:** A clear cola leverages positive test data to capture demand for a "purer" alternative, diversifying the portfolio against slowing category growth before competitors act.

**Attack:** The core failure mode is not execution, but category contradiction. Cola flavor is psychologically anchored to dark color; removing the color may signal "diet" or "chemical" rather than "pure," causing immediate rejection after the first novelty purchase. Test markets notoriously overstate demand for novel formats due to curiosity buying, masking poor repeat rates. If repeat rates are low, the massive national marketing spend becomes a sunk cost accelerating brand erosion. Furthermore, launching a major extension risks cannibalizing the flagship without expanding the total category pie, effectively paying to split existing volume. The risk of rival copies means any success is short-lived, while failure damages the master brand's equity permanently.

**Better Way:** Instead of a costly nationwide launch with a large campaign, pursue a limited regional rollout with minimal marketing to validate *repeat* purchase behavior over six months. This treats the product as a niche variant rather than a major brand extension, reducing downside risk. Alternatively, invest in reformulating the flagship to taste "purer" without changing its visual identity, preserving brand equity.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed if projected repeat purchase rates (not trial) do not exceed 40% within the first 90 days of a controlled launch. Do not proceed if the campaign cost exceeds 15% of projected first-year revenue.

**Verdict:** The proposal relies on trial data to justify a permanent, high-cost national commitment in a category where visual cues drive flavor expectation. The risk of high trial/low repeat is extreme, and the cannibalization threat to the flagship is unmitigated. The "purer" positioning is unproven against deep-seated consumer associations between color and taste. The plan fails to distinguish between curiosity and sustainable demand.

DECISION: NO-GO
