**Steelman:** Rapidly acquiring 130–150 sites leverages immediate scale to preempt the entrenched competitor, accepting high execution risk to secure market share before local prices adjust.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on unproven capabilities (cross-border logistics, bilingual regulation, new inventory systems) while attempting a "big bang" rollout. Opening nearly all stores within two years leaves no operational runway to fix supply chain failures before capital is fully deployed. If the new inventory system glitches or logistics stall—even briefly—130+ stores will face simultaneous stockouts, destroying the "discount" value proposition immediately upon entry. The entrenched competitor can exploit these early stumbles to cement loyalty. The risk is not just failure, but catastrophic brand damage in a new market due to overreach.

**Better Way:** A phased approach (e.g., 10–20 pilot stores) would validate the supply chain and inventory system with limited exposure. This allows for iterative learning on regulation and labeling without betting the entire expansion budget on untested processes. "Do nothing" domestically is safer than a flawed international launch that drains resources.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Assuming the goal is sustainable international growth, this pace contradicts the need for operational stability. In a pre-mortem, the cause of failure is "simultaneous system collapse across 130 locations due to unvetted logistics," resulting in massive lease liabilities and inventory write-downs. This is a one-way door; once leases are signed and stores opened, retreat is costly.

**Blockers:** The plan lacks a proven pilot for the new supply chain and inventory system. The assumption that these can be perfected while rolling out 130 stores simultaneously is contradicted by the complexity of cross-border operations and the presence of an entrenched rival. There is no evidence the team can manage this velocity without prior experience.

**Risks:** Price perception mismatch, regulatory delays, supply chain breakdown, competitor retaliation.

**Kill Criteria:** Halt if pilot stores (if they existed) show >5% stockout rates in month one. Since no pilot exists, the current plan fails the "proven capability" test required for this scale.

**Decision:** The lack of validated operations for the core enablers (supply chain, inventory system) at this velocity creates an unacceptable probability of total market entry failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
