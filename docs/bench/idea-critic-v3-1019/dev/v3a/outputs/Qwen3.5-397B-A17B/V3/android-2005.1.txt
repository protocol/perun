Steelman: Acquiring the startup secures a free, open mobile OS to preemptively expand mobile web usage, directly fueling long-term search ad revenue before competitors lock down the ecosystem.

**Skeptic Analysis**: The claim relies on the assumption that handset makers will adopt a new OS despite having no immediate incentive and facing carrier resistance. In 2005, the market is dominated by entrenched players (Symbian, Windows Mobile, BlackBerry) with established carrier relationships. A $50M expenditure for a six-person team with zero revenue and an unproven product represents a massive financial risk for a non-hardware company. The "free" software model does not solve the distribution bottleneck controlled by carriers and manufacturers who prioritize stability and existing contracts over open-source experimentation.

**Better-Way Analysis**: The alternative is "do nothing" or wait for the market to mature. Investing $50M now assumes a multi-year payback with high uncertainty. A cheaper, lower-risk alternative would be to form partnerships with existing OS vendors to optimize search integration, avoiding the colossal overhead of building and maintaining an operating system ecosystem. The proposal fails to demonstrate why building an OS is superior to licensing or partnering, given the company's core competency is search, not systems engineering.

**Kill-Criteria & Pre-mortem**: If handset adoption remains below 5% within three years due to carrier blocking, the $50M is sunk with no path to ad revenue growth. The proposal lacks a specific kill criterion for adoption rates. The pre-mortem reveals a likely failure mode: the OS becomes a fragmented, niche project that never achieves the critical mass required to move search metrics, while distracting leadership from the core search business. The contradiction is stark: the plan counts on ecosystem adoption to drive revenue, yet the stated facts confirm the ecosystem gatekeepers (carriers, makers) have "no reason to adopt." Without a mechanism to force adoption, the core claim collapses.

Blockers: The stated lack of incentive for handset makers and carrier control of distribution directly contradicts the path to the projected upside. No mechanism exists in the proposal to overcome these verified market barriers.

DECISION: NO-GO
