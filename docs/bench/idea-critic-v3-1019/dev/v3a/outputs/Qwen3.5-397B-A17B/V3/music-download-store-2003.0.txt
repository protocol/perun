**Steelman:** A dominant hardware vendor leverages its installed base to launch a legal, per-song music store at $0.99, securing major label licenses to combat piracy and lock users into its ecosystem, despite low margins and platform exclusivity.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that free file-sharing renders paid downloads obsolete. However, free sharing offers no guarantee of quality, safety, or ease of use, whereas a curated store offers reliability. The risk of labels withdrawing licenses is mitigated by their desperate need for a legal alternative to save revenue; they are partners, not adversaries, in this specific context.

Regarding margins: while per-song profit is thin, the strategic value is not direct software profit but hardware differentiation. If this store makes the portable player significantly more attractive than rivals', it drives high-margin device sales. The "platform lock-in" (works only on own computers) is not a bug but the core mechanism to prevent churn and justify the hardware premium.

The rival shift to subscriptions is a valid long-term threat, but in 2003, infrastructure for streaming is immature, and consumer willingness to pay recurring fees for ownership-like access is unproven. Per-song ownership aligns better with current consumer psychology. The "do nothing" alternative cedes the digital music future to pirates or competitors, eventually eroding the hardware moat.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if labels demand unsustainable royalties or if the proprietary format frustrates users. However, the vendor's market power suggests they can negotiate favorable terms, and the simplicity of the proposed solution reduces friction compared to complex subscription models or illegal downloads.

**Blockers:** None. The facts provided do not demonstrate an impossibility of execution or a guaranteed financial loss when hardware uplift is considered.
**Risks:** Low software margins, potential future shift to subscriptions, platform exclusivity limiting total addressable market initially. These are manageable strategic risks, not fatal flaws.

**Kill Criteria:** If major labels refuse licensing within 6 months, or if device sales do not increase by 15% within one year of launch, pivot to a broader platform or subscription model.

DECISION: GO
