The proposal attempts to enter a duopoly dominated by incumbents with entrenched loyal customer bases and iconic first-party software libraries. The claim rests on the assumption that "easier tools" and "cheaper discs" are sufficient to dislodge developers from established ecosystems. This ignores the critical reality that developers follow users, not just manufacturing margins. Without an installed base, cheaper media offers no revenue upside to offset the risk of porting to an unproven platform.

The hardware margin structure presents an immediate financial blocker. Consoles selling at thin or negative margins require massive capital reserves to subsidize hardware adoption, betting on long-term software royalties. A consumer electronics maker, lacking a history of platform subsidies and facing an incumbent launching a competing 3D system simultaneously, faces a catastrophic cash burn risk. The "no experience" factor compounds this; platform holding requires distinct operational muscles compared to manufacturing peripherals or licensed add-ons.

The timing is fatal. Launching alongside an incumbent's own 3D console splits the market narrative. Developers will not fragment efforts for a newcomer when a known quantity offers similar 3D capabilities with a guaranteed audience. The "easier tools" argument is weak; incumbents can and will replicate toolchain improvements once the market viability is proven by others. There is no unique technological moat here, only a cost structure disadvantage in a business model defined by upfront losses.

The pre-mortem is clear: the console launches, fails to secure key third-party exclusives because the install base is too small, hardware subsidies drain corporate reserves without generating software attach-rate revenue, and the product is discontinued within two years. The risks are not merely "high"; they are structural barriers that the proposed value proposition does not overcome. The plan relies on developers acting against their own economic self-interest by prioritizing marginal manufacturing savings over established market access.

DECISION: NO-GO
