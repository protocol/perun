**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only viable channel to demonstrate the integrated hardware-software music strategy, as third-party staff actively suppress the brand to push higher-margin competitors. Without controlling the narrative, the digital pivot fails.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is financial fragility. A loss-making manufacturer with 3–5% share cannot absorb the high fixed costs of prime mall leases during a recession. Industry history shows poor retail track records for PC makers. If sales do not immediately spike, the cash burn from leases will accelerate bankruptcy before the brand strategy matures.

*Alternative 1 (Do Nothing):* Continue relying on resellers. Risk: Brand remains suppressed, music strategy stalls. Cost: Low.
*Alternative 2 (Shop-in-Shop):* Negotiate dedicated, staffed zones within existing retailer footprints. Risk: Less control than owned stores. Cost: Moderate, variable.
*Alternative 3 (Staged Rollout):* Open only 4–6 flagship stores in key cities to prove the unit economics before a national mall rollout.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
While the strategy fits the "digital music" vision, the execution method (immediate national mall chain) contradicts the company's financial reality. Assuming the recession deepens, the company locks into long-term liabilities with no revenue buffer. The "channel conflict" risk is real; resellers may drop the brand entirely if bypassed, leaving the new stores as the sole, unproven outlet.

**Blocker Test:**
The proposal demands a capital-intensive expansion (high lease costs) by a company explicitly described as running losses and financially weak. The math does not work: you cannot fund a high-burn retail network with negative operating income during a downturn without external capital, which is not mentioned. The plan assumes immediate success to fund its own expansion, a fatal flaw given the "poor retail track record" of peers. The risk is not theoretical; the balance sheet cannot support the fixed costs if the "demonstration" effect takes quarters to materialize.

**Decision:**
The strategic goal is sound, but the proposed vehicle (immediate full-chain rollout) is a lethal financial mismatch for a struggling manufacturer. The plan must be revised to a low-capital pilot (Shop-in-Shop or single flagships) to verify unit economics before committing to mall leases. Approving this as proposed invites insolvency.

DECISION: NO-GO
