Steelman: Acquiring the dominant cross-platform messaging app is the only viable defense against mobile displacement, securing the network effect before a competitor does, regardless of current revenue.

**Skeptic**: The price ($19B) represents a >12x increase from the prior $1.5B valuation in just one year, despite minimal revenue and an explicit anti-advertising stance by the target. The unit economics are nonexistent; paying this premium for 50 employees and unproven monetization assumes the status quo (no ads, no fees) can be radically altered without destroying the user base. Privacy regulations and founder retention are critical single points of failure; if the founders leave or regulators block data integration, the asset becomes a costly, isolated silo.

**Better-way**: The alternative is "do nothing" or build/buy smaller. Building internally leverages existing engineering talent at a fraction of the cost, though it risks being too slow. Buying a smaller competitor reduces financial exposure but may not secure the necessary scale. However, given the "hundreds of millions" of users, no other target offers comparable immediate defensive value. The cost of delay is likely total irrelevance in mobile messaging, making "do nothing" an existential threat.

**Kill-criteria**: This is a one-way door with massive capital outlay. The pre-mortem reveals failure via: (1) inability to monetize without churn, (2) regulatory breakup, or (3) founder exodus post-acquisition. The proposal lacks specific kill criteria for these scenarios, relying entirely on the strategic imperative of user count over financial logic.

**Decision**: While the financials are indefensible by traditional metrics, the strategic necessity of preventing mobile displacement overrides them. The "blocker" of price is negated by the "blocker" of existential threat from competitors. The acquisition must proceed to secure the user network, but only with immediate, binding retention agreements for founders and a clear, staged plan for monetization that does not trigger immediate churn. The risk of not acting exceeds the risk of overpaying.

DECISION: GO
