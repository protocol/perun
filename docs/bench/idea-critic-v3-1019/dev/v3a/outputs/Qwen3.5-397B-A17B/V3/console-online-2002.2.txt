The claim is that a mandatory, paid, broadband-only online service will succeed despite low adoption, free competitor alternatives, and zero internal operational experience. The steelman: a unified, high-quality paid network creates a premium ecosystem that justifies the cost through superior reliability and social features, differentiating the console as a serious gaming platform.

However, the blocker test fails immediately on three verified facts provided in the proposal. First, the infrastructure prerequisite (broadband) is scarce ("few homes have it"), meaning the total addressable market is too small to sustain the service or justify the development cost. Second, the pricing model contradicts established user expectations ("players expect online play to be free") and competitor offerings (free or publisher-managed), creating an immediate adoption barrier. Third, the company lacks the specific "hosting and support operations" required, introducing a high probability of service failure at launch.

The "better way" analysis shows that a voluntary, free, or publisher-managed model (the status quo) carries significantly less risk and aligns with current market conditions. A "do nothing" approach avoids the massive sunk cost of building an unused infrastructure. The pre-mortem is clear: the service launches to a tiny user base, generates insufficient revenue to cover the novel operational costs, and damages the console's reputation due to the company's inexperience in running such a service.

The risks are not merely theoretical; they are structural contradictions between the proposal's requirements and the market reality of 2002. The plan requires users to change hardware (buy broadband), change behavior (pay for something free), and trust an unproven operator, all simultaneously. No kill criteria can save a plan where the fundamental premises (market readiness, willingness to pay, operational capability) are currently false. This is a one-way door with a high cost of delay only if the market were ready, which the evidence states it is not.

DECISION: NO-GO
