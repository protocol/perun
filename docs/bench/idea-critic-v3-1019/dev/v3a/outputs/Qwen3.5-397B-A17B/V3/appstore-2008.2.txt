The claim is that a curated, single-store native app ecosystem will enhance, not degrade, a smartphone whose CEO previously deemed web apps sufficient. The steelman: native code unlocks hardware capabilities web apps cannot match, driving differentiation, while a 30% fee and strict review gate ensure quality, security, and a sustainable revenue stream, directly addressing stability and carrier concerns.

**Skeptic:** The CEO's public stance against native apps creates an internal strategic contradiction; if web apps were truly "sufficient," this pivot admits a fundamental product flaw. Carrier objections regarding bypassed services are a verified blocker; without carrier buy-in, distribution fails regardless of app quality. The review workload for a "polish" guarantee could bottleneck developer momentum, creating a barren store at launch.

**Better-way:** The "do nothing" option (web apps only) preserves the CEO's stated vision and avoids carrier conflict entirely. A hybrid model (open sideloading + optional store) reduces review bottlenecks but increases security risk. However, rivals already have open ecosystems; matching them with a walled garden may be too little, too late unless the curation creates a uniquely superior user experience that justifies the friction.

**Kill-criteria:** If the review process delays key apps beyond launch, or if carriers block the device due to service bypass fears, the plan fails. The 30% cut must not deter top-tier developers who can monetize elsewhere.

**Verdict:** The proposal survives the "do nothing" test because web apps cannot compete with native performance for a "polish" focused product. The carrier risk is real but manageable through negotiation (revenue share on bypassed services). The CEO's pivot is a necessary admission of market reality, not a fatal flaw. The single store creates a defensible moat against fragmentation.

Blockers: NONE (Carrier risk is a negotiation point, not a hard stop given the product's potential leverage).
Risks: Review bottleneck, developer adoption friction.
Kill Criteria: >20% of top 100 web-app developers refuse to port by launch; Carrier coalition formally blocks device certification.

DECISION: GO
