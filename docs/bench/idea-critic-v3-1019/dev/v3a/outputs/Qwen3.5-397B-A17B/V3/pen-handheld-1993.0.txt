The claim is that a $700 pen-based PDA with heavy CEO-led publicity will successfully create and lead a new handheld computer category in 1993.

Steelman: Only a bold, integrated hardware-software launch with top-down advocacy can define an unproven category before competitors fragment the market.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts provided in the text:
1.  **Core Mechanism Failure:** The primary input method (handwriting recognition) is explicitly "unreliable in testing." A device whose main feature does not work reliably cannot lead a category; it defines the category as broken.
2.  **Form Factor Mismatch:** The device is "too big for a pocket," violating the fundamental utility of a "handheld" personal assistant.
3.  **Ecosystem Void:** There is "little software." Without applications, the hardware has no utility, regardless of publicity.

Alternatives considered:
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids sinking capital into a defective product.
*   **Delay/Revise:** Postpone launch until recognition is reliable and the form factor fits a pocket. This is the only path to viability.
*   **Cheaper Pilot:** Release a developer kit or software-first approach, but the hardware flaws block even this.

Pre-mortem: The product launches to massive publicity, users immediately encounter unusable handwriting input and an awkward size, reviewers pan the reliability, developers ignore the platform due to low adoption, and the company burns cash while cementing the category as a gimmick. The "unproven" category becomes a "failed" category.

The risks listed are not merely potential issues; they are current, verified states that directly contradict the proposal's requirement for a market-leading launch. Unreliable input and poor portability are not risks to be managed; they are disqualifiers for a consumer electronics launch. Publicity amplifies failure when the product is defective.

Blockers:
1.  Unreliable core input mechanism (handwriting).
2.  Non-portable form factor (too big for pocket).
3.  Lack of software ecosystem.

Risks:
1.  High price point resistance.
2.  Category education costs.

No amount of CEO publicity can overcome a product that fails its primary function. The plan must be halted until the core technology works and the design fits the use case.
