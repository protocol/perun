**Steelman:** Transforming from a pure retailer into a hybrid marketplace creates immediate inventory depth and revenue via fees without capital expenditure, leveraging existing traffic to solve profitability constraints.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the conflict between short-term margin protection and long-term platform viability. The proposal admits sellers will undercut internal prices. If the retailer blocks this to protect internal sales, the marketplace fails due to lack of selection/price competitiveness. If allowed, internal retail margins collapse immediately. Since the entity is *not yet profitable*, accelerating margin erosion on core goods before fee volume scales is a fatal cash-flow risk.

Alternatives:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain control, slower growth, preserve current (albeit negative) margin structure.
2.  **Segregated Marketplace:** Launch a distinct "Used/Third-Party" section to protect brand trust and isolate cannibalization, though this reduces the "single page" conversion benefit.
3.  **Category Pilot:** Restrict third-party selling to non-core categories (e.g., out-of-print books only) to test operations without threatening primary supplier relationships or new-book margins.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
The strategy fits a "profitability via scale" vision but ignores the "valley of death" where internal sales drop faster than fee income rises. A pre-mortem reveals suppliers delisting products in retaliation for hosting competitors, causing an inventory crisis that fees cannot offset. The risk of customer trust erosion from variable seller quality is high given the lack of stated governance mechanisms.

**Blocker Test:**
*Blocker:* The proposal lacks a financial model demonstrating that fee revenue from undercutting sellers exceeds the lost contribution margin from displaced internal sales during the unprofitable phase. Without proof that the net unit economics are positive *immediately*, the plan accelerates cash burn.
*Risks:* Supplier retaliation, brand degradation, operational complexity of fraud/quality control.

**Decision:**
The plan fails the blocker test. It proposes a structural shift that threatens the only existing revenue stream (internal retail) with unproven replacement income (fees) while the company is already unprofitable. The cannibalization risk is not mitigated; it is the core feature. Approval requires a pilot with capped exposure or a proven net-positive unit economic model, neither of which is present.

DECISION: NO-GO
