Steelman: Transforming from a pure retailer into a hybrid marketplace creates immediate inventory depth and revenue via fees without capital expenditure, leveraging existing traffic to solve profitability constraints.

**Skeptic Analysis:** The proposal admits sellers will undercut the retailer's own prices. Since the retailer is not yet profitable, cannibalizing high-margin new sales with lower-fee third-party sales (especially used items) risks deepening losses unless the fee structure and volume uplift mathematically offset the margin erosion. The claim that trust will not suffer contradicts the admitted risk of "sellers misbehave" and "less control." Without a verified escrow system or strict SLA enforcement mechanism detailed in the proposal, customer trust is an unsupported assumption.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" option preserves current margins and brand control while the retailer focuses on core inventory profitability. A safer alternative is a pilot program limited to non-competing categories or used-only items to test cannibalization rates before exposing core new inventory to competitors.

**Kill-Criteria & Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Customer service complaints spike due to third-party failures, causing a net loss of lifetime value that exceeds fee revenue. The proposal lacks a specific metric to monitor seller performance or a clear "stop" condition if cannibalization exceeds a defined threshold (e.g., if internal sales drop >15% without a corresponding 20% increase in total page revenue). The risk of supplier retaliation is treated as a possibility rather than a blocker; if key publishers withdraw supply, the core business collapses.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal presents a fundamental contradiction: it seeks profitability while intentionally introducing lower-priced competitors to undercut its own offers on the same page, without providing evidence that fee revenue will outpace the loss of direct sales margin. It relies on unproven assumptions regarding customer trust retention and supplier tolerance. The risks are existential (supplier revolt, brand erosion), and the mitigation strategies are absent. A plan that explicitly invites competitors to degrade the primary value proposition (price/availability) without a validated economic model or safety gates is a blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
