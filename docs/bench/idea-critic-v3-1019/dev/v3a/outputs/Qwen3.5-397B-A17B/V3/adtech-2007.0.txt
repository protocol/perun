**Steelman:** Acquiring the agency and platform instantly grants critical scale and technology to challenge the search leader's recent move, preventing permanent market exclusion.

**Attack:** The $6 billion cash price represents a massive premium in a fast-changing market, risking severe capital depletion for unproven display technology. The proposal admits inherent conflicts with existing agency customers, threatening immediate revenue erosion from the buyer's core base. Unlike search, display ad technology is volatile; buying a static asset now may result in rapid obsolescence, leaving the company with a depreciating monopoly on outdated tech while the competitor integrates more flexibly. The "fast-changing" nature of the market contradicts the logic of a large, rigid cash acquisition rather than a partnership or smaller staged investment.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve cash, avoid customer conflicts, but cede display leadership to the competitor.
2.  **Partner/License:** Access technology without ownership risk or customer conflict, though with less control.
3.  **Staged Acquisition:** Buy a smaller stake first to validate technology stability and customer retention before full integration.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-acquisition, the company has lost key agency clients due to conflicts, the technology has shifted rendering the purchase obsolete, and the $6 billion cash outflow cripples R&D for core search products. The competitor wins by default.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Confirmed Customer Conflict:** The proposal explicitly states conflicts with existing customers, directly undermining current revenue stability.
2.  **Market Volatility vs. Fixed Cost:** Paying a large premium for "fast-changing" technology is a fundamental financial mismatch; the asset's value could halve before integration completes.
3.  **Cash Concentration:** Deploying the largest ever cash reserve on a single, non-core experimental bet violates prudent capital allocation during a technological shift.

**Risks:** Integration failure, regulatory scrutiny, accelerated client churn.

**Conclusion:** The explicit admission of customer conflicts and the volatility of the target market make the proposed all-cash, full-premium purchase a fatal strategic error. The risks outweigh the defensive necessity.

DECISION: NO-GO
