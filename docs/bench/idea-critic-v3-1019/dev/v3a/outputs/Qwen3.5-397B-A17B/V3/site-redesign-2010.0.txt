The claim is that a full rewrite with altered voting mechanics and a forced migration will reverse slowing traffic. The steelman: only a radical overhaul can modernize the platform enough to outpace rivals and attract publishers, justifying the high risk of alienating the core base.

However, three fatal blockers exist under the stated constraints:
1. **Community Alienation:** The core community explicitly values existing voting mechanics. Changing these to favor publishers directly attacks the primary value proposition for the loyal users driving current traffic. Losing them guarantees a traffic drop before new publisher content can compensate.
2. **Irreversibility:** A "move all users at once" strategy with "no rollback" violates basic risk management for a large engineering rewrite. If the new version fails (a high probability given the scope), the site dies instantly with no recovery path.
3. **Unproven Demand:** There is no evidence provided that publishers want this specific placement or that new users prefer the proposed surfacing method over the incumbent rival. The plan assumes a market shift without validation.

Better ways exist that do not carry existential risk: A/B testing the new surfacing algorithm on a subset of traffic, or launching a parallel "publisher" section without altering the core voting mechanics for existing stories. These approaches allow learning without betting the entire company on a single untested build.

The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed launch. Waiting to validate the new mechanics with a segment of users prevents total collapse. The proposal fails the "one-way door" test; it treats a reversible product iteration as an irreversible infrastructure mandate.

Because the plan guarantees the loss of the core user base while offering no safety net for engineering failure, it cannot succeed as proposed. The risk is not just high; it is total and unnecessary.

DECISION: NO-GO
