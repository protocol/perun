**Steelman:** Acquiring the leading mobile-native photo network for $1B eliminates an existential threat to the buyer's core business during its IPO window, securing mobile relevance instantly despite the lack of current revenue.

**Analysis:**
The valuation appears absurd based on traditional metrics (no revenue, ~12 employees). However, the strategic context overrides financials. The buyer is entering a public market while its own mobile product is weak. The target is the "leading mobile threat" with tens of millions of users and rapid cross-platform growth.

*   **Do Nothing:** The threat remains independent, likely growing faster than the buyer can build. Post-IPO, the market will punish the buyer for lacking a credible mobile photo strategy if the competitor succeeds. The cost of building a competitor from scratch exceeds $1B in lost opportunity and time.
*   **Alternatives:** Building internally is too slow given the "fast growth" of the target. Waiting for regulatory clarity risks the target being bought by a different rival or growing unassailable.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if integration destroys the culture that drives growth, or if regulators block the deal. The cultural risk is mitigated by keeping the team autonomous. Regulatory risk is real but manageable compared to the certainty of competitive displacement.
*   **Evidence:** The proposal states the target is the *leading* threat. In platform wars, owning the distribution layer (mobile photos) is worth a premium to prevent a rival from locking it down. The IPO timing creates a "now or never" pressure; delaying weakens the buyer's stock currency and market narrative.

**Blockers:** NONE. The high price is a feature, not a bug, reflecting the cost of survival in a winner-take-all mobile shift. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal that prevents success; the risks are executional, not fundamental flaws in the thesis.

**Risks:** Cultural attrition post-acquisition; regulatory delays.
**Kill Criteria:** If user growth stalls >20% within 6 months post-close, or if key founders depart within 3 months, initiate strategic review.

DECISION: GO
