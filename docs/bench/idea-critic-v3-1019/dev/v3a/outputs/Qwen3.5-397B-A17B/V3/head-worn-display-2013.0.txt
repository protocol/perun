The proposal fails the blocker test. The stated facts explicitly identify that there is "no clear everyday use for most people," yet the strategy positions the device as an "everyday consumer product" for the general public. This is a direct contradiction between the verified market reality (no use case) and the core revenue claim (mass adoption).

Furthermore, the "invitation-only" launch at $1,500 does not resolve the fundamental utility gap; it merely delays the confrontation with the lack of demand while adding social friction. The risks listed are not merely uncertainties; they are known defects that prevent the product from functioning as proposed in public spaces. Privacy objections and social unacceptability act as immediate adoption barriers that price reductions or software updates cannot easily fix post-launch.

A "cheaper, smaller, staged" alternative would be to target specific enterprise verticals (e.g., manufacturing, medical) where the camera and hands-free operation have proven, high-value workflows, rather than forcing a consumer narrative that the facts say does not exist. The current plan bets on creating a new mass market behavior without evidence that the behavior is desirable or feasible.

Because the proposal's own evidence ("no clear everyday use") contradicts its primary goal ("everyday consumer product"), and because the listed risks represent fundamental blockers to public usage rather than solvable execution details, the plan cannot succeed as written. Proceeding would burn capital on a positioning strategy that the market facts already refute.

DECISION: NO-GO
