Steelman: An invitation-only launch at $1,500 validates demand for a head-worn camera while mitigating initial privacy backlash through exclusivity.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The stated facts reveal a fundamental contradiction between the product's core mechanism (a always-on wearable camera) and the market conditions required for an "everyday consumer product." The risks listed are not merely execution hurdles; they are structural blockers that the proposed pricing and launch strategy do not resolve.

First, the "high price" ($1,500 in 2013) combined with "no clear everyday use" creates a value gap that early adopters cannot bridge solely through novelty. Early adopters tolerate bugs, not undefined utility. Without a killer app, the device remains a solution searching for a problem, making the transition from "invitation-only" to "general public" impossible.

Second, "privacy objections" and "social acceptance" are not risks to be managed post-launch; they are adoption barriers that render the product unusable in the very contexts (public spaces) where an "everyday" device must function. A camera that triggers hostility or legal bans in public spaces cannot achieve the network effects required for a consumer platform. The proposal offers no technical or policy mechanism (e.g., hardware recording indicators, geofencing) to neutralize this friction.

Finally, "short battery life" contradicts the "everyday" positioning. A device that cannot last a full day fails the primary utility test of a wearable.

The proposal attempts to use an expensive, limited beta to solve problems that are actually product-definition failures. No amount of exclusivity fixes a device that society rejects, that lacks a daily use case, and that dies before noon. The plan assumes the product works as intended; the facts state it does not yet meet the basic thresholds for consumer viability.

Blockers:
1. No defined everyday use case to justify $1,500 price point.
2. Unmitigated privacy/social rejection prevents public usage.
3. Battery life insufficient for "everyday" wear.

Risks:
1. Brand damage from perceived surveillance.
2. Inventory write-downs if general public launch fails.

DECISION: NO-GO
