**Steelman:** Acquiring the startup secures a free, open mobile OS to accelerate mobile web adoption, directly expanding the search advertising market before competitors lock down distribution.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the mismatch between a software-only ad company and a hardware-dependent ecosystem. Carriers and handset makers currently control distribution and have little incentive to adopt an unproven, revenue-less platform from an outsider. The $50M price tag for a six-person team with no revenue model represents a massive valuation premium on pure speculation.

Alternatives include:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Wait for the market to mature, risking exclusion if incumbents close their gardens.
2.  **Partnership:** License technology without full acquisition, reducing capital risk but offering less control over the open-source trajectory.
3.  **Internal Build:** Attempt to build an OS internally, which is likely slower and lacks the startup's secretive head-start.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if carriers refuse to preload the OS or if handset makers prioritize their own proprietary platforms. The "open source" angle may attract developers but not distribution gatekeepers. The multi-year payback conflicts with quarterly earnings expectations, potentially causing internal strategic drift.

**Evidence & Fit:** The proposal relies on the assumption that "free software" alone disrupts carrier/handset dynamics. There is no evidence provided that carriers are willing to cede control or that users demand a new OS over established options. The strategic fit depends entirely on the unproven hypothesis that mobile web usage will explode *if* this specific OS exists.

**Blocker Test:**
*   **Blocker:** No verified path to distribution (carrier/handset adoption). The proposal admits carriers control distribution and have "no reason to adopt." Without a committed partner or a mechanism to bypass them, the asset cannot generate the required ad revenue.
*   **Risk:** Long payback and no revenue model are severe but manageable if distribution were solved. However, without distribution, the revenue model is irrelevant.

The lack of a concrete distribution strategy constitutes a factual blocker. The plan fails as proposed because it assumes the market will accept the OS simply because it is free and open, ignoring the entrenched power of carriers and handset makers.

DECISION: NO-GO
