**Steelman:** A mandatory client centralizes updates and anti-cheat, solving critical delivery and integrity failures that boxed media cannot address, while creating a future digital distribution platform.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the 2003 reality: home broadband penetration is low, and mandatory online requirements for single-player experiences alienate the core retail customer base. Retail partners, fearing obsolescence, may retaliate by delisting titles. The "store" vision is a distant second-order benefit that cannot justify immediate friction on current revenue drivers.

**Better Way:** Ship the mandatory client *only* for multiplayer components or as an optional "premium" updater, keeping boxed versions fully functional offline. This preserves retail relationships and respects connectivity constraints while still deploying anti-cheat where it matters most. A forced, universal mandate risks a consumer backlash that could tank the very games funding the platform.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If adopted as proposed, the most likely failure mode is a coordinated retail boycott combined with a user revolt over connectivity issues, leading to a sharp sales decline before the digital store can mature. The assumption that "players will accept it" contradicts the stated risk of dislike without a mitigation strategy.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Connectivity Mismatch:** The plan mandates online access in an era where universal broadband is not a fact; this excludes a significant portion of the addressable market defined by current retail sales.
2.  **Channel Conflict:** The proposal offers no strategy to prevent retail partners from dropping the product, directly threatening the primary revenue stream.

**Risks:** Early client bugs could permanently brand the ecosystem as broken; competitors copying the idea is irrelevant if the first mover fails due to adoption friction.

**Kill Criteria:** If retail pre-orders drop >15% upon announcement of the mandatory requirement, or if beta testing shows >20% of users cannot complete the initial handshake, the rollout must halt.

The plan fails because it treats a future strategic advantage (digital store) as a justification for breaking current distribution mechanics without a bridge. It bets the company on an infrastructure shift that has not yet fully occurred.

DECISION: NO-GO
