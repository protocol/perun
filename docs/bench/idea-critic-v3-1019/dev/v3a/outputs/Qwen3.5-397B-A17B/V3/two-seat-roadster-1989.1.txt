**Steelman:** A mid-size manufacturer can profitably capture an underserved niche by launching a lightweight, affordable roadster, leveraging low development costs to offset limited volume, despite the segment's historical decline and lack of brand heritage.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on the assumption that "affordable" and "lightweight" can coexist with 1989 safety regulations without eroding margins. If safety compliance forces heavy reinforcement, the car loses its performance advantage and price edge, becoming a slow, expensive compromise. Furthermore, entering a shrinking market without heritage requires capturing enthusiasts who typically demand proven pedigree; if the brand cannot command premium perception, volume will fail to cover fixed costs. The risk of "sales fade after early enthusiasts" suggests a business model dependent on a one-time surge rather than sustainable demand.

**Better Way:** Before full launch, validate the "affordable" claim against current regulatory costs. If compliance destroys the margin, the project fails. Alternatively, license an existing platform to reduce R&D risk, though this may compromise the "lightweight" goal. Doing nothing preserves capital in a declining segment.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Assuming the goal is brand revitalization, a failure here damages reputation more than silence. If this is a one-way door with high tooling costs, a premature launch based on optimistic volume projections is fatal. The "shrinking segment" trend contradicts the growth required to justify new tooling.

**Blockers:**
1. **Contradiction:** The proposal cites a "shrunk segment" yet implies sufficient volume for a new tooling investment. Without evidence that the shrinkage has bottomed out or that the specific "affordable" price point expands the total addressable market significantly, the revenue model is unsupported.
2. **Cost Structure:** No data proves "affordable" pricing covers the increased unit cost of 1989 safety-compliant convertibles. If the margin is negative, the plan fails immediately.

**Risks:** Brand dilution if quality/perception misses; rapid sales collapse post-launch; regulatory cost overruns.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal presents a high-risk entry into a declining market with unverified unit economics. The core contradiction between a shrinking niche and the need for sustainable volume, combined with unproven cost assumptions regarding safety compliance, constitutes a blocker. The plan does not demonstrate how it overcomes the structural decline of the segment.

DECISION: NO-GO
