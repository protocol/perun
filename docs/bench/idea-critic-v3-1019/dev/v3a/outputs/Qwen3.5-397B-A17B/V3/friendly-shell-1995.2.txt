**Steelman:** A $100 cartoon-house shell makes PCs approachable for novices on high-end hardware, creating an immediate bridge before the OS update arrives.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails on three verified constraints within the text itself. First, the hardware mismatch is fatal: the target audience ("many new home users") typically owns standard PCs, yet the product requires a "high-end PC." The market for users who are both novices *and* possess expensive, cutting-edge hardware in 1995 is too small to sustain a $100 product launch.

Second, the timing creates a guaranteed obsolescence risk. With a "major new operating-system release" arriving in months featuring its own interface, investing in a separate shell is strategically unsound. Users will not pay $100 for a temporary fix when a native, likely integrated solution is imminent. This renders the product a "bridge to nowhere."

Third, the value proposition is weak against the "patronizing" risk. Experienced users (who often influence household purchases) will reject it, while the novice segment is better served by waiting for the OS vendor's native solution, which will have better performance and no extra cost. The lack of third-party add-ons further limits longevity, making the "rooms" concept a dead end rather than a platform.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Wait for the OS vendor's upcoming release. Cost: $0. Risk: Low (native integration).
2.  **Educational Bundle:** Create low-cost tutorials for the existing OS. Cost: Low. Risk: Moderate (less novel).
3.  **The Proposal:** High cost, high hardware barrier, immediate obsolescence.

**Conclusion:**
The plan relies on a non-existent market segment (novices with high-end PCs) and ignores the imminent competitive threat from the OS update. The "blocker" is the hardware requirement conflicting with the target demographic's reality, combined with the short shelf-life due to the upcoming OS release. This is a "no-go" as proposed; it should be killed or radically pivoted to software training for the current OS.

DECISION: NO-GO
