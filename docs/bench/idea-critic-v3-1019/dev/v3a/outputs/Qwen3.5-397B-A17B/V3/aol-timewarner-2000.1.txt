**Steelman:** Merging dominant content with the fastest-growing distribution channel creates an unbeatable synergy, monetizing legacy assets through new technology while accelerating broadband adoption via cable infrastructure.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on the internet provider's volatile stock valuation remaining high to fund the acquisition, yet its core product (dial-up) is technologically obsolete compared to the very broadband the merger promises to deliver. The "synergy" assumes seamless integration between a fast-moving digital culture and a slow, rights-fragmented traditional media bureaucracy, ignoring historical friction in such mergers. Furthermore, the pitch depends on cable partners granting network access, yet these partners are now direct competitors who may block or degrade the combined entity's service. The premium paid to the media conglomerate locks in value based on a bubble that may burst before integration completes.

**Better Way:** The media conglomerate should license content digitally to multiple providers rather than merging with one specific, overvalued distributor. The internet provider should focus on upgrading its own infrastructure or partnering with multiple cable firms without the baggage of a massive stock-swap merger. A smaller, cash-based joint venture for specific content portals would test synergies without risking the entire corporate structure.

**Kill Criteria:** If the internet provider's subscriber count drops by more than 10% in the first year post-merger, or if cable partners refuse open access terms within six months, the synergy thesis fails. The deal assumes regulatory approval despite creating a monopoly-like vertical integration that regulators in 2000 are increasingly scrutinizing.

**Pre-mortem:** In two years, the stock has collapsed, dial-up revenue has evaporated faster than broadband profits emerged, and cultural infighting has paralyzed decision-making, leaving the company unable to compete with nimbler, pure-play digital rivals.

**Decision:** The risks of overpaying with inflated currency, the technological obsolescence of the primary asset, and the unproven cultural/operational fit constitute a blocker. The "synergy" is theoretical; the financial exposure is immediate and catastrophic if the bubble bursts.

DECISION: NO-GO
