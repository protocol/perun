**Steelman:** Leveraging a dominant hardware footprint to create the first legal, convenient, and integrated alternative to piracy, accepting thin software margins to secure hardware loyalty.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces four existential threats: ubiquitous free substitutes, supplier power (labels), platform isolation, and a shifting industry model (subscriptions).

1.  **Substitute Good:** Free file-sharing is superior on price (zero) and selection. To win, the paid product must offer superior *convenience* and *reliability*. The "own computers only" restriction is currently a fatal flaw; if the store does not work on Windows, it cedes the majority of the PC market to pirates. The ecosystem must be opened immediately or the user base is too small to pressure labels.
2.  **Supplier Risk:** Labels are litigious and fearful. They may withdraw licenses if sales underwhelm or if piracy persists. The 99-cent price point leaves minimal margin, making the venture vulnerable to royalty hikes. However, labels have no viable legal alternative; they need this channel to survive. This creates a fragile but necessary symbiosis.
3.  **Model Mismatch:** Rivals backing subscriptions argue that ownership is obsolete. While subscriptions offer better value long-term, 2003 broadband penetration is too low for reliable streaming, and consumer psychology still favors "owning" a collection. Per-song sales match the current CD-replacement mindset better than a rental model.
4.  **Financials:** Thin margins are acceptable if the primary goal is driving high-margin hardware sales. If the store is treated as a profit center itself, it fails. It must be a loss-leader ecosystem play.

**Critical Blocker:** The restriction "works only on the company's own computers" contradicts the goal of combating "widespread" file-sharing. The addressable market of proprietary computer owners is insufficient to legitimize the catalog or convince labels to stay. Without cross-platform compatibility (specifically Windows), the store cannot achieve the critical mass required to make piracy inconvenient enough to pay for.

**Verdict:** The core value proposition (convenience over free) fails if the convenience is gated by hardware ownership. The proposal must be revised to include immediate cross-platform support before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
