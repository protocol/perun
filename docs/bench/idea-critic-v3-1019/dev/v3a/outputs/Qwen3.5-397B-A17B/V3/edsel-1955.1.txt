Steelman: Capturing the growing mid-priced segment with a dedicated brand prevents customer defection to competitors and maximizes lifetime value across the corporate portfolio.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test on three critical facts provided. First, the timing relies on a segment that "could shrink" amidst a known "recession risk." Launching a capital-intensive venture requiring new infrastructure exactly when consumer discretionary spending is likely to contract contradicts basic economic prudence. Second, the requirement for a "new dealer network" creates a massive fixed-cost burden before a single unit is sold, drastically increasing the break-even point during an economic downturn. Third, the admission of "overlap with the firm's own other brands" suggests internal cannibalization rather than net market share growth; in a shrinking market, stealing sales from oneself while incurring duplicate infrastructure costs is a path to losses, not profit.

The "extensive market research" cited is backward-looking, based on a growing market that may not exist by the 1957 debut. Research cannot predict the depth of a recession or shift consumer preference from mid-priced to low-priced essentials quickly enough to alter a hard launch schedule. The risks are not merely "maybes"; they are structural flaws in the plan's logic relative to the stated economic environment. A cheaper, safer alternative exists: delay the dealer network build-out and badge-engineer a limited mid-priced model using existing dealerships to test demand without the irreversible capital expenditure. The current plan bets the company's liquidity on a specific economic continuation that the proposal itself admits is at risk.

Blockers: 1) Launching high-fixed-cost infrastructure during a probable recession. 2) Cannibalizing existing sales while adding duplicate dealer overhead. 3) Irreversible capital commitment based on data from a potentially ending growth cycle.

Risks: Brand dilution, dealer bankruptcy, liquidity crisis.

DECISION: NO-GO
