The proposal fails the blocker test. The core claim relies on a "headline feature" (3D display) and a "one-touch" shopping tool to overcome a massive structural deficit: a forked OS lacking mainstream apps in a two-platform duopoly.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Value Mismatch:** The primary differentiator (3D display) addresses a niche use case, while the fatal flaw (missing mainstream apps) addresses the primary use case (communication, social, banking). A novel display cannot compensate for the absence of essential daily utilities.
2.  **Price vs. Utility:** Selling an unsubsidized flagship-priced device with a reduced app ecosystem contradicts basic market logic. Consumers pay flagship prices for flagship ecosystems, not for hardware gimmicks that limit functionality.
3.  **Distribution Constraint:** Exclusivity with one carrier caps the addressable market precisely when the product needs maximum traction to overcome the app gap.

**Better Way:**
The "do nothing" or "accessory" option is superior. If the goal is to drive retail purchases via camera identification, this software feature should be deployed as an app on existing dominant platforms (iOS/Android) or integrated into the successful e-reader/tablet lines. This captures the shopping utility without incurring the billions required to build and sustain a competing hardware/ecosystem play that lacks the necessary network effects.

**Pre-mortem:**
Assume failure in 18 months. The cause is not poor execution of the 3D feature, but the refusal of top-tier developers to support a niche, forked OS. The "one-touch" shopping feature becomes irrelevant if the phone cannot run the apps users need to stay connected. The high price point accelerates rejection by early adopters who realize the device is a secondary toy, not a primary communicator.

**Conclusion:**
The plan attempts to solve a software/ecosystem problem with a hardware novelty. The missing apps are a verified fact stated in the proposal; this alone blocks the value proposition at a flagship price point. No kill criteria can save a product where the core utility (apps) is absent by design.

DECISION: NO-GO
