**Verdict: REVISE**

**Steelman:** A dedicated device with frictionless cellular delivery and aggressive pricing will unlock a latent mass market for digital reading that PC-tethered competitors have failed to capture, creating a defensible ecosystem despite hardware inexperience.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The fear of print cannibalization is a false negative; digital expands total readership by enabling impulse buys and travel reading impossible with physical inventory. However, the proposal fails the **margin and publisher alignment test**. Selling new releases at a "single low price" while absorbing cellular data costs creates a unit economics black hole unless the hardware is subsidized significantly, which contradicts the "never designed consumer hardware" constraint. Without a verified model showing positive contribution margin per device over its lifetime (hardware loss leader + content margin > CAC + data costs), this is a cash-burn strategy, not a business.

**Better Way:** Instead of building custom hardware from scratch (high capex, slow time-to-market), license an existing reference design or partner with an ODM to reduce execution risk. More critically, the pricing model must be revised: the "single low price" cannot be universal if it alienates publishers who control the supply. A tiered pricing model or a revenue-share agreement that guarantees publisher minimums is required to secure content inventory.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed until a financial model proves that the lifetime value of a customer acquired through this device exceeds the sum of hardware subsidy and data costs within 12 months. Additionally, secure signed letters of intent from at least three major publishers accepting the proposed royalty structure before tooling begins.

**Decision Logic:** The strategic fit is strong (owning the customer interface), but the operational plan ignores the company's lack of hardware DNA and the fragility of publisher relations. The "single low price" is a blocker without publisher buy-in, and the cellular cost assumption is unverified against projected usage. This requires a revised go-to-market plan with validated unit economics and supply chain partnerships before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
