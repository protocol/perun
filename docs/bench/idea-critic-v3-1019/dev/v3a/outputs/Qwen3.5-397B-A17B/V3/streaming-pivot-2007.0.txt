The claim is that a dominant DVD rental leader should immediately launch a free, limited streaming tier in 2007 despite high costs, poor quality, and cannibalization risks.

**Steelman:** Offering free streaming now leverages the existing subscriber base to gather critical usage data and build a digital brand before competitors lock up exclusive rights, treating early losses as a necessary R&D cost for long-term survival.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Suicide:** The proposal adds high variable costs (licensing, bandwidth) to a fixed-revenue model while offering a superior (instant) but inferior (quality/selection) product. This guarantees margin compression without a clear path to new revenue.
2.  **Cannibalization without Migration:** With a "small, mostly older" catalog and "poor" quality, the service cannot replace DVDs for most users. It merely adds cost for a feature few will use due to limited broadband, destroying profitability on both fronts.
3.  **Strategic Misstep:** Entering a commodity market against "big technology and retail companies" with a half-measure invites a price war the company cannot win. It signals desperation rather than leadership.
4.  **Door Type:** This is a one-way door regarding capital allocation and brand perception. Once launched as "free," raising prices or cutting the service later will trigger massive churn.

**Better Way:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve high margins while the DVD market peaks.
*   **Paid Pilot:** Launch a separate, paid beta to validate demand and tech without subsidizing non-users or devaluing the core product.
*   **Wait:** Let competitors burn capital proving the model and broadband penetration increases before entering with a competitive catalog.

**Verdict:**
The proposal fails the blocker test. It voluntarily destroys the current profitable unit economics (DVD) to enter a loss-leading segment with a non-competitive product, against better-funded rivals, with no mechanism to recoup costs. The "free" aspect removes the only valid signal for true user valuation. The risks are not theoretical; they are immediate mathematical certainties under the stated constraints.

DECISION: NO-GO
