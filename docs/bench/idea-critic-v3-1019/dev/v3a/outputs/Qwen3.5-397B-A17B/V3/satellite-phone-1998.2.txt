**Steelman:** This is the only viable solution for true global coverage, serving high-value travelers who cannot rely on patchy terrestrial networks, justifying the premium price to service massive debt.

**Attack:** The fundamental economics are broken. The proposal relies on a $3,000 handset and high per-minute rates for a niche business traveler segment. However, the identified risks are not merely hurdles; they are fatal contradictions. Ground cellular networks are expanding rapidly, directly eroding the unique value proposition before commercial launch even begins. If the primary advantage is "coverage where others can't," and that gap is closing weekly, the window to recoup $5 billion in borrowed capital via a tiny, price-sensitive niche is nonexistent.

The "better way" analysis favors *doing nothing* regarding this specific commercial launch structure. The cost of delay is actually beneficial here; waiting allows terrestrial networks to mature further, potentially allowing this asset to be pivoted to government/emergency use or sold at a loss rather than forcing a commercial failure that guarantees default. The debt payment schedule requires massive, immediate subscriber growth that a $3,000 bulky device cannot physically achieve in a shrinking market.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-launch, the company is bankrupt. Why? The addressable market for $3,000 phones was overestimated by 10x, while ground coverage reached 80% of target business hubs. The debt burden crushed cash flow before breakeven.

**Blocker:** The combination of $5 billion debt requiring rapid scale and a product ($3k handset, poor indoor performance) that inherently limits scale to a tiny fraction of the needed user base creates an immediate solvency risk. The spreading ground network contradicts the core revenue assumption. This is not a risk to manage; it is a mathematical impossibility under the current terms.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not launch commercially until handset costs drop below $500 and a clear path to 1 million subscribers in Year 1 is proven against current terrestrial coverage maps. Neither exists today.

DECISION: NO-GO
