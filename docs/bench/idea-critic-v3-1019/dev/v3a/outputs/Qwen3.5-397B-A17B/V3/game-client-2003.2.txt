**Steelman:** A mandatory client solves critical update and cheating failures, secures revenue, and creates a platform moat, outweighing short-term friction.

**Attack:** The proposal assumes broadband ubiquity and retailer tolerance that did not exist in 2003. Mandating online access excludes a significant portion of the PC market without reliable high-speed connections, directly contradicting the goal of maximizing sales for "very popular" titles. Retail partners, currently the primary distribution channel, face existential threat from a digital store and will likely retaliate by delisting boxed goods before the digital store gains traction. The "early bugs" risk is catastrophic here: a broken mandatory gatekeeper stops all gameplay, not just multiplayer, turning technical debt into immediate reputation suicide.

**Better Way:** Make the client optional for single-player updates and anti-cheat only for multiplayer modes. This preserves retail relationships and offline accessibility while solving the core cheating problem where it matters most.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** The strategy aims for a platform shift, but the "one-way door" of mandating it immediately invites a pre-mortem where sales collapse due to retail boycotts and consumer backlash against forced connectivity. The cost of delay is low; the cost of a botched mandatory rollout is the company itself. Competitors copying the idea is irrelevant if the first mover fails due to infrastructure mismatch.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Infrastructure Mismatch:** 2003 home broadband penetration is insufficient to support a *mandatory* online requirement for all users without alienating a massive revenue segment.
2.  **Channel Conflict:** Retail partners will drop products that undermine their physical sales model before the digital alternative is proven.

**Risks:** Early client bugs causing total game unavailability; consumer backlash against forced accounts.

**Decision:** The proposal fails the infrastructure and channel reality checks for 2003. It must be staged: optional client first, mandatory only for multiplayer, with retail safeguards. As proposed (mandatory for all new games immediately), it is too risky.

DECISION: NO-GO
