The claim is that removing the headphone jack justifies launching proprietary wireless earbuds at $159 despite high risks of ridicule, loss, and customer anger. The steelman: this forces a market shift to wireless, creating a high-margin ecosystem lock-in that offsets short-term backlash.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The primary justification relies on a "forced adoption" model, yet the evidence provided lists only negatives: odd aesthetics leading to ridicule, high loss probability, inferior battery life (5 hours), and a price point 159x higher than the included alternative (free wired). There is no stated evidence of consumer demand for wireless audio in 2016, nor any proof that the proprietary chip offers a pairing experience superior enough to overcome the $159 barrier.

The "better way" analysis shows a fatal flaw: the alternative of "do nothing" (keep the jack, offer optional accessories later) avoids all listed risks while preserving customer goodwill. The proposal admits the product looks "odd" and is "easy to lose"—functional blockers for a mass-market launch. Furthermore, angering the core user base to sell a niche, unproven accessory at a premium price contradicts basic retention logic. The risk of ridicule is not merely reputational; if the product fails visually, it becomes a cultural punchline that damages the flagship phone's brand, not just the accessory's sales.

The pre-mortem is clear: the product launches, is mocked for its design and price, users lose them immediately, battery anxiety sets in daily, and the removal of the jack is cited as the sole reason for the frustration. The company loses customers and gains no offsetting revenue because the value proposition (automatic pairing) does not outweigh the friction (cost, loss, battery).

This is a one-way door with high reversal costs (re-engineering phones to re-add jacks) and no verified demand. The plan relies on the assumption that removing a standard feature creates value rather than destroying it. Without evidence that the wireless experience is miraculously superior, this is a bet against the customer.

DECISION: NO-GO
