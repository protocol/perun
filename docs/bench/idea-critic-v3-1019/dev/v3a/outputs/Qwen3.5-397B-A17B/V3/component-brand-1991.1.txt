Steelman: Transform an invisible component into a consumer demand driver by subsidizing partner ads, forcing buyers to specify the brand and compelling manufacturers to comply.

**Attack:**
1.  **Channel Conflict:** Computer makers (the direct customers) may view this as an intrusion into their branding sovereignty, potentially triggering retaliation or reduced cooperation.
2.  **Budget Cannibalization:** Diverting a "large share" of the marketing budget to subsidies leaves little for brand-building narrative or crisis management. If the campaign fails, the core brand equity is starved.
3.  **Measurement Lag:** Unlike direct consumer goods, the link between a consumer asking for a logo and a completed computer sale is long and noisy. Attributing sales lift to the campaign will be difficult, risking premature cancellation before the effect matures.
4.  **Precedent Risk:** Being "unprecedented" means no playbook exists for execution efficiency or expected ROI timelines.

**Better Ways:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Continue relying on technical superiority and OEM relationships. Risk: Clone makers erode margins via price competition as buyers remain indifferent.
*   *Trade-Only Incentives:* Reward manufacturers directly for volume without consumer-facing branding. Risk: Does not solve the commoditization threat; buyers still don't care.
*   *Co-op Lite:* A smaller pilot program with one willing partner to test mechanics before full budget commitment.

**Kill Criteria & Pre-mortem:**
Assume failure: Manufacturers quietly refuse to place the logo prominently, or consumers ignore the mark. The kill criterion is a defined market share gain (e.g., +2%) within 18 months. If OEM participation drops below 60% of top-tier makers within 6 months, the channel revolt is real, and the program must pivot to direct incentives.

**Verdict:**
The risk of commoditization by clone makers is an existential threat that technical superiority alone cannot stop. While the channel conflict is real, it is manageable because manufacturers need the chip's performance reputation to sell their own high-end units. The "unprecedented" nature is the only path to breaking the commodity trap. The budget risk is high, but the cost of inaction (margin collapse) is higher. The proposal addresses the root cause: buyer indifference.

DECISION: GO
