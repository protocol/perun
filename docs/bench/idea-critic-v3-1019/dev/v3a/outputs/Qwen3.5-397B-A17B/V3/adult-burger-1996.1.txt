The claim rests on the belief that massive advertising can overwrite a deeply entrenched brand identity built over decades. The steelman argues that sheer spend can force a perception shift from "kids' value" to "adult premium." However, the pre-mortem reveals a critical flaw: the core customer base (families) may feel alienated by a pivot to adult exclusivity, while the target demographic (adults) inherently distrusts a "kids' brand" attempting premium status, regardless of ad volume.

The "better way" analysis highlights that doing nothing preserves the dominant market position in the family segment, whereas this plan risks cannibalizing core sales without guaranteeing new adoption. The proposed solution ignores Chesterton's Fence: the current identity exists because it works. Trying to serve two opposing masters (value-conscious families and premium-seeking adults) under one roof often dilutes the brand for both.

The strongest attack is the mismatch between the brand's foundational equity (fun, value, kids) and the proposed value proposition (premium, adult, expensive). History shows that heavy spending on a contradictory message often accelerates failure by highlighting the dissonance rather than resolving it. The risk of deterring regular buyers is not theoretical; it is a direct consequence of the pricing strategy. The proposal lacks a pilot or a reversible step, opting instead for an irreversible, industry-record budget before validating demand. This violates the principle of testing assumptions before scaling. The evidence suggests the brand cannot simply "buy" a new identity without destroying the old one first, a risk not mitigated by the plan.

Blockers: The plan assumes advertising can instantly override deep-seated brand perception without evidence, and the pricing strategy directly conflicts with the established value proposition of the core customer base.
Risks: Alienation of the primary family demographic; failure to attract adults due to brand stigma; financial loss from unproven demand.

DECISION: NO-GO
