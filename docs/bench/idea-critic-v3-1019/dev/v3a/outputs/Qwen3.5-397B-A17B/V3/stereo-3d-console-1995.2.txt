The claim is that a $180 tabletop stereoscopic 3D console with red monochrome displays will successfully bridge the gap between handheld and home console generations. The steelman is that this device offers a unique, affordable 3D experience unavailable elsewhere, leveraging existing technology to fill a market void before the next home console arrives.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The "better-way" analysis reveals fatal flaws in the value proposition compared to "do nothing." The hardware constraints (red-on-black visuals, mandatory eyepiece, tabletop-only form factor) directly contradict the core user needs for both portable and comfortable home gaming. A device that is neither truly portable nor comfortable for extended play offers no clear advantage over existing handhelds or the impending home console.

The pre-mortem confirms this: the product launches to poor reviews citing eye strain and headaches, a direct result of the stereoscopic 3D implementation on low-fidelity displays. With few games at launch, the software library cannot overcome the hardware discomfort. The "do nothing" option preserves brand reputation and capital, waiting for the superior home console due within two years. The cost of delay is negligible because the proposed product does not actually serve the target market effectively; launching it risks damaging the brand's reputation for quality and comfort.

The strongest attack is that the specific hardware limitations (red monochrome, eyepiece requirement) create a physical barrier to entry (discomfort, eye strain) that no amount of novelty can overcome for a mass-market product at $180. This is not a risk; it is a fundamental design failure that prevents the product from achieving its upside of bridging the generation gap. The alternative of waiting for the next home console or improving the current handheld line offers a safer path with higher potential upside.

Blockers: The hardware design causes physical discomfort and limits visual fidelity, making it unsuitable for mass-market adoption.
Risks: Poor software launch, brand damage, financial loss.

DECISION: NO-GO
