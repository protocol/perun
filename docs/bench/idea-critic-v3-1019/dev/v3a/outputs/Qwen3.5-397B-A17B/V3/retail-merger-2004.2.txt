Steelman: Acquiring the distressed department chain provides immediate scale and real estate to arrest the discount chain's post-restructuring sales decline, betting that merged operations and cross-selling can reverse trends faster than organic growth.

However, this claim fails the blocker test. The proposal admits both entities have declining sales, old underinvested stores, and uncertain cost savings. Combining two negative growth trajectories with distinct customer bases does not create a positive one; it aggregates liabilities. The "synergy" of selling older brands in discount stores is unproven and risks brand dilution without addressing the core issue: the physical assets are deteriorating while big-box rivals are actively growing.

The pre-mortem is clear: the merged entity burns cash integrating incompatible systems and cultures while competitors steal market share. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed integration; waiting allows the acquirer to stabilize its own post-restructuring operations first. A cheaper, safer alternative is to invest strictly in the existing discount footprint or acquire specific real estate assets without the operational baggage of a dying department store model.

The fundamental contradiction is attempting to solve a sales problem by adding a larger sales problem. The proposal offers no evidence that the combined format works, only hope that it might. The risks listed (declining sales, underinvestment, customer mismatch) are not potential future issues; they are current, verified facts that directly contradict the path to upside. There is no "go-if" condition here that can be met now; the premise itself is flawed.

Blockers:
1. Both chains have verified declining sales; combining them aggregates decline rather than reversing it.
2. Uncertain cost savings cannot justify the capital outlay for underinvested assets.
3. Customer mismatch creates execution risk with no proven bridge strategy.

Risks: Integration failure, brand dilution, accelerated cash burn against growing big-box rivals.

DECISION: NO-GO
