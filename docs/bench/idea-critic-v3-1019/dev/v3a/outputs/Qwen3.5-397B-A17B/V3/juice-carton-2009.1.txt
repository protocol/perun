The claim is that a radical visual overhaul of a legacy brand's packaging will succeed without losing shelf recognition. The steelman: the current orange-with-straw icon is dated, and a modern, minimalist aesthetic with a functional orange-shaped cap will refresh the brand, attract new buyers, and signal quality without altering the product itself.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The stated facts explicitly identify the "old image" as the brand's "main shelf cue" and note the buyer base is "loyal and older." In consumer packaged goods, visual discontinuity for this demographic is a primary driver of sales collapse. Shoppers do not read; they scan for patterns. Removing the dominant pattern (the orange with a straw) while targeting a demographic resistant to change creates a near-certain risk of "shelf blindness," where loyalists simply walk past the product, assuming it is out of stock or a different brand.

The "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the "all stores at once" approach. A nationwide, big-bang launch removes the ability to measure sales impact before total commitment. If the hypothesis that "shoppers will adapt" is false, the brand suffers catastrophic, unrecoverable revenue loss in week one. A staged rollout or A/B testing in limited markets would allow verification of the recognition hypothesis with capped downside. The proposal rejects this safer alternative in favor of maximum exposure to the identified risk.

The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of error. Waiting to test the new design in a controlled region prevents a potential national disaster. The current plan relies on the assumption that advertising can override decades of muscle memory instantly, a claim unsupported by the provided facts which suggest the opposite: the old image is the *only* cue keeping the brand visible to its core base.

Because the proposal ignores the explicit warning that the old image is the primary recognition tool and mandates a high-risk, irreversible nationwide launch without a pilot, it contains a factual blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
