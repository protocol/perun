The claim is that launching four months early in May 1995 at $399 with limited inventory and software will secure a market advantage against a rival launching in September. The steelman is that seizing the "first-mover" narrative and shelf space before the competitor arrives creates an insurmountable lead that outweighs the friction of a partial rollout.

However, the attack reveals fatal structural flaws. First, the pricing strategy is a blocker: being $100 higher than the rival's announced price for a machine with fewer games and difficult 3D programming capabilities destroys the value proposition before it starts. Consumers will not pay a premium for an inferior, unfinished ecosystem when a cheaper, fully-supported alternative is known to be arriving shortly. Second, alienating major retailers by excluding them from the initial shipment invites active retaliation, potentially freezing the console out of key distribution channels right when the competitor arrives. Third, the "few games ready" constraint contradicts the core driver of console adoption; hardware without software is a demo unit, not a product.

The pre-mortem is clear: by September, the early launch is remembered not as a triumph of speed, but as a botched, overpriced release that exhausted early adopters and angered partners, leaving the brand weakened exactly when the competitor launches with a stronger library, lower price, and full retail support. The cost of delay (waiting until September) is zero compared to the cost of a failed early launch which permanently damages retailer relationships and brand reputation.

There is no cheaper or safer alternative within the "early launch" framework; the only viable path to beat the competitor is to arrive in September with a complete lineup and competitive pricing, or to delay further to ensure superiority. Launching early with these specific constraints guarantees failure.

Blockers: Price premium without software parity; retailer alienation; incomplete developer support.
Risks: Brand damage, inventory stagnation.

DECISION: NO-GO
