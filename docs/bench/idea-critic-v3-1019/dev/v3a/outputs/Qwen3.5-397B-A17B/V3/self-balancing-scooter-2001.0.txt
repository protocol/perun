**Verdict: HOLD (Internal Recommendation to Executive Team)**

The proposal fails the blocker test on **market fit** and **regulatory viability**.

1.  **Regulatory Blocker:** The admission that legal status on sidewalks and roads is "unclear" is a fatal flaw for a personal transporter. Without explicit legal classification, the product cannot be sold for its primary use case (commuting) in major markets. Launching invites immediate bans, seizures, and liability suits, rendering the $5,000 unit unsellable to the mass market.
2.  **Economic Blocker:** At $5,000, the price point excludes the mass commuter market while the "unproven" utility beyond institutional buyers suggests insufficient value density. The factory capacity (40,000 units) implies a revenue target of $200M/year. Achieving this requires penetrating a market that currently has no defined legal lane and perceives the device as "conspicuous" with limited utility (cannot carry much). The "institutional buyer" niche (e.g., security, warehouses) is unlikely to absorb 40,000 units annually at this price point given the low carrying capacity.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "pilot only" alternative is superior. Deploy a limited batch (e.g., 500 units) strictly to controlled private campuses or institutional partners where legal status is pre-cleared. This validates the "unproven" use cases and generates real-world data on rider behavior without the catastrophic risk of a public regulatory backlash or a $200M inventory overhang.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The argument that "heavy publicity and prominent investors" guarantee market adoption was tested. History shows that hype cannot override statutory law or unit economics. Publicity may actually accelerate regulatory scrutiny if the devices appear illegally on public roads before laws are updated.

**Recommendation:** Do not approve the full factory launch. Pivot to a constrained, legally vetted pilot program to resolve the regulatory ambiguity and prove non-institutional demand before committing to mass production.

DECISION: NO-GO
