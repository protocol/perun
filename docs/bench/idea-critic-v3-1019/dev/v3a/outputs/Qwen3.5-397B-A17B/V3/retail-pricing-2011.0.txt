The claim is that replacing a coupon-driven model with everyday low pricing (EDLP) and branded shops will reverse falling sales for a mid-market department store chain. The steelman: this strategy successfully transformed a branded electronics retailer by simplifying the value proposition and elevating the brand experience, and it will do the same here by stopping revenue erosion from deep discounting.

However, the attack reveals a fatal misalignment between the strategy and the customer base. The proposal admits most sales historically come from discounted items and the customers are "trained to wait for coupons." In the electronics sector, purchase cycles are long and specs drive value; in mid-market apparel/home goods, the target demographic's behavior is explicitly defined by promotional conditioning. Removing the coupon mechanism removes the primary trigger for this specific cohort's purchase behavior.

The "better way" analysis shows the "do nothing" or "staged pilot" options are superior. A full chain-wide rollout without a pilot ignores the massive risk that the core customer base will simply stop shopping rather than adapt to the new pricing psychology. The electronics success relied on a different product category and likely a different customer mindset; assuming transferability without verification is a blocker. The cost of delay (waiting for a pilot) is low compared to the cost of a failed chain-wide implementation that alienates the entire existing revenue base immediately.

The pre-mortem is clear: sales plummet because the loyal, coupon-dependent customers feel the "everyday price" is higher than the "sale price" they expect, and they leave before new customers can be acquired. The pace of changing the entire chain at once eliminates the ability to course-correct. This is a one-way door with no evidence that the specific customer base will follow. The contradiction is stark: the plan relies on retaining customers while removing the specific mechanism (coupons) that the proposal states drives their historical purchasing.

Blockers: The proposal admits the customer base is coupon-trained and most sales are discounted, yet offers no evidence these customers will accept EDLP, creating an immediate revenue cliff.
Risks: Total alienation of the core demographic before new branding takes hold; inability to pivot due to lack of a pilot.

DECISION: NO-GO
