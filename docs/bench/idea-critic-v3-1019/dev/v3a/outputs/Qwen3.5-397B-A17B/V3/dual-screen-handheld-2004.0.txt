The proposal advocates for asymmetric competition: rejecting a direct hardware arms race in favor of a novel form factor (dual-screen, touch) to access a non-consumer market segment at a lower price point. This steelman relies on the premise that software innovation and accessibility outweigh raw graphical fidelity for a broader audience.

However, significant blockers exist within the stated facts. The "unusual hardware" risk directly threatens the software ecosystem; if developers cannot or will not adapt, the device lacks content, rendering the hardware irrelevant regardless of price. The "gimmick" perception is a valid market barrier that could prevent adoption before the value proposition is understood. Furthermore, maintaining two distinct hardware lines (old and new) while developing a complex new architecture introduces substantial execution risk and potential cost overruns that could negate the intended price advantage.

Yet, the alternative—competing directly on graphics against a well-funded rival with superior media features—is a likely path to defeat for a company trying to broaden its audience. A direct fight cedes the initiative to the competitor's strengths. The "do nothing" option preserves the core but forfeits growth. The proposed path, while risky, is the only one that creates a distinct market category where the rival's graphical superiority is less relevant. The risks regarding developers and gimmickry are real but manageable through strong first-party software support and clear marketing, whereas the risk of a direct hardware clash is structural and likely fatal to the goal of audience expansion. The kill criterion here is clear: if first-party launch titles do not demonstrably utilize the dual-screen/touch mechanics to create unique gameplay impossible on the rival's device, the strategy fails. Without that proof of concept, the hardware is just a costly curiosity. Given the necessity of differentiation to achieve the stated goal of broadening the audience, the potential upside of creating a new market segment outweighs the execution risks of the proposed hardware shift.

DECISION: GO
