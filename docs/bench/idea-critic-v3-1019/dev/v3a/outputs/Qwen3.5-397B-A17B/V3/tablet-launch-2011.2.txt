The proposal attempts to enter a mature market with an unproven ecosystem, relying on marketing spend to overcome structural deficits. The steelman is that brand loyalty and heavy advertising can substitute for network effects in the short term. However, the "better-way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw: the market leader dominates not just on hardware, but on a massive app catalog that users treat as a primary purchasing criterion. A "small app catalog" is not a temporary hurdle but a fundamental value gap that advertising cannot fix; it accelerates burn rate without securing retention.

The pre-mortem is clear: the device launches, early adopters buy it based on brand, discover the lack of essential apps, and return it. The "operating system has almost no market share" creates a chicken-and-egg problem where developers will not prioritize the platform without users, and users will not stay without apps. Investing heavily in outreach while the core product (the software ecosystem) is incomplete is a classic distribution-over-product error. The leadership transition adds execution risk to an already fragile strategy.

The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed launch that damages the brand's premium positioning. A cheaper, safer alternative exists: wait until the app catalog reaches a critical viability threshold or bundle the OS with a proven desktop ecosystem to leverage existing developer tools, rather than launching a standalone tablet into a hostile environment. The current plan bets the farm on a two-sided market dynamic that currently favors the incumbent entirely. There is no kill criterion offered that would stop the bleed if adoption stalls due to app scarcity, because that scarcity is the starting state, not a future risk. The facts provided indicate the product does not yet meet the minimum viable threshold for this specific market category.

DECISION: NO-GO
