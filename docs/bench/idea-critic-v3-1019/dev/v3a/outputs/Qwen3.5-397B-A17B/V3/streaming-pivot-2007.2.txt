The claim is that adding a low-quality, small-catalog streaming tier at no extra charge in 2007 will preserve long-term viability despite immediate margin pressure and cannibalization risks.

**Skeptic:** The premise relies on broadband adoption and licensing economics improving faster than competitors can leverage their scale. Currently, the value proposition is weak: poor quality and old titles may alienate users accustomed to DVD reliability, while offering it for free erodes pricing power before the technology is mature. If big tech enters with superior catalogs and quality, this "foot-in-the-door" strategy may fail to retain users who simply switch to the better option, leaving the company with a degraded core business and no streaming foothold.

**Better-way:** The alternative is to maintain the profitable DVD monopoly while licensing streaming rights selectively or launching a separate, premium-priced streaming brand later when technology matures. Doing nothing preserves cash flow. A cheaper path is to negotiate revenue-share deals rather than high upfront licensing fees to limit downside exposure. However, waiting risks obsolescence if the market shifts abruptly; the "do nothing" option fails if broadband adoption accelerates unexpectedly.

**Kill-criteria:** If subscriber churn exceeds 2% within six months due to service confusion or dissatisfaction, or if licensing costs consume more than 15% of operating capital without corresponding retention gains, the initiative must pivot to a paid tier or pause. The one-way door is eroding the DVD brand equity; the two-way door is adjusting the streaming catalog mix.

**Decision:** The risk of being displaced by larger entrants outweighs the short-term cannibalization. Even a flawed streaming offering establishes critical infrastructure, user data, and licensing relationships necessary for survival. The cost of delay is total market irrelevance. The proposal must proceed but with strict caps on licensing spend and immediate monitoring of churn metrics.

DECISION: GO
