The proposal demands a $1 billion capital commitment for automated warehouses and fleets across a dozen cities before validating unit economics in the single launch market. This violates the fundamental logic of startup scaling: learn cheaply, then scale.

**Steelman:** The only defensible version of this claim is that first-mover advantage in 1999 is so absolute that losing speed to competitors outweighs the certainty of burning capital on an unproven, low-margin model.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unit Economics Unknown:** Groceries have notoriously thin margins. Without data from the first city on pick-density, delivery cost per order, and spoilage rates, the model is a guess. Scaling a broken unit economy multiplies losses, not profits.
2.  **Capital Intensity vs. Flexibility:** Building "highly automated" warehouses locks the company into specific processes before knowing how customers actually behave. If the 30-minute slot proves unsustainable or too costly, the infrastructure becomes a stranded asset.
3.  **Demand Density:** Success relies on hyper-local density to make delivery viable. Expanding to 12 new cities dilutes operational focus and capital before proving density can be achieved in even one.
4.  **Historical Context:** In 1999, internet penetration and consumer trust for online grocery were nascent. A "land grab" strategy assumes immediate mass adoption which may not exist.

**Better Way:**
The alternative is a "crawl, walk, run" approach. Use the raised capital to dominate the first metropolitan area, optimize the 30-minute slot logistics, prove positive contribution margin per order, and *then* replicate the model. If automation is required, validate it in one pilot warehouse first. This preserves cash to pivot if the 30-minute promise proves economically impossible.

**Pre-mortem:**
Assume it is 2001 and the company is bankrupt. The cause: $1 billion spent on empty warehouses in cities with no customers because the cost to acquire and serve a customer in the first city exceeded the lifetime value, a fact hidden by rapid expansion.

**Blocker:**
The proposal explicitly rejects learning the unit economics of the first market before scaling. This is a factual blocker: you cannot scale what you have not proven works. The lack of an operating track record combined with low margins makes this a bet on perfection without evidence.

DECISION: NO-GO
