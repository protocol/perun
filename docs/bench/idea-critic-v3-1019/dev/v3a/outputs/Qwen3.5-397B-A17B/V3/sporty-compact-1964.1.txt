The claim is that a sporty, customizable coupe built on economy underpinnings will capture young buyers despite unproven demand. The steelman: leveraging existing mechanics minimizes capital risk while styling and marketing create a new "pony car" category, turning the "cheap" perception into "affordable fun."

**Skeptic:** The primary assumption is that style overrides the stigma of economy parts. If buyers perceive the shared components as "low quality" rather than "smart value," the brand suffers. However, the long hood/short deck proportion visually distances the product from its compact donor, mitigating this risk. The unproven demand is a risk, not a blocker; the market entry of post-war boomers creates a demographic tailwind that did not exist previously.

**Better-way:** Alternatives include upgrading the existing compact (too dull), launching a true luxury sports car (too expensive/slow), or waiting for data (cedes first-mover advantage). Doing nothing guarantees missing the demographic shift. A cheaper path fails to deliver the emotional hook required. The proposed "low base price + high-margin options" model is superior to a single high-spec trim, allowing market penetration while maximizing profit per unit from enthusiasts.

**Kill-criteria:** The one-way door is the heavy launch spend. If the advertising fails to define the category, inventory will stagnate. However, the reversibility of production is high due to shared parts; if sales flop, the line can be curtailed without unique tooling waste. The risk of "looking cheap" is neutralized by the options list, letting buyers upgrade perceived value.

**Decision:** The plan beats "do nothing" by attacking a specific, growing demographic with a low-capital-entry vehicle that has high-margin upside. The risks are managed by the shared platform (lowering failure cost) and the options strategy (maximizing revenue). The potential reward of defining a new segment outweighs the risk of unproven demand, especially given the low incremental engineering cost.

DECISION: GO
