**Steelman:** A low-cost, portable, playback-only cassette player targeting youth can create a new personal music market despite dealer skepticism and lack of prior research.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces four critical blockers: dealer resistance, perceived antisocial use, unproven demand, and easy copyability. However, these are risks, not factual contradictions.
1.  **Dealer Doubt:** Dealers often reject discontinuous innovations that cannibalize high-margin units or require new sales pitches. Their doubt reflects current market structure, not future potential.
2.  **Antisocial Concern:** "Listening alone" is the core value proposition (privacy/portability), not a bug. The risk is framing; if marketed as freedom rather than isolation, this flips to an advantage.
3.  **No Market Research:** True demand for a category that does not yet exist cannot be measured by traditional research. Waiting for proof guarantees missing the first-mover window. The "do nothing" option preserves margins but cedes the emerging youth lifestyle segment.
4.  **Copyability & Volume:** Competitors can copy only after the market is validated. The large initial run is a necessary gamble to hit the psychological price point for mass adoption; without low price, the form factor loses its purpose.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because the price remained too high due to cautious production volumes, or marketing failed to sell the "lifestyle" over the "specs."

**Kill Criteria:** Since no data exists, the decision relies on strategic conviction. The alternative ("do nothing") guarantees zero growth in this segment. The cost of delay is allowing a competitor to define the category. The risk of failure is financial (inventory), but the upside is creating an entirely new revenue stream. The lack of recording function reduces cost and size, directly enabling the price and portability goals.

**Verdict:** The objections are based on extrapolating the past (recorders must record, hi-fi is stationary). The proposal attacks the status quo with a coherent logic: lower cost + portability = new market. No factual blocker exists, only execution risks which are inherent to innovation.

DECISION: GO
