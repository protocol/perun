The claim is that building a massive, unproven wide-body jet for a single customer will secure market dominance despite existential financial risks and emerging supersonic competition.

**Skeptic:** The proposal rests on three fragile assumptions: that subsonic demand will outpace supersonic replacement, that the new high-bypass engine will mature without delaying the airframe, and that one anchor order guarantees sufficient volume to justify a factory larger than the company's entire existing footprint. If any fail, the company faces insolvency. The "raised cockpit" feature assumes freight conversion is a viable secondary market, which is unproven for this scale.

**Better-Way:** The alternative is "do nothing" or incrementally stretch current models. Developing a completely new engine supply chain and greenfield factory creates a one-way door with catastrophic reversal costs. A safer path would be partnering on the engine development before committing to airframe production or waiting for multiple airline commitments to de-risk the supersonic threat. However, delaying might cede the high-capacity niche to competitors if the supersonic timeline slips.

**Kill-Criteria:** The pre-mortem is clear: the company burns cash building a factory for a plane nobody else wants because supersonic travel renders subsonic giants obsolete, or the engine fails certification. The blocker test reveals no factual contradiction yet; the risks are severe but inherent to the opportunity size. The single order is a leading indicator, not a guarantee.

**Decision:** This is a classic "go big or go home" scenario where the cost of delay (losing the defining aircraft of the era) outweighs the risk of failure, provided the kill criteria are strict. The proposal includes specific design choices (freighter conversion) that mitigate the risk of passenger obsolescence. While the financial exposure is terrifying, the strategic necessity to define the next generation of flight forces a go decision, contingent on the engine supplier meeting milestones.

DECISION: GO
