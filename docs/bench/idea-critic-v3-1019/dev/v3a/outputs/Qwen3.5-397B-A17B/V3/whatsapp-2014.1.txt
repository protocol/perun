**Steelman:** Paying $19B secures the dominant mobile messaging layer to prevent existential displacement of the core social network, buying time to integrate a neutral utility that refuses ads.

**Attack:** The price is 12x the prior valuation with zero revenue justification; the target explicitly rejects the buyer's ad-based monetization model, creating a fundamental strategic clash. Privacy regulations and founder retention are unquantified liabilities that could erase value post-close. The "fear of displacement" is a narrative, not a verified metric proving this specific asset is the only solution.

**Better Way:** Build a competing messaging feature internally or acquire a smaller, compatible app for 1% of the cost. Wait for the target's growth to stall without monetization, lowering the entry price.

**Fit:** While mobile access is critical, paying a premium for an anti-ad philosophy contradicts the buyer's core business model. The acquisition assumes the target can be forced to monetize or that its utility alone justifies the cost, both unproven.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, founders leave within a year due to cultural friction over monetization, regulators impose heavy restrictions on data sharing between apps, and the $19B becomes a permanent impairment charge while the core business continues to lose mobile engagement to other platforms.

**Blockers:**
1. **Valuation Contradiction:** The proposal admits "no monetization plan" and "refused to show advertising," yet commits $19B based solely on user count. Without a path to ROI, the math fails the basic capital allocation test.
2. **Strategic Incompatibility:** The target's explicit refusal of ads directly blocks the buyer's only known revenue engine, making integration high-risk with no proven alternative.

**Risks:** Founder flight, regulatory breakup, inability to monetize, cultural collapse.

**Kill Criteria:** If the target does not agree to a concrete, written monetization roadmap compatible with the buyer's model within 30 days of signing, the deal terminates.

The lack of a monetization strategy and the explicit conflict regarding advertising constitute a fundamental failure of the investment thesis at this price point.

DECISION: NO-GO
