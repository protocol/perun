**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant video platform eliminates the buyer's biggest competitive threat, instantly secures the largest user base, and captures the defining cultural shift of the web before a rival does, justifying the premium as a strategic monopoly play rather than a revenue calculation.

**Attack:** The valuation ($1.65B) is roughly 100x the site's implied annual run-rate if it were even close to profitability, which it is not. The core asset—user-uploaded copyrighted content—is not an asset but a massive, unquantified liability that could result in statutory damages exceeding the purchase price if courts rule against "fair use" or safe harbor protections. The buyer's existing video service failure suggests the unit economics of streaming are broken; adding a site with higher traffic and zero revenue exacerbates the burn. The "unproven ad model" is a polite way of saying the business model may not exist. If media companies succeed in shutting down the content library via injunction, the buyer pays billions for an empty shell. The cost of bandwidth alone could bankrupt the combined entity before monetization is solved.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Continue building the smaller, compliant service; avoid massive litigation risk.
2. *License Content:* Partner with media companies legally, though this lacks the viral user-generated volume.
3. *Wait:* Let the copyright lawsuits resolve; buy the ashes for pennies if it survives, or let it die if it doesn't.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-acquisition, major studios win a landmark lawsuit, forcing the removal of 80% of popular videos. User engagement collapses. The stock price tanks as the market realizes the acquisition added massive debt/liability without revenue. The integration distracts leadership from the core search business.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Legal Existence Risk:** The primary value proposition relies on potentially infringing content. If the legal premise fails, the asset value drops to near zero immediately.
2. **Economic Viability:** No evidence exists that video ads can cover bandwidth costs at this scale; the unit economics are negative and unproven.

**Risks:** Catastrophic litigation, inability to monetize, culture clash, bandwidth insolvency.

**Kill Criteria:** If within 12 months, legal costs exceed $100M or ad revenue covers less than 20% of bandwidth costs, divest or shut down.

DECISION: NO-GO
