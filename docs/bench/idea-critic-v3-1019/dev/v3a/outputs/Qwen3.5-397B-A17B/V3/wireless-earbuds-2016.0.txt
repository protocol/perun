Steelman: Removing the headphone jack forces a transition to wireless audio, creating a high-margin accessory ecosystem that differentiates the brand and frees internal hardware space, provided the user experience (pairing, battery) feels seamless enough to offset the loss of the universal standard.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists between the proposal's premises and its intended upside. The risks cited are adoption hurdles, not logical impossibilities. The price point ($159) targets a premium segment willing to pay for convenience, distinct from the "free" wired alternative which serves a different utility tier.

**Risks:** 
1. **Cultural Ridicule:** Unfamiliar form factors often face initial mockery before becoming standard.
2. **Loss/Churn:** Small size increases replacement frequency; the charging case mitigates battery anxiety but adds cost.
3. **Customer Backlash:** Removing a legacy port creates friction for existing accessory owners.
4. **Battery Life:** Five hours may be insufficient for long-haul travelers without the case.

**Decision Logic:**
This is a **two-way door** regarding the accessory; if it fails, the company can revert to including a dongle or re-adding the jack in future iterations without existential damage. However, the strategic move to remove the jack is a **one-way door** for this specific device generation. The cost of delay is losing the first-mover advantage in defining the post-jack ecosystem. The "do nothing" option cedes the definition of the new standard to competitors. The proposed solution (custom chip for auto-pairing) directly addresses the primary friction point of wireless audio (connectivity), making the high price defensible as a premium convenience feature rather than a commodity cable replacement.

The risks are manageable through marketing (normalizing the look), insurance programs (addressing loss), and the charging case (solving battery). The potential upside—owning the entire audio stack and driving recurring accessory revenue—outweighs the transient backlash.

DECISION: GO
