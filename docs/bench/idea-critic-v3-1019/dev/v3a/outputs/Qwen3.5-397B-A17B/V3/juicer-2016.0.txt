**Steelman:** A premium, connected juicer creates a recurring revenue moat by locking users into a proprietary ecosystem of fresh, pre-packed produce, solving convenience and consistency better than manual juicing.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unit Economics Failure:** At $699, the device price is prohibitive for a single-function appliance. The subscription model requires a per-bag premium over grocery store produce to cover custom engineering, packing, and cold-chain logistics. Consumers rarely accept a 300%+ markup on raw ingredients for marginal convenience.
2.  **Friction vs. Value:** Mandatory Wi-Fi connectivity for a mechanical pressing action introduces unnecessary failure points (outages, setup friction) without delivering commensurate value (e.g., remote control is irrelevant; freshness checking via app is redundant if packs are sealed).
3.  **Market Size & Behavior:** The "countertop real estate" war is fierce. A single-use device demanding a recurring subscription competes against cheap blenders and immediate fresh juice bars. The addressable market for users willing to pay hardware premiums *plus* subscription markups for juice is likely too small to sustain a "well-funded" startup's burn rate.
4.  **Better Way:** Sell the hardware at cost (or loss) to drive subscription volume, or remove the connectivity requirement to lower price and complexity. The current model maximizes friction while minimizing the value proposition.

**Pre-mortem:** The company runs out of cash 18 months post-launch. Hardware sales stall due to price; subscription churn spikes when users realize the per-juice cost exceeds daily coffee shop visits. The "smart" features are cited in reviews as buggy gimmicks.

**Blocker:** The combined cost structure (high CAPEX for user, high OPEX per unit) contradicts mass-market adoption required to justify the custom supply chain. The value proposition does not clear the barrier of existing, cheaper alternatives.

**Decision:** The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" test and relies on unproven consumer willingness to subsidize a complex logistics network via a high upfront hardware cost.

DECISION: NO-GO
