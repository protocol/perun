**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled e-reader removes friction for book buyers, creating a closed ecosystem that secures publisher cooperation against piracy while expanding the total addressable market beyond physical logistics.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that hardware inexperience guarantees failure. However, the proposal mitigates this by targeting a specific "paper-like" utility rather than general computing, reducing the complexity surface. The "do nothing" alternative cedes the digital transition to competitors, risking long-term irrelevance as print potentially declines. Building a general tablet is costlier and invites direct competition with tech giants, whereas a single-purpose device leverages the company's core asset: the book catalog.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the vision of "any book, anywhere." If it fails, it will likely be due to battery life shortcomings or publisher refusal, not the hardware itself. The cellular cost is a customer acquisition expense, justifiable if it locks users into the store. Cannibalization is a feature, not a bug; it shifts margin from low-frequency physical sales to high-frequency digital delivery.

**Evidence & Contradictions:**
The proposal admits risks but offers no internal data proving failure. The "poor sales" of earlier e-readers likely stemmed from poor user experience (slow screens, PC dependency), which this proposal explicitly solves with cellular connectivity and paper-like displays. There is no factual contradiction between "hardware inexperience" and "success," provided the scope remains narrow.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*Blockers:* NONE. No fact presented proves the plan cannot work; earlier failures are attributed to solvable UX flaws this plan addresses.
*Risks:* Publisher pushback on pricing, hardware supply chain delays, margin compression. These are managed via the "low price" volume strategy and focused device scope.

**Decision:**
The plan transforms a risk (digital shift) into a moat. The specific constraints (cellular, single-purpose) neutralize the company's lack of general hardware experience.

DECISION: GO
