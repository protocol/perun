The claim is that acquiring the UK analytics firm for $11.7 billion will successfully pivot the hardware giant to software. Steelman: This purchase provides immediate market share, proprietary technology, and talent that would otherwise take a decade to build organically, justifying the 11x revenue multiple as a strategic necessity rather than a financial calculation.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current proposal. First, the price is detached from verified value; an 11x revenue multiple is extreme, especially when the buyer's own finance chief objects and the target's accounting has been under scrutiny since 2009. Paying a 64% premium on potentially flawed financials creates an immediate, massive impairment risk. Second, the buyer lacks software integration experience; combining rigid hardware culture with agile software operations often destroys the very value being purchased. Third, the due diligence window of "a few weeks" is grossly insufficient to validate complex enterprise accounting irregularities and cultural fit for a deal of this magnitude.

The "do nothing" or "build/buy smaller" alternatives offer better risk-adjusted returns. A smaller tuck-in acquisition or organic build avoids the catastrophic downside of overpaying for fraudulent or inflated earnings. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a $10 billion write-down.

This fails the blocker test. The combination of internal finance objections, external accounting doubts, an exorbitant premium, and rushed due diligence constitutes a concrete failure scenario: the acquisition likely destroys shareholder value immediately upon closing due to overpayment and integration failure. The risks are not merely "execution difficulties"; they are fundamental flaws in the deal structure and timing. The proposal does not address how the accounting risks are mitigated nor why the price is justified despite internal warnings.

Blockers: Internal finance objection unaddressed; accounting irregularities unverified due to rushed timeline; price exceeds reasonable strategic value without proof of synergy.
Risks: Cultural clash, talent exodus, technology obsolescence.

DECISION: NO-GO
