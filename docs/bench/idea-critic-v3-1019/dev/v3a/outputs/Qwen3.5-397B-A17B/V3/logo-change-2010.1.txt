The claim is that an untested, unannounced, immediate global logo replacement will successfully modernize a mature brand without damaging equity. The steelman: a radical, clean break signals contemporary status faster than evolution, and avoiding pre-announcement prevents "design by committee" dilution.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The facts state the old square is the primary recognition asset and loyal customers are deeply attached. Removing this identifier instantly, without testing or communication, severs the visual link to the brand's heritage and trust. In 2010, social media amplifies confusion into immediate backlash; there is no mechanism to gauge reaction or pivot. The proposal admits no customer testing was performed, meaning the core assumption—that the new design resonates—is unsupported.

Alternatives exist: a phased rollout, a hybrid transition period, or a limited market test. These reach the same upside (modernization) with significantly lower risk of alienating the existing revenue base. The cost of delay (waiting for testing) is negligible compared to the cost of a botched rebrand that erodes decades of equity. The "one-way door" nature of a simultaneous global switch, combined with zero validation, creates a high probability of failure.

The strongest attack is that the plan relies on the hope that customers will accept a stranger's face without introduction. If the new mark fails to connect (a likely risk given no testing), the brand loses its old recognition before gaining new value, creating a vacuum competitors can exploit. The lack of a kill criterion or pilot phase means failure is total before correction is possible.

Blockers: Unverified assumption that untested design works; removal of primary recognition asset without transition; high risk of immediate, unmanageable backlash.
Risks: Alienation of core loyalists; permanent brand damage; revenue drop during confusion.

DECISION: NO-GO
