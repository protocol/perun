The proposal relies on a premium pricing model ($3,000 handsets, high per-minute rates) targeting international business travelers to service $5 billion in debt. This assumes the target market values global coverage enough to accept bulky hardware, poor indoor performance, and exorbitant costs.

However, the stated facts reveal a fatal contradiction. The proposal acknowledges that ground cellular networks are "spreading fast." As terrestrial coverage expands, the specific pain point this satellite network solves (lack of coverage) diminishes precisely among the wealthy, urban-centric demographic it targets. The value proposition shrinks as the competitor's network grows.

Furthermore, the financial structure creates a blocker. Servicing $5 billion in borrowed capital requires rapid, massive subscriber growth. The identified constraints—high price, bulky devices, poor indoor utility, and unready sales channels—actively prevent mass adoption. A niche luxury product cannot generate the volume required to service debt built for a utility-scale network. The "risks" listed are not merely potential downsides; they are structural barriers that make the required revenue trajectory mathematically impossible under the current plan.

The "do nothing" alternative (waiting for costs to drop or technology to mature) or a "better way" (partnering with existing carriers rather than building a standalone consumer brand) would avoid the immediate debt crush. Launching now locks the company into a cycle where high prices limit users, limiting revenue, preventing debt service, and forcing further price hikes or bankruptcy.

The pre-mortem is clear: the company launches, fails to attract enough subscribers to cover debt payments due to the high cost and poor user experience, and collapses before ground networks even fully saturate the market. The contradiction between the debt load's need for scale and the product's limitation to a tiny niche is a definitive blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
