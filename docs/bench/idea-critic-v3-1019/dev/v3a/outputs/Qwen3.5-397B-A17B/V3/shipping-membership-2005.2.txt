**Steelman:** An annual $79 membership eliminates per-order friction, locking in loyalty and increasing purchase frequency enough to offset shipping losses through volume and retained margin.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker is the unit economics assumption: that increased frequency covers the fixed shipping subsidy. In 2005, without established logistics density or dynamic routing, heavy buyers could easily cost $150+ annually to serve against $79 revenue, creating a negative margin loop. The "hard to reverse" risk is real;一旦 sold, revoking access destroys trust.

Alternatives include:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain status quo; retain margin but lose price-sensitive repeat buyers to emerging competitors.
2.  **Threshold-Based Free Shipping:** Offer free shipping only on orders over $50. This protects margins on small baskets while incentivizing larger cart sizes without a fixed upfront fee.
3.  **Pilot Program:** Limit the offer to a specific geographic zone or customer segment to validate the "heavy buyer" cost model before a full launch.

**Pre-mortem:** The program launches, heavy users double their order frequency of low-margin items, shipping costs skyrocket, and the company cannot raise the fee or cancel memberships without a PR disaster, leading to a quarterly earnings miss.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal lacks a verified kill criterion or a capped pilot. It assumes the "long tail" of light users subsidizes heavy users without data proving the distribution curve supports this in the current 2005 logistics environment. The risk of a structural margin hole outweighs the unproven loyalty benefit. A "go" decision requires a time-boxed, capped pilot with a specific metric (e.g., "average shipping cost per member must remain under $65") before a full rollout. As proposed, it is an uncapped bet on unproven elasticity.

**Blockers:** No evidence provided that average shipping cost per member will remain below $79; no pilot mechanism to limit downside.
**Risks:** Irreversible customer expectation; immediate margin compression; logistics capacity strain.

DECISION: NO-GO
