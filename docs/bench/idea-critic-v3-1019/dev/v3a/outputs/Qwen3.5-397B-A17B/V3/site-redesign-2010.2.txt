The claim is that a full rewrite with altered voting mechanics and a forced migration will reverse slowing traffic. The steelman: a modernized interface favoring publishers will attract new volume sufficient to offset community churn.

However, concrete blockers exist. First, the proposal admits the core community values existing voting mechanics; changing these directly attacks the loyalty of the only segment currently driving traffic. Second, moving all users at once eliminates the ability to rollback if engagement drops, turning a reversible experiment into a catastrophic single-point failure. Third, favoring publisher content over community voting contradicts the site's established value proposition, risking alienation of the very users who generate content.

The "do nothing" option preserves the loyal base while allowing time for incremental testing. A cheaper, safer alternative is an A/B test of the new surfacing logic on a small traffic segment, preserving the core mechanics for the majority. This approach validates the "publisher favor" hypothesis without risking the entire community. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed all-at-once launch, which could cede the market entirely to the growing rival by destroying the incumbent's network effect.

The pre-mortem is clear: the launch occurs, long-time users revolt over the changed voting rules, traffic plummets, and the inability to rollback forces a prolonged outage or a humiliating reversal that damages brand trust. The evidence provided (community values existing mechanics, high engineering risk, no rollback) directly contradicts the feasibility of the proposed "all-at-once" strategy.

Blockers:
1. Changing valued voting mechanics threatens the core content supply.
2. All-at-once migration removes rollback capability, creating existential risk.
3. Shifting focus to publishers may invalidate the community-driven model.

Risks:
1. Rival captures disaffected users during transition.
2. Engineering rewrite delays delay market response.

Kill criteria: If a small-scale A/B test shows a >5% drop in returning user engagement within one week, the full rewrite is aborted.

DECISION: NO-GO
