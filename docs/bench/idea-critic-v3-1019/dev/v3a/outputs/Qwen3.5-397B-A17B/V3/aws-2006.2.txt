**Steelman:** Leverage existing, underutilized spiky infrastructure to create a high-margin revenue stream, turning a cost center into a market-leading platform despite internal inexperience.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "fit" and "evidence" gates. The core business operates on thin retail margins with zero history of running a developer platform; the skills required (API reliability, multi-tenant security, developer support) are orthogonal to retail logistics. The market is explicitly "unproven," and incumbents are entrenched. Most critically, investors are already hostile to heavy capital spending; launching a capital-intensive, unproven venture during a credibility crisis violates basic fiduciary stewardship. The "spiky" internal capacity is likely insufficient or architecturally incompatible with the consistent, high-availability demands of external paying customers without massive, unjustifiable new investment.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "wait" option is superior. Wait for the market to mature, validate the "pay-per-hour" model via smaller experiments, or license existing solutions rather than building a competing platform from scratch while the core business faces investor scrutiny.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: Operational distraction leads to retail outages during peak seasons; the new unit burns cash trying to match incumbent reliability without the necessary culture or talent; investors pull funding due to missed retail targets and mounting platform losses.

**Blocker:** The combination of "unproven market," "no internal experience," and "active investor hostility to capital spend" constitutes a fatal blocker. Proceeding now ignores the contradiction between the company's current fragile financial narrative and the aggressive, capital-heavy pivot proposed. The risk of margin dilution and core business distraction is not theoretical; it is immediate given the stated constraints.

**Verdict:** This is a one-way door with a high probability of catastrophic failure for both the new venture and the core retail brand. The proposal lacks the evidence, capability, and political capital to succeed.
