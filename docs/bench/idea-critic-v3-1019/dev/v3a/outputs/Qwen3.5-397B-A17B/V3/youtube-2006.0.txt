**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant video platform eliminates the primary competitor, secures the category leader despite current losses, and bets on future ad-monetization and scale efficiencies that the buyer's smaller internal service cannot match.

**Skeptic Attack:** The target has no revenue, massive operating costs (bandwidth), and existential legal threats from media owners. Paying $1.65 billion for a liability-rich entity with unproven economics contradicts basic valuation logic. If copyright lawsuits succeed, the platform could be shut down, rendering the asset worthless.

**Better-Way:** "Do nothing" and continue growing the buyer's existing, compliant video service avoids immediate legal exposure and cash burn. Alternatively, wait for the legal cloud to clear or for the target to fail, then acquire assets cheaper.

**Pre-mortem & Door:** Assume failure: The acquisition drains stock value, lawsuits force a shutdown or massive settlements, and the ad model never covers bandwidth costs. This is a **one-way door**; once stock is issued and integration begins, reversal is impossible without massive reputational and financial damage. The cost of delay is losing category dominance, but the cost of error is catastrophic.

**Blocker Analysis:**
1.  **Legal Existence:** The core asset (user uploads) is allegedly illegal. If courts rule against fair use or safe harbor, the business model vanishes. This is not a risk; it is a pending verdict on the company's right to exist.
2.  **Economic Viability:** No revenue vs. huge costs. The proposal assumes an ad model will work at scale, but this is unproven.
3.  **Valuation:** $1.65 billion for a company with negative earnings and high litigation probability is unsupported by current facts.

The proposal relies on the assumption that legal threats will be settled favorably and monetization will materialize. However, the *current* facts show a company potentially operating illegally with no path to profitability. The legal blocker is fundamental: you cannot buy a business that may be ordered to cease operations entirely. Without a guaranteed legal shield or proven revenue, the price is unjustifiable.

DECISION: NO-GO
