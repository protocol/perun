**Steelman:** A premium, self-balancing transporter can create a new urban mobility category for early adopters and institutional users, leveraging strong IP and capital to dominate before competitors emerge.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" and "evidence" tests. At $5,000 (approx. 2001 dollars), the unit cost exceeds many motorcycles and a significant portion of a car's price, yet it offers less utility (no cargo, weather exposure, safety concerns). The "institutional buyer" market is too small to sustain a 40,000-unit factory initially; relying on mass consumer adoption at this price point is speculative without proven demand.

The legal ambiguity regarding sidewalks and roads is a critical blocker, not just a risk. If regulators ban it from sidewalks (too fast/heavy) and roads (too slow/unstable), the addressable market shrinks to private property, destroying the value proposition. The "conspicuous rider" risk suggests a social adoption barrier that high pricing exacerbates rather than solves.

A "do nothing" or "pilot first" approach is superior. Building a factory for 40,000 units assumes success before validating regulatory pathways or consumer willingness to pay. A smaller pilot run would reduce capital risk and allow time for legal frameworks to develop. The current plan bets the company on unproven assumptions about price elasticity and regulatory acceptance.

**Blockers:**
1. **Regulatory Void:** No clear legal classification exists; a ban on public pathways eliminates the primary use case.
2. **Price-Utility Mismatch:** $5,000 is too high for a device with limited range, cargo, and weather protection compared to existing vehicles.
3. **Premature Scale:** A 40,000-unit factory commitment precedes evidence of mass-market demand or institutional orders.

**Risks:** Social stigma limiting adoption; safety liabilities; rapid competitor entry with cheaper models.

**Kill Criteria:** If regulatory approval is not secured in key markets within 6 months, or if pilot sales to non-institutional users fall below 1,000 units in the first year, the project must pivot or stop.

**Decision:** The proposal commits excessive capital to an unvalidated market with significant regulatory headwinds. A staged approach is required.

DECISION: NO-GO
