Steelman: Rapidly acquiring 130–150 sites leverages existing lease opportunities to achieve immediate scale, theoretically deterring competitor retaliation and capturing market share before local prices adjust.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The plan relies on a "new supply chain" and "new inventory-management system" that are explicitly "not yet proven." Simultaneously launching a complex cross-border logistics network, navigating unfamiliar regulations, and implementing untested software while opening ~75 stores per year creates a single point of catastrophic failure. If the new inventory system glitches (a high-probability event in first-time deployments), the entire network faces stockouts or overstocking immediately, with no existing domestic buffer to absorb the shock.

The "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the "do nothing" vs. "all-in" comparison. A phased approach (e.g., 10–15 pilot stores) would validate the new supply chain and inventory system with capped downside. The proposed "big bang" approach offers no unique upside that justifies the existential risk; speed to market is irrelevant if the operational backbone collapses under load. The entrenched competitor can easily exploit early operational stumbles to cement loyalty against the newcomer.

The pre-mortem is clear: 18 months in, the inventory system fails to handle bilingual labeling or cross-border tax nuances, causing widespread compliance fines and empty shelves. The cost of delay (waiting to prove the system) is negligible compared to the cost of a total market exit due to a botched launch. The proposal assumes the new systems will work at scale without prior validation, a contradiction to the stated risk that they are "unproven."

Blockers:
1. Unproven critical infrastructure (supply chain + inventory system) deployed at full scale before validation.
2. No operational buffer; failure of the new system compromises 100% of the investment immediately.
3. Pace (75 stores/year) exceeds the learning rate required for new regulatory and logistics environments.

Risks:
1. Competitor price wars targeting new, unstable operations.
2. Currency or regulatory shifts impacting the thin discount margin.
3. Brand damage from early stockouts affecting domestic reputation.

DECISION: NO-GO
