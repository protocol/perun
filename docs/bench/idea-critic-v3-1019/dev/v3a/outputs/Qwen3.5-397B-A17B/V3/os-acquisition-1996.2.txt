**Steelman:** Acquiring a proven, modern object-oriented OS core is the only viable path to stop market share collapse after two failed internal attempts, leveraging the founder's unique insight despite historical friction.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Continued decline leads to insolvency. Internal rebuilds have already failed twice; a third attempt wastes critical time.
*   **Licensing:** Renting a competitor's core cedes strategic control and margin, failing to differentiate the hardware.
*   **Financials:** While revenue is low, the asset's value lies in its architecture and talent, not current sales. The "high price" is a premium for survival, not a multiple of current earnings.
*   **Risks:** Migration difficulty is inherent to any OS change but is lower than building from scratch. The founder's "difficult history" is mitigated by an advisory role rather than immediate operational command. The low unit sales of the target are irrelevant if the technology solves the acquirer's existential bottleneck.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if integration stalls due to cultural clash or if the founder's influence destabilizes the team. However, the alternative is certain obsolescence. The "one-way door" nature of this decision is outweighed by the immediate need to halt bleeding.

**Blockers:** None. The facts state internal replacement failed twice, making external acquisition the only logical remaining option. The risks are manageable compared to the status quo.

**Kill Criteria:** If the core technology cannot be integrated into a prototype within 12 months, or if the founder refuses the advisory role, the strategy must pivot. Leading metric: Developer adoption rate of the new OS beta within 6 months of announcement.

The proposal addresses the root cause (lack of modern OS) with the only available asset that fits. The cost is justified by the avoidance of total market exit.

DECISION: GO
