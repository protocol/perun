**Skeptic:** The proposal assumes a 30% cut is acceptable to developers when rivals offer open distribution with no fee. It assumes the company can scale review processes instantly without creating a bottleneck that stalls innovation. The CEO's public stance against native apps creates an internal credibility gap; reversing this requires admitting the web-app strategy was insufficient for premium experiences. Security risks are real; a single malicious app could destroy consumer trust in the new platform.

**Better-way:** The "do nothing" option (web apps only) avoids all security and carrier conflicts but cedes the emerging mobile software market to rivals. A hybrid approach (sideloading allowed) reduces review workload but increases security risk. The proposed walled garden is the only path to guaranteed quality and revenue share, provided the review scale can be met.

**Kill-criteria:** If the review team cannot clear 90% of submissions within 7 days by launch, the ecosystem stalls. If carriers block the device due to bypassed services, distribution fails. If the first 100 apps include a critical security breach, the model collapses.

**Decision:** The risks are manageable via strict gating; the opportunity cost of waiting (ceding the ecosystem to rivals) is fatal. The 30% cut aligns incentives for platform investment. The CEO's pivot is necessary to compete.
