**Recommendation: NO-GO (Hold)** until Operations & Maintenance (O&M) costs are quantified and a binding performance guarantee is secured. The current proposal relies on an unverified assumption that municipal water prices will remain static for five years and lacks the critical O&M data required to calculate true ROI. Presenting a €950,000 capital expenditure with an unknown recurring cost exposes the company to negative cash flow if membrane replacement or energy usage exceeds conservative estimates.

**Three Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **O&M Cost Overrun:** If membrane cleaning, replacement cycles, or energy consumption exceed 15% of the projected annual savings (€27,000), the net benefit evaporates, turning the asset into a liability.
2.  **Static Price Assumption Error:** If municipal water prices drop or remain flat while inflation drives up your energy and maintenance costs, the projected €180,000 annual saving will not materialize, extending the payback period beyond acceptable limits.
3.  **Throughput Shortfall:** If the system fails to consistently treat 300,000 L/day due to feed-water variability, the 40% reduction target is missed, and the per-liter treatment cost skyrockets.

**Kill Criteria & Trigger:**
The precise trigger to kill this project is a **verified Levelized Cost of Water (LCOW) from the new system exceeding €0.90 per cubic metre** (current effective cost). If the vendor cannot contractually guarantee a minimum recovery rate of 85% with a maximum specific energy consumption of 2.5 kWh/m³, Luca should walk away immediately. Without these guarantees, the risk of stranded capital is too high.

**Stronger Alternative:**
If the economics slip or O&M remains uncertain, pursue a **modular pilot installation (10% capacity)** for six months. This limits capital exposure to ~€100,000, provides real-world data on your specific wastewater composition, and forces the vendor to prove performance before full-scale deployment. Alternatively, negotiate a "Water-as-a-Service" model where the vendor owns the equipment and charges per liter treated, transferring the performance and maintenance risk to them.
