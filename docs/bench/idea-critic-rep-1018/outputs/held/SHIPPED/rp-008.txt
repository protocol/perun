**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" Conditional Pilot**
Do not sign the full annual contract yet. Authorize only the €400k implementation phase as a fixed-cost, 6-week proof of value with a hard cap. Release the remaining €2M annual license fee *only if* the pilot verifies that legacy PLC data mapping can be completed within 4 weeks and that real-time sensor data quality exceeds 90%. This converts the highest risks (integration time and data fidelity) from assumptions into verified gates before committing to recurring spend.

**Top Failure Modes**
1.  **Data Fidelity Gap:** The 12% savings claim relies on 95% sensor data quality. If actual noisy or missing data drops below 90%, model accuracy will collapse, rendering the €2M investment worthless.
2.  **Integration Underestimation:** Mapping legacy PLC logs is estimated at 3 weeks without hard data. If this expands to 8+ weeks, implementation costs will balloon, and the "4-week integration" promise will fail, delaying ROI realization.
3.  **Apples-to-Oranges Benchmarking:** The competitor's 15% reduction over 18 months may involve different machinery or baseline downtime. Expecting 12% in 12 months without validating baseline comparability creates unrealistic CEO expectations.

**Precise Kill Criteria (Pull-the-Plug Trigger)**
Stop the project immediately if, at the end of Week 6 of the pilot:
*   **Condition A:** The IT team cannot demonstrate a stable, automated feed from legacy PLCs covering at least 80% of critical assets.
*   **Condition B:** Verified data quality metrics show >10% noise or gaps in the training window.
*   **Condition C:** The revised projection for Year 1 net savings (Savings minus License minus Implementation) falls below €500k.

*Note: The unknown PLC mapping time is the single biggest threat to your timeline; treat the 3-week estimate as optimistic until a sample mapping is tested in Week 1.*
