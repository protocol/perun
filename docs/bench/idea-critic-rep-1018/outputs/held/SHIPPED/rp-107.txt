**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **go-if** decision: launch a 6-month, capped feasibility study (budget R2.5M) to secure a binding Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) and validate feedstock logistics before committing the R45M capital. Do not approve the full pilot yet.

**Biggest Failure Points:**
1.  **Revenue Overestimation:** Your calculation assumes continuous operation at 50% capacity. Gasification units often face 20–30% downtime due to feedstock inconsistency (moisture/content), potentially slashing annual revenue from R27M to ~R19M, making the project cash-flow negative against R3M O&M and debt service.
2.  **Grid Access & Fees:** The assumption of "no additional fees" is critical. South African grid connection often requires costly infrastructure upgrades or wheeling charges not included in your R1.20/kWh model, which could erase margins entirely.
3.  **Feedstock Volatility:** Relying on a fixed 150,000t supply ignores seasonal fluctuations and competition from other recyclers. A 10% drop in volume disproportionately increases unit costs.
4.  **Carbon Credit Reliance:** Building a business case on "up to R5M" in unverified carbon credits is speculative. If prices stagnate or verification fails, this revenue vanishes.

**Abort Trigger (Kill Criterion):**
Abort the full pilot if the binding PPA offers less than **R1.35/kWh** (to cover downtime risk) OR if the feasibility study reveals grid upgrade costs exceeding **R5M**.

**Hidden Risks:**
*   **Ash Disposal:** Gasification produces toxic ash requiring hazardous waste disposal, adding an unstated operational cost and liability.
*   **Technology Lock-in:** A 2.5MW unit may be too small to achieve economies of scale yet too large for flexible testing, creating a "pilot purgatory" where it's too expensive to fail but too small to profit.
