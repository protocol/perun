**Recommended Course of Action:** Proceed with a **6-week, single-van micro-pilot** capped at **$67,500** (50% of equipment + 6 weeks of ops), targeting **750 patients** instead of 3,000. Do not purchase the van; lease or rent a chassis to preserve capital. Make the release of the second half of funding strictly conditional on achieving a **45% referral conversion rate** and a **cost-per-acquisition under $45** by week 4.

**Critical Failure Modes & Flawed Assumptions:**
1.  **Throughput Illusion:** Seeing 25 patients daily in peri-urban Lagos while traveling 200km is likely impossible. Traffic congestion, patient no-shows, and the time required for point-of-care testing (15–20 mins per patient) suggest a realistic cap of 10–12 patients/day. At 12 patients, you reach only 1,440 patients in 12 weeks, destroying your unit economics.
2.  **The Conversion Fallacy:** Assuming a 70% follow-up rate without historical data is dangerous. In mobile outreach, friction (transport costs to clinics, lost wages) often drives conversion rates below 30%. If conversion drops to 30%, your revenue pipeline collapses by more than half.
3.  **Hidden Capital Trap:** The $5,000 contingency (3.7%) is insufficient. Refurbished vans in high-mileage environments often require immediate, unplanned maintenance (tires, suspension, generator repair). One major breakdown could consume your entire contingency in 48 hours, halting operations.
4.  **Fuel & Logistics:** "No fuel overruns" assumes static routing. Peri-urban density fluctuates; finding 200km of efficient daily routing without backtracking is operationally fragile.

**Kill Criteria (Pull the Plug):**
Terminate or pivot immediately if, by the end of **Week 4**:
*   **Daily Average Patient Count < 10:** This indicates the throughput model is fundamentally broken.
*   **Referral Conversion < 35%:** This proves the link between diagnosis and treatment is severed by external barriers you cannot fix with the van.
*   **Unplanned Maintenance Costs > $2,500:** This signals the refurbished asset is a liability rather than an enabler.

**Condition to Scrap the Idea Entirely:**
Abandon the mobile van model completely if the **cost-per-converted-patient exceeds $150** during the micro-pilot. At that price point, static community health worker hubs or partnership models with existing local clinics will invariably offer superior ROI and reach without the heavy overhead of vehicle ownership and staffing.
