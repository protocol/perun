**Recommendation: Do not launch the pilot as proposed; revise to a 30-day, capped-risk trial starting after the next major holiday spike.**

The current proposal fails the basic math test once labor is included. You project $3,600 in monthly savings from waste reduction, but the total monthly cost is $3,000 ($2,500 vendor fee + $500 clerk), leaving only a $600 net gain. This slim margin does not justify the $8,000 one-time setup fee, which would take over 13 months to recover even if the 15% reduction is perfectly achieved. Furthermore, your assumption that "buying patterns will hold steady" directly contradicts your stated concern about unmodeled holiday spikes (Chuseok, Korean New Year). Launching on January 1st ignores the post-holiday slump and fails to stress-test the model against the very volatility that causes the most waste.

**Key Failure Modes:**
1.  **Negative ROI:** If the model reduces waste by less than 12.5% (the break-even point including labor), you lose money every month compared to the status quo.
2.  **Holiday Blindness:** The model, trained on "steady" patterns, may over-produce during post-holiday lulls or under-produce during unexpected spikes because it hasn't seen those specific transitions in the training data.
3.  **Operational Drag:** The $500/month clerk cost assumes perfect data entry; any lag or error in feeding daily numbers degrades forecast accuracy immediately, widening the gap between predicted and actual demand.

**Halting Criterion:**
Stop the experiment immediately if, after the first 14 days of operation, the net savings (waste reduced minus vendor and labor costs) are less than $150. This threshold ensures the pilot is on track to at least cover the recurring monthly costs before committing to the full 90 days or paying the setup fee.
