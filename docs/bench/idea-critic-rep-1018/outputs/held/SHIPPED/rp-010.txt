**Recommendation:** Proceed only with a **capped, time-boxed pilot** of 250 fishers (10% of the network) costing no more than $170,000, conditional on obtaining a written letter of intent from Chilean customs regarding data equivalence before any hardware purchase.

**Key Failure Points:**
1.  **Regulatory Rejection:** If customs authorities do not accept blockchain records as legal equivalents, the promised 12% price premium vanishes instantly, leaving you with $1.7M in sunk costs and no revenue uplift.
2.  **Adoption Collapse:** The $200 scanner assumes willing users; if fishers find the devices cumbersome or lose them, missing data breaks the "trust" chain, rendering the export certification invalid and causing buyers to reject the premium claim.
3.  **Fee Escalation:** The model assumes a static $0.02 transaction fee; if volume spikes or the provider changes terms, the operational cost could erode the margin gained from the price premium.

**Kill Criteria (Pull the Plug/Renegotiate Triggers):**
*   **Cost Breach:** Total committed spend exceeds **$1.5 million** (hardware + software) before achieving 50% adoption.
*   **Adoption Lag:** Less than **50% of targeted fishers** are actively scanning and transmitting valid data by **March 2027**.
*   **Regulatory Stall:** No formal written recognition or pilot acceptance from Chilean export regulators within **6 months** of the pilot start date.
*   **Revenue Miss:** The realized price premium on exported salmon averages below **6%** (half the projected gain) over two consecutive quarters post-rollout.

*Note: Do not commit to the full $500k hardware outlay until the regulatory letter is secured; hardware is the least reversible cost.*
*The "unknown" regarding customs timing is the single biggest risk; treat it as a binary gate, not a sliding scale.*
