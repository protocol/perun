**Key Failure Points:**
1.  **False Analogy & Elasticity Risk:** Commuters are captive but price-sensitive; unlike ride-share users, they cannot easily switch modes during rush hour but may revolt against perceived unfairness, causing political backlash rather than just ridership drops. MetroCity's 20% higher base fare suggests their users have different elasticity than yours; applying their 15% lift to your lower-fare structure is unsupported.
2.  **Unbudgeted OpEx:** The $200k annual data license creates an immediate budget variance. Without a pre-approved funding source, this violates financial governance before day one.
3.  **Revenue vs. Load Factor Conflict:** A 12% revenue lift might rely on price hikes that suppress the targeted 10% load factor increase. If volume drops while prices rise, public perception of "gouging" will spike even if revenue targets are technically met.

**Abort Trigger (Kill Criterion):**
Abort the pilot if **net ridership on the Red Line declines by more than 3% or customer complaint volume regarding pricing increases by >20% above baseline within the first 45 days**. Do not wait for the three-month mark; early signal detection is critical to prevent entrenched opposition.

**Smarter Route:**
Replace real-time volatility with a **tiered discount structure** (e.g., off-peak incentives) for the initial phase. This achieves load balancing without the reputational risk of surge pricing, allowing you to gather elasticity data safely before enabling dynamic upward pricing.

*Note: The 3-year amortization horizon assumes the software remains viable; ensure the contract includes an exit clause if the pilot fails the 45-day trigger.*
