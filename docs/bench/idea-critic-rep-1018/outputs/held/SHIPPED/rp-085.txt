**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Infrastructure Bankruptcy:** The 8% conversion assumption implies 96,000 concurrent buyers. Without priced server capacity, transaction spikes could crash the service or incur unplanned costs that erase margins.
2.  **Regulatory Whiplash:** Launching before EU legislation is finalized risks an immediate forced shutdown or costly retrofitting if "loot boxes" are classified as gambling, turning the feature into a liability.
3.  **Fantasy Revenue:** The $9.6M projection relies on a 16x higher conversion rate (8%) than the rival's reported 5%. If real conversion matches the rival (5%) or your current engagement baseline, revenue drops to $6M or lower, potentially failing to cover the unpriced infrastructure surge.

**Single Red Flag to Monitor:**
**Net Revenue Per User (NRPU) after Infrastructure Costs.** If the cost of server scaling per transaction exceeds $2.50 (eroding the $10 average spend margin), the model is financially unsustainable regardless of conversion volume.

**Abandonment Triggers:**
*   **Regulatory:** Any EU member state explicitly classifies randomized loot boxes as gambling requiring age verification or licensing before August 1, 2024.
*   **Financial:** Server cost overruns exceed 15% of the projected gross revenue in the first week of the pilot.
*   **Performance:** Crash rate exceeds 1% during peak transaction hours, indicating the architecture cannot handle the 8% conversion load.

**Note:** The revenue calculation assumes all 1.2M MAU are eligible and willing to buy, ignoring that only a fraction of "active" users are payers; your actual addressable market is likely smaller. The rival's 5% conversion is a stronger benchmark than your optimistic 8% assumption.
