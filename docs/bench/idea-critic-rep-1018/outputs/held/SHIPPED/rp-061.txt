**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed only if the licensing fee is contractually capped at $150k and a pilot confirms at least 15% adoption (half your estimate) in the first two events.

**Three Plausible Failure Modes**
1.  **Throughput Bottleneck:** With 5,000 seats and a 2.5-hour event, serving 1,500 users (30%) requires cycling a user every 6 minutes per headset. If hardware setup takes longer than 5 minutes, queues will form, causing fans to abandon the experience and lowering actual adoption well below 30%.
2.  **Concession Cannibalization:** The $7 concession spend may not be additive; if fans spend 15 minutes in VR, they miss peak concession windows. The net lift could be near zero if VR time directly displaces food/beverage purchases rather than adding to them.
3.  **Hidden Recurring Costs:** The model treats licensing as a fixed $150k. If the vendor shifts to a per-user or revenue-share model after the pilot, or if maintenance exceeds the one-year contract (requiring immediate renewal to keep hardware running), the 3-year ROI collapses.

**Pull-the-Plug Indicator**
Stop expansion if **verified adoption is below 15%** or **average transaction value per VR user is under $10** after the first two events.
