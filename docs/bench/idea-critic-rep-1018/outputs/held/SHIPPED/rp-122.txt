**Recommendation:** Do not launch the e-shop with a €0 marketing budget. Your break-even calculation is mathematically incorrect, and your reliance on "organic story" traffic is a critical failure point. Revise the plan to include a specific customer acquisition cost (CAC) and lower the initial investment to a capped pilot.

**What You Are Missing & How It Could Flop:**
1.  **Math Error:** At €45/order and a 25% average margin (typical for food), you make ~€11.25 profit per order. With €16,800 in fixed costs (€12k site + €4.8k logistics), you need **1,493 orders** to break even, not 500. Your projected 1,200 orders result in a **~€3,300 loss** in Q1 before accounting for variable costs like packaging or payment fees.
2.  **The "Zero Marketing" Fallacy:** A 3% conversion rate applies to *visitors*, not the total market. Without ad spend or influencer partnerships, getting enough qualified visitors to generate 1,200 orders is highly improbable. The "artisan story" rarely drives volume without paid distribution.
3.  **Logistics Risk:** €4,800 for 1,200 orders implies €4/order. Shipping chilled cheese is expensive; if actual shipping + packaging exceeds €4, your losses deepen per unit sold.

**Better Route:**
Run a "Pre-Order Pilot" instead of a full build. Spend €2,000 on a landing page and targeted social ads to test the 3% conversion hypothesis with real money. Cap total spend at €5,000. If you cannot acquire 100 paid pre-orders within 30 days, the full model is invalid.

**Kill Criteria (The Trigger):**
Pull the plug immediately if **Cash Burn exceeds €4,000 in the first 45 days** without achieving **200 confirmed paid orders**. This signals that either the conversion rate is below 1.5% or the unit economics are negative, making the projected €54,000 revenue impossible.

*Note: Your assumed 25% in-store uplift is a dangerous dependency; do not count it toward e-shop viability. Verify chilled shipping costs with your 3PL before signing, as €4/unit is likely optimistic for dairy.*
