**Recommendation: Pull Back (Pilot First)**

Do not launch the full 500-household target tomorrow. The unit economics look attractive on paper ($13 gross margin per bag), but the model contains a fatal blind spot: **logistics and churn are unverified variables that will almost certainly erase your projected profit.**

At $30 revenue with $17 in direct costs (beans, packaging, shipping), your gross margin is 43%. However, you have zero data on Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) or warehousing. In subscription coffee, CAC often ranges from $40–$80 per customer. If your CAC is $60, you need **five months of perfect retention** just to break even on acquiring that single customer. If churn exceeds 10% monthly (common in food subs), you will bleed subscribers faster than you can replace them, turning the $180k revenue target into a cash-burn exercise.

Furthermore, shipping 1kg bags individually is logistically inefficient compared to your current bulk café model. One delayed shipment or damaged bag creates a disproportionate support cost that your current model doesn't account for.

**Principal Failure Modes:**
1.  **Churn > 8% monthly:** You lose the lifetime value before recovering acquisition costs.
2.  **Hidden Logistics Costs:** Returns, failed deliveries, and customer service for individual households will spike the $3/bag estimate.
3.  **CAC Underestimation:** If it costs more than $50 to acquire a subscriber, the LTV:CAC ratio breaks.
4.  **Inventory Cash Trap:** Buying 500kg of premium beans monthly ties up cash flow differently than your current just-in-time café roasting.

**Walk-Away Trigger:**
Abort the full rollout if, after a **60-day pilot with 50 subscribers**:
*   **Net Contribution Margin** (Revenue minus COGS, Shipping, *and* estimated CAC amortization) is **below 15%**.
*   **Monthly Churn** exceeds **7%** after the first month.
*   **Subscriber Count** is below **30** (60% conversion of pilot goal) by Day 60.

Run a 50-subscriber pilot for two months to get real data on churn and delivery costs. If the metrics hold, *then* scale to 500. Do not bet the roastery on unverified assumptions.

*Note: Your current café model provides steady cash flow; do not jeopardize it for an unproven DTC channel without validation.*
