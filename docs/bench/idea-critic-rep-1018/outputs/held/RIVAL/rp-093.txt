**Evaluation for COO Maya Patel**

**The Single Red Flag: The "Short-Haul Fleet" Equivalence Claim**
Your proposal claims the 24,000 tonnes of offsets equal the emissions of your entire 28-aircraft short-haul fleet. This is almost certainly a gross underestimate. A single narrow-body jet (e.g., A320) flying typical short-haul rotations emits roughly 4,000–6,000 tonnes of CO₂ annually. Your total fleet likely emits over 100,000 tonnes per year. Marketing a $5 subscription as "neutralizing the short-haul fleet" when it covers barely 20% of those emissions exposes Skyvale to immediate accusations of greenwashing. If regulators or auditors challenge this specific equivalence claim before launch, the reputational damage will outweigh the $360k revenue. **Pull the plug if you cannot substantiate the total fleet emission figure with verified internal data before printing marketing materials.**

**Key Failure Routes**
1.  **Price Volatility Mismatch:** You assume offset prices stay within ±10% ($13.50–$16.50). High-quality aviation-specific offsets are volatile; a spike to $25/tonne (common in tight markets) turns your $360k revenue into a $240k loss instantly, as your costs would jump to ~$600k.
2.  **Verification Liability:** You listed a $300k verification fee but treated "actual carbon-reduction verification" as an unknown. If the third-party verifier fails to certify the projects due to methodology changes (a frequent occurrence), you are contractually obligated to refund subscribers or buy expensive spot offsets, blowing the $2.5M budget.
3.  **Regulatory Reclassification:** If regulators deem this a "tax-deductible benefit" or a financial product rather than a donation, the administrative burden and tax implications could erase margins.

**Recommended Alternative: The "Verified Mile" Model**
Do not sell a fixed "$5 for neutralizing the fleet" subscription. Instead, launch a **"Skyvale Green Mile" dynamic contribution**:
*   **Mechanism:** Passengers opt-in to pay $0.05 per mile flown (approx. $20–$40 per trip for frequent flyers), explicitly tied to *their* specific flight's calculated emissions.
*   **Benefit:** This removes the false "fleet neutrality" claim, aligns revenue directly with variable offset costs (passing price risk to the consumer slightly), and offers a defensible, granular verification story.
*   **Pivot:** Use the $2.5M budget to build the tracking integration rather than aggressive mass marketing. This protects the brand from greenwashing claims while still capturing the willing-to-pay segment.
