**Recommendation: Conditional Go-Ahead with Hard Gates**

Do not recommend a full, unconditional go-ahead. The current math is fragile, and the "to be determined" maintenance cost is a red flag. Recommend a **Phased Rollout**: approve funding for only **2 units** initially (March–August 2025), with the remaining 2 units contingent on hitting specific success metrics. This protects $300k+ of your capital if the network upgrade slips or adoption stalls.

**Critical Holes in the Plan**
1.  **The Maintenance Black Box:** The $45k/year estimate is unpriced ("to be determined"). If this jumps to $60k–$70k (common in RFID contracts), your operational budget breaks. You cannot sign without a capped, fixed-price 5-year maintenance clause.
2.  **Revenue vs. Circulation Confusion:** An 8% lift in *circulation* (borrowing books) rarely equals an 8% lift in *revenue*. Libraries typically earn fines or fees, not direct sales per checkout. If your $210k "revenue boost" relies on fines, patron backlash could kill the project politically. If it relies on grants tied to circulation, verify the grant terms explicitly.
3.  **The Network Single Point of Failure:** The June 2025 network upgrade is a hard dependency. If it slips to September, your kiosks are $600k paperweights for a quarter. There is no offline mode mentioned.

**The "Pull the Plug" Trigger**
Stop or pause the rollout if **Patron Adoption is <45% after 90 days** of live operation at the first two units.
*   *Why 45%?* Your model assumes 70%. If you hit only 45%, the efficiency gains vanish, and staff still need to man the desks, negating the ROI.
*   *Secondary Trigger:* If the vendor cannot provide a fixed-price maintenance contract within 14 days of contract negotiation, walk away.

If we hit 45% usage by June, we unlock the second half of the funding. This ensures we don't spend the whole budget on hardware that sits idle if the network isn't ready."
