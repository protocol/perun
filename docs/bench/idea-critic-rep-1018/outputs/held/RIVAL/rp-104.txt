**Recommendation: Do not launch the pilot under the current terms.**

While the vendor promises $3,600 in monthly savings, your actual net gain is only $600 per month ($3,600 savings minus $2,500 vendor fee minus $500 clerk wage). At this rate, it will take 14 months just to recover the $8,000 setup fee, ignoring the 90-day trial constraint. The financial margin is too thin to justify the operational complexity of adding a new staff role and integrating an unproven model during your critical holiday season.

**Key Ways This Could Unravel:**
1.  **Holiday Volatility:** The model relies on the last 12 months of data but has not been trained on Chuseok or Korean New Year spikes. If the algorithm interprets these spikes as outliers rather than patterns, it may drastically under-produce during your highest-revenue days, causing lost sales that far exceed your current waste costs.
2.  **Operational Friction:** Requiring a part-time clerk to manually feed daily numbers introduces a single point of failure. If data entry is delayed or erroneous even once, the forecast for the following day becomes unreliable, potentially leading to panic baking or excessive waste that negates the projected savings.
3.  **False Precision:** A 15% reduction assumes static buying patterns. If the model creates a false sense of security, you might lose the intuitive "baker's feel" that currently manages minor fluctuations, leaving you vulnerable when the AI inevitably misses a local event or weather shift.

**Specific Halt Trigger:**
Stop the experiment immediately if **waste reduction falls below 8% in the first 30 days** or if **stock-outs occur during any holiday spike**. An 8% floor ensures you are at least covering the variable costs (vendor + clerk) before amortizing the setup fee; anything less means you are paying to lose money. Similarly, missing inventory during a holiday proves the model cannot handle the non-linear demand curves essential to your business, rendering it unsafe for long-term use.
