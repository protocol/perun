**Recommendation: Do not proceed with the national rollout on May 1, 2027, under the current financial model.**

The toughest objection to your plan is the **unit economics fallacy**. Your pilot generated a profit margin of only 18.5% ($7,800/$42,000) while relying on a one-time boutique photography asset and a hyper-local shipping radius. You are assuming that scaling to 1,200 members will magically absorb a $30,000 photographer fee into "increased margin," yet your pilot data proves the opposite: your variable costs (shipping + ads) consumed 83% of your revenue.

Specifically, your shipping cost of $12/case is a local rate. A national rollout will inevitably incur higher zone-based shipping costs, likely pushing that average to $18–$22 per case. If shipping rises to $18, your shipping budget alone jumps from $48,000 to $72,000, erasing your entire projected profit buffer before you pay for a single new marketing click. Furthermore, expecting a 25% lift in Average Order Value (AOV) to offset these rising logistical costs is speculative hope, not a operational strategy. You are planning to scale a loss-making operational structure and hoping volume will fix the leak.

**Primary ways this could fall apart:**
1.  **Shipping Cost Explosion:** National zones increase per-case shipping by 50%, turning every new member into a net loss despite the higher subscription fee.
2.  **CAC Inflation:** The $8,000 spend for 280 members implies a ~$28 Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC). National Facebook ads typically have significantly higher CPMs; if CAC rises to $45, your $180,000 marketing budget acquires only 4,000 members total over time, failing to hit your 1,200 member *run-rate* target within the first year while burning cash.
3.  **The Customs/Support Black Hole:** Without data on U.S. customs delays, you are ignoring a critical risk. If 15% of shipments are delayed, support ticket volume could triple, requiring staff you haven't budgeted for, further degrading the "boutique" experience that justifies your premium pricing.

**Better Route:**
Pivot to a **Regional Density Strategy**. Instead of a national blast, expand only to contiguous states or regions where you can negotiate bulk shipping rates similar to your pilot. Re-invest the saved shipping differential into a scalable content engine (user-generated content or in-house photography) rather than another $30,000 boutique fee. Validate the AOV lift with a small cohort before committing the full $180,000 marketing budget.

**Abandonment Trigger:**
Abort the expansion immediately if a **limited 200-member test in one new state** reveals a blended shipping cost exceeding **$15 per case** or a Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) exceeding **$35**. These thresholds indicate that the unit economics do not support national scale, and proceeding would simply amplify losses.
