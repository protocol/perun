**Recommendation:** Proceed with the launch only if the $120,000 annual maintenance fee is contractually capped and excluded from the base ROI calculation; otherwise, renegotiate the vendor agreement immediately.

**How the plan collapses:** The initiative fails if the recycled yarn requires a price discount to move. Your model assumes zero price penalty, but if the market demands even a 5% discount on the recycled output to absorb the additional volume, the 18% ROI evaporates, turning the project into a net loss when combined with the unpriced maintenance fees.

**Strongest objection:** You are double-counting optimism by assuming both flat disposal costs and full-price market absorption while ignoring a known $120,000 annual liability. The $45/ton disposal saving is real, but it is being used to subsidize a risky revenue assumption rather than secure the baseline return. The maintenance fee alone reduces the effective annual return by approximately 2.1%, dragging the ROI closer to 16% before any market friction is applied.

**Better alternative:** Pivot to a "toll-processing" pilot. Instead of a $3.2M capital outlay for full ownership, contract a third-party recycler to process the 750 tons for a fixed fee per ton for 12 months. This validates the market's willingness to pay for the recycled yarn and locks in the maintenance variable without exposing ThreadCrest to the full capital risk or the uncertain government incentive landscape.

**Trigger for Anita to pull the plug:** If the vendor refuses to cap the maintenance fee in writing or if preliminary off-take agreements indicate the recycled yarn must be priced more than 3% below virgin equivalent to sell.
