**Verdict:** Proceed with a **Pilot-First Mandate**, not a full rollout. The current business case is mathematically optimistic and operationally fragile; approving $120,000 capex without field reliability data in desert conditions is indefensible.

**How This Could Collapse:**
1.  **Hardware Failure in Heat:** If sensor accuracy drops below 60% due to desert temperatures (a high probability without ruggedized units), the system generates false alarms. Technicians will ignore alerts, rendering the network useless while still incurring the $30,000 annual connectivity fee.
2.  **Labor Illusion:** The projected $2M savings assumes 100% redeployment of saved labor hours. If technicians cannot be productively reassigned (e.g., no additional service demand), the 30% reduction becomes idle cost, not savings. The actual cash benefit may be near zero.
3.  **Replacement Cliff:** With no reliability data, if the 18-month replacement assumption is wrong and units fail at month 12, you face an unplanned $120,000 emergency capex spike, destroying Year 2 ROI.

**Trigger to Pull the Plug or Re-evaluate:**
Initiate a **90-day pilot at 3 depots** (45 sensors total, ~$36k capex) before any broader commitment.
*   **Pull the Plug:** If pilot sensor accuracy falls below **75%** or if **<15%** of technician time saved can be verified as successfully redeployed to revenue-generating tasks.
*   **Re-evaluate:** If hardware survives but connectivity costs exceed **$35/unit/year** due to signal issues in remote depots, requiring a infrastructure rethink.

Do not present the full rollout as a "go." Present the pilot as the only responsible path to validate the $2M claim.
