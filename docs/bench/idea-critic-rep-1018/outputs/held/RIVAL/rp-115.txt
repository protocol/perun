**Recommendation: Proceed with a guarded pilot, not a full rollout.**

Your unit economics are positive but fragile. The pilot showed a strong ROI (₹1.2M revenue vs. ~₹1.2M cost for 800 users), but scaling to 4,500 users introduces execution risk. Your assumption that new learners will complete a module is the critical lever; if completion drops below 60%, the perceived value collapses, and churn will spike before you recoup the ₹2.2M development cost. Do not commit the full budget until you validate that the lower price point still drives the same completion rates as the higher-priced pilot.

**Biggest Ways This Could Flop:**
1.  **Completion Cliff:** If the gamification feels like "busy work" rather than skill validation, users will drop off after the first module. With no data on post-track churn, you risk acquiring 4,500 low-LTV users who inflate your top line but drain support resources and dilute brand value.
2.  **Margin Erosion:** You are banking on a 15% conversion lift. If the lift is only 5–8%, your fixed development cost (₹2.2M) spreads over too few users, pushing your net margin well below the 10% safety rail within four months.
3.  **Cannibalization:** Existing learners might downgrade to the cheaper gamified track without gaining extra value, reducing your average revenue per user (ARPU) from ₹3,000 to something significantly lower.

**Early Abort Signal:**
Watch the **Day-45 Active Completion Rate**. If fewer than 40% of new enrollees have finished their first gamified module by day 45, abort the rollout immediately. This leading indicator predicts long-term churn and LTV far earlier than your six-month financial review. If users aren't engaging deeply by week six, they will never reach the "certified" state that justifies the price, and your margin will inevitably dip below 10%.
