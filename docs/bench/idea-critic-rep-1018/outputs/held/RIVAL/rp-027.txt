**Top Failure Modes & Blind Spots**
1.  **The Budget Gap:** The NOK 4.5m budget excludes the mandatory NOK 200k hardware upgrade. While small relative to the total, approving a budget that misses known capex signals poor financial discipline to the director and risks a funding freeze mid-project when the discrepancy is found.
2.  **The Winter Blind Spot:** The pilot (March–April) avoided both deep winter and peak autumn leaf-fall. Snowstorms don't just slow trucks; they invalidate the algorithm's static map data (e.g., temporary no-parking zones, blocked alleys). If the system tries to force optimized routes through unplowable streets, you risk stranded trucks and missed collections, which creates a political crisis far exceeding fuel savings.
3.  **Adoption Friction:** Assuming 85% adoption after a single three-week sprint is aggressive for a demographic that may be resistant to digital oversight. If veteran drivers manually override the system "just for today" during bad weather, your data poisons itself, making the algorithm dumber over time rather than smarter.

**Walk-Away Trigger**
**Abort if post-winter audit shows <5% net fuel reduction.**
Your pilot showed a 7.2 km/day reduction. Winter will degrade this. If, after the November storms, the net average reduction falls below 5% (roughly 3.6 km/day), the operational risk and maintenance cost of the software will outweigh the marginal fuel savings. At that point, the technology is not mature enough for Oslo's specific climate constraints, and you should scrap the full rollout to avoid sinking costs into a system that only works in fair weather.
