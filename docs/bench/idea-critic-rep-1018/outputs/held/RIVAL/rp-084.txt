**Recommendation:** Do not approve the full $125,000 rollout. Instead, authorize a **$25,000 "Connectivity-First" Pilot** at the single site with the known 2 Mbps constraint. Use this phase solely to validate that the custom curriculum functions on low-bandwidth networks and to quantify the true cost of headset maintenance and software licensing before committing to the remaining two schools.

**Key Failure Points:**
1.  **Bandwidth Collapse:** The 2 Mbps peak speed is likely insufficient for 30 concurrent VR units downloading or syncing high-fidelity educational assets. If the content requires constant streaming, the system will be unusable during school hours, rendering the $20,000 content investment worthless.
2.  **The "Free" Infrastructure Trap:** Relying on NGOs for routers introduces a single point of failure outside your control. If they cannot provide enterprise-grade equipment or stable power, the hardware becomes bricked.
3.  **Hidden OpEx Cliff:** The budget lacks line items for Year-2 software licensing, headset replacement (broken controllers/lenses are inevitable in student hands), and content updates. The current arithmetic implies the project dies or requires emergency funding after 12 months.

**Trigger Metric:**
**"The 50% Sync Failure Rate."** During the initial pilot week, if more than 15 of the 30 headsets fail to fully download or update the daily lesson module within a 2-hour window due to bandwidth limitations, **pull the plug on the current content architecture immediately.** Do not attempt to "optimize" the software; pivot to an offline-first distribution model (e.g., local caching servers) or cancel the expansion.

**Note:** The current budget is effectively a "best-case" scenario that ignores the operational reality of remote hardware deployment.
**Note:** Securing a committed maintenance budget from the board now is cheaper than explaining why the units are inactive next year.
