**Recommendation: NO-GO (Conditional Hold)**

Do not authorize the full deployment of 12 units. The proposal contains a critical unverified technical risk and a mathematical error in the business case that invalidates the projected ROI.

**Primary Failure Modes**
1.  **Surface Compatibility Failure (Critical):** The proposal admits unknown compatibility between the robot grippers/sensors and the new "alloy-X" surface finish. If the robots cannot reliably grip or inspect this alloy, the line stops immediately. This is a single point of failure that testing has not yet addressed.
2.  **Installation Scope Creep:** The assumption that the existing line accommodates the 30 cm clearance "without re-tooling" is highly optimistic. Modifying safety cages, conveyor transitions, or emergency stops to fit this clearance typically requires custom fabrication, which is rarely zero-cost or zero-time.
3.  **Uptime Sensitivity:** The business case relies on 95% uptime. In mixed-material environments, if alloy-X causes even minor jamming, uptime could drop to 85%, erasing the labor savings entirely due to increased maintenance labor and production delays.

**Arithmetic & Budget Discrepancies**
*   **Capital Expenditure Error:** 12 units × ¥2.8 million = **¥33.6 million**.
    While this fits within the ¥38 million base budget, it leaves only ¥4.4 million for installation, training, and integration. Given the "no re-tooling" assumption is likely false, the ¥5 million contingency will be consumed entirely by physical line modifications, leaving zero buffer for the alloy-X compatibility fixes.
