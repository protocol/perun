**Recommendation: Proceed with the pitch, but reframe the narrative from "cost savings" to "risk mitigation and capacity unlock," and explicitly flag the migration/audit costs as Phase 2 dependencies.**

Your core math holds: even ignoring the massive revenue unlock ($7,800/mo), the direct arbitrage is positive ($2,400 current vs. ~$1,850 new). However, presenting this as a guaranteed net gain is fragile because your "fully billable" assumption is aggressive. If partners cannot immediately sell those 126 freed hours, your actual cash flow impact is neutral or slightly negative once migration amortization hits. Pitch Iron Quill as the infrastructure required to handle scale without linear headcount growth, rather than a immediate P&L miracle. This protects your credibility if billability lags.

**Principal Failure Modes:**

1.  **The Billability Gap:** The model assumes 100% of freed legal time converts to billed revenue. If the firm only realizes 50% billability, the $7,800 benefit drops to $3,900. Combined with the $5,000 migration cost (amortized over 12 months = ~$417/mo) and a $3,000 security audit, your break-even point stretches from Month 1 to Month 6+.
2.  **Regulatory Rejection:** You noted the unknown regulator stance. If the NY bar or local courts deem blockchain-signed contracts inadmissible or require redundant wet-ink signatures for enforceability, Iron Quill becomes a parallel cost center rather than a replacement, doubling admin work instead of cutting it.
3.  **Adoption Friction:** The 30% efficiency gain assumes seamless lawyer adoption. If the UI/UX causes friction, lawyers may bypass the system or spend more time troubleshooting than the manual process saves, eroding the 126-hour gain entirely.

**Early Indicator to Pull the Plug:**

Monitor the **"Effective Hourly Rate" of the first 50 contracts post-migration.**

Calculate: *(Total Fees Generated from Iron Quill Contracts - Iron Quill Fees) / Total Lawyer Hours Spent on Those Contracts*.

If this metric is lower than your current baseline effective rate after 60 days, the system is creating friction or failing to unlock billable time. This is a faster signal than waiting for monthly P&L statements. If the first 50 contracts show no efficiency gain or require rework due to compliance doubts, halt further rollout and revert to manual processing for high-stakes agreements until the regulatory or workflow bottleneck is resolved.
