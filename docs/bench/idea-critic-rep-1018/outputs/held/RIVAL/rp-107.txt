**Recommendation:** Do not pitch this as a fixed-scope pilot; instead, propose a **conditional feasibility study** that validates the waste supply contract and grid interconnection costs before committing to the R45 million capital expenditure. The current financial model is dangerously optimistic and likely overstates net revenue by 30–40%.

**How This Plan Could Fall Apart:**
1.  **Revenue Overestimation:** Your calculation assumes a 50% capacity factor (4,380 hours/year). For a first-of-its-kind gasification unit handling heterogeneous organic waste, a realistic Year 1 availability is closer to 30–35% due to maintenance and feedstock inconsistencies. This alone drops annual revenue from R27m to ~R16–18m, turning your projected operating profit into a significant loss.
2.  **Hidden Interconnection Costs:** You assumed the grid accepts power "without additional fees." In South Africa, wheeling charges, grid reinforcement upgrades, and licensing compliance can cost millions upfront or slice 15–20% off your per-kWh price.
3.  **Feedstock Volatility:** Relying on a static 150,000-tonne supply is risky. Seasonal fluctuations or competing waste-to-landfill contracts could starve the plant, forcing you to buy expensive supplemental feedstock to keep the unit running.
4.  **O&M Underestimation:** R3m/year (6.7% of CAPEX) is low for gasification technology, which typically requires 8–10% of CAPEX annually for specialized maintenance and ash disposal.

**Abort/Rethink Triggers:**
*   **Abort:** If the signed off-take agreement includes wheeling charges or grid upgrade costs exceeding R5 million, or if the waste supply contract lacks a "minimum guaranteed tonnage" clause with penalties for the supplier.
*   **Rethink:** If the pilot's actual capacity factor drops below 35% in the first six months, or if carbon credit verification costs exceed R500,000/year.

**Hidden Risks:**
*   **Ash Disposal:** Gasification produces toxic bottom ash requiring hazardous waste disposal, a cost not listed in your O&M.
*   **Technology Lock-in:** A 2.5 MW unit may be too large for a pilot; a modular 500 kW unit would reduce exposure while proving the technology.
*   **Regulatory Delay:** NERSA licensing for embedded generation can take 12–18 months, delaying revenue start dates significantly.

**Note:** Your model breaks even only if you achieve >45% capacity factor and secure full carbon credits; neither is guaranteed. Restructure the pitch to focus on de-risking these variables before full deployment.
