**Go‑No‑Go Judgment: Conditional GO**

Proceed with the Riverbend pilot only if you secure a binding price‑lock agreement with the specialty flour supplier and establish a $150,000 reserved marketing fund before signing the first franchisee. The current financial model is fragile; it relies entirely on static input costs and ignores the defensive marketing spend required to survive the local cooperative's likely reaction. Without these two safeguards, the projected 18% ROI is illusory, and the risk of a margin‑destructive price war is too high to recommend launch.

**Key Failure Points:**
1.  **Input Cost Volatility:** The model breaks if flour prices rise above $0.30/lb or if the supplier cannot guarantee volume for ten new sites.
2.  **Competitive Response:** The local bakery cooperative has the density to sustain a price war that a single new franchisee cannot match, potentially driving the new location to insolvency within 12 months.
3.  **Capital Underestimation:** The $2.5M startup cost estimate excludes the regional marketing campaign necessary to protect brand equity, meaning actual capital requirements are likely 20–30% higher than planned.

**Early Indicator to Pull the Plug:**
If the local cooperative announces a unified loyalty program or a coordinated price drop exceeding 10% within 60 days of the Riverbend site selection, **halt the rollout immediately**. This signals an organized defensive posture that your current unit economics cannot withstand. In that scenario, pivot the model from a standard franchise to a company‑owned joint venture to retain enough margin flexibility to fight, or abandon the region entirely.
