**Gearflare 2.0 Pitch Sanity Check**

**Critical Gaps & Risks**
1.  **Integration Fee Underestimation:** $200k for licensing, training, and installation across three units is likely insufficient. Industry standard for custom warehouse integration often runs 20–30% of hardware costs ($210k–$315k). You are budgeting ~19%. If custom API work or safety certification delays occur, this line item will bleed into contingency.
2.  **Battery Degradation Unknown:** Deploying without cycle-life data under continuous load is a major operational risk. If batteries degrade to 70% capacity within 6 months, throughput drops, requiring more frequent charging swaps and effectively reducing your active fleet size. This invalidates the 120k items/month throughput assumption.
3.  **Space Efficiency:** 2,000 sq ft for three large robots (plus charging stations and safety buffers) is tight. Verify the turning radius and charging footprint. If congestion occurs, throughput plummets.
4.  **ROI Sensitivity:** Your 18-month ROI relies entirely on hitting 120k items/month. At 90k items, labor savings likely drop proportionally, extending ROI to ~24 months.

**Better Approach**
*   **Pilot First (2 Units):** Deploy two units initially. This reduces upfront capex to $900k ($700k hardware + ~$150k adjusted integration). It allows you to validate battery performance and actual throughput in the specific Seoul environment before committing the third unit.
*   **Renegotiate Integration:** Push for a fixed-price integration contract with a penalty clause for delays, or demand a post-deployment audit clause where you pay the balance only after hitting 90% of promised throughput for 30 consecutive days.

**Abandonment Trigger Metric**
*   **Primary Trigger:** If **actual throughput averages <100k items/month** (not 90k) after the first **8 weeks** of full operation.
    *   *Reasoning:* 100k is the break-even point where your labor savings still justify the capex within a 22-month window. Below this, the operational complexity outweighs the benefit.
*   **Secondary Trigger:** If **battery runtime drops below 6 hours** per charge cycle within the first **3 months**, indicating premature degradation that requires unscheduled battery swaps.

**Note:** The 15% cost advantage claim is fragile; ensure your competitor comparison includes total cost of ownership (maintenance, energy), not just upfront price.
**Note:** Secure a clause allowing return of the third unit if the pilot (first two) fails these metrics.
