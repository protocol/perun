**Executive Summary for COO Approval**

**Most Plausible Objection:** The plan assumes a linear 15% quarterly reduction in failure rates, but this ignores the "learning lag" between alert generation and crew response. Without verified changes to maintenance workflows, predictive alerts often create "alert fatigue," leading crews to ignore warnings or dispatch unnecessarily, which can initially *increase* mean-time-to-repair (MTTR) and labor costs before any reliability gains are realized. The ROI model treats technology deployment as an instant fix rather than a socio-technical change requiring training and process adaptation.

**More Efficient Route:** Instead of a full 120-locomotive rollout, execute a **controlled A/B pilot** on 20 locomotives (10 equipped, 10 control) for 90 days. This isolates the variable of crew response time and validates the 15% reduction assumption before committing $2.7M of the budget. Alternatively, leverage existing onboard telemetry (if available) to build a baseline failure model before installing new sensor kits, potentially reducing the $1.8M hardware spend.

**Defensible Halt Trigger:** Your proposed 8% net maintenance cost increase is reasonable, but it is a lagging indicator. A sharper trigger is: **"If the ratio of valid predictive interventions to total alerts falls below 40% after Month 2, or if unscheduled downtime does not decrease by at least 5% in the first 60 days of operation."** This catches workflow failures early, before they compound into massive labor overruns.

**Arithmetic & Hidden Cost Check:**
*   **ROI Math:** The math holds superficially ($2.1M savings vs. $3.2M cost implies a ~18-month payback if savings are annualized correctly). However, the $2.1M savings figure assumes the 5-hour reduction applies immediately across the fleet. If the 15% reduction is truly *quarterly* (compounding), the Year 1 savings are significantly lower than stated because Q1 and Q2 will see minimal impact. The model likely overstates Year 1 cash flow by treating the end-state efficiency as an average.
*   **Hidden Costs:** The budget lacks line items for **data integration engineering** (connecting the new platform to existing maintenance logs), **change management training** for field crews, and **ongoing sensor calibration/maintenance**. These often consume 15–20% of the initial hardware budget in industrial IoT deployments. Additionally, the $500K licensing fee likely scales with data volume; if the sensors generate high-frequency raw data, this cost could spike unexpectedly without a capped contract.

**Recommendation:** Condition approval on a 90-day validation phase with strict alert-accuracy KPIs and a revised ROI model that accounts for the ramp-up curve of crew adoption.
