**Verdict:** Do not pitch this plan in its current form; the financial model contains a critical arithmetic gap that renders the projected ROI impossible to achieve, and the single largest cost driver remains undefined.

**The Fatal Flaw:** Your budget is mathematically insufficient for the scope. You have allocated $90,000 per phase, but with a $250,000 total budget, you can only fund 2.7 phases. A district-wide rollout across 12 schools inherently requires multiple phases (Pilot → Iteration → Full Deployment). If "Phase 1" is the pilot, you have barely enough left for a partial rollout, let alone the full 12-school launch. Furthermore, you are banking on a 20% reduction in print costs to offset expenses, but you have not quantified the *current* print spend. If the current spend is $50,000, a 20% drop saves only $10,000—nowhere near enough to cover the $90,000/phase burn rate. The enrollment lift (540 students) is a lagging indicator that won't help cash flow in Year 1.

**Hidden Expenses & Unpriced Risks:**
1.  **The Unknown Variable:** The video hosting licensing fee is the single biggest risk. If this is a per-seat fee, it scales directly with your success metric (enrollment). Without a capped contract, your marginal cost per new student could exceed the revenue that student brings in.
2.  **Infrastructure Reality:** You assume 80% of schools have >15 Mbps broadband. In a district-wide rollout, the remaining 20% (2–3 schools) will fail to stream content. The cost to upgrade infrastructure in those specific buildings is likely six figures, completely blowing your budget.
3.  **Teacher Time:** You budgeted for "training," but not for *substitute teachers* to cover classes while staff train. This is a massive, often overlooked line item in education tech rollouts.

**The Walk-Away Trigger:**
Walk away or pivot immediately if the video hosting vendor cannot provide a **fixed-cost, district-wide cap** rather than a per-seat model. If the cost scales linearly with the 540 extra students you are hoping to attract, the unit economics will collapse as you grow.

**Smarter Route:**
Pivot to a "Platform-Agnostic Pilot." Instead of building/licensing a custom platform for $90k, use existing, low-cost infrastructure (like standard LMS integrations) for the 3-school pilot. Dedicate the saved capital to fixing the broadband gaps in the lowest-performing schools first. Prove the enrollment lift *before* committing to the expensive content development phase. Sell the *outcome* (enrollment), not the *tool* (video platform).
