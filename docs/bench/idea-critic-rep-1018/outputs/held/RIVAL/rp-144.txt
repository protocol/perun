**Course of Action:** Do not launch the mobile van pilot. Instead, immediately run a 4-week "pop-up" feasibility study using a rented space or existing community center in one target district, staffed by only one nurse and utilizing portable, hand-carried diagnostic kits. **Critical Failure Modes:**
1.  **Logistical Fragility:** The assumption of 200km/day in Lagos peri-urban traffic without fuel overruns is dangerously optimistic. Traffic congestion, road quality, and fuel price volatility will likely slash daily reach by 40%, destroying your unit economics.
2.  **Conversion Delusion:** Projecting a 70% referral conversion without first-month uptake data is speculative. In low-trust environments, patients often seek immediate cheap alternatives rather than formal follow-ups; if conversion drops to 30%, the model becomes a cost sink, not a pipeline.
3.  **Maintenance & Downtime:** Refurbished vans and sensitive point-of-care labs in humid, dusty conditions face high failure rates. A single week of mechanical or equipment downtime destroys your 25-patients/day average, making the 3,000-patient target mathematically impossible.

**Hidden Costs & Flawed Assumptions:**
*   **The Contingency Trap:** $5,000 (3.7% of budget) is insufficient for a mobile health operation. It cannot cover a major engine repair, a generator failure, or a month of fuel inflation. You are effectively operating with zero safety margin.
*   **Staffing Burnout:** Three nurses for a 7-day operation (implied by "daily" targets) is understaffed once leave, sickness, and training are accounted for. You will face immediate coverage gaps.
*   **Regulatory Friction:** Mobile labs often face unpredictable local permitting or harassment costs not listed in your CAPEX.

**Pull the Plug / Pivot Trigger:**
If the pop-up study yields a referral conversion rate below 45% or if the cost-per-acquired-patient exceeds $15, abort the van plan entirely. These metrics indicate the fundamental demand or operational efficiency does not exist to support the heavy fixed costs of a vehicle.
