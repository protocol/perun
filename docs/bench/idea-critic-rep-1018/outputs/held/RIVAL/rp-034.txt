**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **staged pilot** rather than a full studio-wide rollout on November 1st. Commit immediately to the license to secure the technology, but restrict AR access to **one** senior designer and a maximum of **10 select clients** for the first 90 days. This caps your exposure to the unproven "3-day turnaround" constraint while validating whether the projected 15% conversion lift materializes in practice before training all staff and promising the feature to 80% of your pipeline.

**Most Likely Collapse Points:**
1.  **The Turnaround Bottleneck:** The 3-day promise is the critical failure mode. If AR integration adds even 24 hours of friction per project, your throughput drops by 33%. With only three senior designers trained, a single complex AR revision could stall the entire queue, causing delays that anger the 80% of clients expecting the feature, effectively negating the conversion gain with reputational damage.
2.  **Opt-In Delusion:** Assuming 80% client opt-in is dangerously optimistic. If actual adoption lands at 40% (common for new tech friction), your revenue per project drops because the fixed license cost is now spread over fewer "premium" conversions, potentially making the unit economics negative despite the conversion lift.

**Specific Halt Trigger:**
Stop the initiative immediately if, after the first 45 days, the **average project margin on AR-enabled contracts falls below 180,000 JPY**. This indicates that the operational drag (extra time/rework) is consuming more value than the conversion lift is generating, proving the 3-day turnaround is unsustainable at current staffing levels.

*Note: The 300,000 JPY training cost is a sunk risk if the workflow proves incompatible with your speed requirements; ensure the vendor contract allows for partial license termination after 6 months.*
