**Strongest Objection:** The unit economics are likely negative before accounting for churn or upgrades. At €25 revenue, you have €9 in direct variable costs (production, packaging, shipping), leaving a €16 gross margin. However, this ignores customer acquisition cost (CAC), payment processing fees (~€0.75), and potential temperature-controlled shipping surcharges. If CAC exceeds €100 (common for food subscriptions), and churn is even moderate, the Lifetime Value (LTV) may not cover the acquisition cost, making the scale-up to 1,500 households a capital drain rather than a growth engine.

**Smarter Route:** Pivot to a "Founding Member" pilot in **Brussels only** for the first 90 days. Cap the cohort at 300 households to tightly control logistics and validate the non-refrigerated shipping assumption without risking brand reputation on melted product in Antwerp. Introduce a **€29 price tier** that explicitly includes "premium thermal packaging," allowing you to test price elasticity and cover potential temperature-control costs immediately. This reduces your maximum exposure while generating higher-quality data on willingness to pay for quality assurance.

**Abandonment Metric:** Abort the pilot if **Month 4 Net Retention falls below 85%** (equivalent to >15% monthly churn after the initial novelty wears off). Alternatively, if **temperature-related complaints exceed 2%** of shipments during the first 60 days, pause expansion immediately, as chocolate quality issues will destroy the brand's premium positioning faster than financial losses.

*Note: Your projected €562,500 revenue assumes perfect retention and full uptake, which is rarely achieved in month one; stress-test the model with a slow ramp-up curve.*
