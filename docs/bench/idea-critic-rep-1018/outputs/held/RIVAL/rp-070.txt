**Recommended Course of Action**
Proceed with a **staged rollout capped at 25,000 households** (approx. $420k initial exposure) rather than the full 150,000. Condition the remaining capital on achieving a <3% default rate in the first 6 months. This limits catastrophic loss while validating the 5% default assumption in Lagos's specific informal economy before committing the full $2.5M.

**Chief Ways This Could Fall Apart**
1.  **Default Rate Sensitivity:** Your model assumes 5% defaults. If the real rate hits 12% (common in unsecured micro-finance during economic shocks), the $800k profit vanishes and you incur a principal loss of ~$1.1M. The high 12% interest rate may ironically *cause* higher defaults by making repayments unaffordable.
2.  **Hardware Reality Check:** Assuming "no performance drop" over 10 years in Lagos's humid, dusty, and high-temperature environment is unrealistic. Panel degradation or battery failure in Year 3 will trigger mass non-repayment, as users won't pay for broken kits.
3.  **Collection Cost Omission:** You budgeted for trainers and parts, but **zero for loan collection/enforcement**. In informal settlements, collecting from 150,000 households requires a significant field agent workforce. If collection costs run even $2/household/year, you lose $3.6M over the loan life.

**Early Warning Sign (Pull the Plug)**
If **delinquency (>30 days late) exceeds 8% in any single month** during the first quarter, pause disbursements immediately. This indicates the 5% annual default assumption is mathematically impossible to meet and the credit scoring or repayment terms are flawed.

**Glaring Arithmetic & Missing Costs**
*   **The "Profit" Math Doesn't Hold:**
    *   Total Loan Principal: 150,000 units × ~$16.67/unit (derived from $2.5M budget minus $550k OpEx) ≈ $2.5M deployed.
    *   Interest Income (12% fixed, 3 years, simple interest approx): $2.5M × 12% × 3 = $900k.
    *   Less OpEx ($550k) = $350k gross margin.
    *   Less **5% Defaults** ($125k principal loss) = $225k net.
    *   Your $800k profit figure implies either a much higher interest yield (compounding?) or a significant underestimation of defaults/costs. The current numbers do not sum to $800k.
*   **Missing Cost: Battery Replacement:** Solar kits rarely last 10 years without battery replacement (typically every 3–4 years). If you warranty this, add ~$400k to costs. If the user pays, it increases default risk.
*   **Missing Cost: Working Capital Gap:** You need cash flow reserves to pay suppliers while waiting for monthly loan repayments. This interest/carry cost is missing.
