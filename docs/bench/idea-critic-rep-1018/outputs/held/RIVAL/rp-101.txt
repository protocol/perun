**Recommendation: Proceed with a conditional pilot, but reject the full rollout proposal in its current form.**

Do not sign the €150k upfront commitment yet. The math relies on unverified assumptions about battery life and hidden integration costs that could erase the projected ROI. Instead, negotiate a revised pilot agreement: cap the initial spend at the €30k pilot fee plus a strict €10k contingency for integration discovery. Make the full rollout contingent on the pilot proving a net savings of at least €20k (not the projected €45k) after accounting for *actual* integration labor and any battery replacements.

**Principal Failure Modes:**

1.  **Battery Life Mismatch:** Your assumption of a 12-month battery life is the single point of failure. If sensors die at 8 months, you incur €150k+ in annual replacement labor and hardware costs, turning a profit center into a massive loss.
2.  **The Integration Trap:** The omitted ERP integration fee is likely a "land-and-expand" tactic. If custom middleware is required to talk to your existing inventory system, that €20–30k hidden cost pushes your breakeven point from 6 months to nearly 10 months, invalidating their core sales pitch.
3.  **Loss Attribution Error:** The 18% loss reduction assumes all lost kegs are theft or misplacement. If your actual loss is due to damage or foaming (which sensors can't prevent), the technology solves the wrong problem, and the €300k savings figure is pure fantasy.

**Abandonment Trigger:**

Terminate the project immediately if the 6-month pilot demonstrates a **net operational savings of less than €12,500** (approx. 4% of total addressable loss).

*Reasoning:* At €12.5k savings per half-year, the annualized return is only €25k. Against a true fully-loaded cost of ~€180k (hardware + integration + overhead), the payback period stretches beyond 7 years. Any result below this threshold indicates the system does not work in your specific environment, and continuing would be throwing good money after bad.
