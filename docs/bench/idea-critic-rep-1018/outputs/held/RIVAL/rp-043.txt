The project is financially fragile due to a critical accounting error in your unit economics. Your model assumes an $8 all-in kit cost, but the $3 shipping component is variable, not fixed. If shipping scales with volume, your margin per unit drops from $7 to $4. At 2,500 subscribers, this creates a $37,500 annual hole in your gross profit, pushing your breakeven point from month 8 to month 14. Furthermore, your acquisition math is dangerously optimistic: converting 5% of an email list is industry-leading; a realistic 1.5% conversion yields only 405 subscribers, leaving you at 20% of your revenue target. Without a confirmed shipping subsidy or a validated conversion rate, this pilot will burn cash without reaching sustainability.

**Principal Failure Points**

1.  **Variable Cost Misclassification:** Treating shipping as a fixed cost included in the $8 base creates a false margin. As you scale, your costs rise linearly while your model assumes they stay flat, eroding profitability with every new sale.
2.  **Acquisition Delusion:** Relying on a 5% conversion rate from a cold email blast is speculative. If conversion lands at the industry standard of 1–2%, you will fail to hit the critical mass needed to cover fixed platform fees.
3.  **Retention Cliff:** The model assumes 80% retention after month 3. If the "novelty" of virtual tasting wears off and churn spikes to 10% monthly (as hinted in your risk notes), customer lifetime value collapses, making the cost of acquiring new subscribers impossible to recoup.
