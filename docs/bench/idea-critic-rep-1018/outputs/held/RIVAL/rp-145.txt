**Recommendation: NO-GO (Conditional Proceed)**

Do not approve the €150,000 total upfront commitment yet. The business case fails on two critical accounting errors and one unquantified risk that could turn a projected €120,000 profit into a loss.

**Key Failure Points**
1.  **Apples-to-Oranges Cost Comparison:** You are comparing a €120k one-time license fee against a €150k *per-batch* lab panel cost. If you run 10 batches a year, the lab costs €1.5M, making Amber Atlas a steal. If you run 2 batches, the lab costs €300k, still a win, but the magnitude changes. You must calculate the *annual* lab spend for your actual batch volume to establish the true baseline savings.
2.  **The "30% Speedup" Trap:** Reducing cycle time from 12 to 8 weeks only yields revenue if your bottleneck is *development time*. If your bottleneck is fermentation capacity, distribution, or marketing bandwidth, launching two extra beers is impossible regardless of speed. The €250k revenue gain is theoretical unless you have verified idle capacity to absorb the accelerated output.
3.  **The Support Cost Black Box:** "Around €10k" is dangerous. If data-science updates (essential for flavor accuracy) are billed separately or scale with usage, this could easily hit €50k/year, eroding 40% of your margin in year one.

**The Plug-Pull Trigger**
Agree to Lucien's €100k revenue floor, but add a secondary kill switch: **If the integration exceeds 5 weeks (nearly double the estimate), pause immediately.** Integration drag on specialized flavor platforms often signals deep data incompatibility that will plague every future batch.

**Better Alternative**
Before signing, propose a **paid pilot for one seasonal beer** (costing approx. €20k–€30k). This validates the 8-week cycle claim and reveals the true support cost structure without exposing the full €150k. If the vendor refuses a pilot, their confidence in the "30% speedup" is suspect.
