**Recommendation: NO-GO on the current dynamic-pricing proposal; pivot immediately to a "Tiered Discount" pilot.**

Approaching the board with a $4.7M unfunded liability based on unverified elasticity assumptions is a fiduciary risk. The proposal relies on a flawed comparison: MetroCity's success was driven by high-base fares where a 20% surge is psychologically different than surging a mass-transit fare. Commuters view transit as a utility, not a luxury service like ride-share; price volatility here risks triggering a political backlash and permanent ridership churn rather than temporary deferral.

**Key Failure Points:**
1.  **Revenue Illusion:** A 10% load factor increase cannot offset the reputational damage and potential ridership collapse if elasticity is higher than assumed. If just 5% of your 150,000 daily riders abandon the system due to price confusion, the revenue "lift" vanishes.
2.  **Unbudgeted OpEx:** The $200k annual data license is a recurring drain not accounted for in the three-year amortization. This undermines the ROI model before day one.
3.  **The "Utility" Mismatch:** Unlike Uber users who expect surge pricing, transit riders expect predictable costs for budgeting. Real-time swings violate this psychological contract.

**The Smarter Route: Tiered Discount Structure**
Instead of charging *more* at peak times (punitive), charge *less* at off-peak times (rewarding).
*   **Mechanism:** Keep base fares flat. Offer 15-20% discounts for travel before 7 a.m. or after 9:30 a.m.
*   **Benefit:** This achieves the same load-balancing goal (shifting demand) without the political toxicity of "price gouging." It frames the initiative as "saving money" rather than "paying more," securing union and public support.
*   **Cost:** Significantly lower software complexity and no need for aggressive real-time data licensing.

**Abort Trigger:**
If you proceed with any form of pricing experiment, the hard stop is: **Net ridership growth under 5% after 90 days.**
Do not wait 12 months. If the Red Line does not show a measurable shift in behavior or revenue within the first quarter, the behavioral hypothesis is false. Continuing beyond this point burns capital on a broken premise.

**Action for Maya:**
Tell the board we are pausing the $4.5M rollout to run a 90-day, low-cost "Off-Peak Discount" trial on the Red Line. This protects the budget, tests elasticity safely, and aligns with our public service mission.
