**Subject:** Nimbus Shelf Project – Strategic Briefing & Risk Assessment

**Recommended Course of Action**
Proceed with a **conditional 6-month pilot** rather than a full 12-month commitment. Reallocate €40,000 from the integration budget to establish a dedicated data collection and translation validation stream for Arabic and Somali before system-wide rollout. Do not sign the annual license until the pilot validates that the AI handles these languages without hallucinating recommendations or requiring costly manual retraining.

**Principal Failure Points**
1.  **Language Data Scarcity:** The assumption that the model handles Arabic and Somali without extra training is the highest technical risk. Unlike Finnish/Swedish, low-resource languages often yield poor recommendation relevance without fine-tuning, potentially alienating the very communities we aim to serve.
2.  **Integration Cost Underestimation:** The €210,000 one-time cost assumes a clean API handshake. Legacy catalog systems often require custom middleware to map AI metadata to existing fields, which could trigger scope creep.
3.  **Adoption Volatility:** The 15% lift projection extrapolates linearly from a short-term language filter test. Recommendation engines suffer from "novelty decay"; usage often spikes then drops if the suggestions lack deep contextual accuracy.

**Stop-or-Pivot Decision Trigger**
**Abandon or pivot the rollout if:**
After 90 days of the pilot, the **click-to-checkout conversion rate for Arabic and Somali recommendations falls below 2%** (or 50% of the Finnish/Swedish baseline).

This metric isolates whether the AI is actually understanding user intent in those languages. If the conversion is low, it indicates the model is guessing rather than knowing. At that threshold, continuing to pay the €45,000 license fee creates negative value. The pivot would be to pause the multilingual expansion and invest the license savings into curating a human-verified "staff pick" dataset for those specific languages to retrain the model locally.

**Note:** The current budget (€500k) covers the first year comfortably (€255k total), but leaves no buffer for the likely need for custom translation middleware.
**Note:** We must define "checkout volume" carefully; if the AI simply pushes popular books rather than discovering hidden gems, the 15% lift may occur naturally without justifying the AI cost.
