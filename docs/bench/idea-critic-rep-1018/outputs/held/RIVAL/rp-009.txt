**Toughest Counterpoint:**
The plan assumes a frictionless adoption curve in a market defined by hardware fragmentation and regulatory ambiguity. Your $5M revenue projection relies on acquiring 150,000 users at $35, but this ignores the "walled garden" reality of VR distribution. If Meta or Apple restricts your access to their headsets or takes a 30% platform cut (which your model seems to omit), your unit economics collapse. Furthermore, banking 30% of your budget on custom content while assuming a 20% market growth rate is a double-leverage bet: if the hardware install base grows at only 10% (a realistic baseline given current VR churn), your content ROI evaporates. The single biggest silent killer is city-level licensing for public installations; if municipalities classify immersive experiences as "public assemblies" or "broadcast media," you could face permit costs and delays that burn 6 months of your runway before you sell a single ticket.

**Cleaner Path:**
Pivot from a B2C "build it and they will come" model to a B2B2C "white-label" strategy. Instead of spending $660k on marketing to chase individual users on TikTok, license your LumenForge engine directly to existing entertainment venues (theme parks, escape rooms, cinema chains) who already have the foot traffic and real estate permits. This shifts the hardware licensing burden to the venue, reduces your customer acquisition cost, and turns your $35/user risk into a guaranteed SaaS licensing fee. You validate the tech with three ArcLight titles in controlled environments before attempting a mass-market consumer launch.

**Regulatory Classification Delay.** If any major target city (e.g., NYC, London, Tokyo) takes longer than 90 days to classify the installation type or signals that it requires a "broadcast license" rather than a standard business permit, **pivot immediately** to the B2B model. Do not wait for a revenue shortfall; by the time revenue misses the $3.2M mark, you will have already burned the $2.2M seed. The regulatory signal is the leading indicator; revenue lag is the autopsy.
