**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **single-project pilot** on a low-risk internal concept before committing to the full Eastgate Pavilion rollout. Do not sign the annual contract yet; negotiate a 90-day trial or a project-based license to validate the 30% efficiency claim and the Mac-Pro compatibility without exposing the firm to a full year of sunk costs.

**Top Three Ways This Could Flop:**

1.  **The "Integration Tax" Erosion:** Your 25k DKK integration budget is an unverified guess. If the legacy rendering pipeline requires custom scripting to read Formspire's parametric data, costs could easily triple, wiping out the 30k DKK annual software savings in year one alone.
2.  **Hardware Bottlenecking:** Parametric modeling is CPU/GPU intensive. If your current Mac-Pro workstations stutter or crash during real-time updates, the promised 360-hour saving vanishes into wait-times and file-corruption recovery, actually slowing the team down.
3.  **Client Aesthetic Rejection:** If the client rejects the "parametric aesthetic" as too industrial or complex, you lose the efficiency gain entirely. You will be forced to manually remodel the façade in the old tool to satisfy the client, meaning you pay for *both* workflows while delivering only one.

**Pull-the-Plug Trigger:**
Terminate the pilot and revert to the legacy workflow if, after the first 40 hours of active modeling, the team has not achieved at least a **15% reduction in modeling time** OR if the integration work requires more than **10 hours of engineering support**. This early checkpoint prevents throwing good money after a bad fit before the 150k DKK training and integration spend is fully realized.
