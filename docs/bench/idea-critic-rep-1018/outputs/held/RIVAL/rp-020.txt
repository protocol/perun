**Recommendation:** Proceed with the pilot only if you secure a **written letter of intent** from at least one major insurer confirming a reimbursement rate within 15% of your blended break-even point before June 1. Without this, the $75,000 investment is a speculative gamble on payer behavior rather than a strategic expansion.

**Top Three Failure Points:**
1.  **Reimbursement Collapse:** If insurers classify tele-rehab as a "convenience" rather than a medical necessity, they may reimburse at $15–$20/session instead of the required ~$35+. This instantly turns your projected margin into a loss per session, making volume dangerous rather than profitable.
2.  **The "Last Mile" Connectivity Gap:** Your model assumes 30% of households have adequate broadband. In Nairobi's informal settlements, even if a patient owns a smartphone (80% ownership), unstable data costs or network congestion often prevent sustained 2Mbps video streams. If >40% of scheduled sessions fail technically or drop out due to data costs, your utilization rate will never reach the 120 sessions/month needed to justify the therapist hours.
3.  **Therapist Bottleneck & Burnout:** Two therapists handling 120 sessions/month implies ~30 sessions/week each, on top of existing in-clinic duties. Tele-rehab often requires *more* administrative time per patient (tech support, follow-up messaging) than in-person care. If your therapists cannot sustain this volume without quality degradation or burnout, patient outcomes will suffer, leading to low retention and poor clinical data.

**Precise Halt/Pivot Metric:**
Track the **Confirmed Reimbursement Yield (CRY)** at the 60-day mark of the pilot.
*   **Formula:** (Total Insurer Payments Received) ÷ (Total Billable Tele-sessions Completed).
*   **Threshold:** If CRY is **< $32.00** by Day 60, **HALT** expansion immediately.
*   **Reasoning:** At $28/session cost, a $32 yield leaves only a $4 margin. This is insufficient to cover the amortized $75k platform cost, tablet depreciation, and administrative overhead within a reasonable ROI window. Do not wait for the full 6-month adoption review; cash flow negativity this deep this early indicates a fundamental payer mismatch, not an adoption problem.
