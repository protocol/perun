**Go-or-No-Go Recommendation: NO-GO (in current form)**

**Single Red Flag:** The assumption that regulatory approval will be granted by Q3 2025. If this slips by even six months, the three-year maintenance contract clock starts ticking against a non-revenue-generating asset, turning your €12M integration budget into a cash burn sink before a single package is delivered. Regulatory timelines for BVLOS (Beyond Visual Line of Sight) urban drone operations are notoriously volatile; banking your FY2028 EBITDA lift on a specific quarter for approval is speculative, not strategic.

**Key Failure Modes:**
1.  **Insurance Underestimation:** €150/drone/month (€450k/year total) is likely aggressive for urban cargo drones. One major incident could spike premiums to €1k+/month, eroding €1M+ of your projected savings annually.
2.  **Seasonal Revenue Mismatch:** Treating fuel savings as a straight line ignores the winter dip. If volume drops 30% in Q4/Q1, your variable cost savings vanish while fixed costs (maintenance contract, insurance, depreciation) remain, widening the gap to your €3.5M EBITDA target.
3.  **Integration Budget Creep:** €12M for software, training, and maintenance across 250 units leaves little buffer for the inevitable custom API work required to mesh drone telemetry with your existing legacy logistics stack.

**Hidden Costs & Risks Not in Draft:**
*   **Battery Degradation & Replacement:** Drones have high-cycle batteries. A 3-year contract likely covers "maintenance," but battery capacity fade below 80% often requires costly replacement packs not classified as standard repair, potentially a €2M unplanned capex by Year 3.
*   **Noise Abatement & Community Pushback:** Urban deployment often triggers noise complaints leading to flight path restrictions or night-flight bans, effectively cutting your usable delivery window and requiring more drones to hit the same volume.
*   **Obsolescence Risk:** Drone tech moves fast. Buying 250 identical units today risks being stuck with inferior hardware by Year 2 if competitors deploy quieter, longer-range models, forcing an early refresh cycle not modeled here.
