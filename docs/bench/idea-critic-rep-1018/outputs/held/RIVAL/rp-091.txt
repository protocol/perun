**Recommendation:** Proceed with the rollout, but condition the launch of the "Elite+" tier on securing a dedicated marketing budget of at least N3.5 million upfront. The "Growth" tier is a low-risk win that captures price-sensitive demand, but the "Elite+" conversion relies entirely on un-funded marketing reach; launching it without guaranteed spend risks creating a "ghost tier" that dilutes brand prestige without delivering revenue.

**How This Could Fall Apart:**
1.  **Cannibalization without Growth:** Existing Pro members downgrade to the new Growth tier to save N50,000, while the predicted new sign-ups fail to materialize due to the unquantified marketing gap. This shrinks average revenue per user (ARPU) instead of expanding it.
2.  **Brand Alienation:** The steep jump to the N550,000 Elite+ tier, coupled with perceived "nickel-and-diming" via meeting room credits on lower tiers, may signal to startups that the club is shifting from a flexible community to a rigid corporate hotel, driving churn above the critical 5% threshold.
3.  **Operational Friction:** The N12 million rollout cost assumes smooth software tweaks. If integration delays prevent accurate tracking of meeting room credits or private office access, member disputes will spike, increasing support costs and delaying revenue recognition.

**The Kill Metric:**
Track **Net New Monthly Recurring Revenue (NN-MRR)** specifically attributed to the new tiers. If, after three months, the net increase (new tier revenue + upgrades − downgrades − churned revenue) is less than **12%** of the current baseline monthly revenue (approx. N24.3 million), you must scrap the Elite+ tier immediately and re-evaluate the Growth tier pricing. This metric isolates whether the structural change is actually generating value or just shuffling existing members into cheaper plans.

*Note: Ensure your finance team isolates "downgrade revenue loss" daily during the first month; this is the leading indicator of cannibalization.*
*Do not announce the Elite+ tier publicly until the marketing budget is signed off; silence is better than a failed luxury launch.*
