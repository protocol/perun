**Three Primary Failure Points**

1.  **Demographic Alienation (NPS Risk):** The 42% of guests who did not use the kiosk during the pilot represent a critical vulnerability. If this segment skews toward older travelers or those with complex booking needs, forcing a digital-first flow could tank your Net Promoter Score. A drop in loyalty scores among high-value repeat guests would erase any operational savings.
2.  **Hardware Reliability & Support Latency:** With only five kiosks per property, a single unit failure during peak arrival windows (14:00–16:00) creates immediate bottlenecks. If the vendor's "2-week rollout" promise lacks a guaranteed 4-hour on-site support SLA, you risk turning a speed improvement into a PR crisis when machines jam.
3.  **Integration Fragility:** The pilot ran in a controlled environment. Scaling to three locations introduces variance in Wi-Fi stability and Property Management System (PMS) sync rates. If the kiosks fail to pull real-time room availability, guests will be directed to non-existent rooms, causing frustration that front-desk staff cannot easily resolve.

**The Halt Trigger Metric**

**Adoption Rate < 45% or NPS Drop > 10 Points.**

Establish a 60-day review window post-rollout. If total kiosk adoption across the three properties falls below 45% (a significant drop from the pilot's 58%), or if the overall property Net Promoter Score drops by more than 10 points compared to the same period last year, immediately halt the program and revert to full staffed check-in. This specific threshold accounts for normal variance while signaling a fundamental rejection of the user experience by your core demographic.
