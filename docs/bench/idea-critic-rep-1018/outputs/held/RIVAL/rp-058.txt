**Recommendation: Proceed with Conditional Approval**

The mobile-money integration is financially viable and strategically necessary to lower acquisition costs, but approval must be contingent on securing the operator commission rate and a firm regulatory timeline. The base case yields a strong return, but the current "guesses" on regulation and commissions represent unacceptable execution risk without guardrails.

**Stress Test: How This Plan Falls Apart**

1.  **The Margin Cliff (Commission Sensitivity):** Your model assumes a 1.2% commission. If this rises to your 2% kill threshold, you lose 0.8% of total transaction volume directly from the bottom line. At scale (12,000 users × $2.50 ARPU), this could extend the break-even period from 14 months to over 22 months, destroying the project's internal rate of return. The vendor's "around 1.2%" is not a contract; it is a placeholder for risk.
2.  **The Regulatory Black Hole:** A 6–9 week estimate for financial regulatory clearance is optimistic. If this slips to 4+ months, two things happen: you burn cash on development with zero revenue, and market conditions may shift. More critically, if regulators impose new capital reserve requirements or data localization rules in response to your application, the $150k budget could become insufficient, requiring a costly refactor before launch.
3.  **Conversion Overconfidence:** The plan hinges on converting 70% of existing borrowers (31,500 users). If friction in the new flow results in only 40% conversion, your volume projections drop by nearly half. Since the operator commission is percentage-based, your fixed costs remain high while variable revenue collapses, potentially making the unit economics negative per user.

**The First Sign to Pull the Plug**

**Trigger:** The operator presents a draft term sheet with a commission rate **above 1.5%** (not 2%).

**Reasoning:** Do not wait for the 2% kill switch. 1.5% is the early warning signal that negotiations are failing. If they open above 1.5%, they will likely settle near 1.8–1.9%, leaving you no buffer for transaction failures or disputes. Walking away at the draft stage saves the development spend entirely.

**Secondary Trigger:** Regulatory clearance exceeds **10 weeks** without a written interim approval. This indicates structural resistance rather than administrative delay, signaling a high probability of hitting the 3-month kill zone.

**Immediate Next Step:** Instruct the vendor to halt detailed architecture work until a non-binding Letter of Intent (LOI) regarding the commission rate is secured from the mobile operator. Do not spend a dollar of the $150k until the variable cost is capped.
