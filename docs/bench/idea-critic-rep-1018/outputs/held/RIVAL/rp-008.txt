**Recommendation: Proceed with a Phased Pilot, Not Full Deployment**

Do not sign the €2M/year contract immediately. Instead, negotiate a 6-month pilot restricted to a single production line for a reduced fee (e.g., €400k). The current proposal relies on unverified assumptions regarding data quality and integration effort that create a high risk of negative ROI in Year 1. A pilot allows you to validate the 12% downtime reduction claim and the actual effort required for legacy PLC mapping before committing to enterprise-wide scale.

**Top Failure Modes to Flag**

1.  **Data Quality Illusion:** The assumption of 95% sensor data quality is likely theoretical. If real-world noise, missing packets, or calibration drift lower effective quality to <85%, the predictive model's accuracy will collapse, rendering the downtime reduction impossible.
2.  **Integration Underestimation:** Mapping legacy PLC logs is notoriously fragile. The estimated 4-week integration could easily expand to 12+ weeks if documentation is missing or protocols are proprietary, delaying value realization and inflating the one-time €400k cost.
3.  **Benchmark Mismatch:** The competitor's 15% savings over 18 months is not comparable to your 12% target over 12 months. Their baseline downtime may have been higher, or their machinery newer. Using their data to justify your timeline creates unrealistic pressure on the implementation team.

**Precise Pull-the-Plug Trigger**

Terminate the engagement if, at the **Month 6 review of the pilot**, the validated annualized savings projection falls below **€1.4M** (not €900k).

*Reasoning:* Your break-even point is €2.4M (€2M license + €400k amortized implementation). A projection of €1.8M (the vendor's claim) already offers a slim margin for error. If the pilot data suggests you will only achieve €1.4M, you are effectively losing money on the operational change relative to the license cost, making the full rollout financially unsound.

**Critical Unknowns to Stress-Test**

*   **Legacy PLC Mapping:** Treat the 3-week IT estimate as a best-case scenario. Plan for a 4-week buffer in your internal timeline. If the vendor cannot provide a detailed schema mapping plan within the first 2 weeks of the pilot, flag this as a critical integration risk.
*   **Apples-to-Oranges Comparison:** Demand the vendor disclose the competitor's baseline downtime rate and machine age. If they cannot, disregard the 15% benchmark entirely and base your success criteria solely on your own historical downtime data.
