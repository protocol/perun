**Three Principal Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Model Collapse:** The proposal incorrectly calculates a 5% lift on *monthly* revenue (€10k gain) rather than *annual* revenue (€833 gain). At €5,250 monthly cost, the project loses €4,417 every month instead of gaining money.
2.  **Regulatory Cost Shock:** Without a priced GDPR scope, fines or mandatory legal retrofitting for processing senior and youth biometric data could exceed the platform's entire annual budget in a single event.
3.  **Adoption Fracture:** If senior adoption falls below 40% due to privacy concerns or device complexity, the "performance insight" value proposition evaporates, leaving you with high fixed costs and fragmented data.

**Abandonment Trigger:**
Pull the plug immediately if the **verified corrected revenue lift is less than €6,000 per month** (the break-even point for costs + contingency) or if **GDPR compliance costs exceed €15,000** as a one-time implementation fee.

**What You Are Not Seeing & What I Would Do Differently:**
You are missing the **liability asymmetry**: collecting performance data on minors (youth) and seniors creates a high-risk profile that standard waivers may not cover. One data leak or injury attributed to "algorithmic coaching" could dwarf any efficiency gains. Furthermore, the 80% opt-in assumption is dangerously optimistic for a demographic (seniors) often resistant to new wearables.

I would restructure this as a **vendor-funded pilot**: demand PulseGear absorb the GDPR setup costs and provide the first 50 devices free for three months in exchange for a case study. Shift the narrative from "buying a platform" to "validating a partnership." If they refuse to share the risk, they do not believe in their own ROI, and neither should you.

**When to Abandon:**
Abandon the plan if the vendor refuses a pilot structure, or if the corrected financial model shows a payback period longer than 18 months.
