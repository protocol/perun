**From:** CFO  
**Re:** Vino Ember Launch Decision

**Course of Action:**  
Proceed with a **staged launch** capped at 200 households for the first 90 days, rather than the full 500. Use this period to validate the true landed cost per box (specifically the variable regulatory fees) and confirm the bottling line's actual throughput before committing the remaining capital.

**Most Plausible Objection:**  
The unit economics are fragile because the "unknown" regulatory fee is treated as a variance rather than a fixed cost. If the average provincial fee exceeds $12 per box, the contribution margin on an $89 subscription turns negative once shipping ($5) and the 8% quarterly churn are factored in. We are effectively betting on regulatory arbitrage without a confirmed floor. At 500 households, a $5 underestimate in fees burns $30,000 annually in pure loss, excluding the $78,000 CapEx.

**Stronger Approach:**  
Shift from a "capacity-first" build to a "demand-verified" model. Instead of installing the $78,000 second line immediately, secure a temporary toll-bottling agreement for the incremental 3,600 bottles (500 subs × 3 bottles × 12 months). This converts fixed CapEx into variable OpEx, preserving cash flow while we prove that the $89 price point holds against real-world logistics costs. Only trigger the permanent line installation once we hit 350 active subscriptions with verified positive unit economics.

**Key Failure Modes:**  
1.  **Regulatory Shock:** A province classifies the subscription model under a higher tax bracket than standard retail, spiking the fee per unit.
2.  **Churn Acceleration:** The 8% quarterly churn assumption proves optimistic; if monthly churn hits 5%, the cost to acquire replacements erodes all margin.
3.  **Bottleneck Lag:** The new line requires more than 4 weeks to calibrate, causing stockouts that trigger a churn spike in the critical first quarter.

**Pull-the-Plug Trigger:**  
Stop the Vino Ember initiative immediately if the **fully loaded cost per box exceeds $58** (which includes wine cost, packaging, shipping, and average regulatory fees) after the first 60 days of the staged launch. This threshold ensures we maintain a minimum 35% gross margin to cover acquisition and overhead. If the math doesn't work at 200 units, it will not work at 500.
