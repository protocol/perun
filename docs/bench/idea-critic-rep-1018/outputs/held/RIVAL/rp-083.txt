**Recommendation for CEO Amina:**
"Do not commit capital until we secure a binding fixed-price contract for battery storage from a local supplier. The current financial model is speculative because it relies on competitor data from a different market (Tanzania) and ignores battery degradation. We must validate that our levelized cost of energy remains under $0.15/kWh over a 10-year lifecycle, not just the first four years, before presenting this as a viable investment."

**Most Likely Failure Modes:**
1.  **Capital Cost Underestimation:** If the actual local battery cost exceeds the $120k estimate (likely, given import duties and logistics differences from Tanzania), the upfront capital requirement will spike, breaking the loan-to-value ratio and potentially making the project unfinanceable at 8% interest.
2.  **Revenue Erosion via Degradation:** The model assumes static operating costs. If battery capacity degrades faster than anticipated, you will need to supplement with diesel or replace units earlier than planned, pushing your levelized cost above $0.15/kWh. At that point, customers will revert to diesel generators, causing revenue to collapse.
3.  **Policy Risk:** The model depends on a 20% government tax credit. If this policy is delayed, reduced, or repealed during the 24-month rollout, the internal rate of return will fall below the cost of debt, turning the project into a loss-maker.

**Walk-Away Trigger:**
Drop the scheme immediately if the verified local battery procurement cost exceeds **$165,000** (a ~38% increase over the estimate) OR if lifecycle modeling shows the levelized cost of energy rises above **$0.14/kWh** after year four. At these thresholds, the margin of safety against diesel competition vanishes, and the risk-adjusted return no longer justifies the deployment capital.
