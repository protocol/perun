**Recommendation: Proceed with Pilot, Withhold Full Rollout Commitment**

Move forward with the Q3 2025 pilot but treat the January 2026 company-wide rollout as conditional, not guaranteed. The projected 3.8-year payback period is attractive, but the financial model contains a critical error and unquantified operational risks that must be resolved during the pilot before signing a full deployment contract.

**Primary Failure Modes**

1.  **Flawed Savings Logic:** Your net annual saving calculation assumes a flat $3 savings per claim ($1.2M × $3 = $3.6M). This is unsubstantiated. If the $3 figure includes variable costs that don't actually decrease (like fixed overhead or non-labor expenses), your real savings could be half of projected, extending payback to 7+ years.
2.  **The "Bilingual Blind Spot":** The vendor quote and accuracy claim (95%) likely rely on English-only training data. If the system fails to parse French claim notes accurately, accuracy could plummet to ~80% in bilingual regions, causing massive rework queues and negating the speed benefit entirely.
3.  **Peak Load Collapse:** Flu season creates non-linear claim spikes. If the AI latency increases from 1.2 days to 4 days under load, the triage system becomes a bottleneck rather than an accelerator, forcing you to pay for both the AI and the legacy manual overflow team.
4.  **Integration Cost Overrun:** The $1.2M integration estimate is a "best case." Legacy claims systems often reveal hidden dependencies during implementation, potentially doubling this cost and eroding Year 1 ROI.

**The Walk-Away Trigger**

You must define a specific "Kill Criterion" for the pilot. Tell Maya:

*"We will terminate the project and walk away if, at the end of the 90-day pilot, the **weighted average processing time for bilingual claims exceeds 3.0 days**."*

**Why this metric?**
*   **3.0 days** is your break-even point. Anything slower than this provides negligible improvement over the current 5-day baseline once rework and exception handling are factored in.
*   Focusing on **bilingual claims** specifically tests the highest-risk variable. If the system works for English but fails for French, the product is unusable for your market.
*   This prevents "average" metrics from hiding failure; a 1.0-day English average could mask a 6.0-day French average, resulting in a deceptive 2.5-day overall average that still destroys operational flow.

**Immediate Action:** Before the meeting, recalculate the $3/claim savings variable with Finance to isolate pure variable cost reductions. Present the pilot as a "validation of the savings model," not just a technology test.
