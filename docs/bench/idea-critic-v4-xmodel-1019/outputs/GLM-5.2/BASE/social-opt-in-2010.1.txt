The proposal presents a critical conflict between rapid growth and user trust. Enrolling the existing user base automatically with visible connections prioritizes immediate scale over consent. This approach carries severe downsides.

First, the privacy risk is substantial. Making connections visible by default violates the expectation of control users have within an inbox. This is not a peripheral feature; it touches sensitive, personal communications. The feeling that privacy is being disregarded is a near certainty, not a mere possibility.

Second, the regulatory attention risk is a direct consequence of the automatic enrollment and default settings. Launching with a design that anticipates such scrutiny is untenable for a company whose core business depends on user data and trust.

Third, the combination of low ongoing use and irreversibility creates a damaging asymmetry. If the social layer gets little sustained engagement, the company will have exposed user connections and breached trust for a feature that fails to stick. The inability to reverse the launch means the damage is permanent, leaving the company with the worst outcome: a failed product and a degraded reputation.

The goal of adding a social layer is sound, but the proposed mechanism is flawed. The default must be privacy, and enrollment must be a deliberate user choice. A launch that makes connections visible without explicit, affirmative consent is too reckless to approve.

DECISION: NO-GO
