The proposal presents a fundamental strategic conflict. The company’s core asset is a reputation for quality watchmaking, yet this product is positioned as a disposable, inexpensive plastic item. Launching twelve fashion models simultaneously magnifies this risk. It directly threatens the premium brand equity that supports the company's existing margins and customer loyalty.

The sales target of one million watches in the first year is highly aggressive for a new entrant in the fashion watch market. Achieving this volume requires massive distribution and marketing spend. If the target is missed, the company risks being left with excess inventory of a product that is already vulnerable to rapidly changing fashion tastes. The combination of high volume ambitions and fashion volatility creates a severe inventory write-down risk.

Furthermore, the competitive landscape is unfavorable. Established brands can cut prices to defend their market share. Because those competitors already possess scale advantages and brand recognition, a price war would likely squeeze the proposed product's margins to unprofitable levels. The company would be fighting entrenched players in a volatile segment without a clear, defensible advantage.

The core issue is that this proposal attempts to be both a high-volume consumer product and a premium brand extension. These objectives are mutually exclusive under the current plan. Introducing a low-cost plastic line under the master brand risks cannibalizing higher-margin products and diluting the quality perception that took decades to build. The potential upside of capturing fashion-driven sales does not offset the existential threat to brand equity and the financial risks of an aggressive launch.

The company must protect its primary source of competitive advantage. A revised strategy could involve a controlled, limited launch under a new sub-brand, with conservative sales targets and a smaller initial collection to test market reception without endangering the parent brand's reputation.

DECISION: NO-GO
