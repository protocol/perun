The proposal targets a legitimate revenue opportunity but carries privacy and reputational risks that outweigh the immediate benefit.

Users often misunderstand how data is shared across sites. A notification step helps, but it still places the burden on users to opt out. Purchases range from routine to deeply personal, and surfacing them in a social feed creates exposure the user may not anticipate. The risk is not merely dissatisfaction; it is a breach of trust that can damage the platform's core relationship with users.

Partner sites face their own risk. If users react negatively to shared purchases, those partners become associated with an unwanted experience. This could deter participation and limit the program's reach.

The difficulty of withdrawing the feature once launched is a significant strategic concern. If adoption is low or backlash emerges, retreat becomes costly. The feature would already have shaped user expectations and partner commitments.

The revenue objective does not require this level of exposure. Less intrusive approaches, such as aggregated or anonymized sharing, could pursue the same goal with reduced risk. The proposal as stated does not adequately separate sensitive from nonsensitive activity, nor does it establish a default that protects users who ignore the notification.

Given the sensitivity of purchase data, the irreversibility of launch, and the availability of less invasive alternatives, the proposal should not proceed in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
