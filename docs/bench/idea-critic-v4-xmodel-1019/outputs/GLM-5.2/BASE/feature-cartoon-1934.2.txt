The core question is whether betting the studio's thin short-film margins on a single ten-times-cost feature is justified by the facts given.

The downside is severe and immediate. If audiences reject a feature-length cartoon, the studio loses not just one project but the reserves generated by its steady shorts. The budget is explicitly at overrun risk, meaning the true exposure could exceed ten times an average short. Family and industry insiders advise against it, which is not decisive but signals that the conventional wisdom and available market evidence do not support confidence. The proposal also appears to rest on self-funding from existing earnings, with no stated cushion or external risk-sharing.

The upside is plausible but unproven. A hit feature could elevate the studio beyond thin short-film profits, but the facts give no evidence of tested audience demand for feature-length animation, no locked cost controls, and no mitigation for the overrun risk. The studio is not presented as facing collapse if it declines; it has steady, if thin, profits. Approving as proposed means accepting maximum exposure on unvalidated assumptions.

Because the proposal asks for approval now, as drafted, and the known risks include budget overruns, demand uncertainty, and unanimous expert caution, the responsible path is to decline approval as proposed. The studio should continue earning from shorts and revisit the feature concept only after testing audience willingness, securing cost discipline, and reducing single-project concentration.

DECISION: NO-GO
