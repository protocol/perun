The proposal centers on selling an unfinished sandbox game immediately and iterating based on player feedback. The core risks identified are substantial and interrelated.

First, the "rough state" of the game poses a significant threat to long-term viability. If early buyers judge the game harshly, this will generate negative word-of-mouth. In a market without a marketing budget, organic player sentiment is the primary driver of sales. Negative early impressions could permanently damage the game's reputation, causing the "stop buying" risk to become a self-fulfilling prophecy.

Second, the concern about a solo developer's ability to support a large audience is critical. If the alpha release is successful, the developer may be overwhelmed by bug reports, feature requests, and technical support. This could stall development and lead to player frustration. Conversely, if the release is unsuccessful, the developer has exposed an unfinished product to the market prematurely, potentially burning the only opportunity to make a strong first impression.

Third, the absence of a publisher or marketing budget means the game must succeed entirely on its own merits and organic visibility. An unfinished game lacks the polish typically required to generate positive viral momentum. Releasing it as-is risks squandering the sandbox genre's main strength: deep, engaging mechanics that encourage player creativity and long-term retention. If the current build does not clearly demonstrate this value, early sales will likely falter.

The proposal asks for approval to release immediately. However, the identified risks are not merely potential issues; they are fundamental threats to the project's survival. The strategy of "release now, fix later" is only viable if the core gameplay loop is already compelling enough to overcome the lack of polish. Given the explicit acknowledgment of the game's "rough state" and the solo developer's support limitations, approving the proposal as currently framed is too hazardous. The potential for irreversible reputational damage outweighs the benefit of early revenue.

DECISION: NO-GO
