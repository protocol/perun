This proposal asks us to throw good money after bad. The core problem is that we are evaluating a consumer product that consumers apparently do not want.

The risks raised are not minor operational hurdles; they are fatal flaws. If smokers dislike the taste and find the product complicated to light, we have no product. The entire value proposition rests on the claim that this is a "cleaner" alternative. The moment regulators challenge that health claim, our marketing strategy collapses and we face potential legal exposure. We cannot launch a premium, "clean" product whose central benefit may be prohibited from being advertised.

The fact that development has already cost a very large sum is irrelevant to the forward-looking decision, and citing it as a reason to proceed is a classic trap. Sunk costs do not justify further investment in a failing venture.

We should not approve this proposal as presented. The test market results, assuming they reflect the concerns about taste and usability, are telling us the product is not ready. Launching widely now would burn through marketing budgets on a product with questionable consumer acceptance and high regulatory risk.

A better path is to hold the launch, revisit the product design to address the taste and usability issues, and consult regulatory experts to determine what claims, if any, can be legally supported. Only when we have a product consumers prefer and a legally sound marketing message should we consider a wide rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
