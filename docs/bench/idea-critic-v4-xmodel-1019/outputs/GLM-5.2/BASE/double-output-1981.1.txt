The core problem is demand, not supply. Only 40% of produced cars have sold, creating a growing inventory of 4,500 unsold vehicles. Doubling monthly production would accelerate cash burn by pouring working capital into assets that are not moving. With limited cash already a constraint, this proposal risks insolvency before market conditions improve.

Recession and high interest rates are suppressing demand on two fronts: consumers face tighter credit and lower confidence, while dealers struggle to finance additional inventory. Flooding a weak market with more supply will not create buyers. Instead, it will likely force deeper discounting, further eroding margins and brand positioning. The company would be paying to produce and store cars that dealers cannot floor-plan and customers cannot afford to finance.

The fundamental issue is a demand shortfall, not a production shortfall. Doubling output addresses the wrong variable. The proposal should be rejected. The company needs a revised plan that reduces production to preserve cash, offers targeted financing incentives to stimulate demand, and clears existing inventory before committing scarce capital to new units.

DECISION: NO-GO
