The proposal asks the executive team to replace the company's only successful product with an unproven business successor under a tight timeline. The core issue is that the current product lacks features business users demand, such as lowercase letters and an 80-column display. Without these features, the company cannot effectively compete in the growing business market.

However, the risks identified are substantial. The new design's complexity combined with a tight schedule increases the likelihood of delays or defects. Hardware reliability remains unproven, which could damage the company's reputation among business users who require dependable systems. Additionally, ending the current product line risks alienating existing customers and software developers who have invested in the current platform. If the successor fails to gain traction, the company could find itself without a viable product in either the consumer or business market.

The proposal presents a false choice: either continue with the current product as-is or replace it entirely within the year. A more prudent approach would involve developing the business successor while maintaining the current product line until the new machine proves its reliability and market acceptance. This would protect existing revenue streams and preserve the ecosystem of software developers and customers.

Given the significant risks associated with ending the current product line and launching an unproven successor under a tight timeline, the proposal should not be approved as presented. The company should pursue the business successor but with a more measured approach that does not jeopardize its only successful product.

DECISION: NO-GO
