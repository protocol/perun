The proposal carries fatal flaws in its financial structure and market positioning. Launching at approximately $700 places the console well above the existing market threshold. Consumers have demonstrated reluctance to absorb high upfront hardware costs. Competitors intentionally subsidize their hardware, selling at a loss to build an installed base, then recoup margins through game sales and licensing fees. By contrast, this model requires electronics makers to manufacture the hardware at a profit, while the start-up also takes a royalty on each console. Additionally, the market already contains several entrenched competitors with vast capital, established developer ecosystems, and strong brand recognition. Entering at a premium price with hardware manufactured by third parties who need their own margins creates a structural disadvantage. If competitors are pricing hardware below cost, this start-up cannot match them without obliterating the profit incentive for its licensees. The royalty-on-consoles model compounds the problem: every console sale must satisfy the manufacturer’s profit, the start-up’s royalty, and potentially retailer margins, all before a single game is sold.

The proposal also assumes early adopters will pay a premium for high-end graphics, but early adopters are typically a narrow segment. Without a broader installed base, game developers have little incentive to create titles for the platform. Without games, the console has no value proposition regardless of hardware specifications. The licensing strategy fragments control over pricing, marketing, and hardware quality, making it difficult to compete against unified, vertically integrated competitors.

The fundamental issue is that the business model is incompatible with the competitive dynamics of the console market. Hardware-at-profit plus console royalties plus a $700 launch price creates an unsustainable cost structure. The proposal does not resolve how to compete with loss-leader hardware pricing while maintaining licensee profitability.

DECISION: NO-GO
