The proposal targets a real opportunity but carries fatal flaws as currently structured.

First, the $1,000 price is the central problem. Game consoles in 1991 sell for roughly $150 to $200. A player priced five times higher cannot reach the volume needed to attract software developers, yet it offers less functionality than a personal computer. The product sits in an untenable middle ground: too expensive for the living room, too limited for the study.

Second, the competitive timing is poor. Personal computers are rapidly gaining multimedia capability. By pricing near a multimedia PC while offering fewer applications, the player invites direct comparison it will lose. Families deciding between a $1,000 disc player and a $1,500 computer with broader utility will likely choose the computer.

Third, the chicken-and-egg problem is unresolved. A small interactive library means early adopters find little value, which means developers see little installed base, which means the library stays small. Without a credible plan to seed content — exclusive partnerships, in-house development, or aggressive developer subsidies — the platform cannot bootstrap.

Fourth, the positioning confusion compounds every other issue. If buyers cannot articulate what the product is for, they will not spend $1,000 on it. "Between a game console and a PC" is not a position; it is an admission of category ambiguity. Retail salespeople will struggle to demonstrate value, and shelf space will be hard to justify.

The underlying insight — that interactive multimedia belongs in the living room — is sound. But the proposed execution asks consumers to pay a premium price for an unclear category with minimal content, against a PC backdrop that is closing the multimedia gap from the other direction. The risk profile is asymmetric: high capital commitment, uncertain demand, weak content flywheel, and a price point that repels the mass market.

A viable path would require a dramatically lower price, a clear single use case to anchor positioning, and guaranteed launch content. None of these conditions are met in the current proposal.

DECISION: NO-GO
