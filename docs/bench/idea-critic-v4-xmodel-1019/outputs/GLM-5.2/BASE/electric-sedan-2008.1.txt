The proposal asks the executive team to commit the entire company to one vehicle type and one large factory, despite the company having insufficient capital for either the facility or the battery packs. This creates an immediate, structural funding gap that cannot be ignored.

The risks identified are not peripheral; they are central to the business model. Mass production experience is lacking, yet the plan hinges on running a large factory. Charging infrastructure is scarce, limiting consumer adoption. Established competitors possess the scale, capital, and dealer networks to respond aggressively. By committing to exclusive production of one drivetrain type in a single large facility, the company concentrates enormous operational and market risk in one bet, with no margin for error and no demonstrated manufacturing competence to fall back on.

The core problem is that the proposal conflates a compelling long-term product vision with a viable near-term execution plan. The design may be strong, but design alone does not yield volume production. Acquiring a large factory from established carmakers imposes fixed costs and integration challenges that a young company with limited capital is poorly positioned to absorb. If battery pack costs remain high or production ramps slowly, the cash burn will outstrip any plausible financing. The scarcity of charging stations further depresses near-term demand, making the revenue side of the plan uncertain precisely when capital costs are highest.

A more prudent path would preserve the product vision while staging capital and risk: secure committed funding for a defined production run before acquiring the factory, partner on battery supply and manufacturing rather than owning all assets immediately, and avoid an irrevocable commitment to a single drivetrain type until unit economics and demand are proven. The proposal as written asks the team to bet the company on unproven mass production, scarce infrastructure, and a capital structure the company does not yet have. That is not a risk the executive team should approve now.

DECISION: NO-GO
