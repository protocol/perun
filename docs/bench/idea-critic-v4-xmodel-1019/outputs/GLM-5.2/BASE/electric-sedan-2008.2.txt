The proposal hinges on a bet that the future of the car is electric. The design exists, but committing the entire company to one technology and one large factory is a leap into the unknown.

The most glaring risk is the gap between the company's capital and the demands of the factory and battery packs. Without a clear path to funding the full production run, the company could stall mid-build. The scarcity of charging stations compounds this: the product's value depends on infrastructure the company does not control. Established makers have the scale and capital to respond, and the company's lack of mass-production experience means quality and delivery are uncertain.

The proposal is bold but brittle. It puts all resources behind a single vehicle and a single technology, with no fallback if the market or the factory fails. The capital risk alone warrants caution: committing to a large factory purchase before proving the design at scale is a gamble that could sink the company before the first car is delivered.

A more prudent path would be to prove the powertrain in a smaller, lower-volume vehicle, secure partnerships for both the factory and the battery supply, and stage the capital commitment. The vision is sound, but the execution as proposed carries too much unmitigated risk for a company with no production experience and limited funds.

DECISION: NO-GO
