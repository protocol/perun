The core creative premise—a story told from a small toy's point of view—is distinctive and emotionally resonant, suggesting strong audience appeal and merchandising potential. The fact that a large studio attempted to hire the lead animator before offering a production deal validates the company's talent. However, the structural risks are severe. The company has never produced a feature, and no one has successfully rendered an entire feature-length film with computer animation. The cost and time required for this unproven process could be ruinous. More critically, the proposed structure grants the larger partner control over distribution, meaning the small company bears the creative and technical risk while the publisher captures the downstream value. The lead animator's talent is the company's primary asset; entering a deal where they could be overextended on an unproven, multi-year technical undertaking jeopardizes that asset without guaranteeing proportional reward. Approving this as proposed would mean betting the company's survival on an unprecedented technical achievement within a deal structure that favors the partner. The upside is real, but the current terms expose the company to catastrophic downside risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
