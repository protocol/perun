The proposal to move the laptop line to internally designed processors is strategically sound. The company already possesses a proven design approach through its phone and tablet chips, demonstrating existing technical capability rather than a speculative leap.

The risks raised are significant but manageable. Existing software compatibility represents the greatest technical hurdle. However, the company can implement a translation layer to run legacy applications during the multi-year transition window. This approach mitigates immediate disruption while developers adapt. The unproven nature of the company's laptop chips against the supplier's is a valid concern, yet it applies primarily to the first generation. Internal testing and controlled rollout phases can validate performance and thermal characteristics before a full-scale launch.

Engineering time and cost are real constraints, but they represent an investment in vertical integration. By designing its own laptop processors, the company gains tighter control over its hardware-software stack, potentially leading to performance and efficiency gains that differentiate its products in a competitive market. The end of the supplier relationship carries strategic implications. Supplier dependency introduces supply chain vulnerabilities and limits customization. Moving to self-designed processors reduces external dependency, though it increases internal manufacturing complexity.

Buyer confusion during the transition can be addressed through clear marketing and a phased rollout. The company's existing brand strength in its phone and tablet lines can be leveraged to build confidence in the new laptop processors. The key consideration is whether the potential long-term benefits outweigh the short-term costs and risks. Given the company's existing design expertise and the strategic advantages of vertical integration, the proposal presents a compelling case for approval. The risks are inherent in such a transition but are not insurmountable with careful planning and execution.

DECISION: GO
