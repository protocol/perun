The proposal presents significant, unmitigated risks that outweigh the potential advertising revenue.

First, the core mechanism—sharing users' purchase and activity data from partner websites with their friends—introduces a severe privacy vulnerability. The proposal includes a notification step and an option to decline, but this relies heavily on users actively understanding and modifying their settings before any data is shared. Given the potential sensitivity of purchases (such as medical supplies, personal care items, or gifts), even a single high-profile incident of unintended disclosure could result in substantial reputational damage and loss of user trust.

Second, the proposal creates liability for partner websites. If a user’s activity on a partner site is broadcast to their social graph, it could lead to unwanted public reactions or expose transaction details the partner intended to keep private. This risk could deter current and future partners from participating, undermining the very ecosystem the proposal seeks to monetize.

Third, the social network operates in a highly competitive environment where user trust is a core asset. While the goal of increasing advertising revenue is valid, the method of achieving it—by commercializing private behavioral data—conflicts with the expectation of a secure user experience. The difficulty of withdrawing the feature post-launch further amplifies these concerns, as any negative backlash would be hard to contain without appearing to retreat from a failed initiative.

The revenue model must not depend on exposing sensitive user behavior without robust, affirmative, and granular user consent. The current design does not adequately ensure this standard. The proposal must be revised to address these privacy and partner liability issues before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
