The proposal presents severe downside risk with limited evidence of upside. The studio operates on thin profits from shorts, meaning the required investment of ten times a normal budget would consume a significant portion of accumulated earnings. If the feature fails, the studio would not merely lose money but would likely face existential threat, having drained the steady revenue source that sustains operations.

The concerns raised are not speculative anxieties but grounded operational realities. Audience behavior represents an untested variable: no market data exists proving families or general audiences would pay full admission prices for roughly eighty minutes of animation when the established format runs roughly eight to ten minutes. The budget risk compounds this uncertainty. A first attempt at a substantially longer production introduces production challenges the studio has never navigated. Cost overruns on a project this size could exceed the studio's entire short-film operating margin for a year or more.

The founder's family and industry insiders opposing the project should be weighted heavily. Insiders understand distribution realities, theater owner willingness to book animated features, and audience reception patterns. Their consensus against the proposal signals that the studio would be betting against informed industry judgment.

Funding this entirely from short-film earnings creates a fragility problem. The studio has no diversified revenue stream, no outside capital, and no margin for error. A single failed feature would eliminate the cash reserves needed to continue the short-film program that pays the bills.

The potential reward does exist: a successful feature could redefine the studio's market position and create a new revenue category. But the probability of success is too uncertain given the absence of market validation, the presence of informed opposition, and the catastrophic consequences of failure. The studio should continue building profitability through proven short-film production while exploring smaller-scale experiments to test audience appetite for longer-form content.

DECISION: NO-GO
