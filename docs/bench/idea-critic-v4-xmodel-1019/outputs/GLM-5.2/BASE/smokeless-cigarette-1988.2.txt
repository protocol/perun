The proposal is to launch the heated-tobacco cigarette after test markets and promote it widely as a cleaner way to smoke.

Key facts support cautious advancement. The product is differentiated: it heats rather than burns tobacco, yielding an aerosol with little smoke. This directly addresses growing social and regulatory pressure around secondhand smoke and odor. Years of development and a very large sunk cost mean the firm has invested heavily; securing a return requires market entry, not abandonment. Test markets are planned, which will generate evidence on taste, ease of use, and consumer acceptance before full rollout.

However, the risks are material and the "promote widely as a cleaner way to smoke" element is premature. Regulators may object to health claims, and making unsubstantiated assertions could trigger restrictions or liability. Smokers may dislike the taste or find the lighting method and product complexity off-putting; without evidence of acceptable consumer experience, wide promotion is risky. The large sunk cost should not drive an aggressive launch before test-market data exists.

The defensible path is to proceed to test markets but hold wide promotional claims until results are in. If test markets show acceptable taste and usability, and if regulators permit limited claims, then broader launch can follow. The core innovation has strategic value in a tightening regulatory and social environment, but the proposal as written—launching after test markets and promoting widely as cleaner—assumes facts not yet established and invites regulatory exposure.

DECISION: NO-GO
