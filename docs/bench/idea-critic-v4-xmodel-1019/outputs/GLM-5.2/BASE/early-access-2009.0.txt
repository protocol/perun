The core idea has real merit: charging early creates revenue and feedback while the game is still malleable, and a sandbox building game is well suited to ongoing updates because players derive value from the creative tools themselves rather than from a finished narrative.

The risks, however, are not merely possible but likely. Releasing an unfinished game for sale invites immediate judgment based on its rough state, and first impressions in the market are difficult to reverse. A solo developer genuinely cannot support a large audience, and if the game attracts attention before it is stable, the resulting backlash could permanently damage its reputation. Players who see the unfinished version may stop buying, and without a publisher or marketing budget, the developer has no buffer against negative word of mouth.

What is missing from the proposal is any gating mechanism. There is a meaningful difference between releasing an unfinished game to everyone for sale and releasing it to a limited, self-selected group who understand they are buying into a work in progress. The latter preserves the benefits of early revenue and feedback while substantially reducing the risks of widespread negative judgment and unsustainable support burden.

The proposal should be revised to include controlled access: a small initial release, clear communication that the game is unfinished, and a plan to scale only as the developer's capacity and the game's stability allow. This would retain the innovative spirit of the proposal while addressing its most serious vulnerabilities. As it stands, the proposal asks us to accept avoidable risks without any mitigating structure in place.

DECISION: NO-GO
