The core opportunity is sound: an inexpensive plastic movement shifts the competitive basis from craftsmanship to volume and style. Entering this segment is strategically necessary because ignoring it leaves a growing market entirely to competitors.

However, the specific proposal is flawed in its execution. Launching twelve models simultaneously is an inefficient allocation of resources for an unproven market entry. It dramatically increases inventory risk and upfront capital requirements without any guarantee of consumer reception. A more prudent approach would be to test the market with a smaller, focused collection to validate demand and refine the supply chain before a full-scale rollout.

The concern regarding reputational harm is valid but manageable. The key is strategic separation. By branding these watches distinctly and distributing them through different channels than the premium lines, the company can capture the mass market without diluting its legacy of quality. This is not an either/or choice; it is a portfolio expansion.

The aggressive sales target of one million units in the first year presents a significant financial risk. If the initial consumer reception is lukewarm, the company would be left with substantial unsold inventory, leading to forced markdowns that could further damage brand equity. This target should be tied to phased milestones, with initial production runs calibrated to real-time sell-through data rather than optimistic projections.

The threat of established brands cutting prices is the most critical risk. If incumbents respond aggressively, a price war could erode margins to the point of unprofitability. The proposal does not adequately address how the company will sustain a competitive advantage if the market becomes a race to the bottom on price. A durable cost advantage or a superior brand resonance would be necessary prerequisites before committing to such a high-volume strategy.

The strategic rationale for entering the plastic watch market is compelling, but the current proposal asks the executive team to take on excessive operational and financial risk without a clear mitigation strategy for competitive retaliation.

DECISION: NO-GO
