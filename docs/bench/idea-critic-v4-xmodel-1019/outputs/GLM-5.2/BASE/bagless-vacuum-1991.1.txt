The core product is technically proven through thousands of prototypes, mitigating the primary technical risk. The proposed premium pricing strategy is sound; it creates the necessary margin to self-fund manufacturing and establishes the product as a superior alternative to cheap, disposable-bag competitors. Entering at a premium avoids a direct price war with established incumbents who possess overwhelming retail distribution advantages.

The lack of a manufacturing record is a significant hurdle, but it is an operational challenge rather than a fundamental flaw in the proposition. The inventor's evident perseverance in building thousands of prototypes suggests the necessary tenacity to navigate production hurdles. This risk can be managed through phased scaling, strict quality control, and potentially outsourcing initial production to established contract manufacturers.

The threat of incumbents copying the cyclone technology is real, but it represents a delayed risk. Large corporations are typically slow to innovate and hesitant to cannibalize their existing profitable bag-and-replacement markets. This hesitation provides a critical window to establish the brand, secure intellectual property, and capture early adopters. By the time incumbents react, the new company can have a first-mover advantage.

The concern that buyers will not pay extra is the most critical assumption. However, the disposable-bag model carries hidden, recurring costs and inconveniences for the consumer. A cyclone cleaner offers a compelling value proposition: consistently strong suction without the ongoing expense and mess of replacement bags. If this benefit is communicated effectively, the premium price is justified. Consumers regularly pay premiums for demonstrably better, more convenient products.

The proposition leverages a clear technological advantage in a stagnant market dominated by a consumable-revenue model. The risks are substantial but manageable, and the potential upside of disrupting a high-volume consumer goods category is immense. The strategy to manufacture independently and price at a premium is a coherent path to capturing this value.

DECISION: GO
