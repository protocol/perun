The proposal faces serious immediate risks, but the core idea has merit if adjusted.

**Key concerns:**
1. **Quality perception**: Releasing too early could permanently damage the game's reputation
2. **Support burden**: A solo developer will struggle with bug reports, crashes, and player demands
3. **Market saturation**: Without marketing, the game may not reach enough players to sustain development

**However, the proposal has strengths:**
- Early revenue could fund continued development
- Player feedback would guide priorities
- A sandbox building game has natural replayability that could sustain interest
- The "unfinished" nature could be framed as "early access" — a model that would later prove successful for many games

**The critical issue is timing and scope.** Releasing immediately as proposed is risky. The game needs to be "good enough" — playable, stable enough to not frustrate buyers, and with enough content to demonstrate promise. If it's truly rough and unfinished, early buyers will leave negative reviews that could doom the project.

**Recommendation:** The concept is sound, but the execution needs refinement. The developer should:
- Polish the core building loop to be genuinely fun
- Ensure stability for at least a few hours of play
- Set clear expectations about the alpha state
- Consider a limited initial release to gather feedback before wider sale

The proposal as stated — release an unfinished alpha immediately with no publisher or marketing — carries too much risk of damaging the game's long-term prospects.

DECISION: NO-GO
