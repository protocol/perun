The core problem is the mismatch between the proposal's structure and the licensor's actual needs. A small-share computer maker seeking a consumer product cannot rely on a structure that yields negligible per-unit revenue, delegates all customer-facing work to a third party, and provides no meaningful brand reinforcement.

The risks raised are not hypothetical edge cases; they are the central operational realities of this plan. Established console makers dominate the market, meaning the licensee must execute a flawless, highly funded marketing campaign to carve out shelf space and consumer attention. However, because the licensee keeps the product revenue and pays only a small royalty, the incentive structure is misaligned for the licensor's strategic goals. If the licensee markets the product hard, the licensor earns only a small royalty and gains little brand recognition. If the licensee markets the product poorly, the product fails, and the licensor earns nothing. 

Furthermore, the proposal ignores the fundamental value of a consumer product: direct consumer mindshare. By leaving marketing to the licensee and allowing the product to be disconnected from the licensor's brand, the company forfeits the primary strategic benefit of entering the consumer space. A small royalty stream from a low-cost console will not generate enough capital to fund a meaningful brand-building exercise of its own. The company would be expending engineering and platform resources to build a product that primarily enriches the licensee, while the licensor remains a minor, invisible participant in a market dominated by giants.

To justify proceeding, the proposal would need a structure where the licensor's brand is front and center, the revenue model provides sufficient return to fund marketing, or the licensee has a proven, dominant distribution channel in the toy and game market that guarantees consumer reach. As proposed, the economics are too weak, the brand exposure is nonexistent, and the reliance on an unproven licensee in a dominated market presents an unfavorable risk-reward balance.

DECISION: NO-GO
