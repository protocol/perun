The proposal presents an unacceptably high risk profile given the stated objectives and constraints. Launching simultaneously across Europe and America with offices in multiple countries creates immediate and severe cost multiplication in logistics, marketing, and operational overhead. This approach directly contradicts the goal of building the largest retailer in the space, as it front-loads complexity before any market validation has occurred.

The concern that online fashion buying remains unproven is particularly damning in this context. Fashion purchases depend heavily on fit, fabric quality, and visual appeal, factors that are difficult to convey through 1998-era web interfaces. Without evidence that the target demographic of young, fashion-minded buyers will purchase clothing online at meaningful volumes, committing to a multi-country launch risks burning capital on a model that may not work at all in a single market.

The venture capital funding constraint compounds every other risk. If the company must sustain offices, staff, logistics infrastructure, and marketing campaigns across multiple countries simultaneously, the burn rate will be extraordinary. The proposal acknowledges that money may run out before sales catch up, yet offers no mechanism to prevent this outcome. A simultaneous launch eliminates the ability to learn from one market before committing to the next, meaning mistakes are replicated across all regions rather than contained.

A more prudent approach would involve launching in one carefully selected market, proving the core assumptions about online fashion purchasing, refining logistics and marketing systems, and then expanding geographically using validated learnings and revenue traction. This would conserve capital, reduce execution risk, and provide evidence to support future funding rounds at stronger valuations.

The proposal as currently constructed asks investors to fund maximum complexity and maximum uncertainty simultaneously, with no demonstrated proof that the underlying business model functions in even a single market.

DECISION: NO-GO
