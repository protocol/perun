The core opportunity is real and time-sensitive: a massively popular genre, a reusable paid-game asset base, and a clear cosmetic revenue model. However, the proposal as written asks us to commit to a two-month build and a free-to-play pivot while four major risks are explicitly unresolved.

First, the timeline is aggressive for a new mode built on assets not designed for it. Two months leaves almost no room for the technical debt, networking scale issues, and gameplay tuning a competitive last-player-standing title requires.

Second, cannibalization is a serious financial risk. If the free mode draws players from the paid cooperative game without generating sufficient cosmetic revenue, we erode our existing revenue base without a proven replacement.

Third, the studio lacks free-to-play operational experience. Cosmetic economies require live-ops, anti-fraud, ongoing content cadence, and community management capabilities we have not demonstrated.

Fourth, the rival's dominance means we must enter with either a clear differentiator or overwhelming execution quality. A rushed, asset-reused clone risks damaging brand equity and failing to capture market share.

The proposal's strategic instinct is sound, but the execution plan does not adequately address these risks. We should not approve the proposal as currently structured. Instead, we should direct a rapid feasibility assessment focused on: technical viability within the timeline, a cannibalization impact model, partnership or hiring to address the free-to-play capability gap, and a differentiation strategy against the incumbent.

If that assessment returns positive within one week, we can then approve a revised plan with appropriate scope, timeline, and resource allocation.

DECISION: NO-GO
