**First call: NO-GO.** Doubling production into a recession with rising rates and limited cash is a bet that demand will recover faster than cash runs out; the stated facts contradict it.

**Steelman:** If the company can finance a production surge through the downturn, it will emerge with scale, cost advantages, and dealer loyalty that competitors shed during the recession.

**Blockers:**
1. **Cash math fails.** Limited cash + doubling production + rising financing costs + falling demand = the company likely runs out of cash before any demand recovery.
2. **Demand direction contradicts the plan.** Recession + high rates are *actively reducing* demand while the proposal *increases* supply. The unsold inventory grows from 4,500 toward an unsustainable level.
3. **Channel can't absorb it.** Dealers facing the same recession and rates "may not be able to finance more stock" — the proposal doesn't address this.

**Alternatives considered:**
- *Do nothing (hold production):* preserves cash, stops inventory bleed. Cheaper and safer.
- *Cut production to match sales:* frees cash, reduces financing burden. Stronger.
- *Double production:* highest cash burn, highest inventory risk, demand falling.

**Kill criteria (if pursued despite the above):**
- Hard cash floor below which production auto-cuts.
- Dealer financing capacity verified before ramp.
- Monthly sell-through threshold; if it drops further, halt immediately.

**Strongest attack survived:** None — the attacks all land. The proposal's own stated facts (limited cash, falling demand, rising rates, dealer financing risk) form a self-contradiction: every condition named argues against doubling production, and no mechanism is offered for how the company survives the cash burn.

**Remaining risk:** Even a production cut may not save the company if the recession is deep and prolonged; but cutting at least stops the bleeding, while doubling accelerates failure.

**Dissent ledger:** The steelman (scale advantage through recession) is real but requires either deep cash reserves or confirmed demand recovery — neither is in evidence. The proposal gambles on an outcome the stated facts do not support.

DECISION: NO-GO
