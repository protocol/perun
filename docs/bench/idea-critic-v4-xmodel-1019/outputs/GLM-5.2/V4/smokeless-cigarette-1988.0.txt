**First call: NO-GO.** The core economic premise is unproven and the bet is one-way.

**Blocker:** The proposal's own evidence shows the product fails its core test. Smokers "may dislike the taste and the way it is lit" — but taste and lighting are *the entire experience* of smoking. If the taste is wrong, no amount of "cleaner" marketing saves it, and the lighting method is described as complicated versus an ordinary cigarette. The plan's upside rests on switching smokers to a product that, by its own admission, may be inferior on the two dimensions smokers care about most. That is a stated negative the plan does not answer.

**Risks (kill criteria):**
- *Unproven demand.* "Cleaner" is a health-adjacent claim regulators may block and smokers may not value. Kill criterion: test-market switch rate below 15% at 8 weeks → stop.
- *Sunk cost trap.* "Development has already cost a very large sum." This is the classic escalation error: prior spend is irrelevant to the forward decision. The question is whether *incremental* marketing spend beats alternatives.
- *One-way door.* A wide launch with health claims, if regulators later reject those claims, creates liability and brand damage that cannot be unwound.

**Better way:** Do nothing on the wide launch. Run a capped, time-boxed test market (3 cities, 6 months) measuring repeat purchase, not just trial. If repeat rate exceeds 40% and regulators clear the claims, then scale. The sunk development cost does not justify throwing good money after bad on an unproven value proposition with regulatory exposure.

The proposal asks for irreversible commitment on unproven demand with known regulatory risk. That fails the operator test.

DECISION: NO-GO
