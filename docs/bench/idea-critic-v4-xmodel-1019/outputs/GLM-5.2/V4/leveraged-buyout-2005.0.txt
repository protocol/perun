**First call: NO-GO.** The proposal's own numbers make it fragile: heavy acquisition debt serviced by seasonal cash flow leaves no margin for a weak holiday quarter, and the stated risks (investment constraints, competitive pressure) directly undermine the only repayment source.

**Steelman:** A premium buyout monetizes real estate value and removes public-market short-termism, letting management rebuild the franchise without quarterly pressure.

**Strongest attack survived:** None survives. The core claim — that the premium justifies loading seasonal, competitively-pressured cash flows with debt — fails on the proposal's own stated facts. Interest payments crowd out exactly the store and online investment needed to compete. Seasonality means one weak holiday season strains debt service. The proposal names "little room for error" as a feature of the structure; that is the blocker, not a risk to manage.

**Blockers:**
1. **Self-contradictory economics.** The debt service requires stable or growing cash flow, but the proposal itself identifies seasonal volatility, competitive pressure, and investment constraints. The repayment source is the thing the debt undermines.
2. **No margin for the stated downside.** "Little room for error" plus a single weak holiday season equals covenant breach or distressed restructuring. The proposal offers no hedge, cap, or contingency for the scenario it itself names.

**Risks (become kill criteria if pursued):** Seasonal cash flow volatility under fixed debt service; competitive erosion from discount and online sellers; underinvestment in stores and digital forced by interest burden.

**Verdict: HOLD.**

**Dissent ledger:** The premium is real. The financing structure is the problem. A counter-structure — less leverage, equity co-investment from the consortium, or real estate sale-leaseback proceeds funding operations rather than just the purchase price — could preserve the premium while leaving survivable margins. The board should counter on structure, not on price.

**Questions parent must resolve:** Can the consortium deliver the same premium with 40%+ equity instead of heavy debt? Is a sale-leaseback structure feasible that funds operations rather than purchase price?

**Remaining risk:** Even a restructured deal carries competitive and seasonal risk; the kill criterion must be a debt-service-coverage ratio that survives a 20% holiday-season decline.

DECISION: NO-GO
