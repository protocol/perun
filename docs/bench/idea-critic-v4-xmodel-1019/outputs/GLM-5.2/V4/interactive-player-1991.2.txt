First call: NO-GO. The proposal's own risks name the blocker.

**Blockers:**
1. **Category confusion is fatal at $1,000.** The proposal concedes buyers won't understand what the product is for. At a price point roughly 3-5× a game console, "I don't know what this does" is not a risk to manage — it is the product failing. A device positioned *between* two understood categories, that itself defies understanding, has no purchase path.
2. **The PC is the better bet on the proposal's own facts.** PCs are *gaining* multimedia ability (stated as trend, not speculation). A household considering $1,000 for interactive media can instead buy a multimedia PC that also does productivity, education, and communications. The proposal does not answer why a consumer buys a *third* category device when the second one (PC) is converging toward the same capability.
3. **No content, no platform.** A small launch library is acknowledged. An interactive disc platform without interactive discs is hardware without software. The proposal presents no mechanism (developer subsidies, first-party titles, exclusive partnerships) to close the content gap before launch.

**Risks (become kill criteria if revised):**
- Price elasticity untested at $1,000 for a new category.
- Incumbent console makers could cut price in response.
- No stated pilot or geographic test market.

**Strongest alternative:** Do nothing now. Wait 12-18 months to observe (a) whether PC multimedia cannibalizes this space entirely, and (b) whether disc-based interactive content matures. The cost of delay is modest — the living room market is not being captured by a competitor in this window, and the technology curve favors waiting.

**Steelman:** A well-executed player could define a new category the way the Walkman did — a device people didn't know they wanted until they held it. Philips and Sony are backing disc-based media, and first-mover advantage in living-room computing could be enormous.

This steelman fails because the Walkman was *understood instantly* and *priced accessibly*. This proposal inverts both conditions and does not answer the convergence threat from PCs on its own stated facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
