**First call: GO.** A maker of fine movements entering the mass-fashion-watch segment with a low-cost plastic product is a classic diversification play: high volume, high margin, and a new market that does not cannibalise the existing luxury customer.

**Steelman.** The proposal is to create a new category — affordable watches as fashion accessories — leveraging manufacturing cost advantages to achieve volume and margin unattainable in the luxury segment.

**Skeptic.** Reputation risk is the sharpest objection: a watch-movement maker's brand equity rests on precision and prestige; flooding the market with plastic fashion items could dilute that equity. But this attack fails on the proposal's own logic: fashion items are positioned as disposable accessories, not heirloom timepieces. The luxury customer and the fashion customer are different segments. The risk is manageable with brand separation.

**Better-way.** The strongest alternative is a smaller pilot — say three models — to test demand before committing to twelve. This is cheaper and more reversible. But it does not block the proposal: a pilot is a refinement, not a replacement, and the proposal's upside (category creation) scales with collection breadth.

**Kill-criteria.** The pre-mortem is clear: the collection flops because fashion tastes shifted between design and launch, or because established brands cut prices to defend share. The proposal names these risks but does not answer them. The kill criterion is: if first-quarter sell-through falls below 40% of the target run-rate, halt production of the underperforming models and pivot the collection.

**Evidence.** The one-million-unit target is aggressive but not unprecedented for a mass-market fashion accessory. The reputation risk is real but addressable through brand architecture. The competitive response (price cuts by incumbents) is a risk, not a blocker: it confirms the market exists and the entrant has a cost advantage.

The proposal survives its sharpest attack. The risks are named, manageable, and convertible into kill criteria. The upside — creating a new category at the intersection of fashion and affordable timekeeping — justifies the bet.

DECISION: GO
