First call: NO-GO. Premium positioning contradicts higher cost than leather; shoe makers won't pay more for an unproven material.

**Blockers:** The proposal positions the material as *premium* while stating it *costs more than leather*. Leather is the incumbent premium material. A more expensive substitute with no demonstrated superiority to leather cannot command premium pricing. The proposal asks shoe manufacturers to pay more, retool factories, and risk wearer comfort complaints — all for a material whose only stated advantage is that it looks like leather, which leather already does.

**Risks (kill criteria if revised):** Factory retooling resistance, wearer comfort, fashion cycle. None resolved by the proposal. Heavy marketing spend amplifies exposure to every one of these.

**Strongest attack survived:** None — the cost contradiction blocks as proposed.

**Cheaper alternative:** Position as a *mid-market* material (below leather on price) targeting manufacturers who want leather's appearance at lower cost. This reverses the cost disadvantage into the value proposition and requires no premium-quality proof. Alternatively, test with one manufacturer at small scale before heavy spend.

The proposal's own facts defeat it: a material that costs more than leather, positioned as premium, sold to manufacturers who must retool to use it, with no evidence wearers will accept it. Revise to either lower the cost below leather or find a genuine performance advantage that justifies premium pricing — then pilot with one manufacturer before committing heavy marketing spend.

DECISION: NO-GO
