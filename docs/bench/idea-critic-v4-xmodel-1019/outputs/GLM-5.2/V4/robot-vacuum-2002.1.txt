**First call: GO.** Two rejections from incumbents is not market rejection; it is channel rejection. The incumbents' incentive was to protect existing product lines, not to validate demand for a low-cost robot. The proposal's edge is that it does not compete on the dimension incumbents care about (precision mapping) but on a dimension they ignore (affordability and simplicity).

**Steelman:** A cheap, simple robot that cleans *most* of a room most of the time, sold at a fraction of mapping-rival prices, captures consumers who want the novelty and convenience of a robot but cannot justify premium pricing. Random coverage is a feature, not a bug: it is mechanically simple, cheap to manufacture, and "good enough" for routine maintenance cleaning.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Random movement cannot guarantee complete coverage, so the product is fundamentally deficient." This fails because the proposal never claims complete coverage; it claims sufficient coverage at a disruptive price. Consumers comparing a $200 robot that cleans 85% of the floor to a $1,800 robot that cleans 98% are not making a coverage decision — they are making a price-utility decision. The attack also assumes consumers know what "mapping" means; in 2002, they do not.

**Blockers: NONE.**
- No stated fact contradicts the core claim.
- The two incumbent rejections reflect channel conflict, not consumer demand.
- The price gap is large enough that "good enough" coverage is a rational consumer tradeoff.

**Risks (kill criteria):**
- Consumer trust: if return rates exceed ~15% in the first quarter, the product is perceived as broken, not simple. Kill or reposition.
- Coverage perception: if consumer reviews consistently cite missed spots as the primary complaint, random movement is the wrong bet.
- No consumer experience: mitigated by low unit cost and limited initial distribution.

**Decision: GO.** A capped first run (limited units, single channel) with return-rate and review-sentiment kill criteria. The downside is small and reversible; the upside is a new category at a disruptive price.

DECISION: GO
