First call: GO. A fast-growing adjacent market is entering the company's space; standing still cedes it.

Steelman: a separate small team using off-the-shelf parts, an open architecture, outside software, and retail channels can enter the personal computer market in roughly a year, at lower margins but with speed and openness as advantages.

The strongest attack: rivals can copy a design built from standard parts, eroding any advantage. This fails as a blocker. The proposal's own logic is that openness and outside software build a platform and ecosystem, not a hardware moat. Copyability is a risk, not a failure of the plan's core claim.

The risks raised are real but each is a known trade-off, not a blocker:
- Lower margins vs. mainframes: expected and acceptable for market entry.
- Dependence on outside software: the point of the open design.
- No retail experience: mitigated by using independent retail stores rather than building a channel.
- Rivals copying: a competitive risk, not a plan-killer.

No fact stated in the proposal contradicts its core claim. The numbers are not given in detail, but the qualitative case is sound: a small, fast team with standard parts and an open design is a reversible, low-bet move into a growing market.

Kill criteria: set a sales target for year one; if missed, stop. Watch adoption of the open platform as the leading metric.

DECISION: GO
