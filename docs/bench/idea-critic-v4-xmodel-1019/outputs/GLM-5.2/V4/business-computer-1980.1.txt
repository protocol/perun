First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets the company's only revenue on an unproven replacement in a compressed timeline, and the listed risks are not mitigated — they are the failure mode.

**Blockers:**
- **Single point of failure.** The current product is the only successful one. Phasing it out before the successor is proven in the market is a one-way door with no fallback.
- **No migration path.** Existing owners and software makers have no stated incentive or mechanism to transition. The installed base is an asset being discarded, not leveraged.
- **Unproven reliability on a tight schedule.** The proposal concedes complexity and a tight timeline while reliability is unproven. Shipping an unreliable business machine destroys the brand trust the current product built.

**Risks (kill criteria if pursued):**
- Revenue drop from premature phase-out of the current product.
- Software ecosystem fragmentation between old and new machines.
- Schedule slippage making the successor late to market.

**Alternative:** Do not phase out the current product. Build the successor as a **parallel line** targeting business users, with a migration program for software makers. Keep the current product alive until the successor's reliability and market acceptance are demonstrated — then phase out. This preserves revenue, hedges the reliability risk, and still captures the business market.

The proposal's core error is sequencing: it couples "build the successor" (sound) with "phase out the current product" (premature). Decouple them, and the plan becomes defensible.

DECISION: NO-GO
