**First call: NO-GO.** One programmer, five weeks, for a film license that cost a fortune — the math doesn't work and the downside is catastrophic inventory.

**Steelman:** A hit film license guarantees shelf attention and retailer pickup; Christmas timing multiplies that; a single programmer shipping a simple game fast is historically plausible (early 1980s titles were built this way); the large run captures maximum seasonal demand. If the game is even mediocre, the license carries it.

**Attack — the blocker is in the proposal's own numbers:**

1. **One programmer cannot finish in five weeks at quality.** The proposal itself flags that five weeks is "far shorter than normal." A game rushed to meet a date, built by one person, will likely be poor. A bad game under a film license doesn't just undersell — it poisons the brand.
2. **The large production run is a one-way door with asymmetric downside.** If the game sells badly, unsold inventory is a direct loss on top of the already-high license fee. The proposal names this risk itself. Payoff is linear upside; failure is a write-off plus brand damage.
3. **The license fee is sunk but the run is not.** The high fee is already paid — that's actually an argument *against* compounding the bet with a massive manufacturing order before anyone has played the game.

**Alternatives:**
- **Smaller pilot run.** Manufacture a conservative quantity; if sell-through is strong in the first weeks, reprint. Slightly lower ceiling, dramatically lower floor.
- **Two programmers, seven weeks.** Still tight but materially de-risks quality. May miss the very front of the season but catches the bulk of it.
- **Kill the project, eat the license fee.** Better than shipping a bad game at scale and warehousing the unsold copies.

**Kill criteria:** Reorder point at X% sell-through in week 1; if quality review by an external tester rates it below threshold, cut the run 50% before printing.

The proposal as stated commits maximum irreversible spend against a quality risk it already admits, on a timeline it already flags as too short.

DECISION: NO-GO
