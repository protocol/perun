First call: GO. The proposal turns the game's biggest risk—being unfinished—into its core advantage: early revenue, direct player feedback, and a community invested in the game's development. This is a reversible, low-cost experiment with high upside potential.

**Steelman:** Selling early access captures revenue and feedback before the game is polished, leveraging a niche audience that values participating in development over a finished product.

**Alternatives:**
- *Do nothing/finish first:* Safer for reputation, but delays revenue indefinitely and risks building features nobody wants.
- *Free public alpha:* Gathers feedback without revenue, missing the validation that paying customers provide.

**Evidence check:** The proposal assumes buyers exist for unfinished sandbox games. This is unverified but testable immediately. The risks raised (rough state, support burden, cannibalization, no marketing) are real but manageable: the alpha can be capped, priced to signal early-stage status, and marketed through community channels rather than traditional advertising.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it's because the early price tag scared off players who would have tried a free alpha, or the developer got overwhelmed by support requests and stopped updating. Both are addressable: limit audience size, set clear expectations, and prioritize development over support.

**Blockers:** NONE. The numbers are simple—any revenue from an already-built game is upside. The risks are inherent to solo development regardless of release strategy. No alternative achieves the same feedback-and-revenue loop at lower cost.

**Kill criteria:** If sales after the first month are below a sustainable development rate (e.g., fewer than X copies per week covering basic costs), or if player feedback indicates the core sandbox loop is fundamentally unappealing, stop adding features and reassess. Leading metric: weekly sales velocity and player retention. Review date: 30 days post-launch.

The proposal is a small, reversible bet with a clear feedback loop. The risks raised are reasons to cap the pilot, not to block it.

DECISION: GO
