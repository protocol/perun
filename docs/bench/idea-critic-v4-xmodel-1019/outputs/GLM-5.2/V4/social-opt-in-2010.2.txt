First call: NO-GO. The upside is social engagement; the stated mechanism is auto-enrolling users into a network that exposes their connections by default. That is a one-way door: once connections are public, re-privatizing does not undo the exposure. The proposal itself names privacy disregard and regulator attention as risks — the core mechanism creates them.

**Blockers:**
1. **Irreversible exposure by default.** The plan makes connections visible by default and acknowledges this is hard to reverse. A one-way door with no rollback is a blocker, not a risk.
2. **The mechanism creates the named risks.** Auto-enrollment plus default-visible connections is exactly what triggers regulator attention and user trust loss. The proposal states these as consequences of its own design, then proceeds with that design unchanged.

**Risks (become kill criteria if revised):** opt-in enrollment; default-private connections with user-chosen visibility; a capped pilot on a small user segment before full rollout; a kill metric (e.g., if trust-survey scores drop below X or regulator inquiry opens within 30 days, halt and revert).

**Strongest alternative:** Launch the social layer as opt-in with default-private connections. Same product, staged rollout, reversible. This reaches the same upside without the one-way-door exposure.

DECISION: NO-GO
