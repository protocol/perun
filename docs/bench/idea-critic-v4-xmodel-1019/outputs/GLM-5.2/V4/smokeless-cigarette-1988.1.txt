First call: NO-GO. The core claim — "a cleaner way to smoke" — is a health assertion the proposal itself flags regulators may object to, and the product is complicated to use against an incumbent that is simpler, cheaper and already habitual.

**Blockers:** The proposal states as fact that the product is harder to use than an ordinary cigarette, and that taste and lighting may alienate smokers. Against a one-way-door mass launch, those are not risks to manage later; they are the stated mechanism by which the product fails to convert its own target users. No pilot result is offered to override them.

**Risks (kill criteria):** Regulator objection to "cleaner" is a stated risk; the fallback is to drop the claim and compete on novelty alone, which the taste problem may defeat. Sunk development cost is noted but irrelevant to the forward decision.

**Strongest attack survived:** "The large sunk cost means we must launch to recoup it." Rebuttal: sunk cost cannot make a weak product succeed; it only raises the pressure to ship badly. The forward cost of a failed mass launch exceeds the cost of a staged test.

**Alternative:** A capped, time-boxed test market with a pre-committed kill criterion — conversion rate and repeat-purchase rate below a named threshold within a set window — before any mass launch or health-claim marketing.

**Decision:** go-if: launch only after a test market hits a named conversion and repeat-purchase threshold, and only with regulator-cleared claims. As proposed — mass launch with unverified health claims — it does not pass.

DECISION: NO-GO
