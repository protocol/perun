**First call: NO-GO.** The numbers don't work: no marketing budget against incumbents, no developer ecosystem, hardware delays on a crowdfunding timeline, and phone games already eating the casual market.

**Steelman:** A $99 open console could capture the niche between phone casualness and big-console cost — OUYA's actual pitch.

**Blockers:**
1. **Chicken-and-egg unsolved.** The proposal names "developers may not build games" as a risk but provides no answer — no dev kits, no launch titles, no revenue share. A console without games is dead hardware. The plan's own risk list concedes the core problem and doesn't address it.
2. **Crowdfunding ≠ demand validation.** Backers funding a concept are early adopters, not a market. The proposal treats the campaign as proof the public will buy, but there's no evidence the broader audience exists beyond the kickstarter spike.
3. **Ship-date fantasy.** Hardware from a small team with no manufacturing experience, delivered to backers within a year, is contradicted by the proposal's own admission that "hardware delays are common." The timeline is the plan's own evidence against itself.

**Risks (become kill criteria if pursued):**
- Incumbent response: Sony/Microsoft can cut prices or add features overnight.
- No moat: open platform means anyone can copy the hardware.
- Post-campaign sales: the proposal assumes public retail demand exists with zero marketing budget.

**Alternatives considered:**
- *Do nothing:* avoids burning capital on a crowded market with no defensible position.
- *Software-only on existing platforms:* reaches players without hardware risk, but abandons the console vision.
- *License the open OS to an established manufacturer:* lower risk, but depends on finding a partner.

The proposal fails its own test: it lists the risks that would kill the project (no games, no marketing, hardware delays, incumbent competition) and provides no mitigation for any of them. A small team entering a market where Sony, Microsoft, and Nintendo spend billions, with no marketing budget and an unproven developer ecosystem, is betting on hope.

DECISION: NO-GO
