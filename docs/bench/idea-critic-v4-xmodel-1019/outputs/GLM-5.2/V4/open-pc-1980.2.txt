First call: GO. A fast-growing adjacent market is entering the company's territory; standing still cedes it.

Steelman: a small, isolated team using commodity parts, an open architecture and external software can ship a personal computer in ~12 months, reaching a new market the company cannot enter through its integrated mainframe model.

Blockers: NONE. The four risks raised are all standard execution risks for any entrant in an emerging market, not contradictions of the plan's own logic. None shows the numbers fail, the target users don't want it, or the core claim is self-defeating.

Risks: rivals copying the open design (real, but openness also builds the ecosystem); supplier dependence for software (manageable through multiple relationships); lower margins than mainframes (expected for a new category and acceptable if volume grows); no retail experience (mitigated by using independent retail stores rather than building a channel). The pre-mortem: the product ships but fails because the open design let competitors standardize on it while the company couldn't capture enough value — a real failure mode, but one that assumes success at shipping first.

The strongest attack actually tried: **an open design built from standard parts is an invitation for competitors to clone it, so the company would be creating a market it cannot defend — the plan's central design choice is its own biggest threat.** This survived because the proposal's logic doesn't depend on hardware lock-in: the value capture comes from being first, brand, and the ecosystem that openness creates. The risk is real but it is a risk of margin and positioning, not a contradiction that breaks the plan. The kill criterion addresses it: if competitors ship compatible products at lower prices within 18 months and unit sales fall below the break-even threshold, stop or pivot to differentiation.

The cost of delay is high: small-computer makers are already growing fast, and every quarter of waiting lets them build scale, brand and distribution that becomes harder to displace.

Kill criteria: unit sales below break-even by month 18; competitor clones reach 30% market share within 24 months. Leading metric: monthly unit sales, reviewed at month 6.

DECISION: GO
