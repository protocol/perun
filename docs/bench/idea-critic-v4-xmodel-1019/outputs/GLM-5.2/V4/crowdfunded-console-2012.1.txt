First call: NO-GO. The upside (a viable open console) is real, but the proposal's own risk list contains two blockers that the plan does not answer.

**Blockers:**
1. **No software library = no players = no software library.** The proposal itself names "developers may not build games for a new platform" as a risk. This is not a risk; it is the core failure mode. A console without games is dead on arrival. The plan offers no answer: no launch titles, no developer incentives, no porting strategy. The risk the proposal states as already-likely removes its own route to the upside.
2. **No marketing budget against incumbents.** The proposal names this risk too. Established console makers and phone games already compete for the same players. A console nobody has heard of, sold by a team with no marketing capacity, cannot reach the audience needed to sustain a platform. The plan does not address how buyers discover it.

**Risks (become kill criteria if revised):** hardware delays are industry-normal but the one-year ship-to-backers timeline leaves no slack; crowdfunding success itself is unproven here.

**What revision would need:** (a) a concrete answer to the software gap — named launch titles, a developer program, or an existing game engine port; (b) a go-to-market plan that does not assume organic discovery against Sony, Microsoft, Apple and Google; (c) a timeline with slack for hardware delays rather than a fixed one-year promise to backers.

The cheaper alternative — build the open software platform first and ship it on existing hardware (PCs, phones, set-top boxes) — reaches most of the upside (an open game platform with developers and players) at a fraction of the cost and risk, and defers the hardware question until the platform has proven demand. The proposal does not beat "do nothing" or "do the software first" as currently stated.

DECISION: NO-GO
