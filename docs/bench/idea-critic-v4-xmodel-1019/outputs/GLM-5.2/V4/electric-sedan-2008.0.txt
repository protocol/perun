First call: GO. The company's own proposal states a commitment to battery-electric vehicles and a sedan built in a purchased factory. The risks raised are real but are risks, not blockers: none shows the plan's numbers fail, its evidence contradicts its core claim, or a negative already true removes its route to the upside.

Steelman: a new carmaker bets that battery costs fall and charging infrastructure grows; buying an existing factory skips the hardest part of mass production. The strongest attack: battery costs in 2008 are high enough that the sedan's price exceeds what buyers pay, and charging scarcity limits demand. This attack fails on the proposal's own logic: the commitment is to the category, the factory purchase is the mass-production shortcut, and the risks named are the known uncertainties the plan accepts, not facts that defeat it.

Alternatives: hybrid-first reaches some market with less capital but abandons the differentiation of pure electric. Do nothing preserves cash but cedes the category. The proposal beats both on its stated upside.

Blockers: NONE. Risks: capital shortfall, charging scarcity, incumbent response, mass-production inexperience. Kill criteria: battery pack cost per vehicle below a stated threshold by a fixed date, or pivot to a lower-cost platform; factory purchase contingent on capital closure.

The decision rests on the company's own figures and stated commitment. The risks are the known uncertainties the plan accepts.

DECISION: GO
