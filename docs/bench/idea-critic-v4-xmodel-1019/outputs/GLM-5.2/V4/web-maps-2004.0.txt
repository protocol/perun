First call: GO. Desktop mapping was a niche download; a free, browser-based map app with satellite imagery and route planning would suddenly make maps as accessible and instant as a web search — a category-defining product at minimal marginal cost per user.

Steelman: The acquired firm already has mapping expertise and desktop rendering code. By moving to the web and adding satellite imagery plus route planning, the company can leverage its core competency (organizing the world's information and making it universally accessible) to capture a new high-value category. Free + browser-based removes the adoption friction that kept desktop mapping niche.

Alternatives:
- Do nothing: the acquired desktop program continues as-is, serving a small niche. The acquisition delivers no strategic value.
- Keep it desktop-only, add satellite/route features: retains download friction, caps reach.
- Web app but no satellite/route features: a me-too map site against established incumbents.

The proposal beats do nothing because the acquisition cost is sunk; the question is whether to unlock its value. It beats desktop-only because the web removes adoption friction. It beats me-too because satellite imagery and route planning are differentiators incumbents lack.

Pre-mortem: The project fails if (a) licensing costs for map data and satellite imagery exceed the company's willingness to fund a free product, or (b) browsers can't render interactive maps fast enough to be useful. Risk (a) is real but the company has deep pockets and a stated mission of universal access. Risk (b) is the sharpest concern — 2004 browsers were slow at interactive graphics. But the proposal already names this risk, and the fix is engineering investment in efficient tile-based rendering, which is feasible.

Blockers: NONE. The risks are real but all are execution risks, not structural flaws. The proposal correctly identifies them.

Kill criteria: If interactive map rendering can't achieve sub-3-second tile load times in a 2004 browser within 6 months of development, pivot to a lighter-weight interface or defer. Leading metric: user retention at 1 week post-first-use; review at 90 days post-launch.

DECISION: GO
