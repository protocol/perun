First call: NO-GO — the proposal's own kill criterion (most recipients say they would buy) is a survey of intent, not a measure of demand, and the prior test already showed demand is weak.

Steelman: free samples in one city could reveal real adoption (people actually using and re-ordering) better than a four-city launch that relied on shelf placement alone.

Blockers:
- The stated evidence contradicts the core claim. A four-city test already showed disappointing sales. The proposal's response is to ask office workers whether they *would* buy — a stated-intent survey. Stated intent overstates real demand by 2–3x (a well-documented bias). The kill criterion is therefore mismeasured: passing it does not establish that a national rollout will sell.
- The numbers do not work as proposed. Free samples cost money with no purchase guarantee; a national rollout needs a large marketing budget. The proposal states no cap on sample cost, no conversion assumption from sample to paid order, and no budget gate before national commitment. It is an uncapped spend against an unverified demand signal.

Risks (become kill criteria if the plan is revised):
- No prior experience re-launching a failed product under a new name.
- One city is a thin sample; office-worker adoption may not generalize to retail consumers.
- Reversibility: a national rollout is a one-way door once marketing spend is committed.

Better way: replace the intent survey with a **paid reorder test**. Give free samples to offices in one city, then measure the share that places a *paid* reorder within 30 days. Set a numeric threshold (e.g., 20% reorder rate) as the kill criterion before any national rollout. Cap sample and test-marketing spend at a fixed amount. This costs less than a national rollout, is reversible, and tests the claim that matters — willingness to pay repeatedly — not willingness to say yes.

Dissent ledger: the idea of re-testing demand rather than abandoning the product is sound; the specific kill criterion (stated intent) is the flaw.

DECISION: NO-GO
