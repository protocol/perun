**First call: GO.** The mission is adoption, not hardware sales; $35 makes classroom sets possible where laptops cannot, and the charity model removes the margin pressure that would otherwise kill the price.

**Steelman:** A $35, hackable, Linux board — bought and owned by the student — is the cheapest credible path to hands-on programming at scale; the charity structure means no investor demanding margin on volume that may never come.

**Better-way:** Laptops and tablets already exist, but they are consumption devices at 10× the price; a school can buy ten of these for one laptop, and the board is *open* — students see the OS, not a sealed app store. "Do nothing" leaves the charity's mission unfulfilled.

**Skeptic:** The four risks are real but none is a blocker. The "bring your own peripherals" model mirrors how the hobbyist market already works; a small first batch selling out is *evidence of demand*, not failure — and it costs little because the batch is small. No manufacturing experience is a governance gap, not a fatal flaw: licensed distributors and contract manufacturing are how small hardware orgs ship. Cheap laptops are a category competitor, not a substitute for a $35, open, programmable board.

**Kill criteria:** If the first batch does *not* sell through within, say, six months, the demand hypothesis is dead and the charity should stop. If defect rates exceed what the distributors can RMA, the support model is broken and must be redesigned or abandoned. If a licensed distributor cannot actually fulfil at the $35 price point, the proposition is false.

**Pre-mortem:** The most likely failure is *not* hardware — it is that schools buy the board, cannot get it working without peripherals and support, and it lands in a drawer. The mitigation is the same charity structure: a community, documentation and teaching materials alongside the board.

The upside (a generation of students who can program) dwarfs the downside (a small batch of unsold $35 boards). The risks are the plan; they do not block it.

DECISION: GO
