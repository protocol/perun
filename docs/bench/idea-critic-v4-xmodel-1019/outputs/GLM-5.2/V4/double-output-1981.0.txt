First call: NO-GO. Doubling production into a recession, with unsold inventory already at 4,500 units and growing, burns cash faster while demand is falling.

The steelman — that confidence and supply create their own demand, and that being seen as a going concern attracts buyers and dealers — fails on the proposal's own numbers. If 4,500 cars are already unsold at the current production rate, doubling output means the unsold stock grows roughly twice as fast. Cash drains sooner, financing costs rise with interest rates, and dealers carrying stock they cannot move will discount heavily, undermining pricing for everyone.

The better way is obvious and cheaper: hold or cut production to match actual sell-through (3,000 sold, not 7,500 produced), preserve cash, and let the existing inventory clear. Doing nothing about the production-sales gap is already the problem; doubling production amplifies it.

Blocker: the proposal's own facts show production (7,500) already exceeds sales (3,000) by 2.5×, and the response is to widen that gap further while the macro environment worsens. The plan does not address how the additional output gets sold when the current output does not. That is a self-contradiction, not a risk.

Kill criteria if pursued anyway: cap the production increase at a number the balance sheet can survive for 90 days; set a sell-through threshold (e.g., monthly retail sales must rise to match the new output within 60 days) below which production reverts; and require dealer financing capacity to be confirmed before the first additional unit ships.

The strongest attack survived: none. No specific failure scenario was needed because the proposal contradicts its own stated facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
