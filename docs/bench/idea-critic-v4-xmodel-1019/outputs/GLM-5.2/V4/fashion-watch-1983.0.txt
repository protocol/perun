First call: GO. Twelve models, fashion positioning, one-million target — the cheapest reversible test of a new category, and the downside is capped.

Steelman: a reputation for quality is earned by what customers experience, not by the materials used in products they never buy. A plastic fashion watch at accessible volume opens a market the premium line cannot reach, and the premium line is not damaged by a product sold under a different expectation.

Strongest attack survived: "cheap plastic damages the reputation for quality." This fails on the proposal's own facts. The maker is known for movements, not for fashion accessibility. A plastic fashion line does not compete with the prestige collection; it competes for customers who were never buying a prestige watch. The reputation risk runs the other way: a movement maker entering fashion signals range, and a failed fashion line can be withdrawn without touching the core.

Alternatives considered: do nothing (preserves the movement reputation but cedes the fashion-accessible segment to incumbents — a one-way door closing); smaller pilot of three models (cheaper test but loses retailer commitment and fashion-season timing, which is the whole advantage of twelve models at launch).

Blockers: NONE. The stated risks are real but they are kill criteria, not blockers. The reputation risk is managed by positioning and by the withdrawal option. The aggressive sales target is a target, not a commitment; it sets the ambition level for the launch. Fashion-taste volatility and incumbent price cuts are competitive risks inherent to any fashion launch.

Kill criteria: if first-year sales fall below 400,000 units (40% of target), withdraw the line or reposition. Leading metric: sell-through rate at retail within the first 90 days; review at month four. The one-million target is the right anchor for ambition — a lower target would have made the launch too small to matter.

DECISION: GO
