First call: GO. The studio's short-film profits can self-fund a bounded first feature; the upside is a new category and the downside is a capped loss.

**Blockers:** NONE. The budget is large but finite and self-funded from existing earnings. No fact stated shows the plan cannot work as proposed.

**Risks:** Audience tolerance for feature-length animation is unproven. Budget overruns are likely given the scale. The studio has no prior feature experience. Family and industry insiders advise against it.

**Kill criteria:** If the feature does not recover its production cost within 18 months of release, stop pursuing features and return to shorts. Leading metric to watch: per-theater engagement in the first two weeks of limited release; review at week three.

**Strongest attack survived:** Audiences have never sat through a feature-length cartoon, so the core assumption — that people will pay feature prices for animation — is untested, and the studio is betting roughly ten shorts' worth of budget on it. This fails to block because the proposal is self-funded from existing profits, the loss is bounded and absorbable, and the upside is a wholly new revenue category with no incumbent. The risk is real but it is a kill criterion, not a blocker.

DECISION: GO
