**First call: GO.** Carmaker has a new plant that must run near capacity to pay off; an 1100 cc compact uses that plant, targets the segment the maker already knows (small cars, home market), and the dedicated dealer network is the route to margin that protects against the low-price rivals.

**Steelman:** A carmaker with small-car experience and a new plant to fill launches a compact family car (1100 cc) through a dedicated dealer network, capturing home-market buyers trading up from cheap small cars.

**Alternatives considered:** Do nothing — plant sits under capacity, payoff at risk. Export existing small cars — depends on foreign demand and trade terms not stated. Build a larger car — moves the maker out of its known segment.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks (costly dealer network, low-price rivals, plant capacity, buyer preference) are risks, not blockers — none shows the plan's own numbers failing or its core claim contradicted.

**Risks / kill criteria:**
- Plant utilization below break-even by month 12 → cut output or renegotiate terms.
- Dedicated dealer network build cost exceeds budget by month 6 → cap spend, use existing dealers.
- Rivals undercut price and compact loses share → review pricing position by month 9.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Rivals already sell small cars at low prices, so a new entrant cannot win." This fails because the maker already sells small cars in this market — it has the cost base, brand, and buyer knowledge to compete, and the dedicated dealer network is the differentiation lever, not a me-too entry.

**Assumptions:** Home-market demand for compact family cars exists at the proposed price point (stated as the maker's existing segment). New plant can produce the 1100 cc model (stated: built at the new plant).

**Remaining risk:** The dealer network build is the longest-lead, highest-cost item; if it slips, the plant fills but the sales channel doesn't.

DECISION: GO
