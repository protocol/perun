First call: NO-GO — simultaneous two-continent launch front-loads burn against unproven demand and a thin runway.

Steelman: be the default fashion retailer for young online buyers across the West, capturing brand and scale before incumbents move.

Blockers:
- Burn-vs-runway: simultaneous multi-country launch multiplies logistics and marketing costs while venture funding is finite. The proposal's own risk list concedes the money may run out before sales catch up — the plan as stated fails on its own numbers.
- Unproven category × unproven geography: online fashion buying is unproven (stated), yet the plan launches into several countries at once, maximizing exposure to the very risk it names.

Risks (become kill criteria): demand unproven; logistics complexity across borders; brand-building cost in multiple markets simultaneously.

Better way: launch in one country first (the cheapest path). Prove conversion, repeat purchase, and per-customer logistics cost. Expand to the second country only after a named metric clears a threshold. This reaches the same upside at a fraction of the initial burn and preserves runway for the learning loop.

Kill criteria:
- Stop / pivot if: single-country pilot shows repeat-purchase rate below X% by month 6, OR customer acquisition cost exceeds lifetime value by month 9.
- Leading metric: contribution margin per order, reviewed at month 6.

The proposal's core claim — capture young fashion buyers online at scale across the West — is not wrong as an ambition. It is wrong as a first step. Simultaneous multi-country launch is a one-way door on runway: if online fashion demand is lower than assumed (and the proposal admits it is unproven), the company has spent the money to learn that in five countries instead of one. The cheaper, reversible path — one country, prove the model, then expand — reaches the same destination without betting the entire runway on unproven demand in unproven geographies simultaneously.

DECISION: NO-GO
