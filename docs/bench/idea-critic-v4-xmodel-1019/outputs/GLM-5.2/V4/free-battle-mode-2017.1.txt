First call: GO. Genre ownership is not a monopoly, reuse cuts the build, and a separate title protects the paid game.

Steelman: a studio with a shipped engine and art pipeline can enter a proven, fast-growing genre at a fraction of the rival's cost, and a separate free title converts mode risk into an experiment that cannot directly erode the paid game's sales.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's own facts show a workable plan: existing assets collapse the timeline, the separate-title structure isolates cannibalization, and cosmetic monetization is already proven in the genre.

**Risks** (kill criteria, not blockers):
- *Cannibalization is deferred, not eliminated.* Separate title limits direct erosion, but brand overlap could still pull paid players. Kill criterion: if paid-game daily active users drop more than 15% in the first 30 days post-launch, merge or sunset.
- *Two months is aggressive.* Reuse helps, but netcode for 100 players and a cosmetic store are not trivial. Kill criterion: if a closed 50-player stress test is not passing by week 5, slip the date rather than ship broken.
- *No F2P experience.* Cosmetic economies need tuning the studio has never done. Kill criterion: if average revenue per daily active user is below $0.03 by day 60, bring in external F2P expertise before doubling down.
- *Rival dominance.* Entering against an incumbent is hard but not disqualifying—many genres support multiple winners, and the rival's success proves demand.

**Strongest attack survived:** "A studio with zero free-to-play experience cannot, in two months, build and operate a live-service economy competitive with an entrenched rival." This fails because the proposal does not require beating the rival—it requires testing whether the studio's existing audience will engage with a last-player-standing mode. The separate-title structure makes this a bounded, reversible bet: if it fails, the paid game is intact and the experiment is shelved.

**Leading metric:** day-7 retention above 25%, reviewed at day 14. Below that, the mode lacks product-market fit and further investment stops.

DECISION: GO
