First call: NO-GO. The stated economics — small per-unit royalty, licensee owns marketing, licensor's brand disconnected — remove the licensor's own route to the upside. The proposal doesn't answer how a small-share computer maker earns meaningful revenue when every lever except the platform license sits in someone else's hands.

Blockers: (1) The proposal's own evidence shows the target market is dominated by established console makers, yet the royalty is described as "small per unit." The numbers don't work: small royalty × small share of a contested market = negligible revenue against any platform licensing cost. (2) Marketing sits with a licensee whose incentive to push hard is unproven — the proposal states this risk as already true and doesn't answer it. (3) Brand disconnection means even successful unit sales don't build the licensor's franchise; the strategic upside is severed from the financial one.

Risks (kill criteria if revised): no minimum sales floor or marketing spend commitment from the licensee; no cap on the licensor's exposure; no date by which unit velocity must clear a threshold or the license terminates.

Strongest attack survived: none — the core claim fails on its own stated facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
