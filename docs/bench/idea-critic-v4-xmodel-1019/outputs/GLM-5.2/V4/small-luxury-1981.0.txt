First call: GO. The facts as stated show the buyers are already there and already American-brand customers; the bet is whether a shared platform with added trim holds a premium. That is a bet worth making if it is capped and time-boxed.

**Steelman:** The strongest version of the claim is that the compact platform's cost structure lets the brand offer a smaller luxury sedan at a premium margin, capturing American-brand buyers who want downsizing without leaving the brand family. The buyers are already self-selecting; the platform is already engineered; the trim premium is the upside.

**Skeptic — assumptions:**
- "Added trim and features" will carry a premium price. **Test:** cost out the trim package against the price gap; if the margin is thin, the platform sharing is the whole game and the risk of buyers seeing through it becomes the blocker.
- American-brand buyers will pay a premium for a smaller sedan. **Test:** the proposal's own buyer data shows they are coming from American brands, not European luxury — which means their price expectations may be anchored lower, not higher.

**Better-way:**
- Do nothing: the buyers are already arriving. But the proposal's premise is that they are arriving *despite* not finding the right product; if so, do-nothing cedes them to rivals' smaller sedans.
- Cheaper alternative: sell the parent's compact under the main brand with minimal trim. This reaches the same buyers at lower risk to prestige — but it sacrifices the premium margin that is the proposal's core upside.

**Kill-criteria:**
- Salespeople refuse to call it by the main brand → the distribution channel is the blocker, not the product.
- Buyers see through the shared platform and the premium collapses → the trim premium was the assumption to test.
- The main brand's prestige suffers measurably → name the metric (e.g., average transaction price of the core line) and the review date.

**Pre-mortem:** It failed because the brand treated a shared platform as a luxury product rather than as a cost structure — and the buyers, who were already American-brand customers, saw the same thing the brand refused to.

DECISION: GO
