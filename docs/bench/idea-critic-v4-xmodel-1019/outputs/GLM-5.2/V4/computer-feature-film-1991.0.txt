First call: GO. A studio just tried to hire your lead animator and is now offering a production deal instead — that is the market telling you your team's value is real and rising. The strategic upside (establishing computer animation as a viable feature medium and the company as its pioneer) outweighs the risks, which are all execution problems, not fundamental flaws.

Blockers: NONE. The risks raised are genuine but not disqualifying:
- "No one has made an all-CG feature" — this is the opportunity, not the blocker. Being first is the whole point.
- "Never produced anything longer than a short" — a capability gap, addressable through the studio partnership and staffing.
- "Rendering is slow and costly" — a technical and budgeting challenge, not a reason to decline.
- "Studio controls distribution" — standard for a publisher model; the company retains creative IP and the medium-defining reputation.

Risks (kill criteria):
- Budget overrun from unproven rendering pipeline at feature scale.
- Creative control erosion given the power asymmetry.
- Timeline slippage if the technology doesn't scale.

The strongest attack: the power asymmetry means the studio captures most of the upside while the small company bears most of the risk — this could be a deal where success still breaks the company. This survives as a risk but not a blocker: the reputation and capability gained from shipping the first all-CG feature is a strategic asset the studio cannot take, and it positions the company for future deals on better terms. The alternative (decline and continue making shorts and commercials) is safer but cedes the defining moment in the medium to someone else.

DECISION: GO
