First call: GO. Plastic, fashion-led, high-volume is a classic two-way door: cheap to make, cheap to kill, reversible if it flops. The downside (reputation) is manageable if branded separately. The upside (new market, volume) is real.

Steelman: an inexpensive plastic watch sold as fashion, not engineering, opens a new consumer segment the company cannot reach at its traditional price point — and does so with low unit cost and fast inventory turns.

Blockers: NONE. The numbers are aggressive but not self-contradictory. No stated fact shows the plan fails as proposed.

Risks (become kill criteria):
- **Reputation contamination.** Mitigate: separate brand, not the movement-maker's name.
- **Fashion cycle.** Kill criterion: if sell-through drops below 50% after two seasons, cut SKUs to four and pivot to classics.
- **Incumbent price cuts.** The cost advantage of plastic should survive a price war; verify unit margin holds if competitors cut 20%.

Strongest attack survived: "Established brands will cut prices and crush this." This fails because plastic construction gives a structural cost floor the incumbents — with metal movements and premium positioning — cannot match without cannibalising their own lines. Swatch's later success confirmed this.

The proposal is sound. The reputation risk is the sharpest objection, and it is answered by a separate brand. The sales target is aggressive but not impossible for a fashion item with twelve SKUs. The fashion-cycle risk is real but manageable with tight inventory and a kill criterion.

DECISION: GO
