**First call: NO-GO.** The premium positioning is contradicted by the proposal's own facts: the synthetic costs more than leather, yet leather is the incumbent the market already accepts. Charging a premium over an already-pricier alternative, against established factory processes, asks manufacturers to pay more *and* retool. That is the blocker.

**Blockers:**
- The proposal states the synthetic costs more than leather while recommending a premium launch. The economics invert: shoe makers are asked to pay a higher material cost *and* bear switching costs, with no margin story stated.
- Premium positioning requires a consumer-perceived advantage the proposal does not name. "Looks like leather" is table stakes against leather itself.

**Risks (kill criteria to add):**
- Factory unwillingness — solvable with pilot partnerships, not heavy marketing spend.
- Comfort deficit — untested against real wear; needs wear trials before premium claims.
- Fashion shift — durable materials outlast trend cycles; this cuts both ways.

**Strongest alternative:** Launch as a *performance* or *utility* material at parity pricing, targeting manufacturers making non-premium shoes where durability and water resistance beat leather. This sidesteps the premium-cost contradiction, lowers switching risk, and builds a proven case before any premium push.

**Steelman:** A durable, leather-look synthetic launched as premium could capture margin if wearers value durability and consistency over natural material variation. Heavy marketing to manufacturers could create pull-through demand.

**Attack that survived:** The cost-premium contradiction. The proposal's own stated fact — higher cost than leather — combined with premium positioning means every unit sold fights an uphill margin battle before factory switching costs are even considered. The alternative (parity-priced utility launch) reaches the same long-term upside without the contradiction.

The core claim fails on its own numbers. Heavy marketing spend amplifies a position the economics do not support.

DECISION: NO-GO
