First call: GO — the proposal is to build electric cars, and the company has a design for one.

Steelman: a young carmaker bets that battery-electric drivetrains are the inevitable future and that buying an existing factory leapfrogs the decades of manufacturing learning it lacks.

**Blockers: NONE.** The risks raised — capital scarcity, charging infrastructure, incumbent response, inexperience with mass production — are real but are *risks*, not *blockers*. None is stated as already-fatal; each is a known uncertainty the plan must navigate. The proposal does not contradict itself, and no fact in hand shows the numbers fail.

**Skeptical checks:**
- *Capital gap:* the proposal acknowledges the factory and battery packs need "far more capital than the company has." This is the sharpest attack. But acknowledging it is not resolving it — the plan must name the funding path (IPO, strategic investors, government loans) or the capital gap kills execution. This is a **kill criterion**, not a blocker: secure staged funding by a date, or stop.
- *Charging scarcity:* a market-formation risk, not a product risk. It caps adoption speed but does not invalidate the bet that electric drivetrains win.
- *Incumbent response:* incumbents moving *validates* the thesis. The risk is they move faster — a competitive risk, not a plan-killer.
- *Mass-production inexperience:* the factory purchase partially addresses this (tooling, layout, workforce), but operating it is unproven. Kill criterion: first production run to spec by a date, or pivot to contract manufacturing.

**Better way considered:** hybrid or niche-first (sports car only) paths defer the capital need but also cap the upside and delay the learning curve on mass production. The full commitment is the stronger bet if the capital path holds.

**Pre-mortem:** the company runs out of money before reaching volume production, because the factory's fixed costs drain capital faster than investor patience allows. This is the single most likely failure mode.

**Kill criteria:** (1) Series funding round closed at or above the factory-purchase threshold within 18 months, else stop. (2) First production-intent sedan built to spec within 36 months, else pivot. Leading metric: capital raised against burn rate; review quarterly.

DECISION: GO
