**First call: GO.** The stated upside (become the defining BEV maker) is real and the move is two-way-door enough at this stage; the risks are the plan's kill criteria, not blockers.

**Steelman.** A new entrant cannot out-build Toyota on ICE, but a BEV-only bet captures a category before incumbents move, and a bought factory removes the hardest learning curve.

**Alternatives.** *Do nothing* keeps you a niche designer, not a carmaker. *Hybrid line* splits capital and dilutes the brand. *Contract manufacturing* is cheaper but cedes the factory learning. The proposal beats do-nothing; the hybrid path is the strongest rival and is rejected for brand and focus reasons.

**Evidence check.** The proposal states capital need "far more than the company has" — that is the proposer's own fact, and it is the one that must be settled before commitment. It does not say capital is unobtainable, only that the gap exists. Charging scarcity and incumbent response are risks the plan must answer, not facts that already defeat it.

**Pre-mortem.** The factory runs below break-even for years while capital runs out; or battery cost never falls fast enough to hit sedan price. Both are plausible; both are exactly what a capped, staged first step addresses.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact stated in the proposal already shows the plan fails as proposed. The capital gap is unresolved, not blocking — it is the first gate to clear.

**Risks / kill criteria:**
- Capital: raise the full factory + pack figure before breaking ground; if the round closes below [stated need], stop.
- Battery cost: set a $/kWh threshold at pilot; below it, proceed; above, pivot to smaller platform.
- Demand: first-pilot deposits must exceed a named number by a named date, or halt factory tooling.
- Incumbent response: monitor for a sub-$40k BEV from a top-5 maker; if shipped before our sedan, reassess.

**Leading metric:** factory-line ramp rate vs. plan, reviewed monthly from pilot start.

DECISION: GO
