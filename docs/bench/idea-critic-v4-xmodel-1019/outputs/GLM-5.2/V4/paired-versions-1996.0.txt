First call: GO. The mechanic is the product.

**Claim:** Ship two versions with different creatures so link-cable trading becomes required to complete the collection.

**Steelman:** The dual-version split turns a single-player game into a social phenomenon. Trading creates a reason to talk about, show off, and connect over the game — free, viral distribution that no marketing budget could buy. The link cable already exists; this design gives it purpose.

**Alternatives:**
- *One version, all creatures:* Safer, but removes the social hook entirely. A completeable solo game on a declining platform.
- *One version, trading still possible:* Loses the scarcity that makes trading meaningful.

**Pre-mortem:** It fails if kids see two boxes and feel nickel-and-dimed, or if the social density isn't there — not enough kids with cables and nearby friends. The declining handheld is the real threat: if the installed base collapses, the social mechanic has no network to spread through.

**Evidence check:** The risks raised are real but mostly addressable. The "price grab" concern is mitigated by clear communication that each version is a complete game. The trading requirement is a feature, not a bug — it's the entire engagement loop. The publisher's uncertainty is the weakest link: if they don't commit marketing support, the social mechanic never reaches critical mass.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact stated contradicts the core claim. The declining platform is a risk, not a blocker — and the social mechanic is the best available answer to platform decline, creating word-of-mouth that extends the hardware's life.

**Risks:** Platform decline could prevent network effects. Price-grab perception possible. Publisher commitment uncertain.

**Kill criteria:** If first-month sales of Version A fall below 50% of the publisher's prior platform-average launch, the dual-version strategy is failing and should be reassessed. Leading metric: trade volume per unit sold at 30 days.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Two versions on a declining platform splits an already-shrinking audience, making it even harder to find a trading partner." This fails because the split *is* the reason to find one — scarcity creates the social pressure that drives engagement and word-of-mouth. The mechanic works precisely because not everyone has the same creatures.

DECISION: GO
