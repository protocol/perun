First call: NO-GO. The proposal's own facts defeat it.

**Blockers:**
1. **Value proposition contradicted by stated risks.** The core claim — "cleaner way to smoke" — depends on smokers accepting reduced smoke. The proposal itself flags taste dislike and lighting difficulty. If the target user rejects the product experience, the health-claim angle is moot: you are marketing a harder-to-use cigarette that smokers don't enjoy to non-smokers who won't start. The stated upsides and stated risks point at different audiences, and neither is validated.

2. **Health claims are the load-bearing wall, and it is already cracking.** Strip the "cleaner" framing and this is a premium, complicated cigarette with no differentiated benefit. But the proposal names regulatory objection to health claims as a risk — meaning the single claim the product needs is the one most likely to be blocked. A product whose only competitive advantage may be legally prohibited before launch is not ready to launch.

**Kill criteria (if revised):**
- Smoker acceptance rate in test markets below a defined threshold → do not scale.
- Regulatory ruling against reduced-harm claims in any major market → pull health-claim messaging.
- Sunk cost must be excluded from the go/no-go decision; its presence in the proposal's own risk list signals it is distorting judgment.

**Remaining risk:** Promoting widely before test-market results are in commits to a spend level the evidence does not yet support. The development cost is sunk; it cannot be recovered by launching faster, only by launching better.

DECISION: NO-GO
