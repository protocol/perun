First call: GO. Desktop mapping required download and install; a browser-native version removes the friction barrier that kept casual users away. Satellite imagery is a visual differentiator no incumbent offered. Route planning captures intent. The acquired firm's codebase gives a head start.

**Steelman:** A free, browser-based map with satellite imagery and route planning collapses the acquisition-to-user distance, makes the product shareable via URL, and creates a surface for future monetization (local advertising, search integration) that desktop software cannot match.

**Skeptic — assumptions:**
- "No revenue model" assumes revenue must be direct. Search advertising already monetizes attention; a map captures local intent at higher CPMs than generic search. **Next action:** estimate local-search ad revenue per map user against imagery licensing cost per user.
- "Browsers are slow at graphics" assumes the current state is fixed. AJAX techniques (Google Suggest, Gmail) already demonstrate responsive browser interaction. Tile-based image loading sidesteps the rendering bottleneck. **Next action:** prototype tile loading in a 2004 browser and measure pan/zoom latency.

**Better-way:**
- Do nothing: the acquired firm's desktop product has a ceiling — download friction, no virality, no ad surface. Staying desktop-bound wastes the acquisition.
- Cheaper alternative: ship the desktop product under the search company's brand. Lower cost, but misses the browser-native distribution advantage that makes the acquisition worth its price.

**Kill-criteria:**
- If imagery licensing cost per active user exceeds projected ad revenue per active user by 2x within 18 months of launch, renegotiate licensing or restrict imagery to zoom levels where cost is manageable.
- If median pan/zoom latency in the top three browsers exceeds 500ms in the pilot, delay launch until tile prefetching brings it under threshold.
- Leading metric: weekly active users at 90 days post-launch, with review at that point against licensing cost per user.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, users try it once, but interactive performance is poor enough that they return to incumbents. Mitigation: pilot with a small tile set and the two dominant browsers before public launch.

The strongest objection — no revenue model — fails because the proposal is not a revenue bet; it is a distribution and attention bet that the search company's existing ad infrastructure can monetize once users arrive.

DECISION: GO
