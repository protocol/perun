First call: GO. The facts state a real market (established makers, phone games competing) with real, named risks (no marketing budget, hardware delays, developer adoption uncertain) — exactly the conditions where a capped, time-boxed first step is the right operator move, not a blocker.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's own numbers are internally consistent: low-cost hardware, crowdfunding-funded (no upfront capital risk to the company), one-year ship window. The risks cited are execution risks, not contradictions in the plan's logic. "Hardware delays are common" is a reason to plan for delay, not to reject the concept. "Developers may not build games" is a market-adoption risk that becomes a kill criterion, not a no-go reason.

**Risks (→ kill criteria):**
1. Developer adoption: if fewer than N indie developers commit titles within 90 days of dev-kit distribution, pivot to a curated app store model or stop.
2. Hardware ship slip: if functional hardware is not demoed by month 8, the one-year backer promise is at risk — trigger scope reduction or timeline reset.
3. Crowdfunding shortfall: if the campaign fails to reach its minimum funding target, the project does not proceed (crowdfunding platforms enforce this automatically).

**Cheaper alternative considered:** shipping a software-only platform on existing hardware (PCs, phones) first to validate developer interest before committing to hardware manufacturing. This is a sound *de-risking first step* but does not achieve the stated goal of a dedicated home console — it changes the product, not just the path.

**Pre-mortem:** the most likely failure is not hardware or funding — it is shipping a console with no games, because developers waited to see adoption before committing. The kill criterion on developer sign-ups catches this early.

The proposal is small in capital exposure (crowdfunding absorbs the risk), reversible if the campaign fails to fund, and addresses a genuine gap. The risks are real but named, and each converts to a measurable tripwire.

DECISION: GO
