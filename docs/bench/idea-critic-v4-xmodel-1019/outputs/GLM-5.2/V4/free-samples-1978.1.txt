**First call: NO-GO** — the proposal's own evidence (disappointing sales in four cities) contradicts its core claim (that this product has demand), and the plan does not answer that contradiction.

**Steelman:** A re-positionable adhesive note is a genuinely useful office product; a fresh name and free samples could overcome the awareness problem that doomed the first test.

**Attack:** The first test already measured demand and found it wanting. The proposal's response is to *rename* the product and *give it away* — but renaming addresses a branding problem the company hasn't diagnosed, and free-sample recipients saying "I'd buy it" is a notoriously unreliable predictor of actual purchase behavior. The proposal conflates *stated intent* with *demonstrated demand*. The four-city test was the real pilot; it failed. This plan adds cost (free samples, national marketing budget) without adding evidence that the first test's failure was a fixable problem rather than a fundamental lack of need.

**Alternatives:**
- *Do nothing:* zero additional cost; company stops bleeding on a weak product.
- *Targeted paid pilot:* sell (don't give) the renamed product in a single office-building cluster; measure repeat purchases, not stated intent. Cheaper, more honest signal.
- *Diagnose before re-launch:* interview the four-city test buyers who *did* purchase; find the wedge use-case before spending national-rollout money.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches nationally, marketing spend is committed, and office workers still don't see a need — because "re-positionable adhesive note" solves a problem most people don't experience acutely enough to change habits. The first test already told us this.

**Blocker:** The proposal's own stated fact — disappointing sales in the first test — is the negative already shown that removes the route to upside, and the plan does not answer *why* the renamed, free-sampled version would succeed where the original failed. Stated intent to buy is not a kill criterion; actual repeat purchase at a real price would be.

DECISION: NO-GO
