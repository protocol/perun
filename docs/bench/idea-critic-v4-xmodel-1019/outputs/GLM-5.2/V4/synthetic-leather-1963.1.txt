First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a premium position with a material that costs more than leather, in an industry it admits is change-averse, against comfort and fashion risks it raises itself.

**Blockers:** NONE that fail the plan on stated facts alone — but the economics are the near-blocker. Premium positioning requires premium margins. A material costing more than leather, sold to manufacturers who must retool, compresses margins at every step. No stated margin math survives the cost gap.

**Risks (becoming kill criteria):** Factory retooling cost and resistance. Material cost premium vs. leather. Wearer comfort unproven. Fashion-cycle exposure. All four are the proposal's own listed risks, none answered.

**Strongest attack survived:** "It costs more than leather and shoe makers won't retool" — this nearly kills the premium-play, but the proposal could target segments where durability commands a premium (work boots, military) where leather cost is already high and retooling is incremental. It does not name that segment, so the attack stands unresolved against the premium shoe-upper claim as stated.

**Cheaper alternative:** License the material to existing leather-goods manufacturers rather than building a shoe-upper marketing program. Lower capital, faster signal on whether wearers accept it.

**Kill criteria if pursued:** Secure one manufacturer pilot within 6 months; wearer comfort rated ≥ parity with leather in a 500-person wear test; unit cost within 15% of leather at pilot scale. Miss any: stop the marketing spend and reassess.

The proposal asks for heavy marketing spend on a premium play whose own stated risks — cost, retooling resistance, unproven comfort, fashion exposure — are unaddressed. The cost gap alone makes premium margins implausible without a segment pivot the proposal does not name.

DECISION: NO-GO
