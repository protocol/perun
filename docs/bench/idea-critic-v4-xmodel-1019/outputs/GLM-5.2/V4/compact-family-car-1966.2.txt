First call: GO. A new plant sitting idle is a cost with no revenue; an 1100 cc compact is the direct way to fill it and to move the maker up from small cars into the family segment where margins are thicker.

Blockers: NONE. The risks raised are all execution risks the proposal can answer, not facts already in hand that defeat the plan.

Risks: the dealer network is costly to build — but a dedicated channel is the point, not a flaw; it protects the new car's positioning from being dragged down by the small-car image. Rivals sell small cars cheaply — but this is an 1100 cc family car, a different segment with different price expectations. The plant must run near capacity to pay off — true, and that is exactly why launching the car matters: an empty new plant is worse than a partially filled one. Buyers may prefer cheaper or larger cars — they may; that is a demand risk, not a reason not to try.

Strongest attack survived: rivals already occupy the small-car segment at low prices, so a new entrant faces a price war it cannot win. This fails because the proposal targets the family-car segment with a larger engine, not the subcompact segment where the rivals compete. The attack hits a different car than the one proposed.

Better way considered: build the new car but sell it through the existing dealer network rather than a dedicated one. This saves the network cost but sacrifices the positioning benefit and risks cannibalising small-car sales. The dedicated network is more expensive but more defensible; the trade-off is reasonable.

Kill criteria: if the new car fails to reach a defined unit threshold within the first full sales year, stop, cut losses, and either reprice or exit. The leading metric to watch is monthly dealer orders against a break-even plant-utilisation figure, reviewed at the six-month mark.

The proposal is a sound bet: a new plant needs a product, the family segment offers better margins than small cars, and the dedicated network protects positioning. The risks are real but manageable, and the alternative of leaving the plant idle is worse.

DECISION: GO
