**First call: NO-GO.** Doubling production into a recession with rising rates and a growing unsold pile is a bet that demand will absorb the surge. The proposal's own numbers say it won't.

**Steelman:** Flood the market to gain share, fill the dealer network, and emerge from recession as the volume leader competitors can't match.

**Better way:** Do nothing — hold or cut production to match the 3,000 sold. This clears cash drain without the inventory gamble. Cheaper, safer, reversible.

**Skeptic — assumptions that don't hold:**
1. *Demand will absorb doubled output.* Sold: 3,000. Unsold: 4,500. Doubling production adds cars to a pile already 1.5× annual sales. No demand evidence is cited.
2. *Dealers can finance more stock.* Raised as a risk; no evidence it's resolved. If dealers can't take stock, the extra production sits at the factory — cash burned, no revenue.
3. *The company has enough cash to survive the ramp.* Limited cash is stated. Doubling output means doubling material and labor costs *before* any sale. The proposal doesn't address how cash lasts through the gap.

**Kill criteria (what would reverse this):**
- Dealer financing capacity confirmed at doubled volume.
- Cash runway modeled through the production-to-sale gap.
- Demand evidence (waitlist, orders, market share data) showing the market absorbs more than current 3,000/year sales.

None of these are in the proposal. The core claim — double production — fails on its own stated facts: unsold inventory is already large and growing, demand is falling, cash is limited, and dealer financing is unconfirmed. That's a blocker, not a risk to manage.

**Dissent ledger:** The bet is that recession + high rates = competitors retreating, leaving room for a volume play. Plausible as strategy, fatal as timing — the company would burn its limited cash before the market turns.

DECISION: NO-GO
