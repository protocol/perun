**First call: GO.** Core mechanism is named (feed share with notification + decline), reversibility is the real risk, and the proposal's own scope is a pilot-grade test of ad monetization.

**Steelman:** Explicit, notifiable, declinable purchase-sharing is a less invasive form of behavioral ad targeting than silent tracking — it gives users control the baseline lacks.

**Skeptic — assumptions:**
- "Users will value the control" assumes the notification is seen *before* the share, not after. The proposal says "notification step," not "opt-in before broadcast." That gap is the sharpest attack: post-hoc notification of a sensitive purchase is the invasion, not its absence.
- "Earn more from advertising" assumes advertisers pay more for social purchase signals than for existing interest targeting. Unverified.

**Better-way:**
- *Do nothing* preserves trust; the proposal must beat it on net revenue, not gross.
- Cheaper alternative: opt-in sharing (ask once, share many) reaches the same upside with lower blast radius. The proposal's notification model is weaker than this and not cheaper.

**Kill-criteria:**
- One-way door acknowledged ("hard to withdraw"). That demands a capped, time-boxed first step with a pre-committed stop.
- Kill metric: if opt-out rate exceeds X% or negative-feed sentiment exceeds Y within the first window, stop and pivot to fully opt-in.
- The "hard to withdraw" risk is partially mitigable: launch to a small cohort, default-off, with a kill switch.

**Blockers:** NONE. The notification-vs-opt-in gap is a real flaw but addressable by revision, not fatal to the core claim.

**Risks (→ kill criteria):** sensitive-purchase embarrassment (needs category exclusion list), partner-site reputational exposure (needs per-partner opt-out), one-way-door irreversibility (needs cohort + kill switch).

**Strongest attack survived:** The proposal's "notification step" language is ambiguous between pre-share opt-in and post-share notice — and if it's the latter, the feature *is* the privacy invasion users fear, not a controlled version of it. This didn't kill the proposal because the fix (require explicit opt-in before first share) is a revision, not a redesign, and the monetization claim survives under either model.

The notification ambiguity must be resolved before launch: opt-in-before-broadcast, not notice-after.

DECISION: NO-GO
