**Steelman:** Reusing paid-game assets to ship a free-to-play last-player-standing mode fast is the cheapest way to test a proven, exploding market before rivals consolidate it.

**Skeptic:**
- "Two months" is asserted, not evidenced. New mode + separate title + cosmetic store + F2P backend in eight weeks is the sharpest risk; the rival had more time and resources.
- "Earn money from cosmetic purchases" is untested for this studio. Cosmetics monetize when players care about identity; a reused-asset mode may lack that pull.
- Cannibalization is dismissed, not priced. If 20% of paid players migrate to the free title, the paid revenue loss may exceed early cosmetic income.

**Better-way:**
- Ship the mode *inside* the paid game as a beta. One title, one backend, no separate-launch risk, direct measurement of cannibalization before full commit. Costs less, reverses more easily.
- Status quo: keep selling the paid cooperative game. Safe, but cedes a market growing fast enough to define a genre by 2017.

**Kill criteria (go-if):**
- **Door:** two-way if built as an in-game beta; one-way if launched as a separate F2P title with its own store.
- **Cap:** smallest playable slice on existing infrastructure within four weeks; measure concurrent players and paid-game retention against pre-launch baseline.
- **Stop / pivot:** if paid-game daily active users drop >15% or the slice cannot sustain >1,000 concurrent players by week six, pull the mode or fold it into the paid title.

**Strongest attack survived:** The studio has never run F2P and the timeline is aggressive, so execution risk is high. It survives only if the first step is a capped, reversible beta rather than a separate-title launch.

The proposal as written—separate title, two months, untested monetization—carries one-way-door risk the evidence does not justify. Revise to the in-game beta path above, then go.

DECISION: NO-GO
