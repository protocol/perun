**Steelman:** Selling early captures revenue and signal now, while the game is still cheap to change; the rough state is itself a filter for buyers who want to shape the game, not judge a finished one.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Players who see the unfinished game will stop buying, permanently capping the ceiling." This fails on the stated facts: the game is *playable*, so early buyers get value now, not a promise; and a sandbox building game is the genre that historically benefits most from player-built momentum (word-of-mouth, mods, viral constructions) — exactly the channel that needs no marketing budget. The rough-state risk is real but bounded: the buyer who would have loved the finished game but rejects the alpha is a later-recoverable loss; the revenue and feedback forgone by waiting is unrecoverable. The proposal already names every risk the team raised; none is a hidden defect.

**Remaining risk:** The solo-developer support ceiling is the sharpest unaddressed concern — but it is an operational scaling problem surfaced *by* early revenue, not a reason to forgo that revenue. The kill signal is observable: if support load or refund rate exceeds what one person can clear, throttle new sales (a reversible door), not abandon the model.
