**Steelman:** A $6.6B premium lets shareholders cash out at peak valuation while private equity restructures the company away from quarterly market pressure.

**Strongest attack survived:** Seasonal cash flow plus heavy secured debt means a single weak holiday season could trigger covenant breach, restructuring, or bankruptcy — the margin for error is nearly zero, and none of the proposal's terms address this.

**Assumptions:**
- The premium exceeds the company's standalone value over the deal horizon.
- New owners will invest in stores and online rather than asset-strip.
- Holiday revenue will cover debt service in a stressed scenario.

**Better ways:**
- Negotiate a lower debt-to-EBITDA ratio or longer payment terms.
- Require minimum capital expenditure commitments from the consortium.
- Seek a strategic buyer (e.g., a larger retailer) whose synergies reduce reliance on leverage.

**Kill criteria:**
- Debt service exceeding 40% of average quarterly EBITDA before seasonal peak.
- No binding floor on store and online investment over three years.
- Holiday revenue falling below 90% of prior year, triggering covenant breach.

**Remaining risk:** Even with protections, the structure loads operational fragility onto financial fragility. A leveraged buyout at a premium price in a competitive, seasonal market leaves little capacity to absorb the exact shocks the business naturally faces.

DECISION: NO-GO
