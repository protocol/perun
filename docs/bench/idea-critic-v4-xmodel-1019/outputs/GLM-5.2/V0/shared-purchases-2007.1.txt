**Steelman:** Monetizing a social network by surfacing members' partner-site purchases and activity in friends' feeds, with notice and a decline option, captures high-value behavioral data advertisers will pay a premium for.

**Skeptic:** The "notification step" and "option to decline" are presented as privacy safeguards but their mechanics are unspecified. If notification is passive (a line in a settings page) rather than active (an interstitial requiring informed consent before first broadcast), the safeguard is theatrical. The proposal does not state which it is. **Assumption:** notification is passive. **Check:** specify the exact UX; if a user can be broadcast before they see the notice, the safeguard fails.

**Better-way:** The strongest cheaper alternative is **opt-in**: users explicitly enroll before any purchase is shared. This sacrifices early reach but eliminates the "invasion of privacy" risk for non-enrollees and makes withdrawal trivial (disable the opt-in flag). The proposal's opt-out design means every user is enrolled by default and must notice and act — the exact pattern that triggers public backlash.

**Kill-criteria:** The proposal names withdrawal difficulty as a risk but provides no tripwire. A concrete kill criterion: if negative-press volume or opt-out rate exceeds a threshold within 14 days of launch, disable new broadcasts within 24 hours and initiate rollback. The proposal does not name this threshold, the review date, or the rollback owner.

**Pre-mortem:** The feature launched. Six weeks later a major news outlet runs "Social Network Shares Your Purchases With Friends" after a user's sensitive purchase (contraceptives, mental-health books, gifts) is broadcast to family and coworkers. Advertisers pull spend. Partners demand exit. The decline option existed but too few knew to use it. Withdrawal is politically and technically costly.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Users will see this as an invasion of privacy" — the proposal acknowledges this risk but does not specify whether the notification step constitutes informed, pre-broadcast consent. Without that specification the privacy safeguard is unverified, and the irreversibility of launch makes an unverified safeguard unacceptable.

**Remaining risk:** Sensitive-purchase exposure is not addressed by a decline option if the broadcast precedes the decline.

DECISION: NO-GO
