verdict: REVISE

claim: Build a business successor with lowercase and 80 columns within the year, phasing out the current product.
steelman: The current machine's installed base and software library are its real assets; a successor that preserves compatibility while adding business features could capture a new segment without abandoning the old one.
hats_run: skeptic, better-way, kill-criteria
assumptions:
- Phasing out the only successful product before the successor is proven reliable is treated as low-risk; it is not. Revenue from the current product funds the new one; killing it early creates a cash cliff if the new machine slips.
- "Within the year" is asserted without a design-freeze date, fabrication lead time, or software-porting estimate. Complex designs under tight schedules slip; the schedule has no slack shown.
better_ways:
- Cheapest path: add an 80-column card and a lowercase ROM to the existing machine as a business bundle. No new platform risk, no revenue gap, and it tests real business-user demand before a full successor.
- Bridge path: ship the successor as a parallel product line, keep the current machine in production, and only phase it out after the successor hits a unit-revenue milestone for two consecutive quarters.
kill_criteria:
- If the successor does not pass a 30-day field-reliability burn-in by a named date before launch, hold the launch; do not phase out the current product.
- If business-user pre-orders for the successor are below a stated threshold by a set date, treat demand as unproven and revisit.
strongest_attack_survived: The tight schedule plus unproven reliability plus early phase-out of the sole revenue source is a compound one-way-door risk: one slip means no revenue from either product. It survives only if the phase-out is gated on the successor's proven reliability and revenue, not on a calendar date.
questions_parent_must_resolve:
- What is the fabrication and software-porting lead time, and does "within the year" survive it?
- What unit-revenue or pre-order threshold proves the successor before the current product is retired?
user_question: NONE
dissent_ledger: Revenue-cliff risk from early phase-out unaddressed; schedule slack not shown; reliability burn-in not named as a launch gate.
remaining_risk: Launching and retiring on a fixed date, before reliability and demand are proven, could leave the company with no successful product.

DECISION: NO-GO
