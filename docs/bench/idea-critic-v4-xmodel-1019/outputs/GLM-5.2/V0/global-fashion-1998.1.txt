**Steelman:** Simultaneous US+Europe launch creates a first-mover position across both markets, building brand scale that competitors cannot match, funded by VC to capture the land-grab window.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Multi-country launch multiplies logistics and marketing costs, burning venture capital before sales catch up" — this is a real risk but the proposal already raised it; the question is whether it is mitigated, and the proposal contains no mitigation, no phased rollout, no cost cap.

**Skeptic findings:**
- **Unproven demand + maximum complexity simultaneously.** Online fashion buying is unproven (stated). Launching into multiple countries with offices in several countries at the same time compounds that uncertainty with logistics, localization, and marketing complexity. No evidence is offered that demand exists at scale or that the operational footprint is achievable in the first window.
- **Venture capital runway is unquantified.** "May run out before sales catch up" is raised but not addressed. No burn rate, no runway estimate, no milestone-gated funding is presented.
- **No kill criteria.** The proposal does not state what metric, at what threshold, by what date, would trigger a pivot or stop.

**Better way:** Launch in ONE region first (the one with highest density of fashion-minded young buyers and simplest logistics). Prove conversion, average order value, and repeat-purchase rate. Use that data to raise the next round and expand. Cost: a fraction of simultaneous launch. Risk: a competitor enters the second market first — acceptable, since an unproven model failed in one market is cheaper than failed in five.

**Remaining risk:** A well-funded competitor establishes a multi-market position during the single-market pilot. This is a real cost of delay, but it is cheaper than burning out across five markets simultaneously with an unproven model and no kill criteria.

DECISION: NO-GO
