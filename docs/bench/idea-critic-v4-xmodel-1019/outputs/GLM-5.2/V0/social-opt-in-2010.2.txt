**Steelman:** Auto-enrolling a massive existing user base into a social layer is the cheapest possible path to network effects; the "do nothing" alternative forgoes that advantage.

**Skeptic:** The proposal conflates distribution (we have users) with desire (users want social inside inbox). No evidence is cited that users asked for this. The four named risks are all one-way-door failures: once connections are exposed, the harm is irreversible and public.

**Better-way:** Opt-in enrollment with private-by-default settings captures willing users without forcing exposure on the unconsenting majority. This is strictly cheaper in risk-adjusted terms: it preserves reversibility and removes the regulator-attention risk almost entirely.

**Kill-criteria:** The proposal names risks but sets no kill criteria — no metric threshold, no date, no stop condition. Without a pre-committed tripwire, a failed launch drifts rather than reverses.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Auto-enrollment is the only way to reach network density quickly." This fails because the proposal itself admits the launch is hard to reverse and may draw regulators — the very speed advantage is what makes the downside uncontainable. Opt-in is slower but two-way-door.

**Remaining risk:** Even opt-in carries reputational spillover if the visible-connections default remains; the privacy default must also be private-by-default.

**Dissent ledger:** The core mechanism (how connections form, what "visible to others" means precisely) is unspecified; a core mechanism left unnamed is unresolved.

DECISION: NO-GO
