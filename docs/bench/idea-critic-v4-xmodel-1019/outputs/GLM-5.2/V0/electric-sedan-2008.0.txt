The strongest case for this plan is that a pure-electric bet, made early and at scale, could capture a defensible position before incumbents move. That is worth taking seriously.

But the plan as stated asks us to commit the whole company to a capital-intensive factory build and proprietary battery packs **before** we have proven we can manufacture a single car at scale. "Little experience with mass production" is not a peripheral risk here; it is the core risk, and the proposal does not separate *learning to build cars* from *betting the company on one powertrain*. Those should be staged, not bundled.

The charging-station scarcity is real but secondary: early adopters will tolerate home charging, and infrastructure follows demand. The incumbent-response risk is also secondary: incumbents are slow precisely because they are optimizing an existing gasoline value chain. The binding constraint is capital versus manufacturing learning curve, and the proposal leaves that unresolved.

A cheaper, reversible first step exists: build a small number of the sedan on a pilot line — in a leased corner of an existing factory, not a purchased one — using bought cells, and sell them at a loss to the earliest buyers. That tests the two things we do not know (can we produce at acceptable quality; will anyone pay for a full-size electric sedan) for a fraction of the capital, and it preserves the option to buy the big factory once the pilot proves demand and process. The proposal's "commit to only battery-electric and buy the large factory now" skips that test.

Because a staged pilot dominates the all-in commitment on cost, risk, and reversibility, and the proposal does not address why we should skip that test, it is not approvable as written.

DECISION: NO-GO
