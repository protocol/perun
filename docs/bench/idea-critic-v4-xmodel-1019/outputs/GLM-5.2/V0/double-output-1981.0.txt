**Steelman:** Doubling production could unlock scale economies that lower unit cost below the price needed to clear inventory, making both the stock and new output profitable if demand recovers.

**Attack:** The proposal asks a cash-constrained company to accelerate the *one* thing that is already bleeding it — inventory build — into a recession with rising rates. Every named risk (unsold stock, recession, financing costs, limited cash, dealer financing) is an argument that the gap between production and sales will widen, not narrow. No evidence is offered that demand will recover, that unit-cost savings from higher volume exceed the carrying cost of a larger unsold pile, or that dealers can absorb more stock. Doubling production is a one-way bet on a demand rebound that the cited macro signals contradict.

**Alternatives:**
1. **Do nothing** (hold current production): cheaper, reversible, stops the bleed from growing.
2. **Cut production to match sales** (≈300/month if the 3,000 sold are evenly spread, or whatever the run-rate is): preserves cash, lets existing stock clear.
3. **Hold production flat and discount to clear stock**: tests price elasticity without committing more capital to inventory.

All three are cheaper and more reversible than doubling output into a demand collapse.

**Pre-mortem:** The company doubles production. Recession deepens, rates stay high. Unsold inventory doubles again. Cash runs out before any scale savings materialise. Dealers refuse the extra stock. The firm enters bankruptcy with a parking lot full of cars it could not sell. **Door type:** one-way — the cash spent building unsold inventory is not recoverable. **Cost of delay:** low; waiting one month to verify the sales trend costs little compared with committing scarce cash to doubling output.

**Kill criteria absent:** The proposal states no metric threshold at which to reverse (e.g., "if monthly sales fall below X, cut production to Y"), no date for review, and no pivot action. This is a bet with no stop-loss.

**Strongest attack survived:** None. No counter-evidence is offered to the recession/rates/cash constraints that make doubling production rational only under an unstated recovery assumption.

DECISION: NO-GO
