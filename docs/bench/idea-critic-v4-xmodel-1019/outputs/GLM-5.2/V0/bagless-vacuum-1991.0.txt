The steelman: a differentiated product with a clear performance edge can command a premium and bypass incumbent retail by selling direct.

**Skeptic.** "Buyers won't pay extra" is asserted, not shown. The premise that matters — does bagless cleaning perform visibly better? — is the whole bet. No performance evidence is cited, so the premium-price claim is unsupported.

**Better-way.** Licensing the patent to an incumbent with manufacturing and distribution is cheaper, faster, and lower-risk than founding a company. The proposal never names why building beats licensing, nor why direct-to-consumer beats retail. The default should be: license first, build only if license terms fail.

**Kill-criteria.** No stop condition is given. Before any capital is committed, set: (1) a capped pilot — sell N units direct at the target price within 90 days; (2) a kill metric — if fewer than N/2 sell at full price, stop; (3) a leading metric — repeat-purchase or referral rate, reviewed at day 60.

**Pre-mortem.** The company burns its capital because the premium price suppresses demand, incumbents launch look-alikes at mid-price within two quarters, and the founder is left with inventory and no retail access. Reversibility: capital spent on tooling and inventory is largely unrecoverable — this is a one-way door.

**Decision.** The core claim — that buyers will pay a premium for bagless — is the bet, and it is stated as a risk, not tested. Approve only a capped, time-boxed pilot with pre-committed kill criteria, not a full company launch. The cheaper path (licensing) must be rejected with a stated reason before building.

DECISION: NO-GO
