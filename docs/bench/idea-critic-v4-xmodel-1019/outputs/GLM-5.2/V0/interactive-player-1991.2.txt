**Steelman:** A first-mover disc platform at $1,000 captures the living-room multimedia category before PCs or consoles close the gap; software attach funds the hardware subsidy later.

**Strongest attack survived:** "At $1,000, against $200 consoles and ever-cheaper multimedia PCs, the box is unbuyable and undefinable." It fails only if the title library is large at launch — which the proposal concedes it will not be.

**Assumptions (unverified, fatal in combination):**
1. Buyers will pay console×5 for a category no one has named ("interactive multimedia" is not a consumer phrase in 1991).
2. A small launch library is survivable. Consoles die on thin libraries; this costs five times more.
3. PCs will *not* close the multimedia gap inside the 18-month window the platform needs. The proposal asserts the risk ("PCs are gaining multimedia ability") yet banks on it not mattering.

**Better ways:**
- **Do nothing now, ship at $400 in 18 months.** Same discs, same living room, console-adjacent price, a real library. Cost of delay: a competitor defines the category — but the category is undefined *today*, so the loss is small.
- **Launch as a PC peripheral (disc drive + codec card) at $300.** Piggybacks on the installed PC base, avoids the "what is it" question, funds the standalone later.

**Kill criteria the proposal lacks:**
- A named attach-rate floor below which the platform is killed (e.g., <2 titles per box by month 6 → stop).
- A price-drop trigger tied to a sell-through number by a date.

**Remaining risk:** Even revised, the core bet — that consumers want a third living-room box for "interactive multimedia" — is untested and the proposal offers no pilot to test it.

DECISION: NO-GO
