**Steelman:** A hit feature redefines the studio from a commodity short-film supplier into the genre-defining originator, commanding pricing power and distribution leverage no short can deliver.

**Do-nothing:** The studio keeps grinding thin-margin shorts indefinitely—a stable but strategically stagnant path that cedes the feature category to whoever moves first.

**Cheaper alternative:** Co-finance with a distributor to cap downside. This halves the budget risk but also halves the upside and surrenders creative control—exactly the leverage the proposal seeks.

**Pre-mortem:** The feature bombs because audiences associate animation with seven-minute novelty, not ninety-minute immersion. The studio burns its shorts cushion and faces insolvency within eighteen months.

**Door class:** One-way. The capital is sunk; a failed feature threatens the studio's survival, not just a quarter.

**Fit:** The north star is escaping thin-margin commodity output. This proposal is the most direct attack on that constraint.

**Kill criteria:** Greenlight pre-production only. Full production greenlights when a test reel screens to a sample audience and at least 60% indicate they would watch a feature-length version. If the reel cannot secure distribution interest within six months, stop.

**Strongest attack survived:** Audience rejection of feature-length animation is the sharpest objection—it is untested, the budget is unrecoverable, and the downside is existential. It fails because the objection is an assumption stated as fact: no market test, preview screening, or distribution inquiry is cited to establish that audiences *will* reject it. The proposal's asymmetry is real: the studio risks its cushion, but the upside is category-defining pricing power and a moat no competitor can quickly replicate. The family and industry insiders are pattern-matching against a paradigm that does not yet exist; their objection confirms the bet is contrarian, not that it is wrong. A capped, test-gated pre-production phase converts the existential one-way door into a reversible first step with a named stop condition.

DECISION: GO
