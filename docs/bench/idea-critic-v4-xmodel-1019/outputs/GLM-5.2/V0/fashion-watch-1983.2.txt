**Steelman:** A cheap plastic fashion watch opens a new market the prestige line never touches, so reputation risk is bounded by segmentation.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Established brands cut prices and crush the launch." It fails because the fashion angle competes on novelty and trend, not on movement precision or craftsmanship — the prestige buyer's purchase criteria are untouched.

**Skeptic — assumptions:**
1. One-year demand for *twelve* models at 1M units assumes broad fashion acceptance with no evidence. **Next action:** cut to a 3–4 model pilot, 200–300k units, and measure sell-through in the first quarter.
2. "Harms reputation" is asserted, not tested. **Next action:** run a small consumer panel showing the plastic line alongside the prestige line and measure perceived-quality shift.

**Better-way:**
- *Pilot first.* Launch a limited capsule collection in select markets before committing to twelve models and one million units. Cheaper to reverse, still captures learning.
- *Do nothing* leaves the low-cost fashion segment entirely to competitors.

**Kill-criteria:**
- **Tripwire:** if first-quarter sell-through across the pilot models is below 50% of shipped units, halt expansion and reduce the collection.
- **Leading metric:** retail sell-through rate per model, reviewed at week 6 and week 12.
- **Reversibility:** a limited pilot is two-way door; a full twelve-model launch at 1M units is nearly one-way given tooling and marketing commitments.

**Decision:** go-if — proceed only as a capped, time-boxed pilot (3–4 models, select markets, ~250k units, 90-day review with the sell-through tripwire above), not as the full twelve-model / one-million-unit launch proposed.
