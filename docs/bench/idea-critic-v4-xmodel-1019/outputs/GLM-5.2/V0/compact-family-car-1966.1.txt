**Steelman:** A dedicated compact at the new plant captures unmet family-car demand and fills capacity, paying off the plant.

**Skeptic:** The proposal bundles three independent bets — new product, new dealer network, new-plant utilization — into one irreversible commitment. "Rivals already sell small cars at low prices" means the car enters a price war it likely cannot win, since the new plant's break-even requires near-capacity volume. No evidence is given that family-car demand is unmet at the proposed price point. The 1100 cc engine sits in a dead zone: too small for families wanting space, too premium-priced for economy buyers. "Buyers may prefer a cheaper or a larger car" is not a risk to mitigate — it is the core demand hypothesis, and it is unstated as such.

**Better-way:** *Do nothing* keeps the carmaker in its profitable small-car niche and avoids the dealer-network sunk cost. *Cheaper path:* contract an existing dealer network rather than building a dedicated one, decoupling the distribution risk from the product risk. *Alternative product:* a larger car at the same plant, differentiating from rivals rather than competing head-on on price.

**Kill-criteria:** None stated. No capacity threshold, no price floor, no volume target at month 6 or 12. Without a pre-committed stop, the plant's break-even pressure will push the carmaker to keep discounting into a loss spiral.

**Strongest attack survived:** The plant is already built, so utilization is a real pressure — but that is a sunk-cost argument, not a demand argument, and sunk costs do not create buyers.

**Remaining risk:** Three coupled bets, no kill criteria, price-war entry, ambiguous segment positioning.

DECISION: NO-GO
