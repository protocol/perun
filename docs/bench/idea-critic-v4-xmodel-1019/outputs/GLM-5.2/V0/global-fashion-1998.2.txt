**Strongest defensible version:** Simultaneous US+Europe launch maximizes first-mover advantage in a nascent online fashion market, captures brand-mindshare before competitors, and the venture funding runway (unspecified) covers the burn.

**Strongest attack tried:** The proposal bets everything on simultaneous multi-country execution when online fashion buying itself is unproven in 1998. If the core assumption — that young buyers will purchase fashion online — fails, it fails in every country at once, having burned capital across multiple markets. The risks raised (logistics multiplication, launch delay, runway exhaustion) are all *amplified* by simultaneity, not mitigated by it. No kill criterion is stated: there is no revenue threshold or date at which the founders stop and reassess.

**Cheaper alternative:** Launch in one country (or one region) first. Prove the unproven assumption — online fashion buying — at a fraction of the cost, then expand geographically once the model works. This preserves capital, generates real learning, and does not forfeit the larger vision.

**Why the attack survives:** The proposal concedes online fashion buying is unproven yet commits to the most expensive possible test of it. A single-market pilot directly de-risks the core hypothesis before multiplying it.

**Questions to resolve:** What is the funding runway, and at what revenue level or date does the team stop and reassess?

**Dissent ledger:** Simultaneity has genuine strategic logic (first mover, brand capture). But the proposal names no kill criterion and tests an unproven model at maximum cost simultaneously across markets.

**Remaining risk:** Burn rate across multiple countries outpaces venture runway before the unproven online-fashion-buying assumption is validated.

DECISION: NO-GO
