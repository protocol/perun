**Strongest version of the claim:** A studio with proven craft and steady cash flow can absorb one outsized bet to open a new market—feature animation—that competitors have not entered.

**Skeptic.** The load-bearing assumption is "audiences will sit through 70+ minutes of animation." No cited evidence supports it. The budget being ten times a short means a single overrun or soft opening threatens solvency, not just quarterly earnings. Insiders and the founder's family opposing it is treated as noise, but their objection maps to the same untested audience-tolerance question.

**Better-way.** "Do nothing" keeps the studio solvent on its existing model. A cheaper middle path exists: a two-reel or hour-length feature at roughly three to four times short cost, proving audience tolerance before committing ten-times scale. The proposal leaps past the de-risking step.

**Kill-criteria.** No stop condition is named. At minimum the studio needs a defined overrun cap beyond which production halts, and a release-window metric (per-theater opening) below which distribution is cut. Without those, this is an uncapped, irreversible spend.

**The sharpest objection actually tried:** the feature fails because no one has shown adults will pay feature prices for animation, and the ten-times budget sinks the studio. It survives *only if* a smaller pilot or a hard cap exists—but the proposal names neither.

**Remaining risk:** even with a cap, the studio is betting its cash reserves on an unproven format; the downside is insolvency, not a missed quarter.

DECISION: NO-GO
