**Steelman:** Targeted purchase-sharing in the feed could materially lift ad relevance and partner spend, because friends' real buying signals are stronger predictors than interest-based proxies.

**Strongest attack survived:** The "hard to withdraw" risk is the sharpest objection — once friends, partners and the press have seen purchase-sharing in the feed, walking it back reads as an admission of fault, not a product refinement. It survives only partially: the notification-and-decline step is a friction layer, not a reversal path. A feature that is hard to withdraw demands a kill criterion set *before* launch, and the proposal names none.

**Assumptions**
- "Earn more" assumes the incremental ad revenue exceeds churn, PR cost and partner-embarrassment exposure. No figure is given.
- The notification step assumes users will read and understand it before the first sensitive purchase surfaces publicly. Behavioral evidence says most won't.

**Better ways**
- **Opt-in, not opt-out-with-notification.** Same data, same revenue logic, but consent precedes exposure. Cuts the "invasion of privacy" charge at the root.
- **Category blocklist before launch.** Health, financial, adult and gift purchases never enter the sharing pipeline. Removes the single most predictable PR trigger.
- **Limited partner pilot, 30 days.** Ship to one partner site, measure opt-decline rate and negative-feedback volume, pre-commit to pull if either crosses threshold. Satisfies "earn more" while bounding blast radius.

**Kill criteria (missing — must be set)**
- Decline rate ≥ X% within 14 days → pull.
- Negative-feedback / complaint volume ≥ Y per 10k exposures → pull.
- Pre-named rollback owner and a public "we heard you" exit path, agreed before launch, not improvised after.

**Remaining risk:** Even an opt-in pilot creates a partner-expectations commitment; the rollback path must be contractually permitted, not just technically possible.

The proposal's core idea is defensible, but as written it launches an irreversible, privacy-sensitive feature with no numeric kill criteria, no category blocklist, and consent framed as notification-plus-decline rather than affirmative opt-in.

DECISION: NO-GO
