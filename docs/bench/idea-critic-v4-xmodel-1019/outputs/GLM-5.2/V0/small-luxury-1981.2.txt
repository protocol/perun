Steelman: the compact captures conquest buyers already leaving US brands for smaller sedans, using shared-platform economics to hit a premium price the parent could never charge alone.

Strongest attack survived: "buyers see through the shared platform and the premium collapses." It failed because the proposal's own finding reframes the buyer — these are mass-market customers trading up, not European-luxury loyalists. The shared platform is a feature (proven, serviceable, cheaper) against that audience, not the liability it would be against the parent's core base.

Remaining risk: salespeople not using the model name, and long-run prestige dilution, are real but second-order to the conquest opportunity and reversible through incentive design and, if needed, discontinuation.

DECISION: GO
