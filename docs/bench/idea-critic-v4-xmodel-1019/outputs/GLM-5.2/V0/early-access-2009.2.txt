**Steelman:** An early paid alpha turns early adopters into both revenue and a feedback loop before the market for sandbox games saturates — the roughness is the pitch, not a defect, if the right players find it.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Players who see the rough state will stop buying, poisoning the long tail." This fails against the proposal's own logic: the target isn't the mass market (no publisher, no marketing budget) but the niche that already tolerates unfinished games and self-selects into the alpha. Word-of-mouth among that niche compounds; the mass market never sees it. The attack describes a different product with a different audience.

**Better ways considered:**
- Free alpha, paid beta: lower immediate revenue, slower signal.
- Finish then release: no revenue until done, no feedback loop, solo timeline risk.
- The proposal is the cheapest path to both cash and signal.

**Kill criteria:** Set a price that filters serious players (not free, not full retail). Watch weekly sales and refund rate. If sales drop below a sustainable floor or refund rate exceeds ~20% after the first month, pull the alpha and return to closed development. Review weekly for the first month, monthly after.

**Leading metric:** Weekly paid conversions from the alpha page, reviewed at day 30.

**Remaining risk:** The solo developer's support load is the real constraint — one person cannot read every email, fix every bug, and add features simultaneously. The alpha price and a clear "buy at your own risk" framing must manage that load, or burnout kills the project before the game does.

DECISION: GO
