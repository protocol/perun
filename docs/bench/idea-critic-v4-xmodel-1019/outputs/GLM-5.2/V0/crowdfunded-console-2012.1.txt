**Steelman:** Crowdfunding lets a tiny team validate demand and recruit developers before building hardware, turning a capital problem into a pre-order problem.

**Strongest attack survived:** Hardware delays are not a risk but a near-certainty, and shipping to backers within a year is the single claim most likely to fail — yet the proposal treats one year as a target rather than naming what happens when it slips. Backers who funded "ship in a year" do not quietly accept year two; the goodwill that made crowdfunding attractive becomes the liability that kills the brand. The proposal does not state a buffer, a late-shipment communication plan, or a kill-and-refund threshold if the schedule breaks. That gap is concrete and unaddressed.

**Assumptions:**
- One year is enough to finalize hardware, manufacture at scale, and ship — assumed, not evidenced.
- Crowdfunding will reach its minimum — assumed.
- Developers will commit to an unproven platform before launch — assumed.

**Better ways:**
- Do nothing: phone games already serve the low-cost segment; the gap is unproven.
- Sell a developer kit first, console later: cheaper validation, smaller blast radius if it fails.

**Remaining risk:** Backer-funded hardware with a fixed one-year promise and no stated delay plan is a one-way door dressed as a low-cost bet.

DECISION: NO-GO
