**Strongest version of the claim:** an inexpensive plastic watch, sold as fashion, opens a new mass-market category the company's prestige lines cannot reach — and the named risks are exactly why incumbents won't preempt it.

**Skeptic — assumptions that must hold:**
1. Plastic fashion watches command enough margin at volume to matter to a movement maker. *Unverified given facts.*
2. The reputation risk is real — but reversible: kill the line, no lasting damage. A two-way door.
3. "Established brands may cut prices" cuts both ways: they have more to lose and more overhead to carry.

**Better-way — alternatives weighed:**
- *Do nothing:* safest, but concedes the low-end fashion category entirely.
- *Smaller pilot (3–4 models, 250k units):* cheaper test of demand and channel fit; lower blast if the reputation concern materializes.
- *Twelve models, one million units:* maximum learning, maximum commitment. Harder to reverse once inventory is built.

The twelve-model launch is a bet that scale itself creates the fashion-brand effect. A smaller pilot tests that assumption at a fraction of the cost. But the proposal's logic — fashion items, not timekeepers — depends on breadth of choice to create the category; a thin pilot may prove nothing either way.

**Kill-criteria — what would reverse this:**
- Sell-through below 40% in the first six months at retail → cut the line.
- Measurable cannibalization or downgrading of the prestige brand's price point → reposition or exit.

**Remaining risk:** the sales target is aggressive, but it is a target, not a commitment; the real irreversibility is inventory and brand association, both bounded by the kill criteria above.

DECISION: GO
