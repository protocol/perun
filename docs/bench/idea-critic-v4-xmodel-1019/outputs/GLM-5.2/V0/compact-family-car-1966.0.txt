**Steelman:** A dedicated compact line at a new plant captures a growing segment, justifies the plant's scale, and uses a fresh dealer channel to avoid cannibalizing the carmaker's existing small-car base.

**Skeptic.**
- *Cannibalization.* A new 1100 cc compact sold alongside the existing small-car range likely steals buyers from the carmaker's own line, not only from rivals. Net volume gain is unproven.
- *Plant utilization as the real driver.* "The new plant must run near capacity to pay off" is a sunk-cost argument: the plant is built, so its cost is committed. Launching a car to justify the plant inverts the causal logic and risks shipping a weak product to cover a fixed asset.
- *Dealer economics.* A dedicated dealer network is a second capital project stacked on the first. The proposal gives no volume or margin figure that shows the network pays for itself before the car's lifecycle ends.

**Better-way.**
- *Do nothing / defer.* Hold the new-model launch until the existing range's sales trend, rival pricing, and the new plant's break-even utilization are quantified. Cheapest; fully reversible.
- *Expand existing dealers.* Sell the compact through the current dealer network rather than building a parallel one. Cuts fixed cost; tests demand before committing to dedicated retail.
- *Re-scope the plant.* Use the new plant for export contract work or an existing model shift, deferring the compact until demand is proven.

**Kill-criteria.**
- Pre-mortem: the car launches, the dedicated dealer network fails to reach break-even within two years, and the compact cannibalizes the existing small-car range faster than it adds net volume—leaving the carmaker with two underperforming small-car lines and an unutilized plant.
- Reversibility: low. A dedicated dealer network and a tooled compact line are not cheaply unwound.
- The proposal lacks a named break-even volume, a rival-price ceiling, and a cannibalization cap. Without them the bet is unbounded.

**Verdict:** REVISE — re-run with quantified break-even, cannibalization cap, and the cheaper "existing dealers" alternative scored against the dedicated network.

DECISION: NO-GO
