**Steelman:** A cheap plastic fashion watch opens a new mass-market segment the company's prestige lines cannot reach, at low unit cost.

**Strongest attack survived:** The reputation-risk objection fails on the company's own history — it already sells lower-priced mechanical movements to third parties without reputational damage, so a separate fashion line under a distinct identity does not erode the premium franchise.

**Assumptions:** The twelve models can be produced and distributed at the cost structure implied by "inexpensive" (unverified). One million units is achievable given the channel and marketing spend proposed (unverified).

**Better ways considered:** A smaller pilot of three to four models would test fashion uptake and cannibalisation risk before committing to twelve — but it forgoes the merchandising impact of a full collection launch and cedes first-mover advantage to competitors. Scaling back the first-year target to 500k would be safer but underfunds the brand-building premise. Neither alternative clearly dominates a full launch with a kill criterion.

**Kill criteria:** If sell-through falls below 40% of shipped units by month six, cut the line to four models and halve the year-two target. If premium-line unit sales decline more than 10% year-on-year, exit or rebrand the fashion line.

**Leading metric:** Sell-through rate of the first 200k units, reviewed at month three.

**Remaining risk:** Fashion-taste volatility could strand inventory across all twelve models simultaneously — a risk the pilot alternative mitigates but the full launch accepts.

DECISION: GO
