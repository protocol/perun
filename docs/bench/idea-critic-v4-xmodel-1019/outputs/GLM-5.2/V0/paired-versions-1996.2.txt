**Strongest defensible version:** Splitting the collection across two versions turns a single-player game into a social phenomenon — trading becomes the core loop, not a feature, and the link cable's limitation (two players, physical proximity) becomes the reason kids carry the console to school and show it to friends. Network effects: each additional player increases every other player's ability to complete the collection.

**Attack — does it beat the alternatives?**
- *One version, full collection:* safer, no price-grab perception, but loses the social/trading mechanic entirely. The game is forgettable.
- *One version, trading still possible:* closer, but with no version-exclusive creatures, the incentive to find another player collapses. Trading becomes optional, not necessary.
- *Do nothing:* the handheld is declining; releasing nothing guarantees irrelevance.

The two-version plan beats all three because the social mechanic *requires* the split.

**Pre-mortem — why it fails:** Players see two versions as cynical price-doubling and refuse to buy either. Reviewers penalize the "incomplete" collection in each box. Trading never catches on because too few players own the cable.

**Assessment:** The price-grab risk is real but manageable — the versions must be priced identically to one version, and marketing must frame trading as the adventure, not the gap. The "incomplete collection" framing inverts if the brand is positioned around collecting *and connecting*: the incompleteness is the feature. The declining-handheld risk is actually mitigated by this design: the game becomes the reason to keep the device, not a victim of its decline.

The publisher's uncertainty is the weakest objection — uncertainty is the condition for every new IP. The question is whether the downside is survivable: two SKUs with shared development cost, priced as one, on an existing platform, is a low-cost experiment with high upside if the social loop ignites.

**Kill criterion:** If first-month sell-through of *both* versions combined is below the publisher's single-version break-even, and fewer than 20% of buyers have engaged in at least one trade, pull the second SKU and consolidate.

DECISION: GO
