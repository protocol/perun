**Steelman:** Open platform plus crowdfunding de-risks an unproven console by validating demand before manufacturing and sidestepping publisher gatekeeping.

**Strongest attack survived:** Hardware delays are industry-normal, not console-fatal; the real killer is the no-marketing-budget claim colliding with a public-sales goal — but crowdfunding *is* the marketing channel and demand test, so the attack lands on execution, not concept.

**Why NO-GO as proposed:** The proposal conflates three bets (fund, ship in a year, sell publicly) and treats the year timeline as feasible for custom hardware by a small team with no manufacturing track record. The single most actionable failure: no named manufacturer, BOM cost, or yield plan, yet the schedule assumes they don't matter. The four risks are listed, not answered — "hardware delays are common" is a note, not a mitigation.

**Concrete next actions before approval:**
1. Name the contract manufacturer and get a written timeline for tooling, DVT, PVT, and mass production. If no CM is engaged, the one-year ship is fiction.
2. Lock the BOM and landing price. A console below ~$99 retail with a viable margin needs a BOM under ~$60; if the BOM is unknown, cost is unknown.
3. Set the kill criterion: if the crowdfunding campaign does not reach $X by date Y, refund and stop. X and Y must be fixed *before* launch, not after.

**Dissent ledger:** The open-platform angle is genuinely differentiated and crowdfunding is the right demand-test vehicle. The concept is sound; the proposal as written is under-specified on the one risk (manufacturing timeline) that most predictably kills hardware startups.

DECISION: NO-GO
