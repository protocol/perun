The steelman: a $35 board that students can wire, boot and program creates hands-on engagement that consumer tablets—sealed, app-store-mediated—cannot match, at a price schools can justify per-seat.

**Strongest attack survived.** "Schools already have cheap laptops; this is redundant." This fails because the proposal's value is *tinkering access*: GPIO pins, a Linux shell, an OS the student can break and reinstall. A sealed tablet or a locked-down school laptop does not provide that. The overlap is in the "computer" label, not in the use case.

**Assumptions.**
1. $35 is the *board only*. Display, keyboard, SD card, power supply add cost. The real per-seat price is higher—likely $55–70. This is acceptable if stated to buyers; it is a flaw if the $35 figure is used as the all-in cost.
2. A first batch could be small and sell out. This is a *risk* but also a *signal*: demand exceeding supply validates the concept and creates a waitlist. The risk is reputational (frustrated early adopters), not existential.
3. The charity has no manufacturing or support experience. Mitigated by licensing *distributors* who handle fulfilment and returns; the charity designs the spec and curates educational content.

**Better way considered.** "Do nothing—direct students to existing resources (Arduino kits, online emulators, refurbished laptops)." Cheaper, but fragmented: no single low-cost platform with both hardware access and a standardised educational on-ramp. The proposal fills a gap.

**Kill criteria.**
- If the first batch does **not** sell through within 90 days, stop—demand is hypothetical.
- If distributor return rates exceed 10% in the first 6 months, the hardware or documentation is inadequate; revise or stop.

**Leading metric:** units sold to distinct schools in the first 90 days. Review at day 90.

The proposal is low-cost, reversible (small first batch, licensed distributors absorb operational risk), and targets an unmet need. The $35-all-in ambiguity is the only material concern and is addressable in marketing copy without redesign.

DECISION: GO
