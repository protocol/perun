**Steelman:** A clean 64-bit break lets two firms leapfrog the dominant architecture and own the next standard, where emulation and compiler maturity are known, fundable problems.

**Strongest attack survived:** "The dominant architecture will simply be extended to 64 bits, preserving binary compatibility and killing the new standard." This is the sharpest objection — and in fact it is exactly what happened: the rival extended its architecture to 64 bits while preserving binary compatibility, and the market chose compatibility. The attack does not just survive; it is the likely outcome.

**Why this fails the decision method:**

1. **Alternatives not weighed.** The cheapest path — extending the dominant architecture to 64 bits — is listed as a *risk* rather than evaluated as the competing option. If the rival does this (and they can, faster), the new architecture is dead on arrival.

2. **One-way door, no kill criteria.** A joint architecture investment is large, multi-year, and reputationally irreversible. No pilot, no observable metric, no date, no stop condition is proposed. "Build a new architecture" is not a capped first step.

3. **Emulation risk is load-bearing and unaddressed.** The entire installed base — the reason the dominant architecture *is* dominant — depends on emulation performing acceptably. No benchmark, no threshold, no fallback. If emulation is too slow, there is no migration path and no customers.

4. **Compiler difficulty treated as a known cost.** Compiler-scheduled parallelism for a new ISA is a research problem, not an engineering estimate. Scheduling it as a line item does not make it one.

**Bottom line:** The proposal bets a multi-year, irreversible investment on the assumption that customers will abandon binary compatibility for a new architecture whose critical dependency (emulation speed) is unmeasured and whose most likely competitor response (extend to 64 bits) is acknowledged but not planned against. Approve a capped, time-boxed emulation benchmark first; do not approve the architecture as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
