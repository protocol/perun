**Strongest defensible version of the claim:** A search company that just acquired a desktop mapping program should, in 2004, convert it into a free, browser-based web application with satellite imagery and route planning.

**Steelman.** Search is the front door to the web; maps and local search are the natural next surface. The acquired program's core value—seeing and manipulating a map—survives the desktop-to-browser move if the browser can be pushed hard enough. Free plus no-download lowers the barrier below every incumbent that requires a plugin or a slow page reload. Satellite imagery is the differentiator incumbents lack. Route planning captures the "how do I get there" intent that plain search misses. The company's infrastructure (cheap compute, bandwidth, storage at scale) is exactly the asset that makes a costly-licensing, no-direct-revenue bet affordable as a *traffic and brand play* rather than a standalone business. The incumbents' existing users are a vulnerability, not a moat: those users are there despite a clunky experience, not because of one.

**Strongest attack survived.** "Browsers of 2004 cannot do interactive graphics, so the core experience is impossible." This is the sharpest objection because it is a technical feasibility gate: if the browser cannot pan and zoom a map smoothly, the entire value proposition collapses. The proposal survives it because the company has a demonstrated engineering culture of making browsers do things others assume they cannot, and the desktop program already contains the algorithms (tiling, projection, rendering) that need only a thin browser shim. The objection is about *difficulty*, not *impossibility*; the company's competitive advantage is precisely that it solves this kind of difficulty.

**Residual risk.** Licensing cost is real but bounded by the company's cash position and is a variable expense that scales with success—a problem worth having. The lack of a direct revenue model is a feature, not a bug, for a company monetizing attention rather than transactions.

DECISION: GO
