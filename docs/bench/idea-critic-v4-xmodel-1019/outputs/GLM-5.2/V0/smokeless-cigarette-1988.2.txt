**Steelman.** A heated-tobacco cigarette could let smokers keep the nicotine hit and ritual while sharply reducing smoke; if test markets confirm appeal, wide promotion of "cleaner" smoking could shift share and justify the sunk spend.

**Skeptic.** The proposal conflates *developed* with *wanted*. Three of the four named risks are adoption risks — taste, lighting method, complexity versus a normal cigarette — and none is resolved by the evidence given. "Test markets" are promised, not reported; the plan asks for wide promotion *after* tests, but states no pass criterion. The health-claim risk is not a marketing nuance: a regulator objection could kill the claim that makes the product worth launching. Treating the very large sunk cost as a reason to launch is exactly the sunk-cost fallacy; it is relevant to *whether to spend more*, not to *whether smokers will buy*.

**Better-way.** Do nothing now: run the test markets, pre-commit a kill criterion (e.g. repeat-purchase below X% at week 8 → stop), and only then decide on wide promotion. This is cheaper, reversible, and forces the adoption risks to be answered with data before money is committed.

**Kill criteria.** The proposal has none. Without a named threshold and a stop date, "launch and promote widely" is an open-ended bet on a product whose ease-of-use and taste are, by the proposer's own admission, in doubt.

**Strongest attack survived.** None. The adoption and regulatory risks are concrete and unresolved, and the plan as written permits spending on wide promotion before the test-market verdict is in.

**Remaining risk.** Launching as proposed burns promotion spend on a product that may fail on taste or lighting, and invites a regulatory challenge to the core "cleaner" claim before adoption is proven.

DECISION: NO-GO
