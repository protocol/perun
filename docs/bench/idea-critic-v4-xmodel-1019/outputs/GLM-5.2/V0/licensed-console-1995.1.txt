**Steelman:** The licensor gains console-market presence and royalty revenue with near-zero capital or marketing risk, leveraging an underutilized platform through a partner with retail channels.

**Strongest attack survived:** The licensee won't market hard enough, the royalties are too small to matter, and consumers won't connect the product to the licensor's brand — meaning the licensor bears reputational risk while capturing minimal financial upside. This fails because the proposal's core value isn't royalty income or brand-building; it's platform proliferation at a time when network effects determine which OS survives. Every console running the platform expands the developer base and software library, which is the actual strategic asset. The royalty need not be large per unit if unit volume is high, and the licensee's incentive to market is aligned: they profit only by selling hardware. The brand-disconnection risk is actually a feature: if the product fails, the licensor's brand isn't damaged; if it succeeds, the platform wins regardless of whether consumers know the licensor's name.

**Assumptions:** The platform has genuine consumer-software value beyond the office (assumed — unverified). The licensee has the retail and manufacturing capability to ship at volume (assumed). The licensor's royalty is structured to scale with unit volume rather than a flat cap (assumed).

**Better ways:** The licensor could build and market the console itself, capturing full margin and brand credit — but this requires capital, retail expertise, and marketing infrastructure the licensor may not have, and it concentrates risk. The licensor could also do nothing and focus on its existing computer market — but this abandons any consumer-platform play and cedes the living room to competitors. Licensing is the cheapest reversible path to testing consumer-market demand for the platform.

**Kill criteria:** If the licensee ships fewer than 100,000 units in the first two holiday seasons, the partnership should be terminated or restructured, as the volume is insufficient to build platform network effects.

**Remaining risk:** The platform may lack consumer appeal entirely, in which case no licensing structure rescues it.

DECISION: GO
