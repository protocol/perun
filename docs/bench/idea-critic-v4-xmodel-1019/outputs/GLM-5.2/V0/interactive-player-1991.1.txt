**Strongman:** A dedicated living-room platform could deliver a more polished, appliance-like interactive experience than a general-purpose PC, at a lower price and with simpler operation.

**Skeptic:** The "between a console and a PC" position is unstable. At ~$1,000 the device costs roughly 3-4× a game console while offering a smaller game library, and PCs are already adding sound, CD-ROM, and video capabilities that subsume the proposed feature set. The category itself is unproven: buyers understand "game console" and "personal computer," but "interactive multimedia player" has no established use case, no killer app named, and a launch library described as small. Every risk raised is a reason the mass market does not adopt at this price.

**Better-way:** Do nothing — let PC multimedia mature and capture the segment without dedicated hardware risk. Alternatively, a cheaper, narrower device (game-focused or education-focused) with a guaranteed launch library would test the core assumption — that consumers want a third living-room box — at a fraction of the capital.

**Kill criteria:** Set a concrete adoption threshold within the first two quarters (e.g., 100K units sold and at least 20 third-party titles shipped). If either milestone is missed, stop production and cut losses.

**Verdict:** The proposal bundles three unproven assumptions — that a new category is wanted, that a $1,000 price is acceptable, and that a thin library will suffice — into one irreversible hardware bet. The cheaper, narrower test path has not been beaten. The full platform should not launch as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
