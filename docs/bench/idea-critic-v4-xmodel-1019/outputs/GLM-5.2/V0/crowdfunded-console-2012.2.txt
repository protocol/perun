**Steelman:** Crowdfunding removes the publisher gate and proves demand before capital is committed; a small team can reach niche players the incumbents ignore.

**Strongest attack survived:** Hardware delays are near-certain for a first-time hardware team, and a missed ship date breaks backer trust — the campaign's only asset. This nearly killed the actual Ouya. But crowdfunding still front-loads the risk onto willing backers rather than investors, so the structure is sound even if the timeline is not.

**Better ways:**
- Ship software on existing phones first, then offer the controller as an accessory. Cheaper, faster, still proves demand.
- Do nothing. The incumbents and phone games already serve this market.

**Kill criteria:**
- If the campaign fails to fund, stop.
- If hardware slips past 18 months, pivot to software-only on existing devices.
- Leading metric: backer conversion rate; review at campaign close.

**Decision: go-if** — fund a small, capped pilot: a crowdfunding campaign with a public ship-date of 18 months (not 12), a software prototype running on a phone with a controller, and the kill criteria above. The 12-month ship promise is the flaw; the structure is worth a small bet.

DECISION: NO-GO
