**Steelman:** A dedicated appliance could deliver a smoother, more reliable living-room multimedia experience than a general-purpose PC, at a fraction of a PC's price.

**Skeptic.** "Smoother and cheaper than a PC" is asserted, not shown. The $1,000 price sits well above game consoles that already connect to the living-room TV; the proposal names no title secured for launch, so "small library" is treated as a footnote when it is the entire purchase reason. Buyer confusion is acknowledged then dropped — no positioning claim resolves it.

**Better-way.** Two cheaper paths compete: subsidize the hardware and earn on disc sales (console model), or ship a multimedia expansion for PCs customers already own. Both avoid asking a household to spend $1,000 on a category no one has named for them. "Do nothing" is also live: if PCs are gaining multimedia ability on their own, a dedicated box is racing a falling price curve.

**Kill-criteria.** The pre-mortem is clear: households don't spend $1,000 for a box with no titles and no clear purpose. The proposal gives no launch-title count, no attach-rate assumption, and no date by which "small library" must become sufficient. Without a named threshold and a date, there is no tripwire to stop the bleed.

**Strongest attack survived.** "PCs will commoditize this category within two years." The proposal does not rebut this; it concedes the trend. A $1,000 dedicated box launched into a falling price curve, with no secured titles and acknowledged buyer confusion, lacks the evidence to justify the spend now.

**Dissent ledger.** Title pipeline unverified; price-vs-value gap unaddressed; positioning claim absent.

**Remaining risk.** Capital committed to a category PCs may absorb before the library matures.

**Decision:** no-go as proposed; revisit with secured launch titles, a subsidy or PC-expansion alternative, and a dated library threshold.

DECISION: NO-GO
