The proposal to double production while sitting on 4,500 unsold cars, facing a recession, rising interest rates, and limited cash is fundamentally flawed. Demand is contracting, not expanding; financing costs are climbing, not falling; and dealer capacity to absorb inventory is shrinking. Doubling output would balloon inventory, strain cash reserves, and force deeper discounts or costly storage—likely triggering a liquidity crisis. The steelman—that volume reduces unit costs and preps for recovery—does not offset the immediate cash burn and inventory risk when sales are collapsing. Cheaper alternatives exist: cut production, discount existing stock, or pause output to stabilize cash. The proposal fails the pre-mortem: it fails quickly and badly with no credible kill criterion beyond bankruptcy. This is a high-blast, low-reversibility decision; the evidence on hand contradicts the plan’s core assumption that demand will absorb more cars. Without a viable path to sell current inventory, doubling output is reckless.

DECISION: NO-GO
