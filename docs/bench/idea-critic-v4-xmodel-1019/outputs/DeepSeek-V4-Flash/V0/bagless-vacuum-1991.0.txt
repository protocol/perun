DECISION: NO-GO

The proposal asks to commit to building a manufacturing company around an unproven premium product in a market dominated by entrenched incumbents with superior distribution and the ability to copy the technology. The inventor has no manufacturing record, and the cost premium over conventional machines is a structural disadvantage, not a fixable detail. The central claim—that buyers will pay extra for bagless—is an assumption with no cited evidence of willingness to pay, and the risks named are not mitigated by the plan. There is no pilot, no licensing alternative, no distribution partnership, and no kill criterion if early sales disappoint. The one-way door of founding a company on this basis is premature when a cheaper, reversible path exists: license the cyclone technology to an established manufacturer, or sell a small batch directly to enthusiasts to test demand. The proposal’s own evidence does not show this beats doing nothing, and the downside—sunk capital and a failed brand—is real. Approving as proposed would commit resources to the riskiest version of a good idea.
