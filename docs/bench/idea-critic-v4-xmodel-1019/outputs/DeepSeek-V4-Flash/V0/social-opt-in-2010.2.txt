DECISION: NO-GO

The proposal fails because automatic enrollment with default visible connections is a one-way door that converts a private communication tool into a public graph without explicit consent. The stated risks are not hypothetical; they are near-certain consequences of the design. Users did not ask for a social layer, and forcing them into it violates the implicit contract of a webmail service: that their contacts and correspondence are private.

The launch is effectively irreversible. Once connections are exposed, the damage to trust is done even if the feature is later rolled back. Regulator attention is likely, and the cost of defending a privacy invasion is high. The claim that a social network inside an inbox will see ongoing use is unproven and contradicted by the reality that inboxes are task-focused, not social-discovery spaces. The proposal offers no opt-in mechanism, no granular privacy control, and no kill criterion tied to user trust or retention.

A better path would be a small, opt-in pilot with default-private settings, tested on a volunteer segment, with clear metrics for engagement and trust. That would preserve the possibility of learning without forcing irreversible exposure on the entire base. The current proposal is a high-risk, low-reversibility bet that fails the most basic test: it does not respect the user's existing expectation of privacy. Approving it as proposed would be approving a foreseeable trust and regulatory crisis.
