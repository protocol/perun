DECISION: NO-GO

The proposal asks for a full commitment to a new, complex machine within a year, while simultaneously phasing out the only proven revenue source. The risks are not speculative—they are concrete: existing owners and software vendors are unlikely to migrate quickly, cutting off the installed base that funds the company. The tight schedule for an unproven, complex design raises the probability of a delayed, buggy launch that damages the brand and burns cash.

The core flaw is the "phase out" mandate. It forces a binary choice before the successor has proven itself in the market. A smarter path is to build the new machine as an *addition*, not a replacement, and let the market decide. This preserves current revenue, allows a longer, safer development window, and gives software makers a reason to support the new platform without abandoning the old one.

The proposal's own risk list confirms this: reliability is unproven, the schedule is tight, and the installed base may not move. Approving it as proposed means accepting all three as acceptable trade-offs, which they are not. The safer, more profitable move is to launch the new machine alongside the existing one, with a clear migration path but no forced sunset date. This is a reversible, lower-risk decision that still captures the business opportunity. The current proposal is a one-way door that locks in failure if the launch slips or the market hesitates.
