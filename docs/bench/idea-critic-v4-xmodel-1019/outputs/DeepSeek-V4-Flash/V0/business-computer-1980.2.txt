DECISION: NO-GO

The proposal fails on its own schedule and risk profile. Launching a complex, unproven successor within a year while simultaneously phasing out the only revenue-generating product creates a single point of failure: if the new machine slips or is unreliable, the company loses both its current cash flow and its future bet. The risks listed are not hypothetical—they are concrete and unmitigated. Existing owners and software makers have no incentive to migrate without a compelling, proven reason; "business features" alone do not guarantee adoption. Hardware reliability is unproven, so a rushed launch could damage the brand irreparably. The plan also ignores a cheaper, reversible alternative: continue selling the current product while developing the successor in parallel, with a longer, milestone-based timeline. This preserves revenue, tests the market's appetite, and allows for course correction. The proposal's "phase out" language is a one-way door that cannot be walked back if the new machine stumbles. The year deadline is arbitrary and self-imposed; there is no external competition cited that justifies the rush. Without a clear adoption plan, a reliability testing window, or a fallback if the new product fails, approving this now would be reckless. The company should not bet its entire future on an unproven, rushed product while discarding its only proven asset.
