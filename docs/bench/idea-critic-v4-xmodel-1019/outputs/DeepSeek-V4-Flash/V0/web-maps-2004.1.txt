DECISION: NO-GO

The proposal’s core premise—that a free, browser-based map with satellite imagery and routing will win users—is untested against the stated risks. The licensing cost alone is a hard blocker: no revenue model is offered to offset it, and established competitors already own the user base. “Build it and they will come” is not a strategy.

The steelman: a superior free product could displace incumbents through sheer usability. But the evidence fails. Browsers in 2004 are slow at interactive graphics, so the experience will likely be worse than the desktop program, not better. The satellite imagery and routing features require expensive data licenses with no payback path. The acquisition gives you a desktop user base, but converting them to a web product with higher costs and no clear advantage is a leap, not a step.

Alternatives: (1) keep the desktop program, add routing as a paid upgrade; (2) license only basic maps, no satellite, and sell premium routing; (3) do nothing and wait for browser tech to mature. Each is cheaper, less risky, and reversible. The proposal is a one-way door: once you commit to the web build and data contracts, you’ve spent money and abandoned the desktop advantage.

Kill criteria are absent. There is no metric, threshold, or date to stop if adoption lags. The pre-mortem is obvious: licensing costs balloon, the beta feels sluggish, and users stay with the incumbent. The cost of delay is low—a year of browser improvement reduces the technical risk and lets you test demand with a minimal landing page first.

No concrete scenario shows this succeeds as proposed. The risks are real, the revenue is missing, and the timing is premature. Do not approve now.
