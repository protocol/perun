First call: GO. The business market is a clear, stated opportunity, and the successor directly addresses the known gap. The risks are execution concerns, not fundamental contradictions.

Steelman: A focused, feature-complete business machine will capture a new, high-value segment, and a disciplined transition will preserve revenue while growing the franchise.

Attack: The proposal banks on a captive market that may not exist. Existing owners are comfortable; software makers have no incentive to port until there's a proven installed base. The tight schedule and unproven hardware mean a rushed, buggy launch could poison the brand in the very segment it targets. Phasing out the only profitable product before the successor is proven is a one-way door that risks the company's core revenue on an unvalidated bet.

Do nothing: Keep selling the current machine, missing the business segment entirely. Cheaper alternative: ship a minimal, low-risk upgrade (e.g., just the display) as a stopgap, deferring the full redesign.

Fit: The goal is growth; this targets it, but the timeline and transition plan are aggressive.

Pre-mortem: The launch slips, hardware fails, and the old product is already discontinued — revenue collapses with no bridge.

Evidence: All cited facts are internal to the proposal; no external validation of demand or reliability exists.

Blockers: The plan states the current product is the *only* successful one and proposes ending it within a year, with no proven replacement and no fallback. That is a self-inflicted, irreversible risk to the company's sole revenue source. This is a blocker.

Risks: Execution slips, ecosystem inertia, reliability issues.

DECISION: NO-GO
