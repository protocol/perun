DECISION: GO

The two uninterested floor-care companies are a weak signal: they are incumbents evaluating a category-disrupting product against their own mapping-based roadmaps, not end users. The proposal’s core bet—that low price and “good enough” random coverage beat expensive mapping—is plausible for a mass market that values cost and simplicity over perfection.

The stated risks are real but not fatal. Random coverage’s imperfection is a feature at this price point, not a bug. Mapping rivals being “smarter” matters only if buyers care; many won’t at a fraction of the cost. Lack of consumer experience is a risk, but a vacuum is a low-stakes, forgiving first product. Trust is addressable with a strong return policy and clear marketing.

The alternatives—do nothing (forego a new market) or partner with the disinterested companies (likely to kill the product’s price advantage)—are weaker. The downside is a failed product launch, a two-way door. The upside is owning a new category. The evidence at hand, thin as it is, does not contradict the proposal’s logic. Proceed, but with a capped first production run and a clear metric (sell-through rate in first 90 days) to validate before scaling.
