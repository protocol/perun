First call: GO. The proposal leverages existing assets, targets a proven popular genre, and creates a new revenue stream, though cannibalization and free-to-play inexperience are real risks.

Steelman: By reusing existing assets, the studio can quickly enter a high-demand market with a free-to-play title, monetizing via cosmetics without diluting the paid game's core audience, thus capturing new players and revenue.

Attack: The rival's dominance is a major barrier. The free mode could directly cannibalize sales of the paid game, especially if it offers a similar experience for free. Two months is extremely tight for a polished, competitive mode, and the studio's lack of free-to-play operational experience (balancing, live ops, monetization) is a significant execution risk.

Strongest attack survived: The cannibalization risk is real but not fatal; the free-to-play title targets a different player segment (last-player-standing fans) and the paid game offers a distinct cooperative experience, so overlap is limited. The two-month timeline is aggressive but feasible given asset reuse, and the rival's popularity proves demand, not insurmountable competition.

Assumptions: The paid game's assets are suitable for a last-player-standing mode without major rework. The studio can learn free-to-play monetization quickly.

Kill criteria: If the free-to-play mode's player retention is below a set threshold (e.g., 20% day-30 retention) or if paid game sales drop by more than 15% within three months of launch, the project should pivot or halt.

DECISION: GO
