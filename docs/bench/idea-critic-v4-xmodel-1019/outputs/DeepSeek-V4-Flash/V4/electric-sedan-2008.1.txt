First call: NO-GO. The stated facts—capital shortfall, scarce charging, no mass-production experience—already show the plan fails as proposed.

Steelman: a bold, all-in bet on EVs in 2008 could leapfrog incumbents, and buying an existing factory accelerates scale.

Attack: the proposal commits the entire company to one product and one technology with no pilot, no cap, and no fallback. The factory and battery packs require capital far beyond what the company has—this is stated as fact, not risk. A young carmaker with no mass-production experience buying a large factory from established makers is a recipe for cost overruns and quality failures. Charging stations are scarce, so demand is unproven. Established makers can respond with their own EVs or price cuts. There is no evidence of a path to profitability, no staged rollout, and no kill criterion. This is a one-way door with no measured exit.

Alternatives: do nothing, or start with a smaller, higher-margin EV or hybrid while leasing a smaller plant and validating demand. These cost less, are reversible, and preserve the option to scale later. The proposal must beat do nothing; it does not.

Pre-mortem: the company runs out of cash before the first car ships, or ships a flawed car that kills the brand. The scarcity of charging stations and lack of production experience are stated negatives that remove the route to the upside, and the plan does not answer them.

Blockers: capital requirement exceeds available funds; no mass-production experience; stated scarcity of charging stations undermines demand. Risks: incumbent response, execution failure.

Kill criteria: none proposed. The plan is a bet with no tripwire.

DECISION: NO-GO
