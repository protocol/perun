DECISION: NO-GO

First call: NO-GO. The proposal fails on its own stated facts: the charity’s stated goal is to rekindle student interest in programming, but the device as proposed requires buyers to source display, keyboard, and power supply separately—raising total cost well above the $35 headline and adding friction that undermines the low-cost, low-barrier appeal. The stated evidence already shows the target users (schools, students, families) are better served by existing cheap laptops and tablets that are complete, supported, and familiar. The proposal names no pilot, no metric for success (e.g., number of students actively programming), no kill criterion, and no fallback if the first batch sells out or support costs balloon. A charity with no manufacturing or support experience is betting on a one-way door with real money and no measured stop condition. The cheaper, safer alternative—partner with an existing device maker or run a small, capped pilot in a few schools with pre-owned hardware—reaches the same upside (rekindling interest) at lower risk and higher reversibility. The proposal’s own numbers do not work: $35 is not the real cost to the user, and the plan does not answer how a charity sustains support or scale. Without a named pilot, cap, or metric, this is a bet, not a plan. Approving as proposed would commit the charity to a path its own evidence contradicts.
