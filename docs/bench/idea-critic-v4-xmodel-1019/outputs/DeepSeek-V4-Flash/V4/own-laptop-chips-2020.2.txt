First call: GO. The company has proven in-house silicon design capability in phones and tablets, and laptops are a natural extension with clear differentiation potential. The risks are real but manageable through careful execution.

Steelman: Leveraging proven in-house chip design for laptops will cut supply dependency, improve integration with its ecosystem, and deliver better performance-per-watt, justifying the engineering cost.

Options: Do nothing keeps supplier dependency and commodity positioning. Alternative: keep buying chips, losing differentiation. Alternative: design only premium laptop chips, limiting scale. The full move is bolder but coherent with existing strategy.

Fit: Aligns with vertical integration and product differentiation. Software compatibility is the critical hurdle, but the company has already solved this for phones and can do so for laptops.

Pre-mortem: If the transition fails, it fails because software breaks, performance disappoints, or buyers see no benefit. All are execution risks, not fundamental flaws.

Evidence: The company has a proven track record in phone and tablet silicon. The laptop chip is unproven, but so was the phone chip before it succeeded. Engineering cost is real but bounded. Supplier relationship loss is a risk, but vertical integration is the strategic direction.

Blockers: NONE. No stated fact shows the plan fails as proposed.
Risks: Software compatibility, unproven laptop chip performance, engineering cost, buyer confusion, supplier relationship loss. All mitigable through phased rollout, compatibility layers, and clear marketing.

Decision: GO. The upside is significant, the risks are known and manageable, and the company has the capability to execute.

DECISION: GO
