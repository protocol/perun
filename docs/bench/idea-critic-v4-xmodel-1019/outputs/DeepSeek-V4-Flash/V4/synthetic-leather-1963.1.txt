First call: NO-GO. The stated economics and adoption barriers contradict the premium-volume premise.

Steelman: A durable, consistent synthetic could reduce waste, enable new designs, and capture a fashion-forward premium niche despite higher cost.

Attack:
- The material costs more than leather, yet the plan targets cost-sensitive shoe manufacturers who must retool factories. No evidence any manufacturer wants to pay more for a material that offers wearers no clear comfort advantage.
- The plan spends heavily on marketing to manufacturers, but the real risk is end-consumer rejection (comfort, fashion). Marketing to buyers does not solve wearer adoption.
- Fashion is cyclical; a premium launch with heavy spend is a one-way door with no stated exit if trend shifts.
- Do-nothing alternative: keep material as a specialty niche or license to a partner, far cheaper and reversible.

Blockers: The proposal’s own facts show higher cost plus factory-change friction plus unproven wearer comfort—no stated path to the upside. The target users (manufacturers) have no incentive to switch.

Risks: fashion shift, comfort complaints, retooling resistance.

Kill criteria: none stated—no pilot, no cap, no metric.

Even as a go-if (small pilot with a single brand, capped spend, comfort test), the proposal as written is not approvable.

DECISION: NO-GO
