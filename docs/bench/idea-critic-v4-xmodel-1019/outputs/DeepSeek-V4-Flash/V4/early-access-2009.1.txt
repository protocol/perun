First call: GO. The proposal is to sell an unfinished alpha immediately, using buyer feedback to steer development.

Steelman: Releasing early builds a community of invested players whose feedback directly shapes the game, creating a loyal base and iterative improvement without publisher constraints.

Attack: The risks are real but not fatal. Buyers of an alpha know it’s unfinished; the rough state is the product’s promise, not a defect. A solo dev can limit sales or scale support by community moderation, and early revenue funds continued work. The lack of marketing is offset by word-of-mouth from engaged alpha players who feel ownership. Waiting for polish risks losing momentum and never shipping. The alternative—building in obscurity—offers no feedback loop and no income.

Blocker check: No stated fact shows the plan fails. The risks are speculative, not proven. The upside—direct user-driven development—is achievable now, and the door is reversible: if sales stall, the dev can pivot to a finished release.

Kill criteria: If after 90 days, sales are below a minimal threshold (e.g., 500 units) and player feedback is negative, stop and reassess. Leading metric: weekly sales and active player feedback.

DECISION: GO
