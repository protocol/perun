First call: GO. The buyer pool is already shifting toward American-brand customers wanting a smaller sedan—this proposal meets that demand with a premium product at a higher margin.

**Steelman:** A compact luxury car leveraging a proven mass-market platform, differentiated by trim and features, captures a growing segment while keeping development costs low.

**Do nothing:** Loses these buyers to rivals; brand stagnates. **Alternative 1:** Develop a bespoke compact platform—costly, slow, risky. **Alternative 2:** Offer a badge-engineered version of an existing European model—higher cost, less distinct.

**Fit:** Aligns with stated buyer trend; expands brand reach without diluting core luxury line.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure looks like buyers rejecting the premium price as a rebadge, or brand prestige eroding. Both are two-way doors—adjustable via pricing, marketing, or withdrawal.

**Evidence:** Buyer shift is stated as fact; platform sharing is proven practice. Risks are speculative, not confirmed.

**Blockers:** NONE. **Risks:** Perceived lack of authenticity; price resistance; internal sales skepticism; minor brand dilution—all manageable via positioning and training.

**Kill criteria:** If after 12 months, sales volume or margin underperforms projections by >20%, pivot to reposition or discontinue. Leading metric: early order rates and dealer feedback.

**Decision:** GO. The upside—capturing a growing segment with low-cost execution—outweighs manageable risks. Proceed with a capped pilot launch and clear metrics.
