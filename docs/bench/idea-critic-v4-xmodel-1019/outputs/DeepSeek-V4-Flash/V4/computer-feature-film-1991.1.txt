**Steelman:** A proven short-film and commercial studio leverages its technical edge to create the first all-computer-animated feature, with a major studio's distribution muscle guaranteeing theatrical reach.

**Attack:**

- **Scale leap:** The jump from shorts to a feature is not linear; it’s a step-change in production complexity, pipeline stability, and storytelling stamina. The proposal does not state how the team will manage a 10x increase in render load and asset management.
- **Power asymmetry:** The studio holds distribution and financing. They can impose creative controls, demand revisions, or shelve the film. A first-time feature producer has minimal leverage to protect its vision or profit share.
- **Market risk:** The proposal admits no precedent exists. The "first of its kind" label is a risk, not a selling point. The target audience for a toy's story is unproven.
- **Cost overrun:** Rendering is slow and costly. Without a defined cap or milestone-based funding, the company could be forced into a bad deal or bankruptcy mid-production.
- **Alternative:** Do nothing, retain independence, and continue building a strong portfolio of shorts and commercials. This preserves the lead animator and creative control.

**Verdict:** The core claim—that this leap is a sound business move—is contradicted by the proposal's own stated risks: no precedent, no feature experience, high cost, and a dominant partner. The plan has no defined kill criteria or financial cap. The risks are not mitigations; they are structural flaws. The company is trading its only leverage (its talent and independence) for a high-risk gamble it cannot control.

DECISION: NO-GO
