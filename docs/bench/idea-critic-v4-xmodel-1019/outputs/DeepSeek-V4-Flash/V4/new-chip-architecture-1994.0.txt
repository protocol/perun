DECISION: NO-GO

The proposal fails on its own stated facts. The dominant architecture’s installed software base is huge, and the only bridge—emulation—is admitted to be slow. That means the new architecture would launch with a crippled user experience and no compelling reason for developers or buyers to switch. The plan offers no path to overcome the network effects of the incumbent, and the two named risks (hard compilers, rivals extending the old architecture to 64-bit) are not answered with a counter-strategy. Betting the company’s future on an unproven compiler model while handing the incumbent a clear window to extend its own 64-bit line is a losing proposition. The upside is speculative; the downside is structural and immediate.
