First call: GO. The stated facts show a concrete, addressable market shift and a low-cost path to enter it.

Steelman: The carmaker can capture American-brand buyers seeking a smaller luxury sedan by offering a premium-trimmed compact on a shared platform, achieving volume and margin without a new architecture.

Attack: The core risk is brand dilution and value perception. But the proposal does not demand a new badge or a new brand; it uses the existing model name. The market shift is stated as fact, not speculation. The shared platform is invisible to most buyers once trim, materials, and ride are upgraded. The risk of salespeople not using the main brand is a training and incentive issue, not a product flaw. The prestige risk is real but manageable: the car is positioned as a smaller, accessible entry point, not a replacement for the flagship.

Better ways: Do nothing—lose the growing segment. Build a bespoke small car—costly and slow. The proposed approach is the cheapest, fastest way to test demand.

Kill criteria: If the car's conquest rate from American brands is below a set threshold in the first year, or if it cannibalizes existing luxury sales without net growth, stop or reposition.

Blockers: NONE. Risks: brand dilution, price resistance, internal sales resistance. These are execution risks, not fatal flaws.

DECISION: GO
