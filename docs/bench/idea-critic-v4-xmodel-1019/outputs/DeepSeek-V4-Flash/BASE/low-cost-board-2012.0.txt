The proposal’s core appeal is price and mission fit. At $35, the device is far cheaper than a laptop or tablet, directly serving the charity’s goal of sparking programming interest. The licensing model limits financial exposure and operational burden, which mitigates the charity’s lack of manufacturing and support experience. The risk of buyers needing peripherals is real but not fatal—schools and hobbyists often have spare displays and keyboards, and the price point justifies the extra setup. A small first batch selling out is actually a positive signal: it creates scarcity, validates demand, and generates word-of-mouth without requiring large upfront inventory. The existence of cheap laptops does not negate the unique value of a dedicated, hackable board for learning programming—it offers a different, more tactile experience. The charity’s inexperience is a concern, but outsourcing distribution to two licensed partners shifts logistics and support to professionals, while the charity focuses on its educational mission. The proposal does not require the charity to build a support team or invest heavily; it simply supplies a product and lets partners handle sales. The risks are manageable, the cost is minimal, and the upside—both in mission impact and cultural resonance—is high. Waiting for a revised plan would delay momentum and miss the timing advantage of being first to market with this price point. The proposal is lean, focused, and directly aligned with the stated purpose.  
DECISION: GO
