The proposal asks for a full launch after test markets, framed as a cleaner alternative. The core product is novel: a heated, carbon-tipped cigarette with reduced smoke. However, the risks are material and interdependent. Taste and usability are not minor friction points—they are the entire reason smokers would switch from a cheap, simple, familiar product. If early adopters find it awkward or unpleasant, the "cleaner" positioning collapses, and the massive sunk cost becomes a trap, not a justification.  

Regulatory objection is not hypothetical; in 1988, any health-adjacent claim would invite scrutiny, and a tobacco company claiming "less smoke" without rigorous, independent proof could trigger bans or forced corrections, killing the product before scale. The test markets may show initial curiosity, but they cannot reveal long-term adoption or legal pushback. The company has already spent heavily—that is a reason to be disciplined, not to double down.  

The right move is not to abandon the technology but to reposition it: launch quietly, without broad "cleaner" claims, perhaps in a single region, targeting existing smokers as a premium novelty, while gathering real-world data on taste, usability, and regulatory tolerance. A full, promoted launch now risks destroying both the product's credibility and the company's legal standing. The proposal as written is premature and overreaching.  

Therefore, I do not approve the launch as proposed.  

DECISION: NO-GO
