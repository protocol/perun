The proposal hinges on a single, high-risk bet: simultaneous multi-continent launch in an unproven market, funded by finite venture capital. The founders’ ambition is clear, but the execution path ignores basic sequencing. In 1998, internet retail is nascent, and fashion—a tactile, try-before-you-buy category—is the hardest vertical to sell online. Young buyers are early adopters, but their loyalty is fickle, and their willingness to pay for shipping and returns is unknown.  

Running offices in several countries from day one means fixed costs (real estate, local staff, localized marketing, customs, and logistics) explode before any revenue curve is validated. Delays in one market will bleed cash into another, and the venture pool will be consumed by overhead, not learning. The risk of running out of money is not hypothetical—it is structural. The founders have no proof of product-market fit in a single city, yet they propose to scale across two continents.  

The correct move is to prove the model in one dense market (e.g., the UK or Germany) with a focused brand, learn the unit economics of shipping and returns, and only then expand. The proposal as written is a recipe for burning capital on unproven assumptions. It should be revised to a phased rollout, with a clear milestone-based funding tranche. The ambition is commendable, but the timing and scope are fatal as proposed.  

DECISION: NO-GO
