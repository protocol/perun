Based on the facts given: the carmaker has a new plant requiring near-capacity output to be viable. The proposal ties this plant to a new compact car with a dedicated, costly dealer network, entering a market where rivals already sell small cars cheaply. The plant’s financial health depends on high volume, but the dedicated network adds fixed costs and slow ramp-up, while price competition from established rivals threatens margins. Buyer preference could swing to cheaper or larger cars, leaving the plant underutilized. The proposal’s success hinges on unproven assumptions: that the new network can reach needed sales quickly, that the 1100 cc size is optimally positioned against cheaper and larger options, and that the plant’s capacity can be filled without price cuts eroding payback. No evidence is given of a unique advantage—no cost edge, brand loyalty, or distribution breakthrough—to offset these risks. Approving now would commit to a high-stakes, interdependent gamble where failure in one element (network build, pricing, demand) cascades into plant losses. A safer path would be to test demand with existing dealers or adjust product/network scope before full commitment, but that is not what is proposed. The risks are not mere cautions; they are structural and unmitigated in the plan as stated. Therefore, the proposal should not be approved as proposed now.
