The proposal doubles output amid a mounting inventory overhang, recessionary demand destruction, and rising financing costs. With 4,500 unsold cars already, doubling production will rapidly consume cash, strain dealer floor-planning capacity, and deepen losses per unit as fixed costs spread over unsold stock. Limited cash reserves make this a liquidity gamble: if sales don’t recover sharply, the company faces insolvency. The rationale for doubling—likely to achieve scale economies or cost reductions—does not offset the immediate cash burn and inventory risk. Recession and high interest rates are exogenous and worsening; no evidence suggests demand will absorb even current output. Dealers, already under financial pressure, cannot be assumed to take more units. The proposal fails to address the core problem (demand shortfall) and instead amplifies the risk (supply surplus). Even if production cost per unit falls, the company must finance and store unsold vehicles, eroding any margin benefit. There is no mention of price adjustments, marketing initiatives, or alternative financing. The prudent path is to slow production, reduce inventory, conserve cash, and wait for demand stabilization. Approving this now would likely accelerate a cash crisis. The proposal should be rejected or revised to a production cut.  

DECISION: NO-GO
