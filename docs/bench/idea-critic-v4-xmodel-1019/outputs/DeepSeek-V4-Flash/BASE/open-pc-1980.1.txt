The proposal asks a vertically integrated mainframe maker to pivot decisively into a nascent, low-margin, open-architecture market. The core tension is between its proven, high-control model and the speed and flexibility required to compete with agile startups.  

The year timeline is realistic only if the team is truly independent, which is proposed. Off-the-shelf parts and outside software are not just compromises—they are the only way to meet that deadline and price point. The risks are real but not fatal. Rivals copying the design is inevitable, but first-mover advantage in a booming market can establish brand and ecosystem before clones mature. Dependence on software suppliers is a concern, but the company’s hardware expertise and service reputation can differentiate it. Lower margins are acceptable if volume scales; the company’s cost structure can adapt if the team is lean. Retail inexperience is the weakest area, but independent stores already exist and are eager for product; the company can learn by partnering with them rather than owning distribution.  

The larger risk is not acting. The mainframe market is mature, and small-computer makers are growing fast. Waiting to perfect a proprietary, in-house solution would cede the market entirely. The proposal’s deliberate break from tradition is its strength. A separate team shields the core business while allowing experimentation. The open design and external software reduce time-to-market and development cost, aligning with the urgency.  

The proposal does not ask for unlimited resources or a reckless bet. It asks for a focused, bounded entry with a clear exit if it fails. The company’s financial strength can absorb a modest loss. The upside—learning the personal computer market, building retail relationships, and establishing a foothold—outweighs the downside of a failed product. The risks are manageable with tight project management and a clear go/no-go milestone at six months. Delaying or revising for more internal control would defeat the purpose.  

DECISION: GO
