The risks are real: copying, supplier dependence, margin compression, and retail inexperience. Outside software is a dependency, but it also provides instant ecosystem credibility that the company cannot build alone. Retail is unfamiliar, but independent stores already serve this customer base and can be managed with clear terms. Lower margins are acceptable if volume grows and the company learns the market, then can later add proprietary services or upgrades. The separate team shields the core business from disruption while allowing speed. The main threat is not copying—it is being irrelevant in a segment that will define future computing. The company’s strengths in manufacturing, quality, and service can still differentiate even with standard parts. The proposal is risky but timely, and the risks are manageable with careful execution.
