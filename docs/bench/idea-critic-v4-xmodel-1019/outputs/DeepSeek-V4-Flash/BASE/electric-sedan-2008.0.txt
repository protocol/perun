The core bet is a full pivot to battery-electric sedans in 2008, using a bought factory. The capital gap is severe: factory purchase, retooling, and battery pack production will strain cash reserves that are already insufficient per the proposal’s own risk note. There is no mention of secured funding, off-take agreements, or a path to profitability before cash runs out.

Charging infrastructure is nearly nonexistent, limiting early adopters to wealthy, tech-savvy buyers—a thin market for a full-size sedan. Established automakers have scale, dealer networks, and service infrastructure; their likely response would be a competitive EV or aggressive pricing on hybrids, crushing a newcomer’s margin. Mass production experience is critical: a young firm has no proven quality control, supply chain management, or recall handling. A single production flaw or parts shortage could be fatal.

The proposal’s ambition is visionary, but it ignores sequencing. Buying a giant factory immediately locks in high fixed costs before demand is proven. A smarter approach would be a small-batch, high-margin roadster to build brand and learn manufacturing, then scale. But as proposed, the risk of bankruptcy within two years is high. The executive team should not commit all resources to this single, capital-hungry bet without a bridge plan or partnership. The timing is too early for mass-market EV acceptance, and the company’s balance sheet cannot absorb a misstep. Approving now would be reckless.

DECISION: NO-GO
