The paid game’s core assets and cooperative mechanics are proven, but the proposed mode is a direct, late entry into a genre now dominated by a rival. Two months is insufficient to iterate on balance, server stability, and matchmaking—failures here would damage the studio’s reputation and waste the asset reuse advantage. Cannibalization is real: existing paid players may shift to the free title, eroding current revenue without a clear path to replace it. The studio has zero free-to-play operational experience, including cosmetic economies, live service cadence, and anti-cheat at scale. A separate title also splits the player base, weakening both communities. The rival’s popularity is not a temporary spike; it is a structural shift. Entering now with a rushed clone, even with good assets, means competing on the rival’s terms while bearing all the risk of a new business model. The proposal’s monetization via cosmetics is untested for this audience, and the two-month timeline leaves no room for player research or soft-launch learning. The only rational move is to not approve this as proposed. The studio should instead double down on its cooperative paid game’s strengths, add a limited-time mode that leverages existing content without splitting revenue, and study free-to-play economics over a longer horizon—only then consider a separate title. Approving now would likely burn cash, alienate loyal customers, and hand the rival more market share. The risks are not mitigable within the stated constraints; they are fundamental to the plan’s structure.  
DECISION: NO-GO
