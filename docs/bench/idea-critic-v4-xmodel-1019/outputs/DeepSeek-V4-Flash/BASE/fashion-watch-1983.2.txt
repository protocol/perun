The proposal hinges on a fundamental repositioning: from precision instrument to disposable fashion accessory. The plastic material, while a risk to heritage, is precisely what enables low cost and bold design. The twelve-model collection directly addresses the need for variety in fashion, mitigating the risk of single-style failure. The one-million target is aggressive but not impossible, given the low price point and novelty appeal.

The primary threat—reputation damage—is real but manageable if the launch is clearly branded as a separate, playful line, not a replacement for the core product. Established brands cutting prices would dilute their own margins and brand equity, making it a less likely sustained response. The fast-changing fashion cycle is a double-edged sword: it creates high demand but also rapid obsolescence. However, a first-year target can be met by riding the initial wave of curiosity and novelty, especially with strong retail placement and marketing that frames the watch as a "statement piece" rather than a timekeeper.

The key is speed and volume. The low cost of goods allows for aggressive pricing and high sell-through. If the first collection fails, the financial downside is limited due to low production cost per unit. The upside, if it catches on, is massive market share in a new segment. The risk of inaction is greater—letting a competitor define the space. The company’s existing manufacturing precision can ensure the plastic watch is at least reliable enough for its price point, avoiding a quality disaster. The plan is bold, but the economics of fashion novelty favor the first mover with a strong, differentiated product.

DECISION: GO
