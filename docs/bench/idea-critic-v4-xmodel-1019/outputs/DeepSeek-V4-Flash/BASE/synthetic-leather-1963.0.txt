The proposal asks for a heavy marketing spend in 1963 on a premium synthetic leather substitute, despite clear adoption barriers. Shoe manufacturers face retooling costs and process risk for a material that is more expensive than the natural incumbent—leather. Consumer comfort is unproven, and fashion is inherently volatile; a premium price point makes the product vulnerable to style shifts. The company has no stated evidence of factory trials, comfort testing, or buyer commitments. The only strength is durability, but that alone does not justify a premium price or factory conversion. A heavy spend now would likely burn capital before the market validates demand. The prudent path is to first secure pilot partnerships, gather wear-test data, and refine cost or performance positioning—then scale marketing only after proof points exist. Approving the launch as proposed now risks significant loss with no demonstrated pull from manufacturers or end users.  

DECISION: NO-GO
