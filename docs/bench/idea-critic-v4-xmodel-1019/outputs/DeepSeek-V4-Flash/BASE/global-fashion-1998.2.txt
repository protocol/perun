The proposal is ambitious but fatally premature. In 1998, online fashion retail is unproven, especially for young, trend-driven buyers who need to touch, feel, and try on items. Simultaneous multi-country launch multiplies operational complexity—localized logistics, returns, marketing, currency, and legal—without any evidence of demand. Venture capital will fund burn, but with no proven sales model, the money will likely run out before scale economies appear. Delays are almost certain, and each month of delay deepens cash burn. A phased approach—launch in one strong market (e.g., US or UK) with a narrow, high-margin product line—would test demand, refine operations, and build a repeatable model before expanding. The founders’ vision is correct for the long term, but executing it now, as proposed, risks catastrophic failure that would kill the company and the idea. The executive team should not approve this broad, simultaneous rollout. It is not a matter of “if” but “when” and “how” to scale. The proposal lacks a pilot, a clear unit economics test, and a contingency for funding shortfall. Therefore, do not proceed as proposed.  

DECISION: NO-GO
