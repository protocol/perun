Based on the facts given: the carmaker has a new plant that must run near capacity to pay off. The proposal’s dedicated dealer network is costly, and rivals already sell small cars at low prices, squeezing margins. Buyers may defect to cheaper or larger options, making the volume needed for near-capacity production uncertain. The home market is the only stated context, with no evidence of export demand or unique product advantage. The 1100 cc engine is not differentiated. The risk of overcapacity and price competition outweighs the benefit of entering an already crowded segment. Without a clear path to high volume, the plant’s payoff is jeopardized. The proposal does not address how to overcome the cost disadvantage of a new network versus existing rivals, nor how to capture buyers who prefer alternatives. Approving now would commit to a high-risk, low-differentiation launch with no stated mitigation. A revised plan might reduce dealer cost, target a niche, or secure pre-orders, but as proposed, the financial and market risks are not adequately countered.  

DECISION: NO-GO
