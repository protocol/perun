The proposal offers a clear revenue path but hinges on a flawed consent model. The notification step is passive—users must actively decline, and most will ignore it, creating implied consent that feels like a violation when exposed. The risk of sensitive purchase data (health, relationships, finances) becoming public in a feed is severe and immediate. Even with an opt-out, the default is disclosure, which erodes trust at the core of the network. The embarrassment risk for partners is real: a negative public reaction to a user’s purchase could damage their brand, making them reluctant to participate, shrinking the ad inventory. The irreversibility is the killer—once this data flows publicly, it cannot be un-shared, and any backlash will be a permanent reputational scar. The revenue gain is speculative, while the trust loss is certain. A better path would be to start with non-sensitive, opt-in sharing for a small test group, but that is not what is proposed. As written, it prioritizes short-term ad dollars over long-term user retention and partner goodwill. The notification step is not a meaningful safeguard; it is a bureaucratic checkbox that will not prevent the core privacy breach. This will likely trigger regulatory scrutiny and user revolt, costing more than it earns. The proposal needs fundamental redesign, not minor tweaks. Approving it now would be reckless.  

DECISION: NO-GO
