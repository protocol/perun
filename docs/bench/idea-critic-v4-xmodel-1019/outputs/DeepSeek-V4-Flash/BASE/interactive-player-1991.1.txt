The proposal targets a real convergence opportunity in 1991, but the positioning is fatal. At $1,000, it sits in no man’s land: too expensive for a toy, too limited for a PC. The interactive disc library will be sparse, and without a clear killer use case—not music, not photos, not games—consumers will see a pricey, confusing box. PCs are already adding CD-ROM drives and multimedia, eroding the need for a separate device. The company’s core strength is in hardware and media standards, but this proposal asks the market to adopt a new format without a compelling reason to switch from existing VHS, audio CDs, or nascent PC multimedia. The risks are not mitigable by marketing; they are structural. A niche audience of early adopters might buy it, but volume will not reach the scale needed to attract third-party content creators, creating a vicious cycle. The price point also signals premium, but the experience (interactive discs) will feel dated quickly as PC multimedia improves. The team should focus on a lower-priced, simpler device that leverages existing CD audio and adds one clear interactive feature, or wait for PC multimedia to mature and then partner. Approving this now would burn capital and brand credibility. Revise the positioning, price, or target use case before launch.  
DECISION: NO-GO
