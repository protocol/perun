The proposal asks the company to abandon its integrated, proprietary model for a fast, open, low-margin entry into a nascent market. The risks are real: copycats, supplier dependency, thin margins, and no retail channel. However, the core question is not whether the risks exist, but whether the current path is safer. The company’s existing model—building, selling, and servicing everything itself—is heavy and slow, precisely the weakness small-computer makers are exploiting. Waiting a year to perfect a proprietary design would likely cede the market entirely. The proposed approach, while risky, is deliberately small and separate, limiting damage to the core business if it fails. Off-the-shelf parts and outside software shorten time-to-market, and independent retail provides immediate distribution without building a costly network. Lower margins are acceptable if volume grows fast, as the market is expanding. The mainframe business will remain the profit engine. The proposal is not a perfect fit, but it is a calculated, contained bet to participate in a wave that will otherwise pass the company by. The risks are manageable through speed and focus, whereas inaction guarantees obsolescence. The executive team should approve this as a bounded experiment, not a full corporate pivot.  
DECISION: GO
