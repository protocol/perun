The proposal hinges on a single inventor launching a premium-priced product against entrenched incumbents with superior distribution and manufacturing scale. The core risk is not the technology—thousands of prototypes suggest functional viability—but the business model. A higher price without a proven brand or retail channel faces a steep adoption curve. Incumbents can quickly replicate the cyclone design, then use their cost advantages and shelf space to undercut or out-market the newcomer. The lack of manufacturing experience means quality control and unit cost are unverified, likely leading to margin pressure or production delays. While the bagless concept has clear consumer appeal (no recurring purchase), that benefit alone rarely justifies a price premium for a household appliance in a recessionary or value-conscious market. The inventor’s strength is invention, not distribution, pricing strategy, or supply chain management. Without a licensing deal, a joint venture with an established player, or a clear niche (e.g., commercial or industrial buyers) that avoids direct head-to-head competition, the venture would likely burn cash before achieving scale. The proposal offers no mitigation for the identified risks—no pilot program, no pre-orders, no strategic partner. Approving it now would commit resources to a high-failure path. A revised plan that secures a manufacturing partner or a focused market entry (e.g., direct-to-consumer with strong testimonials) would be stronger, but as presented, the execution gap is too wide.  
DECISION: NO-GO
