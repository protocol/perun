The proposal asks for a simultaneous multi-country launch in 1998, targeting a niche demographic with unproven online demand, funded entirely by venture capital. The core risk is execution: multi-country logistics and marketing in an era of nascent e-commerce will burn cash far faster than revenue can scale. Fashion purchases online were not yet a proven behavior, and the target audience, while desirable, is not guaranteed to convert at sufficient volume or speed. The founders’ ambition is grand, but the timeline and geographic breadth create a high probability of running out of capital before the business model is validated. A focused, single-market launch would allow for testing the value proposition, refining operations, and building a repeatable model before expanding. Without that proof, the venture capital will likely be consumed by fixed costs and operational complexity, not by learning what actually sells. The proposal as written is not financially disciplined and carries an unacceptable risk of premature failure. It should not proceed in its current form. A revised plan—starting smaller, proving demand, then scaling—would be a stronger path. The current proposal is a bet on scale before viability, which is inverted logic for a new and unproven market. Therefore, the executive team should not approve this as proposed.  

DECISION: NO-GO
