The proposal to double output despite 4,500 unsold cars, a recession, rising interest rates, and limited cash directly contradicts the core business need: matching supply to demand. With sales at 3,000 against 7,500 produced, inventory is already 150% of sales. Doubling production would add roughly 1,000+ cars per month, flooding a market where buyers are retreating due to recession and high borrowing costs. Dealers, already strained by financing costs, will likely refuse additional stock, pushing the burden onto the company’s own cash reserves—which are explicitly limited. This risks a liquidity crisis, forced discounting, or bankruptcy. The proposal offers no plan to stimulate demand, cut costs, or manage inventory, only a reckless bet on volume. Prudent action would be to halt or reduce production, negotiate dealer terms, and conserve cash until the economy stabilizes. Approving this now would be irresponsible.

DECISION: NO-GO
