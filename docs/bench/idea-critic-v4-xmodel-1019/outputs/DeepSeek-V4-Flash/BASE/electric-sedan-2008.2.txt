The proposal asks for a full strategic pivot to battery-electric-only production, plus acquisition of a large factory, in 2008. The company is young, undercapitalized, and untested in mass manufacturing. The listed risks are severe: capital shortfall for both factory and batteries, minimal charging infrastructure, likely competitive retaliation from established automakers with deeper pockets, and zero proven production experience.  

The timing is also hostile—2008 is a period of economic crisis, oil price volatility, and consumer skepticism toward EVs, which were seen as niche and unproven. The proposal offers no mitigation for any of these risks, no phased approach, no partnership strategy, no revenue forecast, and no contingency if the factory purchase or battery supply fails. Buying a large factory from incumbents does not transfer their manufacturing expertise; it only transfers physical assets and liabilities.  

A young company with limited capital cannot absorb the cost overruns typical of ramping a new vehicle line, especially one with novel powertrain technology. The scarcity of charging stations alone would cap early sales, while incumbents could flood the market with hybrids or cheaper EVs, crushing the newcomer. There is no evidence of a pre-sold order book, supplier contracts, or a viable service network.  

The bold vision is admirable, but the execution plan is reckless. The company should first prove the sedan on a smaller scale, contract manufacturing, or partner with an existing plant, and secure battery supply and infrastructure commitments before committing its entire future to one unproven bet. As presented, it risks immediate insolvency.  

DECISION: NO-GO
