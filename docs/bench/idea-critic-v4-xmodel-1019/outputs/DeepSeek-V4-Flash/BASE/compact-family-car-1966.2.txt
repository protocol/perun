The proposal asks for a major capital commitment in 1966, betting on a new car, a new plant, and a new sales channel simultaneously. The home market already has low-priced rivals, and the dedicated network adds fixed costs before a single sale. The plant’s need for near-capacity output pressures pricing and volume targets, while consumer preference could swing to either cheaper or larger options—both outside this car’s niche. The 1100 cc engine is a middle bet, not a clear advantage. Timing is also poor: the new plant is unproven, and the dealer network will take years to build, during which rivals can adjust. There is no evidence of unique demand, cost advantage, or brand pull that would offset the three simultaneous risks. Approving now would lock in high fixed costs with uncertain demand. A smaller pilot, a shared network, or a phased plant ramp could reduce exposure, but none are in the proposal. The executive team should reject this as presented because the risk profile is not justified by the facts given. The car may be viable, but the full package is not.  
DECISION: NO-GO
