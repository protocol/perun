**Steelman:** Transforming from a pure retailer into a hybrid marketplace creates immediate inventory depth and variety without capital expenditure, turning fixed inventory risk into variable commission revenue while capturing transactions even when the retailer cannot offer the lowest price.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is brand erosion: if third-party sellers ship poor-quality used items or counterfeit goods under the retailer's trusted product pages, customer trust collapses, driving long-term churn that outweighs short-term fee income. The proposal lacks a verification mechanism for seller quality or a clear policy distinguishing retailer-fulfilled vs. seller-fulfilled items to the customer.

Alternatives include:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain full control over quality and margins but lose sales to competitors offering lower prices or better availability.
2.  **Separate Storefront:** Host third-party sellers on a distinct section of the site to isolate brand risk, though this reduces the "single page" convenience and likely conversion rates.
3.  **Strict Vetting:** Allow only pre-approved sellers with high barriers to entry, slowing growth but mitigating trust risks.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
Given the company is not yet profitable, the pressure to generate immediate cash flow via fees is high, potentially leading to lax enforcement of seller standards. In a pre-mortem scenario one year later: the site is flooded with counterfeit books and damaged used goods; major publishers boycott the platform due to channel conflict with their own distribution; customers associate the brand with unreliability. The "one-way door" aspect is significant; once trust is broken and suppliers alienated, rebuilding the exclusive retail model is nearly impossible.

**Kill Criteria & Evidence:**
The proposal offers no specific metrics for seller performance, no cap on the percentage of third-party listings per page, and no stated recourse for customer disputes involving external sellers. Without a "go-if" condition requiring a pilot program with strict quality gates and a defined rollback plan for supplier backlash, the risk of irreversible brand damage is too high for an unprofitable company to absorb. The absence of a concrete trust-and-safety framework renders the plan incomplete.

DECISION: NO-GO
