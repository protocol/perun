The claim assumes US demand exists for small, non-traditional motorcycles sold outside conventional channels. The steelman: a latent, unserved segment of commuters and students wants affordable, approachable transport, accessible via general retail rather than intimidating specialty dealers.

**Skeptic:** The premise that Americans favor only large machines may be a false generalization; however, the brand's unknown status and potential mechanical failure of larger models at US highway speeds are verified risks based on the proposal's own data. Relying on unproven distribution channels (sporting-goods stores) introduces execution risk if those partners lack motorcycle expertise.

**Better-way:** Alternatives include licensing the technology to an established US manufacturer to avoid currency drain, or targeting only coastal urban centers first to validate demand before a full subsidiary launch. Doing nothing preserves capital but cedes potential first-mover advantage in a nascent niche.

**Kill-criteria:** If the small bikes fail to achieve a minimum sales velocity in pilot cities within six months, or if mechanical failure rates on larger models exceed 5% during initial highway testing, the expansion must halt. The "do nothing" option remains viable if foreign currency reserves drop below a critical threshold defined by the board.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs because the company insists on selling larger models that break down, tarnishing the brand before the small bikes gain traction, or because sporting-goods stores fail to provide adequate after-sales support, leading to customer dissatisfaction.

**Decision:** The proposal addresses a specific, underserved niche with a differentiated product and novel distribution, directly countering the "big bike" orthodoxy. While risks regarding currency and mechanical suitability exist, they are manageable through phased rollout and strict monitoring of the larger models. The potential to create a new market category outweighs the preservation of the status quo.

DECISION: GO
