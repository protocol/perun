**Steelman:** Leveraging mobile dominance to disrupt handheld gaming via a hybrid device at $300, using memory cards for distribution.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Usability Failure:** Removing the battery to swap games is a catastrophic user experience flaw. It renders the device unusable for its primary function (calling) during gameplay interruptions and risks data loss. No competitor forces this friction.
2.  **Ergonomics:** A device "awkward to hold for calls" fails the core mobile value proposition. Users will reject a phone that hurts to use, regardless of gaming capability.
3.  **Market Reality:** At $300, the price is significantly higher than the market leader's dedicated console, which already possesses a massive library. Without publisher commitment, the content gap is unbridgeable. Consumers will not pay a premium for an inferior, awkward hybrid with fewer games.
4.  **Better Way:** Do nothing. The risk of brand damage from a clumsy, expensive failure outweighs the potential market share gain. Alternatively, partner with the existing leader rather than competing directly with an unproven, flawed hardware form factor.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, reviewers mock the battery-swap mechanic, gamers stick to the cheaper, comfortable leader with better games, and the mobile brand suffers reputational harm for releasing an unpolished gadget.

**Fit:** This does not move the north star of mobile dominance; it dilutes it by entering a specialized market with a compromised tool. The "hybrid" advantage is currently a disadvantage in both categories.

**Kill Criteria:** If the device cannot swap games without power loss or costs >20% more than the dedicated leader without a superior library, it must not launch.

**Verdict:** The proposal contains fundamental design flaws (battery removal) and strategic misalignments (price vs. library) that guarantee failure. The friction introduced destroys the value of both functions.

DECISION: NO-GO
