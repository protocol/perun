The claim is that a front-wheel-drive, compact-platform van will save a cash-poor carmaker by creating a new consumer category. The steelman: this vehicle uniquely combines car-like handling and garage fitment to capture families abandoning wagons, leveraging existing assets to minimize capital risk.

However, critical failures loom. First, the "no such category exists" risk is a feature, not a bug, only if marketing successfully reframes the vehicle from "commercial van" to "family hauler." Without a massive, costly education campaign—which the cash-strapped company cannot afford—buyers will reject it as a work truck. Second, reusing a compact-car platform creates a hard ceiling on interior volume; if the third row or cargo space feels cramped compared to full-size vans, the value proposition collapses. Third, rivals can copy the design quickly once the market is proven, eroding first-mover advantage before the company recoups its conversion costs.

The "do nothing" alternative preserves remaining cash but guarantees continued decline as wagon sales fade. A cheaper path might be modifying an existing wagon into a larger "utility vehicle" rather than retooling a factory for a completely new body style, though this sacrifices the garage-fit differentiator.

The pre-mortem reveals the most likely failure mode: the company spends its last reserves on retooling and launch, but the market perceives the vehicle as a small commercial van. Sales remain flat, the company runs out of cash, and the government guarantee is called. The kill criterion is clear: if pre-order reservations from non-commercial buyers do not exceed 5,000 units within 60 days of unveiling, the project must halt to preserve liquidity for core models.

Given the existential cash constraint and the high probability of category confusion without a marketing war chest, proceeding "as proposed" is reckless. The plan lacks a verified, low-cost method to validate consumer perception before committing to factory conversion. The risk of total collapse outweighs the potential reward in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
