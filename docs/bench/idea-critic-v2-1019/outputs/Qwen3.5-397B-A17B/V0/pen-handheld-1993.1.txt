The claim is that a $700 pen-based handheld with unreliable handwriting recognition will successfully create and lead a new market category following heavy CEO publicity.

Steelman: A bold, first-mover entry defines the PDA category before competitors, leveraging CEO visibility to overcome early technical flaws and high costs, betting on network effects from software developers once a user base is established.

However, the failure hypothesis is immediate and catastrophic. The core value proposition (handwriting input) is admitted as "unreliable in testing." Releasing a primary input method that does not work reliably guarantees user frustration and returns, regardless of marketing spend. The price point ($700) is prohibitive for an unproven category with "little software," creating a vacuum where early adopters pay a premium for a broken experience. The device being "too big for a pocket" violates the fundamental utility of a "handheld" or "personal" device, limiting its use cases severely.

Alternatives:
1. **Do nothing:** Wait for recognition technology to mature and component costs to drop, avoiding brand damage from a flawed flagship.
2. **Pilot launch:** Release a limited, lower-cost developer kit to build the software ecosystem without exposing the mass market to reliability issues.
3. **Hybrid input:** Launch with a keyboard or button-based primary input, treating pen as secondary until reliable.

The proposal fails the "fit" test: a device that is too large, too expensive, and functionally unreliable cannot move the north star of "personal utility." The pre-mortem is clear: the product launches, reviews destroy its reputation due to input failures, the software ecosystem never materializes because the user base is tiny, and the company loses millions while ceding the eventual mature market to a competitor who waited for technical viability.

There is no kill criterion that saves this; the flaws are inherent to the launch configuration. The "heavy publicity" amplifies the failure, not the success. Without verified reliability, lower cost, or pocketable form factor, this is a bet on hope, not evidence.

DECISION: NO-GO
