**Steelman:** Transforming from a pure retailer into a hybrid marketplace leverages existing traffic to generate high-margin fee revenue without inventory risk, essential for reaching profitability.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is brand erosion: if third-party sellers provide poor service or sell counterfeit goods, customers blame the platform, not the seller. Additionally, aggressive undercutting by competitors on the same page could destroy the retailer's own sales volume, turning their infrastructure into a loss-leading utility for rivals. Suppliers may retaliate by withholding stock if they perceive used goods as cannibalizing new sales.

Alternatives include:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain full control and margin but bear all inventory risk and capital costs, delaying profitability.
2.  **Affiliate Model:** Link to external sellers without hosting them on-site; lower risk but significantly lower revenue per transaction and fragmented user experience.
3.  **Restricted Marketplace:** Allow only non-competitors or verified partners; reduces competitive undercutting but limits inventory breadth and fee potential.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
Given the urgent need for profitability and the "growing range of goods" straining capital, the marketplace model aligns with a capital-light strategy. However, assuming this can be launched in 2000 without robust seller vetting, escrow payment systems, or a dedicated trust-and-safety team is fatal. A pre-mortem reveals that without strict performance metrics and immediate suspension capabilities for bad actors, a few high-profile fraud cases could permanently destroy customer trust, making the site a "flea market" rather than a premium destination.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal lacks critical guardrails. It does not specify how service quality will be enforced, how supplier conflicts will be managed, or what criteria will trigger seller removal. Launching "as proposed" without these operational mechanisms invites the very risks identified. The potential for irreversible brand damage outweighs the immediate fee revenue opportunity unless strict controls are defined *before* approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
