**Steelman:** A cash-strapped carmaker can survive by creating a new "minivan" category using existing compact-car parts and a converted factory, offering car-like driving in a garage-friendly package before rivals react.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary risk is financial collapse if the new category fails to gain immediate traction. However, the "do nothing" alternative guarantees continued decline in the shrinking station wagon market. The "commercial van" perception risk is mitigated by marketing the vehicle strictly as a family hauler with car amenities, distinguishing it from work trucks.

Regarding the platform constraint: reusing the compact-car chassis is not a limitation but the core economic advantage. It enables the low capital expenditure required given the company's lack of cash. A clean-sheet design would bankrupt the firm before production. The garage-fit requirement directly addresses a specific pain point for families that full-size vans ignore, creating a defensible niche despite the lack of an existing category label.

Rival copying is inevitable but slow; retooling for a unique size class takes competitors years. By then, brand loyalty and production scale should be established. The converted factory reduces upfront tooling costs, aligning with the cash constraint.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because marketing positioned it as a cheap commercial van rather than a premium family vehicle, or because the ride quality felt too truck-like. The proposal addresses the latter via the car platform; the former is a marketing execution risk, not a product flaw.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders do not cover the conversion costs within six months of announcement, halt production tooling. The leading metric is the ratio of family-oriented feature inquiries versus commercial fleet inquiries during the launch window.

**Verdict:** The proposal turns the company's greatest weakness (lack of cash) into a strategic necessity that forces a lean, innovative solution. The market gap is real, the technical approach is financially viable, and the timing is critical to preempt competitors. The risks are manageable compared to the certainty of stagnation.

DECISION: GO
