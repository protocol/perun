The claim is that a $180 tabletop stereoscopic 3D console with red monochrome displays is a viable bridge product between handheld and home generations. The steelman posits that unique 3D gameplay justifies the hardware despite ergonomic and visual compromises, capturing market share before the next home console arrives.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws. First, the value proposition is inverted: the device sacrifices portability (requiring a tabletop) while failing to deliver comfort (eyepiece usage causes strain/headaches), alienating both handheld and home audiences. Second, the "red-on-black" limitation severely restricts game design and appeal, evidenced by the admitted lack of launch titles; without software, hardware is useless. Third, the timing is disastrous: with a true home console due in two years, this product risks cannibalizing brand excitement or becoming an embarrassing footnote rather than a profitable bridge. The cost of delay is low compared to the reputational risk of launching a physically uncomfortable, visually limited device.

The "do nothing" option preserves capital and brand equity. A "better way" would be to accelerate software development for the existing handheld or wait for the next home generation. The pre-mortem shows this product failing due to consumer rejection of physical discomfort and poor visuals, leading to massive inventory write-downs. There is no observable metric where this specific configuration (red monochrome, tabletop, eyepiece) succeeds against modern ergonomic standards or consumer expectations for immersion. The kill criterion is clear: if the core mechanism causes physical pain (headaches/strain) and limits visual fidelity to one color, the product cannot sustain engagement.

This proposal fails the fit check against any reasonable north star of user satisfaction or long-term brand health. It solves no urgent user problem and introduces significant friction. The risks are not mitigated by the price point; $180 is too high for a niche, uncomfortable novelty with no games.

DECISION: NO-GO
