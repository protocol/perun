**Steelman:** Removing the headphone jack forces a transition to wireless audio, creating a cohesive ecosystem lock-in via proprietary chips and justifying a premium accessory ($159) that solves the immediate connectivity gap while signaling future innovation.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is consumer backlash over losing a universal standard, perceived as greed rather than progress. At $159, the price is ~6x the cost of traditional wired earbuds, creating a high barrier to entry. The "odd" design and lack of tethering increase loss risk, potentially driving negative press and returns. Battery life (5 hours) is insufficient for long-haul travel without the case, creating friction.

Alternatives considered:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Keep the jack, launch earbuds later. *Risk:* Delays ecosystem shift, misses first-mover narrative.
2.  **Include Adapter Only:** Remove jack, sell earbuds separately later. *Risk:* Fragmented user experience, missed revenue window.
3.  **Lower Price Point:** Launch at $99. *Risk:* Margins too thin to subsidize R&D for the proprietary chip; devalues the brand.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with a vision of a port-less, wireless future. However, if the market perceives the removal as forced obsolescence without a seamless replacement, sales could dip, and brand trust may erode. The "one-way door" aspect is significant: once the jack is removed, returning to it admits defeat. The cost of delay is losing the narrative lead to competitors.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:**
No external data is available, but historical precedents suggest early adopters tolerate friction for design leaps. The claim hinges on the proprietary chip providing a pairing experience so superior it offsets the price and form factor risks.

**Decision:**
The proposal is a high-risk, high-reward strategic pivot. While the risks of ridicule and anger are real, they are manageable through marketing that frames the change as courageous innovation. The proprietary integration offers a defensible moat. The price is justified by the R&D and ecosystem value.

DECISION: GO
