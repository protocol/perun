The claim that launching the "cheapest car in the world" at $2,500 will succeed relies on three fragile assumptions: that price alone overrides safety concerns, that the "cheapest" label attracts rather than repels aspirational buyers, and that land protests will not cripple the dedicated factory's volume targets.

Steelman: A ultra-low-cost vehicle unlocks a massive market of scooter families if it delivers acceptable safety and avoids stigma.

However, the "cheapest" marketing angle is a critical vulnerability. In automotive markets, low price often signals dangerous inferiority, especially for family transport. If buyers perceive the car as a "death trap" compared to used alternatives, volume targets will fail regardless of price. Furthermore, a dedicated factory facing active farmer protests introduces severe execution risk; delays here destroy the high-volume economics required to sustain the $2,500 price point. Used cars and scooters remain functional, safer, and immediately available substitutes without the social stigma or supply chain fragility.

The proposal lacks a contingency for land acquisition failure or a strategy to pivot branding from "cheapest" to "smart/safe entry." Without verified safety data to counter quality doubts and a resolved land dispute, the risk of a catastrophic launch failure—where the factory is delayed and the product is rejected as unsafe—is unacceptably high. The cost of delay is lower than the cost of a failed mass-market launch that damages the brand permanently.

Strongest attack survived: The argument that no one else can hit this price point was tested against the reality of used car markets; however, the unique value of a *new* warranty and modern design for scooter upgraders remains a valid differentiator, provided safety is proven. Yet, the current plan markets stigma ("cheapest") alongside unproven safety amidst operational chaos (protests).

The missing kill criterion is a verified safety rating and a resolved land agreement before full-scale production commitment. Proceeding now ignores these existential blockers.

DECISION: NO-GO
