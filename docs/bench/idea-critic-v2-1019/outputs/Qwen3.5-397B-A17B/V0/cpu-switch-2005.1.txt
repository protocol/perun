The proposal to shift from a lagging, inefficient proprietary architecture to the dominant industry standard addresses an existential threat: the inability to ship competitive thin laptops. The steelman is that without this pivot, the product line becomes obsolete due to thermal and performance constraints, regardless of brand distinctiveness.

However, the risks are severe and specific. Announcing the transition two years in advance creates a " Osborne effect," likely freezing sales of current inventory immediately. Relying on a translation layer introduces performance penalties that could alienate power users, while the requirement for developers to rebuild software risks a temporary ecosystem collapse. The memory of a painful prior transition suggests organizational fatigue and potential execution failure.

Critically, the proposal lacks a phased mitigation strategy. A hard "whole line" switch within two years ignores the possibility of a hybrid portfolio during the interim. There is no evidence provided that the translation layer can achieve near-native performance for key applications, nor is there a plan to incentivize developers to rebuild before the hardware ships. The risk of weakening brand distinctiveness is real if the resulting machines are indistinguishable from generic competitors, yet no counter-strategy for differentiation is offered.

The "do nothing" alternative leads to stagnation, but the proposed "all-in" approach risks catastrophic revenue drop-off during the transition window. The failure mode is clear: sales freeze upon announcement, translated performance disappoints, and the developer ecosystem lags, leaving the company with neither its old advantage nor a functional new one. Without a concrete plan to manage the sales cliff, guarantee translation performance benchmarks, or secure developer commitment prior to announcement, the current plan is too brittle. The timeline and scope require revision to include a staggered rollout and verified performance metrics before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
