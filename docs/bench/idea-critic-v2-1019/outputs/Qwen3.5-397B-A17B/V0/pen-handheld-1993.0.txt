The claim is that a $700 pen-based handheld with unreliable handwriting recognition will successfully create and lead a new market category following heavy CEO publicity.

The steelman: A visionary device can define a nascent category despite technical immaturity if the brand signal is strong enough to attract early adopters willing to tolerate flaws for novelty.

However, the failure hypothesis is concrete and unaddressed: core utility (handwriting) is broken, price is prohibitive, and form factor contradicts the "personal" promise. History shows that launching a premium device with a broken primary input mechanism destroys brand credibility rather than building a category. The "heavy publicity" amplifies the disappointment when the product fails to perform basic tasks reliably.

Alternatives exist:
1. Do nothing: Wait for recognition accuracy to improve and component costs to drop, avoiding a high-profile failure.
2. License the tech: Let smaller players fail first while learning from their mistakes.
3. Pivot: Launch as a specialized enterprise tool where price is less sensitive and use cases are constrained, rather than a mass-market PDA.

The proposal fails the "what would have to be true" test. For this to succeed, handwriting recognition must work "well enough" for daily use immediately, or users must accept a $700 paperweight. Current evidence states recognition is unreliable. There is no software ecosystem to justify the hardware cost. The size prevents the "always-with-you" utility required for adoption.

Pre-mortem: The device launches with great fanfare, reviewers demonstrate failed transcription within minutes, sales stall due to price and poor utility, the category is declared dead for a decade, and the company loses millions while competitors learn what not to do.

There is no kill criterion or pivot plan mentioned, only a bet on publicity. The risk of defining the category as "broken and expensive" is irreversible. The cost of delay (waiting 12-18 months for better tech) is lower than the cost of a failed flagship launch that poisons the well.

DECISION: NO-GO
