The claim is that a $700 pen-based PDA with unreliable handwriting recognition will successfully create a new handheld category via CEO-led publicity. The steelman: first-mover advantage in an unproven market justifies the premium price and technical risk, betting that marketing can override current product flaws to establish a category standard.

However, the failure hypothesis is concrete and fatal: launching a core feature (handwriting) known to be unreliable in testing will destroy credibility immediately, regardless of publicity. At $700 (a significant premium for 1993), early adopters expect polish, not beta-quality input methods. The "too big for a pocket" constraint violates the fundamental utility of a "handheld" device, while "little software" ensures low retention even if units sell initially.

Alternatives analysis:
1. **Do nothing:** Wait for technology to mature (smaller form factor, reliable recognition). Cost: lost first-mover hype. Risk: competitors enter later with better products.
2. **Cheaper path:** Launch a keyboard-based organizer or a sync-only companion device at a lower price point ($300-$400) to build the ecosystem before attempting pen input.
3. **Pivot:** Delay launch until recognition accuracy meets a strict threshold, using the interim to shrink the chassis.

The cost of delay is likely lower than the cost of a high-profile failure. A botched launch by a major computer maker creates a "category killer" reputation for the *concept* itself, poisoning the well for future iterations (a "one-way door" brand damage). The proposed kill criteria are absent; there is no metric for recognition accuracy or form-factor acceptance before the go decision. The reliance on CEO publicity cannot fix a broken core mechanism. The evidence provided (unreliable testing, high price, poor form factor) directly contradicts the requirements for a successful premium consumer launch.

This proposal bets the category's viability on a flawed execution. The risks are not merely operational but existential to the product line's reputation. Without a working core feature, the marketing push will accelerate negative word-of-mouth.

DECISION: NO-GO
