The claim is that a $180 tabletop stereoscopic 3D console with red monochrome displays will successfully bridge the gap between handheld and home console generations. The steelman posits that this device offers a novel, affordable 3D experience that leverages existing brand loyalty to maintain market presence during a hardware transition.

However, the attack on this claim reveals fatal flaws in value proposition and user experience. The "better-way" analysis shows that "doing nothing" preserves capital for the upcoming home console, while a standard handheld upgrade offers proven portability without the physical discomfort of an eyepiece. The proposed device fails the "fit" test: it is neither truly portable nor a compelling home experience, alienating users who value comfort.

The pre-mortem indicates a high probability of market rejection due to the red-on-black visuals causing eye strain and headaches, a known physiological barrier. With few launch games, the hardware lacks immediate utility. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the reputational damage of releasing a defective or uncomfortable product. The evidence suggests the technical constraints (monochrome display, eyepiece requirement) directly contradict the core user needs of comfort and visual fidelity.

The strongest attack survived is the argument for novelty; however, novelty without usability is a gimmick, not a product strategy. The risk of brand erosion outweighs the potential revenue from a niche experiment. The kill criteria are clear: if the device causes physical discomfort or lacks a robust game library, it fails. Since these conditions are inherent to the current proposal's design and launch plan, the project cannot proceed as stated.

This proposal represents a high-risk investment in a compromised format that threatens the company's standing before its next major home console release. The constraints are not merely challenges to overcome but fundamental defects in the concept itself.

DECISION: NO-GO
