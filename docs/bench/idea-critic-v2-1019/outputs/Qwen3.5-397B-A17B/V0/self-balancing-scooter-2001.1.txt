The claim is that launching a $5,000 self-balancing transporter with 40,000-unit capacity in 2001 is viable. The steelman: this creates a new micro-mobility category where no substitute exists, leveraging brand hype to secure early adopters despite regulatory ambiguity.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current plan. First, the regulatory environment is undefined; selling a vehicle illegal for sidewalk or road use renders the core utility valueless immediately upon purchase, creating massive liability and consumer backlash. Second, the price point ($5,000 in 2001) exceeds most consumer vehicles' down payments, restricting the market to novelty seekers rather than commuters, contradicting the "personal transporter" vision. Third, the factory capacity (40,000 units) assumes demand that has not been proven outside institutional niches; if consumer adoption lags due to cost or social stigma ("conspicuous riders"), the fixed costs of an oversized factory will bankrupt the venture before product-market fit is found.

Better ways exist: a limited pilot program with institutional partners (police, campuses) where legal status can be negotiated and utility proven, coupled with a smaller, flexible manufacturing setup to avoid catastrophic overcapacity. The "do nothing" option preserves capital until regulations clarify.

The pre-mortem shows failure caused by inventory glut and regulatory bans. This is a one-way door; building the factory commits irreversible capital. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of building a factory for a product that may be illegal to operate.

No evidence supports the assumption that consumers will pay $5,000 for an unproven, legally ambiguous device. The kill criterion is clear: without confirmed legal pathways in major markets and pre-orders covering 20% of capacity, the launch must not proceed. The proposal lacks these validations. The risk of total capital loss due to regulatory shutdown or inventory obsolescence is too high to approve the full factory build and wide launch now.

DECISION: NO-GO
