The claim is that acquiring this specific space-fantasy franchise for $4 billion will successfully synergize theme parks, consumer products, and film studios despite creative leadership loss and recent mixed performance.

**Steelman:** The purchase secures a dormant but globally recognized IP with deep lore, allowing immediate exploitation across underutilized park zones and high-margin merchandise, where the $4 billion cost is amortized over decades of multi-platform revenue rather than just box office returns.

**Attack:** The valuation relies on future potential while ignoring three immediate failure modes: (1) The "creator vacuum" often alienates the core fanbase, causing the new trilogy to flop and dragging down spin-off viability; (2) Existing licensing agreements may legally cap near-term merchandise revenue, delaying ROI and straining the $4 billion cash/stock outlay; (3) Mixed recent reception suggests brand fatigue, meaning the "synergy" assumes a revival that has no guaranteed catalyst. The proposal treats the IP as a static asset rather than a fragile cultural sentiment that could turn toxic if the new films fail.

**Better Way:** Instead of a full $4 billion acquisition, negotiate a limited partnership for the trilogy first. This tests audience reception and creative viability without assuming the massive balance sheet risk or the operational burden of integrating a retiring founder's studio. If the trilogy succeeds, exercise an option to buy; if it fails, the company avoids a billions-dollar mistake.

**Kill Criteria:** Proceed only if legal due diligence confirms that at least 40% of high-margin merchandise rights can be reclaimed within 18 months. Without this, the theme park and product synergies are theoretical, leaving the company with an overpriced film library and no lever to drive the broader business strategy. The current proposal lacks this verification and assumes synergy where legal barriers may exist.

The risk of paying a premium for a declining brand without a guaranteed creative successor is too high given the unverified status of licensing constraints.

DECISION: NO-GO
