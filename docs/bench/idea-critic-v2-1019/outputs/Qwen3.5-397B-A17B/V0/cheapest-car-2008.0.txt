The claim that launching the world's cheapest car at $2,500 will succeed relies on three fragile assumptions: that land protests will not delay the dedicated factory, that extreme cost-cutting will not trigger fatal safety reputational damage, and that families prefer a new "cheap" car over safer used alternatives or scooters.

Steelman: A dedicated high-volume factory enables a price point low enough to convert millions of scooter owners, creating a new market segment.

However, the "cheapest" positioning is a double-edged sword. In automotive markets, low price often signals low safety. If early units suffer quality failures, the brand becomes synonymous with "death trap," destroying demand permanently. Unlike software, hardware recalls are capital-intensive and brand-lethal. Furthermore, the dedicated factory is a one-way door with massive fixed costs; if volume targets miss due to land protests or slow adoption, the company faces insolvency without a flexible fallback.

Better ways exist. Contract manufacturing with existing partners could launch the car without the political risk of new land acquisition and without the burden of building a greenfield factory. This reduces upfront capital and allows pivoting if demand is softer than projected. Additionally, marketing should focus on "family safety upgrade" rather than "cheapest," decoupling the value proposition from the stigma of poverty.

The strongest attack survived is the price barrier: no alternative currently matches the $2,500 new-car price point, suggesting a genuine gap. Yet, the risk of the "cheap" label outweighs the benefit if not managed by a robust quality narrative and a flexible production strategy. The current proposal locks the company into a high-risk, single-path strategy with no margin for error on land acquisition or public perception.

Without a contingency for the factory delays or a rebranding strategy to mitigate safety concerns, the proposal exposes the firm to existential risk. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
