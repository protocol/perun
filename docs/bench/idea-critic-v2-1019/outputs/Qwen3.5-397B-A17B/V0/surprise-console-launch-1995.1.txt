The claim is that launching four months early in May 1995 at $399 with limited inventory and software will secure a market advantage over a rival launching in September. The steelman is that capturing early adopter mindshare and shelf space before the competitor arrives creates an insurmountable lead that outweighs the costs of alienating retailers and developers.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in this specific context. First, the "first mover" advantage relies on a compelling product; a console with "few games ready" and a reputation for being "hard to program for 3D" offers no software hook to justify the $100 price premium over the upcoming rival. Early adopters buy games, not hardware shells. Second, the strategy explicitly trades long-term channel health for short-term visibility. Alienating major retailers by excluding them from the initial shipment invites retaliation just as the holiday season approaches, potentially leaving the console shelf-less when the competitor launches with full support. Third, surprising developers with a rushed deadline without adequate tools ensures a barren library for the critical Q4 launch window, ceding the software war to the rival.

The pre-mortem is clear: by September, the company is known as the expensive, hard-to-find console with no games, while the competitor launches at a lower price point with full retailer backing and a robust library. The cost of delay (waiting until September) is zero compared to the cost of a botched early launch, which permanently damages brand reputation and retailer relationships. There is no reversible path once retailers are antagonized and developers are burned. The proposal fails the "fit" test against the north star of sustainable market penetration; it prioritizes a PR stunt over product readiness.

The strongest attack survived is the fear of competitor timing, but this is negated by the reality that being first with an inferior offering is worse than being second with a superior one. The evidence suggests this move sacrifices the entire holiday season for a four-month head start with nothing to sell.

DECISION: NO-GO
