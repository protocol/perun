Steelman: A mid-calorie cola captures young men seeking reduced sugar without the "diet" stigma, leveraging a premium price to signal quality before the low-carb trend fades.

However, the attack on this claim reveals fatal flaws in the current proposal. First, the "better-way" analysis shows the product fails the core value test: it satisfies neither the taste-seeking full-sugar drinkers nor the health-conscious diet drinkers, creating a "middle child" syndrome with no loyal base. Second, the premium pricing directly contradicts the goal of driving trial among young men, who are typically price-sensitive, especially for an unproven flavor profile. Third, the market timing is precarious; if the low-carb trend is a fad, the product launches just as demand collapses, while a rival's similar entry suggests a crowded, potentially doomed niche.

The pre-mortem indicates the most likely failure mode is rapid shelf rejection: retailers delist the SKU within six months due to poor repeat purchase rates, leaving the company with high campaign sunk costs and inventory write-offs. The proposal lacks a specific kill criterion or a measured pilot plan to validate the "young male" demographic's willingness to pay a premium for half-sugar. Without evidence that this specific segment exists and differs from existing diet buyers, the launch is a speculative bet against established consumer behavior.

The strongest attack survived is the potential for a new category creation, but only if the "diet" stigma is proven to be the sole barrier for young men. The current proposal assumes this without data. The risk of cannibalizing existing full-sugar sales without gaining new diet switchers is high.

Given the lack of validation for the target demographic's price sensitivity, the high risk of the "middle child" failure mode, and the potential for the underlying trend to be transient, the proposal as stated is too risky to approve. It requires a revised strategy focusing on a limited market test with clear metrics for repeat purchase before a national campaign.

DECISION: NO-GO
