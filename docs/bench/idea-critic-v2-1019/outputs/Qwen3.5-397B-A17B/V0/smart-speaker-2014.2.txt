The claim assumes customers will trade privacy for convenience and that voice technology is ready for a standalone home device, despite recent hardware failure and market skepticism.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, always-listening speaker at an aggressive price point ($99/$199) creates a new "smart home" category, leveraging existing retail relationships to bypass the poor phone reception, provided the voice accuracy is sufficient to justify the always-on microphone.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Privacy & Trust:** The "always-on" feature is a critical failure point. In 2014, consumers are not accustomed to listening devices in private spaces. Without a clear, physical mute switch and transparent data policies, adoption will stall.
2.  **Technology Readiness:** Voice recognition failure rates are high. If the device frequently misunderstands commands, it becomes a frustrating novelty rather than a utility, repeating the smartphone's failure pattern.
3.  **Category Risk:** Creating a new category requires massive education spend. Phone-based assistants already exist; the value prop of a stationary, single-purpose device must be overwhelmingly superior to overcome inertia.
4.  **Hardware Stigma:** The recent poor smartphone sales indicate execution risks in supply chain, quality, or market fit. Launching another complex hardware product immediately compounds this reputational risk.

**Better Ways:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for voice tech to mature and privacy concerns to ease.
*   **Software First:** Integrate the assistant deeply into existing mobile apps or partner with established hardware makers to test the waters without manufacturing risk.
*   **Pivot:** Launch a non-always-on Bluetooth speaker with limited voice features to build trust before the "always-listening" version.

**Kill Criteria & Pre-mortem:**
Assume failure: The device sits unused because users fear eavesdropping or find voice commands unreliable.
*   **Door:** Two-way (can discontinue), but brand damage is one-way.
*   **Delay Cost:** Waiting 12-18 months allows competitors to define the category and tech to improve, reducing execution risk.
*   **Metric:** Pre-order conversion rate > 15% and return rate < 5% in the first month.

The proposal ignores the fundamental trust barrier and technical immaturity of 2014. The risk of compounding the recent hardware failure with a controversial, unproven category is too high without a phased approach or significant technological guarantees not present in the brief.

DECISION: NO-GO
