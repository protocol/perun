**Steelman:** A cash-strapped carmaker can survive by creating a new "minivan" category using existing compact-car parts and a converted factory, offering car-like driving in a garage-friendly package before rivals react.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker claim is "no such vehicle category exists." Historically, unmet demand often signals opportunity, not impossibility. Families currently compromise between unwieldy vans and small wagons; a garage-fit vehicle solves a verified pain point.

The financial constraint ("little cash") is directly addressed by the proposal's specific tactics: reusing the compact-car platform and converting an existing factory. This minimizes capital expenditure compared to all-new development. While reusing a platform risks space limitations, the "front-wheel-drive" configuration maximizes interior volume relative to exterior footprint, directly countering the space objection better than rear-wheel-drive rivals.

The risk of buyers perceiving it as a "commercial van" is a marketing challenge, not a product flaw, solvable by positioning and trim levels rather than engineering changes. Rival copying is inevitable but slow; being first to define the segment grants a crucial head start to establish brand dominance while the company recovers.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because the company marketed it as a utility vehicle instead of a family hauler, or because the conversion costs exceeded the lean budget. However, the proposal explicitly targets the family demographic ("families buy station wagons") and leverages low-cost assets.

**Verdict:**
There are no factual blockers. The risks (category creation, perception, competition) are inherent to innovation but are mitigated by the low-cost execution strategy. The alternative ("do nothing") guarantees continued decline given the company's near-collapse status. The proposal turns constraints (low cash, existing platform) into the very mechanism for survival. It beats "do nothing" and offers the only plausible path to creating a new revenue stream without massive capital outlay.

**Kill Criteria:** If prototype testing shows the compact platform cannot accommodate three rows or standard garage doors, stop. If pre-orders from family-focused demographics fall below 20% of projections, pivot.

DECISION: GO
