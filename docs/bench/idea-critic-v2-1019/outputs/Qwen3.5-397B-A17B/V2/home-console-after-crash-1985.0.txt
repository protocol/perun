If retailers view the underlying technology as identical to failed consoles, the rebrand fails. However, the 1983 crash was driven by market saturation of low-quality software; a lockout chip directly addresses the root cause of lost consumer confidence, not just the symptom. The proposed bundle (robot/light gun) creates a unique value proposition distinct from "just another console," making it superior to a standard relaunch.

**Kill-Criteria:** The plan fails if the buy-back promise triggers massive returns due to zero consumer demand, or if developers bypass the lockout chip en masse, restoring the "shovelware" environment. However, these are risks, not current blockers. The proposal explicitly accounts for the primary blocker (retailer refusal) via the buy-back clause and rebranding strategy. The "passed fad" narrative is countered by the Japanese market success, proving the technology's viability when managed correctly.
