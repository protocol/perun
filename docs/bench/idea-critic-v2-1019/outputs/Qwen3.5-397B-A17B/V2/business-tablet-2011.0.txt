**Steelman:** Launch a niche, phone-tethered 7-inch tablet on an unproven OS to test a new ecosystem without cannibalizing the core business email franchise.

**Attack:**
1.  **Value Mismatch:** The company's brand equity is "business email." Shipping a device explicitly *without* built-in email, calendar, or contacts destroys the primary purchase trigger for its existing customer base.
2.  **Pricing Failure:** Matching the price of a rival's larger, established tablet with a full app store creates an immediate, unwinnable comparison. The proposed device offers less screen real estate, fewer apps, and missing core functionality at the same cost.
3.  **Ecosystem Trap:** Relying on phone pairing limits the addressable market to existing phone owners, while the lack of native apps discourages new users from entering the ecosystem. Developers will not commit to an OS with a artificially restricted installed base.
4.  **Strategic Confusion:** Entering the tablet market requires a clear value proposition. "A smaller, more expensive screen that requires your phone to work" is a negative value proposition.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve brand reputation; wait for the new OS to mature before launching hardware.
*   **Full-Featured Launch:** Delay release until native email/calendar apps are ready, even if it misses the immediate holiday window.
*   **Price Cut:** Significantly undercut the rival to justify the reduced functionality (risky for margins).

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, reviewers pan the lack of email and high price, sales flatline, and the new OS is deemed a failure before it gains traction. The brand suffers long-term damage in the business sector.

**Blockers:**
*   **Functional Blocker:** The core brand promise (email productivity) is absent from the hardware.
*   **Economic Blocker:** Price parity with a superior competitor guarantees low adoption.

**Verdict:** The proposal fails the basic test of product-market fit for the company's specific audience. Launching a business device without business tools at a premium price is not a viable strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
