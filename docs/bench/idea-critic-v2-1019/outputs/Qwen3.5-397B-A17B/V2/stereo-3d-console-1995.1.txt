Steelman: A low-cost ($180) interim device leveraging existing IP to bridge the gap between handheld and home console generations using novel stereoscopic 3D.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Product Definition Failure:** The proposal claims a "tabletop" device that is "neither portable nor comfortable." A handheld manufacturer's core competency is ergonomics and mobility; a stationary device requiring an eyepiece contradicts the brand's value proposition and user expectations.
2.  **Health Liability:** The explicit admission that "some users may get eye strain or headaches" constitutes a known, unmitigated safety defect. Releasing a product with a confirmed physiological negative effect creates immediate reputational damage and potential liability, violating the duty of care to the customer base.
3.  **Content Starvation:** "Few games at launch" for a hardware platform relying on software sales guarantees commercial failure. Without a strong library, the hardware is a novelty that will be abandoned before the next home console arrives.
4.  **Cannibalization vs. Bridge:** With the next home console due in two years, this device risks confusing the market rather than bridging it. The red monochrome limitation severely restricts the visual fidelity expected of a "next-gen" experience, making it a downgrade rather than an innovation.

**Risks:**
*   Market rejection of non-color 3D.
*   Inventory obsolescence when the true next-gen console launches.
*   Brand erosion due to discomfort reports.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "fit" check against the company's north star of accessible, enjoyable gaming. The identified risks are not merely potential; the health issues and lack of content are stated facts in the proposal itself. A cheaper alternative exists: wait for the home console or iterate on the existing handheld. The cost of delay (waiting two years) is lower than the cost of launching a defective, uncomfortable product that damages brand trust. The "bridge" strategy collapses when the bridge itself is structurally unsound (health risks) and leads nowhere (few games).

DECISION: NO-GO
