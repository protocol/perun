The claim is that launching a $5,000 self-balancing transporter with 40,000-unit capacity in 2001 will succeed despite regulatory ambiguity and unproven consumer demand.

Steelman: This device creates a new micro-mobility category where high margins justify early-adopter pricing, and publicity overcomes adoption friction before regulations catch up.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The primary blocker is the **regulatory vacuum**. The proposal explicitly admits the legal status on sidewalks and roads is "unclear." Deploying 40,000 units into a market where the product may be immediately banned from public use is a fatal flaw. Without a verified legal pathway, the inventory becomes stranded assets. A "go" decision requires resolved legality, not hope.

Secondly, the **demand assumption** is unsupported. The proposal concedes uses beyond institutional buyers are "unproven" and highlights high price, conspicuousness, and low utility (cannot carry much) as risks. Launching with factory capacity for 40,000 units assumes mass consumer adoption to replace walking or short car trips, yet the value proposition for the average consumer is weak compared to the $5,000 cost. The "institutional" niche mentioned is too small to absorb 40,000 units annually at launch.

Alternatives:
1. **Do nothing:** Avoids capital risk while regulations clarify.
2. **Pilot program:** Deploy 500 units to institutional partners to prove utility and lobby for specific regulations before building a massive factory.
3. **Proposed plan:** High capital expenditure with undefined market access.

The pre-mortem is clear: The product launches, cities immediately ban it from sidewalks due to safety conflicts with pedestrians, consumer sales stall due to price and awkwardness, and the company collapses under inventory debt. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a failed full-scale launch.

This is a one-way door with irreversible capital commitment based on assumptions that contradict the stated risks. The lack of a defined regulatory strategy and the mismatch between factory scale and proven demand constitute concrete blockers.

Blockers: Undefined legal status prevents public use; factory scale exceeds proven institutional demand.
Risks: Consumer rejection due to price/stigma; regulatory bans.

DECISION: NO-GO
