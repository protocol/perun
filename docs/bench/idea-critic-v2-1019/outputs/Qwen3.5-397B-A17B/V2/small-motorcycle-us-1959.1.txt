The claim is that a small 50cc motorcycle, successful in Japan, will succeed in the US despite a market preference for large machines and potential mechanical failures at highway speeds. The steelman posits that an untapped demographic of commuters and students exists who prioritize ease of use and low cost over power, and that non-traditional retail channels can bypass the "rough" motorcycle image.

However, critical blockers exist. First, the proposal admits the company's larger models fail at US highway speeds; while the 50cc unit avoids highways, this signals a broader engineering gap in durability for American conditions that may taint the brand before it starts. Second, the market premise contradicts stated facts: Americans favor big motorcycles, and the brand is unknown. Entering a small, hostile market with scarce foreign currency creates a high risk of immediate capital loss with no proven demand. The "do nothing" alternative preserves capital and avoids reputational damage from a potential mechanical failure in a new territory.

The "better way" analysis suggests that before committing to a full subsidiary and inventory shipment, the company should verify demand through limited exports or partnerships without establishing a subsidiary, thereby reducing currency exposure. The current plan requires a massive upfront investment (subsidiary, inventory) based on an unproven assumption that US consumers will change their preference from "large and rough" to "small and easy." Without evidence that the specific 50cc model can withstand US road conditions (distinct from the failed larger models) or that the "commuter" segment is large enough to sustain operations, the risk outweighs the potential upside.

The strongest attack is the combination of unknown brand equity, a market culturally opposed to the product type, and a known history of mechanical inadequacy in the target environment (highway speeds), coupled with the irreversible cost of establishing a foreign subsidiary. The proposal fails to demonstrate that the specific 50cc unit solves the durability concern or that the distribution strategy overcomes the cultural barrier.

Blockers: Unproven demand in a hostile market; known engineering failures in US conditions (larger models); high irreversible cost (subsidiary) vs. scarce currency.
Risks: Brand damage, total capital loss, cultural rejection.

DECISION: NO-GO
