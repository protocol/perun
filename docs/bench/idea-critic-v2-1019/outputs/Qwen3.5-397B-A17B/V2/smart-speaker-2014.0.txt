The proposal suggests launching a cylindrical, always-listening smart speaker in 2014 at $199 ($99 for members) via invitation, despite a recent failed smartphone launch and significant market skepticism.

**Steelman:** This device creates a new "ambient computing" category, leveraging existing retail logistics to embed the brand permanently in homes, turning voice queries into direct shopping actions that mobile apps miss.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Privacy & Trust:** In 2014, an "always-on" microphone is a massive liability. The recent smartphone failure suggests weak consumer trust in the brand's hardware. A privacy scandal here could destroy the core retail business.
2.  **Technical Feasibility:** Voice recognition in 2014 is unreliable outside controlled environments. If the device fails to understand commands frequently, it becomes a expensive novelty, reinforcing the "failed hardware" narrative.
3.  **Market Fit:** No category exists because the problem isn't clearly defined. Mobile assistants already exist; the incremental value of a stationary, $199 cylinder is unproven.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital and reputation. A cheaper, non-always-on prototype could test voice shopping without the privacy backlash. Waiting 12–18 months allows voice tech to mature and privacy norms to shift.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sells initially to enthusiasts, then sits unused due to poor recognition. A news break reveals accidental recordings, triggering a regulatory inquiry and a boycott. The division is shut down, and the brand's hardware credibility is set back a decade.

**Blockers:**
*   **Trust Deficit:** The recent poor smartphone sales indicate the market does not yet trust this retailer with complex hardware, making a high-risk privacy product a potential existential threat.
*   **Tech Immaturity:** Current voice failure rates contradict the "seamless" experience required for mass adoption.
*   **Price vs. Value:** $199 is prohibitive for an unproven category, especially from a distrusted hardware vendor.

**Risks:** Privacy backlash, technical failure, category rejection, brand damage.

**Conclusion:** The combination of low consumer trust (post-smartphone failure), immature technology, and high privacy risk creates a fatal blocker. The cost of failure (brand destruction) vastly outweighs the potential upside of pioneering a category that competitors can enter later with better tech and trust.

DECISION: NO-GO
