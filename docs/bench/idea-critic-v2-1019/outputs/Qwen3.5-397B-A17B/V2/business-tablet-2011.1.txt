The proposal presents a high-risk entry into the tablet market with significant structural flaws. The core contradiction is targeting business email dominance while launching a device without native email, calendar, or contact applications unless paired with a specific phone. This directly undermines the value proposition for the primary target audience: business users who need standalone productivity.

The "better way" analysis reveals a superior alternative exists: leveraging the existing, trusted operating system to ensure immediate app compatibility and native email support, rather than betting on an untested, newly acquired OS with no developer commitment. The current plan forces a "two-way door" decision (OS choice) to be treated as irreversible, while ignoring that the lack of apps is a one-way door to market irrelevance.

The pre-mortem indicates a near-certain failure mode: business customers reject a "tablet" that cannot function as a communication hub without a specific phone tether, especially when a rival offers a larger screen, a mature app store, and standalone utility at the same price point. The price parity with a superior, larger competitor removes any cost advantage, leaving the product with no differentiating value other than a compromised ecosystem.

The assumption that developers will commit to an untested OS for a niche form factor, despite a rival's entrenched large app store, is unsupported by the provided facts. The risk of low developer adoption is a blocker, not merely a risk, because without apps, the device has no utility beyond the limited built-in features, which explicitly exclude the company's core strength: email.

While the strategy might aim to seed a new OS, doing so by compromising the flagship business use case is a strategic error. The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test; maintaining focus on the phone or launching a tablet with the existing, proven OS and native email would be a safer, higher-upside path. The current proposal sacrifices the core brand promise for an unproven platform gamble.

DECISION: NO-GO
