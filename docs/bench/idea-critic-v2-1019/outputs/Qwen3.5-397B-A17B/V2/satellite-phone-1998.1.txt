The claim is that a $5 billion satellite network can succeed in 1998 by targeting business travelers with $3,000 handsets and high per-minute rates. The steelman: this is the only solution for true global coverage where terrestrial cells cannot reach, creating a monopoly niche for urgent connectivity.

However, the alternatives crush this plan. "Do nothing" and wait for terrestrial networks to expand is cheaper and offers better indoor performance and lower handset costs, which are critical for adoption. The "better way" is to license the technology for niche government/military use only, avoiding the massive capital burden of a global consumer launch that requires impossible growth rates to service debt.

The pre-mortem is clear: the service launches, but the bulky phones fail indoors, the price excludes all but the most desperate users, and terrestrial networks cover major travel routes faster than anticipated. The company defaults on its massive debt within two years because subscriber growth cannot match the repayment schedule. The cost of delay is negative; waiting reduces the addressable market as cellular coverage expands.

The blocker is factual and immediate: the unit economics do not work. The debt service on $5 billion requires millions of subscribers quickly. A $3,000 handset plus several dollars per minute restricts the total addressable market to a tiny fraction of what is needed to break even. The proposal admits sales channels are not ready and the product works poorly indoors, which are fatal flaws for a mass-market consumer launch. The risk is not just execution; the core value proposition is being erased by the rapid spread of the cheaper, superior alternative (ground cellular) before the product even launches. This is a classic case of building a expensive solution for a problem that is disappearing.

Blockers: Debt payments require subscriber growth rates impossible to achieve given the high price, poor indoor performance, and shrinking unique coverage advantage.
Risks: Rapid terrestrial expansion, channel unreadiness, bulky hardware.

DECISION: NO-GO
