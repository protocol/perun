The claim is that launching the world's cheapest car at $2,500 from a new factory will successfully transition scooter families to cars. Steelman: Unbeatable price creates a new market segment where none existed, leveraging volume to offset thin margins.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts provided in the text. First, the "new dedicated factory" faces active farmer protests over land; without a secured site, high-volume production cannot commence, making the launch date and cost targets impossible. Second, the core value proposition ("cheapest car in the world") directly triggers the identified risk that buyers may reject a vehicle defined by low status and perceived poor quality, especially when safer used cars and scooters are cheaper alternatives. Third, the cost-cutting required to hit $2,500 exacerbates safety doubts, which is a fatal flaw for a product marketed to families.

The "better way" analysis shows the proposal does not beat "do nothing" or a phased approach. Entering a price war with used cars and scooters while facing production stoppages and brand stigma offers no clear path to profitability. The risks are not merely executional; they are foundational to the business model. The land dispute blocks the supply side, and the "cheapest" branding blocks the demand side. A plan that relies on a factory that may not be built and a brand position that may repel customers cannot proceed as proposed.

Blockers:
1. Land acquisition for the factory is contested by protests, threatening the entire production model.
2. The "cheapest" positioning conflicts with buyer preference for safety/status, with cheaper alternatives already available.
3. Extreme cost-cutting required for the $2,500 price point validates safety concerns, undermining the core family-use case.

Risks:
Brand damage, prolonged legal delays, failure to achieve volume targets, and margin collapse.

DECISION: NO-GO
