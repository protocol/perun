The claim is that a $700 pen-based PDA with unproven handwriting recognition will successfully create and lead a new handheld category. The steelman posits that first-mover advantage and CEO-driven publicity can overcome technical immaturity and high cost to define a market.

However, concrete blockers exist within the provided facts. First, the core mechanism (handwriting recognition) is explicitly "unreliable in testing." For an input-first device, unreliable input is a functional failure, not a feature risk; it renders the primary value proposition unusable. Second, the form factor ("too big for a pocket") contradicts the essential utility of a "handheld" or "personal" device, violating the basic definition of the category it seeks to lead. Third, the price ($700) is high without the software ecosystem to justify it, and the proposal admits "little software" exists.

Alternatives were not sufficiently weighed. "Do nothing" avoids sinking capital into a broken core mechanism. A "better way" would be to delay launch until recognition is reliable and the form factor fits a pocket, or to license the technology to establish the software base first. The cost of delay is lower than the cost of launching a flawed product that defines the category as "broken" in the public mind, potentially killing the market before it matures.

The pre-mortem is clear: the device launches, users cannot reliably input data, the device is inconvenient to carry, and developers ignore the platform due to low adoption. The result is a high-profile failure that drains resources and brand equity. The risks cited are not merely uncertainties; they are verified negative states (unreliable, too big, little software) that directly contradict the requirements for success. No kill criteria can fix a product that fails its primary function at launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
