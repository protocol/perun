The proposal to launch four months early in May 1995 at $399 fails the blocker test against its own stated facts.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Developer Readiness:** The proposal admits developers "did not plan for the early date" and the machine is "hard to program for 3D." Launching with "few games ready" creates a fatal content vacuum. A console without software cannot sustain momentum, regardless of marketing hype.
2.  **Channel Alienation:** Shipping "only to a few retailers" while excluding others invites immediate retaliation from major partners who will prioritize the competitor's broader launch. This cedes shelf space to the rival permanently.
3.  **Price Disadvantage:** At $100 above the competitor's announced price, the value proposition relies entirely on software quality and availability. Since the proposal confirms software will be scarce, the price premium is unjustifiable and will drive consumers to the cheaper, better-supported alternative.

**Risks:**
*   Early announcement burns marketing ammunition before inventory is widespread.
*   Technical reputation damage if the "hard to program" architecture leads to poor launch titles.

**Analysis:**
The "first mover" advantage is illusory here because the product ecosystem (games) and distribution channel (retailers) are explicitly unready. The steelman argument—that surprising the market steals headlines—collapses when the product on shelves cannot satisfy the generated demand. The competitor's later launch allows them to learn from these missteps, undercut on price, and offer a fuller library.

**Kill Criteria:**
If a full game library and broad retailer support are not secured by the new May date, the launch must not proceed. Since the proposal states these are missing, the condition fails.

**Recommendation:**
Do not approve the early launch. Stick to the September date to allow developer tooling to mature, secure broader retailer buy-in, and potentially adjust pricing strategy based on the competitor's final moves. Launching a half-finished ecosystem at a premium price is a guaranteed path to market irrelevance.

DECISION: NO-GO
