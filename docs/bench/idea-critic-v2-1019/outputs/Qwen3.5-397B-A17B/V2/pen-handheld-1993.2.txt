**Steelman:** Launching a $700 pen-based PDA with heavy CEO publicity will define a new handheld category before competitors can react, leveraging first-mover advantage despite technical immaturity.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts inherent to the plan. First, the core mechanism (handwriting recognition) is explicitly stated as "unreliable in testing." Shipping a primary input method that does not work reliably renders the device unusable for its stated purpose, creating an immediate product failure regardless of marketing. Second, the form factor ("too big for a pocket") contradicts the essential utility of a "handheld" or "personal" device intended for mobile use; if it does not fit the user's context, it cannot lead the category. Third, the high price ($700 in 1993 is roughly $1,500 today) combined with "little software" and an "unproven category" creates a fatal adoption barrier. Early adopters in unproven categories require functional novelty or extreme value; this offers neither, offering instead an expensive, bulky device with broken input.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids brand damage from a flawed flagship launch.
2.  **Delay & Refine:** Wait for recognition reliability and shrink the form factor before launch.
3.  **Software-First:** Seed the ecosystem with development tools before hardware release.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to heavy press coverage, reviewers immediately demonstrate the handwriting failures, the size prevents daily carry, and developers ignore the platform due to low install base. Sales flatline, the category is declared dead for a decade, and the company burns capital on a failed bet.

**Blockers:**
1.  Core input mechanism is unreliable (stated fact).
2.  Form factor violates the "pocket" requirement of the category (stated fact).
3.  Price-to-value ratio is negative given the lack of software and broken features.

**Risks:** Market timing, competitor response, execution speed. These are irrelevant because the blockers prevent a viable product from existing.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not launch until recognition accuracy exceeds 95% in blind testing, the device fits a standard shirt pocket, and at least 10 killer apps are committed.

DECISION: NO-GO
