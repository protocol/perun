Steelman: Launching two carrier-bound, data-plan-dependent social phones without apps or games captures the youth market immediately using an acquired team's momentum, bypassing the delay of the main platform.

However, the claim fails the blocker test. The proposal requires price-sensitive young buyers to purchase a device costing as much as a smartphone, plus a full data plan, while offering zero utility beyond a single social function (no apps, no games). This economic mismatch is a fatal blocker: the value proposition is negative compared to existing smartphones or cheaper feature phones. The "no app store" constraint eliminates the primary driver of smartphone adoption in this demographic, making the high price unjustifiable.

Furthermore, launching two niche devices distracts from the company's main platform releasing later that year, cannibalizing attention and resources for a product with a capped ceiling. The history of internal conflict between teams suggests execution risk is high, but the market mismatch is the immediate killer. A cheaper, simpler alternative exists: integrate the social features into the upcoming main platform or license the software to existing carriers/devices, avoiding hardware costs and plan barriers entirely.

The pre-mortem is clear: the phones launch, carriers struggle to sell them due to the high total cost of ownership, youth reject the "walled garden" without games/apps, and inventory piles up while the main platform launch is diluted. The cost of delay is negligible because the target demographic will not wait for an overpriced, under-featured device; they will buy competitors' smartphones now.

Blockers:
1. Economic mismatch: Price-sensitive target asked to pay smartphone prices for sub-smartphone utility (no apps/games).
2. Strategic conflict: Launching niche hardware distracts from the core platform arriving same year.
3. Value gap: No app store removes the primary reason young users adopt smartphones.

Risks:
1. Internal team conflict delaying execution.
2. Carrier reluctance due to likely poor sell-through.

This plan cannot succeed as proposed because the core economics and value proposition are inverted relative to the target market's reality.

DECISION: NO-GO
