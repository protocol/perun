**Steelman:** Launching a $10,000 graphical workstation in 1983 captures the high-end market by offering unmatched usability despite higher costs and limited software.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" and "value" tests immediately.
1.  **Price Dissonance:** At $10,000, the unit costs several times more than leading business PCs. Without a dominant software ecosystem or superior speed, office workers cannot justify the premium. The value proposition is inverted: higher cost for lower performance.
2.  **Internal Cannibalization:** A cheaper machine with a similar interface is already in development internally. Launching the expensive version first risks brand confusion and renders the upcoming cheaper unit redundant, or worse, makes the company appear gouging when the cheaper model inevitably launches.
3.  **Ecosystem Void:** "Little third-party software" is a fatal blocker for business adoption. Office workers need specific tools (spreadsheets, word processors); a beautiful interface without applications is a toy, not a business tool.
4.  **Performance Deficit:** Running "slowly" contradicts the efficiency needs of office environments.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" option regarding this specific $10k configuration is superior. Resources should shift immediately to the cheaper internal project. If a launch is mandatory, the price must align closer to competitors, or the software gap must be filled first. A "go-if" condition would require the price to drop below $4,000 or a commitment of 50+ business titles at launch, neither of which is present.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price:** $10,000 is unsupported by performance or software volume.
*   **Competition:** Internal cheaper alternative makes this SKU obsolete before release.
*   **Utility:** Lack of software prevents actual business work.

**Risks:** Market rejection, inventory write-downs, brand damage due to poor price/performance ratio.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed if an internal cheaper alternative exists with similar capabilities (Verified: Yes). Do not proceed if price > 2x market leaders without 10x value (Verified: Yes).

The proposal asks to launch a slower, expensive computer with no software when a better, cheaper version is coming soon. This guarantees commercial failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
