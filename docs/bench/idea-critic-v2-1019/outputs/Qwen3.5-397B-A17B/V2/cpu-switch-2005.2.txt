**Steelman:** Migrating to the dominant architecture secures the thermal efficiency required for thin laptops, aligning the product roadmap with market leaders while a translation layer mitigates immediate software incompatibility.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the "announcement freeze": revealing the plan two years early risks cannibalizing current sales before the new units ship. However, staying on the current path guarantees product irrelevance in the emerging thin-and-light segment due to thermal constraints; "do nothing" leads to market exit. The alternative of waiting for the current supplier to improve is invalidated by a decade of failure.

Developers rebuilding software is a friction cost, not a blocker, as the translation layer bridges the gap. While translated performance is lower, native performance on the new architecture outweighs this for most users. Brand distinctiveness may dilute, but a non-functional brand has no value. The pain of a previous transition is a sunk cost; the pain of obsolescence is existential.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it is because the translation layer creates a user experience so poor that early adopters reject the hardware, or the sales freeze starves the company of cash before the new chips arrive.

**Kill Criteria:**
1.  **Blocker Test:** Does the current supplier have a verified, shipping solution for thin laptops within 12 months? Fact: No.
2.  **Metric:** Achieve native performance parity with competitors on thermal throttling tests within 18 months.
3.  **Stop Condition:** If the translation layer delivers less than 70% of native speed for core applications by the beta phase, pivot to a hybrid release strategy.

**Verdict:** The risk of sales freezing is manageable via communication strategy (e.g., vague roadmaps), whereas the thermal limitation of the current architecture is a hard physical constraint that cannot be negotiated. The proposal addresses the only variable that ensures long-term survival.

DECISION: GO
