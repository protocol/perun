The claim is that a cylindrical, always-listening speaker will succeed despite a recent hardware failure and a non-existent category. The steelman: an invitation-only launch at a aggressive price point ($99 for members) mitigates financial risk and creates scarcity, while the utility of voice shopping leverages the retailer's core asset to create a new, defensible home category.

However, the blocker test fails. The proposal explicitly admits the company's last hardware launch "went badly," yet offers no evidence that the root causes (supply chain, quality control, or market fit) have been resolved. Launching a complex hardware device with always-on microphone technology—a significant privacy hurdle in 2014—immediately after a failure ignores the "do nothing" alternative, which preserves capital and brand trust while competitors validate the market. The risks of voice recognition failure and consumer privacy rejection are not merely "risks"; combined with the recent track record, they form a concrete failure scenario where the product flops, damaging the core retail brand further.

The "cheaper, safer" alternative is to wait. Let competitors (who are already exploring phone-based assistants) bear the cost of educating the market and solving the privacy trust deficit. The company should only enter once the category is proven and internal hardware confidence is restored via a smaller, lower-stakes pilot. The current plan bets the brand's reputation on an unproven form factor and a skeptical public, using a strategy (invitation-only) that cannot fix fundamental product-market fit or hardware execution issues. The lack of a verified fix for the previous launch's failure is a factual blocker that prevents approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
