The claim is that a mass-market economy manufacturer can successfully launch a separate luxury brand with a dedicated dealer network and a single flagship sedan to compete with established European leaders. The steelman version posits that structural separation (brand and dealers) effectively insulates the new venture from the parent's economy reputation, while aggressive underpricing creates an irresistible value proposition for quality-conscious buyers.

However, critical blockers exist. First, the "separate dealer network" requires massive upfront capital expenditure at a time when development costs are already described as "heavy." This creates a dangerous cash-flow crunch before a single unit is sold. Second, a single-model launch offers no buffer against market rejection; if the flagship fails to convince buyers on its own merits, the entire brand collapses immediately. Third, the premise that price alone overcomes decades of entrenched prestige is unproven; luxury buyers often prioritize heritage over value, potentially viewing the lower price as a signal of lower status rather than a bargain.

While a "better way" might involve leveraging existing dealerships to reduce cost or launching with multiple models to ensure breadth, the proposal explicitly rejects these to maintain purity. The risk of brand dilution remains if the separation is not absolute, yet the cost of that separation threatens solvency. The pre-mortem suggests a scenario where the high fixed costs of the new network drain resources while sales volume remains too low to achieve profitability, forcing a retreat that damages the parent company's core financial stability.

The strongest attack is the financial exposure: combining heavy sunk development costs with the requirement to build a parallel retail infrastructure creates a binary outcome with a very high probability of catastrophic loss if the single model does not achieve immediate, massive scale. There is no phased approach to validate demand before committing to the dealer network.

DECISION: NO-GO
