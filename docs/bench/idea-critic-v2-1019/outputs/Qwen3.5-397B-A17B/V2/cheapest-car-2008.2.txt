The claim is that launching the world's cheapest car at $2,500 from a new factory will successfully transition scooter families to cars. The steelman: an ultra-low-cost vehicle unlocks a massive, untapped market segment by offering superior safety and weather protection compared to scooters at a psychologically accessible price point.

However, three critical blockers exist in the proposal as stated. First, the "cheapest" positioning creates a fatal branding trap; if buyers associate the car with inferior quality or social stigma, they will prefer used cars or scooters, which carry no "poverty" label. Second, the aggressive cost-cutting required to hit $2,500 directly conflicts with safety expectations for families; a single high-profile accident could destroy the brand before it scales. Third, the factory site faces active farmer protests; without a resolved land acquisition strategy, production cannot begin, making the high-volume targets impossible to meet regardless of demand.

Alternatives include positioning the car as "smart and efficient" rather than "cheapest" to avoid stigma, or starting with a lower-volume assembly line to mitigate land risk while proving demand. Yet, the proposal explicitly ties the business case to a new dedicated factory and the "cheapest" marketing hook.

The pre-mortem reveals that the project fails because land protests delay the factory indefinitely, or because the "cheapest" label repels the very families it aims to serve, leaving them with cheaper, safer-feeling used alternatives. The cost of delay is high due to fixed capital costs, but the cost of proceeding with unresolved land rights and a potentially toxic brand position is total capital loss.

Blockers: Unresolved land protests halting construction; "cheapest" branding potentially driving away target demographic; safety perception gaps unaddressed by the current plan.
Risks: Used car market saturation; supply chain inability to meet cost targets without quality collapse.

The proposal lacks a confirmed resolution for the land acquisition and offers no mitigation for the branding stigma beyond the price itself. Proceeding now exposes the company to immediate operational stoppage and long-term market rejection.

DECISION: NO-GO
