**Steelman:** Transforming from a pure retailer into a hybrid marketplace creates immediate inventory depth and variety without capital expenditure, turning fixed inventory risk into variable fee income while capturing long-tail demand the retailer cannot stock itself.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal contains no internal contradictions, impossible math, or facts that preclude execution. The risks cited (cannibalization, quality control, supplier backlash) are valid but manageable trade-offs, not fatal flaws. Historical precedent in this specific domain confirms that the net effect of expanded selection outweighs the loss of margin on commoditized items.

**Risks:** 
1. **Cannibalization:** Used items will reduce new item sales volume. *Mitigation:* Accept lower margin per unit in exchange for higher total transaction volume and fee revenue; customers who would not buy a new item at full price may buy used, keeping the transaction on-platform.
2. **Quality Control:** Third-party sellers may damage brand trust. *Mitigation:* Implement strict performance metrics and suspension policies; the fee model aligns platform success with seller reliability.
3. **Supplier Relations:** Publishers may retaliate. *Mitigation:* Leverage the growing "range of goods" beyond books to dilute dependency on any single aggrieved category; frame the used market as secondary to new release promotion.

**Decision Logic:** The retailer is "not yet profitable." The current model carries high fixed costs (inventory) and limited selection. The proposed model shifts the cost structure to variable fees and instantly expands the catalog. Doing nothing preserves the current burn rate and limited selection. The alternative of waiting for profitability before expanding is a trap; this mechanism is a likely accelerator to profitability. The risks are operational hurdles, not structural blockers. The "do nothing" option guarantees continued losses; the proposal offers a path to positive unit economics via fee generation and reduced inventory exposure.

**Kill Criteria:** If third-party seller defect rates exceed 5% within six months, or if major suppliers cut off new inventory supply due to the used market, the program must be paused.

**Leading Metric:** Percentage of total Gross Merchandise Value (GMV) contributed by third-party sellers vs. first-party inventory, tracked quarterly.

DECISION: GO
