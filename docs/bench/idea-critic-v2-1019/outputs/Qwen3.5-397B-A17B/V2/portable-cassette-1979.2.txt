**Steelman:** A portable, affordable cassette player focused exclusively on playback via headphones captures a latent demand for private, mobile music among youth, leveraging existing components to achieve a disruptive price point despite dealer skepticism.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Dealer/Staff Doubt:** The belief that a tape machine must record is an industry bias, not a user constraint. Users prioritize portability and battery life over recording, which adds bulk and cost.
2.  **Antisocial Concern:** Headphones create a personal sound sanctuary, a feature likely to be embraced by urban youth rather than rejected. The "antisocial" label is a generational misunderstanding of the value proposition.
3.  **Lack of Market Research:** Absence of data reflects a lack of imagination, not a lack of demand. No prior product defined this category; research validates existing markets, not new behaviors.
4.  **Copycat Risk:** Competitors using existing parts is a temporary threat. First-mover advantage, brand identity, and rapid iteration will outpace clones.
5.  **Large Production Run:** High volume is necessary to lower unit costs to the target price. This is a calculated risk required to unlock the mass market; without it, the product remains a niche luxury.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because marketing focused on technical specs rather than the lifestyle benefit of "music anywhere," or because distribution channels refused to stock it due to their own biases. However, the low price point and unique form factor mitigate these risks by appealing directly to the end consumer.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** None. The objections are based on industry convention, not factual impossibilities or financial insolvency.
*   **Risks:** High initial inventory commitment and potential channel resistance. These are manageable through aggressive consumer marketing that bypasses skeptical sales staff.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If initial sales do not meet 50% of forecast within 6 months, halt production expansion.
*   **Leading Metric:** Units sold per week in urban centers; adoption rate among demographics under 25.

The proposal challenges outdated assumptions with a clear, focused value proposition. The risks are inherent to innovation but do not constitute blockers. The potential to create a new market category outweighs the fear of copying or dealer hesitation.

DECISION: GO
