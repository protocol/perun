**Steelman:** A dominant hardware vendor monetizes piracy by offering a legal, frictionless, per-song alternative at a fixed price, using exclusivity to drive hardware sales rather than maximizing software margin.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that 99 cents cannot cover licensing costs, leading to immediate financial failure. However, recomputing the unit economics reveals the core claim relies on a **hardware subsidy model**, not software profit. If the device margin absorbs the loss or break-even status of the store, the low price is a feature, not a bug, designed to kill free alternatives.

*Alternative 1 (Subscription):* Rivals are pursuing this, but in 2003, broadband penetration is too low for reliable streaming, and consumers resist recurring fees for ownership. This path has higher churn risk.
*Alternative 2 (Do Nothing):* Continued erosion of the value proposition for portable music devices as users fill them with free, disorganized files. This guarantees hardware commoditization.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits the "integrated appliance" strategy: control the whole widget. The pre-mortem failure scenario is label withdrawal. Yet, labels are desperate for *any* revenue stream to replace collapsing CD sales; they have no better alternative than this proposal today. The "walled garden" (own computers only) is a temporary friction that secures the hardware moat, not a permanent flaw.

**Blocker Test:**
Is there a fact showing the math fails? Only if the company refuses to subsidize the software to sell hardware. Given the portable player is "selling well," the installed base provides the leverage to negotiate licenses. The risk of low margin is real but irrelevant if the strategic goal is ecosystem lock-in. The "free" competitor offers poor UX (search, viruses, no metadata); 99 cents buys convenience and safety.

**Verdict:**
No factual blocker exists. The risks (margin, labels, platform limits) are managed by the strategic intent to use content as a loss-leader for high-margin hardware. The alternative (subscriptions) is premature for the 2003 infrastructure.

DECISION: GO
