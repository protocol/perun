The claim is that a premium, mid-calorie cola targeting young men will capture value lost to low-carb trends and diet growth. The steelman: a distinct "best of both worlds" product fills a gap between full-sugar indulgence and artificial diet taste, justifying a premium for early adopters before the trend fades.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The primary objection is not merely a risk but a structural contradiction in the value proposition. The "half-sugar" formulation explicitly targets the intersection of two mutually exclusive preferences: taste (full sugar) and health (zero/low calorie). The proposal admits that calorie-conscious consumers already buy diet, and taste-focused consumers reject reduced sweetness. A "compromise" product satisfies neither core need fully, creating a "middle trap" where the product is too sweet for the health segment and not sweet enough for the indulgence segment.

Furthermore, the premium pricing acts as a direct blocker to trial for a product with an unproven value proposition. If the taste is compromised, consumers will not pay extra to discover that compromise. The presence of a rival launching a similar drink suggests the market signal is noisy, not clear, increasing the likelihood that both companies are chasing a phantom segment. Finally, relying on a potentially fleeting "low-carb fad" for a major capital investment in manufacturing and marketing introduces massive obsolescence risk if the trend collapses before the brand establishes loyalty.

The pre-mortem is clear: the product launches, fails to gain repeat purchase from either diet or regular drinkers due to the "satisfy neither" flaw, and the premium price accelerates its shelf death. The "mid-calorie" category assumes a demand that the stated consumer behaviors (buying diet vs. avoiding diet) explicitly deny. Without evidence that a significant third segment exists that specifically desires *exactly* half the sugar at a *higher* price, the plan is built on a contradiction.

Blockers: The formulation satisfies neither the taste nor the health requirement of existing segments; premium pricing blocks trial for an unproven compromise.
Risks: Low-carb trend is a fad; rival saturation.

DECISION: NO-GO
