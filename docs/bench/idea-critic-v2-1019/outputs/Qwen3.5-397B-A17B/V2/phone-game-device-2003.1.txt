Steelman: Leveraging mobile dominance to disrupt handheld gaming via a hybrid device at $300.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Fatal Usability Flaw:** The requirement to remove the battery to change games creates a catastrophic user experience, violating basic convenience standards for both phones and consoles. This single mechanic renders the product non-viable compared to competitors.
2.  **Price Disadvantage:** At $300, the device is significantly more expensive than the market leader's dedicated handheld, offering inferior gaming ergonomics and a tiny library.
3.  **Ecosystem Void:** Without committed publishers, the hardware has no software value, a classic "chicken-and-egg" failure mode that price alone cannot solve.

**Risks:**
*   Awkward form factor compromises core phone utility.
*   Market leader's entrenched library creates an insurmountable moat.
*   High price point limits adoption before network effects can occur.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "do nothing" test; maintaining status quo is superior to launching a product with a known, deal-breaking mechanical flaw (battery removal). The "better way" analysis suggests that without a reversible battery door or internal storage, the core value proposition collapses. The cost of delay is negligible because the current design is fundamentally broken; rushing to market guarantees immediate reputational damage and inventory write-offs.

The pre-mortem is clear: The device launches, users attempt to swap games, encounter the battery hurdle, and abandon the platform. Publishers refuse to support a device with such friction. The project fails within six months.

There is no path to "GO" with the current specifications. The battery mechanic is a hard stop. Even if the price were lowered, the usability flaw remains a blocker. The lack of publisher commitment further ensures failure. This is not a risk to be managed; it is a design failure that invalidates the entire premise.

DECISION: NO-GO
