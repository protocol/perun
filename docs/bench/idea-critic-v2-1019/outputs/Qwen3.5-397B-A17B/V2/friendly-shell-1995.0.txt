**Steelman:** A $100 cartoon-house shell makes computing approachable for novices on high-end PCs, capturing the home market before competitors.

**Attack:**
1.  **Hardware Mismatch:** The proposal admits a need for "high-end" hardware, yet the target is "many new home users" who historically possess lower-spec machines. In 1995, high-end PCs are prohibitively expensive for the mass market this product targets. The addressable market is too small to sustain development.
2.  **Imminent Obsolescence:** A "major new operating-system release" is months away. If the incumbent OS leader is launching a native interface update, a $100 third-party shell becomes instantly redundant. Users will wait for the free/included OS upgrade rather than pay for a temporary overlay.
3.  **Ecosystem Void:** "Few third-party add-ons" indicates a dead platform trajectory. Without developer support, the "rooms" will remain empty, failing to deliver long-term utility.
4.  **Segment Alienation:** Risk of patronizing experienced users is secondary; the primary failure is that novices buying their first PC in late 1995 will likely buy a machine with the upcoming native OS pre-installed, bypassing this shell entirely.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Wait for the upcoming OS release; cost $0, risk low.
*   *Partner/OEM:* License the interface to PC manufacturers for pre-installation only; reduces user friction but still fights the OS tide.
*   *Pivot:* Build educational software *within* the existing/c upcoming OS paradigm rather than replacing the shell.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, the major OS update ships free with every new computer. Sales of the $100 shell collapse. Inventory piles up. The company is stuck maintaining a deprecated interface layer while the industry moves to the new native standard. The "high-end" requirement limits sales volume, preventing price reduction.

**Blockers:**
*   **Market Timing:** The imminent free OS update destroys the value proposition of a paid shell.
*   **Hardware Barrier:** The "high-end" requirement contradicts the "mass market" goal.

**Risks:** User rejection of cartoon aesthetic; lack of developer ecosystem.

**Kill Criteria:** If the upcoming OS release includes a graphical shell update, this project must terminate immediately.

**Decision:** The combination of hardware mismatch, imminent competitive obsolescence from the OS leader, and lack of ecosystem support creates a fatal business case.

DECISION: NO-GO
