**Steelman:** Launching a $10,000 graphical computer in 1983 captures the high-end office market before competitors, leveraging superior usability to justify a premium despite lower speed and limited software.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts provided in the text:
1.  **Price Dislocation:** At $10,000, the unit costs "several times" that of leading business PCs. Without a demonstrated productivity multiplier (impossible with "little third-party software"), office workers cannot justify the capital expenditure.
2.  **Performance Deficit:** The machine "runs slowly." In a business context, speed is a primary utility. A slower, more expensive machine offers negative value compared to existing text-based leaders.
3.  **Internal Cannibalization:** A "cheaper machine with a similar interface" is already in development internally. Launching the expensive, slower version now guarantees immediate obsolescence upon the internal competitor's release, destroying inventory value and brand trust.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Avoids sinking costs into a non-competitive product.
*   *Wait:* Delay launch until the cheaper internal option is ready or until software availability increases.
*   *Pivot:* Reprice significantly or target a niche where graphics outweigh speed (not evidenced here).

**Pre-mortem:**
Assume failure one year post-launch. Cause: Sales teams could not overcome the price objection against faster, cheaper rivals; early adopters complained of lag; the internal cheaper model launched, making the $10k unit unsellable. The company is left with high-cost inventory and a reputation for overpricing.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Price is multiples of competitors without corresponding utility (software/speed).
*   **Fact:** Internal substitute exists that undermines the value proposition immediately.
*   **Fact:** Performance is inferior to current market leaders.

**Risks:**
*   Market rejection of GUI without software ecosystem.
*   Opportunity cost of diverting resources from the cheaper internal project.

**Conclusion:**
The proposal asks to launch a product that is slower, more expensive, and less useful than current options, with a known superior internal alternative pending. This violates basic economic logic for business hardware. There is no "go-if" condition that fixes the fundamental mismatch of price versus performance.

DECISION: NO-GO
