**Steelman:** A converged device leveraging existing mobile scale to disrupt a dedicated gaming monopoly by offering communication and gaming in one $300 unit.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on basic usability and economic reality. The "change game, lose power" flaw is a fatal design error; no consumer accepts resetting a device to swap media. The form factor compromises both primary functions: too bulky for calls, too awkward for gaming compared to the ergonomic leader. At $300, it is significantly priced above the market leader, yet offers a inferior gaming experience due to the lack of a committed publisher library. Without games, the hardware is a toy; without publisher buy-in, the platform dies. The "mobile maker" advantage is irrelevant if the core gaming value proposition is broken.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid brand damage from a clunky, overpriced failure.
2.  **Software Only:** License games to existing phones rather than building flawed hardware.
3.  **Redesign:** Fix the battery/card conflict and lower costs before launch.

**Pre-mortem:** Launch occurs. Reviewers mock the battery removal requirement. Gamers stick to the cheaper, comfortable leader with better games. Publishers refuse to port titles to a failing install base. Inventory piles up at $150, destroying margins. The brand becomes synonymous with "failed gaming attempt."

**Blockers:**
*   **Usability Failure:** Removing the battery to change games is a non-starter for consumer electronics.
*   **Price/Value Mismatch:** Higher price than the leader with fewer games and worse ergonomics ensures zero adoption.
*   **Content Void:** No publisher commitment means no software library, rendering the hardware useless for its secondary goal.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, inventory write-downs, opportunity cost of distracted engineering focus.

**Conclusion:** The product as described is fundamentally broken. The battery mechanic alone disqualifies it. The price premium without content parity is suicidal. This cannot be approved in its current state; it requires a complete hardware redesign and a secured content strategy before reconsideration.

DECISION: NO-GO
