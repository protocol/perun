The claim is that a $180 tabletop stereoscopic 3D console with red monochrome displays will successfully bridge the gap between handheld and home console generations. The steelman posits that this device offers a unique, affordable 3D experience unavailable elsewhere, leveraging existing technology to capture market interest during a hardware transition.

However, the "better-way" analysis reveals fatal flaws. The proposed form factor fails the core utility test: it is neither truly portable like a handheld nor comfortable for home use like a console. Requiring an eyepiece for a tabletop device creates an ergonomic barrier that isolates the user, contradicting the social nature of living room gaming. The "red-on-black" visual limitation severely restricts game design and appeal compared to emerging full-color competitors.

The pre-mortem indicates high probability of failure due to physical discomfort (eye strain/headaches) and a weak software library. With few launch titles and a known two-year horizon for the next true home console, this product risks cannibalizing sales from the current generation while failing to retain users against upcoming full-color portable or home solutions. The cost of delay is low; waiting for better display technology or the next home console avoids releasing a compromised product that could damage the brand's reputation for quality.

The strongest attack is the ergonomic and visual mismatch: a tabletop device requiring an eyepiece offers the worst of both worlds (immobility and isolation) without the visual fidelity to justify the $180 price point. This is not a bridge; it is a dead end that consumes resources better spent on the upcoming home console or a genuine portable color device. The risks are not merely theoretical; they are inherent to the chosen hardware specifications and form factor.

Blockers: The form factor contradicts user comfort and portability needs; the display technology is inferior to market expectations; the software lineup is insufficient to drive hardware adoption. These are not risks to be managed but fundamental defects in the value proposition.

DECISION: NO-GO
