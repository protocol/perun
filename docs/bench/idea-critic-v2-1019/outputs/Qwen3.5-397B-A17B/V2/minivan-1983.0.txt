The claim is that a front-wheel-drive, compact-platform van will save a cash-poor carmaker by creating a new consumer category. The steelman: this vehicle uniquely combines car-like handling and garage fitment to unlock families who reject commercial vans, leveraging existing assets for a capital-efficient turnaround.

Alternatives considered:
1. **Do nothing:** Rely on station wagons/full-size vans. Risk: Continued decline as the company lacks a competitive product in growing segments.
2. **Traditional RWD Van:** Proven commercial utility. Risk: Fails to attract families; requires expensive new platform investment the company cannot afford.
3. **Upscale Wagon Extension:** Cheaper. Risk: Cannot match the space/utility of a van shape, missing the core family need.

Fit: Aligns with survival (north star) by targeting an unserved "family hauler" gap without heavy capital expenditure.

Pre-mortem: The project fails if buyers perceive it as a "cheap commercial van" or if the compact platform compromises cargo space critical for families. However, the "garage-fit" and "car-like drive" are distinct differentiators against bulky RWD rivals. The risk of rivals copying is mitigated by the company's immediate cash crisis; speed to market via platform reuse is the only viable path.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the premise that families want van space but hate van dynamics. While no category exists, this indicates a market void, not a lack of demand. The cash constraint makes the converted factory and existing platform not just a cost-saver, but a prerequisite for existence.

Blockers: NONE. The risks (category creation, perception, platform limits) are inherent to innovation but do not factually contradict the plan's feasibility given the constraints. The alternative of doing nothing guarantees failure; this plan offers a plausible, low-capital path to a new market.

Risks: Market education costs, potential space compromises. These are managed by the unique value proposition (garage fit, car drive) and the lack of other options.

DECISION: GO
