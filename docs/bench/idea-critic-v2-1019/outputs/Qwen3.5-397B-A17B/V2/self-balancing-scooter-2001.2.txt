**Steelman:** A novel, self-balancing electric transporter solves short-range urban mobility, leveraging high-profile backing to create a new premium category despite regulatory ambiguity and a $5,000 price point.

**Attack:** The proposal assumes immediate consumer adoption at a luxury price point for a device with unproven utility beyond niche institutional use. The "conspicuous" factor and lack of cargo capacity severely limit daily practicality compared to established alternatives (walking, bicycles, cars). Crucially, the legal status on sidewalks and roads is undefined; launching at scale (40,000 units) before resolving this invites immediate regulatory crackdowns or bans, stranding inventory and brand reputation. The factory commitment creates massive fixed costs before product-market fit is verified in the wild.

**Better Way:** Start with a limited pilot program targeting specific institutional buyers (security, large campuses) where legal status can be negotiated and utility is highest. Use this data to refine the product and lobby for regulations before committing to mass production or a broad consumer launch. A smaller initial run reduces downside risk while testing the "conspicuous" hypothesis.

**Kill Criteria:** If regulatory clarity isn't achieved in key markets within 6 months, or if institutional pilot retention drops below 80%, halt mass production.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the blocker test. Committing to a 40,000-unit factory capacity and a broad consumer launch while the legal operating environment is "unclear" is a fatal flaw. This is not a mere risk; it is a fundamental precondition for the product's existence. Launching now invites immediate obsolescence via regulation. The price and utility concerns further suggest the demand curve is unproven at this volume. The proposal must pivot to a staged approach: secure legal footing and validate institutional demand before scaling hardware.

**Dissent Ledger:** Proponents argue first-mover advantage outweighs regulatory risk. Counter: Being first to be banned is not an advantage.
**Remaining Risk:** Even with a pilot, public perception of "conspicuousness" may permanently cap the market size.

DECISION: NO-GO
