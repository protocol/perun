The claim is that a playback-only cassette player with headphones, priced for youth, will succeed despite dealer skepticism, lack of market research, and easy replicability.

**Steelman:** A portable, affordable, personal music device fills an unmet need for mobility and privacy among young people, creating a new category where recording is irrelevant.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Dealer/Staff Doubt:** This is an incentive mismatch, not a product flaw. Staff sell what they understand; dealers fear inventory risk. If the value proposition is personal mobility, traditional demo methods (speakers) fail. *Counter:* Bypass standard demos; rely on youth word-of-mouth and visible adoption (headphones as a badge).
2.  **Antisocial Perception:** Listening alone is framed as a negative. *Reframe:* It is "freedom" from shared household radios and parental control. The risk is real only if marketing emphasizes isolation rather than liberation.
3.  **No Market Research:** In 1979, no data exists for a non-existent behavior. Waiting for research guarantees missing the window. The "do nothing" alternative preserves margin but cedes the emerging portable lifestyle to a future competitor.
4.  **Copycat Risk:** Using existing parts makes copying easy, but first-mover brand association in a new category ("walkman" vs. "tape player") creates a moat that specs cannot.
5.  **Large Production Run:** This is the only true financial blocker. If demand is misjudged, inventory costs could bankrupt the initiative. However, the low unit cost of existing parts mitigates this compared to custom tooling.

**Pre-mortem:** The product fails if dealers refuse to stock it due to confusion, or if the price remains too high because the initial run was too conservative. The "antisocial" critique sticks only if the cultural narrative isn't seized immediately.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** None. The lack of research is a feature of innovation, not a bug. Dealer doubt is a distribution challenge, not a product failure.
*   **Risks:** Inventory overhang; competitor speed.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If the first production run does not sell through to consumers (not just dealers) within 6 months, halt second run.
*   **Leading Metric:** Units sold per capita in urban centers aged 15–25.

The proposal creates a new market rather than fighting in an old one. The risks are executional, not fundamental.

DECISION: GO
