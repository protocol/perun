**Steelman:** Removing the headphone jack forces a transition to wireless audio, creating a cohesive ecosystem lock-in via proprietary chips and justifying a premium accessory that solves the immediate connectivity gap while signaling future-forward design.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker is the value proposition gap. Customers lose a free, universal, infinite-power wired solution and gain a $159, fragile, battery-dependent device that looks "odd." The risk of ridicule is not superficial; it threatens the core brand equity of the phone itself. If the earbuds are mocked, the phone removing the jack is validated as a mistake.

The "better way" is a transitional dongle or bundled low-cost wireless option, not a premium standalone launch. However, the strategic intent is to kill the legacy standard entirely. A transitional approach delays the inevitable cultural shift required to make wireless the default. The high price creates a perception of greed rather than innovation unless the user experience (automatic pairing) is flawless.

The pre-mortem reveals the most likely failure mode: consumers reject the phone entirely due to the missing jack, regardless of the earbuds, because the alternative (competitors with jacks) remains superior for the mass market during the transition year. The battery life (5 hours) is insufficient for heavy users, and the "easy to lose" factor exacerbates the high replacement cost anxiety.

However, the "do nothing" option cedes the definition of the future to competitors or stagnates the category. The risk of anger is real but finite; the reward is owning the post-jack ecosystem. The kill criterion is clear: if initial pre-orders drop >15% specifically citing the missing jack, the strategy fails. But based on the data provided, the strategic necessity to force the industry standard outweighs the short-term consumer friction. The high price anchors the value of wireless, even if attachment rates are initially low. The ridicule is a marketing problem, not a product fatal flaw.

Blockers: None proven; risks are high but manageable as growth pains.
Risks: Brand damage, low attachment rate, consumer backlash.

DECISION: GO
