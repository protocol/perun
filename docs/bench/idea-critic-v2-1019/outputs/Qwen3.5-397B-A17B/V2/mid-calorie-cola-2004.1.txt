**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men avoiding full-sugar drinks but rejecting artificial sweeteners, leveraging a premium price to signal innovation before the low-carb trend fades.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The core claim fails the "satisfy neither group" test. Dieters seek near-zero calories; taste-seekers accept full sugar. A hybrid risks alienating both, offering no distinct value proposition. The "do nothing" alternative protects margin by avoiding cannibalization of existing high-volume SKUs with a confusing, premium-priced niche product.

The "cheaper path" is reformulating existing lines or waiting for the rival to test the market, avoiding the massive campaign cost. The low-carb trend's volatility makes a large, immediate campaign a high-risk bet on a potential fad. If the trend collapses, the premium inventory and marketing spend become sunk costs with no residual brand equity.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Value Proposition Gap:** No evidence suggests a significant demographic exists that specifically demands *exactly* half-sugar at a *premium* price. The stated risk that it satisfies neither group is a confirmed blocker based on typical consumer segmentation logic provided in the prompt.
2.  **Trend Volatility:** Basing a major launch on a trend explicitly flagged as a "possible fad" without a staged rollout contradicts sound risk management for a "large campaign."

**Risks:**
Cannibalization of core/diet lines, rival pre-emption, and brand confusion. These are secondary to the fundamental lack of product-market fit indicated by the "satisfy neither" objection.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal demands a "large campaign" and "premium price" immediately. However, the primary objection (satisfying neither group) remains unrefuted by the provided facts. A "go-if" requiring a small-scale test to validate the "half-sugar" demand would be prudent, but the proposal as stated commits to a full launch. Since the central hypothesis (that this specific middle ground is desirable and profitable) is directly challenged by the raised risks which remain valid, the plan fails the blocker test. It attempts to solve a segmentation problem with a product that likely exacerbates it.

DECISION: NO-GO
