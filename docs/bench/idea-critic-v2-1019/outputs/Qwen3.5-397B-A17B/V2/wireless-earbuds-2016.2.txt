**Steelman:** Removing the headphone jack forces a transition to wireless audio, creating an ecosystem lock-in via proprietary chips while monetizing the necessary accessory at a high margin ($159), despite aesthetic and functional risks.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal bets that convenience (auto-pairing) and form factor outweigh the loss of universal compatibility and the shock of paying for previously free accessories. The "odd" look is a temporary branding risk; if the design becomes iconic, it drives status. The "easy to lose" risk is mitigated by the charging case, which also solves the 5-hour battery limitation by providing multiple recharges on the go.

The primary blocker is the price perception ($159 vs. free). However, this is not a factual failure but a positioning challenge. If the wireless experience is significantly superior (no tangles, instant connection), the value proposition shifts from "audio cable" to "seamless utility." The anger over removing the jack is a one-time transition cost that competitors will eventually follow, making early adoption a strategic advantage rather than a defect.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Keep the jack. Result: Missed opportunity to lead the industry standard and lower accessory margins long-term.
2.  **Launch wired USB-C/Lightning earbuds:** Cheaper, but fails to solve the "tangle" problem or establish a wireless ecosystem moat.
3.  **Lower price to $99:** Reduces margin and perceived premium status; $159 anchors the product as a flagship accessory.

**Pre-mortem:**
If this fails, it is because the battery life degrades rapidly after one year, or the proprietary chip creates connectivity issues that frustrate users more than wires did. However, given the control over both phone and chip, integration risks are lower than third-party solutions.

**Verdict:**
No factual blocker exists. The risks are execution and marketing challenges, not fundamental flaws. The strategic upside of owning the audio standard and ecosystem outweighs the transient customer friction. The "odd" design is a necessary differentiator.

DECISION: GO
