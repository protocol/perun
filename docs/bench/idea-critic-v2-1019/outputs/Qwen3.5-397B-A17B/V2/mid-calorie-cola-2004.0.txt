The claim is that a premium, mid-calorie cola targeting young men will capture value lost to low-carb trends and diet growth. The steelman: a "best of both worlds" product unlocks a latent segment of calorie-conscious consumers who reject the taste of artificial sweeteners but fear full-sugar loads, provided the taste profile is indistinguishable from the flagship.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The primary blocker is the **contradictory value proposition** exposed by the risks: the product attempts to straddle two distinct psychographics (taste-first vs. calorie-first) and risks satisfying neither. Full-sugar loyalists will reject the formulation change or premium price; diet drinkers will reject the remaining carbohydrates. The "half-sugar" position is a classic "stuck in the middle" failure mode, not a differentiated niche.

Furthermore, the **economic logic is flawed**. Pricing a trial product at a premium during a trend characterized by cost-consciousness (or skepticism of fads) creates a high barrier to entry for a product with unproven utility. If the low-carb trend is a fad, the long-term ROI on a dedicated SKUs and large campaign is negative. If it is permanent, the diet segment already serves it. The existence of a rival launching a similar drink suggests a "me-too" battle for a potentially non-existent market size, doubling the risk of cannibalization without expanding the total addressable market.

The pre-mortem is clear: the launch burns campaign budget, confuses the brand portfolio, and achieves negligible share because the core user base rejects the compromise. The "young men" demographic is too narrow to sustain a premium CPG launch without a functional benefit that this product lacks (it offers neither full taste nor zero calories).

No evidence is provided that a significant cohort exists who *cannot* buy diet due to taste but *will not* buy regular due to carbs, specifically at a higher price point. Without proof of this specific gap, the plan is a speculative bet against established consumer behavior.

Blockers: Contradictory positioning satisfying neither segment; premium pricing on unproven demand; high cannibalization risk.
Risks: Trend transience; rival saturation; brand confusion.

DECISION: NO-GO
