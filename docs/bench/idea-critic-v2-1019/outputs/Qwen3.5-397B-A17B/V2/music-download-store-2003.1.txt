**Steelman:** A dominant hardware vendor leverages its installed base to launch a licensed, per-song store at $0.99, creating a legal, convenient alternative to piracy that locks users deeper into its ecosystem.

**Attack & Options:**
The "free" competitor is superior on price but inferior on reliability, safety, and integration. Subscription models (the rival path) fail in this era due to low broadband penetration and consumer preference for ownership. The "walled garden" limitation (own computers only) is initially a constraint but functions as a strategic moat, driving hardware sales. Margin concerns are valid but secondary; the primary value is not store profit, but increased hardware velocity and ecosystem stickiness. Label withdrawal is a risk, yet labels are desperate for a revenue model to replace collapsing CD sales, making them motivated partners despite friction.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the north star of selling more portable players. Failure would occur if the interface is clunky or if labels demand pricing that breaks the impulse-buy threshold. However, the proposed price point hits the psychological sweet spot. The "do nothing" option guarantees ceding the digital future to pirates or rivals.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
No factual blockers exist in the prompt. The risks (margin, licensing, platform limits) are execution hurdles, not fundamental flaws. The "free" alternative lacks the seamless integration this proposal offers. The subscription alternative is premature for the 2003 market context.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** None. The risks are manageable trade-offs, not show-stoppers.
*   **Risks:** Label relations, margin pressure, platform exclusivity slowing initial adoption.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If label licensing deals cannot be signed within 6 months, or if initial attach rates (songs per device) remain below 0.5 after one quarter, pivot to a subscription model or renegotiate pricing.
*   **Leading Metric:** Songs sold per active device.

The proposal transforms a threat (piracy) into a revenue stream and a hardware differentiator. It beats "do nothing" and is better timed than subscriptions.
