The claim is that launching the world's cheapest car at $2,500 from a new factory will successfully transition scooter families to car ownership. Steelman: Unbeatable price creates a new market segment, driving volume sufficient to offset thin margins and land risks.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test on three fronts. First, the "cheapest" positioning is a strategic liability, not an asset; in automotive markets, low price often signals unsafe or inferior quality, directly contradicting the core need of families protecting their children. If buyers perceive the car as unsafe due to cost-cutting, volume targets will not be met regardless of price. Second, the land protests represent a critical path blocker; a new dedicated factory cannot reach the required high volumes if construction is delayed or halted by civil unrest, destroying the economic model before production begins. Third, the alternatives (scooters and used cars) are not just cheaper but proven; a used car offers higher status and perceived safety for a similar price, while scooters avoid the financing burden entirely.

The pre-mortem reveals the most likely failure mode: the brand becomes synonymous with "dangerous cheapness," sales stall at early adopter levels, and the fixed costs of the unfinished or underutilized factory bankrupt the venture. The cost of delay is high, but the cost of proceeding with a compromised site and a stigmatized brand is total loss.

Blockers:
1. Land protests threaten the physical existence of the dedicated factory, making high-volume targets unachievable as proposed.
2. "Cheapest" marketing actively undermines the safety trust required for family buyers, likely suppressing demand below breakeven.
3. Used car alternatives offer better perceived value/safety, invalidating the assumption that price alone drives switching.

Risks:
1. Margin erosion preventing R&D for safety improvements.
2. Financing hurdles for first-time buyers despite low sticker price.

This plan relies on assumptions about consumer behavior and site stability that are directly contradicted by the raised risks. The factory constraint and brand positioning are fatal flaws in the current formulation.

DECISION: NO-GO
