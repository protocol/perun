The claim is that a consumer electronics manufacturer should enter the home console market with a CD-based, 3D graphics system after a partnership cancellation, competing directly against two entrenched incumbents by leveraging cheaper media and developer tools.

**Steelman:** The shift from cartridges to CDs drastically reduces manufacturing costs and increases storage, enabling full-motion video and richer audio that cartridges cannot match. By lowering barriers for third-party developers, the company can bypass the incumbents' reliance on first-party franchises and rapidly build a software library that appeals to a maturing audience seeking cinematic experiences.

**Attack:** The proposal faces existential blockers. First, the company has zero experience as a platform holder; managing an ecosystem of external developers requires entirely new competencies in licensing, certification, and marketing that differ fundamentally from hardware manufacturing. Second, the "thin or negative hardware margins" model requires massive upfront capital to subsidize units, creating extreme cash-flow risk if adoption lags. Third, timing is critical: an incumbent launching a competing 3D system simultaneously creates a "format war" scenario where the new entrant lacks the brand loyalty to survive a split market. Finally, without a killer first-party franchise, relying solely on external developers is speculative; developers follow installed bases, not just technical specs.

**Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option preserves capital and avoids a likely costly failure in a hostile market. A "licensing only" approach (providing the chip/CD tech to others without building the console) captures the upside of the media shift without the platform risk.

**Verdict:** The lack of platform experience combined with the capital intensity of negative-margin hardware and the immediate threat of a simultaneous 3D launch from a dominant rival constitutes a fatal risk profile. The assumption that cheaper discs alone will woo developers away from established ecosystems is unproven and likely false without a guaranteed installed base. The proposal fails to address how to bridge the gap between "no experience" and "market leader" while burning cash on hardware subsidies.

DECISION: NO-GO
