**Steelman:** A novel, self-balancing electric transporter will create a new urban mobility category, justified by strong IP, investor backing, and a premium launch strategy targeting early adopters and institutional buyers despite regulatory ambiguity.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" and "fit" tests. At $5,000 (roughly three times the cost of a high-end bicycle or motorcycle in 2001), the device targets a vanishingly small niche. The "institutional buyer" use case is weakened by the unclear legal status; police and security firms cannot deploy assets that may be deemed illegal on sidewalks or roads tomorrow.

The pre-mortem is stark: The device becomes a "laughing stock" rather than a revolution. The specific design (two wheels, standing posture) makes riders highly conspicuous, directly triggering the identified risk of social embarrassment. Unlike a car or bike, it offers no weather protection, negligible cargo capacity, and limited range, yet demands a premium price. The "heavy publicity" creates a high-visibility failure mode; if the public perceives it as a toy for the wealthy rather than a serious transport solution, the brand is permanently tainted.

The "do nothing" or "wait" alternative is superior. Delaying launch allows for:
1. Clarification of legal frameworks.
2. Reduction in battery/component costs to lower the price point.
3. Development of models with seating or cargo capacity.

Launching now with a factory sized for 40,000 units assumes demand that does not exist outside of hype. The unit economics likely fail once marketing costs to overcome social stigma are included. The risks (legal, social, utility) are not merely "unproven"; they are structural flaws in the current value proposition. A capped pilot (e.g., 500 units for controlled campus environments) would be a valid "go-if," but a full factory launch is unjustified.

**Blockers:**
1. **Price/Value Mismatch:** $5,000 price point exceeds utility compared to established, legal alternatives (bikes, mopeds).
2. **Regulatory Void:** Launching before legal status is defined invites bans that destroy the primary use cases.
3. **Social Friction:** High conspicuousness without clear status signaling risks market rejection.

**Risks:** Battery technology limitations, range anxiety, weather dependency.

**Kill Criteria:** If pilot users do not log >5 miles/day consistently within 3 months, halt expansion.

DECISION: NO-GO
