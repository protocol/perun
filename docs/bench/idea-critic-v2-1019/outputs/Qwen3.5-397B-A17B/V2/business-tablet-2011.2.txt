**Steelman:** Launch a niche, phone-tethered 7-inch tablet on an untested OS to seed a new platform, betting that early adopters will tolerate missing native email to access a unique form factor before the rival dominates.

**Attack:**
1.  **Value Mismatch:** The company's core strength is business email. Shipping a device explicitly *without* built-in email, calendar, or contacts (unless paired) contradicts its primary brand promise and alienates its most loyal customer base.
2.  **Pricing Failure:** Matching the price of a rival's larger, established tablet with a superior app store makes this smaller, feature-deficient device economically irrational for consumers.
3.  **Platform Risk:** An untested OS lacks developer commitment. Without a critical mass of apps, the device offers no advantage over the rival's ecosystem, rendering the "new form factor" insufficient to drive adoption.
4.  **Strategic Confusion:** Positioning a business leader as a media-only companion device creates market confusion. The "tethering" requirement adds friction, reducing the standalone value proposition to near zero.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid brand dilution and financial loss on a likely failed product.
*   **Delay:** Wait until the new OS has native email and a viable app ecosystem before launching hardware.
*   **Differentiate:** Launch with a lower price point to justify the reduced functionality, rather than matching the premium rival.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to poor reviews citing the lack of email and high price. Developers ignore the sparse user base. Sales flatline, and the new OS fails to gain traction, wasting the acquisition cost.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal ships without core functionality (email) that defines the manufacturer's market leadership.
*   **Fact:** The price equals a superior competitor product, offering no economic incentive to switch.
*   **Fact:** The OS is untested with no guarantee of developer support.

**Risks:** Brand erosion, financial loss on unsold inventory, failure of the acquired OS strategy.

**Conclusion:** The proposal fails to leverage the company's core competency, offers poor value relative to competitors, and relies on unproven technology without a clear path to ecosystem viability. It cannot beat "do nothing" or a delayed, feature-complete launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
