**Steelman**: This is the only viable solution for truly global, seamless mobile connectivity, serving a high-value niche of international travelers that ground networks cannot reach due to infrastructure gaps.

**Attack**: The economics are fragile. With $5 billion in debt, the service requires rapid, massive adoption to service interest and principal. However, the value proposition is misaligned with the market reality of 1998. Ground cellular networks are expanding exponentially, eroding the "global" gap this system fills, specifically in the urban centers where business travelers actually spend most of their time.

The pricing strategy ($3,000 handsets, high per-minute rates) restricts the Total Addressable Market to a tiny elite. Yet, even this niche faces superior alternatives: roaming agreements between expanding cellular carriers are becoming cheaper and more convenient. Furthermore, the product has critical functional deficits: bulky handsets and poor indoor penetration make the service unreliable exactly when high-value users need it most (inside offices, hotels, and conference centers).

**Alternatives**:
1.  **Do Nothing/Wait**: Allow ground networks to mature and roam. Cost: $0. Risk: Missing early mover advantage, but avoids massive debt burden.
2.  **Pivot to Niche/Gov**: Target maritime, aviation, or government/defense sectors where bulk/price matter less and coverage is non-negotiable. This reduces the required subscriber volume.
3.  **Proceed as Proposed**: High risk of immediate cash flow failure due to slow subscriber uptake against rigid debt obligations.

**Pre-mortem**: Two years post-launch, subscriber growth is 10% of projections. Ground coverage hits 80% of global GDP. The company cannot service its debt, leading to bankruptcy or distressed asset sale. The "global" feature becomes a commodity provided by terrestrial carriers via roaming, rendering the satellite constellation a stranded asset.

**Blockers**:
1.  **Unit Economics Mismatch**: Debt service requires mass adoption; product price/utility limits adoption to a micro-niche.
2.  **Competitive Obsolescence**: Rapid ground network expansion invalidates the core "coverage" differentiator before the business can mature.

**Risks**: Execution delays, handset miniaturization failures, regulatory hurdles in key markets.

**Kill Criteria**: If subscriber acquisition costs exceed $5,000 per user or if ground network coverage exceeds 60% of target business hubs within 18 months, the project must be paused or restructured immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
