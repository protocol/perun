Steelman: A distinct luxury brand with separate dealers and a flagship sedan can capture value by offering European-level quality at a lower price, overcoming the parent's economy reputation through structural separation.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly mitigates the primary brand-risk via a separate name and dealer network, preventing the "economy" stigma from contaminating the luxury product. The high development cost is sunk; abandoning now guarantees total loss, whereas launching offers a path to recovery. A single model is a standard, focused entry strategy for new luxury entrants.

**Risks:** 
1. **Brand Perception:** Consumers may still associate the new brand with the parent company despite separation.
2. **Dealer Viability:** High fixed costs of a new network could bleed cash if volume targets are missed.
3. **Incumbent Retaliation:** Established European rivals may drop prices or increase marketing to crush the new entrant.
4. **Line-up Thinness:** Lack of body-style variety may limit market penetration compared to established competitors.

**Analysis:**
The "do nothing" alternative results in writing off heavy development costs and remaining confined to the low-margin mid-market. The proposed path attacks the only viable growth vector: moving upmarket without dragging the economy brand baggage. Pricing below incumbents while matching quality creates a compelling value proposition that disrupts the status quo. The separate dealer network is not merely a cost but a necessary signal of luxury status and service quality, essential for credibility. While the single-model launch is risky, it concentrates resources to ensure the flagship succeeds before expanding. The risks are executional and market-based, not fundamental flaws in the logic; they are manageable compared to the certainty of stagnation or total loss of sunk costs.

The pre-mortem suggests failure would stem from under-investing in marketing to establish the new brand's distinct identity or failing to maintain the quality gap. However, these are operational challenges, not reasons to halt a strategically sound pivot. The cost of delay is high, as competitors will not stand still.

DECISION: GO
