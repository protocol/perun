**Steelman:** A hardware incumbent can leverage its manufacturing scale and developer relations to disrupt a duopoly by offering a superior 3D architecture and cost-effective media, overcoming its lack of platform experience.

**Skeptic:** The proposal admits zero experience as a platform holder while facing two entrenched rivals with loyal user bases and iconic first-party software. Consoles operate on razor-thin or negative hardware margins, requiring massive upfront capital recouped solely through software licensing—a model unproven for this company. The timing coincides with a direct competitor's 3D launch, risking immediate obsolescence or a brutal price war the newcomer cannot sustain without an existing installed base.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" option avoids catastrophic capital loss in a high-churn market. Alternatively, partnering as a peripheral maker for existing consoles leverages current brand strength without the risk of building a walled garden from scratch. Courting developers is futile without users; users won't buy without developers. This chicken-and-egg problem is the primary blocker for new entrants.

**Kill-Criteria:** If the company cannot secure binding commitments from top-tier third-party developers before tooling begins, the library will be too weak to sell hardware. If hardware subsidies exceed cash reserves within 18 months, the venture collapses.

**Pre-mortem:** The console launches to poor reviews regarding game quality, developers reject the new ecosystem due to low install numbers, and the company is left with millions of unsold units and no software revenue to offset hardware losses, damaging the core brand.

**Blockers:**
1. **No Platform Experience:** The team has never managed a two-sided marketplace (gamers/developers) or subsidized hardware economics.
2. **Competitor Timing:** Launching against an incumbent's 3D entry creates an insurmountable marketing deficit.
3. **Developer Chicken-and-Egg:** No mechanism is proposed to break the cycle of needing users to get games and games to get users.

**Risks:** High capital burn, brand reputation damage, failure to secure key software titles.

The risks are existential, and the blockers are fundamental to the business model, not merely executional hurdles. Without a proven path to secure software content or absorb hardware losses, the proposal fails the viability test.

DECISION: NO-GO
