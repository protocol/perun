**Steelman:** Selling convenient, legal, high-quality singles at a fixed low price captures users unwilling to navigate illegal networks, creating a ecosystem lock-in that offsets thin hardware margins.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Free Competition:** File-sharing offers $0 but carries legal risk, poor quality, and friction. Convenience is the product, not just the file.
*   **Subscription Rivals:** Subscriptions require broadband penetration not yet universal in 2003; ownership appeals to dial-up and mobile users.
*   **Platform Lock-in:** Restricting to own computers initially limits reach but drives hardware sales, the company's core profit engine.
*   **Label Risk:** Labels are desperate for revenue control; a unified store is their best alternative to piracy chaos.
*   **Do Nothing:** Cedes the digital future to pirates or competitors, eventually cannibalizing hardware sales as music becomes the primary driver.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if labels demand unsustainable royalties making the 99-cent price unprofitable, or if encryption frustrates users enough to return to piracy. However, the brand's leverage with consumers forces label cooperation; they need this channel more than the maker needs any single catalog.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of seamless user experience. The "walled garden" is a feature, not a bug, ensuring reliability that open systems cannot match.

**Blockers:** None. The margin concern is real but secondary to the strategic necessity of controlling the music interface to sell hardware. The 99-cent price point is a psychological anchor to break the "free" expectation, not a standalone profit center.

**Risks:** Slow adoption if the catalog is incomplete at launch; competitor bundling. These are execution risks, not fundamental flaws.

**Kill Criteria:** If major labels refuse licensing within 6 months, or if attachment rate (songs per device) remains below 10% after one year, pivot to subscription or licensing the software to other hardware makers.

**Decision:** The proposal transforms a threat (piracy) into a revenue stream and a hardware differentiator. The integrated model beats the fragmented alternatives.

DECISION: GO
