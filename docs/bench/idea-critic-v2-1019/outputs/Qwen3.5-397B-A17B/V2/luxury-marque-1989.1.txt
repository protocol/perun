**Steelman:** A dedicated luxury brand with a separate dealer network and an all-new flagship sedan, priced below European incumbents but engineered to match their quality, will allow a mass-market economy manufacturer to capture the luxury segment without diluting its core brand equity.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker is **brand dissonance**. History shows economy manufacturers struggle to command luxury premiums under any badge if the parent company's reputation for "cheap" is dominant. However, a *separate* brand and *separate* dealer network directly mitigate this by physically and psychologically distancing the product from the economy lineage.

The **financial risk** is severe. Building a dealer network from scratch and engineering a flagship from the ground up requires massive upfront capital with no guarantee of volume. A single-model launch is fragile; if the flagship fails to resonate, there is no portfolio to absorb the shock. Yet, the proposal explicitly prices "well below" competitors. This creates a compelling value proposition: 90% of the prestige at 70% of the cost. This undercuts the incumbents' moat of "exclusivity" by redefining luxury as "attainable refinement."

The "thin line-up" risk is real but manageable for a launch; it forces focus. The "sunk cost" of development is irrelevant to the go/no-go decision; only future ROI matters. If the car matches European quality as claimed, the price differential creates an irresistible market wedge. The separate network is the critical enabler; without it, the brand dies. With it, the brand has a fighting chance to rewrite its narrative.

**Blockers:** None identified in the text. The risks (cost, brand perception, single model) are severe but are addressed by the specific mechanics of the proposal (separate network, price undercutting, focused engineering). They are risks, not facts proving failure.

**Risks:** High cash burn before profitability; potential failure to convince dealers to invest; lingering consumer skepticism despite separation.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders do not reach 15% of production capacity within 6 months of reveal, or if initial quality scores (J.D. Power equivalent) fall below the top 3 European rivals, halt expansion and pivot to a niche strategy.

**Decision:** The strategy creates a distinct entity with a clear value advantage. The risks are high but inherent to the opportunity, not fatal flaws in the logic.

DECISION: GO
