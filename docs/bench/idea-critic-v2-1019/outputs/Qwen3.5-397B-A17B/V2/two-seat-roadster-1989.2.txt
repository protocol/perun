**Steelman:** A low-volume, lightweight roadster can revive a dormant niche by prioritizing driving purity over specs, leveraging existing components to keep costs low, and targeting enthusiasts underserved by heavy, expensive competitors.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker is the claim that "mass-market makers regard this as a dead niche." If true, their exit signals structural unprofitability due to safety compliance costs and shrinking volume, not just lack of vision. However, the proposal counters this with "affordable" and "lightweight," implying a strategy of component reuse (front-engine, rear-drive) rather than clean-sheet engineering.

*Option A (Proposed):* Launch now. Upside: First-mover advantage in a blue ocean; high margin per unit if BOM is controlled. Risk: Volume too low to amortize tooling; safety regulations erase margin.
*Option B (Do Nothing):* Avoid risk. Result: Zero investment, zero return, continued irrelevance in the enthusiast segment.
*Option C (Wait/Validate):* Build a mule, test regulatory compliance costs, and secure pre-orders. Result: Delays launch but validates the "affordable" premise against real safety mandates.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
Assuming the goal is brand revitalization and margin growth, this fits if the "affordable" claim holds. If the car costs as much as a premium sedan due to low-volume parts and safety structures, it fails.
*Pre-mortem:* The car launches but is priced 20% above competitors due to unexpected safety reinforcement needs. Sales stall after the initial enthusiast wave because the two-seat limitation alienates practical second-car buyers. The "dead niche" was dead because regulations made the business model impossible, not because of lack of desire.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*Blockers:* None explicitly proven in the text. The "shrinking segment" is a trend, not a hard stop, provided the low-cost hypothesis is true. The lack of heritage is a marketing hurdle, not a technical blocker.
*Risks:* Regulatory cost overruns; limited total addressable market; rapid sales fade.

**Decision:**
The proposal survives the attack because the risks are manageable execution challenges, not fundamental contradictions. The "dead niche" is an opportunity if the cost structure differs from the incumbents who abandoned it. The specific constraints (lightweight, existing layout) suggest a viable path to profitability that avoids the pitfalls that killed previous attempts. The potential brand equity and margin density outweigh the volume risks.

DECISION: GO
