The claim is that a $5 billion satellite network can succeed in 1998 by targeting business travelers with $3,000 handsets and high per-minute rates. The steelman: this is the only solution for true global coverage where terrestrial cells cannot reach, creating a monopoly on connectivity for high-value users.

However, the attack reveals fatal blockers. First, the cost structure is inverted: the price point ($3,000 hardware + high usage fees) restricts the addressable market to a tiny fraction of the projected user base, making it mathematically impossible to generate the cash flow required to service the massive debt load mentioned. Second, the "do nothing" alternative (waiting for ground cellular expansion) is rapidly eroding the unique value proposition; ground networks are cheaper, work indoors, and use smaller phones. Third, the product itself is flawed for the target demographic: bulky handsets that fail indoors are unacceptable to business travelers who primarily operate in urban centers and office buildings where ground networks excel.

The pre-mortem is clear: the company launches, fails to hit aggressive subscriber targets due to price and product limitations, cannot meet debt obligations, and declares bankruptcy within months. The risk is not just "execution difficulty"; it is a fundamental mismatch between the fixed costs (debt service) and the realistic revenue potential of the niche market. A cheaper, staged approach is impossible here because the capital is already sunk, but proceeding with the *current* commercial terms guarantees failure. The only viable path would be a drastic price reduction and subsidy model, which the current debt structure cannot support, or a pivot to government/defense contracts only, which is not the proposal.

The evidence provided (spreading ground networks, poor indoor performance, high price, debt pressure) directly contradicts the core assumption that sufficient volume can be achieved quickly enough to survive. This is a classic case of a solution looking for a problem that is being solved more cheaply by competitors.

Blockers: Debt service requires mass adoption that the $3,000 price point and poor indoor utility physically prevent; ground networks are rapidly eliminating the coverage gap.
Risks: Sales channel unpreparedness, bulky hardware rejection.

DECISION: NO-GO
