The claim rests on a contrarian bet: that the absence of small British roadsters represents an unserved demand rather than a validated market death. The steelman is that a lightweight, affordable, front-engine rear-drive convertible can revive a dormant niche by prioritizing driving purity over mass-market utility, capturing enthusiasts ignored by safety-bloated, heavy alternatives.

However, the skeptic identifies a fatal blocker in the provided facts. The proposal admits the segment has "shrunk for years" and that mass-market makers already regard it as a "dead niche." These are not mere risks; they are market signals from entities with superior data and heritage. The proposal offers no evidence of a changing trend, a new customer demographic, or a cost structure that defies the "costly" safety rules cited. To launch now is to assume the entire industry is wrong without a single fact to support why.

The "better way" analysis fails because the only alternative presented is "do nothing," which aligns with the current market reality. A cheaper, staged approach (e.g., a concept car to test demand) is not proposed; the plan is a full launch. The pre-mortem is self-fulfilling: sales fade after early enthusiasts buy because the total addressable market is too small to sustain production.

The kill criteria are absent. There is no metric defined to prove the niche is alive before committing to a launch. Without a leading indicator of demand or a strategy to overcome the admitted safety cost disadvantage, the plan relies entirely on hope against established industry consensus. The risk of fading sales is not a future possibility; it is the logical conclusion of entering a shrinking market with a limited two-seat product and no heritage to justify a premium.

The strongest attack is the admission that the segment has shrunk for years. This fact alone contradicts the core premise of a viable launch. Until the proposer demonstrates why this historical trend will reverse immediately upon their entry, the project is a capital incinerator.

DECISION: NO-GO
