The proposal addresses a critical stagnation: the current supplier fails to deliver processors that are cool and efficient enough for modern thin laptops, causing the product roadmap to lag behind the market leader. Remaining on the current path guarantees continued technical inferiority in a segment defined by portability and performance. While the risks are substantial, they are manageable with precise execution rather than reasons to halt.

Announcing early does carry the risk of freezing current sales, but delaying the announcement until launch creates a chaotic transition with no runway for customer expectation management. A controlled, transparent timeline allows the market to anticipate the shift rather than react with confusion. The concern regarding developers rebuilding software is mitigated by the proposed translation layer. Although translated software will run slower, this is an acceptable temporary compromise to ensure immediate compatibility while the ecosystem naturally migrates to native applications over the two-year window.

The fear of weakening brand distinctiveness is outweighed by the necessity of competitive relevance. A distinctive brand built on obsolete technology quickly becomes irrelevant. The reference to a painful earlier transition serves as a vital lesson, not a prohibition. It highlights the need for rigorous project management and customer communication during the migration, not an avoidance of the migration itself. The status quo poses a greater existential threat than the transition risks. Without this move, the company cannot compete in the thin laptop sector, effectively ceding the market to rivals using the dominant architecture. The two-year timeline provides sufficient duration to engineer the translation layer, optimize thermal performance, and manage the supply chain shift without rushing critical quality controls. Approval is required immediately to initiate the engineering roadmap and secure supply agreements with the dominant manufacturer.

DECISION: GO
