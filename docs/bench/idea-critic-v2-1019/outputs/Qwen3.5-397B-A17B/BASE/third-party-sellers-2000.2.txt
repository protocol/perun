The retailer currently lacks profitability and holds limited inventory across books, music, and general goods. The proposal introduces a marketplace model where independent sellers, including direct competitors, list items alongside the retailer's own stock for a transaction fee. This shifts the business from a pure inventory holder to a platform operator, generating revenue without upfront inventory costs.

The primary risk is price undercutting, where third-party sellers offer lower prices on the same product pages. However, for an unprofitable entity, losing a sale to a fee-generating third-party transaction is financially superior to losing the sale entirely or holding costly inventory that does not turn. Cannibalization of new sales by used copies is a valid concern, yet used items often capture a customer segment unwilling to pay full price, thereby expanding total market reach rather than simply displacing new sales.

Regarding supplier objections, the long-term leverage of becoming the dominant traffic hub may eventually force publishers and suppliers to accept the new reality, as seen in similar industry shifts. While control over service quality and potential trust issues are significant, these can be mitigated through strict seller performance metrics and clear customer disclosures, which are standard operational adjustments rather than fundamental blockers.

The critical fact is the current lack of profitability. Continuing with a capital-intensive inventory model without profit is unsustainable. This proposal offers an immediate path to positive cash flow through fees while leveraging existing traffic. The risks of inaction and continued losses outweigh the operational challenges of managing third-party sellers. The potential to become the definitive destination for both new and used goods creates a network effect that competitors cannot easily match once established. Delaying this transition allows other platforms to capture this liquidity first. The strategic necessity of pivoting to an asset-light, fee-based revenue stream to achieve solvency makes immediate approval the only logical course.

DECISION: GO
