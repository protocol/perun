The proposal to advance the US launch from September to May 1995 carries excessive strategic risk. Introducing the console at $399, which is $100 higher than the competitor's announced price, immediately positions the product as a premium outlier without the software library to justify the cost. Consumers are unlikely to pay a significant premium for a machine with few available games, especially when a cheaper alternative arrives shortly thereafter.

Furthermore, the decision to ship only to select retailers creates immediate channel conflict. Excluded partners may retaliate by deprioritizing the brand in the future or favoring the competitor, damaging long-term distribution relationships. The technical constraints compound these issues; since the hardware is difficult to program for 3D and developers were not prepared for an early release, the software gap will likely widen rather than close before the competitor's arrival.

Launching early under these conditions sacrifices the opportunity for a coordinated, full-scale market entry. It exposes the company to negative consumer perception based on high price and low content availability, while simultaneously alienating key retail partners. The potential first-mover advantage is negated by the inability to support the launch with adequate software and competitive pricing. Proceeding now invites failure against a better-prepared, lower-priced rival. The risks of retailer retaliation, developer unpreparedness, and consumer rejection outweigh the benefits of an early, limited release.

DECISION: NO-GO
