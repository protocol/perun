The proposal presents a high-risk venture with significant unresolved barriers. The legal ambiguity regarding sidewalk and road usage creates an immediate regulatory hurdle that could halt operations or invite costly litigation before market penetration occurs. Without clear legal standing, widespread consumer adoption is impossible, regardless of publicity.

The price point of $5,000 is prohibitive for the mass market, limiting the potential customer base to early adopters or niche institutional buyers. Since uses beyond institutional buyers remain unproven, relying on them alone cannot justify a factory sized for 40,000 units annually. This production capacity assumes a demand volume that currently lacks evidence, creating a dangerous mismatch between supply capabilities and realistic sales projections.

Furthermore, the product's limitations—specifically the inability to carry cargo and the social stigma of riders feeling conspicuous—directly undermine its utility and appeal for daily personal transport. Heavy publicity and prominent investors do not negate these fundamental product-market fit issues. Launching now with a large-scale factory locks the company into high fixed costs before validating whether consumers will actually use the device in real-world scenarios.

The combination of unclear legality, excessive pricing, unproven use cases, social friction, and limited utility suggests that proceeding immediately would likely result in significant financial loss. The risks outweigh the potential rewards at this stage. A pause is required to resolve legal status, validate consumer demand at a lower scale, and potentially adjust pricing or design before committing to mass production.

DECISION: NO-GO
