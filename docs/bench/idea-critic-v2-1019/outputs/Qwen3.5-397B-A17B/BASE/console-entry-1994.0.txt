The proposal outlines a high-stakes entry into the home console market following the cancellation of a partnership with a leading game console maker. The plan involves launching a proprietary CD-based console featuring 3D graphics in 1994, aiming to attract third-party developers through superior tools and lower disc manufacturing costs compared to cartridges. However, the risks presented are severe and arguably insurmountable given the current constraints.

The company possesses zero experience as a console platform holder, creating a fundamental operational gap. The market is a duopoly controlled by two entrenched competitors with fierce customer loyalty and powerful first-party software franchises. Entering this arena requires overcoming significant switching costs for consumers who are already invested in existing ecosystems. Furthermore, an incumbent rival is scheduled to launch a competing 3D console simultaneously, ensuring immediate, direct competition from an established entity.

Financially, the model relies on selling hardware at thin or negative margins, a strategy that demands substantial upfront capital and confidence in long-term software revenue recoupment. Without a proven track record in platform management or a guaranteed library of hit games, the risk of catastrophic financial loss is extreme. Securing outside developers is critical yet uncertain; these partners are unlikely to commit resources to an unproven platform when established alternatives offer guaranteed audiences. The combination of no industry experience, intense competitive pressure, simultaneous rival launches, and a precarious financial model creates a failure scenario that cannot be mitigated under the current proposal. The lack of existing networks or files further exacerbates the inability to execute the necessary developer outreach and supply chain logistics required for success. Proceeding now would expose the organization to unacceptable liability without a clear path to viability.

DECISION: NO-GO
