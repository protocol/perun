The proposal targets a segment deemed dead by mass-market competitors, relying on the assumption that nostalgia for small British roadsters translates into viable modern demand. However, the risks presented are substantial and currently unmitigated. The market has shrunk consistently, suggesting a structural decline rather than a temporary dip. Limiting the vehicle to two seats drastically reduces the potential customer base, making volume targets difficult to achieve without premium pricing that contradicts the "affordable" goal.

Furthermore, adhering to modern safety regulations for convertibles typically increases weight and cost, directly undermining the core value propositions of being lightweight and affordable. The company lacks specific heritage in this class, meaning there is no existing brand equity to justify the purchase to enthusiasts who might otherwise forgive flaws in a legacy model. Finally, the reliance on a finite pool of early enthusiasts creates a high probability of rapid sales fatigue once that initial group is saturated, with no clear strategy to attract broader, sustained demand.

Launching now without a demonstrated method to overcome the cost implications of safety rules, a plan to expand the buyer base beyond two-seat limitations, or a strategy to sustain sales after the initial enthusiast wave constitutes an unacceptable financial exposure. The combination of a shrinking market, high regulatory costs, and limited audience scope presents a failure scenario that outweighs the potential niche appeal. The current facts do not support immediate approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
