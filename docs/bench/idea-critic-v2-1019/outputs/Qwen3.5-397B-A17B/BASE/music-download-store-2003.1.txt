The proposal leverages the company's existing strength in portable music players to address the crisis of widespread file-sharing. By offering a legal, convenient alternative at a fixed price of 99 cents per song, the strategy directly competes with free piracy through ease of use rather than price alone. While the risk of labels withdrawing licenses is significant, securing catalogs from all major partners creates a comprehensive library that illegal networks struggle to match in reliability and quality.

The concern regarding thin margins is valid; however, the primary objective here is not immediate high profitability from song sales, but rather the preservation and acceleration of hardware sales. If the ecosystem locks users into the company's computers and players, the long-term value of the installed base outweighs short-term software margins. The limitation of the store working only on the company's own computers initially acts as a strategic differentiator, driving hardware adoption rather than serving as a fatal flaw.

Regarding rival strategies focusing on subscriptions, consumer behavior in 2003 suggests a strong preference for ownership over renting. Users want to collect and keep specific tracks, a model subscriptions do not fully satisfy. The light copy protection strikes a necessary balance between label security and user convenience, reducing the friction that drives people back to piracy.

Delaying this launch to revise the model or wait for market clarity would be a catastrophic error. The window to define the digital music standard is open now. If rivals establish their subscription models first, or if piracy becomes the sole entrenched habit, the opportunity to monetize the digital shift will vanish. The company must act decisively to legitimize the market before it solidifies around free theft or an unproven rental model. The risks of inaction far exceed the operational challenges outlined. The proposal offers a clear path to control the ecosystem, satisfy label partners, and give consumers a reason to pay.

DECISION: GO
