The proposal aims to democratize car ownership in India by targeting families currently using scooters, with an aggressive price point of 100,000 rupees. While the market opportunity is vast, the identified risks present immediate, critical threats to viability that cannot be ignored at this stage.

First, the factory site faces active protests from farmers regarding land acquisition. Proceeding without a resolved land dispute invites legal injunctions, construction delays, and severe reputational damage. A dedicated high-volume factory cannot function if the physical site is contested, making the production timeline uncertain.

Second, marketing the vehicle explicitly as "the cheapest car in the world" creates a significant psychological barrier. In the automotive sector, low price is often conflated with poor safety and low quality. Since safety doubts are already a raised risk, this branding strategy amplifies consumer fear rather than alleviating it. Families prioritizing safety may reject the brand entirely, opting for slightly more expensive used cars or sticking with scooters, which are proven and cheaper.

Third, the competitive landscape includes established used car markets and scooters. Without a clear value proposition beyond price, the product lacks defensibility. If the "cheapest" label tarnishes the brand image, volume targets will not be met, undermining the economic model required to sustain such low margins.

The combination of unresolved land conflicts, a potentially toxic branding strategy, and inherent safety concerns suggests the current plan is too fragile to execute. Launching now exposes the company to operational stoppages and market rejection. The proposal requires substantial revision to address land security, reframe the marketing narrative to emphasize value and safety rather than just low cost, and validate consumer willingness to adopt a new entry-level segment before committing capital.

DECISION: NO-GO
