The retailer currently lacks profitability and holds limited inventory across books, music, and general goods. The proposal introduces a marketplace model where independent sellers, including direct competitors, list items alongside the retailer's own stock for a transaction fee. This shifts the business from a pure inventory holder to a platform operator, generating revenue without upfront capital expenditure on stock.

The primary risk is price undercutting, where third-party sellers offer lower prices on the same product pages. However, since the retailer is not yet profitable, matching or beating every competitor's price on owned inventory is likely unsustainable. Allowing lower-priced options keeps customers on the site rather than driving them to competitors, preserving traffic and enabling fee collection. Even if a customer buys a cheaper used copy from a third party, the retailer earns a fee without bearing the cost of goods sold or the risk of unsold inventory.

Concerns regarding service quality and seller misbehavior threaten customer trust. Yet, these risks can be managed through strict seller agreements, performance monitoring, and clear customer recourse policies, which are standard operational adjustments rather than structural blockers. Supplier objections are valid but may be mitigated by distinguishing between new and used markets; publishers often lack control over the secondary market anyway. The potential loss of new sales to used copies is offset by the fact that the retailer currently makes no profit on new sales. Capturing a margin on a used sale via fees is superior to losing the customer entirely or selling new units at a loss.

Given the current lack of profitability, the immediate need is to diversify revenue streams and expand selection without increasing inventory risk. The fee-per-sale model aligns costs with revenue, protecting cash flow. The risks identified are manageable operational challenges, whereas the status quo guarantees continued losses. The strategic pivot to a hybrid model offers the only clear path toward margin generation in the short term.

DECISION: GO
