The proposal targets a massive demographic shift by offering families an upgrade from scooters to a dedicated four-seat vehicle at an unprecedented price point. While the ambition to democratize car ownership is compelling, the current plan carries unacceptable levels of unmitigated risk that threaten immediate failure.

The most critical threat is the active opposition from farmers regarding the factory site. Proceeding without a resolved land dispute invites legal injunctions, production delays, and severe reputational damage before a single unit is sold. A new dedicated factory cannot operate if the ground it stands on is contested.

Furthermore, the core marketing strategy hinges on being the "cheapest car in the world." In the automotive sector, where safety is paramount, this label often equates to "dangerous" or "flimsy" in the consumer's mind. With existing alternatives like used cars and scooters offering proven reliability at lower or comparable costs, a brand new vehicle perceived as unsafe will struggle to gain trust. The fear that cost-cutting compromises quality is not just a doubt; it is a likely barrier to adoption for families protecting their loved ones.

Launching now, with these specific unresolved issues, exposes the company to financial loss and brand erosion. The land conflict must be settled, and the value proposition must shift from "cheapest" to "safe and affordable" to overcome consumer skepticism. Without addressing these foundational flaws, the high volume targets are unachievable. The risks outweigh the potential rewards in the current configuration.

DECISION: NO-GO
