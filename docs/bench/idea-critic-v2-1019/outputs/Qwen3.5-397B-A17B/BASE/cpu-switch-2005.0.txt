The current processor supplier has failed to deliver chips that are cool and efficient enough for thin laptops, causing the company's roadmap to lag behind the dominant market leader. This technological deficit threatens the viability of the product line in a market increasingly demanding portability and performance. Staying with the current architecture guarantees continued inferiority in form factor and efficiency.

The proposal to migrate the entire computer line to the dominant architecture within two years directly addresses this critical hardware shortfall. While the risks are substantial, they are manageable compared to the certainty of obsolescence. The concern regarding sales freezing upon early announcement is a short-term tactical issue that can be mitigated through careful marketing phrasing rather than delaying the strategic pivot. The requirement for developers to rebuild software is an inevitable industry shift; delaying it only prolongs the period where the platform is less attractive to new development.

Performance degradation in translated software is a known temporary trade-off. As native applications are rebuilt over the two-year window, performance will normalize. The risk of weakening brand distinctiveness is outweighed by the necessity of offering competitive hardware; a unique brand with unusable, hot, and thick laptops holds no market value. Finally, while the previous transition was painful, the stakes today are higher due to the specific failure of the current supplier to meet thermal and efficiency standards. The cost of inaction exceeds the disruption of migration. The two-year timeline provides a reasonable buffer to execute the switch and develop the translation layer without rushing critical quality controls. Approval is required immediately to initiate the supply chain negotiations and engineering roadmap necessary to meet the two-year shipping target.

DECISION: GO
