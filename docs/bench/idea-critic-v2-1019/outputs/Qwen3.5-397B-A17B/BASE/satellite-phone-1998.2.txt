The proposal presents a catastrophic financial mismatch between fixed obligations and realistic market potential. With $5 billion in debt, primarily borrowed, the company faces immediate, rigid payment schedules that demand rapid, massive subscriber adoption. However, the product offering is fundamentally misaligned with market realities. A $3,000 handset price point combined with calls costing several dollars per minute restricts the addressable market to a tiny fraction of international business travelers. This niche is insufficient to generate the revenue volume required to service such enormous debt loads.

Furthermore, the competitive landscape has shifted unfavorably. Ground-based cellular networks are expanding quickly, offering cheaper, more convenient alternatives that erode the unique value proposition of satellite connectivity. The product itself suffers from critical functional deficits: bulky hardware and poor indoor performance severely limit utility for the very executives who would be expected to pay premium prices. If the device fails to work inside offices or hotels, its primary use case collapses.

Operational readiness is also absent. Sales channels are not prepared to distribute the product, creating a bottleneck that will delay revenue generation precisely when cash flow is most critical. The combination of high fixed costs, a shrinking target market due to pricing and competition, product performance issues, and unready distribution creates a perfect storm for default. Launching now guarantees immediate cash burn without a viable path to the subscriber numbers needed for survival. The risks overwhelmingly outweigh any potential first-mover advantage, which is already being negated by terrestrial network growth. Proceeding as proposed invites certain financial failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
