**Steelman:** Direct-to-consumer (DTC) distribution captures full lifetime value and data ownership, preventing dependency on rivals who may become existential threats, justifying short-term losses for long-term monopoly potential.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Contradiction:** The proposal relies on "billions" in existing licensing revenue while simultaneously funding "multi-year operating losses." Eliminating the guaranteed cash flow (licensing) to fund a speculative burn removes the financial floor required to sustain the loss period. If the DTC service fails to hit "tens of millions" quickly, the company faces a liquidity crisis with no fallback revenue.
2.  **Capability Gap:** The entity has "never run" a technology platform of this scale. Building a global streaming infrastructure while simultaneously competing against an entrenched leader with "well over a hundred million" users introduces massive execution risk. Technical failures during launch would permanently damage the brand and accelerate churn before the audience builds.
3.  **Cannibalization Without Guarantee:** The plan admits to cannibalizing cable and theatrical revenue. Without a proven mechanism to migrate those users digitally at a higher margin immediately, this is simply swapping high-margin, low-churn revenue for low-margin, high-churn subscriptions, diluting total enterprise value.
4.  **Pricing Trap:** Launching "below the market leader" while burning cash creates a race to the bottom. The leader has scale economics this newcomer lacks; a price war favors the incumbent, extending the time-to-profitability indefinitely.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal lacks a specific stop-loss threshold for the operating loss or a verified technical readiness milestone before pulling content. It assumes audience migration without evidence that users will follow content to a new, unproven platform. The "multi-year loss" is undefined in magnitude relative to the lost licensing billions.

**Verdict:** The strategy swaps certain, massive current revenue for uncertain future growth while introducing unproven operational capabilities and removing the cash engine needed to fund the transition. The financial math relies on perfect execution in a field where the company is a novice.

DECISION: NO-GO
