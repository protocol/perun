The claim is that a retailer should launch a pay-per-use cloud infrastructure business despite lacking experience, facing incumbents, and operating with thin margins.

Steelman: The company's massive, spiky internal infrastructure represents sunk capital that can be monetized during idle periods, creating high-margin revenue to subsidize retail operations and hedge against cyclical demand.

However, the blocker test fails immediately. The proposal admits the market is "unproven" and the company has zero experience running a developer platform at scale. In 2006, established hardware and software vendors already dominate the enterprise mindshare. Entering a capital-intensive business with thin retail margins creates a fatal conflict: reliability expectations for external paying customers are orders of magnitude higher than for internal batch jobs. Meeting these expectations requires distinct engineering cultures and operational processes the retailer does not possess.

The "do nothing" alternative preserves capital for the core retail business, which investors are already questioning due to heavy spending. A "better way" would be to license the internal tooling as software to other enterprises rather than operating the infrastructure themselves, thereby avoiding the operational risk and capital drag of a service business. However, even this requires capabilities the company lacks.

The pre-mortem is clear: The initiative distracts leadership during a critical retail period, the spiky internal capacity proves insufficient or incompatible with steady external demand, reliability failures churn early adopters, and the capital required to fix gaps dilutes retail margins further. The cost of delay is low because the market is unproven; waiting for a competitor to validate the model reduces risk without sacrificing opportunity, as the company has no first-mover advantage in trust or technology.

The strongest attack is the mismatch between "thin margins/retail culture" and "high-reliability platform economics." No evidence suggests the internal infrastructure can be productized without prohibitive re-architecture costs. The risk is not just execution difficulty; it is strategic incoherence.

Blockers: The company lacks the core competency (developer platform operations) and the market validation required to justify the capital risk against investor skepticism.
Risks: Distraction, margin dilution, brand damage from reliability failures.

DECISION: NO-GO
