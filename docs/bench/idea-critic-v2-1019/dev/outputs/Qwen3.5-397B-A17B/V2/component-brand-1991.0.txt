Steelman: Transform an invisible component into a consumer demand driver by subsidizing partner ads, forcing buyers to specify the brand and compelling manufacturers to comply.

**Skeptic:** The core assumption is that end-users care about or understand internal components. Historically, component marketing fails because buyers trust the integrator (the computer maker), not the supplier. If consumers ignore the logo, the entire budget is wasted with zero sales lift. The risk of manufacturer resentment is high; partners may view this as brand encroachment rather than support, potentially leading to channel conflict or refusal to participate, leaving the sponsor with sunk costs and no distribution leverage.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" alternative preserves capital and avoids channel friction. A superior alternative is a technical certification program ("Intel Inside" verified) without consumer advertising, relying on B2B reputation. Another option is a smaller pilot with one willing partner to prove demand elasticity before committing a large share of the marketing budget. Spending heavily on unproven consumer psychology is inferior to staged validation.

**Kill-Criteria:** This is a one-way door; once the brand promise is made to consumers, retreating damages credibility permanently. The cost of delay is low; waiting for a pilot study prevents catastrophic budget misallocation. The proposal lacks a specific metric for success (e.g., "% lift in specified demand") and a stop-loss condition. Without a pre-committed kill switch based on early sales data, the program could bleed cash indefinitely.

**Decision:** The proposal fails the blocker test. It commits a large share of the budget to an unprecedented strategy without evidence that consumers drive component selection. The risk of channel alienation and total budget waste constitutes a factual blocker: the plan assumes consumer behavior that may not exist. A capped pilot is required before full approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
