**Steelman:** Acquiring the agency and platform instantly grants the technology and scale needed to challenge the search leader's recent move, preventing permanent market exclusion.

**Attack:** The $6 billion cash price represents a massive premium in a fast-changing market, creating severe financial risk if display technology shifts again. More critically, owning an agency directly conflicts with the buyer's existing agency customers, who may defect to competitors to avoid feeding data to a rival-owned entity. The proposal assumes these customers will stay despite the conflict, a fragile premise given the industry's sensitivity to data neutrality. While the competitor's move creates urgency, overpaying for an asset that alienates the current customer base undermines the core business rather than securing its future. The "fast-changing" nature of the market further suggests that today's premium platform could be obsolete quickly, making the irreversible cash outlay unjustifiable without stricter safeguards or a lower price.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid immediate financial shock and customer conflict, though ceding ground to the search leader.
2.  **Partner/Invest:** Secure technology access or minority stakes without full ownership, preserving customer trust and reducing capital exposure.
3.  **Build/Buy Smaller:** Develop internal capabilities or acquire a non-conflicting tech piece at a fraction of the cost.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the blocker test. The confirmed conflict with existing agency customers and the exorbitant price in a volatile market constitute factual blockers that the proposal does not resolve. The urgency of a competitor's move does not validate a strategy that risks the current revenue base.

**Blockers:** 
1. Direct conflict with existing agency customers likely to cause churn.
2. $6B cash premium in a rapidly obsolescing market.

**Risks:** Technology shift rendering the platform useless; integration failure; regulatory scrutiny.

**Kill Criteria:** If customer churn exceeds 5% within six months or ad-tech standards shift away from the acquired model within one year, divest immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
