The claim is that a sporty, customizable coupe built on economy underpinnings will capture the emerging youth market despite unproven demand. The steelman posits that style and perceived value outweigh mechanical pedigree for this demographic, and that customization offsets the "cheap" stigma of shared parts.

Alternatives include doing nothing (ceding the segment), launching a true high-performance model (higher cost/risk), or a minor styling refresh (insufficient impact). Doing nothing fails the north star of capturing the baby boom. A dedicated platform is too costly and slow. The proposed path beats "do nothing" by targeting a specific, growing demographic with low marginal development costs due to parts sharing.

However, the "cheap" perception is a genuine blocker if the marketing fails to decouple the new image from the economy donor car. The pre-mortem suggests failure occurs if the advertising campaign focuses on reliability rather than lifestyle, or if the options list is confusing rather than empowering. The cost of delay is high; competitors could identify this demographic shift within a quarter.

The evidence relies on the assumed existence of a large, underserved postwar cohort wanting style over utility. The financial model works only if the high-margin options attach rate is significant; base price alone may not cover the heavy launch spend. The risk of unproven demand is mitigated by the low incremental cost of reusing the compact chassis.

Blockers: None identified in the facts provided; the parts-sharing risk is a marketing challenge, not a fatal flaw, solvable by the proposed "large launch advertising campaign" if correctly targeted.
Risks: Misaligned messaging causing the car to be perceived as merely an "expensive economy car"; options complexity reducing conversion.

Kill criteria: If pre-order reservations do not cover 20% of the launch advertising spend within 30 days of the campaign start, halt further media buys and pivot to dealer incentive programs. Leading metric: Options attachment rate per unit sold.
