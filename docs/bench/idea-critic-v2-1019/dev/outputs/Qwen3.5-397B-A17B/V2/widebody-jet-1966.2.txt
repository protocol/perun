**Steelman:** Building a massively larger, efficient wide-body jet with freighter conversion capability secures long-term market dominance against the specific demand of the largest customer, despite high upfront costs.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces existential financial risk; the development and factory outlay exceeds any prior company commitment. If the single ordering airline cancels, the company faces bankruptcy. The reliance on an unproven high-bypass engine introduces technical failure modes that could ground the fleet. Furthermore, the strategic timing is precarious: if supersonic transport rapidly replaces subsonic jets as industry experts predict, this massive subsonic investment becomes obsolete before paying off. The "raised cockpit" feature, while clever for future freighter conversion, adds complexity to an already over-constrained design.

However, the "do nothing" alternative is worse. Ceding the growth trajectory to a competitor who *does* build the larger aircraft would permanently lose the dominant market position. The single airline order provides a critical anchor, but it is insufficient to de-risk the factory build alone. The supersonic threat is a known uncertainty, not a confirmed blocker; subsonic travel volume may still grow even if supersonic exists for premium routes.

**Kill Criteria & Verdict:**
The primary blocker is the lack of diversified demand. Relying on one customer for a project of this magnitude is a fatal flaw in the current proposal structure. The unproven engine is a secondary risk that requires a binding guarantee from the supplier regarding performance timelines. The supersonic threat cannot be fully resolved now but suggests the need for a modular design strategy.

Because the financial exposure is total and currently hinged on a single customer without mentioned commitments from others or government backing, the plan as stated is too fragile. It requires securing additional launch customers or financial guarantees before committing to the factory construction. The risk of bankruptcy upon a single cancellation outweighs the potential upside in the current configuration.

DECISION: NO-GO
