**Steelman:** Building a massively larger, wide-body freighter-capable jet secures dominance against supersonic threats and meets urgent airline demand for lower seat costs.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Existential Risk:** The development and factory outlay exceed all prior company history. A single failure or delay bankrupts the firm.
2.  **Market Misread:** If supersonic airliners replace subsonic jets as expected, this massive subsonic investment becomes obsolete scrap before paying back.
3.  **Unproven Dependency:** Relying on an unproven high-bypass engine introduces a single point of failure for the entire aircraft program.
4.  **Demand Fragility:** Committing to massive production based on a single airline order is reckless; if that carrier cancels, no other buyer can absorb the capacity.

**Better Way:**
*   **Do Nothing/Wait:** Let competitors test the supersonic market first; upgrade current models incrementally.
*   **Staged Approach:** Develop the engine and freighter conversion capability separately before committing to a new airframe and factory.

**Kill Criteria & Pre-mortem:**
Assume failure: The engine fails certification, or the supersonic market materializes faster than anticipated, leaving the factory idle. The "raised cockpit" feature, while clever for freighters, adds complexity without immediate passenger revenue. The cost of delay (losing the airline customer) is high, but the cost of error is total corporate collapse.

**Verdict Logic:**
The proposal relies on three unverified assumptions: that supersonic travel will *not* dominate soon, that the new engine will work, and that one customer guarantees success. These are not risks; they are unproven premises for a bet larger than the company's entire history. The "do nothing" or "staged" alternatives offer similar upside potential (if the market exists) with drastically lower immediate ruin probability. Committing now violates the principle of reversible decisions; this is a one-way door into potential insolvency based on speculative market shifts and unproven technology.

The strongest attack is the **supersonic substitution risk**: if the market shifts to supersonic as predicted by industry experts, this subsonic leviathan has zero residual value. The proposal offers no hedge against this specific, existential threat.

DECISION: NO-GO
