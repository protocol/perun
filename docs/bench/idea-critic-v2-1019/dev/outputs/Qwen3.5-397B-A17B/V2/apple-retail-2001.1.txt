Alternatives analysis: "Do nothing" guarantees continued margin erosion and brand dilution in existing channels. Training reseller staff has historically failed due to competing incentives. A "shop-in-shop" model lacks the control required for the proposed music ecosystem demonstration. Only company-owned stores fully align incentives with the strategic pivot.

Fit: The move directly serves the north star of integrating music with computing, which current loss-making operations fail to achieve. It transforms the customer experience from a commodity transaction to a brand immersion, essential for a differentiated recovery.

Pre-mortem & Door Type: Failure scenario: A deep recession reduces mall foot traffic while rigid leases drain remaining cash reserves, forcing bankruptcy before the music strategy gains traction. This is a one-way door; once leases are signed and capital spent on build-outs, reversal is costly and slow. The cost of delay is the continued loss of market relevance as competitors solidify the digital music space.

The risks (recession, inventory, channel conflict) are severe but manageable through tight inventory control and positioning stores as marketing hubs rather than pure volume drivers. The potential upside of saving the company via the music pivot outweighs the certainty of decline under the status quo.
