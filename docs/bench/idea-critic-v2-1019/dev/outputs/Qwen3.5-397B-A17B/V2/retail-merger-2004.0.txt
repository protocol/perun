**Steelman:** Acquiring the larger chain provides immediate scale and shelf space to cross-sell brands, betting that merged operations and format conversion will reverse declining sales faster than organic growth or rivals can capture the market.

**Attack:** This is a "merger of equals" in distress, not a rescue. Both entities suffer from structural decline: aging stores, underinvestment, and shrinking relevance against big-box competitors. The proposal admits cost savings are "uncertain" and customer bases "differ," yet relies entirely on these synergies to justify the capital outlay. Merging two failing models rarely creates a winning one; it typically compounds liabilities, distracts management during a fragile post-restructuring period, and accelerates cash burn while attempting complex store conversions. The risk of cultural clash and operational paralysis is extreme given the lack of clear integration certainty.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve cash, focus on restructuring the core discount model, and wait for a healthier target or organic turnaround.
2.  **Pilot Partnership:** Test brand placement in a few locations without full acquisition to validate the "different customer" hypothesis.
3.  **Asset Strip:** Buy only the real estate or specific supply contracts, ignoring the operating company.

**Pre-mortem:** By 2006, the combined entity faces liquidity crisis. Integration costs exceeded projections, store conversions confused customers, and the debt load from the acquisition prevented necessary renovations. Big-box rivals steal the remaining market share while management fights internal fires. The "uncertain" savings never materialize, leading to a second restructuring or liquidation.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Logic Failure:** The core premise relies on "uncertain" cost savings to fix two declining revenue streams. Without guaranteed synergies, the ROI is negative.
*   **Strategic Mismatch:** Differing customer bases and old store formats increase execution risk rather than reducing it.
*   **Timing:** Emerging from court supervision requires stability; this adds maximum complexity.

**Verdict:** The proposal asks to gamble the restructured company's survival on unproven synergies between two deteriorating assets. The risks are existential, and the evidence suggests the combination weakens rather than strengthens the market position.

DECISION: NO-GO
