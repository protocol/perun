However, the "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in timing and leverage. The target lacks a permanent CEO, meaning its valuation is depressed and its strategic direction fluid. Buying now locks in a $7.5B price tag while the asset is unstable. A superior alternative exists: wait for the leadership crisis to force a lower valuation or a distressed sale, or invest in building the free competitor users are already touting. The "do nothing" option retains $7.5B in cash while the target's losses continue to erode its own leverage.

The lack of a CEO means there is no counterparty to guarantee stability during the transition. The risk of developer defection is not a "risk" but a near-certainty given the stated hostility and the availability of a free alternative. This is a blocker: the core value (the community) is likely to evaporate post-announcement, rendering the $7.5B expenditure a purchase of empty infrastructure.
