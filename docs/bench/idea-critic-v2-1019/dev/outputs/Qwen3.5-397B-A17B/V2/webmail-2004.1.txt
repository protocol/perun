Steelman: Offering 1,000x more storage than incumbents, paired with fast search and context-aware ads, creates a defensible moat that justifies infrastructure costs through superior user retention and ad targeting, despite privacy concerns.

**Skeptic**: The core claim relies on storage costs being low enough to sustain a free tier while generating sufficient ad revenue. In 2004, a gigabyte of storage is expensive compared to megabytes, but hardware costs are dropping rapidly (Moore's Law). The privacy objection is valid; scanning mail for ads may trigger regulatory scrutiny or user backlash, potentially limiting adoption among privacy-conscious segments. However, the value proposition (never deleting email) may outweigh privacy concerns for the mass market.

**Better-Way**: Alternatives include: (1) Stick to megabyte limits with paid upgrades (status quo, low differentiation); (2) Launch with less storage (e.g., 100MB) to reduce risk (fails to disrupt); (3) Do nothing (ceding market to competitors who might eventually scale storage). The proposed 1GB leap is the only path to clearly distinguish the brand and force incumbents into a costly catch-up game.

**Kill-Criteria**: Pre-mortem suggests failure if ad revenue per user < storage cost per user within 18 months, or if privacy lawsuits halt scanning. This is a two-way door; if adoption lags, storage limits can be tightened or ads removed. Cost of delay is high: waiting allows competitors to increase storage increments, blunting the first-mover advantage of a massive leap.

**Blockers**: NONE. The math works if ad CPMs are standard and storage costs follow historical decline curves. The privacy risk is manageable via transparent terms.
**Risks**: Privacy backlash, slower-than-expected hardware cost declines, low initial ad fill rates.

The proposal beats "do nothing" and smaller iterations by creating a step-change in utility that redefines user expectations. The invitation-only launch controls initial load while generating scarcity.

DECISION: GO
