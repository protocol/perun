**Steelman:** A mandatory client centralizes update delivery and anti-cheat enforcement, turning a fragmented retail distribution model into a persistent, monetizable platform that secures long-term recurring revenue against piracy and obsolescence.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal addresses existential threats (cheating, update failure) that retail boxes cannot solve. The lack of universal broadband in 2003 is a constraint, not a blocker, as the target audience for "very popular PC games" disproportionately owns capable hardware and connections. Retail pushback is a transitional cost, not a fatal flaw, given the leverage of "very popular" titles.

**Risks:**
1.  **Adoption Friction:** Mandatory accounts and downloads may alienate casual users or those with dial-up.
2.  **Channel Conflict:** Retailers may reduce shelf space or demand higher margins in retaliation.
3.  **Execution Risk:** Early client bugs could damage brand reputation more than current cheating issues.
4.  **Competitive Response:** Rivals may launch similar clients, eroding first-mover advantage.

**Better Ways Considered:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Guarantees continued revenue loss from cheating and high support costs for patching; fails to capture downstream store value.
*   *Optional Client:* Fails to solve the cheating problem universally; fragmented user base prevents a viable third-party store.
*   *Partner with Existing Platforms:* In 2003, no neutral, dominant PC platform exists that offers the required control over anti-cheat and updates without ceding margin or data.

**Kill Criteria & Metrics:**
*   **Door Type:** Two-way door for the client software (can be patched or rolled back); one-way door for the strategic shift away from pure retail dependence.
*   **Cost of Delay:** High. Every month without anti-cheat reduces game lifespan and community trust; every month without a store delays diversification.
*   **Success Metric:** >60% activation rate of new sales within 90 days; <5% increase in support tickets related to installation.
*   **Stop Condition:** If activation remains <40% after two major patches due to technical barriers (not preference), pivot to a hybrid optional model for legacy titles while maintaining mandatory status for new releases.

The strategic necessity of controlling the distribution pipeline and securing game integrity outweighs the transitional friction with retail and low-connectivity users. The "store" upside creates an entirely new revenue vector that justifies the initial investment.

DECISION: GO
