Steelman: Acquiring the larger chain provides immediate scale and brand diversity to arrest the discount chain's post-restructuring sales decline, leveraging synergies to offset individual weaknesses.

However, the claim fails the blocker test. The proposal relies on merging two entities with identical negative momentum: both have declining sales, aging infrastructure, and underinvestment. Combining two weakening balance sheets does not create strength; it concentrates risk. The stated cost savings are explicitly "uncertain," removing the primary financial justification for such a complex integration. Furthermore, the customer bases differ significantly, making the cross-selling strategy speculative rather than proven.

The market context presents a definitive blocker: big-box rivals are currently growing. Engaging in a massive, distracting merger while agile, well-capitalized competitors are expanding is a strategic error. The integration effort required to merge corporate functions and convert stores will consume management attention and capital precisely when the company needs to focus on fixing its core sales issues.

The "better way" analysis shows that "doing nothing" (focusing on organic turnaround of the restructured entity) or pursuing smaller, targeted acquisitions carries less risk. This specific plan attempts to solve a sales problem with a structural overhaul that has no guaranteed payoff and high execution risk. The uncertainty of savings combined with the certainty of integration distraction creates a scenario where the combined entity is more likely to fail than either standalone company.

This is not a case for "go-if" conditions because the fundamental premise—that merging two declining, underinvested chains creates value in the face of growing big-box competition—is contradicted by the provided facts. The risks are not merely potential downsides; they are inherent characteristics of the targets that directly undermine the proposal's success criteria.

Blockers: Combining two declining sales trajectories without certain cost savings; executing a complex merger while big-box rivals grow; mismatched customer bases undermining cross-selling.
Risks: Integration distraction, capital depletion, brand dilution.

DECISION: NO-GO
