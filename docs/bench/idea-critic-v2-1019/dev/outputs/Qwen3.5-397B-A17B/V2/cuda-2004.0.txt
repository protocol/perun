The claim is that investing heavily in general-purpose programmability for mass-market graphics chips (2004–2006), despite no existing large market, will create a valuable new revenue stream.

**Steelman:** Embedding general-purpose compute features in every consumer chip now creates a massive, distributed supercomputer base that will become indispensable for emerging high-performance computing workloads, securing a dominant moat before rivals react.

**Attack:** The proposal requires raising unit costs on every chip to subsidize a feature with zero current paying demand. The core business faces an existential race with a main rival; diverting engineering focus and margin to a speculative, non-gaming use case risks losing the primary gaming market share. Without committed customers, the ROI relies entirely on creating a market that may never materialize or may be served more cheaply by CPUs or future specialized accelerators. The "niche demand" from academics using hacked APIs does not prove mass-market viability.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Focus entirely on winning the graphics race; let others experiment with general compute.
2.  **Wait:** Add programmability only after a clear, paying market emerges.
3.  **Targeted Approach:** Build a separate, high-end product for scientists rather than bloating every consumer chip.

**Pre-mortem:** The program burns cash for years; the gaming division loses ground to the rival due to distraction; the software stack remains immature because no major applications exist; the board cancels the program in 2007, having raised costs for no return.

**Blocker:** The plan explicitly raises unit costs and diverts engineering resources from the core survival battle against a main rival to serve a market with **no committed customers** and only "niche demand." This violates basic capital allocation for a company in a fierce duopoly; it bets the core business on a speculative future without a single anchor tenant.

**Risks:** Market never forms; rival wins graphics race; toolchain fails to gain traction.

DECISION: NO-GO
