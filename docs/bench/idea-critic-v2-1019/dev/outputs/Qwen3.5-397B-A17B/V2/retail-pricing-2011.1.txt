The claim is that replacing a coupon-dependent model with everyday low pricing (EDLP) and branded shops will reverse sales declines for an older, discount-trained demographic. The steelman: eliminating promotional complexity restores margin and modernizes the brand, leveraging the CEO's prior electronics success to create a curated destination.

However, the skeptic hat identifies a fatal blocker: the proposal ignores the verified behavior of the existing customer base. These customers are explicitly "trained to wait for coupons," and historically, "most sales" occur only at a discount. Removing the primary purchase trigger without a transitional bridge assumes immediate behavioral change that contradicts the stated facts. The "better-way" analysis shows a superior alternative: a phased pilot in select stores to test price elasticity and shop layout before a chain-wide rollout. This mitigates the risk of total revenue collapse. The "kill-criteria" perspective highlights that this is a one-way door; announcing a full chain transformation without a test makes reversal costly and reputationally damaging if the hypothesis fails.

The pre-mortem is clear: revenue plummets because the core constituency stops buying, waiting for discounts that no longer come, while new customers do not arrive fast enough to replace them. The cost of delay (waiting for pilot results) is negligible compared to the cost of immediate, systemic failure. The evidence provided—that sales are historically discount-driven—directly contradicts the core assumption that customers will accept everyday pricing. This is not a risk; it is a factual mismatch between the strategy and the customer profile. The lack of a pilot turns a manageable hypothesis into an unchecked gamble.

Blockers: The plan relies on customers abandoning deep-seated coupon habits immediately, contradicting the fact that most sales are discount-driven. No pilot exists to validate the new model.
Risks: Total revenue collapse, brand alienation, inability to reverse course quickly.

DECISION: NO-GO
