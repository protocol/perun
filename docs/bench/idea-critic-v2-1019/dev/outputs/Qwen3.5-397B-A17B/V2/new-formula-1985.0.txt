The claim is that replacing the flagship formula with a sweeter alternative, validated by blind taste tests, will reverse market share loss. The steelman: sensory superiority drives purchase, and removing the old formula forces migration to the superior product, maximizing efficiency.

However, the attack reveals a fatal blocker. The evidence cited (blind taste tests, surveys, focus groups) explicitly excluded the critical variable: the permanent removal of the original option. Consumers tested a new product against an existing one, not the *loss* of a heritage asset. This invalidates the data for the specific decision proposed. The "sweeter wins" finding applies to line extensions, not replacements.

The "do nothing" alternative (keeping the original, perhaps launching the sweet version as "New Coke" alongside it) captures the taste-preference upside without triggering the heritage backlash. The proposal ignores the non-sensory value of the brand: emotional attachment and heritage are not "risks" to be managed but core components of the product's value proposition, which the blind tests deliberately stripped away. By withdrawing the original entirely, the company converts neutral or positive taste-testers into angry advocates for the rival, as the act of removal creates a psychological reactance that no amount of sweetness can overcome.

The pre-mortem is clear: launching this plan triggers a public relations crisis, boycotts, and a surge in rival sales driven by nostalgia, not taste. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the reputational damage of a forced switch. The testing methodology itself is the blocker; it measured isolated sensory preference, not holistic brand loyalty under conditions of loss. A cheaper, safer path exists: introduce the new formula as an addition, not a substitution. Since the proposal demands total withdrawal based on flawed, incomplete data that ignores the primary driver of brand value (heritage), it fails the blocker test.

Blockers: Testing methodology excluded the actual decision variable (removal of original), rendering the core evidence invalid for this specific action.
Risks: Mass consumer backlash, brand erosion, bottler revolt.

DECISION: NO-GO
