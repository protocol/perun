**Steelman:** Leveraging massive existing distribution (search, mail) to solve cold-start problems, offering superior privacy controls (circles) and real-time video to dislodge an incumbent complacent on usability.

**Attack:**
1.  **History as Data:** Three prior failures indicate a structural inability to execute social products, not just bad timing. This suggests deep cultural or product-fit mismatches that "integration" cannot fix.
2.  **Forced Integration Risk:** Bundling social layers into utility products (search, mail) risks degrading core user experience. Users seeking efficiency may churn to competitors if utilities feel cluttered by unwanted social features.
3.  **Incentive Distortion:** Tying executive bonuses to adoption metrics encourages gaming the system (e.g., dark patterns, forced sign-ups) rather than organic value creation, potentially accelerating user resentment.
4.  **Network Effect Reality:** The incumbent's hundreds of millions of users create a moat that "better features" rarely breach unless the incumbent self-destructs. Circles and Hangouts are features, not platform shifts.
5.  **Opportunity Cost:** A company-wide push diverts engineering talent from the core search business, which faces its own existential threats, to fight a war on unfavorable terrain.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserve core product integrity; acquire a social player if the market shifts.
*   *Niche Play:* Launch Hangouts/Circles as standalone tools for specific verticals (education, enterprise) without forcing a full social graph.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch succeeds in sign-ups due to forced integration but fails in retention. Core search users complain of bloat; social users find the network empty of meaningful connections outside early adopters. Bonuses are paid out based on vanity metrics before the collapse, damaging morale and credibility.

**Blockers:**
*   **Historical Precedent:** Three consecutive failures in the same category is a factual indicator of incapability, not just bad luck.
*   **Core Product Risk:** The proposal admits "forced integration may annoy existing users," yet proceeds anyway, threatening the primary revenue engine.

**Decision:** The combination of repeated historical failure, the high risk of damaging core utilities, and the reliance on forced adoption rather than organic pull creates an unacceptable risk profile. The incentives are misaligned with long-term health.

DECISION: NO-GO
