**Steelman:** Paying $19B secures the dominant mobile messaging layer to prevent existential displacement of the core social network, buying time to integrate utility before competitors lock in users.

**Attack:** The price is 12x the prior valuation with zero revenue and no monetization path. The target explicitly rejects ads, the buyer's core model. With only 50 employees, integration risk is extreme; founder departure post-payout could leave a hollow shell. Privacy regulations could fracture the global user base. However, the "do nothing" alternative risks the core platform becoming irrelevant as communication shifts entirely to this closed app. The cost of delay is the permanent loss of the mobile social graph.

**Alternatives:** Building a competitor internally is slower and has already shown limited traction against this network effect. Waiting for a lower price is impossible; the target's growth trajectory makes it more expensive monthly. Acquiring a smaller rival fails to capture the critical mass needed to stop displacement.

**Verdict Logic:** While the financial metrics (revenue, valuation multiple) suggest a catastrophic overpayment, the strategic necessity of owning the mobile communication channel outweighs the immediate financial absurdity. The blocker test requires a fact showing the plan *fails* as proposed. The lack of a monetization plan is a risk, not a proven failure, given the user scale. The fear of displacement is the primary driver; if the displacement threat is real, the acquisition is the only viable defense. No concrete evidence proves the integration will fail or the users will leave immediately upon acquisition. The risk of inaction (obsolescence) is greater than the risk of overpayment.

**Kill Criteria:** If monthly active users drop by >20% within 18 months post-acquisition, or if the founders depart within 12 months without a viable successor, the strategy is deemed a failure. Leading metric: Daily active users on the integrated messaging layer vs. standalone competitors.

**Dissent:** The price is indefensible by traditional metrics and relies entirely on the unproven assumption that messaging is the future of social networking.

DECISION: GO
