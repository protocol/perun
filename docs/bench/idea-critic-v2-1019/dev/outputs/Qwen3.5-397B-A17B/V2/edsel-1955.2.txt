**Steelman:** Capturing the growing mid-priced segment with a distinct brand prevents customer defection to competitors and maximizes lifetime value across the corporate portfolio.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on extrapolating recent growth into a 1957 launch, ignoring the high probability of cyclical contraction. Mid-priced discretionary purchases are the first casualty in a recession. Furthermore, creating a new dealer network requires massive fixed capital expenditure precisely when consumer liquidity may vanish. The "perceived gap" may be a mirage created by temporary prosperity rather than a structural market feature. If the economy turns, the brand launches into a shrinking pool with inflated overhead, cannibalizing the parent's stable low-end sales without securing sufficient premium volume to offset losses.

**Alternatives:** 
1. **Do Nothing:** Retain current market share; avoid fixed infrastructure costs during potential downturn.
2. **Badge Engineering:** Utilize existing dealer networks and shared platforms to test the segment with lower capital risk, sacrificing brand distinctiveness for survival.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Assuming the goal is long-term dominance, launching at the peak of a cycle before a downturn is fatal. In a pre-mortem scenario where the brand fails by 1959, the cause is a recession-induced collapse in mid-tier demand combined with an unsustainable, underutilized dealer network that drains cash flow from the profitable low-end division.

**Blockers:** None explicitly proven in the text, but the "recession risk" combined with "new dealer network" creates a fragile cost structure that violates the principle of counter-cyclical resilience. However, without data confirming an imminent downturn, this remains a risk, not a factual blocker.

**Risks:** High fixed costs, channel conflict, cyclical timing error, brand cannibalization.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal admits a recession risk while committing to maximum fixed costs (new dealers) and high exposure (mid-priced goods). This is a pro-cyclical bet that assumes continuous growth. While no definitive fact proves a recession will occur, the structure of the deal leaves zero margin for error. A "GO" requires assuming the economy will not turn for several years. Given the explicit acknowledgment of recession risk, proceeding "as proposed" with full infrastructure build-out is reckless without a phased approach or contingency. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
