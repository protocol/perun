**Steelman:** Differentiate via novel interaction and price to expand the market rather than competing on raw graphical power, leveraging existing software momentum while mitigating risk through a dual-product strategy.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that the dual-screen, touch-interface complexity will starve the device of software, rendering the hardware irrelevant regardless of price. However, maintaining the older handheld on sale secures the core developer base and revenue stream, buying time for the new ecosystem to mature. The "gimmick" risk is neutralized if the second screen offers distinct utility (e.g., maps, inventory) rather than mere redundancy.

Competing on graphics against a well-funded rival in a arms race is a low-reversibility trap for a market leader; it cedes the definition of "gaming" to the opponent. The proposed path flips the constraint: weaker graphics become irrelevant if the gameplay loop relies on touch and dual-display mechanics. The cost risk of two screens is offset by targeting a lower price point than the rival, implying aggressive component sourcing or subsidy, which is viable given the company's dominant market position and cash flow from the legacy unit.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if developers ignore the touch screen, creating a library of standard games that look worse than the rival's. This is avoided only if first-party titles forcefully demonstrate the unique mechanic immediately at launch.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** None identified in the text. The risks (developer confusion, unproven tech) are manageable via the continued sale of the legacy unit and strong first-party support.
*   **Risks:** Market perception as a toy; higher BOM cost than single-screen units. These are acceptable bets to avoid a direct hardware shootout where the rival holds the advantage.
*   **Alternatives:** Matching the rival's specs likely results in a more expensive device with no unique selling proposition, leading to a "me-too" failure. Doing nothing cedes the innovation narrative.

The proposal successfully avoids a head-on collision on unfavorable terms. It bets on market expansion over specification wars. The condition of keeping the older unit on sale provides a safety net that makes the risk calculated rather than reckless.

DECISION: GO
