**Steelman:** Acquiring the target secures a sticky, high-margin enterprise segment (construction/business) via its unique push-to-talk service, creating immediate revenue synergies that outweigh the $36B cost and integration friction.

**Attack:** The core flaw is technological incompatibility. In 2005, merging distinct network architectures (e.g., CDMA vs. GSM or proprietary iDEN vs. cellular) requires maintaining parallel infrastructure indefinitely or forcing a costly, disruptive migration. The proposal admits the target's tech is "limited for data," yet the industry trajectory is decisively toward broadband data. Integrating a legacy, data-poor network into a modernizing carrier creates a permanent drag on CAPEX and operational complexity. The $36B valuation likely prices in the customer base but underestimates the "integration tax" of running two incompatible networks while trying to migrate users who joined specifically for the legacy walkie-talkie functionality. If the unique value proposition (push-to-talk) relies on the incompatible legacy tech, migrating users risks churn; if kept separate, the deal fails to achieve network efficiencies. The goodwill asset is fragile—if integration degrades service, the high-value business customers will leave.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid $36B debt and integration risk; lose the enterprise segment to competitors.
2.  **Partnership:** License the push-to-talk technology or form a roaming alliance to capture revenue without asset integration.
3.  **Acquire & Isolate:** Buy the company but run it as a standalone subsidiary to preserve the tech, delaying integration until the technology naturally sunsets (high operational cost).

**Decision Logic:** The proposal lacks a credible technical migration path or a costed plan for running dual networks. The risk of technology obsolescence combined with massive integration costs constitutes a blocker. The "large goodwill" becomes an impairment risk if the legacy service degrades during integration. Without a defined strategy to resolve the incompatibility beyond "must integrate," the deal threatens to destroy value through execution failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
