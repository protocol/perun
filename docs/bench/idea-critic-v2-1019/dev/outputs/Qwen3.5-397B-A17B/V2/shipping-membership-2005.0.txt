**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee converts variable shipping friction into fixed revenue, locking in loyalty and increasing order frequency to offset per-unit margin erosion.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Retain per-order fees; continue losing repeat customers to friction.
2.  **Free Shipping Threshold:** Waive fees only above a high cart value; protects margin but fails to solve frequency friction for small repeat orders.
3.  **Proposed Membership:** High upfront commitment ($79); maximizes loyalty but risks severe short-term cash flow if uptake skews toward heavy users.

**Fit:** Aligns with a "customer obsession" north star by removing the primary barrier to repeat purchases. Assumes the retailer has sufficient capital to absorb initial margin hits.

**Pre-mortem & Door:** If heavy buyers dominate early adoption, shipping costs could exceed $79 per user within months, creating a loss spiral. This is a **one-way door**; revoking benefits from paying members destroys trust and brand equity permanently. Cost of delay: competitors capture the loyalty lock-in first.

**Evidence & Economics:** No internal data exists to verify elasticity or shipping cost curves. The proposal admits economics are "unproven." Relying on the assumption that increased frequency will mathematically offset the fixed fee without a pilot is a fatal gap. A single heavy user ordering weekly could cost $150+ in shipping, instantly negating the $79 revenue.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unverified Unit Economics:** The core claim (frequency offsets cost) rests on an unproven assumption with no pilot data.
*   **Irreversibility Risk:** Launching broadly before validating the loss ratio on a one-way door violates safety constraints for unproven models.

**Risks:** Short-term margin collapse; adverse selection (only heavy buyers join); operational strain from surged volume.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not launch broadly without a capped, time-boxed pilot proving that average shipping cost per member remains below $79 annually.

**Decision:** The proposal demands a binary, irreversible commitment based on unproven math. While the strategic logic is sound, executing this as a full launch without a validated pilot exposes the firm to uncapped downside on a one-way door. The "unproven" status of the economics is a blocker for immediate approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
