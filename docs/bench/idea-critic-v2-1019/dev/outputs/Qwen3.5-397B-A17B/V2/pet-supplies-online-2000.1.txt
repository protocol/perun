**Steelman:** Aggressive capital deployment via IPO will secure market dominance through scale, acquisition, and below-cost pricing before competitors can react, accepting short-term losses for long-term monopoly power.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on continuous access to public capital markets to fund negative gross margins. Selling products below cost creates a "burn rate" directly tied to sales volume; every unit sold accelerates cash depletion. The plan assumes an indefinite window for an IPO and subsequent secondary offerings despite noted risks of capital markets closing. If markets tighten even slightly, the company cannot fund its own growth engine or shipping costs for heavy, low-margin goods. The strategy lacks a fallback to profitability; it is a "one-way door" that leads to insolvency if the specific sequence of funding events fails. The "kill criteria" are absent: there is no defined metric to stop the bleeding if customer acquisition costs do not drop as projected.

**Better Way:** A staged approach focusing on unit economics in limited regions, proving a path to positive contribution margin before scaling national TV spend or acquiring competitors. This preserves capital and validates the model without betting the entire firm on perpetual market liquidity.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-IPO, the stock price drops 40% due to missed earnings and rising interest rates. The company cannot issue more shares to fund the ad campaign or the acquisition. Cash reserves vanish due to shipping losses on below-cost items. The company enters bankruptcy because it scaled operations to a volume it could not afford without constant external funding.

**Blockers:**
1. **Funding Dependency:** The core mechanism (selling below cost) requires infinite capital availability, contradicting the stated risk that "capital markets could close."
2. **Unit Economics:** Negative gross margins on heavy items mean scaling increases losses linearly; no evidence suggests this reverses quickly enough to survive a market downturn.

**Risks:** Competitor retaliation, shipping cost volatility, IPO window closure.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the blocker test. It requires favorable market conditions that are explicitly identified as uncertain, with no contingency for their absence.

DECISION: NO-GO
