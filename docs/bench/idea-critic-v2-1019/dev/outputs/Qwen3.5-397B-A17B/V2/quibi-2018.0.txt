Steelman: A premium, mobile-first short-form service can succeed by leveraging A-list talent and superior adaptive technology to solve vertical/horizontal viewing friction, differentiating from free, user-generated clutter.

However, the claim fails the blocker test. The proposal seeks over $1 billion for a subscription model in a market where "subscription fatigue" is explicitly cited as a rising concern and free, ad-supported short video is already abundant. The core assumption—that users will pay a premium for professional short-form content when high-quality alternatives are free—is contradicted by the stated market conditions. No evidence is provided that the "technology feature" (orientation adaptation) creates sufficient value to overcome price resistance or habit inertia.

Alternatives analysis:
1. **Do nothing:** Avoids massive capital destruction in a saturated, unproven category.
2. **Ad-supported model:** Aligns with existing user behavior for short-form content; lowers barrier to entry.
3. **Pilot launch:** Test demand with a fraction of the budget before committing $1B+.

The proposal ignores the "do nothing" and "ad-supported" options which better fit the stated reality of abundant free content. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the risk of immediate, total loss. The pre-mortem is clear: the service launches, fails to convert free users to paid due to fatigue and price, and burns through capital before finding product-market fit.

Blockers:
- **Economic mismatch:** High production costs + subscription price vs. abundant free competitors.
- **Unproven demand:** Explicitly noted as a risk with no mitigation strategy other than "A-list talent," which has not historically guaranteed subscription conversion for short-form.
- **Capital inefficiency:** Raising $1B+ before validating willingness-to-pay violates basic lean startup principles for unproven categories.

Risks:
- Execution difficulty in securing exclusive A-list short-form content.
- Technology feature may not be a sufficient differentiator.

This is a one-way door with a high probability of failure based on current facts. The plan does not beat "do nothing" or a cheaper, staged alternative.

DECISION: NO-GO
