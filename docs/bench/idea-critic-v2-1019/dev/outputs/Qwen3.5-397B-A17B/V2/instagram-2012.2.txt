**Steelman:** Paying $1B eliminates the primary mobile threat to the core business before the IPO, securing the mobile future despite zero revenue.

**Attack:** The valuation is indefensible by traditional metrics (no revenue, tiny team). Buying a "culture" often destroys it. Regulatory blocks could waste time. The buyer's own mobile weakness suggests integration failure.

**Alternatives:**
1. *Do Nothing:* Save $1B, but risk the IPO narrative collapsing if the threat grows.
2. *Build/Buy Cheaper:* Impossible; the network effect and "leading" status are already established. Time-to-market for a competitor is too slow.

**Fit:** Aligns with the "mobile-first" survival imperative during an IPO window. The cost of delay is potentially catastrophic to the stock debut.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the app's user base flees post-acquisition due to culture clash or product changes, leaving the buyer with $1B of goodwill and no threat neutralized. This is a one-way door; the cash is gone forever.

**Blocker Test:**
*Blockers:* NONE. While the price is astronomical, the strategic necessity of removing the sole credible mobile threat prior to a public offering creates a unique market condition where standard revenue multiples do not apply. The "no business model" risk is mitigated by the buyer's existing ad infrastructure.
*Risks:* High integration risk, regulatory delay, culture erosion. These are managed via kill criteria, not reasons to stop.

**Decision Logic:** The threat is existential to the IPO valuation. No cheaper alternative exists to instantly acquire tens of millions of mobile users and neutralize the competitor. The cost of inaction exceeds the financial overpayment.

**Kill Criteria:** If user growth stalls by >20% within 6 months post-close, or if key founding engineers depart within 3 months, initiate immediate strategic review for divestiture or pivot. Leading metric: Daily Active Users (mobile) growth rate. Review date: 90 days post-close.

DECISION: GO
