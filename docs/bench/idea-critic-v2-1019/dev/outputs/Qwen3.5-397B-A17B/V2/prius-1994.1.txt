Steelman: Committing now secures a decade-long technological moat and defines the segment before regulators force a rushed, inferior response. However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The plan relies on a "bold public schedule" for 1997 while admitting battery durability is "unproven" and there is "no near-term demand signal." In the mid-1990s, nickel-metal-hydride technology for volume automotive use represents a massive unquantified liability; a single widespread failure mode could bankrupt the program via warranty exposure. Furthermore, with fuel cheap and no regulatory mandate, the market economics do not support the premium pricing required to recoup R&D and production costs. The "first mover" advantage is illusory if the first product is unreliable or financially unsustainable.

The pre-mortem is clear: The 1997 deadline forces the use of immature batteries, leading to high failure rates in the field. The resulting warranty costs explode, brand reputation collapses, and the program is cancelled after bleeding cash, leaving the company behind competitors who waited for technology maturity. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of failure; waiting two years for battery data and clearer regulatory signals reduces technical risk without sacrificing the ultimate market opportunity, as no competitors are currently active.

This is a one-way door with irreversible financial damage if failed. The proposal lacks a kill criterion for battery cycle life validation before tooling commitment. It attempts to solve a non-existent market problem (demand) with unproven technology under an arbitrary deadline. The correct path is a limited technical pilot, not a mass-production commitment.

Blockers: Unproven battery durability creates undefined warranty liability; no market demand signal invalidates the business case for mass production; arbitrary 1997 deadline conflicts with technical readiness.
Risks: High unit cost, consumer rejection of unproven tech, competitor leapfrogging if they wait for better batteries.
