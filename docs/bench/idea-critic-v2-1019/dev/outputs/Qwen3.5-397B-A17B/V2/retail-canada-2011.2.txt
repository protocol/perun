**Steelman:** Rapidly acquiring 130–150 sites establishes immediate scale to deter the entrenched competitor and capitalize on price differentials before they narrow.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on unproven capabilities (cross-border logistics, bilingual regulation, new inventory systems) while executing a high-velocity rollout (nearly all stores in two years). This creates a single point of failure: if the new supply chain or inventory system falters during the initial wave, the retailer faces simultaneous stockouts across 130+ locations with no organic learning curve. The "newness" of both the operational model and the market means there is no fallback data to correct course quickly.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Retain domestic margins; avoid catastrophic capital loss in an unfamiliar regulatory environment.
2.  **Staged Entry:** Open 5–10 pilot stores to validate the supply chain and inventory system before committing to 130 leases. This sacrifices first-mover speed for survival probability.
3.  **Acquisition:** Buy the entrenched competitor (likely too costly) or a smaller local chain to inherit their logistics.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** The strategy assumes the price advantage outweighs operational friction. In a pre-mortem, the failure mode is clear: the new inventory system cannot handle cross-border complexity, leading to empty shelves in month six. With 130 leases signed, the fixed costs bleed cash faster than the brand can build loyalty against a dominant rival. The "pace" risk is not just execution difficulty; it is an existential threat when combined with unproven systems.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Critical Path:** The supply chain and inventory system are explicitly "not yet proven." Launching 130 stores on untested infrastructure violates basic operational safety.
*   **Irreversibility:** Long-term leases on 130 sites create massive fixed costs before product-market fit in the new region is verified.

**Risks:** Regulatory delays, bilingual labeling errors, price perception mismatches, and competitor retaliation.

**Conclusion:** The proposal demands perfection in new systems while removing the ability to iterate. The risk of total capital destruction via a broken supply chain across 130 simultaneous openings is a definitive blocker. A staged approach is required to validate the "new" systems before scaling.

DECISION: NO-GO
