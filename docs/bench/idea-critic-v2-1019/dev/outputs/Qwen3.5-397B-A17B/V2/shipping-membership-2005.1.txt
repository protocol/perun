**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee decouples shipping cost from purchase frequency, removing the primary friction for repeat buyers and locking in loyalty, betting that increased order volume and retention will outweigh the short-term margin hit from heavy users.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal; the "unproven economics" is a risk, not a proven failure. The model assumes elasticity exists, which is a valid hypothesis to test, not a blocker to initiation.

**Risks:** 
1. **Adverse Selection:** Heavy buyers may consume more in shipping costs than the $79 fee covers, creating a loss leader scenario.
2. **Irreversibility:** Once customers buy in, canceling the program damages trust and brand reputation significantly.
3. **Margin Compression:** Immediate P&L impact could be severe before volume efficiencies or cross-selling benefits materialize.

**Analysis:** The proposal correctly identifies shipping fees as the biggest friction. While the risks of margin hit and adverse selection are real, they are inherent to the business model shift, not flaws in the logic. The "unproven" nature of the economics is the very reason to launch: data cannot be gathered without market interaction. A "do nothing" approach guarantees continued friction and lost repeat business. The alternative of waiting for perfect data ensures the opportunity is lost to competitors. The risk of irreversibility is mitigated by the fact that memberships are annual; the program can be stopped for *new* sign-ups immediately if metrics fail, honoring existing members while capping exposure. The potential upside of transforming customer behavior and securing annual cash flow upfront justifies the calculated risk.

**Kill Criteria:** If the average shipping cost per member exceeds $79 within the first 6 months, or if repeat purchase frequency does not increase by at least 20% compared to non-members, the program must be pivoted or capped.

**Decision:** The proposal survives the attack because the risks are manageable hypotheses rather than fatal flaws, and the status quo guarantees stagnation.

DECISION: GO
