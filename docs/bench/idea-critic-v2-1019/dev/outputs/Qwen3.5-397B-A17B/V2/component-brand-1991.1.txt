**Steelman:** Component buyers cannot differentiate products; branding the invisible component forces downstream manufacturers to adopt it to satisfy end-user demand, creating a moat against clones.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unprecedented Model:** No component supplier has ever successfully branded to consumers. The assumption that end-users care about internal specs is unproven; they may prioritize price or the computer brand itself.
2.  **Channel Conflict:** Subsidizing ads dictates creative control to manufacturers. This risks severe resentment from powerful partners who may refuse participation or demand excessive subsidies, eroding margins without guaranteeing volume.
3.  **Budget Cannibalization:** Allocating a "large share" of the marketing budget to unproven consumer ads diverts funds from proven B2B sales engineering and manufacturer relationships. If the campaign fails to move needle, core revenue streams suffer.
4.  **Measurement Lag:** Benefits may not show in sales for quarters. Without immediate leading indicators, the company cannot pivot quickly, locking capital into a failing strategy.

**Better Way:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Maintain status quo; risk clone erosion continues but preserves cash and partner harmony.
*   *Pilot:* Run a limited, time-boxed campaign with one willing partner in a single region to test consumer response before global rollout.
*   *Co-op Only:* Offer subsidies without the logo requirement, letting manufacturers choose to highlight the chip organically.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
The strategy assumes a "pull" market where consumers drive B2B procurement. If consumers remain indifferent to the logo, manufacturers will stop cooperating once subsidies end, leaving the brand invisible and the budget wasted. The "one-way door" risk is high: once the brand promise is made to the public, retreating signals weakness.

**Blockers:**
No factual blocker exists in the text; the risks are hypothetical but severe. The proposal lacks a defined kill criterion or pilot phase. However, the potential upside (commoditization defense) outweighs the status quo risk of being replaced by clones. The unprecedented nature is a feature, not a bug, if it works.

**Decision:**
The proposal addresses an existential threat (clones) with a novel solution. While risks are high, doing nothing guarantees slow erosion. The plan must proceed but requires strict kill criteria (e.g., specific lift in consumer mention within 6 months) to prevent endless budget bleeding.

DECISION: GO
