The proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts embedded in the claim. First, the pricing strategy contradicts the value proposition: an unsubsidized flagship price requires a flagship ecosystem, yet the device explicitly lacks mainstream apps and runs a forked OS with a smaller store. In a two-platform dominated market, users pay for network effects, not just hardware; charging premium rates for a degraded software experience is a direct path to inventory obsolescence.

Second, the "3D-like display" and "one-touch product identification" are niche differentiators that do not compensate for the core utility gap. History shows that novel hardware features cannot sustain a platform when the primary use cases (social, messaging, banking) are unsupported by the app store. The "head tracking" novelty is a one-way door investment with high manufacturing complexity and low retention value compared to the sticky utility of missing mainstream applications.

Third, carrier exclusivity combined with a high price point severely caps the total addressable market while increasing customer acquisition costs. In a saturated market, growth comes from switching incentives or price disruption; this proposal offers neither. It asks users to switch carriers, pay full price, and abandon their preferred apps for a 3D screen and a shopping button. The "do nothing" alternative (continuing success with e-readers and tablets) preserves capital and brand equity, whereas this launch risks significant sunk costs in hardware tooling and carrier marketing for a product likely to fail the "why switch?" test.

The risks are not merely theoretical; they are structural contradictions. The plan assumes users will tolerate a secondary app store at a primary market price, which contradicts basic consumer behavior in saturated duopolies. No kill criteria can save a unit economics model built on this mismatch. The cheapest alternative is to license the shopping feature as an app on existing platforms or bundle it with current tablets, reaching more users without the hardware risk.

Blockers: Premium price without premium app ecosystem; niche hardware features cannot offset missing mainstream utility; exclusivity limits scale in a saturated market.
Risks: High inventory write-downs, brand dilution, carrier relationship strain.

DECISION: NO-GO
