**Steelman:** A clear cola leverages test market enthusiasm to capture demand for a "purer" alternative, diversifying the portfolio before soft-drink growth stalls further.

**Attack:** The core failure mode is category confusion. Cola flavor is psychologically anchored to dark color; removing the color may signal "diet" or "chemical" rather than "pure," causing immediate rejection after trial. Test markets often inflate novelty demand; nationwide launch amplifies this risk. If repeat rates collapse, the massive marketing spend yields only temporary volume while cannibalizing the flagship brand's shelf space and mindshare. Rivals can copy the formula instantly, eroding any first-mover advantage.

**Better Way:** Instead of a costly nationwide launch, execute a controlled regional rollout with strict repeat-purchase tracking over six months. This validates whether the "purer" positioning drives loyalty or just one-time curiosity. Alternatively, position the product explicitly as a mixer or niche variant to avoid direct flagship cannibalization.

**Fit:** While diversification addresses slowing growth, a major brand extension risks diluting the core identity if the product fails. The proposal assumes test market positivity translates to national retention, an unproven leap given the psychological link between color and flavor.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales have plummeted. Repeat purchases are near zero because consumers distrust the taste of a clear cola. The flagship brand lost 3% share to the confusion. Competitors launched identical SKUs, fragmenting the niche. The marketing budget is wasted.

**Kill Criteria:** Proceed only if a specific repeat-purchase threshold (e.g., >30% at 90 days) is proven in extended testing. Without this data, the risk of permanent brand damage and sunk marketing costs outweighs the potential upside. The current proposal lacks this validation, relying on initial trial data that notoriously overpredicts success for novel CPG products.

**Verdict:** The plan ignores the high probability of novelty fade and category dissonance. Launching nationwide without confirmed repeat behavior is a reckless bet on a psychological mismatch.

DECISION: NO-GO
