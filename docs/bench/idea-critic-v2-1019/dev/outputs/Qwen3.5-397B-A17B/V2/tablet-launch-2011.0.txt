The claim is that a computer manufacturer should launch a tablet in 2011 using a recently acquired mobile operating system, priced at parity with the market leader, despite a tiny app catalog and negligible OS market share.

Steelman: Leveraging existing hardware brand equity and a unified ecosystem strategy can overcome initial app scarcity if marketing creates perceived value and developer momentum quickly.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The primary blocker is the pricing strategy relative to value. In a two-sided market like tablets, the utility to the user is directly correlated with the app catalog size. The proposal admits the catalog is a "small fraction" of the leader's while the OS has "almost no market share." Pricing "in line with the market-leading tablet" ignores the massive disparity in network effects. Consumers will not pay a premium price for a severely constrained utility, regardless of advertising spend. This is a fundamental unit economics failure: the price does not match the delivered value.

A better way exists: penetrate the market with a lower price point to subsidize the lack of apps, or delay launch until the catalog matures. The "do nothing" option (waiting) avoids burning capital on a product likely to be rejected by rational consumers who compare features-per-dollar.

The pre-mortem is clear: The device launches, reviewers praise the hardware but pan the software ecosystem, sales flatline due to high price/low value, and the company exits the tablet market, wasting the acquisition. The risk of leadership transition exacerbates execution uncertainty.

While developer outreach is necessary, it cannot instantly replicate years of ecosystem growth. Heavy advertising on a flawed value proposition accelerates cash burn without securing adoption. The proposal assumes marketing can override the structural disadvantage of a small app store at a premium price, which contradicts historical data on platform adoption.

Blockers:
1. Price parity with a superior ecosystem creates a negative value proposition that advertising cannot fix.
2. Small app catalog vs. market leader creates an insurmountable utility gap at the proposed price point.

Risks:
1. Leadership transition causing strategic drift.
2. Developer outreach failing to gain traction before cash runs out.

DECISION: NO-GO
