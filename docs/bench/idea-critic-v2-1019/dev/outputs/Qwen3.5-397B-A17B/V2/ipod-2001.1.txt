**Steelman:** A premium, high-capacity device restricted to a niche ecosystem can succeed if it offers a qualitatively superior user experience that justifies the price, creating a halo effect for the brand despite limited initial compatibility.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on three critical blockers derived strictly from the provided facts. First, the addressable market is functionally zero for the mass market: restricting usage to owners of "small-share" computers without consumer electronics experience severely limits the user base below the threshold required to sustain a $400 price point. Second, the value proposition is inverted; cheaper competitors exist, and without legal downloads at scale, the device solves a distribution problem that does not yet have a legal supply chain, making the hardware a solution in search of a viable content ecosystem. Third, the timeline creates an execution risk that compounds the market risk: sourcing components for a 5GB drive in under a year while entering a new hardware category without prior experience invites delays that will miss the narrow window before competitors lower prices or the market shifts.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "wait" option is superior. Waiting for the music industry to license downloads at scale reduces the content risk. Entering the market later with a broader compatibility strategy (Windows support) addresses the addressable base issue. A cheaper, flash-based prototype would test the waters without the sunk cost of hard drive sourcing.

**Kill Criteria:** The plan lacks a mechanism to expand compatibility immediately. Without a path to non-proprietary computer users within the first six months, the project cannot achieve the volume necessary to justify the component costs. The reliance on a "small market share" computer base is a structural flaw, not a manageable risk. The absence of legal music sources means the primary use case (filling 5GB legally) is currently impossible for most users.

The combination of a tiny compatible base, high price, lack of content ecosystem, and inexperience in the sector constitutes a fatal blocker. The proposal asks to bet the company on a constrained hardware play in a vacuum of content.

DECISION: NO-GO
