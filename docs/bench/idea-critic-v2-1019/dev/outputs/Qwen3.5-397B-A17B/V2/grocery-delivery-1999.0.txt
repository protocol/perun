**Steelman:** Secure dominant market share in online grocery by building an unassailable logistics moat before competitors can react, betting that scale will eventually drive unit economics positive.

**Attack:** The proposal violates the fundamental logic of startup validation. It commits irreversible capital ($1B+) to a specific, unproven operational model (highly automated warehouses) before verifying the core hypothesis: that customers will adopt this service at a density sufficient to cover thin margins. The "first metropolitan area" has zero operating history; unit economics are entirely theoretical.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing/Wait:** Preserve capital until the first city proves a path to profitability.
2.  **Iterative Expansion:** Use existing third-party logistics or smaller, manual fulfillment centers to test demand in 2-3 additional cities with minimal capex.
3.  **The Proposal:** Bet the entire company on a single, rigid infrastructure strategy across 12+ cities simultaneously.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** In a low-margin business, fixed costs are fatal if volume lags. Assuming the first city fails to reach critical density (a high probability given the novelty in 1999), the company is left with billions in stranded, specialized assets and no cash to pivot. This is a one-way door; once the warehouses are built, the strategy cannot be reversed without total liquidation. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of building the wrong system at scale.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Validated Learning:** The plan explicitly ignores the need to learn unit economics before scaling.
*   **Irreversibility:** $1B in specialized construction eliminates agility.
*   **Margin Mismatch:** High fixed-cost automation contradicts the volatility of early-stage demand in a thin-margin sector.

**Risks:** Customer adoption failure, technological obsolescence of automation, inability to achieve required density, cash burn exceeding runway before profitability.

**Verdict:** The proposal substitutes hope for evidence. It creates maximum downside (bankruptcy) with no mechanism to verify the upside. A sound strategy requires a capped, time-boxed experiment to prove the model, not a nationwide rollout based on a hypothesis.

DECISION: NO-GO
