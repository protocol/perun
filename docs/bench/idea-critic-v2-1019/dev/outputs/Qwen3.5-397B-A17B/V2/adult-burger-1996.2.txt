The claim is that a premium burger, priced above the core menu and backed by record advertising, will successfully shift a child-centric fast-food chain's demographic to adults. The steelman is that a distinct, high-visibility product can decouple the brand from its "kids only" perception and capture adult spending without alienating the base.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The primary blocker is a fundamental contradiction between the brand's entrenched identity and the proposed positioning. The facts state customers associate the chain with "kids' meals and value" and view it as "fast, not premium." A higher price point directly attacks the "value" pillar, while the "child-centered image" creates a psychological barrier for adults seeking a premium experience. No amount of advertising can instantly overwrite years of brand conditioning; in fact, heavy spending on a mismatched message accelerates capital destruction.

The "better way" analysis reveals a superior alternative: introducing adult-oriented items within the existing value framework or creating a sub-brand, rather than forcing a premium price point onto a value brand. The proposed plan bets the entire growth strategy on changing deep-seated consumer perception via price and volume alone, ignoring the "Chesterton's fence" of why the menu is currently structured for value and families.

The pre-mortem is clear: the launch fails because the core demographic (families) feels priced out or ignored, while the target demographic (adults) rejects the food as inconsistent with the brand's cheap/fast image. The massive ad spend amplifies this confusion rather than resolving it. The evidence provided—that customers come for value and kids—directly contradicts the core mechanic of selling a premium product. This is not a risk; it is a structural flaw in the premise. The plan does not beat "do nothing" or a "test market" approach because it assumes demand exists where the brand equity suggests otherwise.

Blockers: Brand identity (child/value) contradicts premium pricing; no proof adults will pay premium prices at this specific chain.
Risks: Alienating core family base; wasting record ad spend on unproven demand.

DECISION: NO-GO
