**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant code-hosting platform secures the industry's central nervous system, neutralizing a potential competitor while capturing the world's largest developer graph to fuel the buyer's enterprise cloud transition, despite short-term cultural friction.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker is the **$7.5B price tag** against a loss-making target with no CEO and active community hostility. If developers defect to the free alternative en masse, the asset's value (network effects) evaporates, leaving the buyer with a costly, hollow shell. The "independent operation" promise is historically fragile given the buyer's past hostility to open source; trust deficits cannot be solved by press releases alone.

However, the **cost of delay** is catastrophic. Waiting allows the free competitor to cement its lead or another giant to acquire the target. The "do nothing" option cedes control of the software supply chain. Alternatives like building a rival from scratch are prohibitively slow and unlikely to dislodge the incumbent network effect.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with a pivot to cloud/services, moving away from pure licensing. In a pre-mortem, failure occurs if the buyer immediately monetizes or integrates too tightly, triggering a mass exodus. The risk is real, but the strategic necessity of owning the platform outweighs the execution risk, provided the "independent" mandate is strictly enforced.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*Blockers:* None verified. The lack of a CEO is an opportunity for the buyer to install leadership aligned with the new vision. Losses are typical for high-growth infrastructure plays.
*Risks:* Developer defection, antitrust scrutiny, cultural clash. These are managed via kill criteria (e.g., monitoring active repository migration rates), not reasons to abort.

The proposal beats "do nothing" because the strategic asset (the developer graph) is irreplaceable. The price is high, but the alternative is irrelevance in the modern cloud era. The plan to run independently addresses the immediate trust crisis.
