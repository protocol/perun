The claim is that a clear cola, validated by test markets, should launch nationwide immediately with a major campaign to counter slowing growth. The steelman is that this extension captures a specific "purity" segment that traditional dark colas cannot, creating incremental revenue before competitors react.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The primary objection is not merely that novelty buyers may not repeat or that rivals might copy; it is that the core premise—cola flavor linked with color—is a fundamental sensory expectation for the category. If the product violates this, positive test market responses are likely artifacts of curiosity rather than sustainable demand. Test markets notoriously overstate demand for novel CPG (consumer packaged goods) items where the "newness" drives trial, but national scale exposes the repeat-rate cliff.

The proposal offers no evidence that repeat purchase rates in test markets met the threshold required for national profitability, nor does it quantify the cannibalization risk against the flagship brand, which remains the primary profit engine. Launching with a "large marketing campaign" maximizes sunk cost if the product fails to retain users after the initial trial. The risk of eroding the flagship's shelf space and brand equity for a potentially fleeting trend constitutes a material threat to the company's core value.

A cheaper, safer alternative exists: a staged regional rollout without a massive national campaign to verify repeat purchase rates over a full quarter. This approach limits exposure to cannibalization and marketing waste while gathering real-world data on whether "clear cola" is a viable long-term category or a short-term fad. The current plan commits significant capital based on early-stage trial data that explicitly ignores the stated risk of low retention. Until repeat rates are proven to sustain the business model independent of novelty, the national launch is premature and financially reckless.

DECISION: NO-GO
