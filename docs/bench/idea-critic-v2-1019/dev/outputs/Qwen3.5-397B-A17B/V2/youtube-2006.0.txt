The claim is that acquiring the dominant but unprofitable video platform for $1.65 billion will secure market leadership despite massive legal and operational risks. The steelman: only by owning the network effect of the largest library and user base can the buyer win the video war, as organic growth is too slow against the incumbent's lead.

However, the skeptic identifies fatal blockers. First, the valuation lacks any revenue multiple basis; paying $1.65 billion for zero meaningful revenue and high burn is speculative gambling, not investment. Second, the copyright exposure is existential: with media companies already threatening lawsuits and the site's core library built on infringing content, the liability could exceed the purchase price, potentially forcing a shutdown or massive payout. Third, the bandwidth costs for scaling user uploads are unproven and likely unsustainable without a verified ad model, which the proposal admits is unproven.

The "better way" is to continue growing the buyer's existing, compliant video service or license content legally, avoiding the toxic liability pile. The pre-mortem is clear: the acquisition closes, lawsuits immediately cripple operations with injunctions, bandwidth costs balloon, and the stock price collapses under the weight of bad debt and legal fees.

This fails the blocker test. The "facts already show" that the target's primary asset (user uploads) is legally toxic, and the financials (no revenue, high cost) do not support the valuation. The risk is not just "uncertainty"; it is a near-certain structural flaw in the business model due to copyright law. A cheaper, safer alternative exists (organic growth or licensing) that does not carry the same existential legal threat. The proposal relies on the hope that laws or enforcement will change, which is not a valid basis for a $1.65 billion commitment.

Blockers: 1) No revenue to justify valuation; 2) Existential copyright litigation threat based on current business model; 3) Unproven unit economics for bandwidth.
Risks: Integration failure, culture clash, ad model failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
