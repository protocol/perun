The claim is that a retailer should pivot infrastructure costs into a public cloud business despite zero platform experience and unproven demand. The steelman: spiky internal capacity creates a cost advantage that, if productized, offsets retail margin thinness and diversifies revenue.

However, the blocker test fails immediately. The proposal admits the market is "unproven" and the company lacks any experience running a developer platform at scale. In 2006, enterprise trust is tied to established hardware/software vendors; a retailer has no brand equity in infrastructure reliability. The risks are not merely theoretical; they are structural. "Distraction from core business" is a guaranteed outcome when building a complex B2B sales and support engine inside a B2C retail operation.

The "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the "do nothing" vs. "go" comparison. The cheaper, safer alternative is to optimize internal infrastructure strictly for retail efficiency or to license excess capacity to existing providers rather than building a competing platform from scratch. Building a cloud requires a cultural shift (DevOps, API-first) that contradicts traditional retail IT operations. The capital spending questioned by investors would skyrocket, not shrink, as reliability SLAs demand redundant global regions.

The pre-mortem is clear: the initiative launches, suffers early outages damaging the retail brand, fails to attract enterprise developers due to lack of trust/features, and drains capital until shuttered as a distraction. The cost of delay is low because the market is undefined; being first matters less than being reliable.

There is no evidence that the "thin margins" of retail can subsidize the massive upfront loss-leader pricing required to displace incumbents. The proposal relies on the assumption that "if we build it, they will come," which is unsupported given the lack of developer relationships. This is a classic case of solving an internal problem (spiky load) by creating an external product without market fit or operational capability. The factual absence of platform experience and the unproven nature of the market constitute a blocker under the decision method: the plan fails as proposed because the core competency gap is unaddressed.

DECISION: NO-GO
