**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to validate demand, refine the product based on early adopter feedback, and build exclusivity before a general consumer release, mitigating the risk of a full-scale failure.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "everyday consumer product" positioning test against the stated facts. The risks identified are not merely hurdles; they are fundamental blockers to the core value proposition for the mass market.
1.  **Privacy & Social Acceptance:** A wearable camera with no clear social norm creates immediate hostility. Unlike a phone camera (used intermittently), a head-worn device implies constant surveillance. Early adopters cannot solve this; they may exacerbate it by normalizing behavior the general public rejects.
2.  **Utility Gap:** The proposal admits "no clear everyday use for most people." Selling a solution without a defined problem at a premium price ($1,500 is smartphone-tier pricing in 2013) violates basic product-market fit logic.
3.  **Battery Life:** Short battery life renders an "everyday" device unusable for its intended duration, creating a functional defect that software updates cannot easily fix in 2013 hardware constraints.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "pivot" alternative is stronger. Instead of positioning it as an everyday consumer product, the device should be restricted to specific enterprise verticals (manufacturing, healthcare) where the camera adds verified value, privacy is controlled, and the high cost is justifiable by productivity gains. Alternatively, delay the general public launch until battery technology improves and use cases are proven in enterprise.

**Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks a mechanism to verify if "everyday use" exists before the expensive general public rollout. The invitation-only program is too small to validate social acceptance but large enough to generate negative press that kills the brand before the real launch.

**Decision:** The plan attempts to bridge a chasm between "niche prototype" and "mass market essential" without addressing the foundational lack of utility and the active social resistance. The risks are not manageable via iteration; they are structural to the proposed positioning.

DECISION: NO-GO
