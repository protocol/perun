**Steelman:** Differentiate via novel interaction and price to expand the market rather than competing on raw graphical power, leveraging existing software momentum.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that dual-screen complexity and unproven touch mechanics will fracture the developer ecosystem, resulting in a software drought that no price cut can fix. However, maintaining the older handheld mitigates this by keeping the core audience engaged while the new platform matures. The alternative—"do nothing" or matching specs—cedes the "broadening" objective entirely, accepting a slow squeeze by superior hardware. A "cheaper single-screen" alternative fails to offer a compelling reason for non-gamers to switch.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a "blue ocean" strategy: making competition irrelevant by changing the value curve. If this fails, it will be because first-party studios treated the second screen as an afterthought rather than a core mechanic, validating the "gimmick" fear. The cost of delay is high; waiting for touch technology to mature allows the rival to cement a monopoly on the "serious" gamer demographic, making later entry impossible.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal relies on the assumption that lower price + novelty > graphical fidelity for a mass audience. There is no factual evidence provided that touch interfaces are viable for long-form play, nor that developers can efficiently target two screens. However, these are *risks*, not *blockers*. A blocker would be proof that the target demographic refuses handhelds regardless of price or that the manufacturing cost cannot actually undercut the rival. Neither is present in the text. The risk of developer confusion is real but manageable through strong first-party titles that demonstrate the hardware's unique value, forcing third parties to follow.

**Decision:**
No factual blocker exists. The risks are inherent to innovation but are countered by the strategic necessity of avoiding a direct hardware war against a superior foe. The dual-screen approach is the only path to the stated goal of audience broadening.

**Kill Criteria:**
If first-party launch titles do not demonstrably utilize both screens within 6 months, or if the price gap narrows to <15% before launch, the strategy fails.

DECISION: GO
