Steelman: A hybrid device leverages existing IP to create a unique market niche, sacrificing raw power for versatility to arrest handheld erosion and recover console relevance.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly accepts modest specs and a thin launch lineup as constraints, not hidden variables. The price parity with superior hardware is a strategic gamble, not a mathematical impossibility. No internal fact contradicts the feasibility of docking a mobile chip.

**Risks:** High. Confusing positioning could repeat past failures. Weak specs may alienate core gamers. Third-party support is uncertain. Supply chains are fragile. However, these are execution risks, not proof of failure. The "do nothing" alternative guarantees continued erosion by phones and irrelevance in the living room, which is a definitive loss. The hybrid approach is the only path that addresses both handheld erosion and home console absence simultaneously.

The pre-mortem suggests failure occurs if the "hybrid" concept is marketed as two half-devices rather than one seamless experience. The kill criterion is clear: if early adopter engagement (software attach rate) does not exceed the previous console's launch window average within six months, the strategy must pivot to aggressive price cuts or software bundling. The cost of delay is total market exit from the dedicated handheld segment.

This is a two-way door; if the hardware fails, the company can retreat to pure software licensing on other platforms (a known fallback). If it succeeds, it redefines the category. The proposal survives the attack because the alternative is stagnation. The risks are managed by the limited initial investment in first-party titles and the modularity of the design.

DECISION: GO
