**Steelman:** A simultaneous, unannounced global rebrand shocks the market into perceiving the retailer as modern, bypassing the inertia of incremental change.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Identity Erasure):** The proposal admits the "old square" is the primary recognition signal. Removing it entirely while keeping only a "small" version destroys immediate brand legibility. Customers will not find the brand; they will assume it vanished or was acquired.
2.  **Blocker (Trust Violation):** Executing this "with no advance announcement" treats loyal customers as obstacles rather than stakeholders. In 2010, social media amplifies feelings of betrayal. A mature brand relying on trust cannot survive a launch that feels like a hostile takeover of its own identity.
3.  **Failure of Alternatives:** The proposal rejects testing. Without data, the claim that "neutral typeface" equals "contemporary" is an unverified assumption. The risk of alienating the core base for an unproven aesthetic gain is asymmetric and catastrophic.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales dip as confused customers drift to competitors. Social channels flood with "Where did the logo go?" and "Sellout" narratives. The company is forced to revert, admitting failure and looking incompetent rather than contemporary.

**Better Way:** A phased transition retaining the iconic blue square as the dominant anchor, introducing the new typeface gradually, and engaging customers in the evolution to turn them into advocates rather than victims of change.

**Kill Criteria:** If recognition drops >10% in week one or sentiment turns >20% negative, halt immediately.

**Verdict:** The plan contains factual blockers: it removes the primary recognition asset and adopts a strategy (no announcement) guaranteed to trigger the exact backlash it fears. It bets the company's equity on an untested hypothesis with no safety valve.

DECISION: NO-GO
