**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly mitigates the primary financial risk (unproven demand) by reusing existing mechanical parts from a reliable compact car, ensuring low variable costs and high margins even at moderate volumes. The "cheap" perception risk is countered by the specific design directive (long hood, short deck) which visually decouples the product from its economy origins, creating a new category rather than competing directly with dull compacts. The heavy advertising spend is a calculated two-way door; if the product fails, the loss is limited to marketing budget, but the upside of defining a new market segment justifies the wager.

**Risks:** 
1. **Category Creation:** Young buyers may prefer established used sports cars over a new "economy" sporty car.
2. **Brand Dilution:** If the options list is poorly curated, the base model may feel too sparse despite the styling.
3. **Execution:** The supply chain must handle a sudden spike in demand if the advertising works, which is harder to scale than the car itself.

**Steelman:** This is not merely a new car; it is a low-cost experiment to capture an emerging demographic using sunk R&D costs, where the maximum downside is a marketing write-off, but the upside is creating a high-margin volume leader that revitalizes the brand's image.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders or first-month sales do not exceed 10,000 units, halt the expanded advertising campaign immediately and reduce production to fleet/rental levels to clear inventory without further brand damage.

**Decision:** The plan beats "do nothing" because it addresses a shifting demographic with minimal capital expenditure on engineering. The risks are manageable via the options list and marketing pivot. The potential to define a market segment outweighs the risk of moderate failure.

DECISION: GO
