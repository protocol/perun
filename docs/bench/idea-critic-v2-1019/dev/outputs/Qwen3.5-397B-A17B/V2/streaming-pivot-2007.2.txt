**Steelman:** Offering free streaming now, despite limitations, is a defensive necessity to retain subscribers and gather data before dominant tech giants lock up the market and make entry impossible.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The proposal faces immediate financial headwinds: high licensing costs for a small, low-quality catalog threaten margins without guaranteed retention upside. Cannibalization of the core DVD profit engine is a near-certainty if the free option gains traction. However, the "do nothing" alternative is fatal. Waiting for broadband ubiquity or perfect content deals cedes the customer relationship to incoming tech giants (Amazon, Apple), turning the company into a legacy logistics provider with no digital future. A "paid add-on" model fails because it slows adoption and data collection, which are the only assets valuable enough to justify the current losses.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
Strategically, this moves the north star from "logistics efficiency" to "digital distribution," essential for long-term survival. If we assume this fails, it will be because licensing costs bled cash faster than subscriber churn decreased, or because the poor user experience drove customers directly to competitors rather than keeping them in the ecosystem. This is a two-way door; the service can be scaled back or restructured, but the market opportunity cannot be reclaimed once lost.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
There are no factual blockers. The costs are high, and the catalog is weak, but these are known variables, not contradictions. The risks (cannibalization, tech competition) are severe but are outweighed by the existential risk of irrelevance. The proposal correctly identifies that doing nothing guarantees defeat against larger entrants. The "free" aspect is the critical lever to drive immediate adoption and habit formation before the market matures.

**Decision:**
The plan survives the attack because the cost of delay exceeds the cost of execution. The cannibalization is acceptable if it occurs within our own ecosystem rather than a competitor's. Proceed immediately to secure early-mover advantage and user data.

DECISION: GO
