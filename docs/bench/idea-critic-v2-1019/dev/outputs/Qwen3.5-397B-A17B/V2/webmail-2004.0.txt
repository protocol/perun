Steelman: Offering 1,000x more storage than incumbents with superior search creates a defensible monopoly via user lock-in, monetized by high-intent contextual ads.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's economics rely on storage costs dropping faster than adoption rates, a historical trend in hardware. While "scanning mail" raises privacy flags, the value exchange (free gigabyte vs. targeted ads) is explicit and voluntary. The mature market is actually an advantage: users are frustrated by deletion limits, creating immediate demand for a "no-delete" alternative.

**Risks:** 
1. **Unit Economics:** If ad yield per user fails to cover the marginal cost of a gigabyte of storage, the model collapses.
2. **Privacy Backlash:** Competitors could weaponize "scanning" narratives to stall adoption.
3. **Incumbent Response:** Existing players could match storage if they deem the threat existential, triggering a price war (though "free" leaves little room to undercut).

**Better Ways Considered:** 
- *Status Quo:* Losing the strategic foothold in user identity and daily engagement.
- *Paid Model:* Fails to achieve the viral scale necessary for ad-network dominance.
- *Smaller Storage:* Defeats the core differentiator; users won't switch for marginal gains.

**Kill Criteria & Metrics:** 
This is a two-way door; if unit economics fail, the service can be throttled or sunset. However, the strategic cost of delay is high: ceding the "infinite storage" narrative to a competitor would be fatal. The leading metric is **ad yield per active user** versus **storage cost per active user**. If this ratio remains negative after 18 months of scaled usage, the model is broken.

**Decision Logic:** 
The proposal attacks the single biggest pain point of the era (storage scarcity) with a solution that becomes cheaper over time (Moore's Law). The revenue model aligns perfectly with the increased engagement caused by infinite storage. The risks are executional, not fundamental flaws in the thesis. The "invitation-only" start correctly manages the blast radius of server costs while manufacturing scarcity.

DECISION: GO
