**Steelman:** A premium, integrated device creates a seamless user experience that fragmented, cheaper competitors cannot match, leveraging a loyal niche to establish a new category before mass adoption.

**Attack:** The addressable market is mathematically insufficient. With a small computer install base and no cross-platform software, the total addressable users are a fraction of a percent of the music market. At $400 (significantly above competitors), adoption requires mass-market appeal, which the compatibility restriction explicitly forbids. Component sourcing for a 5GB drive in under a year is a severe operational bottleneck with no margin for error. The "cheaper players haven't sold well" signal suggests the category is unproven; entering with a higher price and higher friction (compatibility) contradicts basic diffusion theory. The risk of inventory obsolescence is extreme if the software ecosystem does not expand immediately.

**Better Way:** License the software to Windows immediately to access the dominant market share, or delay launch until legal downloads are viable to drive hardware demand. Alternatively, build a flash-based player with lower capacity but higher durability and lower cost to test the waters.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it is because the walled garden was too small to sustain the supply chain costs, leaving the company with unsold inventory and a damaged brand. The cost of delay is low; the cost of a failed hardware launch is high.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Market Size:** The compatible user base is too small to justify the R&D and tooling costs for a proprietary hard-drive device at this price point.
2. **Ecosystem Gap:** No legal download store exists to drive hardware utility, and the hardware does not work for the vast majority of potential music buyers.

**Risks:** Supply chain failure, price resistance, category rejection.

**Verdict:** The proposal relies on a miracle of adoption within a artificially constrained market. The economics do not close without immediate cross-platform support or a pre-existing digital music store, neither of which are present.

DECISION: NO-GO
