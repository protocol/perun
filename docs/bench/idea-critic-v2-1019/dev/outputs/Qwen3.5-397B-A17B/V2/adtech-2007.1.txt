**Steelman:** Acquiring the agency and platform instantly grants the scale and technology needed to challenge the search leader's emerging dominance before the market consolidates further.

**Attack:** The $6 billion cash price represents a massive premium in a fast-changing sector, creating immediate financial risk. The core business model relies on serving existing agency customers; owning a competing agency introduces an irreconcilable conflict of interest that could drive those key clients to competitors. Furthermore, paying a peak premium for technology in a rapidly evolving market risks immediate obsolescence if the underlying ad-serving mechanics shift again.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids financial overextension and client conflicts but cedes the display market to the search leader, potentially relegating the company to a secondary role long-term.
2.  **Partner/Build:** Develop technology internally or form non-exclusive alliances. This is slower and cheaper but may fail to achieve the necessary scale before the competitor locks in the market.

**Fit:** While the goal of competing with the search leader aligns with growth ambitions, the method contradicts the company's established neutral posture toward agencies.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-acquisition, the company has lost major agency clients due to perceived competition, the technology has shifted to a new model not covered by the purchase, and the $6 billion expenditure has severely restricted cash flow for other innovations. The acquisition is viewed as a costly mistake that distracted from core search strengths.

**Blockers:**
*   **Conflict of Interest:** The proposal admits concerns about conflicts with existing agency customers but offers no mitigation. This threatens the core revenue stream.
*   **Valuation vs. Volatility:** The "large premium" paid in "cash" for "fast-changing" technology creates a high probability of permanent capital loss if the tech stack pivots.

**Risks:** Market consolidation accelerates faster than integration; cultural clash between software engineer and agency creatives.

**Decision:** The unresolved conflict with existing customers and the excessive premium for volatile technology constitute fatal flaws in the current plan. The risks outweigh the strategic necessity given the lack of a clear path to retain current clients.

DECISION: NO-GO
