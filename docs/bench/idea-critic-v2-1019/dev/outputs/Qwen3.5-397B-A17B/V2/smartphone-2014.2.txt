**Steelman:** A differentiated hardware feature (3D/shopping) justifies a premium price and OS fork despite app gaps, using carrier exclusivity to subsidize distribution.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "must beat do nothing" test on three verified facts provided:
1.  **App Gap vs. Price:** The device lacks mainstream apps yet charges flagship prices. In a two-platform dominated market, users will not pay a premium for a secondary ecosystem missing core utilities. The value proposition is inverted.
2.  **Differentiation Fragility:** The headline feature (3D/head tracking) is a novelty, not a utility. History shows niche display tech fails to drive mass adoption against general-purpose utility.
3.  **Distribution Cap:** Exclusivity with one carrier severely limits reach in a saturated market, preventing the volume needed to sustain a forked OS ecosystem.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Protect margins on successful e-reader/tablet lines.
*   *Cheaper Path:* Launch a low-cost phone running the dominant OS to drive retail integration, sacrificing hardware margin for ecosystem access.
*   *Staged:* Prove the 3D feature on an existing tablet platform before committing to a phone.

**Pre-mortem:** The phone launches to poor reviews citing the app gap. Early adopters return it due to lack of daily-use apps. The carrier, seeing low activation, drops marketing support. The project is written off as a costly distraction from core retail operations.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** "Unsubsidised-equivalent price in line with leading flagship phones" combined with "without the leading platform's own mainstream apps." This is a fatal mismatch; consumers do not pay flagship prices for incomplete platforms.
*   **Fact:** "Sold exclusively through one US carrier" in a "market already saturated." This caps total addressable market below the threshold required to launch a new OS ecosystem.

**Risks:** Novelty feature fatigue; carrier relationship dependency.

**Decision:** The plan requires paying a premium for a degraded software experience with limited distribution. This contradicts basic market dynamics where ecosystem completeness drives smartphone value. The blockers are fatal as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
