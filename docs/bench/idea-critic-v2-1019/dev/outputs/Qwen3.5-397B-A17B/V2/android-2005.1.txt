**Steelman:** Acquiring the startup secures a free, open mobile OS to accelerate mobile web adoption, directly expanding the search advertising market before competitors lock down distribution.

**Alternatives:**
1.  *Do nothing:* Risk ceding the mobile web to proprietary OS vendors who may bias search results or exclude the company's engine.
2.  *Build internally:* Too slow; the window to influence the emerging smartphone standard is closing.
3.  *Partner/License:* Established vendors have no incentive to adopt a rival's stack; carriers prefer controlling closed ecosystems.

**Fit:** Aligns with the core north star of maximizing search queries. Mobile is the next growth frontier; owning the platform layer ensures access regardless of handset or carrier politics.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if handset makers ignore the OS despite it being free, or if carriers block installation. However, the "open" nature creates a fallback: developers and enthusiasts can drive adoption where carriers resist, eventually forcing industry support (a two-way door; the cost is capped at $50M).

**Evidence & Economics:** The $50M price tag is negligible for a search giant relative to the lifetime value of a single new daily user across a global mobile fleet. The lack of a direct revenue model for the OS is irrelevant; the OS is a loss leader for the high-margin ad business. The six-person team represents high leverage: minimal integration overhead for potential category control.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal. The risks (adoption, carrier resistance) are execution challenges inherent to any platform play, not flaws in the strategic logic. The cost of delay (losing the mobile standard to incumbents) far exceeds the financial risk of the acquisition.

**Risks:** Long payback period; potential failure to gain traction. These are managed by treating the acquisition as a capped experiment with a clear kill criterion: if developer adoption does not reach a critical threshold within 24 months, cease major investment.

**Decision:** The strategic necessity of securing mobile search distribution outweighs the modest financial cost and execution risks.

DECISION: GO
