**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform instantly grants the scale and technology needed to challenge the search leader's dominance before the market consolidates further.

**Attack:** The $6 billion cash price represents a massive premium in a fast-changing sector, risking severe value destruction if display technology shifts again. Buying a direct competitor to your own agency customers creates an immediate channel conflict; those customers may flee to neutral providers, eroding the acquired revenue base. Furthermore, the target's value relies entirely on future growth in a volatile market, making the fixed cash outlay a high-stakes bet with limited reversibility.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid overpaying and channel conflict, but cede the display market to the search leader, potentially weakening the core search business long-term.
2.  **Partnership/Minority Stake:** Gain technology access without the cultural clash, premium price, or customer alienation, though this offers less control.
3.  **Build/Buy Smaller:** Develop internally or acquire a niche player to avoid the "conflict of interest" poison pill, accepting a slower path to scale.

**Fit:** While the strategic goal of competing in display is sound, the specific mechanism (buying a direct competitor to your clients) contradicts the company's existing service model. The premium price assumes stable growth in a sector defined by rapid flux.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-acquisition, major agency clients have left due to conflict concerns, the technology has shifted to a new standard not owned by the target, and the $6 billion cost has depressed stock value, leaving the company unable to pivot.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Channel Conflict:** The proposal lacks a verified plan to retain existing agency customers who now view the buyer as a competitor. This is a factual risk based on current business relationships.
2.  **Valuation vs. Volatility:** The fixed $6 billion cash price ignores the "fast-changing" nature of the tech, creating a high probability of asset impairment.

**Risks:** Integration failure, technology obsolescence, regulatory scrutiny.

**Decision:** The channel conflict and excessive premium in a volatile market constitute clear blockers. The plan fails to address how to keep the acquired revenue stream intact while alienating the current customer base.

DECISION: NO-GO
