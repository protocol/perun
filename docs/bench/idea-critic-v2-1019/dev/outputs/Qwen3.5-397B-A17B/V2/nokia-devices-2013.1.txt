Steelman: Vertical integration is the only viable path to prevent the sole hardware partner from defecting to the dominant rival, thereby preserving the operating system's existence despite the high cost and integration risks.

Alternatives analysis reveals "do nothing" leads to platform irrelevance if the manufacturer switches. Licensing to other makers is ineffective without a reference device to showcase the OS. Selling the division later is a two-way door; buying now is a one-way door with massive sunk costs. The cost of delay is total platform collapse.

However, a critical blocker exists in the proposal's own logic. The primary stated goal is to solve an "app-ecosystem problem" caused by the market leader's dominance. The proposal admits the target's sales are already falling. Acquiring a declining hardware business does not inherently attract developers; developers follow users, not ownership structures. If the combined entity cannot reverse the sales decline quickly, the €5.4 billion expenditure merely delays the inevitable while burdening the company with a losing hardware division and a hostile integration challenge. The premise that "hardware ownership can fix an app-ecosystem problem" contradicts the evidence that the specific hardware being bought is losing market share. Without a proven mechanism to instantly reverse the handset's sales trajectory, the acquisition fails its core objective: it secures a sinking ship rather than saving the fleet. The risk of alienating other handset makers further shrinks the remaining user base, accelerating the developer exodus.

Blockers: The claim that owning this specific declining hardware division fixes the app ecosystem is unsupported by the fact that the division's sales are falling; no mechanism is provided to reverse this trend immediately.
Risks: Workforce integration failure, competitor retaliation, continued developer abandonment.

The proposal relies on the hope that ownership changes market dynamics, but the facts suggest it only consolidates losses. Without a concrete plan to make the hardware profitable independent of the OS subsidy, this is a defensive spend with no offensive upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
