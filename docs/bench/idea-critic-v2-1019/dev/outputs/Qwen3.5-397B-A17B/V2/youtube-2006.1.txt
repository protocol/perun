**Steelman:** Acquire the dominant user-generated video platform to instantly secure market leadership, network effects, and user behavior data that organic growth cannot match within a reasonable timeframe, despite current monetization gaps.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists between the proposal's premises and the decision to proceed. The high price is a strategic premium for monopoly positioning, not a mathematical error. Copyright risks are existential but manageable via future licensing deals (Content ID) rather than reasons to abort; litigation is a cost of entry, not a stopper. The lack of revenue is typical for category-defining platforms at this stage.

**Risks:** Massive cash burn from bandwidth; potential shutdown by copyright injunctions; failure to monetize users; integration culture clash. These are severe but do not constitute immediate blockers given the strategic necessity of owning the category leader. The "do nothing" alternative cedes the video future to a competitor who might buy them instead.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if copyright lawsuits force a shutdown before ad-tech matures, or if bandwidth costs bankrupt the division. However, the buyer's existing cash flow and technical infrastructure provide a unique ability to absorb these shocks compared to any other potential acquirer or the target itself.

**Kill Criteria:** If monthly active users decline by >20% post-acquisition due to content removal, or if legal counsel confirms an injunction probability >50% within 6 months, initiate divestiture or shutdown. Leading metric: Daily uploads and watch time retention. Review date: 6 months post-close.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The argument that "$1.65B is irrational for a company with near-zero revenue" was tested. It fails because the value lies in the entrenched network effect and the prohibitive cost for any competitor to replicate the user base organically. The premium buys time and dominance, not current earnings.
