**Steelman:** A well-funded entrant can disrupt a complacent leader by offering superior hardware, a seamless ecosystem, and a user experience that justifies switching costs, even as a late mover.

**Attack:** The proposal contains a fatal internal contradiction. It explicitly states the new device will "drop the copy-protection standard that the company had promoted to partner stores and device makers." In 2006, major record labels mandated strict Digital Rights Management (DRM) to license content for digital stores. By abandoning the very standard the company previously championed to secure industry buy-in, the proposal guarantees that labels will refuse to license their catalogs for the new store. Without major label content, the store fails; without a viable store, the hardware cannot compete against the incumbent's entrenched ecosystem, regardless of screen size or marketing budget.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "partner-only" approach is safer. A viable alternative would be to leverage the existing promoted standard to secure content first, then differentiate on hardware, rather than alienating suppliers at launch.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Assuming the goal is market share, this plan fails the basic supply chain test. The pre-mortem is clear: Launch day arrives, the store opens with only independent music due to label boycotts over the dropped DRM, consumers reject the limited library, and the marketing budget is wasted on an empty value proposition. The "wireless sharing" feature likely exacerbates label fears regarding piracy, further ensuring content denial.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Supply Chain Failure:** Dropping the promoted copy-protection standard directly contradicts the prerequisite for securing music licenses from major labels in the 2006 market context.
2.  **Ecosystem Lock-in:** Entering late without content exclusivity or price advantage against a loyal user base is statistically improbable to succeed.

**Risks:** High capital burn with zero content library; brand damage from breaking prior industry commitments.

**Conclusion:** The proposal demands entering a content-dependent market while simultaneously severing the relationship with content providers. This is not a risk; it is a structural impossibility given the stated constraints.

DECISION: NO-GO
