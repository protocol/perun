**Steelman:** Transform an invisible component into a consumer demand driver by subsidizing partner ads, forcing retailers to stock computers with this specific chip.

**Attack:**
1.  **Channel Conflict:** Computer makers (OEMs) guard their brand identity. Requiring them to feature a component logo risks resentment, potentially causing key partners to boycott the chip to avoid becoming "generic" commodities.
2.  **Budget Efficiency:** Diverting a large share of the marketing budget to subsidize others is unprecedented for a B2B firm. If consumers ignore the logo, the spend yields zero direct sales lift while starving technical R&D marketing.
3.  **Measurement Lag:** Unlike direct response, brand awareness in a complex supply chain has a long latency. Sales data may not validate the spend before the budget is exhausted, making early termination difficult.
4.  **Clone Confusion:** If clone makers use similar part numbers, a consumer asking for "the chip with the logo" might still receive a competitor's lookalike if the OEM complies only superficially with the ad requirement but swaps parts in production.

**Better Way:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Maintain technical superiority sales; risk being commoditized as clones improve.
*   *Technical Certification:* Create a rigorous "Powered By" performance standard that OEMs *want* to display for quality signaling, rather than paying them to do so. This aligns incentives without the resentment of a "tax" on their ad spend.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   **Stop Condition:** If fewer than 40% of top-tier OEMs sign on within 6 months, the network effect fails; halt spending.
*   **Metric:** Track "logo presence" in national ads vs. unit sales correlation quarterly. If ad spend increases by 20% but premium-priced unit sales do not rise by 5% in two quarters, pivot.

**Verdict:**
The risk of OEM rebellion is real, but the threat of commoditization by clones is existential. The "do nothing" option guarantees margin erosion. The proposed subsidy is the only mechanism fast enough to shift consumer perception before clones close the performance gap. The kill criteria provide a strict leash on the budget.

DECISION: GO
