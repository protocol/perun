**Steelman:** Pay $19B to neutralize the existential threat of mobile messaging displacing the core business, acquiring hundreds of millions of engaged users instantly rather than building a competitor from scratch.

**Attack:** The price is 12x the valuation from one year ago ($1.5B) for a company with 50 employees, minimal revenue, and an explicit anti-advertising stance. There is no defined path to monetization; the target charges $1/year and refuses ads, directly conflicting with the buyer's ad-revenue model. Integrating a privacy-focused, fee-based culture into an ad-driven giant risks massive user churn or total cultural collapse. Founder retention is critical; if they leave, the $19B buys only a fleeting user base with no engine. The "fear" of displacement is real, but paying a record-breaking premium for an unproven business model converts a strategic risk into a guaranteed financial loss if synergies fail.

**Better Way:** Build or buy smaller. Develop an internal mobile messaging solution or acquire a cheaper, more compatible asset. Wait for the target's growth to stall without a revenue model, potentially lowering the price.

**Kill Criteria:** If user growth drops below 10% month-over-month within 18 months, or if the founders depart within two years, the acquisition thesis fails. The leading metric must be daily active users (DAU) retention post-integration, not just raw download numbers.

**Verdict:** The proposal lacks a monetization plan and relies entirely on the assumption that the target's growth justifies a price tag disconnected from current financial reality. The cultural clash regarding advertising and privacy presents a blocker that the proposal does not resolve. Buying a company that explicitly rejects your core business model at a record premium is speculation, not strategy. The risk of paying $19B for a feature set that users might abandon if the "cool factor" vanishes upon acquisition is too high without a concrete integration and monetization roadmap.

DECISION: NO-GO
