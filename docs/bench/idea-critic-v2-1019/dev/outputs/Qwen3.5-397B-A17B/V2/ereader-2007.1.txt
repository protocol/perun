**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled e-reader removes friction for book buyers and solves publisher piracy fears via a closed ecosystem, creating a new high-margin revenue stream that complements rather than replaces print.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the company's total lack of hardware experience, which historically leads to costly delays and quality failures. However, the proposal mitigates this by focusing on a single, static function (reading) rather than a general-purpose device. The "do nothing" alternative cedes the digital future to competitors who will eventually erode print sales anyway. Software-only alternatives (PDFs on existing computers) fail to address the "paper-like" reading experience and piracy concerns that currently stall publisher participation.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the core vision of selling books, not just printing them. If this fails, it will likely be due to excessive device subsidization costs or publisher refusal to license titles at the proposed low price point, not technical inability. The "cannibalization" risk is a feature: it shifts margin from low-growth print to high-growth digital. The cost of delay is losing the chance to define the standard for digital reading.

**Evidence & Constraints:**
The proposal relies on the assumption that cellular connectivity costs can be absorbed by book margins. While hardware is new, the supply chain for screens and chips exists. The single low price is a strategic wedge to break publisher deadlock.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of experience is a risk, not a factual impossibility, and the market conditions (publisher fear, poor prior hardware) create a unique opening for a specialized, carrier-subsidized device that previous generalist attempts missed.

**Risks:** Hardware execution delays; margin compression if book prices remain too low; slower than expected consumer adoption of digital formats.

**Kill Criteria:** If device production costs exceed 1.5x the target retail price after prototype validation, or if fewer than three major publishers agree to the unified pricing model within six months, the project must pivot to a software-only strategy or cease.

**Leading Metric:** Ratio of digital book sales to active device units within the first quarter of launch. Review date: 90 days post-launch.

DECISION: GO
