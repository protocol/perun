The proposal asserts that a mandatory, paid, console-wide online service will succeed despite 2002 market conditions where broadband penetration is low, competitors offer free or publisher-managed play, and the company lacks operational experience in hosting.

**Steelman:** A unified, high-quality paid service creates a superior user experience (single identity, voice chat) that differentiates the console as a premium ecosystem, justifying the subscription fee and forcing infrastructure investment to secure long-term recurring revenue.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Market Fit:** In 2002, "few homes have broadband" is a hard blocker for a *mandatory* service. Requiring broadband excludes the vast majority of the installed base, artificially capping the player base and making the subscription model financially non-viable immediately.
2.  **Pricing Psychology:** Players expect free online play. A mandatory fee on top of the console price creates a double barrier to entry that rivals do not impose.
3.  **Operational Risk:** The company has no history of running hosting/support operations. Building this from scratch while demanding 100% uptime for a paid service introduces extreme execution risk.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "freemium" model (free basic matchmaking, paid premium features) or a publisher-partnered model reduces adoption friction. Making broadband optional (supporting dial-up for basic features) expands the addressable market.

**Pre-mortem:** The service launches, but adoption is <5% due to broadband requirements and subscription fatigue. The company burns capital building unused server capacity. The console is perceived as "expensive to play," driving users to rivals. The lack of operational expertise leads to early outages, damaging the brand before trust is established.

**Blocker:** The combination of low broadband penetration and a *mandatory* subscription fee contradicts the market reality of 2002. The plan fails as proposed because the prerequisite infrastructure (broadband) is not widespread enough to support a mandatory paid gate. This is not a risk; it is a current state fact that invalidates the core revenue assumption.

**Decision:** The plan requires revision to remove the "mandatory" and "broadband-only" constraints before it can succeed. As proposed, it is destined for low adoption and operational failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
