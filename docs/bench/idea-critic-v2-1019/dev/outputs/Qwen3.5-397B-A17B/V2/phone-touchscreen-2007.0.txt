**Steelman:** A premium, keyboard-less touchscreen phone integrating a proven music player and full web browser, sold via an exclusive carrier partnership that cedes design control and shares service revenue, creating a new high-margin category.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that consumers and carriers will reject a high-priced device lacking a physical keyboard and third-party apps, especially from a company with zero phone experience. However, the "do nothing" alternative guarantees erosion of the core music player business by competing handsets. Licensing the OS to multiple carriers (the industry norm) would dilute the user experience and surrender the revenue share, failing to protect the ecosystem. A cheaper, keyboard-equipped variant would cannibalize existing products without defining a new market.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the north star of integrating hardware, software, and services. A pre-mortem suggests failure if the carrier dictates features or if the touch interface proves unusable. Yet, the proposal explicitly secures design control and revenue share, mitigating the carrier risk. The lack of apps is a temporary constraint, not a fatal flaw, as the device itself provides immediate unique value (music + web + phone) unavailable elsewhere.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
No internal evidence contradicts the claim; the risks cited (no experience, premium price, exclusivity) are execution challenges, not factual blockers. The market is ripe for disruption because current "smartphones" are business-focused and cumbersome. The "no keyboard" risk is offset by the superior screen real estate for media consumption. The "no apps" risk is mitigated by the strength of built-in features. There is no fact in hand showing the target users reject touch interfaces or that the premium price prevents adoption given the unique value proposition.

**Decision:**
The plan beats "do nothing" and standard licensing models. The risks are managed through the specific terms of the carrier deal (control + revenue) and the product's unique integration. No blocker exists; the uncertainties are risks to be managed, not reasons to stop.
