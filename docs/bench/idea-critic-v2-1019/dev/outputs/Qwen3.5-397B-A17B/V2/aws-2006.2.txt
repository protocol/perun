**Steelman:** Leveraging existing, spiky infrastructure to sell unused compute capacity creates a high-margin revenue stream that subsidizes core retail capex, turning a cost center into a profit center before competitors recognize the model's viability.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the **capability gap**: running a retailer differs fundamentally from operating a developer platform requiring 99.99% reliability, API stability, and enterprise support. The proposal admits zero internal experience here. Failure mode: reliability incidents damage the core retail brand during peak seasons, causing disproportionate revenue loss compared to new cloud gains.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid brand risk and distraction; continue bearing full infrastructure costs.
2.  **Partner/License:** Rent excess capacity to an existing tech vendor (white-label) to test demand without building operations.
3.  **Internal Optimization:** Focus solely on reducing spikiness for retail efficiency.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This diverges from the "North Star" of retail dominance. In a pre-mortem (assuming failure in 2008): The initiative failed because engineering culture clashed with product demands, leading to frequent outages that drove developers to established incumbents (IBM, Dell, Microsoft). The "unproven market" risk is real; if developers refuse to trust a retailer for mission-critical infrastructure, the unit becomes a money-losing distraction.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal relies on the assumption that "spiky infrastructure" equals "sellable inventory." However, retail spikes (holiday shopping) may not align with developer usage patterns, leaving capacity either unusable or overcrowded. The lack of any named leader with platform experience is a factual blocker for immediate execution at scale. The "thin margins" of retail suggest the company cannot absorb the initial losses required to gain developer trust against well-funded incumbents.

**Decision Logic:**
While the economic theory (selling idle capacity) is sound, the **execution risk** is a blocker. Launching a distinct business line without leadership experience, against entrenched competitors, while investors already question capex, violates the "do nothing" baseline safety. The risk of brand dilution and capital drain outweighs the unproven upside. The company must first validate the market via a smaller, contained pilot or partnership before a full distinct business line launch. As proposed, it is too broad and risky.

DECISION: NO-GO
