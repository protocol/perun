**Steelman:** Differentiate via novel interaction (dual-screen/touch) and price to expand the market beyond core gamers, rather than losing a specs war against a superior graphical rival.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that unusual hardware will fracture the developer ecosystem, resulting in a software drought. However, maintaining the older handheld on sale mitigates this risk by retaining the core audience and existing library while the new device cultivates a broader, casual market. The "gimmick" risk is real but unavoidable; if the device matches the rival's graphics, it loses on price and features. If it matches the price, it loses on power. Asymmetric innovation is the only viable path to avoid direct commoditization.

The cost risk of two screens is offset by the strategic necessity of a lower retail price point to drive mass adoption. While touch-screen gaming is unproven in this specific context, the potential to create entirely new genres (puzzle, education, casual) outweighs the inertia of sticking to proven buttons for a shrinking core demographic. The proposal explicitly accepts the risk of developer confusion to gain a monopoly on a new type of play.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if the price target is missed due to screen costs, or if first-party studios fail to demonstrate the "second screen" utility immediately at launch. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental flaws in the strategic direction. The alternative ("do nothing" or "match specs") guarantees a loss against a well-funded rival with superior media features.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** None. The risks are known and mitigated by the dual-console strategy.
*   **Risks:** Developer adoption, perceived gimmickry. These are addressed by strong first-party software support (implied necessity) and the safety net of the legacy device.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If the unit cost prevents the lower price point, or if launch titles fail to utilize the touch interface within 6 months.

The proposal correctly identifies that competing on the rival's terms (graphics/media) is a losing battle. It forces a new competitive axis where the incumbent can define the rules.

DECISION: GO
