**Steelman:** A free email service with 1GB storage, fast search, and context-aware ads will disrupt a mature market by solving the universal pain point of storage limits, turning email from a filing cabinet into a searchable database, funded by high-relevance advertising.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal bets that storage costs will drop fast enough to make 1GB sustainable before user growth bankrupts the operation. In 2004, disk prices are falling, but a gigabyte per user is still 100x the industry standard. If adoption spikes, the capital expenditure for servers could be catastrophic before ad revenue matures.

However, the "do nothing" alternative cedes the strategic high ground. Email is the identity layer of the web; owning it secures the search company's future against competitors who might bundle search with communication. The incumbents are complacent, treating email as a storage-constrained utility rather than a data asset. Their reluctance to scan content for ads is a vulnerability, not a barrier; users historically trade privacy for convenience and utility (free storage).

The invitation-only launch is a critical control mechanism. It creates artificial scarcity, driving demand while capping initial infrastructure load, allowing the company to validate cost-per-user metrics against ad yield before opening the floodgates. The risk of privacy backlash is real but manageable if the value exchange (1GB free vs. targeted ads) is transparent. The lack of direct revenue at launch is standard for platform plays; the metric that matters is engagement and data richness, which directly improves the core search ad algorithm.

The strongest objection is unit economics: if the cost of serving a user exceeds their lifetime ad value, the model collapses. Yet, given the company's core competency in ad matching and the trajectory of hardware costs, the probability of achieving positive unit economics within 18 months is high. The alternative—letting a competitor own the email graph—is an existential threat.

**Blockers:** NONE. The hardware cost risk is mitigated by the phased rollout.
**Risks:** Privacy backlash, slower-than-expected ad monetization.
**Kill Criteria:** If cost-per-user exceeds $0.50/month after 6 months of beta, pause expansion.

DECISION: GO
