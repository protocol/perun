**Steelman:** Acquire the definitive mobile photo network now to neutralize a existential threat and capture its user base before the IPO makes it unaffordable or independent.

**Attack:** The $1B price tag lacks revenue justification, creating massive financial risk if the "mobile threat" proves fleeting. Integrating a dozen-person culture into a giant often destroys the very innovation being bought. Regulatory bodies may block the deal to preserve competition. The buyer's own weak mobile position suggests execution risk in leveraging this asset.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Save $1B; risk the target becoming a dominant, independent competitor post-IPO, potentially eroding the buyer's core business value by far more than $1B.
2.  **Build Internally:** Attempt to replicate the app; historically failed for this buyer due to speed and culture mismatches.
3.  **Wait:** Let the IPO happen; price likely increases, and independence solidifies, making acquisition impossible later.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of mobile dominance. The target is the bridge the buyer cannot build itself.

**Pre-mortem:** Deal closes, culture clashes cause key founders/engineers to quit within six months, the app stagnates, and regulators force a breakup. Result: $1B wasted, competitor still emerges from the ashes.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** None confirmed. Valuation is high but strategic, not financial. Regulatory risk is present but manageable given the era's precedents. Cultural risk is real but mitigatable via autonomy guarantees.
*   **Risks:** Overpayment, integration failure, regulatory delay.
*   **Cost of Delay:** Catastrophic. If the target IPOs and succeeds, the buyer's mobile relevance collapses. The "weak mobile product" confirms the inability to solve this organically.

This is a **two-way door** regarding capital (cash is available), but a **one-way door** regarding market position. Once the target IPOs and scales, the window closes forever. The strategic necessity of owning the mobile layer outweighs the lack of current revenue. The cost of *not* buying exceeds the cost of overpaying.

DECISION: GO
