**Steelman:** The brand must modernize its visual identity to signal freshness and premium quality, betting that a bold, minimalist redesign will attract new buyers without alienating the core base, provided the recipe and price remain unchanged.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "shelf cue" test. For a legacy brand with an older, loyal demographic, the orange-with-straw image is not merely decoration; it is the primary heuristic for rapid identification. Removing this specific cue creates a high probability of "search failure" on the shelf. Shoppers will not see their usual product, assume it is out of stock or discontinued, and switch to a competitor immediately. The risk is not just confusion; it is invisible inventory.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" option preserves current revenue. A superior alternative is a phased evolution: retain the iconic straw-orange but simplify the background, or test the new design in a limited geographic region before a nationwide rollout. A "big bang" launch removes the safety valve of learning from early failures.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch failed. Why? Because the new vertical text and glass image did not register at eye-level speed for the core demographic, causing a 20% drop in repeat purchases within the first month that the ad campaign could not reverse. The cost of re-printing millions of cartons becomes a sunk loss, and restoring the old design signals panic.

**Blockers:** The proposal explicitly acknowledges the risk that "shoppers may fail to recognize the carton" yet proceeds with a simultaneous nationwide launch. This contradicts the evidence that the old image is the "main shelf cue." Proceeding without a verification step (e.g., in-store eye-tracking or a regional pilot) constitutes a blocker: the plan assumes recognition will transfer instantly despite evidence to the contrary.

**Risks:** High execution cost, alienation of older buyers, loss of shelf visibility.

**Conclusion:** The plan ignores its own identified primary risk by removing the critical recognition trigger without a staging mechanism. It bets the entire brand equity on an untested assumption of instant visual translation.

DECISION: NO-GO
