**Steelman:** A premium, integrated device leverages a loyal niche to establish a beachhead in a fragmented market, betting that superior user experience outweighs compatibility limits and high cost.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "do nothing" and "cheaper alternative" tests. Existing players are cheaper; the addressable market is mathematically tiny (small computer share × early adopters). Without legal downloads, the value proposition relies on users ripping CDs, a friction point competitors already handle at lower prices. Sourcing a 5GB drive for under a year with no consumer electronics supply chain is a high-probability operational failure. The price ($400) excludes the mass market required to recoup R&D before the technology commoditizes.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: Inventory glut of $400 units because the installed base of compatible computers was insufficient to reach break-even volume, while component costs remained too high to drop the price competitively. The "walled garden" software strategy prevents adoption by the dominant OS users, capping growth artificially.

**Evidence & Fit:** No evidence suggests the music industry will license downloads soon, limiting the device to a CD-ripping utility. No evidence suggests the manufacturer can source components faster or cheaper than established electronics firms. The strategy contradicts the "mass market" requirement for hardware profitability.

**Blockers:**
1. **Market Size:** The compatible computer install base is too small to support the volume needed to justify custom hardware development at this price point.
2. **Supply Chain:** No existing consumer electronics sourcing capability makes the <1 year timeline unrealistic for a first-gen hardware product.
3. **Price/Value:** At $400, the device offers no functional advantage over cheaper competitors to justify the premium, given the software restriction.

**Risks:** High inventory risk, brand damage from a failed hardware debut, opportunity cost of diverting focus from the core computer business.

**Kill Criteria:** If a supply contract for the 5GB drive cannot be signed within 30 days at a margin supporting a $299 eventual street price, the project must stop.

The proposal relies on unproven supply chain execution and a market segment too small to sustain the business case.

DECISION: NO-GO
