**Steelman:** A controlled native app store creates a defensible ecosystem that balances developer innovation with user security and product polish, turning a "web-only" phone into a platform while retaining carrier trust.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker hypothesis is that a 30% tax and strict review will stifle developer adoption compared to open rivals, while the review bottleneck delays critical updates. However, open distribution on mobile in 2008 invites malware and fragmentation, directly threatening the "polish" and "stability" the brand promises. Carrier objections are real but manageable if the manufacturer retains final approval authority, preventing apps that bypass billing from reaching the device.

*Do Nothing:* Relying solely on web apps cedes the platform war immediately; web capabilities in 2008 cannot match native performance or hardware access, ensuring third-party developers prioritize rivals.
*Open Side-loading:* Maximizes developer speed but guarantees security incidents and carrier revolts, likely resulting in blocked sales or bricked devices.
*Hybrid (No Review):* Reduces workload but fails the stability guarantee.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This moves the North Star from "hardware vendor" to "platform owner." If this fails, it will be because the review process becomes a bureaucratic choke point, causing developers to abandon the platform for faster rivals. The risk is executional (hiring enough reviewers), not conceptual. The cost of delay is total irrelevance in the emerging app economy.

**Blocker Test:**
No factual blocker exists in the proposal. The concerns (security, carrier relations, polish) are precisely what the proposed controls (review, single store) are designed to solve. The 30% cut is standard for providing distribution and billing infrastructure. The "web-only" stance is obsolete given the competitive landscape. The plan beats "do nothing" by capturing the ecosystem value required for long-term hardware survival.

**Decision:**
The proposal addresses the identified risks with appropriate gates. The alternative is market exit.

Blockers: NONE
Risks: Review scalability, developer friction vs. open rivals.
Kill Criteria: If developer submission volume does not reach 1,000 apps within 6 months, or if critical security breaches occur due to review failure, pivot to a lighter review model.

DECISION: GO
