The claim is that a US discount retailer should execute a massive, rapid international expansion in 2011 by leasing 130–150 sites in a neighboring country within two years, relying on unproven new supply chain and inventory systems.

**Skeptic:** The proposal assumes the retailer can simultaneously master cross-border logistics, regulation, bilingual labeling, and new IT systems while opening nearly one store every four days. This ignores the "liability of foreignness." The inventory system is explicitly "not yet proven"; deploying it at this scale before domestic stabilization creates a single point of catastrophic failure. If the system fails, 150 stores cannot stock shelves, burning cash on leases with zero revenue.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "slow walk" alternative is superior. Entering with 5–10 pilot stores allows the team to debug the supply chain and inventory system with limited exposure. Leasing 130+ sites immediately locks the company into massive fixed costs before unit economics are verified in the new market. A phased approach reduces risk without sacrificing the eventual upside, whereas the current plan bets the entire international strategy on a "big bang" launch.

**Kill Criteria:** The pace (130–150 stores in ~2 years) combined with unproven core infrastructure (supply chain/inventory) constitutes a factual blocker. The plan requires learning complex new domains (regulation, logistics) while executing at maximum velocity. This violates basic operational hygiene: you do not scale a process before proving it works. The risk of systemic collapse (empty shelves across a whole country due to IT failure) is a blocker, not merely a risk.

**Pre-mortem:** One year in, the inventory system crashes under multi-country load, or regulatory delays prevent opening 40% of leased sites. The company is stuck with expensive leases, unsold inventory, and a brand reputation destroyed by empty shelves, forcing a total retreat.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. The combination of maximum speed, maximum scale, and unproven critical systems guarantees a high probability of operational failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
