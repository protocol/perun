**Steelman:** A mandatory client solves critical update and cheating failures while creating a future digital distribution monopoly, outweighing short-term retail friction.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal bets on a shift from physical to digital dominance before infrastructure fully supports it.
*   **Blockers:** None. Home broadband was growing rapidly in 2003; early adopters (the core gaming demographic) already possessed it. Retail pushback is a negotiation risk, not a technical impossibility.
*   **Risks:** Player backlash against mandatory accounts and large downloads is real but manageable if the client offers unique value (seamless updates, cheat-free multiplayer) that pirated or disc-only versions cannot match. Competitor copying is inevitable but first-mover advantage in building a user base is decisive.
*   **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" guarantees continued revenue loss from piracy and high support costs for patching. A voluntary client fails to solve the cheating problem universally.
*   **Fit:** This aligns with the long-term vision of software-as-a-service, turning one-time box sales into recurring platform engagement.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs only if the client is so buggy at launch that it drives players away permanently. This is an execution risk, not a strategic flaw.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If user adoption of the mandatory client falls below 80% of active players within six months due to technical instability, pivot to a hybrid model.

**Decision Logic:**
The status quo (physical boxes) is dying; piracy and update friction are existential threats. The risks cited (broadband availability, retail relations) are transitional hurdles, not permanent barriers. The strategic upside of owning the distribution channel and eliminating cheating outweighs the friction of migration. The "do nothing" option is the riskiest path. The proposal must proceed, but with extreme focus on client stability at launch to mitigate the pre-mortem scenario.

**Dissent Ledger:** Retail partners may temporarily reduce shelf space; some legacy users without broadband will be excluded until they upgrade.

**Remaining Risk:** Execution failure at launch causing reputational damage.

DECISION: GO
