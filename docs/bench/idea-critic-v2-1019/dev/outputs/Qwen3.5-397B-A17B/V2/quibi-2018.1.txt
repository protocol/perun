The claim is that a $1B+ mobile-only, premium short-form subscription service will succeed despite market saturation and fatigue. The steelman: high-production, orientation-agnostic video fills a gap between amateur clips and long-form TV, leveraging A-list talent to overcome subscription resistance.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. First, the "orientation-agnostic" technology feature solves a non-existent problem; users naturally rotate devices for premium content, and no evidence suggests this friction drives subscription decisions. Second, the core assumption contradicts stated facts: free, abundant short-form video already dominates mobile attention, while "subscription fatigue" is explicitly cited as a rising concern. Launching a paid service in a category where the dominant competitor (free) is entrenched, during a period of consumer pushback against new subscriptions, presents a fundamental market mismatch.

Alternatives were not sufficiently weighed. "Do nothing" or a low-cost, ad-supported pilot (mirroring the successful free models already mentioned) offers a safer path to validate demand for premium short-form without risking $1B. The proposal demands a one-way door (massive irreversible spend) based on unproven demand for a specific format (premium short-form) that has no historical success pattern comparable to long-form streaming.

The pre-mortem is clear: the service launches, users refuse to pay for short content they get for free elsewhere, A-list talent fails to drive retention without a library of long-form hits, and the $1B burns before a viable business model is found. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of failure; waiting for proof of willingness to pay for short-form premium content is essential.

Blockers:
1. Contradiction between "subscription fatigue" and the strategy of launching a new paid tier in an unproven category.
2. No evidence that "orientation-agnostic" tech is a value driver worth billions.
3. Lack of a validated demand signal for *paid* short-form professional video.

Risks:
1. Intense competition for mobile attention from free platforms.
2. High churn due to limited content library depth in short-form.

DECISION: NO-GO
