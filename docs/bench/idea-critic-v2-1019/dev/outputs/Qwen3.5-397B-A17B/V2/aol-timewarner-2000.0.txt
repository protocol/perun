**Steelman:** Merging dominant content with the leading internet distributor creates an unbeatable ecosystem, monetizing media through new digital channels while upgrading users to broadband via cable infrastructure.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on the internet provider's volatile stock valuation remaining high post-merger to justify the premium paid to media shareholders. If the dot-com bubble bursts, the "majority" ownership becomes diluted in value, leaving the media conglomerate overpaying for a dying dial-up asset. The "synergy" of cross-promotion assumes users want bundled content rather than best-in-class standalone services, ignoring that cable partners may block access to protect their own interests. Culturally, a fast-moving internet firm cannot easily integrate with a slow, traditional media bureaucracy without talent flight.

**Better Way:** Wait. The internet provider's valuation is historically anomalous. A licensing deal for content distribution captures upside without the catastrophic risk of an all-stock merger at peak valuation. Alternatively, the media conglomerate could invest in broadband infrastructure directly rather than buying a dial-up company facing obsolescence.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-merger, the stock collapses 80% as dial-up revenues vanish faster than broadband conversion occurs. The promised cable synergies fail due to regulatory hurdles and partner resistance. The combined entity is bloated, culturally fractured, and unable to pivot, destroying trillions in shareholder value.

**Fit:** This moves the north star of "digital dominance" but introduces existential financial risk. The cost of delay is low; the cost of error is total capital destruction. The valuation mismatch is a factual blocker: paying peak bubble prices for a depreciating asset class violates basic capital allocation logic.

**Blockers:**
1. **Valuation Mismatch:** Trading stable media assets for overvalued, volatile dial-up equity at market peaks creates immediate downside exposure with no hedge.
2. **Obsolescence Risk:** The core asset (dial-up) is being displaced by the very technology (broadband) the merger claims to unlock, creating a race against time the combined entity may lose.

**Risks:** Cultural clash, regulatory denial, partner retaliation.

**Kill Criteria:** If the internet provider's subscriber count drops by 5% in any quarter post-announcement, halt integration spending.

DECISION: NO-GO
