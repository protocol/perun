The claim is that a closed, curated native app store will succeed despite the CEO's prior public stance against native apps and existing open rival ecosystems. The steelman: a single controlled store balances developer reach with the device's signature stability and security, creating a premium marketplace that carriers cannot easily block, while the 30% fee funds the curation necessary to prevent the "app fatigue" plaguing open rivals.

**Skeptic**: The CEO's previous public argument that web apps are sufficient creates a credibility gap; reversing this without admitting error damages leadership trust. If the review process becomes a bottleneck, the "polish" advantage turns into a "dead platform" perception within months. The 30% cut may deter early adopters if rivals offer 100% revenue retention.

**Better-way**: The cheapest alternative is doubling down on web apps, avoiding the security and review overhead entirely while aligning with the CEO's stated vision. A hybrid model (sideloading allowed but warned against) reduces carrier friction by not forcing a total walled garden immediately. However, these alternatives sacrifice the unique value proposition of a guaranteed, high-quality user experience that differentiates this specific hardware.

**Kill-criteria**: If the review team cannot clear 90% of submissions within 7 days by launch, the ecosystem stalls. If carriers demand veto power over specific app categories as a condition of network access, the single-store model collapses.

**Decision**: The proposal beats "do nothing" because the rival open ecosystems are already fragmenting user experience, creating an opening for a curated premium alternative. The CEO's past stance is a reputational risk, not a functional blocker; the market shift justifies the pivot. The 30% model is standard enough to attract developers seeking volume over margin percentage. The security and stability concerns are the very features this phone sells; opening the device without a gatekeeper destroys the product's core identity. The risks are manageable operational hurdles, not fundamental flaws.

DECISION: GO
