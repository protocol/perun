**Steelman:** A simultaneous, unannounced global rebrand shocks the market into perceiving the retailer as modern, shedding legacy associations instantly.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on three verified fronts. First, it violates **Chesterton's Fence**: the "dark blue square" is the primary recognition asset for a mature brand; removing it destroys distinctiveness before new equity is built. Second, the **pre-mortem** is certain: executing a high-visibility change with "no advance announcement" in the social media era guarantees a backlash narrative of "corporate arrogance" rather than "modernization," alienating the loyal base required for revenue. Third, the **evidence gap** is fatal: the design was "not tested with customers." Rebranding without validation is gambling with core equity, not strategy.

**Better Way:** A phased approach preserves the blue square (the asset) while updating the typography. Test the new wordmark with loyalists first. Announce the change as an evolution honoring heritage, not an erasure. This captures the "contemporary" upside with reversible, low-risk steps.

**Fit:** The goal is to look contemporary, not to confuse the market or trigger a boycott. The current plan achieves confusion and hostility, not modernity.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Asset Destruction:** Removing the recognized blue square eliminates the brand's primary visual anchor without a proven replacement.
2.  **Zero Validation:** Launching an untested design globally creates an undefined but high probability of rejection.
3.  **Communication Failure:** "No advance announcement" ensures the story is controlled by critics, not the brand.

**Risks:** Immediate sales dip, social media outrage, permanent loss of legacy customers, costly reversal if the new logo fails.

**Kill Criteria:** If customer testing shows <60% recognition retention or negative sentiment >20%, the rollout stops. The current proposal has no such gate.

The plan demands we destroy our strongest asset (the square) and blindfold ourselves (no testing) while inviting an attack (no announcement). This is not a strategy; it is self-sabotage disguised as boldness. The cheaper, safer alternative (phased update retaining the square) reaches the same upside without the existential risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
