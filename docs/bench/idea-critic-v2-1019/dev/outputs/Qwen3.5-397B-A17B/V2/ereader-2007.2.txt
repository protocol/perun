**Steelman**: A dedicated, cellular-enabled e-reader removes friction for book buyers, creating a closed ecosystem that secures publisher trust against piracy while expanding the total addressable market beyond physical logistics.

**Skeptic**: The company lacks hardware DNA; failure here could bankrupt the core retail business. Publishers may resist low pricing, and cellular costs could destroy unit economics. However, the "paper-like" screen specifically targets the reading experience gap that caused previous dedicated devices to fail, and the closed system directly addresses the primary publisher objection (piracy).

**Better-Way**: Alternatives include licensing software to existing hardware makers or waiting for tablets. Yet, in 2007, no existing device offers weeks-long battery life or sunlight readability. Licensing surrenders the customer relationship and pricing control. "Do nothing" cedes the digital future to competitors who will eventually erode print sales anyway. The proposed path is the only one that controls the entire value chain.

**Fit & Pre-mortem**: This aligns with the vision of "any book, anywhere, instantly." If it fails, it will likely be due to high device subsidies or slow publisher adoption, not lack of demand. This is a one-way door for capital but a two-way door for strategy (the store can survive without the device, though growth would be slower). The cost of delay is high: whoever defines the digital reading standard captures the market.

**Blockers**: None. The risks (hardware inexperience, margin pressure) are execution challenges, not fatal flaws. The proposal explicitly mitigates the piracy fear, which is the key to publisher buy-in. The cannibalization risk is acceptable because if the company does not cannibalize its print sales, a competitor will cannibalize its entire business.

**Kill Criteria**: Stop if device subsidy costs exceed 30% of hardware price after 18 months, or if publisher participation remains below 50% of top titles by launch.

**Strongest Attack Survived**: The argument that "hardware is outside our experience" was tested. While true, the unique requirement (cellular + e-ink + closed store) means no existing partner can deliver this specific value proposition today. Building internally is the only way to achieve the strategic moat.

DECISION: GO
