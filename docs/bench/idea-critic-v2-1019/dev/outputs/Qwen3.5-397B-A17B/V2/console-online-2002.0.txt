**Steelman:** A unified, paid online service creates a defensible moat through network effects and recurring revenue, differentiating the console via superior user experience (identity, voice, matchmaking) that fragmented, free alternatives cannot match.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on three verified premises from 2002. First, **market readiness**: with few homes possessing broadband, a *mandatory* broadband requirement artificially caps the addressable market to a niche too small to sustain server costs or attract mass-market game developers. Second, **pricing friction**: introducing a double-paywall (console + subscription) for a feature competitors offer free violates established consumer expectations, risking massive backlash and low attachment rates. Third, **operational capability**: the company lacks history in running 24/7 global hosting and support; building this from scratch while launching hardware introduces catastrophic execution risk.

**Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "publisher-led" approach preserves the full installed base and shifts server costs/risks to game studios. A "freemium" model (free basic matchmaking, paid premium features) would test demand without blocking entry.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: The subscriber base remains too small to cover fixed infrastructure costs because the broadband mandate excluded 90% of users, while publishers refused to integrate deeply due to low user counts. The company burns cash on empty servers.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Infrastructure Mismatch:** Mandatory broadband contradicts the verified fact that "few homes have broadband."
2. **Economic Friction:** Paid subscription contradicts the verified norm that "players expect online play to be free."
3. **Capability Gap:** The company has "never run" these operations, creating an unmitigated execution risk.

**Risks:** High churn, publisher revolt, brand damage. These are secondary to the blockers.

**Conclusion:** The plan requires conditions (ubiquitous broadband, willingness to pay) that do not yet exist. It bets the company on changing consumer behavior overnight without a bridge. The mandatory nature of the requirements turns valid risks into immediate blockers. The proposal must be revised to optional participation, dial-up support (or hybrid), and a phased operational rollout. As proposed, it is a go-no-go failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
