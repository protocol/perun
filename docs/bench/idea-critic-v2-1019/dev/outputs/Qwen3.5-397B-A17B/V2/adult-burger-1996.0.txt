Steelman: A massive ad spend can overwrite the "kids' meal" brand identity, forcing adult trial of a premium burger that generates sufficient margin to justify the acquisition cost.

**Skeptic:** The proposal assumes advertising alone can invert a deeply entrenched brand position (fast/cheap/kids) without a corresponding operational shift. In 1996, media fragmentation was lower, but "image" is sticky; a premium price point contradicts the core value proposition that drives volume. The risk isn't just low adoption; it's brand confusion that alienates the existing base while failing to attract the new one. The claim that "largest budget" equals "success" ignores the possibility of burning cash on awareness that converts to zero sales because the product-channel fit is broken.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" or "incremental" alternative is superior. Introduce a single "adult-oriented" item as a limited-time offer (LTO) in specific markets with mature demographics before committing to a permanent menu change and industry-record spending. This tests price elasticity and brand perception with capped downside. A cheaper path exists: reposition existing higher-margin items for adults via targeted local marketing rather than a global premium launch.

**Kill-Criteria:** If the brand is truly defined by "value for kids," a premium product is a one-way door that damages equity. The cost of delay is low; the cost of failure (massive write-down on ad spend + menu clutter) is existential. The proposal lacks a pilot phase, treating a strategic pivot as a binary switch.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Unproven Demand:** No evidence provided that the current customer base or the target adult demographic wants premium food from *this* specific chain.
2. **Contradictory Positioning:** The core brand (kids/value) actively fights the proposed premium positioning; no mechanism is offered to bridge this gap other than "spend more."
3. **Financial Risk:** Committing the "largest budget" before validating unit economics violates basic capital allocation logic.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, cannibalization of value meals, massive sunk cost in advertising.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It bets the company on an unproven assumption (ads can fix product-brand mismatch) with maximum exposure and zero staging. It must be revised to include a market pilot with defined kill metrics before any national rollout or record-breaking spend.

DECISION: NO-GO
