**Steelman:** Leveraging unmatched distribution and marketing scale to convert a niche, high-growth tea brand into a mass-market powerhouse, capturing value the current fragmented network cannot.

**Attack:** The core value of the target is its "cult image" and loyalty, driven by its quirky, independent identity. The proposal explicitly plans to erase this identity by forcing mass-market advertising and supermarket distribution. This is not synergy; it is cannibalization. The "independent distributor loyalty" risk is actually a certainty of churn; these partners will likely drop the brand once corporate terms apply. Furthermore, entering a space where giants are already launching teas suggests the buyer is late to the trend, paying a premium ($1.7B) for growth that may stall the moment the brand loses its authentic edge. The plan assumes the brand's success is accidental rather than intrinsic to its current operating model.

**Better Way:** Maintain the brand's independent distribution and niche marketing to preserve growth momentum, using the buyer's capital only for production scaling or selective geographic expansion without altering the brand's core positioning. Alternatively, invest the $1.7B in internal innovation to launch a new tea line from scratch, avoiding the integration risk entirely.

**Kill Criteria:** If the brand's sales growth drops below 10% within 12 months post-acquisition, or if more than 30% of independent distributors terminate contracts within 6 months, the integration strategy has failed.

**Verdict:** The proposal relies on a fundamental contradiction: destroying the very attributes that make the asset valuable to extract value. The plan to "fix" the distribution and marketing is the primary risk, not a solution. The $1.7B price tag assumes continued high growth, which is unlikely under the proposed corporate stranglehold. The evidence suggests this acquisition will kill the goose to sell the eggs.

DECISION: NO-GO
