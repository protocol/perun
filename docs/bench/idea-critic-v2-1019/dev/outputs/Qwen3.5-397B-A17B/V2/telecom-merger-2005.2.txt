**Steelman:** Acquire the target to capture its sticky, high-value business segment and push-to-talk dominance, accepting short-term integration friction to secure long-term revenue diversity.

**Attack & Options:**
The core flaw is the $36 billion price tag against incompatible, legacy technology. In 2005, the industry trajectory is clearly shifting toward 3G data and smartphones, not legacy voice-centric networks.
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids $36B debt and the massive engineering burden of running two incompatible networks. Misses the business segment but preserves capital for modern infrastructure.
2.  **Build/Buy Tech Only:** Attempt to license the push-to-talk protocol or poach key enterprise contracts without acquiring the broken network. Lower cost, no legacy baggage.
3.  **The Proposal:** Forces the buyer to maintain "stranded" legacy equipment while diverting critical engineering resources from next-gen data deployment to integration. The "large goodwill" implies paying a massive premium for assets that are technologically obsolete.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This contradicts the north star of modernizing for mobile data. If we assume the market moves to data (as it did), we are buying a shrinking revenue stream at a peak price.
*Pre-mortem:* Two years post-merge, integration costs have blown out, draining cash flow. The legacy network requires expensive maintenance while customers churn to modern smartphones. The debt load prevents competitive pricing on new data plans. We own a "zombie" network.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blocker:** The proposal admits the technologies are "incompatible" and the target is "limited for data." Paying $36B for incompatible, non-strategic legacy tech creates an immediate negative ROI compared to organic growth or smaller tuck-ins. The cost of integration directly cannibalizes the investment needed for the future (data).
*   **Risks:** Culture clash, customer churn during migration. These are manageable, but the blocker is fundamental economics and technology direction.

The deal bets on the past (voice/PTT) rather than the future (data). The incompatibility turns the acquisition into a capital sink rather than a synergy play.

DECISION: NO-GO
