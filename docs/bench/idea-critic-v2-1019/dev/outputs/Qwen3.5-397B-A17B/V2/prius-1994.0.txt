Steelman: Committing now secures a decade-long technological moat and defines the category before regulators force a frantic, inferior catch-up later.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. It commits to mass production by 1997 using a novel transmission and unproven nickel-metal-hydride batteries. The premise that this can be achieved relies on durability assumptions that are explicitly stated as "unproven." In automotive engineering, launching volume production with unverified battery life creates an immediate, calculable warranty liability that threatens corporate solvency if the failure rate exceeds trivial margins.

The "better way" analysis reveals a superior alternative: a technology demonstrator program. Since fuel is cheap and regulators have not mandated hybrids, there is no market pull to justify the massive capital expenditure of retooling for mass production. The risk of being first is not just high cost, but catastrophic brand damage if the first widely available hybrid proves unreliable. A competitor watching from the sidelines can let this automaker absorb the R&D costs and warranty claims, then enter the market three years later with a refined, cheaper solution once the technology matures.

The cost of delay is negligible because the regulatory and economic drivers for adoption do not yet exist. Waiting allows the company to validate battery durability in limited fleets without exposing the entire brand to a potential recall disaster. The proposal attempts to skip the learning phase and jump straight to volume, ignoring the "one-way door" nature of a failed mass launch.

Blockers:
1. Unproven battery durability creates an undefined but potentially unlimited warranty exposure that cannot be modeled or capped.
2. No market demand signal (cheap fuel, no mandates) means the unit economics will be negative at scale, burning cash without volume leverage.

Risks:
1. Competitors free-riding on proven technology.
2. Regulatory shifts occurring slower than anticipated.

The plan must be revised to a limited pilot program with strict kill criteria based on real-world battery cycle data before any mass production commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
