The claim is that a computer and music player maker can disrupt the mobile market by launching a premium, keyboard-less touchscreen phone with exclusive carrier control and revenue sharing, despite having no phone industry experience.

Steelman: Leveraging existing design mastery and music ecosystem to create a superior user experience that justifies a premium price, while flipping the carrier power dynamic through exclusivity and revenue share to fund innovation without compromising the product vision.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing: Risk erosion of music player sales as competitors integrate music into phones.
2. License software to existing handset makers: Faster reach but cedes control of user experience and hardware integration, likely resulting in a mediocre product.
3. Build a keyboard-based business phone: Safer entry matching current market leaders but fails to differentiate or leverage core touch/music strengths.

Fit: Moves the north star of integrating computing, music, and communication into a single, superior device.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the premium price alienates consumers accustomed to subsidized keyboards, or if the exclusive carrier partnership limits market penetration too severely to achieve scale. The lack of third-party apps initially may make the device seem limited compared to established smartphones.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the assumption that users value a full web browser and music integration enough to pay a premium and adapt to a touchscreen interface. No direct evidence exists of market demand for a keyboard-less phone at this price point in 2007.

Blockers: NONE. The risks (no experience, premium price, exclusivity, no apps) are significant but represent execution challenges and market uncertainties, not factual contradictions or impossible constraints. The "no experience" blocker is mitigated by the team's proven track record in adjacent hardware and software integration. The "premium price" is a strategic choice, not a failure condition.

Risks: Market rejection of touch-only interface; carrier exclusivity limiting volume; slow app ecosystem development.

Kill criteria: If pre-orders or initial sales within the first quarter do not meet a threshold of X units (to be defined based on production capacity), or if carrier negotiations fail to secure the required design control and revenue share, the project must pivot or halt.

DECISION: GO
