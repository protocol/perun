The claim is that a 1999 grocery startup should immediately commit $1B to build automated warehouses in a dozen cities before validating unit economics in its single launch market.

Steelman: Aggressive infrastructure pre-commitment secures first-mover dominance and prevents supply chain bottlenecks during hyper-growth.

However, this plan fails the blocker test. The proposal explicitly admits "no operating track record" and "low-margin business" while proposing to scale before learning "unit economics." In 1999, online grocery logistics were unproven; customer density and pick-rates were unknown variables. Building "highly automated" warehouses assumes a stable operational model that does not yet exist. If the first city reveals that humans pick faster than machines for diverse SKUs, or that 30-minute slots destroy margins due to low density, the $1B investment becomes stranded, non-adaptable assets.

The "do nothing" alternative regarding expansion (focusing only on the first city) costs less and preserves capital to iterate the model. A "staged" alternative (build one warehouse after proving the first city's economics) offers the same upside potential with drastically lower risk of total capital loss. The cost of delay in building the other 11 warehouses is negligible compared to the cost of building the wrong infrastructure.

The pre-mortem is clear: The company runs out of cash because fixed costs from 12 automated facilities overwhelm revenue from unproven markets, forcing bankruptcy before the model can be corrected. This is a one-way door; once concrete is poured and robots installed, pivoting is prohibitively expensive.

Blocker: The plan commits irreversible capital ($1B) based on unverified assumptions about unit economics and automation suitability in a nascent market (1999), contradicting the stated need to "learn" those economics first. No evidence suggests the current model is ready for replication.

Risks: Margin erosion, low customer density, technology mismatch, rapid cash burn.

Kill Criteria: Halt expansion if the first city does not show positive contribution margin per order within 6 months. Leading metric: Contribution margin per order in the pilot city.

DECISION: NO-GO
