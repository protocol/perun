**Steelman:** Acquiring the startup secures a free, open mobile OS to commoditize handset software, forcing widespread adoption of mobile web browsing and locking in future search-ad dominance before competitors establish closed ecosystems.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the mismatch between a software-only ad company and the hardware-heavy reality of 2005 mobile distribution. Carriers and handset makers (Nokia, Motorola) control the pipeline and have no incentive to install an unproven OS from a search vendor. The $50M price tag for six people with no revenue model is speculative; the "free" aspect does not offset the integration costs for manufacturers.

Alternatives include:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Wait for the market to mature, risking entry barriers later.
2.  **Partnership:** License the tech without full acquisition, reducing upfront cash risk.
3.  **Internal Build:** Too slow; the window for mobile search is opening now.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because carriers refuse to preload the OS, handset makers stick to incumbent vendors, and the startup burns cash for five years with zero market share. The "open source" angle fails to penetrate the walled gardens of carrier distribution.

**Fit & Evidence:** The proposal assumes open source alone breaks carrier control, a claim unsupported by 2005 market dynamics where carriers dictated handset specs. The north star (search ad revenue) is valid, but the path relies on a distribution miracle. The cost of delay is high if mobile search becomes proprietary elsewhere, but the cost of failure is $50M+ with no salvage value.

**Blockers:**
*   **Distribution Reality:** No verified path to pre-installation on major handsets; carriers hold the keys and have stated no interest in new OS entrants.
*   **Revenue Gap:** No mechanism to monetize the OS itself; total reliance on a future mobile web boom that requires mass adoption first.

**Risks:** Multi-year burn rate, competitor retaliation (closed ecosystems), and the startup team's inability to scale beyond a prototype.

**Decision:** The distribution blocker is fatal under current 2005 conditions. Without a guaranteed path to handset pre-installation, the acquisition is a speculative bet on a market that does not yet exist, with no leverage to force entry.

DECISION: NO-GO
