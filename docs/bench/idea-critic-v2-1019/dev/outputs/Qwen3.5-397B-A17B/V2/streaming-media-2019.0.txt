The claim is that withdrawing licensed content to launch a subsidized, standalone streaming service will generate sufficient long-term value to offset immediate billions in lost licensing revenue and massive operational cash burn.

**Skeptic:** The proposal assumes the conglomerate can successfully build and scale a global technology platform despite having zero prior operational experience in direct-to-consumer streaming infrastructure. It further assumes that a price undercut alone drives migration, ignoring that the market leader's hundred-million-subscriber moat relies on content breadth and habit, not just price. The "multi-year operating loss" is treated as an investment rather than a potential bottomless pit if subscriber acquisition costs exceed lifetime value.

**Better-way:** The "do nothing" or "hybrid" alternative retains billions in guaranteed licensing revenue while launching a smaller, niche service or partnering with an existing tech giant. This avoids the binary risk of burning cash on unproven tech while alienating current distribution partners. A staged rollout tests the technology and content appeal without committing the entire library immediately.

**Kill-criteria:** If the company cannot demonstrate a functional, scalable streaming platform before pulling content, the plan fails. If subscriber growth does not offset the lost licensing revenue within 24 months, the cash burn becomes unsustainable. The one-way door of burning bridges with distributors cannot be reversed if the new service stalls.

**Decision:** The proposal fails the blocker test. The core assumption—that the company can execute a complex tech pivot it has never attempted while simultaneously sacrificing its most reliable revenue stream—is unsupported by evidence of capability. The risk of catastrophic cash burn combined with technical failure outweighs the speculative upside of chasing a saturated market leader. The lack of a verified technical foundation is a factual blocker, not merely a risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
