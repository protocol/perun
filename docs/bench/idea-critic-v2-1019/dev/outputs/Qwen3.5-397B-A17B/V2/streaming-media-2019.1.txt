The claim is that withdrawing licensed content to launch a subsidized, standalone streaming service will generate sufficient long-term value to offset immediate billions in lost licensing revenue and massive operational cash burn.

**Steelman:** Direct-to-consumer ownership captures full lifetime value per user and data control, which licensing dilutes; a low-price entry disrupts the incumbent's monopoly, forcing market share shift that justifies short-term losses if subscriber acquisition costs remain low and churn is managed.

**Better-way:** The "do nothing" option preserves guaranteed, high-margin licensing income with zero execution risk. A hybrid model (licensing non-core content while launching a smaller, niche service) reduces capital exposure. Chesterton's Fence applies: the licensing model exists because it monetizes content without requiring the conglomerate to master consumer tech, marketing, and customer support—capabilities it currently lacks.

**Pre-mortem:** The initiative fails because the company underestimates the technology debt and content volume required to retain users against a deeply entrenched rival. The "low price" triggers a price war the incumbent can win due to scale. Cash burn exceeds projections as content costs rise to fill the library, leading to a forced pivot or fire sale before reaching profitability. The loss of licensing revenue creates a cash crunch that starves theatrical production, degrading the very asset the streamer relies on.

**Fit & Evidence:** The proposal assumes tens of millions of subscribers are achievable solely via price undercutting, ignoring that the incumbent's library depth and network effects create high switching costs. The "multi-year operating loss" is treated as an investment, but without verified unit economics (LTV > CAC), it is simply burning cash to buy low-quality users. The risk of cannibalizing high-margin cable/theatrical revenue is acknowledged but not quantified against the speculative streaming upside.

**Blockers:** No evidence proves the company can build/operate a competitive tech platform or that the price differential alone drives sufficient migration to replace lost licensing billions. The plan relies on unproven execution capabilities and speculative market response while sacrificing guaranteed revenue.

**Risks:** Massive cash burn, technological failure, brand dilution, and irreversible loss of licensing partnerships.

DECISION: NO-GO
