**Steelman:** Combining German engineering prestige with US mass-market profitability creates a dominant global entity through platform sharing and balanced governance.

**Attack:** The "merger of equals" structure is likely a facade masking a cultural collision that will paralyze decision-making. The German firm's premium identity risks dilution by association with mass-market trucks, while the US partner's current profitability relies entirely on a cyclical light-truck boom that may burst post-1998. Distinct labor cultures (codetermination vs. at-will) and engineering philosophies (over-engineering vs. cost-efficiency) create unbridgeable operational friction. History suggests such mergers often become de facto takeovers, breeding resentment and talent exodus rather than synergy.

**Alternatives:** 
1. **Do Nothing:** Preserve distinct brand values and avoid integration chaos; pursue loose alliances for specific platforms instead.
2. **Minority Stake:** Gain market access without full cultural integration or balance-sheet risk.
3. **Asset Swap:** Trade specific platforms without merging corporate entities.

**Pre-mortem:** Five years post-merger, the combined stock underperforms both predecessors. Integration costs skyrocket as teams fight over standards. The truck market crashes, dragging down the German parent. The "equal" board deadlocks on strategic pivots, causing missed EV transitions. The premium brand suffers reputational damage from cost-cutting measures aimed at mass-market margins.

**Blockers:** 
- **Cultural Incompatibility:** No proven mechanism exists to merge German codetermination labor models with US management styles without massive productivity loss.
- **Cyclical Risk:** Basing a permanent merger on peak-cycle truck profits violates basic valuation discipline; the US partner is a "sell" at the top of the market, not a "buy."
- **Identity Contradiction:** Premium and mass-market brands rarely share platforms successfully without cannibalizing the premium price point.

**Risks:** Integration failure, brand dilution, cyclical downturn, leadership power struggles.

**Kill Criteria:** If integration costs exceed 15% of projected synergies within 24 months, or if premium brand margins drop below 8%, trigger divestiture.

The proposal ignores the high probability that "complementary" actually means "incompatible." The cyclical timing is dangerous, and the governance structure invites deadlock.

DECISION: NO-GO
