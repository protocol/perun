**Steelman:** Combining dominant content with emerging digital distribution creates an unbeatable ecosystem, monetizing legacy assets through high-growth internet channels while accelerating broadband adoption via cable infrastructure.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on three fragile premises. First, it values the internet provider at a peak bubble valuation using stock as currency; if the dial-up market collapses before broadband integration yields revenue, the majority shareholder stake becomes worthless, diluting the media conglomerate permanently. Second, "synergy" assumes cultural compatibility between a fast-moving tech firm and a traditional media bureaucracy; history shows such mergers often result in talent exodus and operational paralysis rather than innovation. Third, the technical promise of delivering high-speed access over cable networks ignores existing contractual friction with cable partners who may block or degrade access to protect their own interests, rendering the distribution advantage illusory.

**Better Way:** Instead of a full merger, pursue strategic licensing deals and minority equity stakes. This captures upside potential without exposing the entire media empire to the internet company's volatile valuation or integrating incompatible cultures. Wait for the tech valuation to stabilize post-bubble before considering consolidation.

**Kill Criteria:** If the internet provider's subscriber count drops by more than 10% within 12 months, or if regulatory approval takes longer than 9 months, the merger thesis fails. The leading metric is broadband conversion rate among existing dial-up users; if this remains below 5% after 18 months, the distribution synergy is nonexistent.

**Verdict:** The risk profile is asymmetric. The media conglomerate risks permanent dilution and cultural destruction for a synergy that is technically unproven and contractually uncertain. The valuation disparity suggests buying the asset later at a fraction of the current cost. The "premium" seen by the board is likely a mirage created by market mania.

DECISION: NO-GO
