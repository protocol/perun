**Steelman:** Paying $19B secures the dominant mobile messaging layer to prevent existential displacement of the core social network, buying time to integrate a non-monetized but essential utility.

**Attack:** The price is 12x the prior valuation with zero revenue and no monetization path. The target explicitly rejects advertising, the buyer's core model. With only 50 employees, integration risk is extreme; founder departure post-payout could leave a hollow shell. Privacy regulations could fracture the global user base. However, the "do nothing" alternative risks the core platform becoming obsolete as user activity migrates entirely to messaging. The cost of delay is the potential loss of the entire mobile future.

**Better Way:** Building a competitor internally is slower and has already failed to gain traction against this network effect. Buying a smaller rival lacks the same defensive moat.

**Fit:** This moves the North Star of "connecting the world" to the mobile-first reality where texting replaces posting.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if regulators block the deal, founders leave immediately, or the user base churns due to privacy backlash. Yet, the strategic necessity outweighs these risks; the blocker test reveals no factual contradiction that the plan fails *as proposed* to solve the displacement threat. The high price is the premium for survival, not just revenue.

**Blockers:** NONE. The astronomical cost and lack of revenue are features of a defensive monopoly buy, not factual blockers proving the plan cannot achieve its strategic goal.
**Risks:** Massive overpayment, regulatory scrutiny, founder retention, cultural clash, failure to monetize.

**Kill Criteria:** If monthly active users drop >20% within 18 months post-integration, or if key founders depart within 6 months without a succession plan, initiate divestiture or fire-sale of assets. Leading metric: Daily active messaging threads per user. Review date: Q4 2015.

DECISION: GO
