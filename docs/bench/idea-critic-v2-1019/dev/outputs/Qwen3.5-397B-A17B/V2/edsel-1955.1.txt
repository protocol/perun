Steelman: Capturing the growing mid-priced segment with a distinct brand prevents customer defection to competitors and maximizes internal market coverage.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Macroeconomic Contradiction:** The proposal admits a recession risk and potential segment shrinkage while committing to massive fixed costs (new dealer network, tooling) for a 1957 debut. Launching a capital-intensive luxury-adjacent product immediately before a documented economic contraction violates basic counter-cyclical strategy; inventory and dealer insolvency risks are mathematically elevated.
2.  **Channel Saturation:** Building a "new dealer network" from scratch requires years of lead time and capital. In a shrinking market, recruiting viable dealers for an unproven brand is historically improbable, creating a distribution bottleneck that marketing cannot overcome.
3.  **Cannibalization vs. Differentiation:** The "perceived gap" relies on styling and branding rather than mechanical distinction. If the segment shrinks, internal competition with the firm's existing low and premium lines will erode margins across the entire portfolio without expanding the total addressable market.

**Risks:**
*   Consumer motivation studies often fail to predict behavior during actual economic hardship.
*   Massive advertising spend yields diminishing returns if the product is unavailable due to dealer ramp-up delays.

**Analysis:**
The plan bets on continued segment growth despite explicit evidence of recession risk. The requirement for a new dealer network introduces extreme operational fragility exactly when capital preservation should be prioritized. The "go-if" condition would require delaying the dealer rollout until economic indicators stabilize, but the proposal demands a 1957 debut regardless. The fixed costs of the dealer network combined with a shrinking market create a loss scenario that outweighs the potential upside of segment capture. The strongest attack—that the timing converts a strategic opportunity into a liquidity trap—survives because the proposal offers no mitigation for the recession other than hope that the segment defies the broader economy.

DECISION: NO-GO
