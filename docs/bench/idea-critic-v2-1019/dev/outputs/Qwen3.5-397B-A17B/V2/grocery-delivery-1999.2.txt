**Steelman:** Rapidly building a nationwide network of automated warehouses secures dominant market share and infrastructure moats before competitors can react, leveraging abundant capital to bypass slow organic growth.

**Attack:** The proposal violates the fundamental logic of high-uncertainty ventures. The startup has zero operating data from its single launch city (June 1999). Unit economics for low-margin groceries depend entirely on local density and order frequency, which are currently unknown. Committing $1 billion to fixed assets (warehouses, trucks) across a dozen cities before validating the model in one creates massive, irreversible downside. If the first city fails to achieve profitability due to thin margins or logistical complexity, the company will be locked into expensive, underutilized infrastructure nationwide, burning cash at an unsustainable rate. The "first-mover advantage" is illusory if the underlying business model is flawed; being the biggest failure is not a win.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing (Organic Growth):** Validate unit economics in the first city for 12–18 months. Expand only after proving a repeatable, profitable playbook. Cost: Low. Risk: Slower expansion, but capital preserved.
2.  **Staged Expansion:** Use a fraction of capital to test 2–3 diverse markets with flexible, leased infrastructure. Cost: Moderate. Risk: Balanced learning vs. speed.

**Pre-mortem:** By 2001, the company is insolvent. The automated warehouses are empty or operating at 10% capacity because customer density never materialized. The $1 billion is gone, and the debt load prevents restructuring. The low-margin nature of groceries means even slight inefficiencies in the unproven automated systems destroy all profit.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** No operating track record exists; unit economics are unproven.
*   **Fact:** $1 billion commitment is irreversible (one-way door) based on assumptions, not data.
*   **Fact:** Low-margin business cannot sustain massive fixed-cost overhead without proven volume.

**Risks:** Capital intensity, execution complexity, unknown customer density requirements.

**Verdict:** The plan bets the entire company on unverified assumptions. It fails the "must beat do nothing" test because the cheaper, staged alternative offers the same upside potential with drastically lower ruin risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
