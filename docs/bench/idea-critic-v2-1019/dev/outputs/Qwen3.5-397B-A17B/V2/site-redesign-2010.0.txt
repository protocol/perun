The claim is that a full rewrite with altered voting mechanics and a forced migration will reverse slowing traffic. The steelman: only a radical redesign favoring publishers can attract new growth sufficient to offset community churn.

However, concrete blockers exist. First, the proposal contradicts verified user values: the core community explicitly prizes existing voting mechanics. Changing this removes the primary retention driver for the loyal base that currently sustains traffic. Second, the "all-at-once" migration eliminates the ability to rollback. Given the high engineering risk of a from-scratch rewrite and the presence of a growing rival, a failed launch results in immediate, unrecoverable loss of the entire user base to the competitor. Third, favoring publisher content over community voting fundamentally shifts the product's north star from "community-driven" to "publisher-driven" without evidence that this specific audience desires such a shift.

The pre-mortem is clear: the rewrite launches, the core community rejects the new voting logic, engagement plummets, and because there is no rollback path, the site cannot revert to the stable version before users migrate to the rival. The cost of delay (waiting to test) is lower than the cost of failure (total collapse). A safer alternative exists: A/B test the new surfacing logic on a segment or launch a parallel beta before forcing migration. The current plan beats "do nothing" only if the assumption holds that new publisher-focused users will arrive faster than legacy users leave, which is unproven and highly risky given the rival's momentum.

Blockers:
1. Changing valued voting mechanics threatens the core retention engine.
2. No rollback path on a high-risk rewrite creates a single point of catastrophic failure.
3. Strategic pivot to publisher content lacks validation with the existing base.

Risks:
1. Rival captures displaced users during the transition.
2. Engineering delays extend the period of vulnerability.

This plan fails the blocker test: it risks the entire asset base on an unproven hypothesis with no safety net.

DECISION: NO-GO
