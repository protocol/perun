**Steelman:** A stock-for-stock merger creates a global powerhouse combining German engineering prestige with US mass-market profitability and SUV dominance, achieving immediate scale and platform synergies.

**Attack:** The "merger of equals" framing masks a fundamental cultural collision between a hierarchical, consensus-driven German model and a quarterly-obsessed, aggressive US culture. History shows such dichotomies paralyze decision-making and bleed talent. More critically, the US partner's profitability relies entirely on a cyclical light-truck boom; acquiring this exposure at the cycle's peak introduces massive downside risk just as the German firm seeks stability. Platform sharing between premium and mass-market segments often dilutes brand equity rather than saving costs, risking the core value of the luxury division. The "entry into the mass market" is a distraction that diverts focus from the high-margin core business during a complex integration.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Preserve the premium brand's purity and avoid integrating a cyclical, culturally disparate entity.
2. *Strategic Alliance:* Share specific platforms or technologies without full corporate integration, limiting cultural friction and financial exposure.

**Pre-mortem:** In five years, the combined entity is broken. The cultural clash caused a brain drain of key engineers; the truck market collapsed, dragging the whole group into loss; and the premium brand suffered reputation damage from perceived "cheapening" via shared components. The "equal" structure resulted in gridlock, preventing necessary rapid restructuring.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Cultural Incompatibility:** No viable plan exists to merge two diametrically opposed corporate governance and labor models without significant value destruction.
2. **Cyclical Peak Risk:** Acquiring a partner dependent on a specific, cyclical boom at its height violates basic acquisition timing logic; the downside is unbounded.
3. **Brand Dilution:** The proposal lacks a concrete firewall to prevent mass-market association from eroding the premium brand's pricing power.

**Risks:** Integration failure, labor strikes, truck market correction, brand erosion.

**Kill Criteria:** If the US partner's truck sales drop >15% within 18 months, or if key German engineering leadership turnover exceeds 20% in year one, the merger must be unwound or the divisions fully separated.

The risks are structural and immediate, not merely executable challenges. The "merger of equals" is a fiction that will hide a toxic takeover dynamic.

DECISION: NO-GO
