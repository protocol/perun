Steelman: Combining two distressed media assets creates sufficient scale and data synergy to disrupt the online ad duopoly, leveraging the carrier's distribution to reverse declining traffic.

However, the claim fails the blocker test. The proposal relies on the assumption that "scale" in declining legacy portals equates to competitive viability against algorithm-driven platforms. The facts state the target's revenue and traffic are in structural decline, management is unstable, and the acquirer (a wireless carrier) has zero history in consumer media. This is not an execution risk; it is a fundamental mismatch of capabilities. The carrier lacks the core competency (media product management) to fix the target, and the target lacks the growth engine to justify the $4.8 billion price tag.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserves capital, avoids integrating two failing cultures.
2. Invest in carrier's own data platforms: Cheaper, leverages existing core strength without legacy baggage.
3. Acquire a smaller, growing mobile-first entity: Higher cost per user initially, but positive momentum vs. negative momentum.

The "cheaper" alternative is doing nothing or building organically, both of which offer better risk-adjusted returns than buying a falling knife. The pre-mortem is clear: integration drags down the carrier's stock, talent flees due to cultural clash and strategic confusion, and ad revenue continues to slide as the market shifts to closed ecosystems (walled gardens) that this open-web combination cannot match. The cost of delay is negligible because the asset is deteriorating, not appreciating.

Blocker: The acquirer has no relevant experience, and the target's core metrics (traffic/revenue) are structurally declining, contradicting the claim that this combination will create a competitive, scaled business. The math does not work: buying decline does not create growth.

Risks: Massive integration failure, continued revenue erosion, distraction from core wireless business.

Kill Criteria: If post-merger integration does not stabilize traffic within 12 months, divest immediately. Leading metric: Monthly Active Users (MAU) trend line.

DECISION: NO-GO
