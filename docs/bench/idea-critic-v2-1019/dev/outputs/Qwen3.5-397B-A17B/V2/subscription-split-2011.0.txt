The proposal to split the DVD and streaming services into separate brands, accounts, and billing systems in September 2011 ignores a critical blocker: the immediate, severe customer backlash to the July 2011 price increase. The rationale claims the DVD business is declining and needs separation, but executing a complex operational split (new names, separate queues, dual logins) while customers are already reeling from a 60% price hike creates a compound failure mode.

**Blocker:** The proposal assumes customers will accept increased friction (two bills, two logins, lost recommendations) immediately after a massive price hike. Historical context (known fact) indicates this specific combination triggered mass cancellations and brand damage. The "declining DVD" argument is valid long-term, but the timing is catastrophic. The cost of delay (waiting for price anger to subside) is lower than the risk of accelerating churn through forced fragmentation.

**Better Way:** Maintain a unified account and brand interface while internally managing the distinct P&Ls. Allow the streaming service to pursue its strategy without forcing a consumer-facing divorce during a pricing crisis. Defer the physical separation of brands until the DVD subscriber base has naturally attrited further and price sensitivity has stabilized.

**Kill Criteria:** If the split proceeds, a >10% churn rate in the first quarter would confirm the failure hypothesis. However, the pre-mortem shows this outcome is highly probable given the starting conditions. The proposal fails the "fit" test against the immediate strategic constraint: customer retention post-price-hike.

The strongest attack is that this move converts a pricing complaint into an existential brand crisis by adding friction to anger. The evidence is the proximity to the July price hike; the premise that "different management" requires "separate consumer brands" right now is unsupported and dangerous.

DECISION: NO-GO
