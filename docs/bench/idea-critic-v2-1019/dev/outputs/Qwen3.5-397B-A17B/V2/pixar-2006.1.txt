**Verdict: REVISE (Internal); Decision for Executive Team: NO-GO as proposed.**

The claim that an immediate $7.4B all-stock acquisition is the optimal path fails the blocker test. While the strategic fit (securing hit-making IP and talent) is sound given the hand-drawn unit's underperformance, the specific *structure* and *timing* contain fatal flaws based solely on the provided facts.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Valuation vs. Output:** The price is explicitly flagged as "very high" against a production rate of only one film per year. Without a guaranteed increase in output or a multi-year slate locked in pre-deal, the ROI math does not close on the stated facts. This is a financial blocker, not just a risk.
2.  **Governance Concentration:** Making the studio's creative leaders responsible for the entire animation division while simultaneously giving the studio's largest shareholder (the founder) a major stake in the conglomerate creates an unchecked power center. This conflates operational control with equity leverage, creating an unmanageable conflict of interest that threatens the broader conglomerate's governance.

**Better Way:**
The "do nothing" option leads to partnership loss, which is unacceptable. However, a **structured joint venture** or a **multi-picture distribution guarantee** with equity kickers achieves the primary goal (securing content and alignment) without the immediate $7.4B cash-equity outlay and the governance trap. This cheaper, reversible alternative reaches the same upside (content security) while mitigating the valuation and control blockers.

**Kill Criteria & Pre-mortem:**
If the deal proceeds as proposed, the pre-mortem failure mode is clear: culture clash causes key creative talent to exit within 18 months, leaving the conglomerate with massive debt/equity dilution and no increased production volume to justify the cost. The kill criterion for any revised deal must be a contractual commitment to increase annual film output by at least 50% within two years; without this, the valuation remains unsupported.

The proposal must be revised to decouple the creative leadership appointment from the equity swap and to include volume guarantees that justify the premium. As it stands, the financial and governance risks are blockers.
