The claim is that a clear cola, successful in test markets, should launch nationwide immediately with a major campaign. The steelman: this extension captures purity-seeking demand before rivals, leveraging existing distribution to offset slowing core growth.

However, critical blockers exist within the provided facts. First, the risk that "shoppers may link cola flavor with color" strikes at the product's fundamental value proposition; if the sensory experience contradicts the visual cue, repeat purchase will collapse regardless of initial trial. Second, "test markets can overstate demand," specifically for novelty items where "novelty buyers may not repeat." A positive test response driven by curiosity is not evidence of sustainable volume. Third, the threat of cannibalization against the flagship brand is high; if the clear version merely shifts existing users rather than expanding the base, the net revenue impact could be negative once the "large marketing campaign" costs are deducted.

The proposal treats these known, structural risks as mere footnotes rather than disqualifiers. Launching nationwide with a "large marketing campaign" assumes the test market data predicts long-term behavior, which the proposal itself admits is uncertain for novelty goods. The cheaper, safer alternative is a staged regional rollout without a massive national spend, allowing verification of repeat purchase rates and cannibalization effects before committing irreversible capital. The current plan fails to prove that the "positive response" translates to profitability after accounting for the specific risks of flavor-color dissonance and novelty decay.

Because the proposal ignores the high probability that test market success is a novelty artifact and risks significant brand equity and capital on an unproven repeat-rate model, it cannot be approved as stated. The evidence provided suggests the plan relies on optimistic assumptions that directly contradict the listed risks.
