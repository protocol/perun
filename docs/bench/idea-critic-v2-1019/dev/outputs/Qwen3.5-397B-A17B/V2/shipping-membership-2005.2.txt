**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee ($79) eliminates per-order friction, locking in loyalty and increasing purchase frequency enough to offset shipping losses through volume and margin expansion.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the unit economics gap: if heavy users ship >8 times annually at a loss >$10/order, the program bleeds cash immediately. The proposal admits economics are "unproven," which is a fatal flaw for a capital-intensive rollout. A "do nothing" approach preserves margin but cedes retention. A superior alternative is a **pilot**: limit the offer to a specific zip code or customer segment for 90 days to validate the "heavy buyer" hypothesis before national commitment. Another alternative is a lower-tier monthly subscription to reduce reversal risk and upfront customer hesitation.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
In 2005, e-commerce trust was lower; a one-way door (hard to reverse once sold) risks brand damage if the service degrades due to cost-cutting later. If the retailer underestimates the surge in order volume, logistics could collapse, destroying the very convenience promised. The cost of delay (waiting for pilot data) is minimal compared to the risk of a failed national launch.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*Blocker:* The proposal explicitly states "economics are unproven." Launching a nationwide financial commitment without verified unit economics is a failure of fiduciary duty. You cannot approve a bet where the core variable (shipping cost vs. frequency lift) is unknown.
*Risks:* Margin hit, operational strain, and reversal difficulty are severe but manageable *if* the math works. Since the math is missing, these remain secondary to the primary blocker.

**Decision:**
The plan fails the evidence test. You cannot greenlight a structural change to the revenue model based on hope. The "unproven" status is a hard stop. The correct path is a time-boxed, capped pilot to generate the missing data, not a full launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
