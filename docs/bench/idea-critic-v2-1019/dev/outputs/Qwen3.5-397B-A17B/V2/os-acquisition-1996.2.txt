**Steelman:** Acquiring a modern, object-oriented OS core from a small vendor is the only viable path to rapidly replace a failing, dated system after two internal failures, leveraging external innovation to arrest market share decline.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The proposal faces severe headwinds: the target has low sales, migration is technically hazardous, the founder's history is toxic, and the price-to-revenue ratio appears irrational. Licensing remains a safer, cheaper alternative.

However, the "do nothing" trajectory is terminal; internal development has already failed twice, proving the organization cannot build a replacement alone. Licensing a competitor's system surrenders strategic control and differentiation, effectively admitting defeat as a platform holder. The target's low sales are irrelevant; the asset being purchased is the *technology architecture* and *engineering talent*, not current revenue. The high price is a premium for speed and survival, not a multiple of past earnings.

Regarding the founder: while their history is difficult, their return as an advisor (not immediate CEO) mitigates governance risk while securing critical institutional knowledge for the migration. The migration risk is real but inherent to *any* OS replacement; delaying increases the cost of failure as market share erodes further. The "cheaper" alternative of licensing fails the strategic fit test: it does not solve the core problem of owning the future stack.

**Blockers:** None. The financial metrics cited as "high price" are misapplied to an asset acquisition rather than a going concern. The risks (migration, founder) are manageable execution challenges, not fundamental flaws that invalidate the strategy.

**Risks:** Integration friction, developer churn during migration, cultural clash with the founder. These are addressed by staging the integration and defining clear kill criteria (e.g., if developer adoption drops >20% in Year 1, pivot to hybrid model).

**Decision:** The proposal beats "do nothing" (death) and "license" (commoditization). It is the only option that preserves the company's independence and provides a modern technical foundation.

DECISION: GO
