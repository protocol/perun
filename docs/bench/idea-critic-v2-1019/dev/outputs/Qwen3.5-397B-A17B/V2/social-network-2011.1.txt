**Steelman:** Leveraging massive existing distribution (search, mail) to solve the cold-start problem that killed prior attempts, using unique video tech to carve a niche before scaling.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Misalignment:** Tying executive bonuses to *adoption* rather than *retention* or *satisfaction* guarantees aggressive, user-hostile forced integration. This validates the risk of annoying the core user base, potentially damaging the primary search business to chase a vanity metric for a social product.
2.  **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** Three previous failures suggest a fundamental mismatch between this company's identity/utility and social networking. Doubling down via "company-wide push" ignores the possibility that the asset (search) is incompatible with the goal (social), rather than under-leveraged.
3.  **Network Effect Reality:** "Friend-grouping circles" are a UX feature, not a network moat. If the user's graph is already established on the incumbent platform (hundreds of millions strong), a new empty network offers zero utility regardless of video quality. The incumbent can copy video features instantly.
4.  **Opportunity Cost:** A "company-wide push" diverts engineering talent from the core search monopoly, which faces its own existential threats, to fight a war where the company has a proven losing record.

**Better Way:**
Acquire an existing growing network or license the video tech to the incumbent. If building is mandatory, spin it out as a separate entity with its own P&L to prevent cross-contamination of the core brand and remove the incentive to force-integrate into mail/search.

**Kill Criteria:**
If forced integration causes a measurable drop in core search/emails usage >1% within 3 months, or if Day-30 retention of invited users is <20%, the project must pivot to a standalone tool or shut down.

**Verdict:**
The proposal fails because the incentive structure (bonuses on adoption) explicitly encourages the specific failure mode (forced integration annoying users) that threatens the company's crown jewel. The plan attacks the symptom (lack of users) with a method (forced integration) that ignores the disease (lack of organic social desire for this brand).

DECISION: NO-GO
