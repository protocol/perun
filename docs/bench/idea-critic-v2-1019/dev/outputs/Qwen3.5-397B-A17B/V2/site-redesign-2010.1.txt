The claim is that a full rewrite with altered voting mechanics and a forced migration will reverse slowing traffic. The steelman: a modernized interface favoring publishers attracts new revenue and users, offsetting community churn.

However, concrete blockers exist. First, the proposal explicitly risks alienating the "loyal community" that values current voting mechanics; changing the core loop without validation invites immediate revolt. Second, moving "all users at once" creates a single point of failure with "no rollback." If the new algorithm fails or bugs emerge, the site cannot revert, potentially causing irreversible traffic collapse while a rival grows. Third, the engineering risk of a "rewrite from scratch" delays value delivery and consumes resources that could fix specific traffic leaks in the existing stable system.

The "better way" is absent. A staged rollout (canary release) to a small segment would validate the new surfacing logic without endangering the whole user base. Keeping the old version as a fallback mitigates the no-rollback risk. Furthermore, favoring "publishers' content" contradicts the ethos of a community-voted news site unless data proves users want this shift; currently, it is an assumption that the community values publisher content over peer curation.

The pre-mortem is clear: the launch occurs, the community rejects the new voting weights as manipulative, traffic tanks, and the team cannot revert because the database schema or codebase was replaced entirely. The rival site absorbs the disgruntled users. The cost of delay (waiting to test) is lower than the cost of a catastrophic, unrollable launch.

This proposal fails the blocker test: it relies on unproven assumptions about user preference changes and eliminates the safety net (rollback) required for such a high-risk architectural change. The lack of a staged approach or A/B testing plan makes the "all at once" strategy indefensible given the stated risks.

DECISION: NO-GO
