Steelman: A modernized, minimalist carton design will signal premium quality and attract new demographics without alienating the existing loyal base, justified by a major advertising push to bridge the recognition gap.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Shelf Recognition Failure:** The proposal admits the old image is the "main shelf cue" for a "loyal and older" demographic. Removing this specific visual anchor creates an immediate, high-probability risk that the core revenue base will fail to locate the product, leading to instant sales collapse before advertising can retrain behavior.
2.  **Contradictory Risk Mitigation:** The plan relies on a "large advertising campaign" to solve a problem created by the design itself (loss of recognition). This assumes marketing spend can instantly override decades of conditioned visual shopping habits, an unproven assumption for an older demographic.
3.  **Cost vs. Value:** The redesign is "costly" and offers no stated improvement to the product (same recipe) or price efficiency. The only value prop is aesthetic, which directly conflicts with the primary functional requirement of shelf identification.

**Risks:**
*   Irreversible loss of market share to competitors if the re-education campaign fails.
*   Alienation of the specific "older" demographic cited as the current base.
*   Massive sunk cost in printing and ad spend with no guaranteed ROI.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "do nothing" comparison. The current design works (loyal base, established cue). The proposed design removes the single most critical functional element (the orange with straw) identified in the risk assessment itself. A "better way" would be incremental testing or retaining the core shelf cue while modernizing secondary elements. Launching "nationwide... at once" ignores the one-way door nature of a failed rebrand for a staple good. The evidence provided (loyal/older base, straw as main cue) directly contradicts the strategy of removing that cue. This is not a calculated risk; it is a deliberate removal of the product's primary identifier based on the hope that advertising can fix the resulting confusion.

DECISION: NO-GO
