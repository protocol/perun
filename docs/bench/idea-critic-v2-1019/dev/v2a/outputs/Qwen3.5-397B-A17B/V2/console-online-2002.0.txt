The claim is that a mandatory, paid, broadband-only online service will succeed in 2002 despite market norms of free, publisher-led play and low broadband penetration. The steelman: a unified, high-quality network creates a defensible ecosystem lock-in that justifies the subscription fee through superior user experience, provided the content library is strong enough to force hardware adoption.

However, the failure hypothesis is immediate and fatal under the stated constraints. The proposal requires broadband, yet in 2002, broadband adoption in target markets was well below 20%. By making the service mandatory for online play and restricting it to broadband, the company artificially caps its addressable market to a tiny fraction of console owners, likely ensuring the player base remains too small to sustain matchmaking or justify the operational costs. Furthermore, charging for a feature competitors offer for free, while the company lacks any prior experience in running hosting or support operations, introduces massive execution risk. The "shared identity" and "friends list" are valuable, but not if the network effect cannot ignite due to access barriers.

The "do nothing" or "publisher-led" alternative allows the ecosystem to grow organically as broadband adoption increases, avoiding the reputational damage of a paid wall that few can use. A cheaper, safer path is to offer the service as an optional premium layer while supporting dial-up or free tier access to build critical mass. The current plan bets the entire online strategy on unproven operational capacity and a market condition (ubiquitous broadband) that does not yet exist. The risk of building expensive infrastructure for a user base that cannot access it is a clear blocker. The proposal fails the "fit" test against current market reality and the "pre-mortem" reveals a high probability of operational collapse due to low uptake.

DECISION: NO-GO
