**Steelman:** A premium, connected juicer creates a recurring revenue moat by guaranteeing freshness and convenience, justifying a high upfront cost through a locked-in subscription model for affluent early adopters.

**Attack:** The unit economics and market fit are critically flawed.
1.  **Price Dissonance:** At $699, the device costs more than high-end blenders and commercial juicers that accept *any* produce. The "convenience tax" requires the subscription bags to be significantly cheaper than grocery store organic produce to justify the lock-in, yet proprietary packaging inherently increases COGS. If the bags are more expensive, the value proposition collapses.
2.  **Connectivity Friction:** Requiring Wi-Fi for a basic mechanical function (checking freshness) introduces a single point of failure. If the cloud service lags or the API changes, the hardware becomes a brick. This is a "one-way door" risk: hardware sold today depends on software support forever.
3.  **Market Size vs. Burn:** A niche appliance for a specific subset of wealthy users cannot support the burn rate of a "tens of millions" funded startup expecting venture-scale returns. The total addressable market for $699 countertop gadgets is too small to sustain the required growth trajectory.
4.  **Better Way:** Sell the press mechanism as an open system accepting standard bags or fresh produce, removing the internet dependency and lowering the price point to compete with existing kitchen appliances.

**Pre-mortem:** The company runs out of cash in 18 months because hardware margins are erased by custom packaging logistics, and churn spikes when users realize they can juice fresh grocery store fruit for 1/10th the cost. The "freshness check" feature is ignored by users as a gimmick.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed unless the subscription bag cost per ounce is proven to be lower than equivalent organic grocery produce *and* the device functions fully without internet connectivity. Neither condition is met in the proposal. The reliance on a proprietary ecosystem for a commodity product (juice) at a luxury price point is a fundamental strategic error.

DECISION: NO-GO
