**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to refine the product with early adopters before a general release, establishing a new everyday computing category despite initial privacy and utility concerns.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "everyday consumer product" positioning test. A $1,500 price point excludes the mass market by definition, creating a disconnect between the stated vision and the initial go-to-market strategy. More critically, the identified risks are not merely hurdles but fundamental blockers for the proposed "general public" phase. Privacy objections regarding a always-on camera in public spaces represent a societal rejection risk that an invitation-only program cannot solve; it may instead amplify backlash by creating a perceived "surveillance elite." Without a verified "killer app" that justifies the cost and social friction for non-enthusiasts, expanding to the general public risks brand damage and massive inventory write-downs. The battery life and lack of clear everyday use cases suggest the technology is not yet ready for the proposed timeline or positioning.

**Better Way:** Pivot the strategy to an enterprise-first model (industrial, medical, logistics) where the high cost is justifiable by productivity gains, privacy concerns are manageable within controlled environments, and utility is clear. Delay the consumer launch until battery life improves, costs decrease, and a definitive everyday use case emerges.

**Kill Criteria:** If pilot data from the invitation-only phase does not show >40% daily active usage beyond novelty testing within 6 months, or if public sentiment analysis shows net negative sentiment regarding privacy exceeding 20%, the general public launch must be cancelled.

**Verdict:** The plan to position this immediately as an "everyday consumer product" is flawed because the current constraints (price, battery, utility, privacy) directly contradict the requirements for mass-market adoption. Proceeding as proposed invites significant reputational and financial risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
