The claim is that opening company-run retail stores will reverse losses by enabling product demonstration and showcasing a new digital-music strategy, despite high fixed costs and industry failure rates.

**Steelman:** Only owned stores can control the customer experience required to sell a premium, complex ecosystem when third-party retailers actively undermine it with cheaper alternatives.

**Attack:** The company has 3–5% market share and recent losses. High-lease mall locations create massive fixed costs during a recession. The industry has a poor retail track record. If sales volume does not immediately spike to cover leases, the company faces accelerated bankruptcy. Channel conflict may cause resellers to drop the brand entirely before the new stores gain traction.

**Better Ways:**
1.  **Do Nothing/Status Quo:** Avoids immediate cash burn but cedes the narrative to competitors pushing cheaper machines.
2.  **Shop-in-Shop:** Negotiate dedicated, branded zones within existing retailer floors. Lower capex, retains retailer traffic, though less control than full stores.
3.  **Online/Direct:** Focus on direct shipping with loaner programs for demonstration. Avoids lease risk but lacks the "prime location" visibility.

**Fit:** The digital-music strategy requires a seamless hardware-software demo that third parties refuse to give. However, the financial fragility (losses, low share) makes a capital-intensive expansion into low-margin retail extremely dangerous.

**Pre-mortem:** The stores open, foot traffic is high due to mall location, but conversion is low because the price point is too high for impulse buys. Resellers, feeling threatened, stop promoting the brand. Fixed lease costs drain the remaining cash reserves within 18 months, forcing liquidation.

**Verdict:** This is a one-way door with catastrophic downside. The proposal ignores the company's inability to sustain losses while taking on high fixed costs. The "better way" of branded zones within existing stores achieves the demonstration goal without the existential lease risk. The current plan fails the viability test given the recessionary context and balance sheet weakness.

DECISION: NO-GO
