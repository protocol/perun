**Steelman:** Leverage existing compact mechanics to rapidly deliver a customizable, sporty-styled vehicle at a low entry price, targeting a specific demographic shift with aggressive marketing to validate unproven demand.

**Critique & Analysis:**
The primary blocker is the risk that sharing parts with a "dull" economy car undermines the "sporty" brand promise, causing the target audience to reject it as merely a cheap car in new clothing. However, the proposal mitigates this through specific styling cues (long hood, short deck) and a business model reliant on a long options list. This allows the base price to remain low (hooking the buyer) while letting customers pay to upgrade perceived quality and performance, effectively decoupling the final product's image from the donor car's reputation.

The "unproven demand" risk is significant but unavoidable if waiting for proof means missing the demographic window. The cost of delay here exceeds the cost of a failed launch because the baby boom cohort is a time-sensitive asset. The heavy ad spend is a necessary gamble to define the category before competitors react.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the emerging youth market to competitors; high risk of long-term irrelevance as the customer base ages.
2.  **Build a ground-up sports car:** Too expensive, misses the low-price constraint, and delays entry too long.
3.  **Wait for market data:** Guarantees missing the initial wave; by the time data exists, the opportunity is closed.

**Verdict:**
The proposal beats "do nothing" because the downside of sharing parts is manageable via styling and options, whereas the downside of missing the demographic shift is existential. The risks are real but constitute execution challenges, not fundamental flaws in the logic. The "long options list" is the critical mechanism that makes the economics work despite the low base price. There is no factual blocker preventing this from succeeding; the risks are inherent to any first-mover strategy in a new segment.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders or first-month sales do not cover 50% of the launch ad spend, halt further marketing expansion and re-evaluate the pricing structure immediately.

DECISION: GO
