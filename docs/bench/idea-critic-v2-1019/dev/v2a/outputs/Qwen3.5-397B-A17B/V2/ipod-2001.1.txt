**Steelman:** A premium, integrated device leverages a loyal niche to establish a beachhead in a fragmented market, betting that superior user experience and storage capacity will overcome high price and limited initial compatibility.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on three critical vectors. First, the addressable market is dangerously small; restricting usage to a minority computer platform while cheaper, universal competitors exist creates an immediate adoption ceiling. Second, the pricing strategy ($400) is unjustified when the primary differentiator (5GB storage) is a spec war, not an experience war, and the ecosystem (legal downloads) does not yet exist to lock users in. Third, the timeline is unrealistic for a company with zero consumer electronics supply chain experience; sourcing custom miniaturized components in under a year invites catastrophic delays or quality failures.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "wait" option is superior. Entering now requires subsidizing the ecosystem (software/store) before the hardware can gain traction. A cheaper, flash-based prototype or a software-only play would test the waters without the capital risk of hard drive sourcing and manufacturing.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume it is late 2002 and the product has failed. Why? The supply chain missed the holiday season due to unproven vendor relationships, the price remained too high for the mass market, and Windows-based competitors released 10GB drives at $250, rendering the 5GB premium obsolete. The limited computer base meant marketing spend yielded insufficient conversion.

**Fit:** This does not align with a "small market share" company's survival need for broad compatibility. It bets the company on a hardware gamble where it has no core competency. The risk of inventory write-downs and brand damage from a delayed or overpriced launch outweighs the potential first-mover advantage in a market that hasn't proven willingness to pay a premium.

**Blocker:** The combination of no supply chain experience, a hard deadline, and a restricted software ecosystem creates a high probability of missing the window with an uncompetitive product.

DECISION: NO-GO
