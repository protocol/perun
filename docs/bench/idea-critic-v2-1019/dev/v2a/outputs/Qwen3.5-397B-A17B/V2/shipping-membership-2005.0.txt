**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee converts variable shipping friction into fixed revenue, locking in loyalty and increasing purchase frequency to offset marginal logistics costs.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is unit economics: if heavy users double frequency while shipping costs remain static, the $79 cap guarantees losses on every loyal customer. However, the "do nothing" alternative cedes repeat buyers to competitors offering free shipping thresholds. A "free shipping over $X" alternative is safer but fails to secure upfront cash flow or behavioral lock-in.

**Pre-mortem & Door Type:**
Assume failure: The cohort of users who buy >15 times annually exceeds 20% of subscribers, causing shipping costs to consume 40% of gross margin, turning the program into a subsidy for the wealthiest customers while alienating non-members. This is a **one-way door**; once customers pay $79, revoking benefits triggers mass churn and reputational damage before the next renewal cycle.

**Evidence & Verification:**
The proposal admits economics are "unproven." Without a pilot cohort, historical data on elasticity, or a capped beta test, the claim that increased frequency offsets margin hits is an unsupported assumption. The risk of a structural margin collapse is a factual gap, not merely a risk. A plan committing to a company-wide, irreversible financial model without verified unit economics fails the blocker test. The "heavy buyer" risk is a specific, plausible failure mode where the core value proposition (convenience) directly drives the financial failure (cost overrun).

**Decision Logic:**
A blocker exists: the core financial mechanism relies on unverified assumptions about user behavior and cost elasticity. Launching broadly without a time-boxed, capped pilot to validate the break-even frequency is reckless. The proposal asks for immediate full-scale approval ("launch"), which ignores the necessity of proving the model works before making it irreversible.

**Kill Criteria Missing:**
There is no defined metric (e.g., "average orders per subscriber must exceed 12") or date to pivot/stop if losses exceed a specific threshold.

DECISION: NO-GO
