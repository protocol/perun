The proposal to open company-run retail stores carries extreme financial risk given the company's current losses, the low-margin nature of computer retail, and the high fixed costs of prime mall leases during a recession. The "better way" analysis suggests that relying on existing third-party retailers, perhaps with enhanced incentive programs or dedicated display units, avoids massive capital expenditure and inventory risk while maintaining market reach. The industry's poor retail track record further supports a "do nothing" or "minimal investment" stance over a full chain rollout.

However, the steelman of this claim is critical: if the third-party channel is actively hostile—pushing competitors due to better margins—and the new digital-music strategy requires a controlled, high-fidelity demonstration to succeed, then the existing channel is not just suboptimal but actively blocking the company's only viable pivot. In this specific context, the cost of the stores is not merely retail overhead but a necessary customer acquisition and brand-survival expense. The risk of channel conflict is irrelevant if the current channel is already failing the brand.

The strongest attack is the fixed-cost burden during a downturn; if sales do not immediately spike, the leases could bankrupt the firm faster than the current losses. Yet, if the digital-music strategy is the sole path to profitability and cannot be demonstrated elsewhere, the "do nothing" option leads to inevitable decline. The proposal survives only if the stores are viewed not as a retail profit center, but as a marketing funnel with a strict, immediate kill criterion: if same-store sales do not cover operating costs within 12 months, the expansion must halt immediately. Without this specific, high-stakes necessity for control, the plan is financial suicide. Assuming the digital strategy is the confirmed north star and cannot be executed via partners, the high risk is justified by the existential need to bypass a hostile channel.

DECISION: GO
