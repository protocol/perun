**Steelman:** Acquiring the category-defining mobile photo threat neutralizes a strategic existential risk, secures the mobile transition for $1B, and prevents a competitor from owning this user base.

**Attack & Options:**
The valuation is indefensible by traditional metrics (no revenue, tiny team). However, "do nothing" carries the risk of the target becoming the primary mobile interface, rendering the buyer's core product obsolete. Building a competitor internally is too slow given the target's network effects and momentum. The only viable alternative is acquiring a smaller player, but none possess comparable scale or cultural cache.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns perfectly with the north star of dominating mobile engagement. If we wait six months, the price likely doubles, or the target partners with a rival search/social giant. A failure scenario involves regulatory blockage or culture clash destroying the asset. Yet, the cost of delay (losing the mobile war) exceeds the cost of overpaying. The "no business model" risk is mitigated by the buyer's existing ad infrastructure, which can be applied post-acquisition.

**Evidence & Verification:**
The proposal cites tens of millions of users and rapid cross-platform growth as the primary asset. In social markets, user attention and data are the currency; revenue is a lagging indicator. The buyer's weak mobile position confirms the necessity of an external buy versus internal build. The $1B price tag represents a premium for speed and certainty of market control.

**Decision Logic:**
There is no factual blocker. The high price is a feature of the strategic necessity, not a bug. The risk of regulatory review is real but manageable compared to the certainty of competitive displacement if the deal fails. The cultural risk requires active management but does not invalidate the thesis. The "do nothing" option fails the survival test.

**Kill Criteria:**
Proceed only if regulatory filing can be initiated immediately and the target's founders remain committed for a minimum vesting period to preserve culture.

DECISION: GO
