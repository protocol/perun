**Steelman:** Radical simplification of pricing and store layout will restore margin integrity and attract a broader demographic by eliminating the "cherry-picker" dynamic that erodes brand value.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the specific behavioral conditioning of the existing customer base, who are explicitly described as "coupon-driven" and accustomed to discounts. Historically, most sales occur only when items are discounted. Removing this trigger without a transitional period or pilot test assumes customers will immediately accept higher effective prices for the sake of "simplicity," a hypothesis contradicted by the store's own sales data.

**Better Way:** A phased approach is superior. Implement everyday pricing in a controlled pilot of 10–15 stores while maintaining the legacy model elsewhere. This allows measurement of elasticity and churn before committing the entire chain. Alternatively, a hybrid model could gradually reduce coupon frequency while introducing everyday low prices on core items, conditioning customers slowly rather than shocking them.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** The strategy fits a "premium turnaround" vision but fails the "stage" reality of a mid-market chain with falling sales. In a pre-mortem scenario: Six months post-launch, same-store sales collapse by 20% because loyal customers defect to competitors offering coupons, while the new target demographic does not materialize quickly enough to offset the loss. The company runs out of cash before the brand perception shifts.

**Evidence & Verification:** The proposal admits "most sales have historically come from items sold at a discount." This is a direct blocker. Changing the primary revenue driver overnight without verifying demand elasticity via a pilot is financially reckless. The lack of a test run means the company is betting its entire liquidity on an unproven assumption about customer adaptability.

**Decision:** This is a one-way door with catastrophic downside. The plan relies on customers behaving contrary to their historical data. The absence of a pilot creates an unacceptable risk of immediate revenue collapse. The cheaper, safer alternative (pilot testing) must be executed first to validate the core hypothesis.

DECISION: NO-GO
