**Steelman:** Leveraging existing mass distribution and advertising scale will accelerate the acquired brand's growth beyond what its niche network can achieve, justifying the $1.7B premium through rapid volume expansion.

**Attack:** The proposal assumes the brand's value derives from product availability rather than its "cult" scarcity and independent distributor relationships. Moving a "quirky," loyalty-driven brand into mass-market channels and corporate advertising often destroys the very exclusivity that commands its premium price. The "small, quirky network" is likely a feature, not a bug; replacing it with supermarket focus risks alienating the core base before new mass consumers are secured. Furthermore, 1994 sees aggressive tea launches from soft-drink giants; entering this fray with a diluted brand identity invites a price war the acquired brand cannot win.

**Better Way:** Maintain the independent distributor network and distinct marketing voice as a separate operating unit to preserve brand equity while selectively testing supermarket placement. This captures growth without triggering the "sell-out" backlash that typically kills acquired cult brands.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If the brand loses its cult status post-acquisition, growth stalls while fixed costs rise, turning the $1.7B cash outlay into a value-destroying burden. The plan treats distribution as a generic utility, ignoring that for premium tea, the channel *is* the message.

**Evidence & Calculation:** The proposal cites "strong consumer loyalty" yet plans actions (mass marketing, distributor replacement) historically known to erode such loyalty. No evidence suggests this specific brand is immune to the "mainstream death spiral." The $1.7B price assumes continued premium positioning, which the proposed strategy directly undermines.

**Kill Criteria:** The plan fails if the integration strategy contradicts the source of the brand's current value (exclusivity/independence). Since the proposal explicitly intends to replace the independent network and apply mass-market tactics, it triggers a blocker: the strategy destroys the asset being purchased.

**Decision:** The plan relies on a contradiction: scaling a cult brand by removing its cult attributes. Without a phased approach preserving the original distribution and image, the acquisition is likely to destroy value.

DECISION: NO-GO
