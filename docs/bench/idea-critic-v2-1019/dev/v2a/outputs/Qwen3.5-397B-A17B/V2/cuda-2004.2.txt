**Steelman:** Embedding general-purpose compute capability in every consumer GPU creates a massive, low-cost parallel supercomputer distributed to millions, seeding a future market where scientific and AI workloads migrate from CPUs to GPUs, securing long-term dominance beyond gaming.

**Attack:** The proposal demands raising unit costs and diverting critical engineering resources from a fierce, existential rivalry in the core gaming market to chase a non-existent revenue stream. In 2004, no software ecosystem exists to utilize these features, and academic experimentation does not prove commercial viability. The "niche" may remain niche forever, leaving the company with expensive chips that underperform in gaming against a rival focused solely on graphics. The risk is not just wasted R&D, but losing the core business that funds the entire operation.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain cost leadership and performance in gaming; wait for clear market demand before adding silicon area.
2.  **High-End Only:** Add programmable features only to expensive workstation cards where unit cost impact is negligible and professional users might pay a premium.
3.  **Software-First:** Invest in compilers and tools for existing hardware architectures before committing to permanent silicon changes.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If the company loses the gaming race due to distraction, it goes bankrupt before the computing market matures. If the "killer app" for GPU computing never arrives, the added silicon area is a permanent tax on every unit sold, reducing margins without return. This is a one-way door: once silicon is taped out, the cost is sunk.

**Verdict:** The proposal fails the blocker test. It bets the company's core profitability on a hypothetical future market while actively weakening its position in the current, vital market. The cost is immediate and certain; the benefit is speculative and distant. A "go-if" condition cannot be met now because the market validation is absent. The prudent path is the "High-End Only" alternative to validate demand without jeopardizing mass-market competitiveness.

DECISION: NO-GO
