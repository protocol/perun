**Steelman:** Leverage vertical integration of hardware and a newly acquired OS to disrupt a complacent market leader, using aggressive marketing to bridge the initial app gap while developer outreach scales the ecosystem.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "chicken-and-egg" test. In 2011, tablet utility is defined entirely by app availability. A "small catalog" is not a temporary hurdle; it is a fatal defect for a premium-priced device competing against a dominant incumbent with thousands of apps. Shoppers perceive "unproven" software as a liability, not an opportunity. Heavy advertising for a product with low utility accelerates reputation damage rather than sales. Leadership transition compounds execution risk during the critical launch window. The "do nothing" or "wait" alternative preserves capital until the OS matures or is abandoned, avoiding a high-cost failure.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-launch, the device is discounted heavily and discontinued. Developers ignored the outreach because the installed base remained negligible due to poor initial sales driven by the app gap. The brand is associated with a failed experiment.

**Evidence & Fit:** The facts state the app catalog is a "small fraction" of the leader's and the OS has "almost no market share." These are not risks; they are current states that directly contradict the requirement for a competitive premium tablet. No evidence suggests the market will accept a premium price for reduced functionality.

**Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks a mechanism to instantly solve the app deficit. Without a killer app or backward compatibility (not mentioned), the value proposition is negative. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed premium launch.

**Verdict:** The plan attempts to buy market share with advertising while selling an inferior core product. This violates basic product-market fit principles. The blocker is the insurmountable app gap at a premium price point.

DECISION: NO-GO
