The claim is that rapid, large-scale international expansion via lease acquisition and new systems will succeed despite operational inexperience and entrenched competition. The steelman: leveraging existing lease inventory allows immediate scale to deter competitors, while the price gap offers a clear value proposition to win share quickly.

However, the "better way" analysis reveals fatal flaws. The proposal ignores the "do nothing" or "staged entry" alternatives. Entering with 130–150 stores simultaneously creates a single point of failure: if the new supply chain or inventory system falters (a high probability given they are unproven and cross-border), the entire network collapses before corrections can be made. A staged approach (5–10 stores) would validate logistics and labeling requirements at a fraction of the risk.

The pre-mortem is clear: assume failure in 18 months. The cause is not lack of demand, but operational strangulation. The retailer lacks experience in cross-border logistics and bilingual regulation. Scaling this learning curve across 150 locations instantly guarantees systemic errors in stock levels and compliance, leading to empty shelves or legal fines. The entrenched competitor can easily absorb a price war while the newcomer bleeds cash on fixed lease costs and broken supply lines.

The evidence provided highlights "higher prices on some goods" locally, but this does not guarantee the retailer's specific assortment matches local tastes or regulatory standards. The risk of "price perception" is secondary to the risk of "product availability." Without a proven supply chain, the core value proposition (low price) cannot be delivered consistently.

This is a one-way door with massive capital commitment. The cost of delay (waiting to test systems) is negligible compared to the cost of a failed nationwide launch. The proposal fails the blocker test: the plan relies on unproven systems operating at maximum scale from day one, a condition that historically leads to catastrophic retail failures. The lack of a pilot phase is a definitive blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
