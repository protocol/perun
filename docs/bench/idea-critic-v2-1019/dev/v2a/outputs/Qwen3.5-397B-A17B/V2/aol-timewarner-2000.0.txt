The proposal relies on the premise that dial-up dominance justifies a majority stake in a combined entity during a peak valuation bubble. The steelman argues that vertical integration of premier content with the largest digital distribution channel creates an unassailable monopoly, accelerating broadband rollout via cable infrastructure while monetizing legacy media through new digital channels.

However, the "better way" analysis reveals fatal flaws. The internet provider's core asset (20M dial-up users) is a depreciating liability as broadband adoption accelerates; paying a premium for a melting ice cube using overvalued stock destroys capital. The "do nothing" alternative preserves the media conglomerate's cash and independence without inheriting the internet company's impending revenue collapse. Furthermore, the assumption that cable partners will grant guaranteed network access ignores existing competitive tensions and regulatory hurdles that could stall the broadband synergy entirely.

The pre-mortem is clear: if broadband adoption outpaces the migration of dial-up users (a near certainty given infrastructure trends), the combined company faces massive goodwill write-downs, culture clashes paralyze operations, and the stock premium evaporates. The kill criterion is the internet provider's valuation relative to its tangible cash flow; currently, it is detached from reality. A cheaper, safer alternative is a strategic content licensing deal or a minority investment, avoiding a full merger that places a volatile, declining business in the driver's seat.

The strongest attack survived is the theoretical synergy of content plus distribution. Yet, this fails because the distribution channel (dial-up) is becoming obsolete, and the proposed high-speed migration depends on third-party cooperation not secured in the deal terms. The risk is not just execution difficulty; it is fundamental asset mispricing. The internet company's shareholders are effectively cashing out at the top using inflated currency to buy hard assets, transferring wealth from the media conglomerate's long-term holders to short-term internet speculators. This fails the "fit" test for sustainable value creation.

DECISION: NO-GO
