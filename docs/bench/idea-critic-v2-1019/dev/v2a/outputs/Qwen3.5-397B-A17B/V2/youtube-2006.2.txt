**Steelman:** Acquire the dominant video platform to monopolize user attention and data, betting that scale will solve monetization and that legal risks can be managed or outlasted.

**Attack:**
The proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts provided:
1.  **Financial Blocker:** The target has "no meaningful revenue" against a $1.65 billion price tag. There is no path to ROI based on current economics; the bet relies entirely on an unproven future ad model.
2.  **Legal Blocker:** "Media companies have threatened lawsuits" over "large volume" of copyrighted content. This is not a risk; it is an active, existential liability that could result in statutory damages exceeding the purchase price or an injunction shutting down the core service.
3.  **Operational Blocker:** "High bandwidth bills" with no revenue means every additional user increases losses. The buyer already runs a smaller service; absorbing this loss leader threatens the parent company's profitability without a guaranteed offset.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid immediate massive cash burn and litigation exposure. Continue building the existing smaller service organically.
*   **Wait:** Let the copyright lawsuits clarify the legal landscape. If the site survives, acquire later at a distressed value; if it dies, the risk was avoided.
*   **License Content:** Partner with media companies legally rather than buying a platform built on infringement.

**Pre-mortem:**
Assume it is 2008. The lawsuits have resulted in massive judgments or forced the removal of all popular content, destroying user engagement. The bandwidth costs drained the parent company's stock value, and the ad model failed to materialize at the scale needed to cover operating losses. The integration distracted engineering from the core search business.

**Fit:**
While this moves the "north star" of video dominance, it does so by assuming away the primary constraints (law and economics) rather than solving them. A strategy requiring the violation of copyright law to function is not a strategy; it is a gamble with undefined downside.

**Conclusion:**
The combination of zero revenue, massive operating costs, and active existential legal threats constitutes a hard blocker. The claim that scale will solve these issues is an assumption, not evidence.

DECISION: NO-GO
