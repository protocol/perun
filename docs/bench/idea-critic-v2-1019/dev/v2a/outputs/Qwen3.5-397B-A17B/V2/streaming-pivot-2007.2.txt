The claim is that adding a free, inferior streaming service in 2007 protects a profitable DVD monopoly against future tech giants. The steelman: this is a low-cost defensive probe to retain subscribers and gather data before the market shifts, leveraging existing cash flow to fund an inevitable transition.

However, the skeptic hat reveals fatal flaws in the 2007 context. Broadband penetration is insufficient for mass adoption, making the addressable market tiny while fixed licensing costs remain high. The catalogue is weak (older titles, poor quality), offering no differentiation against free piracy or emerging competitors. Most critically, offering this at "no extra charge" immediately devalues the core product without a clear migration path, accelerating cannibalization of the high-margin DVD revenue stream that funds the entire operation. There is no evidence that a small, low-quality library reduces churn among DVD-centric users; it may instead signal brand decline.

The "better way" analysis suggests a staged approach: launch streaming as a paid premium tier or a limited beta to validate willingness-to-pay and technical feasibility before exposing the entire subscriber base to a diluted value proposition. Doing nothing preserves the cash cow until the technology and content library mature. The cost of delay is minimal given the current infrastructure limits; the cost of premature full-scale rollout is the potential erosion of the primary business model before the new one is viable.

The pre-mortem shows failure occurring when licensing debts mount against stagnant DVD revenues, forcing a distress sale before the streaming unit achieves scale. This is a one-way door that locks in high fixed costs with uncertain returns. The proposal lacks a kill criterion for licensing spend relative to DVD attrition rates. Without a paid tier to test true demand or a strategy to secure premium content, this move bets the company on a hypothesis that the market cannot yet support. The risks outweigh the defensive benefits.

DECISION: NO-GO
