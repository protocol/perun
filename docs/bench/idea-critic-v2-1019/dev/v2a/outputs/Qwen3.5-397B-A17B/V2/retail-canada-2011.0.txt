**Steelman:** Rapidly acquiring 130–150 sites leverages first-mover advantage against a complacent incumbent, using scale to force supply chain maturity immediately rather than incrementally.

**Attack:** The proposal conflates "opportunity" with "readiness." The core failure mode is operational collapse, not market rejection. Building a cross-border supply chain, inventory system, and regulatory compliance framework simultaneously while opening ~70 stores per year creates a single point of catastrophic failure. If the new inventory system glitches (highly probable in unproven cross-border setups), 150 stores go dark simultaneously, burning cash on leases without revenue. The "learn as we go" approach is incompatible with fixed lease liabilities across 150 locations.

**Better Way:** A "beachhead" strategy: secure 5–10 sites to validate the supply chain, labeling, and inventory system. Prove unit economics and logistical flow before signing 140 more leases. This preserves capital and allows system iteration without existential risk.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months in, the inventory system fails to handle bilingual labeling or cross-border tariffs, causing stockouts in 80% of stores. Lease obligations drain domestic profits; the brand reputation is ruined before it launches.

**Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits the supply chain and systems are "new" and "unproven." Scaling unproven infrastructure to 150 nodes violates basic operational hygiene. The risk of "price perception" is secondary to the certainty of logistical friction.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed unless the inventory system has processed live cross-border transactions for 90 days with <1% error rate. This condition is not met.

**Decision:** The plan bets the company on a "big bang" launch of unproven systems. The cost of delay (waiting to validate systems) is negligible compared to the cost of failure (150 leased empties). The lack of a proven pilot makes this a one-way door to insolvency.

DECISION: NO-GO
