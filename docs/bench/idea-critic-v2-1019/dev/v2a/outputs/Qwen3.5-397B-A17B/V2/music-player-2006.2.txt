The proposal attempts to dislodge a dominant incumbent by attacking its greatest strengths: ecosystem lock-in and industry relationships. The "steelman" argument is that superior hardware (larger screen) and a seamless wireless sharing feature will convince users to abandon their existing libraries and devices. However, this logic collapses under scrutiny.

First, dropping the copy-protection standard that the company previously championed creates an immediate trust deficit with record labels. If partners restricted sharing due to piracy fears, removing those restrictions without explicit, pre-secured agreements guarantees legal blockades, not adoption. The proposal assumes labels will cooperate despite the company burning that bridge; this is a fatal blocker.

Second, the "late to market" disadvantage is compounded by the lack of a price advantage or exclusive content. In a network-effect business, a "better" product fails if it cannot access the content library users already own. Wireless sharing is a novelty, not a replacement for a catalog. Without a mechanism to migrate user libraries or a cost incentive severe enough to break brand loyalty, the marketing budget will simply educate users on why the incumbent is safer.

The "do nothing" or "partner" alternative is superior: license the software to existing hardware makers or focus on the backend services where margins are higher, rather than funding a capital-intensive hardware war with no clear path to content acquisition. The risk of alienating labels while failing to attract users represents a total loss of the marketing spend and reputational capital.

The strongest attack is the contradiction between the company's history of promoting copy-protection and this proposal's sudden abandonment of it. This signals instability to content owners, ensuring they will withhold licenses. Without licenses, the store fails; without the store, the device is a toy. The plan relies on assumptions about label cooperation that directly contradict the stated risks.

DECISION: NO-GO
