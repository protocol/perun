The claim rests on the assumption that "premium" short-form video requires high production budgets and A-list talent to overcome subscription fatigue. This contradicts the nature of mobile consumption, where brevity and native vertical formatting often outweigh cinematic production value. The proposal attempts to solve a content problem with a technology feature (orientation adaptation) that is now a commodity standard, not a differentiator.

**Better Ways:** The "do nothing" or "wait" option is superior given the unproven demand for paid short-form content. A cheaper alternative exists: licensing existing premium short-form libraries or producing mid-budget content specifically for mobile natives, rather than burning capital on film-grade productions for 5-minute episodes. The current plan ignores the success of ad-supported models (AVOD) which dominate this specific format, suggesting a revenue model mismatch.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the service launches and fails within 18 months. The cause is likely the inability to retain subscribers against free, abundant alternatives despite high content costs, leading to an unsustainable burn rate. The "one-way door" here is the massive upfront capital commitment ($1B+) before validating unit economics. The cost of delay is low; the market for short-form is growing, but the willingness to *pay* for it remains unproven. Waiting for clearer signals on subscription conversion for short-form reduces the risk of catastrophic capital loss.

**Blocker:** The proposal cites "subscription fatigue" as a risk yet proposes a premium subscription model as the primary solution without evidence that users will pay for this specific format over free options. The core mechanism (high budget + A-list talent) does not logically resolve the stated friction (fatigue/price sensitivity); it exacerbates it by increasing the break-even point. The numbers do not work: high fixed costs + unproven willingness to pay = negative ROI probability.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed until a pilot demonstrates a conversion rate from free-to-paid for short-form premium content that supports the projected $1B valuation. Without this data, the plan is a bet on hope, not evidence.
