Steelman: Transform an invisible component into a consumer demand driver by subsidizing partner ads, forcing retailers to stock computers with this specific chip.

**Skeptic:** The premise that end-users care about internal components contradicts the stated fact that "buyers do not know which chip is in their computer." If awareness is zero, asking for a brand by name is impossible without massive, sustained education first. The risk of partner resentment is high; computer makers may view this as brand dilution or interference, potentially leading to sabotage of the campaign or reduced effort in selling the machines themselves.

**Better Way:** Before committing a "large share of the marketing budget," test the hypothesis with a single major partner in one region. Alternatively, invest in technical certification seals for retailers rather than consumer-facing ads, leveraging sales staff as the education vector instead of expensive mass media. "Do nothing" preserves capital but cedes ground to clone makers using similar part numbers to confuse buyers.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a two-way door only if contracts allow immediate termination. However, brand damage from a failed, confusing campaign is hard to reverse. The cost of delay is high if clone makers successfully cement confusion in the market now.

**Evidence & Logic:** The proposal relies on changing consumer behavior from "ignorant" to "demanding." No evidence is provided that consumers will bridge this gap quickly enough to justify the immediate large spend. The risk that benefits "may not show in sales" is a fatal flaw for a campaign consuming a large budget portion; without a guaranteed leading metric tied to sales lift within a short window, the financial exposure is unjustified. The "unprecedented" nature of the risk suggests a lack of data to model ROI.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It commits significant resources based on an unproven behavioral shift (consumers caring about components) while ignoring the high probability of channel conflict. A cheaper, staged pilot is required to validate demand before a full launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
