The proposal to split the DVD and streaming services into separate brands, accounts, and billing systems fails the primary test of strategic fit and customer value. While the rationale cites different management needs for a declining DVD business versus a growth-oriented streaming future, the execution plan ignores the immediate context: a recent 60% price increase for the combined service.

**Steelman:** Separating the brands allows distinct strategic focus and prevents the declining DVD logistics from dragging down the streaming valuation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Compounded Friction:** Customers just absorbed a massive price hike. Forcing them now into two separate logins, two bills, and broken recommendation engines creates a "double penalty" perception. This is not just confusion; it is a degradation of the core value proposition (convenience) immediately after a cost increase.
2.  **Loss of Synergy:** The proposal explicitly kills cross-service recommendations. In 2011, streaming libraries were thin; DVDs were deep. Severing this link removes a key retention lever for early streaming adopters who relied on the DVD catalog to fill gaps.
3.  **Better Way:** Maintain a single brand and account. Manage the divisions internally if necessary, but present a unified face to the customer. If the DVD business must be wound down, do so via gradual sunsetting or distinct "add-on" tiers, not a hard brand fracture that alienates the remaining hybrid user base.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume the split happens. Result: Mass churn due to perceived greed and complexity. Competitors capitalize on the simplicity of a single subscription. The "separate management" goal is achieved at the cost of the customer relationship.

The risks identified (confusion, lost recommendations, backlash) are not merely potential; they are direct consequences of the proposed architecture given the recent pricing trauma. The proposal solves an internal organizational problem by creating an external customer experience disaster. It fails to beat "do nothing" or "internal restructuring only" because it actively destroys user value.

DECISION: NO-GO
