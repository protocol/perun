The proposal describes a "blitzscale" strategy reliant on perpetual capital availability and negative unit economics to buy market share. The steelman is that speed creates an unassailable monopoly before competitors can react, allowing future price hikes to recoup losses.

However, the attack reveals fatal structural flaws. First, selling products below cost (negative gross margin) while incurring high shipping costs on heavy items means every additional sale increases total losses. This is not an investment; it is active value destruction per unit. Second, the plan requires simultaneous execution of four capital-intensive actions (IPO, TV ad buy, acquisition, warehouse construction) during a period where "capital markets could close." If the IPO fails or windows shut due to the very risks cited (negative margins, high burn), the company cannot fund the ads, the acquisition, or the warehouses, leaving it with inflated costs and no cash. Third, acquiring a competitor while both parties bleed cash consolidates losses rather than solving the unit economics problem.

The "better way" analysis shows that "doing nothing" regarding the IPO and acquisition, and instead fixing unit economics to approach profitability on a subset of goods, preserves cash and extends runway. The proposed path bets the entire company on a favorable public market window to fund a strategy that loses money on every transaction. The pre-mortem is clear: markets tighten, the IPO is withdrawn, cash runs out within months due to accelerated burn from ads and below-cost pricing, and the company enters insolvency with expensive, underutilized warehouses.

The strongest attack survived is the claim that "growth justifies losses." This fails because the losses are structural (negative margin + high shipping), not temporary setup costs. No volume of sales fixes a model where revenue < cost of goods + shipping. The kill criterion is immediate: if the core unit economics are negative, scaling amplifies failure. The proposal lacks any path to positive contribution margin.

DECISION: NO-GO
