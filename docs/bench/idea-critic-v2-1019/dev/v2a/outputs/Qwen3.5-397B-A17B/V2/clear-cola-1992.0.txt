The steelman: A clear cola can capture a distinct "purity" segment and reverse growth stagnation without eroding the flagship's core identity.

However, the claim fails the blocker test. The proposal relies on test market data that explicitly "can overstate demand," particularly for novelty products where initial trial is high but repeat purchase is low. The risks identified within the proposal itself constitute the blocker: if shoppers psychologically link cola flavor to dark color, the product fundamentally contradicts consumer expectations, leading to mass rejection after the first sip. Furthermore, a "large marketing campaign" for a product with unproven retention creates a high probability of expensive cannibalization of the flagship brand with no net gain in total volume.

The "better way" is to reject the nationwide launch. The data does not support scaling; it supports skepticism. A cheaper, safer alternative is to limit the product to specific regional test beds with strict repeat-purchase metrics before committing national ad spend. Alternatively, position it as a limited-time novelty to harvest initial sales without the overhead of a permanent brand extension.

The pre-mortem is clear: six months post-launch, sales have collapsed because the novelty wore off and the taste-color mismatch prevented habit formation. The company is left with excess inventory, wasted ad spend, and a confused brand image. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a failed national rollout. Since the proposal admits the test markets may be inflated and the core consumer psychology is unverified, proceeding nationwide is not a calculated bet; it is a gamble with the company's second-largest asset.

The strongest attack survived is the fear of cannibalization, but that is irrelevant if the product fails on its own merits due to the flavor-color cognitive dissonance. The evidence provided (positive test response) is explicitly flagged as unreliable within the text. Therefore, the plan fails as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
