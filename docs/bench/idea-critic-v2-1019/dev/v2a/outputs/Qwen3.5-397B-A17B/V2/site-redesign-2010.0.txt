The claim is that a full rewrite with altered voting mechanics and a forced migration will reverse slowing traffic. The steelman: a modernized interface favoring publishers will attract new volume outweighing community churn.

However, three fatal blockers exist under the stated constraints:

1.  **Community Alienation:** The core value proposition is the existing voting mechanic. Changing this to favor publishers directly attacks the loyal community's incentive to participate. In 2010, network effects are fragile; alienating the base to chase publishers risks a "ghost town" scenario where neither group stays.
2.  **Irreversibility:** Moving all users at once to a rewritten platform eliminates the ability to rollback. If the new mechanics fail or the rewrite contains critical bugs, the site fails completely with no safety net. This is a one-way door with catastrophic downside.
3.  **Engineering Distraction:** A full rewrite consumes all engineering capacity. While distracted, the rival growing site can capture the dissatisfied community. The cost of delay in fixing the core product while rewriting is unquantified but likely high.

The proposal offers no staged rollout, no A/B testing of the new voting logic, and no fallback plan. It bets the entire company on an unproven hypothesis that publishers matter more than the existing community, without evidence. The risk of total failure outweighs the potential gain of "new volume," especially since traffic is only "slowing," not collapsing.

A better approach would be iterative changes to the current platform or a beta opt-in for the new design. As proposed, the plan lacks kill criteria, ignores the primary asset (the community), and introduces existential technical risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
