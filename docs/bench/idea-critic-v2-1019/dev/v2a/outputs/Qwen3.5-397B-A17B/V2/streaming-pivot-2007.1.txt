The claim is that a profitable DVD leader should immediately add free streaming despite a weak catalogue, poor quality, high licensing costs, and imminent giant competitors. The steelman: this is a defensive necessity to retain subscribers who will eventually demand digital access, using the current cash cow to fund the transition before it is too late.

However, the facts provided suggest a fatal mismatch. The streaming offering is inferior (small, old catalogue; poor quality) while the core business is superior (profitable, 7M subscribers). Launching a bad digital product now invites direct comparison with future entrants who will have better tech and content, potentially accelerating churn rather than preventing it. Furthermore, paying high licensing fees for a subpar product destroys margin without guaranteeing retention. The risk of cannibalizing a highly profitable unit with a loss-leading, low-value alternative is extreme when the market infrastructure (broadband) is not yet universal.

The "do nothing" alternative preserves current profits while allowing competitors to bear the early costs of educating the market and fixing quality issues. A "wait and see" approach allows the company to enter later with better technology and lower licensing costs once the market matures, using their existing subscriber base as a ready-made launchpad. Entering now with a weak product signals desperation and wastes capital.

The strongest attack is that launching a deficient product invites customers to leave for better upcoming options, whereas waiting leverages their current satisfaction with DVDs. There is no evidence that a small, old catalogue prevents churn in 2007; conversely, it may highlight the service's limitations. Without a clear path to quality or a unique content advantage, this move sacrifices certain profit for uncertain, low-quality growth. The kill criterion is missing: there is no metric defined where this specific weak launch succeeds where a delayed launch would fail.

DECISION: NO-GO
