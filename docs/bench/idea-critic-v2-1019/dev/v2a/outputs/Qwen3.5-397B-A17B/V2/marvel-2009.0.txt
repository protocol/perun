**Steelman:** Acquiring the publisher instantly solves the boy-demographic gap by owning a deep library of male-skewing characters, leveraging the conglomerate's global distribution to scale the target's nascent film division beyond its current limited slate.

**Attack:** The $4 billion price tag assumes the target's new film strategy will succeed, yet their slate is short, unproven, and their biggest assets are locked in long-term deals with rivals. Paying a premium for future film potential while being unable to monetize the most valuable existing characters immediately creates a massive value gap. The "cyclical nature" risk is compounded by paying cash-plus-stock at a market peak for an asset whose core revenue drivers (licensing) are encumbered and whose growth engine (films) lacks a track record.

**Better Way:** Instead of a $4 billion outright buy, negotiate a phased acquisition or a joint venture specifically for film production. This allows the conglomerate to test the film strategy without assuming full balance-sheet risk for unproven slates and licensed-away characters. Alternatively, wait for the long-term licensing deals to expire or for the target's film slate to prove consistency, likely lowering the entry multiple.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If the first two self-produced films under new ownership flop, the conglomerate is stuck with $4 billion of debt/equity dilution and a library it cannot fully exploit due to rival licenses. The "creative-control disputes" risk suggests the target's leadership may resist the conglomerate's family-friendly sanitization, leading to brand dilution or stalled production.

**Kill Criteria:** The deal fails if the cost per usable, unencumbered character exceeds internal development costs, or if the projected ROI relies entirely on film success rates historically unseen by the target.

**Verdict:** The proposal asks to pay a premium for control of assets that are currently legally restricted (licensed to rivals) and operationally unproven (short film slate). The financial exposure outweighs the strategic benefit given the immediate inability to monetize the crown jewels.

DECISION: NO-GO
