Steelman: Transform an invisible component into a consumer demand driver by subsidizing partner ads, forcing buyers to pull the product through the channel.

**Skeptic**: The premise that end-users care about internal components is unproven; historically, B2B ingredients fail in B2C markets. If consumers ignore the logo, the entire marketing budget is wasted with zero sales lift. The risk of partner resentment is high if compliance policing feels like overreach.

**Better-way**: Alternatives include direct technical partnerships with OEMs for "Powered By" badges without consumer ad subsidies, or focusing solely on trade marketing to influence buyer specifications. However, these maintain the status quo where clone makers compete purely on price. Only a consumer pull strategy breaks the commodity trap.

**Fit & Pre-mortem**: This aligns with the goal of escaping price wars. If this fails, it will be because the message was too technical or the logo placement was inconsistent. It is a two-way door; the campaign can be paused if quarterly brand tracking shows no lift.

**Evidence & Math**: The proposal relies on the fact that part numbers are confusing. Subsidizing ads shifts the cost burden but gains control over messaging. The "large share" of the budget is the necessary cost of entry to create a new category.

**Kill Criteria**:
1.  **Blocker**: None found. The risk is execution, not concept validity.
2.  **Metric**: Track "specified by name" requests in retail channels within 6 months.
3.  **Stop Condition**: If consumer awareness remains below 20% after one year despite full funding, pivot to trade-only incentives.

**Decision**: The potential to own the category outweighs the unprecedented nature of the tactic. The alternative is slow erosion by clones. The subsidy is not a cost but an investment in pricing power.

DECISION: GO
