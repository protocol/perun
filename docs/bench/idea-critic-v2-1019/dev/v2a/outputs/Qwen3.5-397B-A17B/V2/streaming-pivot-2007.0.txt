**Steelman:** Leveraging the existing seven-million-subscriber base to seed a streaming ecosystem now, despite technical and content limitations, is the only viable defense against inevitable market displacement by larger technology entrants.

**Attack:** The proposal admits the streaming catalogue is small, quality is poor, and broadband penetration is low in 2007. Offering this "no extra charge" risks devaluing the core brand without immediate revenue upside. Furthermore, high licensing costs for a limited library threaten margins, while cannibalization of the highly profitable DVD rental unit could accelerate revenue decline before the streaming model matures. Competing against deep-pocketed tech giants with superior infrastructure seems strategically suicidal for a mail-order company.

**Better Way:** Maintain the DVD monopoly while licensing content to emerging streamers as a pure profit center, avoiding capital expenditure on unproven delivery infrastructure. Alternatively, delay entry until broadband saturation and video compression technology improve, preserving cash.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 2010. The cause: The company burned cash on licensing fees for unwanted old movies, alienated DVD customers with a confused value proposition, and was crushed by tech giants who treated streaming as a loss-leader for broader ecosystems. The "free" add-on diluted the premium perception of the core service without securing a foothold in the future market.

**Fit & Evidence:** The strategic risk of *not* entering is existential; if streaming becomes the standard, the DVD business collapses entirely regardless of current profitability. While the "do nothing" approach protects short-term margins, it ignores the one-way door of technological shifts. The "better way" of licensing content cedes control of the customer relationship to competitors. The proposed "no extra charge" model, though risky, is the only path to retaining subscriber data and habit formation in the new medium.

**Decision:** The blocker test reveals no factual contradiction; the risks are real but represent the cost of survival rather than execution errors. The alternative (doing nothing) guarantees obsolescence. The plan must proceed as a capped, time-boxed experiment to learn, not to profit immediately.

DECISION: GO
