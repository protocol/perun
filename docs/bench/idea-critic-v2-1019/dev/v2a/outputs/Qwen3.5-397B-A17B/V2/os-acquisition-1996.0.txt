The claim is that acquiring a small, unproven OS vendor for a premium price will rescue a failing giant. The steelman: this is the only available asset with modern object-oriented architecture compatible with the maker's needs, and the founder's return is the sole mechanism to ensure technical fidelity, making the high price a necessary cost of survival rather than a valuation of current revenue.

Alternatives considered: licensing another vendor's system reduces integration control and long-term differentiation; continuing internal development has already failed twice, suggesting structural incapacity; doing nothing guarantees continued market share erosion and eventual irrelevance. The proposal beats "do nothing" because the status quo is terminal.

However, specific blockers exist. The target company has sold few units, meaning the technology is unproven at scale. Migrating the existing developer ecosystem to a new core presents a massive execution risk that could alienate the remaining user base. The founder's "difficult history" introduces severe cultural and operational friction that could derail the integration before technical benefits are realized. The price, high against small revenue, assumes future success that is not guaranteed by the asset's current state.

The strongest attack is the integration risk: buying the technology does not guarantee the ability to migrate the legacy ecosystem or successfully reintegrate the founder. If the migration fails or the founder clashes with leadership, the company burns hundreds of millions with no viable OS, accelerating collapse. The proposal lacks a verified mechanism to mitigate the founder's historical conflicts or a concrete plan for the developer migration beyond the acquisition itself. Without a guaranteed path to resolve the founder's friction and a proven migration strategy, the bet relies entirely on hope rather than verified operational capacity. The risk of total integration failure outweighs the potential upside given the lack of proven scale for the target OS.

DECISION: NO-GO
