The claim is that a closed, curated native app store will succeed despite prior executive skepticism of native apps and existing open rival ecosystems. The steelman: a single controlled store balances developer reach with product integrity, turning security and stability from risks into selling points while capturing revenue via a 30% cut.

Skeptic check: The CEO's public stance against native apps contradicts this pivot; if the core leadership believes web apps are sufficient, internal execution may lack conviction or resources. Furthermore, carrier objections could block distribution entirely if they perceive bypassed services as an existential threat. The review workload for "every app" creates a bottleneck that could stifle the ecosystem at launch, making the platform appear barren compared to open rivals.

Better-way analysis: The "do nothing" option (stick to web apps) avoids security risks and carrier conflict but likely cedes the emerging platform war to rivals. An open distribution model matches competitors but invites the very instability and security flaws the company fears. A hybrid approach (web-first with limited native APIs) might reduce review load but dilutes the developer value proposition. The proposed model beats "do nothing" only if the curation creates a quality differential users pay for; it beats open distribution only if stability is the primary buyer concern.

Pre-mortem: The store launches with too few high-quality apps due to slow review times, causing developers to abandon the platform for open rivals. Carriers demand removal of key features, fracturing the user experience. The 30% cut is deemed too high compared to emerging alternatives, driving price-sensitive developers away.

Kill criteria: If the review process cannot scale to approve at least 500 quality apps within three months of opening, the ecosystem fails to reach critical mass. If carriers successfully block over-the-air installation or key APIs, the value proposition collapses.

The strongest attack is the contradiction between the CEO's stated philosophy and this capital-intensive pivot; without a demonstrated shift in belief, the strategy lacks the necessary internal alignment to overcome carrier and execution hurdles. However, the potential upside of owning the ecosystem outweighs the risk of sticking to web apps, provided the review bottleneck is solved immediately.

DECISION: GO
