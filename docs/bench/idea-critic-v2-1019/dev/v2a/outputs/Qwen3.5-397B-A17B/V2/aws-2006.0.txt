The claim is that a retailer should pivot infrastructure costs into a public cloud business despite zero platform experience and unproven demand.

**Steelman:** Leveraging existing spiky capacity creates a marginal-cost advantage, allowing the company to undercut incumbents while monetizing idle assets, turning a cost center into a high-margin revenue stream that diversifies beyond retail.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capability Gap:** Running internal infrastructure for retail peaks differs fundamentally from selling multi-tenant, self-service APIs with strict SLAs. The organization lacks the DNA for developer relations, billing granularity, and platform reliability.
2.  **Market Risk:** In 2006, the "hourly server" market is theoretical. Enterprises trust established hardware vendors (IBM, HP) or managed hosts, not an online bookstore.
3.  **Strategic Distraction:** With investors already questioning capital intensity, launching a capital-heavy, operationally complex new vertical could dilute focus and margins during a critical retail growth phase.
4.  **Competition:** Incumbents have deep enterprise relationships and dedicated sales forces this retailer lacks.

**Better Way:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Continue optimizing internal costs; avoid the risk of a failed public launch damaging the core brand.
*   **Internal-Only:** Use the build-out strictly to lower retail operational costs, proving reliability internally before any external consideration.
*   **Partnership:** License the infrastructure management to an established vendor rather than building a go-to-market motion from scratch.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because enterprise CIOs refuse to trust a retailer with mission-critical data, support tickets overwhelm the retail engineering team, and the complex billing system creates revenue leakage. The core retail site suffers outages due to resource contention, causing a stock drop.

**Fit:** This contradicts the "focus on core competency" principle unless the margin differential is proven to be 10x retail margins, which is currently assumed, not evidenced. The "unproven market" status makes this a one-way door with high irreversible reputation risk.

**Verdict:** The proposal relies on unverified assumptions about market readiness and internal capability transferability. The risks of distraction and brand damage outweigh the theoretical upside of monetizing idle capacity without a pilot or partnership strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
