**Verdict: HOLD (Internal Recommendation to Executive Team)**

**Claim:** Acquire the animation studio for $7.4B in stock to secure IP and creative leadership.

**Steelman:** Buying the studio is the only viable path to prevent a rival from capturing its high-value IP and talent, instantly revitalizing the conglomerate's struggling animation division with proven creative leadership.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Valuation Blocker:** At ~$7.4B for one film per year, the price implies an unprecedented multiple on future cash flows. Unless the backlog of unreleased hits is guaranteed (unverified), this overpays significantly compared to building internal capacity or licensing.
2.  **Concentration Risk:** The model depends entirely on a few creative leaders. If they depart post-acquisition (a common culture-clash outcome), the asset value collapses, leaving the conglomerate with massive debt/dilution and no engine.
3.  **Governance Hazard:** Making the founder a major shareholder in the buyer creates a conflict where one individual can block strategic shifts or demand excessive rents, effectively holding the conglomerate hostage.
4.  **Alternative:** A long-term exclusive distribution deal with profit participation secures the IP flow without the $7.4B balance sheet risk or integration trauma.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   **Stop Condition:** If due diligence cannot verify a 5-year slate of greenlit projects with budget/cast attached, the valuation fails.
*   **Metric:** Projected ROI must exceed the conglomerate's cost of capital by >15% even if only 60% of the creative team remains after 24 months.

**Recommendation:**
Do not approve the acquisition *as proposed*. The price and dependency structure present a fatal downside. Return with:
1.  A verified 5-year production slate.
2.  Binding retention agreements for all key creative leads.
3.  A comparative financial model showing why an exclusive partnership option was rejected.

Without these, the risk of value destruction outweighs the strategic benefit.

DECISION: NO-GO
