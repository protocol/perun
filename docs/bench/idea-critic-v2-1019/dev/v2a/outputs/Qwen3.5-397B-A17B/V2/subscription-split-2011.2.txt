The proposal to split the DVD and streaming services into separate brands, accounts, and billing systems fails the steelman test when viewed against the stated risks and recent context. The strongest defensible version of this claim is that separation allows distinct strategic focus for a declining legacy business versus a growth engine. However, the execution plan ignores critical friction points that directly contradict the goal of retaining value.

First, the timing is catastrophic. Implementing a complex operational split immediately following a 60% price hike compounds customer anger. The proposal admits "reaction to the price increase" is a risk, yet the solution adds friction (two logins, two bills) rather than alleviating it. This looks less like strategic clarity and more like punishing loyal dual-users to hide the price hike's impact on the streaming brand.

Second, severing data sharing destroys a key competitive advantage: unified recommendations. Losing cross-service insights weakens the streaming product's ability to retain users, which is counterproductive if streaming is the primary growth vector. The "declining" DVD business still generates cash flow; alienating its most engaged users (those who also stream) accelerates churn faster than necessary.

Third, the operational cost of building a separate brand, website, and billing infrastructure diverts resources from the very streaming strategy this move intends to protect. The "better way" is to keep the accounts unified while managing the businesses internally as distinct units, perhaps with separate branding for marketing but a single login and bill. This achieves strategic separation without imposing immediate, high-friction costs on the customer base during a volatile pricing transition.

The pre-mortem is clear: customers perceive this as a deceptive attempt to obscure the total cost of ownership, leading to mass cancellations of both services rather than just the DVD portion. The loss of shared data blinds the streaming algorithm, reducing its stickiness. The blocker is the combination of recent price trauma and the removal of unified value.

DECISION: NO-GO
