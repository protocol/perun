**Steelman:** Acquiring the agency and platform instantly grants critical scale and technology to challenge the search leader, preventing permanent market exclusion following the competitor's similar move.

**Skeptic:** The $6 billion cash price is enormous for 2007, representing a massive premium in a fast-changing market. Paying entirely in cash depletes reserves needed for organic R&D or other opportunities. The "fast-changing" nature of display technology suggests the acquired assets could become obsolete quickly, turning a premium price into a stranded investment. Furthermore, owning an agency creates immediate conflicts with the buyer's existing agency customers, who may flee to neutral providers, eroding the core business while chasing the new one.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" or "partner" alternative avoids the $6 billion outlay and customer conflict. Building or licensing technology incrementally reduces obsolescence risk. A smaller, tuck-in acquisition of just the technology (excluding the conflicting agency arm) would address the capability gap without alienating current customers or exhausting cash reserves.

**Kill Criteria:** If the integration causes more than 5% of existing agency customers to leave within 12 months, the strategic cost outweighs the gain. If the display ad market shifts format (e.g., away from standard banners) within 18 months, the specific platform technology purchased becomes worthless.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal fails because the cultural clash between a software engineer-led firm and a creative agency stalls integration, while the high cash payment leaves the company vulnerable to a downturn. The conflicting business lines force a breakup of the acquired agency later at a loss, meaning the company paid $6 billion for technology it could have built for far less.

**Decision:** The proposal fails the blocker test. The price is excessive for a volatile market, the all-cash structure creates undue financial risk, and the inherent conflict with existing customers threatens the core revenue stream. The acquisition of the agency arm specifically introduces liabilities that outweigh the benefits of the technology platform. A revised approach targeting only the technology or pursuing partnerships is required.

DECISION: NO-GO
