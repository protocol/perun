**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee converts variable shipping friction into fixed revenue, locking in loyalty and increasing purchase frequency to offset margin compression.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is unit economics: if heavy users double frequency, shipping costs could exceed the $79 cap, creating a loss-leader spiral. However, the "do nothing" alternative guarantees continued friction for repeat buyers, capping lifetime value and leaving the retailer vulnerable to competitors removing this barrier. A "per-order discount" alternative fails to solve the psychological friction of recurring fees or guarantee upfront cash flow.

**Pre-mortem & Reversibility:**
Assume failure: The cohort of heavy buyers expands faster than modeled, draining cash reserves before retention benefits materialize. This is a **one-way door** for the launch window; once sold, memberships are hard to revoke without brand damage. However, the proposal allows for a **two-way door** via operational controls: eligibility can be tightened (e.g., excluding oversized items) or the program paused for new sign-ups if loss thresholds are breached.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:**
The proposal admits economics are "unproven," which is a critical gap. Without a pilot or modeled break-even frequency, this is a blind bet. Yet, the strategic necessity to remove friction for repeat customers outweighs the unquantified risk, provided strict guardrails exist. The "short-term margin hit" is the cost of acquiring locked-in recurring revenue.

**Decision Logic:**
No factual blocker proves the plan *will* fail; the risks are executional, not structural. The alternative (status quo) guarantees stagnation in repeat purchase rates. The proposal survives the attack because the risk of inaction exceeds the risk of a managed margin hit. To proceed safely, the launch must include immediate tracking of "average orders per member" against a break-even threshold. If members do not reach the required frequency to cover shipping costs within Q1, the program triggers a review. The potential upside of dominating customer loyalty justifies the calculated risk.

DECISION: GO
