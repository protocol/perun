**Steelman:** Committing now secures a decade-long lead in hybrid technology, forcing competitors to play catch-up while defining the segment before regulators mandate efficiency.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "do nothing" test. With cheap fuel and zero regulatory pressure, the market signal is absent. A bold public schedule combined with unproven battery durability and a novel transmission creates a massive warranty liability trap. If the batteries degrade early, the brand suffers irreversible reputational damage before the market even values the technology. The cost premium cannot be recovered in a low-fuel-price environment.

**Better Way:** The optimal path is a limited technical validation program or a small fleet lease to specific partners. This proves the power-split transmission and battery life without exposing the entire corporation to a public failure or massive warranty costs. Waiting for a regulatory signal or fuel price shift reduces financial risk while retaining the technical knowledge gained.

**Fit:** This moves the "north star" of innovation but violates the constraint of financial prudence given the lack of demand. It is a one-way door; once the public schedule is announced and tooling committed, retreat signals weakness and wastes capital.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume it is 1999. The cars are recalled due to battery cell failure in hot climates. The warranty costs exceed the program's total R&D budget. Sales are negligible because the price premium makes no sense at $1.50/gallon. The company is forced to discount heavily, destroying residual values.

**Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks a verified demand signal and relies on unproven core components for volume production. The risk of catastrophic warranty exposure in a non-demanding market is a definitive blocker. A cheaper, safer alternative (limited pilot) exists that achieves the technical learning without the existential financial risk.

**Decision:** The combination of unproven technology, high fixed costs, and zero market pull constitutes a fatal flaw. The plan must not proceed as a mass-production commitment with a bold public schedule.

DECISION: NO-GO
