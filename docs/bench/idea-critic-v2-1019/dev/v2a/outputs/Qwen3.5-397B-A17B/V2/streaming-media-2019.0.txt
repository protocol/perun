The claim is that withdrawing licensed content to launch a subsidized, proprietary streaming service will generate sufficient long-term value to offset immediate billions in lost licensing revenue and massive operating losses.

**Skeptic:** The premise assumes the brand alone drives migration, ignoring that rivals bundle content from multiple studios. Removing content from rivals eliminates a guaranteed, high-margin revenue stream ($billions annually) for a speculative, low-margin subscription model. The company has no proven track record running a global technology platform at this scale; execution risk is extreme. Cannibalization of existing cable and theatrical windows is not modeled as a net negative but treated as an acceptable trade-off without evidence of net uplift.

**Better-way:** The "do nothing" or "hybrid" alternative (licensing while building a smaller, niche service) preserves cash flow and reduces execution risk. A cheaper path is licensing content to the highest bidder while investing in production only, avoiding the capital intensity of global distribution and tech infrastructure. The proposal must beat the risk-adjusted return of simply collecting licensing fees, which it fails to demonstrate given the unproven tech stack and massive burn rate.

**Kill-criteria:** If subscriber acquisition costs exceed lifetime value within 24 months, or if licensing revenue loss exceeds 50% of projected streaming revenue by year three, the strategy fails. The "one-way door" of burning cash with no pivot back to licensing (once relationships are severed) creates an irreversible trap. The cost of delay is low; waiting to build tech competence preserves optionality.

**Verdict:** The proposal relies on unproven technical execution and optimistic subscriber growth while intentionally destroying a proven, high-margin revenue engine. The "multi-year operating loss" is not a funded investment but a cash incinerator with no guaranteed payoff. The risk of cannibalization and tech failure outweighs the speculative upside of a crowded, low-price market entry.

DECISION: NO-GO
