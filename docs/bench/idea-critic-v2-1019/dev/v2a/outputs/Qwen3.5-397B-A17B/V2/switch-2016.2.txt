**Steelman:** A hybrid device leverages existing IP to unify fragmented audiences, sacrificing raw power for unique utility and portability, creating a blue ocean distinct from direct specification wars.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the **price-to-performance mismatch**. Launching a modest-power device at parity with significantly more powerful rivals, coupled with a thin first-party lineup, invites immediate consumer rejection. History shows that "confusing positioning" combined with "weak specs" caused the previous failure; this proposal risks repeating that error by asking users to pay a premium for a compromise rather than a clear leader.

*Alternative 1 (Do Nothing/Wait):* Delay launch to secure a stronger game lineup or reduce price. Risk: Continued handheld erosion.
*Alternative 2 (Low-Cost Entry):* Price significantly below rivals to offset spec weakness. Risk: Deepened financial losses.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to lukewarm reviews citing poor graphics and lack of games. Third-party developers ignore the platform due to low install base and architectural quirks. The "hybrid" novelty wears off within six months, leaving a library too small to sustain hardware sales. The company cannot recover R&D costs before the next generation cycle begins.

**Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits to "financial losses" and "eroding handheld line." Adding a high-risk hardware launch with "supply constraints" and "limited support" directly threatens solvency. The "short window to recover" contradicts the time needed to build a software ecosystem essential for hardware survival. A hardware platform is only as strong as its software; launching with a "thin" lineup is a fatal flaw when the price point offers no other justification.

**Kill Criteria:**
1.  **Blocker:** Price parity with superior hardware + thin software lineup = negative value proposition.
2.  **Metric:** Pre-order conversion rate vs. competitors (if launched).
3.  **Stop Condition:** If third-party commitment letters are not secured for 5+ major titles pre-launch.

**Verdict:** The proposal fails the value equation. Without a dominant software slate or a price advantage, the hardware will not sell enough to reverse financial losses. The risks of repeating the previous console's failure are too high given the admitted constraints.

DECISION: NO-GO
