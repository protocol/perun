The claim is that offering 1,000x more storage than incumbents, funded by content-matched ads, will disrupt a mature email market despite high infrastructure costs and privacy risks.

**Skeptic:** The core assumption is that users value storage over privacy enough to accept automated scanning. In 2004, a gigabyte per user implies massive capital expenditure before revenue validation. If privacy backlash occurs, adoption stalls, leaving sunk costs with no ad inventory to monetize. The "invitation-only" gate slows viral growth needed to justify server farms.

**Better Way:** Instead of a full gigabyte immediately, offer a tiered approach (e.g., 100MB free, expandable) to validate storage demand vs. cost without over-provisioning. Alternatively, license the search technology to existing providers rather than building a consumer brand from scratch, reducing capital risk. Doing nothing preserves capital while competitors struggle with small quotas; waiting for storage costs to drop naturally improves unit economics.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a one-way door regarding brand reputation (privacy scandals are hard to undo) and capital commitment. The cost of delay is low; storage prices fall annually, improving margins later. A pre-mortem suggests failure via regulatory intervention or user revolt against ad scanning.

**Decision:** The proposal fails the blocker test. The unit economics rely on unproven ad yield per user against confirmed, massive storage costs. The privacy risk is a concrete blocker in a trust-sensitive category; once users perceive surveillance, acquisition costs skyrocket. The "mature market" argument is weak only if the value proposition is undeniable, but here the funding mechanism (scanning) directly undermines the value (privacy/trust). The cheaper alternative (waiting for storage costs to fall or licensing tech) reaches similar upside with less capital exposure.

DECISION: NO-GO
