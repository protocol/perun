The claim is that acquiring a struggling handset manufacturer for €5.4 billion will secure the ecosystem against a dominant rival by aligning hardware and software incentives. The steelman posits that without vertical integration, the manufacturer will defect to the market leader, causing the operating system to lose its last significant hardware partner and collapse.

However, the "better-way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the logic: owning a shrinking hardware business does not solve the core "app-ecosystem" problem. The proposal admits the rival platform wins because it has the apps and developers. Buying a failing device maker diverts €5.4 billion and massive management attention into low-margin hardware integration risks exactly when capital should be concentrated on incentivizing developers or simplifying the OS. History suggests that propping up a weak hardware partner via acquisition often alienates other handset makers, accelerating their exit rather than securing their loyalty.

The pre-mortem is clear: The acquisition closes, integration chaos ensues, the acquired brand continues to lose market share due to the app gap, and the remaining hardware partners flee fearing unfair competition or neglect. The result is a cash-draining hardware subsidiary and a stillborn OS. The "do nothing" or "partnership-only" alternative preserves cash to attack the developer gap directly, which is the actual bottleneck. Ownership of a sinking ship is not a life raft for the software platform; it is an anchor.

The strongest attack survived is the fear of immediate defection to the rival. Yet, if the device is already losing sales due to the lack of apps, the manufacturer is arguably already doomed regardless of ownership. Paying a premium to own that failure does not reverse the network effect favoring the leader. The proposal mistakes control of distribution for control of demand. Without a concrete plan to fix the app deficit using these funds, the hardware purchase is a defensive reflex, not a strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
