The claim is that rapid, large-scale international expansion via lease acquisition and new systems will succeed despite operational inexperience and entrenched competition. The steelman: leveraging existing lease inventory allows immediate scale to deter competitors, while a clean-slate supply chain avoids legacy debt, capturing cross-border price arbitrage before the incumbent reacts.

However, the "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the "do nothing" vs. "all-in" comparison. The proposal ignores the "Chesterton's Fence" of the incumbent's dominance; entering with 130+ unproven stores simultaneously creates a massive fixed-cost burden before unit economics are validated. A staged approach (5–10 pilot stores) using existing logistics partners rather than building a new system from scratch offers the same market intelligence at 5% of the risk. The proposed "new inventory-management system" is a classic pre-mortem failure point: deploying unproven core technology alongside new regulatory compliance and bilingual labeling in a foreign market is a compound risk that historically leads to stockouts or compliance fines.

The evidence provided admits the retailer must "learn" cross-border logistics and regulation. Learning requires iteration, which the proposed two-year, near-total rollout explicitly forbids. The price perception risk is compounded by the inability to adjust pricing dynamically if the new inventory system fails to track local costs accurately. The "cheaper, safer alternative" is a phased entry that proves the supply chain and local demand before committing to 130 leases. The current plan bets the entire international strategy on a single, untested operational model.

The strongest attack survived is the fear of missing the window of opportunity; however, moving too fast into a complex foreign market with unproven systems is a faster route to failure than moving slightly slower. The lack of a proven inventory system is a blocker, not a risk, because it underpins the entire value proposition. Without a pilot to validate the new supply chain and local demand elasticity, the scale proposed is unjustifiable.

DECISION: NO-GO
