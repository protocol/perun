The claim asserts that a $1B+ mobile-only subscription service for premium short-form video will succeed despite market saturation with free alternatives and rising subscription fatigue. The steelman is that high-production, A-list short-form content fills a quality gap free platforms ignore, and a proprietary orientation-switching tech feature justifies a premium price point.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. First, the core assumption of "proven demand" is explicitly admitted as unproven ("no proven demand for premium short-form"), yet the plan commits over $1B before validating this. Second, the "better way" analysis is missing: free, abundant short-form video already dominates user attention, and the proposed solution (high-cost production) drastically increases the break-even point compared to lower-cost competitors. Third, the "orientation-switching" feature is a technical convenience, not a value proposition strong enough to overcome subscription fatigue or displace entrenched free habits.

The pre-mortem is clear: the service launches with high burn rates, fails to convert users away from free alternatives due to lack of unique value beyond video quality, and collapses under the weight of unsustainably high content costs before achieving scale. The cost of delay is low; waiting to validate demand with a smaller, cheaper pilot (e.g., a single show or limited rollout) would prevent catastrophic capital loss. The proposal's own cited risks (no proven demand, price sensitivity, competition) are not mitigated but ignored in favor of a massive, irreversible upfront bet.

A cheaper, safer alternative exists: produce a slate of pilot episodes with A-list talent, test them on existing platforms or a minimal viable app, and measure willingness to pay before raising billions. The current plan bets the farm on an unverified hypothesis.

DECISION: NO-GO
