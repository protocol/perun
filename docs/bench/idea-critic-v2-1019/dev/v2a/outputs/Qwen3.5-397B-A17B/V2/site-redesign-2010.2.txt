The claim is that a full rewrite with altered voting mechanics and a forced migration will reverse slowing traffic. The steelman: only a radical redesign favoring publishers can attract new growth that the loyal community cannot provide, and a clean break is the only way to ship it fast enough to beat rivals.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. First, it directly antagonizes the stated core asset: the loyal community that values existing voting mechanics. Changing these mechanics risks immediate revolt and churn, destroying the baseline traffic before new publisher content can compensate. Second, the "all users at once" launch creates a one-way door with no rollback; if the new algorithm fails to engage users (a high probability given the community mismatch), the site collapses with no recovery path. Third, the engineering risk of a full rewrite delays the response to the growing rival, whereas iterative changes could be deployed immediately.

The "better way" is clear: A/B test the new surfacing logic on a small segment of traffic or new users only, keeping the core community experience intact while validating the publisher-focused hypothesis. This approach reduces engineering scope, allows rollback, and preserves the community while testing growth. The current proposal ignores the cheaper, safer alternative that reaches the same upside (validating the new model) without the existential risk.

The pre-mortem is straightforward: The launch occurs, the loyal community rejects the new voting rules, traffic plummets, and the team cannot revert because the old codebase is deprecated and the database schema changed. The rival site captures the displaced users. The cost of delay in this specific "big bang" approach is actually higher because the failure mode is catastrophic rather than learnable.

This plan commits the cardinal sin of startup evolution: alienating the early adopters who built the platform in pursuit of unproven mainstream growth, without a safety net. The risks are not theoretical; they are inherent in the design.

DECISION: NO-GO
