The steelman: A clear cola captures novelty demand and signals purity, expanding the portfolio without eroding the flagship's core identity.

However, the attack reveals a fatal blocker in the "cannibalize the flagship" risk. The proposal assumes the clear cola acts as a brand extension, but the primary differentiator (clarity) directly contradicts the established sensory schema for cola (dark color = cola flavor). If shoppers cognitively link cola flavor exclusively with dark color, the product fails the basic category test. If they accept it as cola, it is functionally identical to the existing lineup aside from aesthetics, making cannibalization of the flagship nearly certain rather than a manageable risk.

The "positive response" in test markets is likely driven by novelty bias, which the proposal admits may not convert to repeat purchases. Launching nationwide with a "large marketing campaign" locks in high fixed costs before proving repeat rates. The cost of delay is low; waiting for longitudinal data on repeat purchase frequency prevents burning capital on a fad.

The "do nothing" alternative preserves the flagship's margin. A "cheaper path" would be a limited regional rollout without a massive campaign to measure actual repeat rates over 6 months, not just initial trial. The current plan bets the brand's equity on an unproven sensory disconnect. The evidence provided (test market positivity) is insufficient to overcome the fundamental category contradiction (cola must be dark) and the high probability of novelty fade.

The strongest attack survived is the potential for category confusion: if consumers reject the taste because it looks wrong, or buy it once because it looks new and never return, the nationwide launch becomes a massive loss leader that dilutes the flagship. The proposal offers no kill criteria or staged approach to mitigate this. It demands a binary, high-cost commitment based on fragile early data.

DECISION: NO-GO
