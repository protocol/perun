**Steelman:** A third-place console maker can escape the graphics arms race by targeting non-gamers with a low-cost, motion-controlled device, creating a new market segment rather than fighting for the existing hardcore one.

**Attack & Alternatives:** The primary failure mode is rejection by both camps: hardcore gamers dismiss it as a "toy," while third-party publishers refuse to support a non-HD, low-margin platform, leaving a content vacuum. The "do nothing" alternative (competing on graphics) guarantees continued third-place status against entrenched rivals with deeper pockets. A "hybrid" approach (high-end + motion) fails the cost constraint and confuses the value proposition.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This fits a "Blue Ocean" strategy but violates the industry norm that power equals success. If we assume the casual audience does not exist or will not pay for dedicated hardware, the venture collapses. The one-way door is the massive capital expenditure on custom silicon and marketing before audience validation. However, the cost of delay is ceding the living room to rivals who may eventually saturate the hardcore market and look elsewhere.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:** The proposal admits thin margins and skeptical publishers. The bet relies entirely on the controller driving unique value.
*   **Blocker Check:** No factual blocker exists; the risks are executional, not impossible. The "toy" perception is a risk, not a proof of failure.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If attach rate (games per console) falls below 2.0 within 6 months, or if third-party support remains under 5 major titles by launch + 1 quarter, pivot to first-party heavy investment or discontinue hardware subsidies.
*   **Leading Metric:** Weekly active users among non-traditional demographics (age 50+, families).

**Verdict:** The proposal survives the attack because the status quo is a guaranteed loss, and the alternative paths are either too expensive or dilute the strategic focus. The risks are real but manageable via strict kill criteria. The potential to expand the total addressable market outweighs the danger of niche failure.
