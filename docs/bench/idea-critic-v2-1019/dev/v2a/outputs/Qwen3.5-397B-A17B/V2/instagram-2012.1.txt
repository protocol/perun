**Steelman:** Acquiring the category-defining mobile photo leader neutralizes an existential threat to the buyer's core business and instantly secures mobile dominance for $1B, a premium justified by strategic survival rather than current revenue.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker is the **valuation disconnect**: $1B for zero revenue and ~13 employees implies a multiplier with no financial anchor. However, the "revenue" metric is a lagging indicator; the asset's value lies in its **network effect velocity** (tens of millions of users, cross-platform momentum) which the buyer lacks. The risk of the buyer's own weak mobile product failing is a verified fact; the cost of delay is likely total loss of relevance in the mobile shift.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** High probability of the target becoming a standalone giant or selling to a rival, cementing the buyer's decline.
2.  **Build Internally:** Too slow; the buyer has already failed to gain traction.
3.  **Acquire:** Immediate market control, talent acquisition, and threat neutralization.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal fails if regulatory bodies block it (antitrust) or if the buyer's management suffocates the app's culture, causing user exodus. The "no business model" risk is mitigated by the buyer's existing ad infrastructure, which can be layered onto the user base immediately.

**Fit:** This moves the North Star from "desktop dominance" to "mobile survival." The $1B price tag is effectively an insurance policy against obsolescence. While the financials look absurd in isolation, the strategic cost of *not* buying is infinite. The cultural risk is real but manageable via autonomy guarantees. The regulatory risk is present but calculable; the speed of the mobile market shift outweighs the probability of a blocked deal.

**Conclusion:** The lack of revenue is a feature, not a bug—it means the app grew purely on product-market fit. The buyer is purchasing a verified growth engine and a killed competitor in one move. No cheaper alternative exists that secures the same upside.

DECISION: GO
