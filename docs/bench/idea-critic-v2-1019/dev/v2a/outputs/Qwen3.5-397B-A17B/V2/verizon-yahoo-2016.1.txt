**Steelman:** Acquiring the declining portal provides immediate scale in mobile media, allowing the carrier to combine assets and challenge the two dominant ad platforms before it is too late.

**Attack:** The claim relies on the assumption that combining two declining, mismanaged assets creates a competitive whole. The target suffers from years of falling traffic and revenue, repeated management instability, and an outdated business model. The acquirer is a wireless carrier with zero history in consumer media, lacking the core competency to reverse these trends. Integration risk is extreme given the cultural and operational mismatch.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" option preserves $4.8 billion in capital. A cheaper alternative is investing in organic mobile infrastructure or acquiring smaller, growing tech firms rather than a dying legacy portal. Buying a "falling knife" to fix a lack of expertise is a high-cost, low-probability strategy compared to building or buying emerging capabilities.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This contradicts the likely north star of sustainable growth; it attempts to buy relevance in a shifting market using deprecated assets. In a pre-mortem, the integration fails due to culture clash, the ad revenue continues its secular decline despite the merger, and the carrier is left with a massive write-down and distracted leadership. The cost of delay is low because the dominant platforms are already pulling away; rushing into a bad deal accelerates value destruction.

**Blocker:** The core mechanism for success (reversing secular decline in a sector where the buyer has no experience) is unsupported by evidence. The proposal admits the target is declining and mismanaged, yet offers no credible plan for how this specific buyer can fix it. The math of combining two negative trajectories rarely yields a positive outcome without a miracle turnaround, which is not a strategy.

**Kill Criteria:** If the combined mobile ad revenue does not grow within 12 months, or if key talent departs post-merger, the initiative must be halted and assets sold.

The risks are not merely executional; they are fundamental to the thesis. Paying a premium for decline without relevant expertise is a definitive blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
