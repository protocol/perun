The claim is that acquiring the dominant but unprofitable video site for $1.65 billion creates sufficient strategic value to justify the massive financial and legal risks. The steelman: only this target offers immediate category dominance, network effects, and user-generated content scale that the buyer's internal project cannot match, making the premium a necessary cost of entry to own the future of online video.

However, the skepticism required here is extreme. The target has no meaningful revenue, exploding bandwidth costs, and faces existential copyright litigation from every major media company. The "unproven ad model" is a critical gap; paying $1.65 billion for a business model that may never work is speculation, not investment. The "better way" analysis suggests building or buying a smaller, compliant competitor for a fraction of the price, avoiding the toxic liability pile. The pre-mortem is clear: the acquisition closes, lawsuits bury the platform in injunctions or damages, bandwidth costs continue to outpace ad revenue, and the stock price collapses under the weight of a failed integration and legal settlements.

The strongest attack survived is the argument that "speed to scale" justifies the price. While true that organic growth is too slow, the specific risk of copyright destruction makes the asset potentially worthless regardless of its current traffic. If the courts rule against fair use for user uploads, the core product is illegal. No kill criteria or leading metrics are provided to manage this binary legal risk. The proposal relies on the assumption that litigation will be settled favorably or cheaply, which is an unsupported gamble given the hostility of media owners.

Without a verified path to monetization that covers bandwidth, or a legal shield against copyright claims, the downside is total loss of the acquisition capital plus ongoing liabilities. The "do nothing" or "build internally" options, while slower, preserve capital and avoid inheriting a lawsuit bomb. The facts presented describe a speculative bubble asset, not a sound business acquisition.

DECISION: NO-GO
