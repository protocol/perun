**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant video platform eliminates the buyer's largest competitive threat, instantly secures the #1 market position, and captures the defining cultural shift of the web, betting that scale will force media companies to license rather than litigate.

**Attack:** The valuation is mathematically indefensible based on current fundamentals: zero revenue, massive operating losses from bandwidth, and existential copyright liability that could result in statutory damages exceeding the purchase price. The "ad model" is unproven for long-form content, and the buyer's own smaller service suggests organic growth was failing. Buying this asset transfers unlimited liability for past infringements to the buyer. If courts rule against fair use or safe harbor protections, the platform becomes a legal toxin, not an asset. The cost of capital ($1.65B in 2006 stock) is immense; if the ad model fails to monetize short clips effectively, shareholder value will be permanently destroyed.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do Nothing:* Continue building the smaller internal service; avoid massive liability and cash burn.
2. *License Content:* Pay media companies directly for hosted content, avoiding acquisition risk.
3. *Wait:* Let the copyright lawsuits clarify the legal landscape before paying a premium.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-acquisition, courts rule that hosting copyrighted clips without explicit licenses is illegal. Media companies win injunctions requiring the removal of 80% of popular content. User engagement collapses. Bandwidth costs remain high while ad revenue plummets due to content scarcity. The $1.65B stock grant dilutes existing shareholders with no return.

**Fit:** While this aligns with a "organize the world's information" vision, it violates basic financial prudence and risk management. The "north star" cannot justify a bet where the downside is total loss plus massive legal fees, based on an unproven hypothesis that infringers will become partners.

**Kill Criteria:** A deal of this magnitude requires a verified path to profitability within 24 months and a capped legal liability framework. Neither exists. The risk is not just "high"; it is unquantifiable and potentially fatal to the buyer's balance sheet. The cheaper, safer alternative is to let the market settle the copyright question first.

DECISION: NO-GO
