The claim is that acquiring the dominant code-hosting service for $7.5B in stock creates sufficient strategic value to outweigh immediate financial losses, leadership vacuums, and severe community distrust.

**Steelman:** Only this buyer possesses the capital and enterprise reach to stabilize the target's operations while preserving its open-source ethos through independent governance, effectively neutralizing the free competitor by integrating the platform into the world's largest developer ecosystem.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is community defection. If developers migrate to the free alternative en masse due to distrust, the asset's value collapses to zero, rendering the $7.5B premium a total loss. The target lacks a permanent CEO, suggesting internal instability that a hostile owner might exacerbate rather than fix.
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids integrating a loss-making entity and sidesteps antitrust scrutiny. The free competitor grows organically, but the buyer avoids reputational suicide.
*   **Minority Investment/Partnership:** Achieves integration benefits without the toxicity of full ownership or the $7.5B price tag.
*   **Build/Buy Smaller:** Acquire a smaller, trusted player to avoid the "hostile giant" narrative.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
Historically hostile companies rarely successfully pivot in the eyes of their adversaries without visible, structural separation. Assuming independence suffices is a dangerous gamble; the community views the brand itself as the threat. A pre-mortem reveals that six months post-close, top maintainers move repositories to the free rival, citing "inevitable integration," triggering a network effect collapse. The cost of delay is low; the target is loss-making and leaderless, weakening its bargaining power over time.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:**
The proposal admits the target is loss-making and the community is vocal about distrust. No evidence is provided that "independent operation" has ever succeeded when the parent company has a history of hostility. The price ($7.5B) assumes continued dominance, which is the very variable at risk.

**Verdict:**
The plan relies on the unproven assumption that a distrusted entity can self-govern under a hated parent. The risk of immediate community exodus to the free alternative is a concrete blocker, not a manageable risk. The lack of a permanent CEO at the target further signals instability that cash alone cannot fix. The "independence" promise is insufficient to counteract the brand toxicity.

DECISION: NO-GO
