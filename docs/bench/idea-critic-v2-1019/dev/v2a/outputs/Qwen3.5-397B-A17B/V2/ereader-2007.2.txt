The claim is that a non-hardware bookseller should launch a subsidized, cellular-enabled e-reader to dominate the emerging digital market. Steelman: Only by controlling the device, distribution, and pricing can the company overcome piracy fears, eliminate the "computer required" friction, and create a seamless ecosystem that earlier fragmented attempts failed to build.

However, the proposal contains fatal blockers under current facts. First, the company has zero hardware experience; entering a capital-intensive manufacturing sector while simultaneously negotiating wary publishers creates a dual-execution risk that historically crushes retailers. Second, the economics are undefined: offering free cellular connectivity and "low price" titles implies heavy subsidies. Without a verified model showing how these costs are recouped (e.g., via device margins or lifetime book value), the plan burns cash with no guaranteed return. Third, the "cannibalization" risk is treated as a side note, yet if the low digital price collapses the high-margin print business before digital volume compensates, the net result is value destruction.

Alternatives exist: licensing existing hardware, partnering with a device maker, or launching a software-only store first. These avoid the manufacturing cliff. The proposal fails to prove why owning the hardware stack is strictly necessary now versus later, nor does it provide a break-even analysis for the connectivity subsidies.

The strongest attack is the lack of a verified unit economics model. If the subsidy per device plus lost print margin exceeds the lifetime profit from a digital customer, the strategy fails. The proposal asserts "low price" and "free connection" as features without demonstrating they are sustainable. A cheaper, safer path is a pilot with a limited catalog on an existing partner device to validate demand before committing to hardware design.

Because the proposal commits to high-fixed-cost hardware and undefined subsidies without a proven economic engine or prior experience, it fails the blocker test.

DECISION: NO-GO
