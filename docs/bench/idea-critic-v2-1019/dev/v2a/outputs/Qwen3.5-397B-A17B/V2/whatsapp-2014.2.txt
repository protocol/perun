The claim is that acquiring a messaging app with minimal revenue, 50 employees, and a recent $1.5B valuation for $19B is a sound strategic move to prevent displacement.

**Steelman:** The buyer must secure the dominant mobile messaging layer immediately to prevent existential obsolescence, paying a premium for network effects that financial metrics cannot yet capture.

**Attack:** The price represents a >12x increase in value in one year without proportional revenue growth or a monetization strategy. The target explicitly rejects advertising, the buyer's core model, creating a fundamental integration conflict. With only 50 employees, the asset is purely its user base; if those users migrate post-acquisition due to privacy concerns or product changes, the $19B buys nothing. The "fear of displacement" drives a panic premium that ignores the lack of a verified path to ROI.

**Better Way:** Wait for market correction, build a competing internal feature, or acquire a smaller, monetizable competitor. Doing nothing preserves capital until the target's business model proves sustainable beyond user growth.

**Fit:** While it addresses the mobile threat, the cost threatens the buyer's entire financial stability for an unproven hedge.

**Pre-mortem:** Regulators block the deal due to privacy monopolies, or the founders leave immediately, causing user exodus. The integration fails culturally, and the $19B is written down as goodwill impairment. This is a one-way door with massive downside.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "no monetisation plan" and "minimal revenue." The valuation jump from $1.5B to $19B lacks fundamental justification in the text provided.

**Decision:** The risks (price, no revenue, cultural clash, regulatory scrutiny) vastly outweigh the unproven benefit. The lack of a monetization plan and the explicit refusal to show ads make the strategic fit dubious. The price is speculative, based entirely on fear rather than verified value.

**Kill Criteria:** If user growth slows by >10% in the next quarter or if key founders depart within 6 months, the acquisition thesis collapses.

DECISION: NO-GO
