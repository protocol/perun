**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee ($79) eliminates per-order friction, locking in loyalty and increasing purchase frequency enough to offset shipping losses through volume and marginal cost efficiencies.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary blocker is the **unit economics gap**. If heavy buyers shift from 5 to 20 orders annually, shipping costs (e.g., $8/order) jump from $40 to $160 against $79 revenue, creating an immediate $81 loss per user before product margins are considered. The proposal admits economics are "unproven" yet seeks full launch. This violates the "bet the company" threshold without a validated model.

*Alternative 1 (Do Nothing):* Retain per-order fees. Risk: Competitors undercut on convenience. Reward: Zero margin erosion.
*Alternative 2 (Pilot):* Limit to a specific zip code or customer segment for 90 days. This validates the elasticity of demand and actual shipping cost lift before exposing the entire ledger to structural losses.
*Alternative 3 (Threshold):* Free shipping only above a high cart value (e.g., $100). This protects margins while encouraging larger baskets, though it fails to solve the "repeat small order" friction.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, the company faces a liquidity crisis. Membership adoption was higher than expected among low-margin, high-frequency buyers. The "two-day" promise forced expensive air freight usage that the flat fee did not cover. Reversal is impossible without mass customer churn and brand damage.

**Fit & Evidence:** The proposal cites no data proving that increased frequency will generate enough *product* margin to cover the *shipping* deficit. Without a proven LTV:CAC shift or a cap on liability, this is a speculative gamble with fixed costs and uncapped variable losses. The "hard to reverse" nature makes this a one-way door requiring near-certainty, which is explicitly absent.

**Kill Criteria:** A go decision requires a pilot proving that the incremental product margin from increased frequency exceeds the shipping subsidy cost. Since the proposal skips validation and launches directly into an unproven economic model with high reversal costs, it fails the blocker test.

DECISION: NO-GO
