Steelman: Acquiring the dominant code-hosting platform instantly grants the historically hostile buyer legitimacy, neutralizes a potential competitor, and captures the global developer graph, provided the community's trust can be preserved through operational independence.

**Skeptic**: The core premise—that a community defined by distrust of the buyer will remain under its ownership even with "independent" operation—is unproven. History suggests cultural assimilation is inevitable, risking mass defection to the free competitor mentioned. The target's lack of permanent leadership and ongoing losses indicate fragility; a $7.5B price tag implies paying a massive premium for a distressed asset whose primary value (the network) is volatile and ready to flee.

**Better-Way**: The "do nothing" or "partner" alternative avoids immediate capital outlay and brand toxicity. Given the target's losses and leadership vacuum, a smaller strategic investment or waiting for further valuation compression could achieve influence without the binary risk of a full acquisition. If the goal is developer goodwill, organic open-source contributions might yield higher ROI than a hostile takeover.

**Kill-Criteria**: This is a one-way door with irreversible brand damage if the community revolts. The cost of delay is low; the competitor is free, not growing exponentially faster than the target can be stabilized. The pre-mortem is clear: integration stumbles, key maintainers leave for the free alternative, and the buyer is left owning an empty shell at a peak valuation.

**Decision**: The proposal fails the blocker test. The "independent operation" guarantee is insufficient to counteract the verified fact of community hostility and the target's leadership vacuum. Paying a premium for a loss-making asset where the primary asset (developers) is actively threatening to leave constitutes a speculative gamble, not a sound strategy. The risk of immediate value destruction via defection outweighs the strategic upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
