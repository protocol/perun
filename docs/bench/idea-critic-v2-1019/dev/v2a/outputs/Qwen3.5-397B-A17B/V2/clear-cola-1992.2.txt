The claim assumes positive test market response and "purer" positioning guarantee nationwide success, ignoring that clear cola contradicts the deep-rooted consumer heuristic linking dark color to authentic cola flavor. The strongest alternative is "do nothing" or a limited regional rollout; a massive national campaign locks capital before proving repeat purchase rates, which are historically low for novelty beverages.

The pre-mortem reveals failure via cannibalization: existing loyalists switch to the clear version without expanding the total user base, while the high marketing spend erodes margins. The "purer" benefit is unproven against the sensory expectation of taste-by-color. Test markets often overstate demand by attracting curiosity buyers who do not convert to habitual consumption.

A specific blocker exists in the risk profile: if novelty buyers do not repeat, the national launch becomes a sunk cost with no path to profitability. The proposal lacks a kill criterion or a staged approach to validate repeat rates before full commitment. Launching nationwide immediately treats a hypothesis as a fact. The cheaper, safer path is a phased expansion tied to verified repeat purchase metrics, not just initial trial.

The evidence provided (positive test response) is insufficient to overcome the structural risk of flavor-color dissonance and potential flagship erosion. Without a mechanism to ensure the new product attracts non-cola drinkers rather than just shifting existing volume, the financial upside is illusory. The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test because the cost of failure (brand dilution + wasted campaign spend) outweighs the unproven incremental gain.

DECISION: NO-GO
