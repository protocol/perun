The claim is that building a massively larger, unproven aircraft with unproven engines will secure market dominance despite existential financial risk and the looming threat of supersonic replacement.

Steelman: Only a drastic increase in capacity can meet projected demand growth where airport slots are constrained, and designing for freighter conversion creates a dual-revenue stream that hedges against passenger market shifts.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The development and factory outlay is "far larger than any the company has made," creating a single-point failure mode where any delay or technical fault bankrupts the firm. The engine is unproven; relying on a supplier for a critical, novel component without a backup or proven track record introduces a high probability of program stall. Most critically, the strategic assumption that subsonic jets will remain dominant is directly contradicted by the stated expectation that "supersonic airliners are expected to replace subsonic jets." Investing billions in a subsonic platform just as the market anticipates a technological paradigm shift is a catastrophic misallocation of capital.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital and wait for the supersonic market to clarify.
2. Upgrade current models: Incrementally improve existing jets with better engines, avoiding new factory costs.
3. Partner on supersonic: Redirect R&D toward the anticipated future technology rather than doubling down on the old.

The "cheaper" path of upgrading current models reaches similar efficiency gains without the existential risk. The risk of supersonic replacement is not a mere uncertainty; it is a stated market expectation that invalidates the long-term viability of a subsonic giant. The fact that only one airline has ordered so far indicates weak market validation for such a massive leap. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of building a white elephant if supersonic travel becomes the standard.

This is a one-way door with insufficient evidence that the target market (passengers preferring subsonic travel) will exist in the timeframe required to recoup the investment. The combination of unproven technology, unprecedented financial exposure, and a potentially obsolete market premise constitutes a hard blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
