The claim is that a 3D display and visual search can overcome a missing app ecosystem and carrier exclusivity to justify flagship pricing in a saturated two-platform market.

Steelman: Unique hardware features create a defensible niche for heavy shoppers, making app gaps tolerable.

However, the failure hypothesis is strong: mainstream smartphone adoption requires mainstream apps. A forked OS without leading apps is a non-starter for the mass market, regardless of 3D capabilities. The "one-touch" shopping feature is valuable but insufficient to drive daily device usage compared to social, messaging, and entertainment apps available on dominant platforms.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital; avoid entering a low-margin, high-churn hardware battle without ecosystem leverage.
2. Low-cost tablet expansion: Leverage existing success in tablets where app dependency is lower and price sensitivity is higher.
3. Software-only approach: License the visual search technology to existing platform holders rather than building a full phone.

Pre-mortem: The phone launches, early adopters try the 3D feature, but daily frustration with missing apps leads to high return rates and negative reviews. Carrier exclusivity limits reach just as word-of-mouth turns negative. Inventory piles up, requiring massive write-downs.

Evidence: The proposal admits the market is saturated and dominated by two platforms with vast app stores. History shows hardware differentiation (e.g., keyboards, styluses) rarely succeeds without software parity in this era. The "app gap" is a known killer for third-party OS attempts.

Blocker: The absence of mainstream apps is a factual blocker for a flagship-priced smartphone in 2014. No amount of 3D novelty compensates for the inability to run standard banking, social, or communication tools expected at that price point. The cost of delay is low; the cost of execution is massive inventory risk.

Kill criteria are irrelevant because the fundamental premise (apps don't matter if 3D is cool enough) is flawed for a general-purpose smartphone. The safer path is integrating the shopping feature into existing ecosystems or sticking to tablets.

DECISION: NO-GO
