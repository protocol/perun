**Steelman:** Offering 1,000x the standard storage with superior search creates a defensible moat against incumbents, turning email from a utility into a searchable knowledge base, monetized later via high-intent contextual ads.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker is the unit economics of 2004. If storage costs exceed the lifetime ad revenue per user, the model collapses. However, "do nothing" cedes the strategic high ground of user data and attention to competitors. Alternatives like charging fees or limiting storage to 100MB fail to disrupt the market or generate the necessary network effect. The "mature market" argument is a fallacy; incumbents are complacent with low storage, creating a massive gap for a premium-free alternative.

**Pre-mortem & Fit:**
Assume failure: The service runs out of capital because ad fill rates are too low to cover disk arrays, or privacy backlash forces a policy reversal. This is a two-way door; storage limits can be adjusted, and ad models tweaked. The cost of delay is high: whoever owns the inbox owns the user's intent graph.

**Evidence & Verification:**
The proposal relies on the assumption that storage costs are falling faster than ad yields decline. In 2004, disk prices were plummeting. The risk of privacy objections is real but manageable through transparency and opt-in mechanics. The lack of direct revenue at launch is a feature, not a bug, prioritizing scale over immediate yield.

**Decision:**
No factual blocker exists. The storage cost risk is outweighed by the strategic value of acquiring high-intent users and their data. The "invitation-only" rollout correctly manages the blast radius and server load. The potential to redefine the category justifies the capital expenditure.

**Kill Criteria:**
If ad revenue per user does not cover 50% of storage costs within 18 months of public launch, pivot to a tiered model or pause expansion. Leading metric: Daily Active Users vs. Storage Cost Per User.

DECISION: GO
