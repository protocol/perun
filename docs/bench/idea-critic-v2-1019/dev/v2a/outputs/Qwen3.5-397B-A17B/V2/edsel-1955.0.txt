The claim is that launching a new mid-priced division in 1957 will capture growth and fill a portfolio gap. Steelman: A dedicated brand with unique styling and a new dealer network can isolate the mid-market opportunity without cannibalizing existing low or premium lines, leveraging extensive research to ensure product-market fit.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test on timing and infrastructure. First, the "mid-priced segment has been growing but could shrink" combined with an explicit "recession risk" for the 1957 debut creates a fatal mismatch. Launching a capital-intensive venture requiring new dealer construction exactly when consumer discretionary spending may collapse contradicts basic economic prudence. The massive advertising spend planned for a shrinking market destroys ROI before the first unit sells.

Second, the requirement for a "new dealer network" is a structural blocker. Building independent dealerships is slow, expensive, and irreversible (a one-way door). In a potential recession, these dealers will face immediate insolvency, damaging the parent brand's reputation and leaving fixed costs with no revenue. The risk of "overlap with the firm's own other brands" is exacerbated when total market volume drops; internal competition becomes a fight for survival rather than growth.

The "extensive market research" cited is likely backward-looking, capturing the growth phase that is ending, not the contraction phase beginning. No alternative considers delaying the launch until economic stability returns or utilizing the existing dealer network to test a single "halo" model without the overhead of a full division. The cost of delay (missing some 1957 sales) is far lower than the cost of failure (bankrupting a new dealer network during a downturn).

The strongest attack survived is the theoretical existence of a market gap; however, the gap is irrelevant if the customer base cannot afford to enter it during a recession. The proposal commits to high fixed costs and irreversible infrastructure expansion at the precise moment flexibility is required. The evidence of recession risk alone, unmitigated by a phased approach or shared infrastructure, constitutes a named blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
