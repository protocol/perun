**Steelman:** A mandatory client centralizes update delivery and anti-cheat enforcement, solving the core inability to patch games or ban cheaters in a retail-only model, while creating a future digital distribution platform.

**Attack:** The proposal assumes universal broadband in 2003, which is false; many target users rely on dial-up, making large mandatory downloads a hard blocker for adoption. Retail partners, currently the primary revenue channel, may retaliate by delisting boxed copies if the client adds friction, causing immediate revenue collapse before digital sales can compensate. Furthermore, forcing a "buggy v1" client on players risks permanent brand damage if the installer fails or crashes systems, confirming player fears about mandatory online software.

**Better Way:** A hybrid approach: keep retail boxes fully playable offline but offer *optional* client installation for auto-updates and multiplayer access. This preserves retail relationships and respects bandwidth limits while gradually migrating users. Alternatively, partner with an existing platform (if available) rather than building a costly, risky proprietary store from scratch.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If launched as mandatory, the most likely failure mode is a consumer revolt combined with retail delisting, leaving the company with no physical shelf space and a digital client nobody can download. The cost of delay is high (cheating continues), but the cost of a botched mandatory rollout is existential.

**Kill Criteria:** The plan fails if broadband penetration in the target demographic is below 50% at launch, or if major retailers contractually forbid mandatory online components. The proposal lacks evidence that these thresholds are met.

**Verdict:** The "mandatory" constraint ignores the 2003 infrastructure reality and threatens the existing retail lifeline. The risk of alienating the entire installed base and losing shelf space outweighs the unproven benefit of a forced client. The proposal must be revised to make the client optional for single-player/retail versions before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
