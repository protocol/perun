The claim is that building a massively larger, unproven wide-body jet with a new factory and engine will secure market dominance despite existential financial risks and emerging supersonic competition.

Steelman: Only a radical capacity increase can meet projected demand growth where current fleets fail, and designing for freighter conversion creates a dual-market safety net if passenger trends shift.

However, the blocker test fails immediately. The proposal relies on a single customer order to justify an outlay "far larger than any the company has made." This concentration risk is fatal; if that airline cancels or falters, the manufacturer faces insolvency. Furthermore, the core assumption that subsonic jets will remain relevant is directly contradicted by the stated industry expectation that supersonic airliners will replace them. Betting the entire company on a subsonic giant just as the market pivot to supersonic begins is strategically incoherent.

The "better way" is clear: wait for supersonic maturity or develop a smaller, lower-risk wide-body that multiple airlines might adopt, rather than a bespoke leviathan for one. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of building a factory for a technology (subsonic) deemed obsolete by industry forecasts.

The pre-mortem is straightforward: the supersonic transition happens faster than expected, or the single anchor customer defaults. The result is a stranded asset and bankruptcy. The unproven engine adds a technical failure mode that could ground the entire fleet before it generates revenue. There is no evidence provided that demand for 2.5x capacity exists beyond this one client, making the volume assumptions speculative at best.

This is a one-way door with no margin for error. The risks (unproven engine, supersonic disruption, single customer) are not merely execution hurdles; they are fundamental flaws in the business case as presented. The proposal does not beat "do nothing" or "wait for supersonic clarity" because it assumes a static market that the text explicitly says is changing.

DECISION: NO-GO
