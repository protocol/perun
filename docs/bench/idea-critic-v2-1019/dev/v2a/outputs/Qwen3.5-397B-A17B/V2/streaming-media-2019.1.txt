**Steelman:** Sacrificing immediate, guaranteed licensing revenue to build a direct-to-consumer monopoly asset is the only way to survive the industry's shift away from cable and third-party distribution, accepting years of losses to secure permanent customer relationships and data.

**Attack & Options:**
The claim fails the "beat do nothing" test on current facts.
1.  **Revenue Cliff:** Pulling content instantly destroys billions in high-margin licensing fees. Replacing this with low-price subscriptions requires acquiring tens of millions of users immediately to match lost cash flow. Given the competitor's entrenched 100M+ lead and the company's zero history in running a global streaming tech platform, this acquisition rate is historically unprecedented and unlikely.
2.  **Capability Gap:** The proposal admits the company has never run this technology. Building a stable, scalable global platform while simultaneously funding a multi-year operating loss creates a dual-execution failure mode: running out of cash before the tech works, or the tech failing under load before the audience arrives.
3.  **Cannibalization:** Aggressively pricing below the market leader devalues the entire library and accelerates the collapse of the existing cash-cow cable business before the new model is profitable.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years in, the platform suffers outages during peak releases; subscriber growth stalls at half the target due to content gaps caused by prior licensing deals; the stock collapses under the weight of sustained losses without a clear path to profitability. The company is forced to re-license content at a fraction of the original value.

**Fit & Evidence:** The strategy assumes "build it and they will come" in a winner-take-most market where the incumbent already has massive scale. There is no evidence provided that this specific company can overcome the switching costs and network effects of the leader, especially while handicapped by self-inflicted revenue loss and technical inexperience. The "multi-year operating loss" is not a fundable strategy without proven unit economics, which are absent here.

**Verdict:** This is a one-way door with a high probability of catastrophic value destruction. The risks (tech failure, cash burn, revenue loss) are certainties based on the proposal's own admissions, while the upside relies on unproven execution against a dominant rival. The cheaper, safer alternative is a hybrid model: license selectively while building tech slowly, rather than a "burn the ships" approach.

DECISION: NO-GO
