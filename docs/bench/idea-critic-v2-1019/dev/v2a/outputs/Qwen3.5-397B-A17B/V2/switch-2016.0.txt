Steelman: A hybrid device leverages existing IP to escape the losing home-console war and the shrinking handheld market by creating a unique, portable home experience.

**Skeptic:** The proposal admits weak specs and a thin launch lineup. History shows that third-party developers avoid underpowered hardware, and consumers often reject "confusing" positioning. If the "hybrid" novelty wears off after six months, the weak chip cannot compete on graphics, leading to rapid software sales collapse. The financial losses from the previous console mean the company likely lacks the runway for a second failure.

**Better-way:** The cheapest alternative is "do nothing" in the home space: retreat entirely to software publishing for rivals' powerful consoles and phones, monetizing IP without hardware risk. A second option is a low-cost, dedicated handheld with strong battery life, ignoring the TV market to avoid direct specification comparisons with powerful rivals.

**Kill-criteria & Pre-mortem:** Assume it is 2018 and sales have halted. Why? Because the "thin" first-year lineup failed to convince early adopters to buy a second unit, and third-party support never materialized due to the weak chip. The kill criterion is clear: if pre-orders do not exceed the install base of the failed previous console by 20% within 30 days, the positioning has failed to overcome consumer confusion.

**Decision:** This is a one-way door with catastrophic downside. The proposal relies on changing consumer behavior (accepting a hybrid) and developer behavior (supporting weak hardware) simultaneously, with no proof of concept. The "thin" launch lineup is a fatal flaw; a new paradigm requires a system-seller at launch, not a promise of future games. The financial fragility of the maker makes this gamble irresponsible without a guaranteed hit title. The risks of weak specs and confusion are not mitigated; they are accepted.

DECISION: NO-GO
