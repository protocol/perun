The proposal to rewrite the entire platform from scratch and force an immediate migration of all users in 2010 contains fatal flaws that outweigh the potential benefits of a redesigned front page.

First, the "big bang" migration eliminates any possibility of rollback. If the new voting mechanics alienate the core community—as the risks explicitly warn—the site will lose its loyal user base instantly with no way to revert. In a competitive landscape where a rival is already growing, losing the core community is an existential threat, not a manageable bug.

Second, a full rewrite introduces massive engineering risk without validated learning. Changing how stories surface and favoring publisher content are unproven hypotheses in this context. Building the entire new system before testing these changes violates basic product hygiene. The cost of delay is high, but the cost of building the wrong thing for two years is higher.

Third, the proposal ignores the "do nothing" or "iterative" alternatives. A safer path exists: A/B test the new surfacing algorithms on a small segment of traffic while keeping the legacy system stable. This validates whether users actually prefer the new mechanics before committing to a rewrite. Favoring publishers might drive short-term content volume but could destroy the community trust that fuels the voting engine.

The pre-mortem is clear: The launch fails because the core users reject the new voting logic, engagement plummets, and the engineering team spends months trying to patch a live system that cannot be turned off. The rival site captures the displaced community.

This plan fails the blocker test: it commits to an irreversible, high-risk execution strategy (full rewrite + forced migration) for unproven changes, ignoring cheaper, safer validation methods. The lack of a phased rollout or kill criteria for the new mechanics makes this a gamble, not a strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
