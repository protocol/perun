The claim is that offering 1GB of free storage, funded by content-matched ads, will successfully disrupt the mature email market despite high infrastructure costs and privacy concerns. The steelman: by providing an order-of-magnitude more storage than competitors (megabytes vs. gigabytes) and eliminating the need for users to delete messages, the service creates a unique value proposition that justifies ad-scanning privacy trade-offs and attracts massive scale, turning storage costs into a profitable moat via targeted advertising.

Alternatives include sticking to the industry standard of limited storage to minimize risk, or launching a paid, ad-free service. However, "do nothing" cedes the market to incumbents who are complacent about storage limits. A paid model fails to achieve the critical mass required for ad revenue. The proposed path beats "do nothing" because the cost of delay allows competitors to eventually match storage limits, eroding the first-mover advantage.

The pre-mortem: The service fails if the cost per user exceeds ad revenue before network effects kick in, or if privacy backlash triggers regulatory bans or user exodus. This is a two-way door; if adoption lags, the invitation-only limit caps liability, and the service can be pivoted or shut down with limited sunk cost compared to the potential upside. The cost of delay is high: storage prices are falling, but competitors could replicate the capacity offer once the concept is proven.

Evidence suggests storage costs are dropping rapidly, making 1GB feasible where it recently was not. The "privacy objection" is a risk, but the value of never deleting email may outweigh it for many users. The strongest attack is that scanning private correspondence for ads is a fundamental violation of trust that no amount of storage can fix. However, if the utility of search and infinite storage is sufficiently high, users may accept this bargain, as they have with other free, ad-supported services. The blocker test reveals no factual impossibility; the numbers work if adoption scales.

DECISION: GO
