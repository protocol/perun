The claim is that merging with the smaller carrier creates sufficient value to justify a $36 billion expenditure despite severe technological incompatibility. The steelman posits that acquiring the target's high-value business niche and push-to-talk dominance immediately secures a lucrative revenue stream that organic growth cannot match, making the integration pain a necessary, one-time hurdle.

However, the "better-way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the cost-benefit structure. The proposal admits the networks use incompatible technologies and the target is limited for data. In 2005, the industry trajectory is decisively moving toward data-centric 3G standards. Acquiring a legacy, voice-only network forces the buyer to operate two parallel infrastructures, doubling operational complexity while the acquired asset depreciates rapidly. The "do nothing" alternative—building a proprietary push-to-talk service on the buyer's existing modern network—avoids the $36 billion price tag and the integration risk entirely. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the sunk cost of maintaining legacy hardware.

The pre-mortem is clear: the integration drags on for years, goodwill impairment charges wipe out projected synergies, and the legacy network becomes a stranded asset as customers migrate to data-enabled devices. The primary blocker is the economic impossibility of achieving a positive return on investment when the core asset (the legacy network) has no future utility and the acquisition price ($36 billion) vastly exceeds the cost of replicating the specific feature (push-to-talk) on the surviving network. The risk is not merely "integration cost"; it is the structural obsolescence of the purchased technology.

No kill criteria can save this specific deal structure because the fundamental premise—paying a premium for a dying technology—is unsound. The buyer is purchasing a problem, not a solution. The existence of a cheaper, safer alternative (internal development) that avoids the incompatibility issue entirely mandates a rejection of this merger as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
