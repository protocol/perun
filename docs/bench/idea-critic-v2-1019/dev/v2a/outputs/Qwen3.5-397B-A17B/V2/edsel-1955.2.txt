The claim is that launching a new mid-priced division in 1957 will capture growth and fill a portfolio gap. The steelman: a dedicated brand prevents cannibalization while targeting the expanding middle market before competitors solidify dominance.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts provided in the text. First, the timing relies on a segment that "could shrink" amid a specific "recession risk." Launching a capital-intensive venture requiring new dealer infrastructure exactly when consumer spending on big-ticket items typically contracts is a fatal mismatch. Second, the plan explicitly requires building a "new dealer network." In a recessionary environment, recruiting and capitalizing independent dealers is historically difficult, creating a supply-side bottleneck that marketing cannot solve. Third, the admission of "overlap with the firm's own other brands" suggests the segmentation strategy is not distinct enough to prevent internal cannibalization, especially when total market volume drops.

The "better way" is to defer the physical rollout and dealer recruitment until the recession risk clears, or to utilize the existing dealer network with a badge-engineered stopgap to test demand without the fixed cost of new infrastructure. The current plan commits to maximum fixed costs (new dealers, new tooling) just as variable demand faces its highest probability of decline.

The pre-mortem is clear: the 1957 debut coincides with an economic downturn, dealers refuse to sign or fail financially, sales miss targets due to shrinking segment size, and the massive ad spend yields no ROI because the product is unavailable or unaffordable. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the sunk cost of a failed network launch.

This is a one-way door decision with high irreversibility. The evidence provided (recession risk, shrinking segment potential, dealer network costs) directly contradicts the viability of the proposed 1957 launch date and scope. The plan does not beat "do nothing" or "delay" on the stated facts.
