Steelman: Acquiring the agency and platform instantly grants the scale and technology needed to challenge the search leader's emerging dominance before the market consolidates.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts provided in the text. First, the **price** is explicitly flagged as a concern for a $6 billion cash outlay, representing the company's largest acquisition ever at a "large premium." In 2007, this concentration of cash risk without proven integration success threatens core liquidity. Second, there is a direct **conflict of interest**: the buyer currently serves agency customers who will now view the acquirer as a direct competitor, likely triggering immediate churn in existing high-margin revenue streams. Third, the **market volatility** is high; display-ad technology is described as "fast-changing," making a static, expensive purchase a high-risk bet compared to organic development or smaller, iterative acquisitions.

The "do nothing" or "smaller partnership" alternatives offer lower risk. The competitor's move creates urgency, but urgency does not justify a potentially fatal financial overreach. The proposal admits these critical flaws (price, conflict, tech speed) without offering mitigation strategies, kill criteria, or a staged entry plan. A plan that acknowledges existential risks in its own premise but proceeds with a "go" decision lacks the necessary safeguards for a bet of this magnitude. The conflicts alone could destroy more value than the acquisition creates by alienating the current customer base.

Because the proposal presents a "large premium" price, admitted channel conflicts, and rapid tech obsolescence risks without a structured "go-if" condition or a capped initial step, it constitutes a blocker. The risks are not merely theoretical; they are stated as immediate threats to the business model.

DECISION: NO-GO
