**Steelman:** Embedding general-purpose compute capabilities into mass-market GPUs now creates a ubiquitous, low-cost parallel computing fabric, positioning the company to dominate an emerging scientific market before rivals react, transforming a commodity hardware race into a platform monopoly.

**Skeptic:** The proposal assumes scientific users will abandon established CPU clusters for unproven, immature GPU toolchains without immediate performance guarantees. It risks bloating unit costs for gamers who derive zero value from these features, potentially losing the core market share war to a focused rival. The "niche" may remain niche forever if the software abstraction layer fails to mature.

**Better-Way:** Instead of baking expensive, unused silicon into every consumer chip immediately, the company could release a separate, high-end "compute accelerator" card for workstations and servers. This isolates the cost to willing buyers, validates the software stack with serious users, and protects the margin on mass-market gaming chips. If the software ecosystem gains traction, the features can be integrated later when transistor budgets allow without compromising the core product's price point.

**Pre-mortem:** By 2006, the toolchain remains too difficult for mainstream scientists, the added silicon cost forces a price hike that cedes the gaming market to the competitor, and the company runs out of cash funding university programs with no commercial return. The "platform" becomes a costly footnote.

**Decision:** The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" test. A dedicated accelerator card achieves the same strategic validation and ecosystem building without jeopardizing the core gaming business or inflating costs on millions of units where the feature is dead weight. The risk of distracting engineering from the primary rival during a critical architecture race is too high given the absence of committed customers. The market must be proven with a distinct product before saturating the entire supply chain.

DECISION: NO-GO
