Steelman: Leveraging existing, underutilized spiky infrastructure to create a high-margin revenue stream that offsets retail volatility, despite lacking platform experience.

**Skeptic Analysis**: The claim assumes technical capability translates to platform reliability. The company has zero experience running a developer business at scale. Incumbents (hardware/software vendors) have deep relationships and proven stacks. The market is unproven; developers may not trust a retailer with their core infrastructure. Investors are already hostile to capital spending; this requires *more* upfront investment in abstraction layers, billing, and support, potentially triggering a capital crisis.

**Better-Way**: The "do nothing" option preserves focus on the core retail margin business. Alternatively, licensing the internal tools to a trusted vendor avoids brand risk and operational distraction. Building a distinct business line diverts critical engineering talent from the primary revenue generator during a period of investor skepticism.

**Fit & Pre-mortem**: This contradicts the implicit strategy of retail dominance. If this fails (high probability given the inexperience and competition), the company faces massive stranded assets, reputational damage with enterprise clients, and accelerated investor exodus. The cost of delay is low; the market is unproven, so waiting for validation costs nothing but potential first-mover advantage, which is negligible without a proven product.

**Evidence & Blockers**: The proposal admits no experience, unproven market, and active investor opposition. These are not risks; they are **blockers**. The core claim—that this is a viable distinct business—fails because the company lacks the fundamental competency (platform operations) and the financial runway (investor trust) to survive the inevitable learning curve against established giants. The "thin margins" of retail make the capital dilution of a failed platform experiment existential.

**Kill Criteria**: A viable plan requires at least one pilot customer or internal proof of multi-tenant reliability at scale. Neither exists. The "unproven market" combined with "no experience" creates a failure mode where the company burns cash building a product no one buys, distracting from the core business until liquidity fails.

DECISION: NO-GO
