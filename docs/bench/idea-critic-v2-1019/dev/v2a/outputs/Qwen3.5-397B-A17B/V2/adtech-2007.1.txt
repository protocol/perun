**Steelman:** Acquiring the agency and platform instantly grants the technology and scale needed to challenge the search leader's recent move, preventing permanent market exclusion.

**Attack:** The $6 billion cash price is excessive for a "fast-changing" technology sector where assets depreciate rapidly. Paying a large premium for a target that may be obsolete in months creates massive downside risk. Furthermore, owning an agency directly conflicts with existing agency customers, who may defect to competitors to avoid feeding data to a rival-owned entity. The proposal relies on the assumption that current display ad technology will remain relevant long enough to justify the acquisition cost, ignoring the speed of market evolution.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "partner" option avoids the $6 billion cash outflow and customer conflict. Instead of buying the whole ecosystem, the company could license specific serving technology or form non-exclusive partnerships with multiple agencies, preserving customer trust while testing the market. A smaller, targeted acquisition of just the serving algorithm (if separable) would reduce cost and integration risk.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If the display market shifts to a new model (e.g., programmatic real-time bidding not yet mature in 2007 terms) within two years, the company is left with a $6 billion sunk cost and alienated partners. The conflict with existing customers is a structural flaw that cannot be easily remedied post-acquisition.

**Kill Criteria:** The deal fails if the premium paid exceeds the net present value of projected cash flows under a conservative adoption curve, or if more than 10% of existing agency customers threaten to leave. The proposal provides no evidence that these thresholds are met. The risk of technological obsolescence combined with channel conflict makes the "go" decision untenable without significant restructuring of the deal terms (price reduction, divestiture of the agency arm).

The plan commits real money to a high-risk, one-way door without addressing the fundamental channel conflict or verifying the longevity of the technology.

DECISION: NO-GO
