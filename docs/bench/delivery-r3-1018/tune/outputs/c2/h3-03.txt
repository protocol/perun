**Delivery Plan: Atlas Load Balancer TLS 1.3 Upgrade**

**Strategy & Constraints**
This is a high-risk migration with irreversible deployment consequences (1-hour freeze on revert). We will execute a **Blue-Green swap** during the Tuesday maintenance window. No direct production edits; all changes flow through the `network-ops` repository. The "certificate-validation" flake requires a specific retry strategy, not a bypass.

**Lane Decomposition & Ownership**

1.  **Lane A: Configuration & Synthetic Validation (Builder: Network Engineer)**
    *   **Work:** Update load balancer config to enable TLS 1.3; update CI pipeline to include TLS 1.3 synthetic traffic.
    *   **Parallelism:** Can run immediately.
    *   **Serialization:** Must complete before Lane B final sign-off.
    *   **Cost Control:** Limit synthetic runs to 2 per candidate SHA to manage the 200-credit cost.

2.  **Lane B: Downstream Certificate Verification (Owner: Product Team Leads)**
    *   **Work:** Twelve teams validate their certificates against a staging environment mirroring the new TLS 1.3 config.
    *   **Parallelism:** All 12 teams run in parallel.
    *   **Serialization:** Must provide "PASS" receipts before G8 (Release).
    *   **Trigger:** Starts once Lane A provides the staging endpoint details.

3.  **Lane C: Rollback Drill & Runbook (Owner: SRE Lead)**
    *   **Work:** Execute the blue-green revert in a pre-production sandbox; document the exact commands and timing. Verify the "1-hour freeze" trigger behavior is understood.
    *   **Parallelism:** Independent of A and B.
    *   **Requirement:** Must demonstrate a successful revert < 5 minutes.

**Gates & Checks**

*   **G1 (Spec):** Define exact TLS 1.3 cipher suites and fallback behavior. Pin base SHA of `network-ops` repo.
*   **G3 (Design):** ADR signed by Security Lead confirming cipher suite selection meets audit requirements.
*   **G4 (Implement):** Lane A commits config. CI runs (45 mins).
    *   *Flake Handling:* If `certificate-validation` fails, trigger one manual re-run. If it fails twice, halt for Network Architect analysis (do not auto-retry indefinitely).
*   **G5 (Verify):**
    *   **Code:** CI green on exact candidate SHA.
    *   **Functional:** Lane B teams submit signed receipts confirming handshake success on staging.
    *   **Resilience:** Lane C submits video/log evidence of successful rollback drill.
*   **G6 (Review):**
    *   **Security Review:** Independent Security Lead (not the Builder) verifies config diff.
    *   **Architecture Review:** Senior Network Architect (Jane Smith) reviews the change and the flake analysis.
    *   **Rule:** Builder cannot approve their own config.
*   **G7 (Integrate):** Merge to `main` by Jane Smith. This updates the Blue environment.
*   **G8 (Release - Human Gate):**
    *   **Approvers:** Security Lead AND Platform Director (Dual Sign-off required).
    *   **Action:** Traffic switch from Green (old) to Blue (new) during Tuesday window.
    *   **Condition:** All Lane B receipts present; Lane C drill passed.

**Landing & Rollback**

*   **Merge Strategy:** Single PR to `main`. No merge train needed for this atomic config change, but strict serialization of approvals.
*   **Human Decisions:**
    1.  Jane Smith approves merge (G7).
    2.  Security Lead + Platform Director authorize the traffic flip (G8).
*   **Rollback Protocol:**
    *   **Trigger:** Any spike in handshake failures or transaction blocks post-swap.
    *   **Action:** Immediate traffic reversion to Green environment (pre-TLS 1.3).
    *   **Consequence:** Acknowledge the automatic 1-hour deployment freeze. No further changes attempted until the window closes or the freeze lifts.
    *   **Owner:** SRE Lead executes; Platform Director declares the incident.

**Risk Mitigation**
*   **Flaky Check:** Explicitly budget for one re-trigger; second failure escalates to Architect.
*   **Cost:** Cap synthetic runs; rely on downstream team validation (Lane B) for broad coverage rather than exhaustive central synthetic tests.
*   **Irreversibility:** The 1-hour freeze makes the "Blue-Green" switch critical; if Blue fails, we revert to Green instantly, accepting the freeze rather than attempting a hotfix during the window.
