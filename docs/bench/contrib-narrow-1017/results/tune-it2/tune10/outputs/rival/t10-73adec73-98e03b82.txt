Do not share the sanitized OmniCorp log. Even with identifiers removed, query patterns, table schemas, and timing distributions can reveal proprietary business logic, operational scale, and internal workflows. Most open-source licenses and contributor agreements implicitly require that you own or have explicit redistribution rights for all data submitted to the repository; sharing employer data without written legal clearance violates this and risks your employment.

Instead, propose a **reproducible synthetic benchmark strategy** that validates your optimization without exposing sensitive data. Here is the safe approach:

1.  **Publish the Benchmark Harness, Not the Data:** Submit a PR that includes the *code* for the benchmark (e.g., a Rust/Go/Python script using the driver) and the exact command-line flags to run it. This satisfies the "reproducible" requirement because anyone can run the harness against *their own* data or a generated dataset.

2.  **Provide a Statistical Data Generator:** Instead of the real log, write a small utility within the benchmark harness that generates synthetic data matching the *statistical properties* of your workload without copying the actual patterns. Configure it to mimic:
    *   **Concurrency levels:** (e.g., "50 concurrent writers").
    *   **Payload sizes:** (e.g., "random blobs averaging 2KB").
    *   **Write distribution:** (e.g., "bulk inserts of 1,000 rows").
    *   **Schema complexity:** (e.g., "tables with 3 indexes").

    You do not need to perfectly replicate OmniCorp's logic; you only need to create a load profile stressful enough to trigger the lock contention you fixed. If your optimization works, it should show improvement on *any* high-contention synthetic load, not just your specific production trace.

3.  **Share Aggregated Results with Methodology:** In your PR description, present a clear table of results comparing `main` vs. your branch using the synthetic generator. Include:
    *   Throughput (ops/sec) with confidence intervals.
    *   Lock wait time reductions.
    *   The exact seed and parameters used for the synthetic generator so results are deterministic.

    Explicitly state: *"Real production data cannot be shared due to proprietary constraints. However, the included synthetic generator reproduces the high-contention conditions where the optimization yields gains. Results show a X% improvement under these modeled conditions."*

4.  **Offer a Private Verification Channel (Optional):** If maintainers remain skeptical that the synthetic data is representative, offer to run the benchmark privately against the real data and share a signed/verified log of the *raw numbers* (not the queries) via a secure channel, or invite a trusted maintainer to a sanitized screen-share session where you run the test live. This proves the result exists without transferring the data.

This approach shifts the burden from "trusting my specific data" to "verifying my code and methodology," which is the standard contract of open-source contribution. It protects your employer while giving maintainers the evidence they need to merge.
