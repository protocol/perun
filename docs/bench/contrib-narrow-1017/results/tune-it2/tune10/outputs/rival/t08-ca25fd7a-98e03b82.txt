Since the project lacks documented standards and exhibits inconsistent historical entries, you cannot infer a single "correct" format from the history alone. Instead, adopt the **safest high-quality default** for open-source release notes: prioritize **user-facing impact** with just enough technical context to explain *why* the change matters, while avoiding deep implementation specifics unless they affect configuration or migration.

Maintainers typically reject notes that are too vague ("Improved performance") or too internal ("Refactored cache invalidation logic in module X"). They expect to know: **What changed?** **Who does it affect?** and **What is the tangible benefit?**

Given your 30% performance improvement under high load, here is a draft entry designed to fit most professional cultures without requiring heavy editing. It balances the significant metric with clear scope:

***

**Draft Release Note Entry:**

> **Performance:** Significantly improved query throughput under high-load conditions by introducing a new distributed caching layer. Users experiencing latency spikes during peak traffic should see response times reduce by approximately 30%. No configuration changes or migration steps are required; the optimization is active by default.

***

**Why this works:**
1.  **Headline-first:** It starts with the category ("Performance") and the primary benefit.
2.  **Quantified Impact:** It explicitly states the "30%" metric and the specific condition ("high-load/peak traffic"), which validates the significance without needing a benchmark report in the notes.
3.  **Operational Safety:** It explicitly answers the maintainer's hidden fear: "Do I need to tell users to change their config?" by stating "No configuration changes... required."
4.  **Neutral Tone:** It avoids hyperbole ("massive," "revolutionary") and sticks to observable behavior.
