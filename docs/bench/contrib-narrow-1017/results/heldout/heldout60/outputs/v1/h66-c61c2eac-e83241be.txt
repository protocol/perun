**Strategy: Secure Internal Clearance First, Then Engage Upstream**

Do not open the pull request (PR) until you have written legal approval from Innovate Inc. Opening a PR without clearance violates your employer's policy and creates an irreversible legal risk that cannot be fixed by adding a comment later. Once code is public, it is effectively distributed; retracting it does not undo the exposure.

**Action Plan**

1.  **Initiate Internal Approval Immediately:** Submit your fix to your legal and security teams today. Explicitly state the upstream project's "10-day stale" rule in your request to see if they can expedite or provide a provisional "intent to contribute" letter.
2.  **Prepare the Contribution Locally:** While waiting, perfect your patch in a private branch. Ensure you have generalized the fix (removing all references to "Innovate Inc," internal device names, or proprietary logs) and created a synthetic reproduction case for the 50ms latency that does not rely on customer data.
3.  **Draft the PR Content:** Write your title, description, and commit messages now. Ensure they describe the technical problem and solution without revealing your employer's specific architecture or the fact that this is internal work.
4.  **Engage the Community (Optional but Recommended):** If the project allows, open a *non-code* discussion issue or visit their chat channel. Ask: "I have a fix for touch-input latency on older devices involving event loop optimization. I am finalizing internal compliance checks and expect to submit a PR in approximately 3 weeks. Is this timeline acceptable, or should I wait?" This signals intent without exposing unapproved code.
5.  **Submit Upon Clearance:** The moment you have approval, push your sanitized branch and open the PR. In the initial comment, briefly state: "This fix addresses the 50ms swipe latency on legacy devices. All corporate approvals are secured."

**Addressing Your Specific Concern**

*   **Risk of Stale Closure:** Maintainers prefer waiting for a high-quality, legally safe PR over closing a premature one. If you wait the 3–4 weeks, you miss one release cycle, but your contribution lands cleanly in the next. If you open early and get closed as stale, you create noise and potentially burn social capital.
*   **Professionalism:** It is far more professional to say, "I am securing final internal sign-off" than to open a PR and then go silent for a month because legal halted the process. Silence after opening looks like abandonment; silence before opening looks like due diligence.

*Commit Message:*
```text
fix(input): eliminate 50ms swipe latency on legacy devices

Optimizes the event loop processing order to reduce touch-input lag.
Adds a synthetic regression test verifying response time under load.

Fixes #<issue-number-if-applicable>
```

*PR Body:*
```text
## Problem
Swiping on older devices incurs a ~50ms delay due to event loop blocking.

## Solution
Reorders event processing to prioritize touch inputs during high-load cycles.

## Testing
- Verified on synthetic load tests simulating legacy hardware.
- Latency reduced from 50ms to <5ms in local benchmarks.

## Compliance
This contribution has been reviewed and approved by the author's organization.
```

**Fallback:** If maintainers indicate the fix is critical *now* and cannot wait, ask if they would accept a minimal, anonymized patch via a private security channel or email while you finish the formal process, though most projects will insist on the public PR workflow.

*Note: Ensure your "synthetic" test data contains no residual patterns from real user logs. Double-check that no internal hostnames or ticket IDs (e.g., "INNOV-123") remain in the commit history.*
