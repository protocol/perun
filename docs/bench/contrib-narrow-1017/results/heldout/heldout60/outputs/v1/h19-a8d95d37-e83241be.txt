### Follow-Up Strategy and Template

**Should you follow up?** Yes. Ten days is an appropriate window for a polite nudge, especially for a first-time contributor. Maintainers are often volunteers managing many requests; silence rarely means rejection or that your contribution is unimportant. It usually indicates bandwidth constraints.

**How to engage:**
1.  **Check for recent activity:** Ensure no new commits have been pushed to the main branch that might conflict with your change or render it obsolete.
2.  **Post a comment on your existing PR:** Do not open a new issue or email unless the project specifically requests it. Keeping the conversation threaded on the PR is standard etiquette.
3.  **Be concise and helpful:** Acknowledge their busy schedule, confirm the build status, and offer assistance if further changes are needed.

**Draft Message to Paste:**

> "Hi @maintainer-handle (or 'hi everyone'),
>
> I wanted to gently bump this PR in case it got buried in the queue. I noticed the automated build checks are passing, and I'm happy to make any adjustments if the typo fix needs reformatting or if there are other documentation sections you'd like me to review while I'm here.
>
> Thanks for your time and for maintaining AccessAid!"

**Next Steps if Silence Continues:**
If there is still no response after another two weeks, you may ask once more if there is a better channel to discuss the contribution (e.g., a community Slack, Discord, or mailing list). If there is still no movement after a month, it is acceptable to close the PR yourself with a note saying you are closing it to clean up the queue, but that you remain happy to reopen it if the team becomes available. This shows professionalism and respect for the project's hygiene.

**Engaging with the Community:**
To increase visibility and build rapport:
*   **Join their communication channels:** Many projects have a chat room where you can introduce yourself as a new contributor.
*   **Look for "good first issue" labels:** If your typo fix is stalled, picking up another small, labeled issue shows sustained interest and helps you learn their workflow.
*   **Review other PRs:** If you have the expertise, offering constructive feedback on other open PRs can get you noticed by the maintainers.

Your contribution matters; documentation accuracy is critical for accessibility libraries like AccessAid. Patience and polite persistence are key traits in open source.

---
*Note: Always verify the specific project's `CONTRIBUTING.md` file for any unique rules regarding follow-ups before posting.*
*Remember that maintainers often prioritize security fixes and major features over documentation, so delays here are common and not a reflection of quality.*
