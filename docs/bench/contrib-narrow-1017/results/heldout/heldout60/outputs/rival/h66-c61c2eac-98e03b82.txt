Instead, execute this three-step strategy to align your corporate constraints with the project's momentum:

1.  **File an Upstream Issue Immediately:** Open a detailed issue on the project tracker describing the touch-input latency bug, its impact on older devices, and your proposed solution architecture. Explicitly state in the issue body: "A fix has been developed internally but is pending final corporate legal clearance expected in 3–4 weeks. This issue tracks the upcoming contribution." This stakes your claim on the problem, alerts maintainers to the incoming work, and prevents others from duplicating effort without exposing them to unlicensed code.
2.  **Engage Maintainers Transparently:** Comment on the issue or reach out via the project's designated communication channel (e.g., Discord, mailing list) to inform the core team of your situation. Ask if they are willing to hold a placeholder or if they prefer you to submit the PR only after clearance. Most maintainers prefer knowing a fix is coming rather than seeing a stale PR appear unexpectedly.
3.  **Submit the PR Only After Clearance:** Once Innovate Inc. grants approval, immediately open the pull request referencing the issue you created. Because you have already discussed the fix and the delay, the maintainers will understand the context. Your PR will then be active, legally safe, and ready for rapid review and merging, fitting their workflow for quick fixes.

Waiting for clearance before submitting code protects both your employer and the open-source project while keeping the community informed through the issue tracker.

Note: If the release cycle is imminent and critical, ask maintainers if they can accept the fix as a patch from them directly once you provide the cleared code, though this is rare. Always prioritize legal compliance over speed to avoid revocation of your contribution later.
