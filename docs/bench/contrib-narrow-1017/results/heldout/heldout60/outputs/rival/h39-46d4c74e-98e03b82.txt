Do not share the raw trace file, even if sanitized. The presence of proprietary microservice names like "Atlas-Deploy" and "Ocean-Store" indicates the trace captures your company's specific architectural topology. Automated sanitization is high-risk; a single missed string or inferred timing pattern could leak confidential infrastructure details, violating your agreement and destroying trust with the open-source community.

Instead, adopt a **Reproducible Synthetic Benchmark** strategy. This approach decouples the *performance proof* from your *private data*.

**Step 1: Construct a Synthetic Load Test**
Write a new Go benchmark test (using `testing.B`) within the MegaBuild repository that artificially recreates the bottleneck conditions you observed.
*   If the bottleneck was "20,000 dependencies with deep circular chains," write a test generator that creates exactly that structure in memory.
*   If the issue was "slow network calls to a registry," mock the network layer to introduce the same latency profile you saw, without calling real services.
*   Ensure this test requires no external services and runs entirely locally or on any standard CI runner.

**Step 2: Document the Correlation (The "Why")**
In your PR description, explain the *class* of problem found in your internal trace without revealing the *specifics* of your infrastructure.
*   **Say:** "Internal profiling of large-scale monorepos (>5k modules) revealed that the current resolution algorithm degrades quadratically when handling deep dependency chains, costing ~20s on standard hardware."
*   **Do not say:** "We saw this on our Atlas-Deploy cluster."
*   Provide a sanitized, abstracted flame graph or timeline diagram that shows the *shape* of the delay (e.g., "Time spent in `ResolveDependencies`") but strips all labels, timestamps, and function names specific to your internal tools.

**Step 3: Execute on Public CI**
Run your new synthetic benchmark on the project's existing public Continuous Integration (GitHub Actions, CircleCI, etc.).
*   Configure the PR to output the benchmark results (`go test -bench=. -benchmem`) directly in the CI logs.
*   Compare the "before" (current main) and "after" (your PR) results within the same CI environment.
*   This provides immutable, third-party verification of the speedup. The maintainer can see the exact same numbers you see, generated on neutral ground.

**Step 4: Offer a Private Verification Channel (Optional)**
If maintainers remain skeptical about whether your synthetic test matches reality, offer a private verification call.
*   Propose a screen-share session where you run the build on your internal machine (blurring sensitive UI elements) to demonstrate the improvement live.
*   Alternatively, offer to run their specific validation scripts against your internal setup and share only the aggregated output logs (ensuring no secrets are in the logs).

**Summary of Deliverable for the PR:**
1.  **Code:** The optimization fix + a new `benchmark_test.go` that deterministically reproduces the heavy-load scenario.
2.  **Evidence:** A link to the public CI run showing the benchmark passing and the timing comparison.
3.  **Narrative:** A description explaining the algorithmic flaw discovered via internal profiling, supported by abstracted diagrams, explicitly stating that raw traces are withheld due to confidentiality.

This method proves the math without exposing the secrets. It shifts the burden of proof from "trust my hidden data" to "verify this reproducible test."

Note: Never attempt to `sed` or script a cleanup of the raw trace; context leakage via timing side-channels is too difficult to audit manually.
If the project strictly requires real-world traces, you must decline to contribute this specific fix until they accept synthetic benchmarks as valid proof.
