### Context & Next Steps

**1. Is 10 days normal?**
Yes. Ten days of silence on a small documentation fix is extremely common in open source. Maintainers are often volunteers working on nights and weekends, or they prioritize critical security patches and complex features over typo fixes. Your PR is likely not "ignored" because it is unimportant, but simply because it is easy to merge later when they have a quiet moment. Do not assume you did anything wrong; the passing build check confirms your technical submission was correct.

**2. How to engage further if there is still no response:**
If another week passes without a reply after your comment, shift your focus from "waiting for merge" to "community presence."
*   **Join their communication channel:** Check the README for a link to Discord, Slack, or a mailing list. Introduce yourself as a user from BrightFuture who wants to help. Sometimes a casual "Hey, I submitted a small docs fix, just wanted to say hi" in a general channel works better than formal PR comments.
*   **Look for "Good First Issues":** Browse their issue tracker for tags like `good first issue` or `help wanted`. Commenting on an open issue with a proposed solution shows you are interested in the project's long-term health, not just a one-off fix. This often prompts maintainers to notice your previous PR organically.
*   **Be patient:** Some projects operate on a "release train" model where they batch small changes together once a month. Your PR might simply be waiting for the next scheduled release cycle.
