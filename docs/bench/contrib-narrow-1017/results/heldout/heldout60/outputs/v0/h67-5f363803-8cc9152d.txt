You cannot structure a single commit that simultaneously lists your colleague as an author in the metadata and keeps their identity hidden from the project maintainers. Git commit metadata is immutable once pushed; if you include their name, it is public. If you omit it, you violate the project's attribution policy.

The only viable path that respects both constraints is to **separate the technical contribution from the historical record of authorship** through a two-step process involving a trusted intermediary (you) and a private disclosure to the maintainers:

1.  **The Commit:** Author the commit solely under your own name and email. In the commit message body, explicitly state: *"This fix was developed in collaboration with a contributor who wishes to remain anonymous for personal reasons. Full details of authorship have been disclosed privately to the maintainers."* This satisfies the technical requirement of having an accountable author (you) while signaling that the provenance is more complex.

2.  **The Private Disclosure:** Before or immediately upon opening the Pull Request, send a private, encrypted message (or use a confidential channel specified by the maintainers) to the project lead or security team. In this message:
    *   Confirm the specific contributions of your colleague.
    *   Explain their request for anonymity.
    *   Request that they be recorded in the project's *internal* contributor ledger (if one exists) or simply acknowledged in a non-public maintainer log, rather than in the public git history.
    *   Ask for their explicit permission to proceed with the commit under your name only, waiving the strict "all authors in commit metadata" rule for this specific case due to extraordinary circumstances.

3.  **The Pull Request Description:** In the public PR description, reiterate the commit message's note about anonymous collaboration. Do not hint at the name. State clearly: *"Per private communication with the maintainers, the co-author of this fix has requested anonymity. The maintainers have been briefed on the full authorship details and have authorized this submission format."*

This approach shifts the burden of "accurate attribution" from the public git log (which cannot be anonymized) to the maintainers' private records. It honors your colleague's wish by keeping their name out of the immutable public history, while honoring the project's integrity by ensuring the maintainers *know* the truth and have explicitly waived the standard rule for this instance. You become the public face of the code, accepting full responsibility, while the maintainers hold the confidential truth.

If the maintainers refuse to waive the rule or accept private disclosure, you must respect their policy over your colleague's wish; in that case, you cannot submit the fix without your colleague's explicit permission to be named, as submitting it would violate the project's governance. Do not attempt to use pseudonyms or fake emails in the commit metadata, as this violates the integrity of the git history and likely the project's code of conduct.

Note: This solution relies entirely on the maintainers' willingness to accept a private waiver.
