Do not close your current pull request or open new ones yet. Closing a large, existing PR to replace it with five new ones often creates more noise, confuses the review history, and can appear disruptive to maintainers who are already overwhelmed. Instead, adopt a "top-down" review strategy to unblock the process while respecting the maintainer's time.

First, post a concise, high-level comment on the existing PR. Acknowledge the size of the change and explicitly state that you understand it is difficult to review in one sitting. Offer a structured breakdown of the PR into the five logical sections you identified, summarizing the purpose and scope of each section in bullet points. Ask the maintainer if they would prefer to review these sections sequentially in the current thread or if they would prefer you to split the PR into separate branches based on that structure. This shifts the decision to them without adding administrative burden.

Second, offer to walk them through the architecture. Propose a brief, asynchronous explanation (a written summary or a short screen-recording link) that explains the "why" and the high-level design before they dive into the code. Often, maintainers delay large reviews because they lack the mental context to start; providing a clear roadmap reduces the activation energy required to begin.

Third, if there has been no response to your offer of help after a few days, send a single, polite ping. Frame it around helping them save time: "I know this is a large change. I've broken down the review into five logical chunks in the comment above. Would you like me to split these into separate PRs to make the review easier, or is it better to keep them here?"

Only split the PR if the maintainer explicitly agrees or if they suggest it. If they agree, then close the original PR with a link to the new, smaller PRs, explaining clearly that this is done per their request to facilitate easier review. This approach demonstrates empathy for their workload, proves you are organized, and keeps the momentum moving without risking the appearance of being pushy or disorganized.

Note: Large architectural changes in Rust often require significant mental load to verify safety and correctness; making the reviewer's job easier is the fastest path to a merge.
Never assume silence is rejection; overwhelmed maintainers often appreciate proactive offers to reduce review complexity.
