First call: GO. The hybrid form factor directly addresses the erosion of the handheld line by phones while leveraging the maker's strong first-party IP, turning a hardware disadvantage into a unique market position.

**Steelman:** A differentiated hybrid device leveraging exclusive software to create a new category, accepting lower raw power in exchange for versatility and portability, thereby recapturing users lost to mobile devices.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue losing handheld share to phones; brand relevance fades.
2.  **High-power home console only:** Directly compete on specs with rivals; high risk of repeating previous financial losses due to poor differentiation.
3.  **Hybrid (Proposed):** Creates a blue ocean between mobile and home console; relies on software strength to offset hardware modesty.

**Fit:** Aligns with the need to stop handheld erosion and recover from home console failure by avoiding direct spec wars.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if third-party support is so weak the library feels empty, or if the "hybrid" message confuses consumers like the previous launch. However, a thin first-year lineup from strong internal studios mitigates the software risk initially. The "confusing positioning" risk is real but manageable if the value proposition (play anywhere) is demonstrated clearly.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits weak specs and limited third-party support but counters with strong first-party studios. The price parity with powerful rivals is a risk, but justified by the unique form factor.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (weak specs, confusion, supply) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The modest chip is a deliberate trade-off for battery life and cost, not an oversight. The "thin" lineup is typical for year one if the first-party titles are hits.

**Risks:** Third-party adoption lagging; consumer confusion on value vs. power; supply chain unable to meet launch demand.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders do not exceed the previous console's launch by 20% within 30 days, or if attach rate for first-party titles at launch is below 0.8, pivot marketing or adjust production. Leading metric: Weekly sell-through vs. supply.

DECISION: GO
