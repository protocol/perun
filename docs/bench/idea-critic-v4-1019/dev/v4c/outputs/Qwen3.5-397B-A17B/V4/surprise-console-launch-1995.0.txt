First call: NO-GO. Launching early with high price, scarce software, and alienated retailers contradicts the core requirements for console adoption, prioritizing a symbolic head-start over market viability.

**Steelman:** An early, limited launch secures mindshare and developer focus before the competitor arrives, establishing a "first-mover" narrative despite logistical friction.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** At $399 versus the rival's $299, the value proposition collapses without a killer app library. Early adopters tolerate bugs, not empty shelves at a premium.
2.  **Channel Poisoning:** Shipping to "a few retailers" guarantees retaliation from excluded major chains (e.g., KB, Toys "R" Us). In 1995, shelf space is the primary bottleneck; burning bridges before the war begins is fatal.
3.  **Developer Reality:** If the machine is hard to program and the date moved up four months, the game gap widens, not shrinks. A console without games is a paperweight.
4.  **Strategic Fit:** The goal is market dominance, not trade show buzz. Being first to market with an inferior product cements a reputation for failure that price cuts later cannot fix.

**Pre-mortem:** By Christmas 1995, the system is labeled "overpriced and empty." Major retailers refuse to stock the holiday bundle. Developers miss the holiday window entirely due to the rushed timeline. The competitor launches in September with a lower price and 20 titles, capturing the entire holiday season.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price/Content Mismatch:** $100 premium with fewer games than the rival creates an immediate rejection by the mass market.
*   **Channel Hostility:** Alienating key distribution partners before the main launch removes the ability to scale during the critical holiday quarter.
*   **Developer Capacity:** The technical difficulty combined with a rushed deadline ensures the software library will be critically thin at launch and remain so through the holidays.

**Risks:** Brand damage, cash flow burn on unsold inventory, loss of third-party trust.

**Kill Criteria:** If sell-through at select retailers is <60% in the first 30 days, or if two major national chains publicly refuse to carry the system by July, the full launch must be delayed or repriced immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
