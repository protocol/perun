First call: NO-GO. The price is astronomical for an asset with zero revenue, active legal threats, and unproven economics.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant platform instantly secures the category leader, neutralizes a future competitor, and captures the emerging shift to online video before traditional media adapts.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financials:** $1.65 billion for a company with negligible revenue implies a bet on a non-existent ad model. If video CPMs remain low or bandwidth costs outpace ad yield, this destroys shareholder value.
2.  **Legal:** The core inventory relies on copyrighted material. Media companies are already litigious. If courts rule against "fair use" for user uploads, the platform's library could be legally stripped, leaving the buyer with a hollow shell and massive liability.
3.  **Alternatives:** The buyer has its own smaller service. Continuing organic growth or licensing content avoids the binary risk of a $1.65B lawsuit exposure. "Doing nothing" preserves capital while the legal landscape clarifies.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume it is 2008. The courts rule that hosting copyrighted clips without licenses is illegal. The site is forced to remove 80% of its content. User engagement collapses. The stock price tanks, and the acquisition is written down as a total loss.

**Blockers:**
*   **Valuation Mismatch:** No revenue trajectory justifies $1.65B; the math relies entirely on speculative future ad dominance which is unproven.
*   **Existential Legal Risk:** The business model currently depends on potential copyright infringement. A single adverse ruling could invalidate the asset's primary value driver (the content library).

**Risks:** Bandwidth costs scaling faster than revenue; failure to monetize long-form content; cultural clash integrating a tiny team.

**Verdict:** The proposal asks to pay a premium for a company whose legal foundation is currently under direct attack and whose revenue model is theoretical. The risk is not just financial loss, but existential legal liability that could cripple the buyer. No kill criteria or capped pilot can mitigate the binary nature of the copyright threat at this purchase price.

DECISION: NO-GO
