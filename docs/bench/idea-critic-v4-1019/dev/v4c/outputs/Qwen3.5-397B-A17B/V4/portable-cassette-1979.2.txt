First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on unproven demand for a device that removes core functionality (recording) and social utility (speakers), while requiring a massive, irreversible capital commitment based solely on cost targets rather than validated sales.

**Steelman:** A low-cost, portable, personal audio device leveraging existing components can unlock a new youth market segment that values mobility and privacy over recording capability, achieving dominance through price and form factor before competitors react.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Contradictory Evidence:** Dealers and staff, the primary interface to the market, explicitly doubt the value proposition of a non-recording player. Ignoring this frontline intelligence without counter-evidence is negligent.
2.  **Unverified Demand:** The proposal admits "no market research shows demand." Proceeding directly to a "large" production run violates basic inventory risk management. The cost advantage is irrelevant if the units do not sell.
3.  **Copy Risk:** Since the device uses existing parts, competitors can replicate the design immediately upon launch. Without a proven brand moat or first-mover sales velocity (which is currently hypothetical), price wars will erode the margin needed to justify the large run.

**Risks:**
*   Social stigma of "antisocial" headphone use may limit adoption beyond a niche.
*   Large initial run creates catastrophic obsolescence risk if the format or trend shifts.

**Analysis:**
The proposal attempts to solve a cost problem (needing a large run for price) by creating a market problem (selling an unproven, functionally reduced device). The absence of recording and speakers removes the two primary use cases of the era. While the "personal music" angle is a valid hypothesis, the strategy skips the necessary validation step. A large production run is a one-way door; entering it without a pilot, pre-orders, or smaller test batch is financially reckless. The "cheaper alternative" is a small pilot run to validate the "antisocial" concern and actual youth willingness to pay, even if unit costs are initially higher.

**Kill Criteria (if proceeded):**
*   Stop if pre-launch reservations do not cover 40% of the first run.
*   Pivot if dealer uptake is below 50% in the first month.

The lack of demand verification combined with the high stakes of a large production run constitutes a factual blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
