First call: NO-GO. Launching a premium-priced tablet with a tiny app catalog against a dominant incumbent, while the OS has negligible share and leadership is unstable, ignores the primary driver of tablet adoption: ecosystem depth.

Steelman: The hardware quality and brand equity can overcome initial app scarcity if marketing convinces developers and users to jumpstart a new platform immediately.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High price, small catalog, heavy ad spend. High cost, extreme risk, low reversibility once inventory is built.
2. **Do Nothing:** Wait for OS maturity or leadership stability. Zero cost, preserves capital, misses immediate window but avoids catastrophic failure.
3. **Low-Price Entry:** Subsidize hardware to gain share, monetize later. High cost, high risk, but addresses the "unproven" barrier better than ads alone.

Fit: Assuming the north star is market share, launching a "me-too" product at a premium price without the key feature (apps) contradicts basic category dynamics.

Pre-mortem: The tablet launches; reviewers praise the hardware but pan the lack of apps. Consumers, seeing no reason to switch from the leader for equal money, ignore it. Developers see low sales and deprioritize the platform. The company is stuck with unsold inventory and a damaged brand perception just as leadership transitions conclude.

Evidence: The proposal admits the app catalog is a "small fraction" and the OS has "almost no market share." History shows tablets are ecosystem plays, not just hardware specs. The "small catalog" is a structural blocker for a premium price point, not a marketing challenge.

Blockers:
1. **Price-to-Value Mismatch:** Charging market-leading prices for a fraction of the utility (apps) is a factual error in value proposition.
2. **Ecosystem Chicken-and-Egg:** Heavy ad spend cannot instantly manufacture the app density required to retain users; the "small catalog" is a hard constraint.

Risks: Leadership transition distraction; shopper perception of "unproven" status.

The plan fails because it attempts to solve an ecosystem deficit with advertising while pricing for an ecosystem surplus. Without a lower price point or a significantly larger app lineup, the unit economics and adoption curve do not work.

DECISION: NO-GO
