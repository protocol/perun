First call: NO-GO. The product fails its core promise of immersion due to severe ergonomic and visual limitations that directly contradict market expectations for a "new generation" experience.

Steelman: A low-cost ($180), intermediate device leveraging existing tech to fill a portfolio gap between handheld and home consoles.
Attack: The steelman collapses under the specific constraints. A "tabletop" device requiring an eyepiece destroys portability, alienating the handheld demographic, while the fixed position frustrates home users expecting social play. Red monochrome graphics in 1995 are a hard sell against emerging 16-bit color competitors.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Preserve brand reputation; wait for the true next-gen home console in two years.
2. True Portable: Refine the existing handheld line with better battery/screen.
3. Proposed Plan: High risk of brand damage, low replay value due to eye strain, and a software library too thin to sustain hardware sales.

Fit: This does not move the north star of "immersive entertainment." It actively detracts from it by causing physical discomfort (headaches/strain). A product that physically hurts the user cannot succeed as a primary entertainment device.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, reviewers pan the uncomfortable form factor and headache-inducing visuals, sales stall at 1/10th of projections, and the brand is associated with "gimmick" rather than innovation. This is a one-way door for brand perception; once labeled a failure, recovering trust for the subsequent home console becomes harder.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the critical flaws: non-portable, uncomfortable, health risks (eye strain), and no software library. These are not hypothetical risks; they are stated features of the design.

Blockers:
1. Health/Safety: Stated risk of eye strain and headaches is a direct blocker for a mass-market consumer electronics device.
2. Value Proposition: "Neither portable nor comfortable" means it fits no use case. It loses the handheld's mobility and the home console's comfort/social aspect.
3. Content Gap: "Few games at launch" ensures immediate consumer rejection.

Risks: Brand dilution, inventory write-downs, distraction from the upcoming true home console.

The stated facts confirm the product fails to deliver a viable user experience. The health concerns and lack of comfort are fatal flaws that cannot be mitigated by a low price point.

DECISION: NO-GO
