First call: NO-GO. The proposal compounds a recent 60% price hike with immediate, high-friction operational fragmentation (two logins, two bills, separate brands), ignoring the primary risk of customer confusion during a period of extreme price sensitivity.

**Steelman:** Separating the declining DVD asset from the growth-oriented streaming business allows distinct strategic focus and prevents the legacy model from dragging down the valuation and agility of the future platform.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing & Compounding Friction:** Executing a complex brand and billing split immediately after a massive price increase is catastrophic for retention. Customers angry about the cost will face the added insult of fractured utility (lost shared queues, dual logins). This validates the "reaction to price increase" risk as a near-certain churn event, not a hypothetical.
2.  **Value Destruction:** Severing data sharing eliminates cross-service recommendations, degrading the user experience for the remaining hybrid users without offering a compensatory benefit.
3.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "internal separation only" options achieve the management focus goal without imposing customer-facing friction. A full consumer-facing rebrand is unnecessary to manage the DVD decline internally.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal introduces high-friction changes (dual bills/logins) precisely when customer goodwill is at its lowest post-price-hike.
*   **Contradiction:** The rationale cites "customer confusion" as a risk, yet the solution *creates* that confusion by design rather than mitigating it.
*   **Missing Mitigation:** No plan exists to migrate users or soften the blow of the dual-system requirement.

**Risks:** Mass churn, brand damage, and permanent loss of cross-platform data synergy. The lack of a pilot or phased rollout for such a fundamental account structure change makes this a one-way door with unquantified downside.

**Kill Criteria:** If the separation proceeds, it must not occur until price sensitivity stabilizes, and it must maintain a unified login/billing layer even if backend operations diverge. As proposed, it fails the basic test of not alienating the entire existing base during a pricing crisis.

DECISION: NO-GO
