First call: GO. The core claim is that a mandatory client solves critical update and cheating failures that retail distribution cannot address, creating a platform opportunity.

**Steelman:** A proprietary client is the only viable mechanism to enforce real-time integrity checks and deliver seamless patches, turning a distribution liability into a recurring revenue platform.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Continue losing sales to cheaters and failing patch delivery; eventual brand erosion.
*   **Status Quo (Retail Only):** Cannot solve the technical constraint of updating boxed software remotely.
*   **The Proposal:** Forces friction (accounts, downloads) but secures the product's future and opens a high-margin store ecosystem.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** In 2003, broadband is growing but not universal; however, the target demographic (PC gamers) leads adoption. Failure mode: The client launches buggy, alienating the core base before the store opens. This is a two-way door; if the client fails, the company can revert to retail-only patches for legacy titles, though new releases would suffer.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal states the current struggle: "struggles to deliver game updates and to fight cheating." No alternative exists to fix this without online connectivity. The risks (player dislike, retail pushback, broadband gaps) are execution challenges, not fundamental blockers. Retail partners dropping boxes is a risk, but the long-term shift to digital is inevitable; delaying accelerates obsolescence. The "mandatory" nature is the feature, not a bug, required to enforce the anti-cheat logic.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The stated problem (cannot update/stop cheats) is unsolvable without this mechanism.
*   **Risks:** Adoption friction, initial bugs, partner retaliation. These are managed via kill criteria, not reasons to stop.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If the client causes a >20% drop in active users within 6 months due to stability, pause mandatory enforcement for new titles. Leading metric: Monthly Active Users (MAU) on the client vs. cheat reports.

The absence of universal broadband is a constraint to manage (e.g., offline modes where possible), not a reason to abandon the only solution to the stated existential threats. The potential store upside dwarfs the transition costs.

DECISION: GO
