First call: GO. The friction of per-order fees directly suppresses repeat purchase frequency, which is the primary lever for lifetime value in retail.

Steelman: An annual prepaid fee aligns customer incentives with the retailer's goal of frequency, locking in loyalty and smoothing cash flow despite short-term margin compression.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (margin hit, heavy usage, irreversibility) are inherent to the business model shift, not factual contradictions. The economics are unproven *because* it has not been tried; waiting for proof before starting guarantees the status quo continues. No alternative reaches the same upside of eliminating the primary friction point.

**Risks:** 
1. Heavy buyers may cause unit-economic losses on individual orders.
2. Short-term earnings will drop due to shipping cost absorption.
3. Reversal is difficult once members join.

These are managed via kill criteria, not by stopping the initiative. The "do nothing" option guarantees continued friction and lost share to any competitor who moves first. The cost of delay is the permanent loss of the most valuable customer segment.

**Kill Criteria:**
1. **Metric:** If the ratio of shipping cost to membership revenue exceeds 1.2x within the first 90 days for the pilot cohort, pause new signups.
2. **Pivot:** If repeat order frequency does not increase by at least 20% within six months, revert to per-order pricing for non-renewals.
3. **Cap:** Limit the initial launch to a specific geographic region or customer tier to contain exposure.

The proposal correctly identifies the core friction. The risks are real but quantifiable and manageable through a staged rollout. The potential reward—transforming transactional buyers into loyal subscribers—outweighs the short-term margin hit. Refusing to test this due to unproven economics is a failure to innovate.

DECISION: GO
