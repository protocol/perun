First call: GO. The proposal leverages existing assets to address an unmet need for families, despite stated risks.

**Steelman:** Reuse the compact-car platform and a converted factory to launch a low-cost, car-like minivan that fits in garages, creating a new category for families before cash runs out.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited are unproven hypotheses, not factual contradictions.
- *No category exists:* This is an opportunity, not a blocker; families currently compromise with wagons or oversized vans.
- *Little cash:* The plan specifically mitigates this by reusing platforms and converting factories rather than building new ones.
- *Commercial perception:* Mitigated by designing it to "drive like a car" and fit home garages.
- *Rivals copying:* First-mover advantage in a new segment creates brand ownership before copycats arrive.
- *Space limits:* A garage-sized vehicle inherently trades maximum cargo for usability; this is a feature, not a failure.

**Risks:** Market education costs, potential platform constraints on future scaling, and speed of competitor response. These become kill criteria: if early pilot sales do not show >60% family (non-commercial) uptake within 6 months, or if production costs exceed the compact-car baseline by >15%, pivot or stop.

**Alternatives:**
1. *Do nothing:* Continue selling wagons/vans; likely loss of market share as needs evolve.
2. *New platform:* Too expensive given cash constraints.
3. *Commercial van only:* Misses the family demographic entirely.

The proposal beats "do nothing" by creating a high-upside market with limited capital expenditure. The lack of a current category is the precise gap this vehicle fills. The constraints (cash, platform) are addressed by the specific tactics (conversion, reuse). No factual evidence provided suggests the target users (families) reject the concept; the fear is speculative. Given the company's precarious state, the cost of delay (continued irrelevance) outweighs the risk of execution.

DECISION: GO
