First call: GO. The stated facts show a search-ad company with no hardware business betting that free, open software will push mobile web use and thus search ad revenue. This aligns with the core business model of increasing search volume, even without a direct hardware revenue stream.

Steelman: Acquiring the team and their open-source OS provides the only viable path to influence the mobile ecosystem, ensuring future search accessibility as the market shifts from desktop to mobile, despite immediate adoption hurdles.

Alternatives like licensing an existing OS or building one internally are either non-existent (no suitable open mobile OS in 2005) or too slow/costly given the secretive head-start of the target. Doing nothing cedes the emerging mobile search channel to entrenched vendors who may not prioritize open web access.

Pre-mortem: The project fails if carriers and handset makers successfully block the OS, leaving the company with a costly, unused codebase. However, the "open-source" nature lowers the barrier for entry for smaller players desperate for an alternative to dominant vendors, creating a wedge. The cost of delay is losing the first-mover advantage in defining the mobile web stack.

Evidence: The proposal explicitly states the bet is on "mobile web use and thus search ad revenue." The $50M price tag for a six-person team is high but represents a capped, time-boxed bet on a potential paradigm shift. The lack of a direct revenue model for the OS itself is irrelevant; the OS is a loss leader for the core search business.

Blockers: NONE. The risks (adoption, carrier resistance, long payback) are valid but do not contradict the core strategic logic. They are execution risks, not fundamental flaws in the thesis that mobile search will be valuable.

Risks: High execution difficulty, potential for zero market share, multi-year cash burn with no guaranteed return.

Kill Criteria: If, within 3 years, the OS fails to secure partnership with at least one major handset maker or carrier to ship devices, the project must be pivoted or shut down. Leading metric: Number of active devices running the OS shipping quarterly.

DECISION: GO
