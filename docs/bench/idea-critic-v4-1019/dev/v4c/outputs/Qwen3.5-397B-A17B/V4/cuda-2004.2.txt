First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits massive, certain costs to every unit sold against a non-existent market, threatening the core business in a fierce competitive race.

**Steelman:** Betting the company's margin leadership on creating a general-purpose computing market from scratch, despite zero current revenue, to secure a dominant position if that market emerges.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain cost leadership in gaming; risk being disrupted if GPU computing takes off elsewhere.
2.  **Niche approach:** Build programmable features only for high-margin workstation cards, limiting unit cost impact.
3.  **Proposed plan:** Subsidize a speculative ecosystem across all mass-market chips.
The proposed plan fails to beat "do nothing" or the "niche approach" on current facts. The cost is universal; the benefit is hypothetical.

**Fit:** This contradicts the immediate survival need: winning the core gaming architecture race. Diverting engineering focus and silicon area to an unproven segment weakens the primary revenue engine.

**Pre-mortem:** The program burns cash for years; the compiler remains immature; academic interest fails to convert to enterprise spend; the core gaming product loses performance parity due to divided engineering focus; the stock collapses. This is a one-way door with a high price of delay only if the competitor launches first (which they haven't).

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "no large paying market exists." Costs are certain (silicon area, toolchain, education); rewards are speculative. The math does not work: you cannot justify raising unit costs on 100% of volume for 0% of confirmed demand.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Economic mismatch:** Universal cost increase for a product with zero confirmed buyers violates basic capital allocation.
2.  **Strategic distraction:** Engineering resources are finite; splitting focus during a "core race" invites defeat in the only market that currently pays.

**Risks:** Missing a future paradigm shift; academic experiments stalling. These are risks, but they do not justify the blocker.

**Decision:** The plan as proposed is financially reckless. It requires a capped, time-boxed pilot on high-end silicon only, not a mass-market rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
