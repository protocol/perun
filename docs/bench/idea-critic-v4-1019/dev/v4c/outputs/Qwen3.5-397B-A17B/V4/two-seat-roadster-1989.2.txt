First call: GO. The market gap for an affordable, lightweight roadster is explicit, and the "dead niche" perception by mass-market makers creates a first-mover advantage for a mid-size competitor.

**Steelman:** A focused, low-complexity vehicle leveraging existing front-engine/rear-drive components can profitably serve a neglected enthusiast segment without requiring heritage or massive volume.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Skeptic:** The claim that safety rules make convertibles "costly" is a relative, not absolute, barrier. If the car is lightweight and simple, it can absorb compliance costs better than a complex grand tourer. The risk of fading sales is real but applies to any niche product; it is mitigated by low development overhead.
*   **Better-Way:** "Do nothing" preserves capital but cedes the segment permanently. A heavy, feature-bloated sports car would fail on cost and weight. The proposed "affordable, lightweight" approach is the only viable alternative to doing nothing.
*   **Fit:** This moves the north star of "profitable niche dominance" by targeting a segment competitors have abandoned.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the car becomes too heavy or expensive trying to mimic luxury rivals. The solution is strict adherence to the "lightweight" constraint.
*   **Evidence:** The proposal states the segment has shrunk, but this implies reduced competition, not zero demand. The lack of heritage is irrelevant if the price-to-fun ratio is superior.

**Blockers:** None. The stated risks (shrinking segment, safety costs, no heritage) are known variables that the "affordable/lightweight" strategy directly addresses. They are risks, not factual contradictions.
**Risks:** Sales volume may be lower than mainstream models; brand perception may take time to build. These are managed by keeping the project scope small and time-boxed.

**Decision:** The plan beats "do nothing" by capturing an uncontested niche with a tailored product. The risks are executional, not fundamental flaws in the premise.

**Kill Criteria:** Halt if prototype weight exceeds target by >15% or if pre-order deposits fail to cover 20% of tooling costs within 6 months of reveal.

DECISION: GO
