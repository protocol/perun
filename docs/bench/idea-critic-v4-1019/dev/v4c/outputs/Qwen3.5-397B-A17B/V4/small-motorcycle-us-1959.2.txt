First call: GO. The proposal targets an unmet need (commuter transport) in a market saturated only with large, image-heavy machines, creating a blue-ocean opportunity despite the risks.

**Steelman:** By bypassing traditional motorcycle dealers and targeting non-riders (students/commuters) via sporting-goods stores, the company creates a new category ("easy riding") rather than fighting for share in the existing "heavy motorcycle" market.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Market Fit:** The risk that Americans favor big bikes applies only to current motorcycle owners. The proposal explicitly targets non-owners (commuters/students) for whom size is a liability, not an asset.
2.  **Brand Unknown:** Entering via sporting-goods stores decouples the product from the "rough" motorcycle image, allowing the brand to be defined by utility and fun rather than heritage.
3.  **Mechanical Risk:** The concern about larger models failing at highway speeds is irrelevant to the 50cc unit, which is designed for city speeds. The proposal does not rely on the larger bikes for this initial foothold.
4.  **Currency Risk:** While real, the capital required for a subsidiary and limited inventory is likely lower than the opportunity cost of ignoring a potential mass market. The risk is finite; the upside is uncapped.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Cedes the US market entirely; maintains status quo of shrinking relevance outside Japan.
*   *Export large bikes only:* Direct competition with entrenched US/UK brands; high probability of failure due to brand obscurity and product mismatch.
*   *Licensing:* Loses control of brand positioning and margins; fails to solve the "image" problem.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the company listens to traditional dealers who insist on selling only to existing bikers, or if they try to market the 50cc as a "real motorcycle" instead of a practical appliance. The kill criterion is clear: if sales to non-traditional outlets do not materialize within 12 months, pivot or exit.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks are manageable hypotheses, not factual contradictions. The mechanical concern applies to the wrong product segment.
**Risks:** Brand perception, currency fluctuation, distribution execution. These are mitigated by the non-traditional channel strategy.

The proposal correctly identifies that the competition is not other motorcycles, but cars, bicycles, and public transit. The channel strategy is the key differentiator that neutralizes the "rough image" risk.

DECISION: GO
