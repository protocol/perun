First call: NO-GO. The price is extreme relative to output, and key risks are untreated.

Steelman: Acquiring the studio secures a proven hit-making engine and prevents rival alignment, but only if the $7.4B price buys scalable IP leverage rather than just one film per year, and if creative leadership integration avoids culture collapse.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Valuation Mismatch:** At ~$7.4B for one film/year, the deal implies a valuation multiple that requires immediate, unproven scaling of output to justify. The proposal offers no mechanism to increase production cadence without diluting the very quality being purchased.
2.  **Key Person Dependency:** The value resides entirely in specific creative leaders and a founder who becomes a major shareholder. There is no "key person" insurance, retention lock-up, or succession plan detailed. If they leave or underperform, the asset value evaporates.
3.  **Culture Clash:** Merging a struggling hand-drawn unit with a dominant CGI studio carries high integration risk. The proposal lacks a specific operating model to prevent the acquired talent from fleeing due to bureaucratic friction.

**Risks:**
*   Shareholder dilution giving the founder undue influence over conglomerate strategy.
*   Stock-price volatility affecting the all-stock deal value during closing.
*   Failure to replicate past success under new corporate ownership.

**Better Way:**
Instead of an outright buy, negotiate a profit-sharing extension or a minority stake with first-look distribution rights. This captures upside and blocks rivals at a fraction of the capital risk, avoiding the integration nightmare until production scalability is proven under the new terms.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   **Stop** if a binding retention agreement for all top creative talent is not signed prior to announcement.
*   **Pivot** if production output does not increase to 2 films/year within 24 months.
*   **Metric:** Box office return on invested capital (ROIC) must exceed 15% annually; otherwise, divest.

The current proposal treats a fragile, people-dependent creative shop as a stable industrial asset at a peak valuation. Without a plan to scale output or lock talent, this is a speculative bet, not a strategic acquisition.

DECISION: NO-GO
