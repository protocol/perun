First call: GO. The current roadmap fails the core requirement for thin, efficient laptops, and the gap versus the dominant competitor is widening; staying the course guarantees product irrelevance.

**Steelman:** Transitioning to the dominant architecture is the only viable path to regain performance-per-watt leadership, with a translation layer mitigating immediate software incompatibility while the ecosystem migrates.

**Attack & Options:**
The risks are real but manageable compared to the status quo. Announcing early risks sales freezing, but hiding the transition risks a chaotic, reactive launch later. Developer rebuilds are inevitable; the translation layer buys time, not permanent compatibility. Performance loss in translation is a tax users will pay for access to modern hardware efficiency, provided native apps follow quickly. Brand distinctiveness shifts from "unique chip" to "best thin laptop," a stronger market position. The pain of a prior transition is a sunk cost; the pain of falling behind is existential.

Alternatives:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Continue shipping warm, sluggish laptops. Result: Market share collapse as competitors offer superior form factors.
2.  **Dual Architecture:** Maintain both lines. Result: doubled engineering complexity, diluted focus, and continued lag in the primary line.
3.  **Wait for Supplier:** Hope the current supplier catches up. Result: High probability of failure given the decade-long stagnation.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the north star of delivering the best personal computers. If this fails, it will be because the translation layer performance was unacceptable or the timeline slipped, allowing competitors to solidify their lead. This is a one-way door; once switched, returning is impossible. The cost of delay is losing the next holiday season to rivals with superior thermal profiles.

**Blockers & Risks:**
Blockers: NONE. The proposal acknowledges the risks and offers a mitigation strategy (translation layer).
Risks: Sales freeze during announcement, developer friction, translation overhead. These are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The proposal includes a clear timeline (two years) and a technical bridge.

DECISION: GO
