First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores the core behavioral reality of the existing customer base and lacks any validation mechanism.

**Steelman:** Rebranding from a discount-driven model to everyday low pricing (EDLP) with branded shops will attract new, higher-margin shoppers and simplify operations, offsetting the loss of coupon-clippers.

**Attack:**
1.  **Customer Mismatch:** The customer base is explicitly "older" and "coupon-driven," with most sales historically occurring at a discount. These customers are trained to wait for promotions; removing coupons does not shift their preference, it removes their reason to buy. EDLP works only when customers trust the baseline price or value convenience over hunting deals. This demographic values the "thrill" and perceived savings of coupons.
2.  **Execution Risk:** The plan rolls out chain-wide immediately without a pilot. Given the high risk of alienating the core revenue source, a "big bang" approach exposes the entire company to catastrophic failure if the hypothesis is wrong. A pilot in 5-10 stores would provide essential data on elasticity and churn before committing the whole chain.
3.  **Leadership Fit:** The CEO's success in electronics (high turnover, tech-focused, different demographic) does not guarantee success in mid-market apparel/home goods with an aging demographic. The contexts are dissimilar.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Continue declining sales but preserve the core customer base while testing incremental changes.
*   *Pilot Program:* Test the new pricing and shop-in-shop concept in a limited region to measure actual churn vs. new acquisition before full rollout.
*   *Hybrid Model:* Reduce coupon frequency gradually rather than eliminating them instantly, allowing customers to adjust.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales have collapsed 40%. The core customers stopped shopping because prices felt "full" without coupons, and the new target demographic did not materialize quickly enough to fill the gap. The company ran out of cash to revert the strategy.

**Blockers:**
*   The proposal eliminates the primary purchase trigger (coupons) for the segment responsible for "most" sales, with no evidence this segment will accept EDLP.
*   Zero pilot testing for a strategy that fundamentally alters the value proposition for the entire existing base.

**Risks:** Brand confusion, immediate revenue cliff, inability to revert quickly due to physical store rebuilds.

**Kill Criteria:** If a 3-store pilot shows a >15% drop in transaction volume within 90 days, halt expansion.

DECISION: NO-GO
