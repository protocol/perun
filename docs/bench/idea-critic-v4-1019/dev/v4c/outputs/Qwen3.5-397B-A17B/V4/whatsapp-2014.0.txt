First call: NO-GO. The price is 12x the prior valuation with no revenue, no monetization plan, and extreme regulatory risk.

Steelman: Paying a massive premium is the only way to instantly neutralize the existential threat of mobile messaging displacing the core business, acquiring hundreds of millions of users who cannot be reached organically.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Risk slow irrelevance as users migrate to the target; low cost, high strategic risk.
2. Build a competitor: Too slow; network effects favor the incumbent target; high failure probability.
3. Acquire: Immediate threat neutralization and user acquisition; extreme financial cost and integration risk.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of maintaining dominant user connectivity, assuming the core business is indeed under existential threat from mobile shifts.

Pre-mortem: The deal fails if regulators block it due to privacy concerns, the founders leave immediately rendering the product stagnant, or the massive debt/equity dilution cripples the buyer's ability to innovate elsewhere. This is a one-way door; delay costs market share daily as the network effect widens.

Evidence: The $19B price vs $1.5B valuation is a factual mismatch requiring justification beyond "growth." The lack of a monetization plan is a stated fact, not a risk; it means the ROI model is purely defensive speculation.

Blockers:
1. No monetization path: The proposal admits no plan exists. Spending $19B without a defined route to recoup capital violates basic fiduciary duty unless the "existence of the company" is the only ROI, which is unproven.
2. Valuation disconnect: A 12x jump in one year without revenue traction suggests a bubble, not a sound investment basis.

Risks: Founder retention, regulatory blockade, cultural clash, opportunity cost of capital.

The absence of a monetization strategy for a $19B outlay is a blocker. A defensive purchase must still have a credible financial thesis or a guaranteed integration path to revenue, neither of which is presented. The price relies entirely on the fear of displacement, not on asset value.

DECISION: NO-GO
