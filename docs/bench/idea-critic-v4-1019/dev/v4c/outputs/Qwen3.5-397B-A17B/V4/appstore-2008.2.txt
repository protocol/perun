First call: GO. The proposal converts a "web-only" limitation into a controlled ecosystem, directly addressing the risk of rivals locking in developers while mitigating security and stability through a single review gate.

**Steelman:** A curated native app store creates a defensible moat by attracting top talent with revenue share (70/30) and ensuring quality via mandatory review, balancing openness with the brand's reputation for polish.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Retaining web-only apps cedes the platform war to rivals with native ecosystems; developers will prioritize platforms offering full hardware access.
*   **Open Distribution:** Allowing sideloading invites malware and instability, validating carrier fears and likely causing them to block the device or demand crippling restrictions.
*   **Carrier Objections:** While carriers may resist bypassed services, a popular device with exclusive apps gives the maker leverage to force acceptance; appeasing carriers now kills the product's long-term value.
*   **Review Workload:** This is an operational scaling issue, not a strategic blocker. A bottleneck in approval is preferable to a flood of broken apps destroying user trust.
*   **Polish Risk:** The review process *is* the mechanism to enforce polish, turning a potential negative into a curated positive.

**Blockers:** NONE. The concerns listed (security, workload, carrier pushback) are execution risks managed by the proposed review process, not facts proving the model fails. The rival ecosystems prove demand exists; the closed model differentiates on quality.

**Risks:** Review delays could frustrate developers; carrier retaliation could limit distribution. These are managed by staffing the review team and leveraging consumer demand.

**Kill Criteria:** If the review process averages >7 days per app within 6 months, or if carrier blocking reduces addressable market by >20%, the model requires revision. Leading metric: Number of approved high-quality apps vs. rival platforms at 12 months.

The proposal correctly identifies that a walled garden with high-quality native apps is the only viable path to compete with established rivals while maintaining the product's premium positioning.

DECISION: GO
