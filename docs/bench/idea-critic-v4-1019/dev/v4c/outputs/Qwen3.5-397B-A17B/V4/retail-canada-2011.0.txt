First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve three high-difficulty unknowns (cross-border logistics, unproven inventory systems, and foreign regulation) simultaneously with a massive capital commitment (130–150 stores) before validating unit economics in the new market.

**Steelman:** Rapid saturation prevents the entrenched competitor from reacting, leveraging the retailer's domestic loyalty to immediately capture share in a higher-priced neighboring market.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Unproven Core):** The plan relies on a "new inventory-management system" that is "not yet proven." Deploying an untested system across 150 international sites while simultaneously building a new supply chain is an operational single point of failure. If the system fails, the entire network stalls.
2.  **Blocker (Pace vs. Learning):** Opening ~150 stores in two years allows no time to learn from early mistakes. The proposal admits the retailer must "learn" logistics and regulation; doing so at maximum scale guarantees that early errors are replicated across the entire fleet before correction.
3.  **Flawed Premise:** "Loyal domestic following" does not transfer across borders automatically, especially against a larger, entrenched local competitor. The higher local prices on some goods may erode the "discount" value proposition before brand loyalty is established.

**Better Way:** A staged entry (5–10 stores) to validate the new inventory system and supply chain locally before committing to lease purchases for 150 sites. This reduces capex risk and allows the "learning" phase to happen without betting the company.

**Kill Criteria:** The absence of a pilot phase for the new inventory system and the decision to scale to 150 stores before proving the cross-border model constitutes a fatal blocker. The risk is not just execution difficulty; it is the certainty of systemic failure due to the coupling of unproven technology with maximum scale.

**Blockers:**
- Unproven inventory system deployed at full scale immediately.
- No pilot phase to validate cross-border logistics before 150-store commitment.

**Risks:**
- Entrenched competitor retaliation.
- Non-transferable brand loyalty.
- Regulatory delays.

DECISION: NO-GO
