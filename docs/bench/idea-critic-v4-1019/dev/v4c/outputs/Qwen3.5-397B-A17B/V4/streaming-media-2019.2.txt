First call: GO. The strategic imperative to own the customer relationship and capture long-term lifetime value outweighs the immediate loss of licensing fees, provided the company can sustain the cash burn.

Steelman: Direct-to-consumer distribution is the only viable long-term model for a content owner; delaying entry cedes permanent market share to incumbents who will eventually raise prices or exclude rivals entirely.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain licensing revenue but remain a vendor; margins compress as buyers consolidate or launch their own services.
2. Hybrid model: Keep licensing while launching a premium service. Risk: Diluted brand focus and slower subscriber growth; buyers may still drop content preemptively.
3. Proposed path: Aggressive pricing and exclusivity to force adoption. Highest risk, highest ceiling.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of owning the audience. Assumes the content library is strong enough to drive switching behavior despite a smaller catalog than the market leader.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the technology platform crashes under load (reputational damage) or if the cash burn exceeds projections before reaching profitability, forcing a fire sale. The door is one-way; pulling content from rivals burns bridges that cannot be easily rebuilt. Cost of delay is high: every quarter waits allows the leader to lock in more exclusive deals and habit formation.

Evidence: The proposal admits the company has never run a tech platform (execution risk) and targets tens of millions against a leader's hundreds of millions (scale gap). However, these are risks, not blockers. The financial model relies on a multi-year loss, which is feasible for a conglomerate with billions in existing revenue.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (cash burn, tech execution, cannibalization) are inherent to the strategy but do not invalidate the core thesis that ownership is required for survival. No alternative reaches the same upside (market control) cheaper.

Risks: Technology failure, slower-than-expected subscriber growth, prolonged negative cash flow.

Kill criteria: Halt expansion if subscriber growth falls below 20% year-over-year after Year 2, or if cash burn exceeds projected limits by more than 15% without a clear path to correction. Leading metric: Monthly Active Users and Churn Rate. Review date: End of Year 1.

DECISION: GO
