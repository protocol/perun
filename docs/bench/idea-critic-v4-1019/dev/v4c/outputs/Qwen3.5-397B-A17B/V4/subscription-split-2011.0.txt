First call: NO-GO. A 60% price hike followed immediately by a forced operational split appears as a punitive unbundling rather than a strategic refinement, inviting severe customer backlash.

**Steelman:** Separating the declining DVD asset from the growth-oriented streaming unit allows distinct management focus and prevents the legacy cost structure from dragging down the streaming valuation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing & Perception:** Executing a brand split only two months after a 60% price increase confirms the customer's worst fear: they are being squeezed out of the combined plan. The market will interpret this not as "strategic focus" but as a double penalty (higher cost + fractured experience).
2.  **Friction vs. Value:** The proposal explicitly removes shared ratings and requires separate logins/billing. This actively degrades the user experience for the most engaged customers (those using both) without offering a compensating benefit. It solves an internal management problem by creating an external customer service crisis.
3.  **Alternatives Ignored:** The "do nothing" or "slow transition" options are dismissed. A better way would be to maintain the unified interface and data while managing the businesses internally as separate P&Ls until the DVD decline naturally separates the user bases.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, churn spikes among dual-users. PR narratives focus on "greedy corporate splitting," causing a stock drop. The anticipated "streaming freedom" is overshadowed by the logistical anger of managing two accounts.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal pairs a massive price hike with a service fragmentation that reduces utility (no shared data, two logins).
*   **Contradiction:** The rationale claims to let streaming "pursue its own strategy," yet the immediate action ties that strategy to a highly controversial pricing event, guaranteeing the new streaming brand launches into a firestorm of negativity.
*   **Missing Mitigation:** There is no bridge for the data loss (ratings) or the login friction, which are core value drivers.

**Risks:** Customer confusion is listed as a risk, but it is a certainty given the deliberate separation of accounts and data.

**Conclusion:** The strategic logic of separation may be sound long-term, but the tactical execution here is catastrophic. It validates the anger over the price hike and destroys cross-service value. This must be delayed or restructured to decouple the brand split from the pricing shock.

DECISION: NO-GO
