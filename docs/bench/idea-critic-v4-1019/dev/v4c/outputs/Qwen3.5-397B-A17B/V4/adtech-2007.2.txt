First call: NO-GO. The $6 billion cash price for a fast-moving technology asset, absent verified synergies or retention guarantees, presents an immediate capital destruction risk that outweighs the reactive strategic motive.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform is the only viable path to prevent the search leader from monopolizing display ad infrastructure, and the premium is a necessary entry fee to secure market relevance before the window closes.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Blocker:** The proposal cites a "large premium" for a sector defined by "fast-changing technology." Paying a peak valuation in cash for an asset with rapid obsolescence risk and no stated earn-out mechanism fails basic capital allocation discipline. If the technology shifts in 18 months, the $6 billion is unrecoverable.
2.  **Channel Conflict:** The buyer serves agency customers; the target competes with them or creates channel confusion. The proposal acknowledges this conflict but offers no mitigation plan. Alienating existing revenue streams to chase a speculative display market is a net-negative strategy.
3.  **Reactive Strategy:** The move is framed as a response to a competitor's agreement rather than a standalone value creation thesis. Chasing a rival into a high-volatility segment without a clear differentiation plan often leads to integration failure.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve $6 billion cash; monitor if the competitor's integration fails (likely in fast-tech).
*   **Partnership:** License the technology or form a joint venture to test the water without full balance sheet exposure.
*   **Build:** Invest internally given the "fast-changing" nature suggests organic innovation might outpace acquired legacy tech.

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition closes, agency clients defect due to conflict, the technology stack requires a costly rewrite within two years, and the company writes down $4 billion of the asset, distracting leadership from core search dominance.

**Blockers:**
*   No financial model justifying a $6 billion cash outlay for a depreciating tech asset.
*   No resolved plan for existing agency customer conflicts.

**Risks:** Technology obsolescence, competitor integration success, cultural clash.

**Kill Criteria:** If due diligence cannot prove retained revenue >90% post-acquisition and a concrete conflict resolution plan, the deal must terminate.

DECISION: NO-GO
