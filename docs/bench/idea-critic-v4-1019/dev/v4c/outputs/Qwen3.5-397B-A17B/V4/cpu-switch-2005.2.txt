First call: GO. The current roadmap fails the core requirement for thin, efficient laptops, and the dominant alternative offers a clear path to competitiveness.

**Steelman:** Migrating to the dominant architecture is the only viable route to regain performance leadership in thin form factors, with a translation layer mitigating immediate software incompatibility.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Guarantees continued product inferiority in power/efficiency, ceding market share to competitors using the dominant chips.
*   **Alternative (Dual Architecture):** Increases complexity and cost without solving the immediate thermal/efficiency deficit of the current supplier.
*   **Risks Addressed:** Sales freeze is a short-term tactic risk outweighed by long-term survival. Developer rebuilding is inevitable; the translation layer buys time. Performance loss in translation is an acceptable trade-off for native efficiency gains. Brand distinctiveness must shift from "unique chip" to "best user experience." Past pain is a execution warning, not a strategic veto.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual evidence presented proves the transition is impossible or that the dominant architecture cannot meet thermal goals. The risks cited are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws.

**Risks:** Execution complexity, temporary performance dips for translated apps, potential short-term sales dip. These are managed via the proposed two-year timeline and translation technology.

**Kill Criteria:** If the translation layer fails to run >90% of key legacy apps at usable speeds within 12 months, or if the new chips exceed thermal envelopes for thin chassis in validation, the plan must pivot. Leading metric: Percentage of top 100 apps running natively vs. translated by Month 18.

The proposal addresses the critical failure of the current supply chain. The risks are manageable execution hurdles, not blockers. The cost of delay is continued irrelevance in the high-growth thin laptop segment.

DECISION: GO
