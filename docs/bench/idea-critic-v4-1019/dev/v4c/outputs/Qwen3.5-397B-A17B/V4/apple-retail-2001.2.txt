First call: NO-GO. A loss-making manufacturer with 3–5% share entering a low-margin, high-fixed-cost business during a recession contradicts basic survival logic.

**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only viable channel to demonstrate the integrated music strategy and stop third-party staff from sabotaging sales, justifying the massive fixed costs as an existential marketing expense rather than a profit center.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Suicide:** The company is already losing money. Adding high mall leases and inventory costs creates a fixed-cost burden that accelerates cash burn. In a recession, traffic is volatile; a few bad quarters could bankrupt the firm before the strategy matures.
2.  **Channel War:** Alienating existing resellers (who currently provide the bulk of reach) to build a tiny proprietary network (starting from zero) sacrifices immediate volume for hypothetical future control. Resellers may retaliate by dropping the brand entirely.
3.  **Execution Risk:** The proposal admits the industry has a "poor retail track record." Attempting to master complex retail operations while fixing a broken manufacturing business splits management focus.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Shop-in-shop" branded sections within existing retailers or dedicated demo kiosks could achieve the "demonstration" goal without the lease liability or channel conflict.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capital Mismatch:** The plan requires massive upfront capital (leases, build-outs, inventory) while the company reports recent losses. There is no stated funding source or path to positive cash flow before reserves run out.
*   **Recession Timing:** Launching a high-fixed-cost model exactly when consumer discretionary spending is contracting creates a structural loss scenario that "demonstration value" cannot mathematically offset in the short term.

**Risks:** Inventory obsolescence, reseller abandonment, brand damage from empty stores.

**Verdict:** The proposal bets the entire company on a high-cost unproven channel during an economic downturn while ignoring cheaper alternatives to solve the "demonstration" problem. The financial math fails immediately under the stated conditions.

DECISION: NO-GO
