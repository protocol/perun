First call: GO. The proposal targets an unmet market segment with a differentiated brand strategy, despite high upfront costs and execution risks.

**Steelman:** Creating a distinct luxury brand with a dedicated network and a high-quality, value-priced flagship allows the manufacturer to capture premium margins without diluting its economy reputation, directly challenging entrenched European rivals on quality-to-price ratio.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary risk is brand perception; however, a separate brand and dealer network effectively insulates the luxury venture from the parent's economy image. While the single-model launch is thin, a focused flagship establishes credibility before line-up expansion. The high development cost is a sunk factor; the decision rests on future profitability, which the value-pricing strategy supports by undercutting incumbents while maintaining quality parity. The "do nothing" alternative cedes the lucrative luxury segment to competitors indefinitely. A cheaper alternative, such as badging an existing model, would fail to convince luxury buyers and damage the parent brand, making the clean-slate approach necessary.

**Blockers:** None. The proposal acknowledges the risks (brand perception, cost, thin line-up) and mitigates them through structural separation (new brand/dealers) and product strategy (quality matching, lower price). The risks are executional, not fundamental flaws in the logic.

**Risks:** Slow brand adoption, high capital burn before volume, and potential failure to match European refinement despite engineering claims. These become kill criteria: monitor initial sales velocity against forecasts and independent quality reviews within the first 12 months.

**Kill Criteria:** If the flagship fails to achieve 60% of projected sales volume in Year 1 or receives critical quality ratings below key European competitors, halt further model development and reassess the brand strategy.

**Leading Metric:** Monthly sales volume of the flagship relative to the break-even point, reviewed quarterly.

The proposal presents a viable, albeit risky, path to market expansion that addresses the core barriers to entry for a mass-market manufacturer entering the luxury space. The structural separation and value proposition provide a defensible position against incumbents.

DECISION: GO
