First call: NO-GO. The price premium and accounting doubts create an immediate, verifiable failure condition that outweighs the strategic pivot.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the target instantly grants enterprise analytics capability, bypassing years of internal R&D to satisfy the new CEO's strategic shift.

**Attack:**
1.  **Valuation Blocker:** Paying 11x revenue and a 64% premium is indefensible when the buyer lacks software integration experience. The finance chief's reservation confirms internal disagreement on value. Without proven synergy mechanics, this is asset destruction, not investment.
2.  **Evidence Failure:** Press questions regarding financial reporting since 2009 constitute a known red flag. A "few weeks" for due diligence is insufficient to validate years of suspected accounting irregularities, especially for a hardware buyer lacking software audit expertise.
3.  **Alternative:** A cheaper, safer path exists: partner with the target or acquire a smaller, cleaner asset to build software competency before attempting a transformative $11.7B merger. The current plan does not beat "do nothing" or "smaller pilot" on risk-adjusted returns.

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition closes; six months later, a restatement of the target's earnings reveals inflated revenue. The stock crashes, the integration stalls due to culture clash, and the hardware division is distracted. The "strategic shift" becomes a decade-long drag on value.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Financial reporting has been questioned publicly for two years; due diligence time is too short to verify truth.
*   **Fact:** 11x revenue multiple assumes perfect execution by a team with zero software experience.
*   **Fact:** Internal finance leadership opposes the price.

**Risks:** Integration failure, culture clash, overpayment even if accounts are clean. These are severe but secondary to the potential fraud/valuation error.

**Kill Criteria:** If independent forensic accounting cannot verify 2009–2011 revenue within 14 days, the deal dies. If the price cannot be reduced to <6x verified revenue, the deal dies.

The proposal fails the basic test of capital preservation given the specific, cited warnings about accounting quality and the buyer's inability to verify them in the allotted time.

DECISION: NO-GO
