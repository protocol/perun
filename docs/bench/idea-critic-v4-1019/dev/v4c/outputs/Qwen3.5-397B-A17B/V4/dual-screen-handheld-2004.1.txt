First call: GO. The proposal avoids a direct hardware arms race against a well-funded rival by pivoting to a unique form factor and lower price point, targeting an underserved demographic rather than fighting for core gamers on graphical fidelity.

**Steelman:** By leveraging a dual-screen touch interface at a lower cost, the company creates a distinct market category that broadens the audience beyond core players, rendering the rival's superior graphics irrelevant to the new target segment while maintaining revenue from the legacy install base.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (developer confusion, unproven touch mechanics, gimmick perception) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The proposal explicitly accepts the cost of two screens but offsets it by targeting a lower price than the rival, implying a viable margin structure through component selection rather than raw power. No evidence suggests the target audience rejects touch or dual screens; the "unproven" nature is the opportunity, not a blocker.

**Risks:** Developer friction may delay software launch; the "gimmick" narrative could hinder early adoption; maintaining two product lines might dilute marketing focus. These are managed by the strategy of keeping the older handheld on sale to serve core users while the new device cultivates a new ecosystem.

**Alternatives:** Matching the rival's hardware (No-Go: higher cost, same features, late to market); doing nothing (No-Go: ceding growth to rival). The proposed path is the only one that changes the competitive axis from "power" to "accessibility and novelty."

**Decision Logic:** The plan beats "do nothing" by attacking a new demographic. It beats "matching hardware" by being cheaper and unique. The absence of a pilot or specific metric is not a blocker; the kill criterion is implicit: if the new demographic does not adopt the touch interface within the first launch window, the strategy fails. The proposal correctly identifies that winning requires changing the game, not playing the rival's game better.

DECISION: GO
