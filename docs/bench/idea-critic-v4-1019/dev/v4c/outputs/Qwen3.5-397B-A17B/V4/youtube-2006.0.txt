First call: NO-GO. The price is astronomical for an asset with no revenue, massive liabilities, and an unproven business model.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant category leader immediately secures the video market, neutralizes a future competitor, and captures network effects that organic growth cannot match, justifying the premium as a defensive monopoly play.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue building the smaller internal service; risk losing the category entirely if the target survives litigation.
2.  **Build/Buy smaller:** Acquire a cheaper, compliant competitor; lacks the scale and cultural momentum of the market leader.
3.  **Acquire as proposed:** Pay $1.65B for dominance; assume total liability for copyright chaos.

**Fit:** Aligns with organizing the world's information, but assumes video is central to that mission (unproven in 2006) and that the buyer can solve copyright faster than courts can shut the site down.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, media giants sue immediately, courts order massive damages or an upload ban, the site becomes a hollow shell, and the stock price collapses under the weight of worthless goodwill and legal fees. This is a one-way door; the cash/stock is gone forever.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits "no meaningful revenue," "high bandwidth bills," and "threatened lawsuits." The math fails: paying $1.65B for negative cash flow and existential legal risk violates basic fiduciary duty without a guaranteed path to monetization or immunity.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Financials:** The valuation implies a revenue multiple that cannot be calculated because revenue is near zero. The price is purely speculative.
2.  **Legal:** The core asset (user uploads) is legally toxic. There is no "safe harbor" guarantee yet tested at this scale.
3.  **Viability:** If the lawsuits succeed, the product ceases to exist. Buying a lawsuit you might lose for a price you can't afford is a blocker.

**Risks:** Bandwidth costs spiral, ad models fail to materialize, culture clash destroys the small team, internal video service is cannibalized without replacement success.

**Kill Criteria:** If copyright litigation results in a judgment exceeding $500M within 18 months, or if ad revenue per view does not exceed bandwidth cost per view within 12 months, the acquisition is a total failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
