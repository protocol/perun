First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a tiny installed base, an unproven legal music supply, and a premium price in a market where cheaper devices have already failed to gain traction.

**Steelman:** A tightly integrated, high-capacity device could create a superior user experience that justifies a premium, eventually expanding the computer maker's ecosystem if the music supply problem is solved.

**Attack:**
1.  **Addressable Market:** With a small computer market share and no consumer electronics footprint, the initial addressable market is too small to sustain the $400 price point or justify the supply chain risk.
2.  **Content Gap:** The music industry has not licensed downloads. A hardware device without a legal, easy-to-fill pipeline is a "brick" waiting to happen. Existing players failed partly due to friction; this adds cost without solving the content bottleneck.
3.  **Timing & Sourcing:** Building a custom hard-drive-based player in under a year while sourcing novel components creates a high probability of delay or cost overrun, missing any potential window of opportunity.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" avoids capital risk. Waiting for the music industry to legitimize downloads reduces the content risk. Cheaper flash-based players exist for the early adopter niche.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches late, costs $500 to fix margins, sells only to existing loyalists, and becomes a costly orphan because no legal music store exists to drive repeat engagement.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Content Pipeline:** The core value proposition (music) cannot be legally or easily delivered at scale today.
*   **Market Mismatch:** Premium pricing requires a mass market or urgent need; neither exists given the tiny compatible computer base and failed cheaper alternatives.

**Risks:** Supply chain failure, continued industry resistance to digital licensing, brand damage from another niche flop.

The absence of a legal music ecosystem is a fatal flaw for a dedicated music device in 2001. The hardware cannot succeed without the service, and the service does not yet exist. The addressable market is insufficient to carry the development risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
