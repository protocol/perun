First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits irreversible capital to an unproven model in a low-margin sector before validating unit economics.

**Steelman:** Secure dominant market share in 1999 by building insurmountable infrastructure moats early, accepting initial losses to lock in customers before competitors emerge.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Unit Economics):** The plan explicitly bypasses learning unit economics in the single active city. Groceries have thin margins; without proven density and order frequency, the $1 billion capex creates a fixed-cost burden that guarantees insolvency if volume lags.
2.  **Blocker (Technology Risk):** In 1999, "highly automated" warehouse tech for mixed-SKU grocery picking is nascent and unproven at this scale. Betting the company on unshipped automation introduces massive execution risk.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" alternative regarding expansion—focusing solely on the first city to prove the model—is superior. It preserves capital, allows iteration on the 30-minute slot feasibility, and validates customer density requirements before replicating failures across a dozen cities.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume it is 2001 and the company is bankrupt. Cause: Overbuilt capacity in cities with insufficient order density, cash burn from unused automated lines, and inability to pivot due to sunk costs in specialized real estate.
5.  **Fit:** Rapid expansion contradicts the stated need to learn unit economics. You cannot learn what you do not measure in a controlled environment.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal contains a fatal internal contradiction: it acknowledges the need to learn unit economics while simultaneously structuring a plan that makes learning impossible before total commitment. The risks (capital intensity, thin margins) are not hypothetical; they are structural features of the industry that this plan amplifies rather than mitigates. There is no pilot, no cap on expansion based on performance, and no fallback if the first city fails to reach profitability. This is not a calculated bet; it is a gamble with existential stakes based on assumptions rather than data.

Blockers: Unproven unit economics, unproven automation tech at scale, contradiction between "learning" and "rapid expansion."
Risks: Cash burn, low customer density, margin compression.

DECISION: NO-GO
