First call: NO-GO. The stated facts describe a product with active, unresolved blockers regarding privacy, utility, and social acceptance that directly contradict the proposal to position it as an "everyday consumer product" for the general public.

**Steelman:** The device offers novel hands-free interaction for early adopters willing to pay a premium, serving as a bridge to mass-market utility once social norms and battery technology mature.

**Attack:**
The proposal fails the blocker test immediately. A "blocker" is a fact in hand showing the plan fails as proposed. Here, the risks are not hypothetical future events; they are stated characteristics of the 2013 context:
1.  **Contradictory Positioning:** The plan targets the "general public" as an "everyday consumer product," yet the stated facts confirm "no clear everyday use for most people" and "privacy objections." You cannot sell an everyday product to the masses that lacks an everyday use case and violates social norms.
2.  **Social & Privacy Blockers:** Unlike a smartphone camera (used intermittently), a head-worn camera implies constant surveillance. The stated "privacy objections" and "social acceptance" issues are not risks to be managed later; they are current market rejection criteria. Releasing this broadly invites immediate backlash and regulatory scrutiny.
3.  **Economic Mismatch:** At $1,500 (2013 dollars), the price point excludes the "general public," restricting it to niche enthusiasts. The revenue model relies on volume that the price and utility profile cannot support.
4.  **Technical Deficit:** "Short battery life" prevents "everyday" reliance. If the device dies before the workday ends, it fails the core utility promise.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserves capital and brand reputation against inevitable privacy scandals.
*   *Enterprise Only:* Restrict sales to controlled environments (factories, hospitals) where privacy is managed, use-cases are specific, and the $1,500 price is justifiable against productivity gains. This removes the social acceptance and "everyday use" blockers.

**Conclusion:**
The proposal to launch to the general public as an everyday product is blocked by the explicit lack of everyday use, privacy hostility, and social friction. These are not risks; they are disqualifiers for the stated target market. The invitation-only early adopter program is viable only if strictly positioned as a developer kit or enterprise pilot, not a consumer launch. As proposed, the plan guarantees market failure and reputational damage.

DECISION: NO-GO
