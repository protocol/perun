First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to massive fixed costs (40k unit factory) before validating demand, legal viability, or product-market fit beyond niche institutional use.

**Steelman:** Launching a premium ($5k) transporter with full-scale manufacturing immediately captures the early adopter and institutional market before competitors emerge, leveraging heavy publicity and investor backing.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fixed Cost Risk:** Building a factory for 40,000 units assumes demand that is explicitly "unproven" outside institutions. If adoption stalls due to price or social stigma ("conspicuous"), the company faces catastrophic overhead with no flexible exit.
2.  **Regulatory Blocker:** Legal status is "unclear" on sidewalks and roads. A mass launch invites immediate regulatory crackdowns that could render the product illegal to operate in key markets, destroying value instantly.
3.  **Market Fit:** High price and limited utility (cannot carry much) restrict the Total Addressable Market. Without proven consumer use cases, a $5k price point relies entirely on novelty, which is fragile.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "better way" exists: pilot production (hundreds, not thousands) to test legal frameworks and consumer behavior without sinking capital into a full factory. This reduces downside while preserving upside.

**Pre-mortem:** The company launches, cities ban the device due to safety/legal ambiguity, sales miss the 40k target due to price/stigma, and the dedicated factory becomes a stranded asset, bankrupting the venture.

**Blockers:**
*   **Regulatory Uncertainty:** Launching a mass-market vehicle where legality is undefined is a fundamental failure of due diligence.
*   **Capacity Mismatch:** Committing to 40k unit capacity without verified demand violates basic capital allocation logic for an unproven category.

**Risks:** Social stigma, high price elasticity, limited utility. These are manageable with a staged approach but fatal at this scale.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal fails the "do nothing" or "pilot first" test. The fixed cost commitment in the face of legal and demand uncertainty is a blocker. The plan must be revised to a capped pilot with legal clearance before scaling.

DECISION: NO-GO
