First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a scale deficit by acquiring a second declining asset, compounding execution risk without addressing the core lack of carrier expertise in consumer media.

**Steelman:** Combining two distressed media assets creates critical mass to challenge ad duopolies, leveraging the carrier's distribution to reverse traffic declines.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Capability:** The acquirer has zero history in consumer media and already struggles to integrate a similar legacy purchase from the previous year. Adding another failing culture and tech stack increases integration complexity exponentially while the core competency gap remains unaddressed.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. The "cheaper alternative" is investing in mobile-first native capabilities or partnering, rather than buying a $4.8B anchor of declining traffic. A declining portal does not provide the growth engine needed to compete with dominant platforms; it provides a shrinking revenue base.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 24 months. Cause: The combined entity fails to arrest traffic decline due to outdated tech and brand perception. Integration costs drain cash flow. The carrier's core business suffers distraction. The ad platforms widen their lead due to superior data and agility, which this merger does not fix.
4.  **Evidence:** The target's revenue and traffic are explicitly falling for years with repeated management instability. This indicates structural obsolescence, not a temporary dip fixable by scale. The carrier's prior acquisition has not yet yielded a proven success model.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capability Mismatch:** The buyer has no proven ability to run consumer media, yet the plan relies on successfully managing two distressed media properties simultaneously.
*   **Negative Momentum:** Buying a declining asset to fix a scale problem assumes the decline can be stopped immediately; history suggests otherwise. The $4.8B price tag for a falling knife contradicts the goal of building a competitive, scaled business.

**Risks:** Continued ad-revenue decline, culture clash, distraction from core wireless business, overpayment for obsolescence.

**Kill Criteria:** If traffic does not stabilize within 6 months post-close, or if integration costs exceed 15% of deal value, halt further investment and prepare for divestiture.

**Decision:** The plan buys scale in a dying model without fixing the operator's lack of experience. It compounds known failures.

DECISION: NO-GO
