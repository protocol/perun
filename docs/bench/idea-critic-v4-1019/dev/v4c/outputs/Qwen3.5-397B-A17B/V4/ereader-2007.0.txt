First call: NO-GO. The company lacks hardware DNA, faces publisher hostility, and enters a graveyard market with a high-cost device (cellular subsidy) while risking core print margins.

Steelman: A frictionless, dedicated reader with free connectivity unlocks immediate impulse buys and creates a closed ecosystem that competitors cannot match, forcing publishers to accept lower margins due to volume.

Attack:
1.  **Capability Gap:** Designing reliable consumer hardware with cellular integration is operationally distinct from retail logistics. Failure here damages the core brand.
2.  **Economic Misalignment:** Subsidizing cellular data assumes high attachment rates immediately. If adoption lags (as with prior e-readers), the burn rate is catastrophic.
3.  **Publisher Relations:** Aggressive low pricing without proven demand invites boycotts, starving the device of content at launch.
4.  **Cannibalization:** Without a clear migration path, this shifts high-margin physical sales to low-margin digital ones without net growth.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for hardware partners (e.g., Sony) to mature the market; enter later as a pure software/store play.
*   **Software First:** Launch e-books on existing PCs/laptops to prove demand and negotiate publisher terms before building hardware.
*   **Partnership:** Co-brand an existing e-reader rather than bearing full R&D and manufacturing risk.

Pre-mortem: The device launches six months late with battery issues; publishers withhold bestsellers due to price disputes; the cellular subsidy drains cash while users prefer reading on emerging smartphones.

Blockers:
*   **Capability:** Zero internal experience in hardware design or cellular supply chains creates an unbridgeable execution risk for a "first try" product.
*   **Content:** Publisher fear is stated as fact; launching without their top titles ensures failure, yet the proposal offers no concession strategy other than "low price."
*   **Unit Economics:** The combination of hardware R&D, manufacturing, and perpetual cellular subsidies creates a negative margin per unit until massive scale is achieved, which previous market data suggests is unlikely.

Risks: Brand damage from hardware defects, accelerated print decline, cash burn.

The proposal requires the company to succeed at three difficult new tasks simultaneously (hardware, telecom, publisher diplomacy) in a proven dead market. The lack of a phased approach or partnership option is fatal.

DECISION: NO-GO
