First call: NO-GO. Launching early with a higher price, scarce software, and alienated retailers contradicts the fundamental requirements for a successful platform launch.

**Steelman:** An early May launch at $399 secures a four-month head start to establish market presence before the competitor arrives, betting that being first outweighs having a complete ecosystem.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price Disadvantage:** At $100 above the rival, the console is positioned as a premium luxury without the software library to justify the cost. In 1995, price sensitivity is high; this cedes the mass market immediately.
2.  **Software Void:** A console is useless without games. Launching with "few games" while developers are unprepared for the early date ensures negative word-of-mouth. Early adopters will buy, be disappointed by the empty shelf, and warn others.
3.  **Channel Hostility:** Shipping only to select retailers guarantees retaliation from excluded partners. They will prioritize the competitor's machine, burying this console upon its wider release.
4.  **Technical Barrier:** If the machine is already "hard to program," rushing the date exacerbates the software drought. Developers cannot magically produce 3D titles faster; they will simply abandon the platform for the easier, cheaper rival.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing (Wait for September):** Launch with a full slate of games, unified retailer support, and a competitive price strategy.
*   **Cheaper Alternative:** Announce in May, ship in September. Secure pre-orders without risking the brand on an incomplete product.

**Pre-mortem:** By Christmas 1995, the console is labeled a "failed launch" due to high price and no games. Retailers refuse to stock it, developers pivot to the competitor, and the four-month head start becomes a four-month reputation deficit that cannot be recovered.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price:** $399 vs. rival's $299 creates an insurmountable barrier for a new entrant with limited software.
*   **Supply Chain:** Alienating major retailers ensures poor distribution when the full launch eventually happens.
*   **Product-Market Fit:** Releasing hardware without software violates the core value proposition of a gaming console.

**Risks:** Developer abandonment, brand damage, inventory stagnation.

**Kill Criteria:** If software count is below 10 titles at launch, or if major retailers are not onboard, the launch must be delayed.

DECISION: NO-GO
