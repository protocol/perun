First call: GO. The hybrid form factor directly addresses the erosion of the handheld line by phones while leveraging the maker's strong first-party IP, turning the "weak specs" constraint into a cost and portability advantage.

**Steelman:** A focused hybrid device leveraging exclusive first-party software can create a unique market niche that powerful stationary consoles and phones cannot replicate, recovering losses through hardware volume and high-attach software sales.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal acknowledges risks (specs, supply, third-party support) but offers a viable path via first-party titles and a differentiated form factor. No fact presented proves failure is inevitable; the "confusing positioning" risk is mitigated by the clear "home + handheld" value proposition if marketed correctly.

**Risks:** Weak third-party support may limit long-term tail; supply constraints could cap initial revenue; marketing must clearly distinguish this from the previous failed console. These are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do nothing:* Handheld line continues eroding; no path to recover console losses. (Higher risk)
2. *Powerful stationary console only:* Competes directly on specs where rivals dominate; ignores handheld erosion. (Higher cost, lower differentiation)
3. *Handheld only:* Cedes the living room; limited upside against phones. (Lower upside)

The proposed hybrid beats "do nothing" by creating a new category and leverages the maker's core strength (first-party games) to offset hardware disadvantages. The price parity with more powerful rivals is acceptable given the dual utility.

**Kill Criteria:** If first-year sell-through does not exceed the previous console's launch window by 20% despite first-party releases, or if third-party attach rate remains below 1.5 games per unit within 18 months, the strategy must pivot to a pure handheld or software-only model.

**Leading Metric:** Monthly active users engaging with both docked and handheld modes (validating the hybrid value prop). Review date: 6 months post-launch.

The absence of a detailed marketing plan or supply chain guarantee is a risk, not a blocker; these are standard operational hurdles to be managed, not reasons to halt a strategically sound pivot. The potential to redefine the market outweighs the speculative risks.

DECISION: GO
