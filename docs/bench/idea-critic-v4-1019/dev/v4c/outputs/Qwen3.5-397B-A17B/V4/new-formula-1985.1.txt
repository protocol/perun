First call: NO-GO. The proposal conflates sip-level preference with brand-level loyalty, ignoring the fatal flaw in the testing methodology.

**Steelman:** Replacing the formula with a sweeter variant maximizes immediate taste satisfaction and halts share loss to competitors.
**Attack:** The evidence is structurally invalid. Blind taste tests measure isolated sensory input, not the holistic brand experience which includes heritage, identity, and ritual. Crucially, the testing protocol withheld the most significant variable: the permanent removal of the original. Consumers preferring a sweeter sip do not necessarily consent to the erasure of the legacy product.

**Options:**
1. **Proposed Plan:** High risk of alienating the core base by removing their choice entirely.
2. **Do Nothing:** Continues share erosion but preserves brand trust.
3. **Better Way:** Launch the sweeter formula as a *new* line extension alongside the original. This captures the taste-preference segment without triggering the backlash of removal.

**Fit:** The plan contradicts the brand's north star of heritage and trust. You cannot honor a century-old relationship by unilaterally deleting its primary symbol based on incomplete data.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch fails catastrophically. Why? Because loyalists perceive the removal not as an improvement, but as a betrayal. The emotional attachment acts as a multiplier for negative sentiment, turning passive drinkers into active protesters. The "one-way door" of withdrawing the original creates a vacuum that competitors will instantly fill with "retro" marketing.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits testing did not disclose the removal. This is a blocker. The data proves people like the *taste*, not the *substitution*.

**Blockers:**
1. **Fatal Data Gap:** The core claim (consumers want the switch) rests on tests that hid the consequence (loss of original). This invalidates the conclusion.
2. **No Fallback:** Withdrawing the original eliminates the safety net if the new formula faces rejection.

**Risks:** Emotional backlash, bottler revolt, permanent brand damage.

**Decision:** The plan fails because it solves a taste problem with a brand solution, based on deceptive testing parameters. A line extension is the only sound path; total replacement is unjustified.

DECISION: NO-GO
