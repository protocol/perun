First call: GO. The removal of the headphone jack creates an immediate, forced market for a wireless alternative; delaying the solution guarantees customer anger without offering a path forward.

**Steelman:** Launching a premium, seamlessly integrated wireless audio solution simultaneously with the jack removal turns a hardware limitation into a curated ecosystem upgrade, justifying the price through convenience and brand integration rather than raw specs.

**Attack & Analysis:**
*   **Risks (Aesthetics/Loss):** The "odd" look is a temporary perception risk outweighed by the functional necessity. Loss is a valid concern but mitigated by the charging case acting as a home base; this is a behavioral education problem, not a product flaw.
*   **Price ($159 vs. Free):** Comparing advanced wireless tech to legacy wired commodities is a category error. The value prop is freedom from wires and automatic pairing, not just audio transmission. The margin supports R&D for the custom chip.
*   **Battery (5 hours):** Sufficient for average daily usage cycles, especially with the charging case extending total capacity. It is not a blocker.
*   **Customer Anger:** This is inevitable due to the jack removal itself. Not launching these earbuds makes the anger permanent and unresolved. Launching them provides the escape valve.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Customers lose audio capability immediately; brand trust plummets. (Worse outcome).
2.  **Adapter Only:** Perpetuates the "dongle life," failing to signal innovation or solve the convenience deficit.
3.  **Cheaper Wireless:** Undercuts the premium brand positioning and fails to recoup the custom silicon investment.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This aligns with a vision of a port-less, wireless future. Failure would only occur if the pairing technology is buggy or the fit is physically painful for a majority of users; neither is stated as a fact in the proposal, only feared. The cost of delay is losing the narrative control of the transition.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited are execution or perception challenges, not factual contradictions of the plan's viability.
**Risks:** Adoption friction, initial mockery, loss rates. These are managed via marketing and the case design.

**Kill Criteria:** If return rates exceed 15% due to fit issues within 90 days, or if pairing failure rates exceed 5%, initiate a hardware revision. Leading metric: Daily active usage hours per unit.

DECISION: GO
