First call: GO. The proposal trades short-term margin protection for long-term platform liquidity and selection, which is critical for an unprofitable retailer needing to become the "everything store."

**Steelman:** By allowing competitors and independent sellers on the same page, the retailer instantly expands inventory without capital expenditure, captures fee revenue on transactions it would otherwise lose, and increases page relevance, driving more traffic that benefits its own direct sales.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Cannibalization:** Yes, used copies and lower-priced third-party new items will undercut direct sales. However, if the customer wants the lower price, they will leave to find it elsewhere if not offered here. Capturing a fee on a lost sale is superior to losing the customer entirely.
*   **Service Quality/Trust:** This is a valid execution risk, not a strategic blocker. It requires a robust rating system and guarantee policy, which are buildable controls.
*   **Supplier Objection:** Publishers may object, but their leverage diminishes as the platform's customer base grows. The alternative—stagnant growth due to limited inventory—is a surer death.
*   **Do Nothing:** Maintaining a walled garden limits selection and keeps the company unprofitable. Competitors will eventually offer broader selection.
*   **Cheaper Alternative:** There is no cheaper way to instantly infinite-scale inventory than leveraging others' capital.

**Pre-mortem:** The initiative fails if the retailer bans third-party sellers at the first sign of supplier complaints or refuses to enforce strict performance standards, leading to a flood of bad actors.

**Blockers:** None. The facts presented show a path to profitability through scale and fees that outweighs the risks of margin compression. The risks are manageable operational challenges, not fundamental flaws in the business logic. The "cannibalization" is actually the point: it keeps the customer on the site.

**Kill Criteria:** If third-party seller defect rates exceed 5% within the first year, or if direct sales drop by more than 20% without a corresponding increase in total site traffic and fee revenue, the program should be paused.

**Decision:** The proposal addresses the core constraint of inventory breadth and capital efficiency. The risks are real but are the cost of entry for a marketplace model.

DECISION: GO
