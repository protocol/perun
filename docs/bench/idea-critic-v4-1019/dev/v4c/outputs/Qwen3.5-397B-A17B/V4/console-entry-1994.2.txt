First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a hardware manufacturer with no platform experience to enter a duopoly dominated by incumbents with loyal user bases and first-party software, while simultaneously launching against a direct 3D competitor, all without a proven method to secure third-party developer support or offset negative hardware margins.

Steelman: A CD-based console leveraging cheaper media and easier tools can disrupt the cartridge-dominated market if developer adoption outpaces incumbent retaliation.

However, the blocker test fails. The facts state the company has "no experience as a console platform holder" and must win over "outside developers" who are currently served by two established companies with "famous in-house game series." In platform economics, software drives hardware sales; without a committed library of games at launch, the hardware is unsellable. The proposal offers "easier tools" and "cheaper manufacturing" as incentives, but these are operational benefits, not market guarantees. Incumbents can match pricing or leverage their existing loyal customer base to lock out new entrants.

Furthermore, the timing presents a fatal conflict: an incumbent is launching a competing 3D console "around the same time." Entering a capital-intensive market with thin margins against a synchronized launch from a dominant player, without a differentiated software lineup or existing ecosystem, creates an immediate cash burn with no clear path to profitability. The risk of being stuck with unsold inventory and no developer support is not a manageable uncertainty; it is a near-certain outcome based on the stated lack of experience and competitive landscape.

The absence of a pilot, a specific signed developer, or a differentiated content strategy constitutes a blocker. Cheaper discs do not compensate for the lack of games.

Blockers: No platform experience; simultaneous launch by incumbent; no secured software library; reliance on unproven developer migration.
Risks: Hardware margin losses; brand damage; failure to gain market share.

DECISION: NO-GO
