First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on forced integration to overcome entrenched network effects despite a documented history of three prior social failures, suggesting a fundamental mismatch between the company's core utility and social engagement.

**Steelman:** Integrating social layers into dominant search and mail products creates unavoidable friction that converts utility users into social participants, leveraging existing scale to bypass cold-start problems where organic growth previously failed.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & History:** The company has failed at this three times. Network effects favor the incumbent (hundreds of millions of users). Forcing integration into utility products (search, mail) risks annoying the core user base, potentially degrading the primary revenue engine to chase a secondary, unproven market.
2.  **Incentives:** Tying executive bonuses to adoption encourages gaming metrics (e.g., forced sign-ups) rather than genuine value creation, likely exacerbating user annoyance without solving the retention problem.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves the core product's integrity. A "cheaper alternative" would be a standalone experiment decoupled from core utilities, avoiding the risk of contaminating the search experience. The proposed "integrate everywhere" approach is a high-cost, high-reversibility gamble.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because users treat the social layer as spam within their email and search tools, leading to mass opt-outs or reduced engagement with core products. The "invite-only" beta masks the lack of organic demand; once opened, churn spikes.
5.  **Blocker:** The proposal admits users "may not switch" and acknowledges "forced integration may annoy existing users." Given the three previous failures, the assumption that *this* attempt succeeds solely due to integration is contradicted by historical evidence. The plan lacks a mechanism to prove users *want* this before risking the core brand. The incentive structure guarantees distorted execution.

**Decision:** The risks to the core business (annoying search/mail users) outweigh the speculative upside, especially given the repeated historical failures and the lack of evidence that integration solves the fundamental social graph problem.

DECISION: NO-GO
