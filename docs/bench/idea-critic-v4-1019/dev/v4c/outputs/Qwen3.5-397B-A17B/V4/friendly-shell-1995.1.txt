First call: NO-GO. The hardware requirement excludes the majority of the target market, and an imminent platform shift renders the investment obsolete before scale.

**Steelman:** A specialized, low-friction interface for novices could expand the total addressable market if it runs on existing hardware and survives the next OS cycle.

**Attack:**
1.  **Hardware Mismatch:** The proposal targets "many new home users" but requires a "high-end PC." In 1995, high-end machines are a tiny fraction of households. Selling a $100 shell that forces a $2,000+ hardware upgrade contradicts the goal of accessibility. The market fit is mathematically broken.
2.  **Strategic Timing:** A "major new operating-system release" is months away. Historically, these releases absorb shell functionality (e.g., improved GUIs, wizards). Launching a standalone shell immediately prior guarantees cannibalization by the platform owner or immediate irrelevance.
3.  **Adoption Friction:** Experienced users (who influence household purchases) will reject "patronizing" software. With "few third-party add-ons," the utility is capped at basic navigation, which the upcoming OS update will likely solve natively for free.
4.  **Better Way:** Wait for the new OS release. If gaps remain, build lightweight accessories compatible with the new standard rather than a competing environment.

**Blockers:**
*   **Market Size:** The "high-end PC" requirement reduces the install base below the threshold needed to sustain a standalone product line.
*   **Obsolescence:** The imminent OS release creates a near-certain risk of total product redundancy within one quarter.

**Risks:**
*   Brand damage from perceived condescension.
*   Inventory write-downs when the new OS ships.

**Conclusion:** The proposal fails on distribution (hardware mismatch) and timing (pre-obsolescence). It attempts to solve a usability problem with a product that most users cannot run and will not need in six months. The $100 price point is unjustifiable against a free, integrated update arriving shortly. Do not allocate resources to a dying architecture.

DECISION: NO-GO
