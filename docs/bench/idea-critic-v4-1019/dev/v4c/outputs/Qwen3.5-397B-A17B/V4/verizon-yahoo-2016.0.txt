First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a scale deficit by acquiring a second declining asset, creating a larger entity with worsening fundamentals rather than a competitive platform.

Steelman: Combining two distressed media portfolios creates sufficient scale to negotiate better rates and cross-sell to the carrier's mobile base, stopping the revenue bleed through integration synergies.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Preserve $4.8B capital; accept current media irrelevance.
2. Build/Buy emerging tech: Higher execution risk, but targets growth rather than decay.
3. This acquisition: Massive immediate cost for assets with proven negative momentum.
The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" because it purchases guaranteed decline at a premium price without a credible mechanism to reverse it.

Fit: The strategy assumes a carrier can successfully manage consumer media despite zero history in the sector and repeated management failures at the target. This contradicts the north star of building a scalable, competitive ad business; you cannot scale a shrinking foundation.

Pre-mortem: Integration drags on while ad revenues continue their multi-year fall. The carrier becomes distracted by turning around a legacy portal, missing the shift to programmatic and mobile-first ecosystems dominated by the two incumbents. The $4.8B becomes a write-down within 24 months. This is a one-way door; exiting requires selling at a massive loss.

Evidence: The proposal admits the target's traffic and revenue are falling, management is unstable, and the buyer lacks relevant experience. These are not risks; they are stated facts that invalidate the core premise of "scaling to compete." No evidence suggests the carrier possesses a unique capability to reverse these specific declines.

Blockers:
1. Negative Momentum: Buying a business with years of falling revenue and traffic for $4.8B contradicts the goal of building a growing competitor.
2. Capability Gap: The buyer has no history in consumer media, while the target has failed under multiple management teams.
3. Economic Logic: Combining two declining assets does not create growth; it aggregates decay.

Risks: Integration failure, continued ad market share loss to dominant platforms.

Kill Criteria: None needed; the premise is fundamentally flawed based on provided facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
