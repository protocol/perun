First call: NO-GO. Launching a premium-priced tablet with a tiny app catalog against a dominant incumbent, while the OS has negligible share and leadership is in flux, ignores the primary driver of tablet adoption: ecosystem depth.

Steelman: The hardware quality and brand equity can overcome a weak app catalog if developer outreach rapidly closes the gap post-launch.

Attack:
1. **Fit & Evidence:** Tablets are app-driven. A "small catalog" at "market-leading price" offers no value proposition. Shoppers already view it as "unproven"; pricing it equally to the leader confirms it as an overpriced compromise rather than a premium alternative.
2. **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "delay" option is superior. Wait until the app count reaches a critical threshold or price significantly below the leader to compensate for the ecosystem gap. Heavy ad spend on a product lacking core utility (apps) burns capital with low conversion.
3. **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. The cause is clear: users buy the device, find their essential apps missing, and return it. The "developer outreach" is a lagging indicator; it cannot instantly create the inventory needed for day-one sales. Leadership transition adds execution risk to this already fragile timeline.
4. **Blocker:** The proposal explicitly admits the app catalog is a "small fraction" of the leader's while demanding a premium price. This is a fundamental unit-economic mismatch. The stated fact that shoppers see it as "unproven" combined with no differentiator (price or features) guarantees market rejection.

Risks (non-blocking but severe): Leadership distraction during transition; ad spend inefficiency; brand damage from a failed flagship launch.

The plan fails the basic test of product-market fit as described. It asks customers to pay a premium for an inferior experience based on a promise of future developer support. This is not a go-if scenario; the core pricing and inventory strategy must change before any launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
