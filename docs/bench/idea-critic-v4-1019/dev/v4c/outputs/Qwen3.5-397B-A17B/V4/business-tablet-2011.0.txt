First call: NO-GO. Launching a business-centric device without native email, calendar, or contacts directly contradicts the company's core strength and value proposition, creating an immediate functional deficit against the rival.

**Steelman:** The 7-inch form factor offers superior portability, and the new OS provides long-term strategic control, with phone-pairing serving as a temporary bridge for enterprise users.

**Attack:**
1.  **Strategic Contradiction:** The proposal targets business email leaders but ships a device incapable of standalone email. This is not a risk; it is a fundamental product failure for the stated market. "Pairing with a phone" adds friction and dependency, making the tablet a peripheral rather than a primary tool.
2.  **Pricing Error:** Matching the price of a larger, established rival tablet with a smaller screen, untested OS, and missing core apps offers negative value. Customers will not pay a premium for a compromised experience.
3.  **Ecosystem Gap:** Without built-in apps or a mature app store, developers have no incentive to commit. The "chicken-and-egg" problem is unsolved; the device launches empty.
4.  **Untested OS:** Relying on an unproven operating system for a critical business launch introduces massive execution risk with no fallback if the OS fails under enterprise load.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Maintain focus on the dominant phone line while monitoring the tablet market.
*   *Delay & Integrate:* Postpone launch until native email/calendar apps are built and the OS is validated.
*   *Differentiate on Price:* If features are missing, the price must be significantly lower than the rival's superior large tablet, not equal.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, reviewers pan the lack of email, enterprise IT refuses to deploy a dependent device, developers ignore the platform, and the project is cancelled within a year, wasting the OS acquisition cost.

**Blockers:**
*   Missing native email/calendar apps for a "business email" focused device.
*   Price parity with a superior (larger screen, established OS) competitor.
*   Untested OS with no proven enterprise stability.

**Risks:** Developer adoption, app store gap, brand confusion.

The plan fails on its own stated facts: it targets business users but removes their primary requirement.

DECISION: NO-GO
