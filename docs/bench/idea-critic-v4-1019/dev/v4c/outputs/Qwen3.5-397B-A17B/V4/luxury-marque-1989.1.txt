First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets the company's solvency on overcoming deep-seated brand perception and massive fixed costs with a single unproven product, despite admitted heavy prior spending.

**Steelman:** A distinct luxury brand, decoupled from the economy parent via separate dealers and engineering, can undercut European incumbents on price while matching quality, capturing margin without diluting the core brand.

**Attack:**
1.  **Brand Gravity:** Buyers pay premiums for heritage and status, not just "refinement." A mass-market origin is a liability, not a neutral fact. Without decades of prestige, the "luxury" claim is marketing fiction, not engineering reality.
2.  **Capital Incineration:** Building a separate dealer network is astronomically expensive and slow. Doing this while already admitting "development has already cost heavily" suggests a sunk-cost fallacy drive rather than a rational go-forward plan.
3.  **Single-Point Failure:** Launching a new brand with *one* flagship sedan is catastrophic risk management. If that single model misses the mark on quality or style, the entire brand dies instantly, taking the huge dealer investment with it.
4.  **Alternative:** A cheaper, safer path exists: launch a "premium" trim line within existing dealers to test price elasticity and brand stretch before committing to standalone infrastructure.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial:** The combination of "heavy prior costs" plus the new capital required for a separate dealer network creates a cash burn rate that a single model cannot support.
*   **Market:** There is no evidence provided that buyers will ignore the "economy car" stigma regardless of engineering quality. The proposal assumes engineering equals prestige, which is false in luxury markets.
*   **Strategy:** A one-model launch for a new luxury brand contradicts the requirement for a full line-up to sustain dealer profitability and brand image.

**Risks:** Brand dilution if the luxury attempt fails publicly; dealer bankruptcy if volume targets aren't met immediately; inability to recoup sunk costs.

The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" test and relies on unproven assumptions about brand perception that directly contradict the company's established identity. The financial exposure relative to the single-product launch is unacceptable.

DECISION: NO-GO
