First call: GO. The proposal secures the franchise's future by transitioning subscribers to an inevitable digital model before external competitors force a chaotic migration.

**Steelman:** Launching a free, limited streaming service now leverages the existing seven-million-subscriber base to gather critical usage data and build a digital habit at minimal marginal cost, positioning the company as a pioneer rather than a laggard despite current technical and content limitations.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The risks of cannibalization and high licensing costs are real but misaligned with the strategic imperative. Cannibalization is self-inflicted survival; if the company does not eat its own DVD revenue, incoming tech giants with deeper pockets will destroy both revenue streams entirely. The "small, old catalogue" is a feature, not a bug, for a low-cost entry tier that validates the technology without triggering immediate, expensive content wars. Poor video quality and spotty broadband are temporary constraints that will resolve faster than the time required to build a platform from scratch later. Waiting for perfect conditions guarantees entering the market as a follower against entrenched incumbents.

The "do nothing" option preserves short-term DVD profits but ignores the one-way door of technological shifts. The cost of delay is the loss of subscriber relevance and data advantage. While licensing costs are high, bundling streaming as a free perk for existing subscribers maximizes perceived value while minimizing churn, creating a defensive moat. The proposal does not require abandoning the profitable DVD business immediately but uses it to subsidize the transition.

**Blockers:** None. The facts presented describe a difficult transition, not a fatal flaw. The financial profitability of the current DVD business provides the exact capital needed to fund this experimentation.

**Risks:** Execution speed against larger entrants and potential short-term margin compression. These are managed by the existing subscriber base providing immediate scale.

**Kill Criteria:** If streaming engagement does not reach 10% of the subscriber base within 18 months, or if licensing costs exceed 15% of DVD profit margins without corresponding retention benefits, the initiative must be pivoted or capped.

**Leading Metric:** Percentage of active subscribers streaming at least one title per month. Review date: 12 months from launch.

DECISION: GO
