First call: GO. The market gap for an affordable, lightweight roadster exists precisely because incumbents deem it too niche to pursue, creating a low-competition entry point for a focused player.

Steelman: A mid-size manufacturer can profitably capture the underserved enthusiast segment by leveraging existing front-engine/rear-drive components to minimize R&D, offering a pure driving experience that mass-market brands have abandoned.

Alternatives: Doing nothing cedes the niche to potential future entrants or kit-car manufacturers. Building a high-end heritage model ignores the "affordable" constraint and increases risk. A front-drive platform compromises the core driving dynamics, failing the "inspired by roadsters" claim. The proposed path beats "do nothing" by capturing unique brand value and margin per unit.

Fit: Assumes the company's north star includes brand revitalization and margin expansion through halo products. This fits a strategy of differentiating from commoditized mass-market sedans.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if safety compliance costs inflate the price beyond "affordable," or if the two-seat limitation restricts volume below break-even. However, these are risks, not blockers. The "shrinking segment" is a barrier to entry for giants, not necessarily for a low-overhead, targeted launch. The lack of heritage is an opportunity to define a new modern classic rather than live up to an old one.

Evidence: The proposal states the segment has disappeared and mass-makers see it as dead. This confirms the absence of direct competition. The risks (safety costs, limited buyers) are inherent to the category but do not contradict the core claim that a gap exists.

Blockers: NONE. No fact presented proves the plan fails as proposed. The risks are manageable through strict cost control and realistic volume targets.
Risks: Safety regulation costs eroding margins; limited total addressable market; fading enthusiast demand after initial launch.

Kill Criteria: Halt production if unit costs exceed 115% of the target price point within the first six months of pilot manufacturing, or if pre-orders fall below 500 units in the first quarter of availability. Leading metric: Pre-order conversion rate from enthusiast channel marketing.

DECISION: GO
