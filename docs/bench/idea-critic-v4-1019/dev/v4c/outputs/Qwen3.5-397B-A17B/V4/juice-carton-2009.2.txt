First call: NO-GO. The proposal discards the brand's primary shelf cue—the orange image—without evidence that the new minimalist design preserves recognition, directly contradicting the stated risk that shoppers may fail to recognize the carton.

Steelman: A modernized design featuring a glass and orange-shaped cap will refresh the brand for new buyers while retaining enough visual equity to keep loyal, older customers.

Attack: The proposal admits the old image is the "main shelf cue" yet replaces it entirely with a glass and abstract cap. For a low-involvement, habit-driven purchase like orange juice, breaking the primary visual anchor is catastrophic. The "large advertising campaign" is a bet on education, but shelf confusion happens in seconds before ads register. The risk of alienating the loyal, older base is not mitigated; it is exacerbated by removing their only navigation tool. The claim that recognition will hold is an unsupported assumption contradicted by the proposal's own risk assessment.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain the orange cue, zero risk of shelf confusion.
2. Hybrid evolution: Introduce the glass/cap elements gradually alongside the dominant orange image to test recognition before a full swap.
3. Proposed plan: High cost, high risk of immediate sales collapse due to invisibility on shelf.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-launch, sales have dropped 20%. Retailers demand slotting fee refunds because shoppers walk past the "unknown" brand. The ad spend failed to retrain muscle memory fast enough to prevent trial cessation. The door is two-way (packaging can change back), but the cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a failed national rollout.

Blockers: The plan relies on the unproven assumption that a glass and rotated name equal "orange juice" to the core demographic, despite explicit internal warnings that the current orange image is the sole recognition driver. No pilot data exists to validate the new cue.

Risks: Total loss of shelf visibility; alienation of older loyalists; massive sunk cost in printed inventory and ads.

Kill criteria: If a blind shelf-test shows <90% recognition of the new pack among current buyers within 4 weeks, halt the national rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
