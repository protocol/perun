First call: NO-GO. The plan eliminates the primary purchasing trigger for the existing customer base without evidence they will adapt, while simultaneously undertaking a costly, irreversible physical rebuild during a sales decline.

**Steelman:** Eliminating coupons clarifies value and rebuilds brand equity, while branded shops modernize the experience to attract higher-margin traffic, mirroring successful electronics retail models.

**Attack:**
1.  **Customer Mismatch:** The proposal admits the base is "older" and "coupon-driven," with most sales historically discounted. Removing discounts removes the reason these specific customers shop. The electronics CEO's past success relies on a different demographic and product velocity; applying it here ignores the core difference in buyer behavior.
2.  **Execution Risk:** Rebuilding stores chain-wide requires massive capital expenditure. Doing this while sales are already falling creates a cash flow crisis before the new model can prove viability.
3.  **No Feedback Loop:** Announcing without a pilot removes the ability to detect failure early. If the customer base abandons the brand immediately (a high probability given their training), the company faces total collapse before a correction is possible.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Continued slow decline, but preserves cash and existing revenue stream.
*   *Pilot Program:* Test everyday pricing and one branded shop in 5–10 locations. Measure conversion and basket size against control stores. This limits downside while validating the hypothesis.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales have dropped 40% as loyal customers leave, foot traffic has not increased because the new shops are unfinished, and the company lacks cash to revert to the old model or finish the renovations.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Most sales come from discounted items.
*   **Fact:** Customers are trained to wait for coupons.
*   **Conclusion:** Removing discounts immediately destroys the current revenue engine without a proven replacement. The lack of a pilot turns a strategic risk into an existential gamble.

**Risks:** Capital exhaustion, brand alienation, inability to reverse physical changes.

**Kill Criteria:** Not applicable as the plan is rejected. A viable plan would require a pilot showing non-discounted sales volume equals 80% of previous discounted volume within 90 days before chain-wide rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
