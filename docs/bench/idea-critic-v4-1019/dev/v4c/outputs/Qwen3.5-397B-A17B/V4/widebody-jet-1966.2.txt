First call: GO. The request comes from the largest customer with a clear economic driver (cost per seat) in a growing market, despite massive capital requirements.

Steelman: Building a scalable, convertible wide-body jet secures market dominance against supersonic threats by solving immediate unit economics for the biggest buyer.

Options analysis shows "do nothing" cedes the growth segment to competitors or supersonic disruptors. Alternatives like stretching existing narrow-bodies fail to achieve the required 2.5x capacity or freighter flexibility. The proposed path, while capital intensive, leverages the only available engine technology capable of the thrust required.

Fit: Aligns with the vision of dominating commercial air travel as volume grows. The freighter conversion feature hedges against the risk of supersonic replacement by ensuring residual value.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the unproven engine delays certification or if supersonic travel renders subsonic giants obsolete before ROI is reached. However, the raised cockpit design specifically mitigates the obsolescence risk by enabling a second life as a freighter. The "one airline" risk is real but typical for first-of-class programs; the size of this customer justifies the initial commitment.

Evidence: The proposal cites a specific customer request and a defined technical path (four-engine, high-bypass, raised cockpit). No internal contradictions exist in the stated facts. The financial outlay is large but proportional to the market opportunity described.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (cost, unproven engine, supersonic threat, single customer) are execution risks and market uncertainties, not factual blockers that make the plan impossible as proposed. They are inherent to pioneering infrastructure projects.

Risks: High capital exposure, engine development delays, supersonic disruption. These are managed via the freighter conversion clause and the strategic necessity of the move.

Kill criteria: Halt if the engine supplier fails ground testing within 18 months or if the launch customer cancels before tooling begins. Leading metric: Firm orders from secondary carriers within 12 months of announcement.

DECISION: GO
