First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits significant capital ($50M+) to a hardware-adjacent OS play with no revenue model, no distribution path, and active resistance from carriers and handset makers, contradicting the company's core competency in search ads.

**Steelman:** Acquiring this team secures a future gateway for mobile search by betting that an open-source OS can break carrier/handset monopolies faster than licensing deals, turning mobile traffic into ad inventory before competitors lock the market.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Capability:** A search-ad firm lacks the operational DNA to manage an OS roadmap, hardware relationships, or carrier negotiations. This is a strategic divergence, not an extension.
2.  **Economics:** $50M for six people is an extreme valuation without a revenue model. The "free software" lever does not guarantee adoption; carriers control the pipe and will block unmonetized platforms that threaten their walled gardens.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A cheaper alternative is licensing existing Java/BREW environments or building a lightweight mobile search site, avoiding the OS quagmire entirely.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** In three years, the OS remains niche, handset makers ignore it, carriers block it, and the $50M is sunk with zero ad revenue lift. The team dissolves.
5.  **Blocker:** The proposal admits "no revenue model" and "carrier resistance" as facts, not risks. Without a defined path to monetization or distribution, the core claim (pushing mobile web use) rests on hope, not mechanics. The cost of delay is low; the mobile web is nascent, and being first with a failed OS is worse than being second with a working one.

**Verdict:** The lack of a revenue model and the impossibility of distribution without carrier buy-in constitute fatal blockers. The price is unjustified for a speculative bet outside the core business.

Blockers: No revenue model; carrier/handset resistance stated as fact; misalignment with core competency.
Risks: Multi-year payback; team retention post-acquisition.

DECISION: NO-GO
