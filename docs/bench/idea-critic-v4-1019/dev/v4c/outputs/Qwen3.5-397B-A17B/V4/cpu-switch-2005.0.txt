First call: GO. The roadmap lag and thermal inefficiency are existential threats to a thin-laptop strategy; staying put guarantees obsolescence.

**Steelman:** Migrating to the dominant architecture is the only viable path to competitive performance and form factor, provided a robust translation layer mitigates immediate software incompatibility.

**Attack & Options:**
The risks are real but manageable compared to the status quo. Announcing early risks a sales freeze, but hiding the transition risks a chaotic, unmanaged shift later. Developer rebuilds are inevitable; the translation layer buys time, not permanent exemption. Performance loss in translated apps is a temporary tax for long-term native speed. Brand distinctiveness shifts from "unique chip" to "best user experience," a stronger moat. The painful prior transition argues for better execution, not inaction.

Doing nothing preserves short-term sales but surrenders the high-end market to competitors with superior thermal profiles. A partial transition creates fragmentation and support nightmares. The "cheaper" alternative is actually the most expensive: irrelevance.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the translation layer is too slow for pro users or if the timeline slips, leaving the company stranded between old stock and new delays. This is a two-way door in strategy but a one-way door in execution once manufacturing retools. The cost of delay is losing the "thin" category entirely.

**Blockers:** None. The facts state the current supplier *has not* delivered; this is a verified failure of the current path.
**Risks:** Sales dip during announcement, developer friction, initial performance gaps. These are execution risks, not strategic blockers. They require a capped, time-boxed rollout with clear kill criteria (e.g., if translation overhead exceeds 20% after six months, pivot support resources).

The proposal addresses the core constraint (thermal/efficiency lag) with a known mechanism (architecture shift + translation). The risks are standard for platform migrations and do not outweigh the certainty of stagnation.

DECISION: GO
