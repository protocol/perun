First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits unproven economics and a large short-term margin hit without a defined cap or pilot to validate the unit model before full launch.

**Steelman:** An annual membership eliminates repeat-purchase friction, locking in loyalty and increasing order frequency, provided the incremental shipping cost per user remains below the $79 fee plus marginal profit.

**Blockers:**
1. **Unproven Economics:** The proposal explicitly states the economics are unproven yet requests a full launch. Without a verified unit model showing that increased frequency does not erode margins beyond the fee revenue, the core financial claim fails.
2. **Uncapped Liability:** Launching immediately exposes the retailer to unlimited downside if "heavy buyers" exploit the flat fee. The absence of a time-boxed pilot or a capped user cohort to test the "margin hit" hypothesis constitutes a failure of basic fiscal control.

**Risks:**
- Reversibility is low once customers hold memberships; a failed launch damages brand trust permanently.
- Short-term cash flow strain could jeopardize operations if the margin hit is larger than modeled.

**Analysis:**
The "do nothing" alternative preserves current margins while allowing targeted shipping promotions to test price elasticity. A cheaper, safer alternative is a limited regional pilot or a capped beta (e.g., 5,000 users) to gather real data on order frequency and shipping costs. This reaches the same upside (validation) with significantly lower risk. The cost of delay (waiting a quarter for pilot data) is negligible compared to the cost of a structurally loss-making nationwide program.

The proposal fails the operator test: no sound operator commits to an uncapped, unproven financial model with admitted negative margin implications. The lack of a pilot, cap, or specific kill criterion is not just a risk; it is a structural flaw in the plan as presented. The plan must be revised to include a bounded experiment before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
