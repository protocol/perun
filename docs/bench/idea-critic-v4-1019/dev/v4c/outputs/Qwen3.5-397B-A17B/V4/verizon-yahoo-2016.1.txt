First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a scale deficit by acquiring a second declining asset, compounding execution risk without addressing the core lack of carrier expertise in consumer media.

**Steelman:** Combining two distressed media assets creates critical mass to challenge ad duopolies, leveraging the carrier's distribution to reverse traffic declines.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Capability:** The acquirer has zero history in consumer media. Integrating one legacy company was likely painful; integrating a second, culturally fractured target with repeated management failures while lacking domain expertise is a force multiplier for failure, not success.
2.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "divest" option is superior. The target's revenue is falling; buying a falling knife to fix a previous falling knife purchase violates basic turnaround logic. A cheaper alternative is investing in the carrier's core connectivity or partnering rather than owning the full operational burden of a dying portal.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume it is 2018. The integration stalled due to culture clashes between telecom engineers and media creatives. Ad revenue continued to slide as advertisers fled to the dominant platforms where ROI is proven. The $4.8 billion is written down as goodwill impairment.
4.  **Evidence & Math:** The proposal admits the target's traffic and revenue are falling for years. Paying $4.8 billion for negative momentum requires a miracle turnaround mechanism, which the proposal fails to name given the acquirer's inexperience.
5.  **Blocker:** The acquirer's lack of media history combined with the target's active decline creates a factual contradiction: the plan requires expertise the buyer does not have to fix a problem getting worse, not better. This is not a risk; it is a structural flaw in the thesis.

**Decision Method:**
*   **Blockers:** Acquirer has no relevant experience; target is in active, multi-year decline; price ($4.8B) implies growth that contradicts stated facts.
*   **Risks:** Integration complexity, further revenue drop, management churn.

The absence of a pilot, cap, or metric is irrelevant because the core premise (inexperienced buyer + declining asset = success) is broken. This is a classic "double down on failure" scenario.

DECISION: NO-GO
