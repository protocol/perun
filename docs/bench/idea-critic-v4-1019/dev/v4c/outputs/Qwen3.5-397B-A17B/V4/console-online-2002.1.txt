First call: NO-GO. The proposal imposes high fixed costs and friction (mandatory broadband, paid subscription) on a market where infrastructure adoption is low and the prevailing expectation is free access, creating an immediate adoption barrier without proven demand.

Steelman: A unified, premium online service creates a differentiated, high-quality ecosystem that monetizes network effects and locks users into the platform better than fragmented, free alternatives.

Options:
1. **Do nothing/Status Quo:** Let publishers handle online play; zero operational risk, but no platform lock-in or recurring revenue.
2. **Freemium/Hybrid:** Offer basic matching free to build scale, charge for premium features (voice, storage); lowers adoption barrier while testing willingness to pay.
3. **Proposed (Paid/Broadband-only):** High revenue per user potential, but maximum adoption friction and operational complexity.

Fit: Assumes the company can pivot from hardware/software to running large-scale service operations. This contradicts the stated lack of experience in hosting/support.

Pre-mortem: The service launches to a tiny fraction of the installed base due to broadband scarcity. Publishers refuse to integrate deeply due to low user counts. The company burns cash on underutilized servers and support staff, forcing a price cut that devalues the brand, or shutting down the service, damaging trust.

Evidence & Verification: The proposal admits "few homes have broadband" and the company has "never run" such operations. These are not risks; they are stated facts that directly contradict the requirement for a massive, immediate subscriber base to justify the operational overhead. The math fails: Low adoption × High fixed cost = Negative ROI.

Blockers:
1. **Infrastructure Mismatch:** Mandatory broadband excludes the vast majority of the 2002 console install base (fact stated in proposal).
2. **Operational Inexperience:** The company lacks the core competency to run the required hosting/support (fact stated in proposal).
3. **Market Pricing Conflict:** Charging for a service competitors offer free, in a market explicitly described as expecting free access, creates an insurmountable adoption barrier.

Risks: Publisher pushback, high churn, brand damage.

The plan relies on changing consumer behavior and infrastructure reality overnight while entering an operational domain where the company has zero track record. The stated facts guarantee failure at current scale.

DECISION: NO-GO
