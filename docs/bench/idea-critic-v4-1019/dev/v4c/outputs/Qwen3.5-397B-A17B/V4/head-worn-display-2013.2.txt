First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores fatal mismatches between the product's current state and the mass-market positioning.

**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 validates demand and refines the device before a general consumer release.

**Attack:** The plan fails because the identified risks are not merely "risks" but active blockers for the stated goal of an "everyday consumer product."
1.  **Value Mismatch:** At $1,500 (equivalent to a high-end laptop in 2013), the device lacks a "clear everyday use." Consumers do not pay premium prices for ambiguous utility.
2.  **Social & Privacy Blockers:** A wearable camera with "privacy objections" and "social acceptance" issues cannot function as an "everyday" item. If users are afraid to wear it in public due to social stigma or legal ambiguity, the core value proposition collapses regardless of technical specs.
3.  **Utility Constraint:** "Short battery life" disqualifies it from being an all-day consumer essential.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Avoids reputational damage from a failed consumer launch.
*   *Enterprise Pivot:* Target specific industrial or medical use cases where the high price is justified by workflow efficiency, privacy is contractually managed, and social acceptance is irrelevant. This path monetizes the tech without requiring mass-market social buy-in.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches to general public; media highlights privacy violations and social awkwardness ("Glassholes"); sales stall due to high price and lack of utility; the brand suffers long-term association with failure.

**Blockers:**
*   The proposal explicitly admits "no clear everyday use" while positioning it as an "everyday consumer product." This is a logical contradiction.
*   Social acceptance and privacy concerns are listed as known negatives that directly prevent daily usage.

**Risks:** High price, battery life (these are solvable; the blockers are not).

**Conclusion:** You cannot sell an "everyday" product that people are afraid to wear, don't know how to use, and find too expensive. The invitation-only program does not solve the fundamental lack of product-market fit for the mass market. Pivot to enterprise or delay until a killer use case exists.

DECISION: NO-GO
