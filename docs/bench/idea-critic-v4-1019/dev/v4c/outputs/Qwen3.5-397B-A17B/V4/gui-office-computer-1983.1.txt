First call: NO-GO. The price is prohibitive, performance is inadequate, and internal competition exists.

**Steelman:** This machine offers an unprecedented graphical interface that could redefine office productivity, justifying a premium for early adopters seeking a competitive edge.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $10,000, the unit costs several times more than established business PCs. Without a massive, immediate productivity gain, the ROI is negative. The "slow" performance directly contradicts the efficiency needed to justify this premium.
2.  **Software Void:** Business utility depends on software (spreadsheets, word processors). "Little third-party software" means the hardware is a solution without a problem. Users cannot adopt a tool that does not run their essential workflows.
3.  **Internal Cannibalization:** A cheaper, similar machine is already in development internally. Launching the expensive, slower version first destroys the market for the upcoming superior/cheaper option and damages brand trust when the better version inevitably arrives.
4.  **Target Mismatch:** Office workers prioritize reliability and speed over novel interfaces, especially at this price point. The risk of rejection is near-certain.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for the internal cheaper machine; avoid market confusion.
*   **Pivot:** Target niche creative markets (if software exists) rather than general office workers.
*   **Delay:** Halt launch until performance improves and the software ecosystem matures.

**Pre-mortem:** We launch at $10k. Sales are negligible because competitors offer 3x the speed for 1/4th the price. The internal cheaper team is demoralized by our premature, flawed entry. The project is cancelled within a year, writing off the launch costs.

**Blockers:**
*   Price is unsupportable relative to performance and competitors.
*   Lack of essential software renders the machine unusable for the target demographic.
*   Internal substitute creates a guaranteed failure path.

**Risks:** Market education costs, inventory write-downs, brand damage.

**Kill Criteria:** If a unit cannot run standard business applications at competitive speeds, it cannot be sold as a business computer. The current state fails this fundamental test.

DECISION: NO-GO
