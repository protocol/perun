First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores stated risks of alienating loyal customers and lacks validation.

**Steelman:** A simultaneous, unannounced global rebrand to a minimalist identity will signal modernity efficiently, assuming the visual shift alone drives contemporary perception without eroding legacy equity.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves current equity. A "staged pilot" (testing in one region or channel) offers the same insight into customer reaction with vastly lower reputational risk. The proposal rejects testing, claiming speed, but speed here amplifies failure mode severity.
2.  **Fit:** While "contemporary" is the goal, destroying the primary recognition asset (the blue square) contradicts the need to retain loyal customers. The plan assumes modernity requires erasing history, which is unproven.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch failed. Why? Because core customers felt betrayed by the sudden removal of a decades-old symbol, triggering an immediate, unified social media backlash that the brand could not contain due to the lack of advance communication or sentiment testing.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the design was "not tested with customers." This is not a risk; it is a confirmed gap in a high-stakes decision. The risks raised (backlash, attachment) are treated as obstacles to ignore rather than variables to measure.

**Blockers:**
*   **Confirmed Lack of Validation:** The core claim (this makes us look contemporary without losing customers) rests on an untested assumption. The proposal explicitly states the design was not tested.
*   **Superior Alternative Exists:** A staged rollout or pre-launch survey reaches the same upside (validation) cheaper and safer. The current plan refuses this.
*   **Contradictory Facts:** The proposal acknowledges loyal customers are attached to the old logo yet chooses a strategy (no announcement, total replacement) guaranteed to maximize their shock and dissatisfaction.

**Risks:** Social media backlash, loss of brand recognition, revenue drop from confused loyalists. These are elevated to blockers because the plan includes no mechanism to detect or mitigate them before full commitment.

**Kill Criteria:** If a small-scale test shows >5% negative sentiment shift among loyalty program members, abort.

The absence of a pilot or metric is a blocker here because the cost of being wrong is existential brand damage, and the proposal explicitly refuses to gather evidence before acting.

DECISION: NO-GO
