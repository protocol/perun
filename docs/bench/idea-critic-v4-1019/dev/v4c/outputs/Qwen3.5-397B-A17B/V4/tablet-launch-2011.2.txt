First call: NO-GO. Launching a premium-priced tablet with a tiny app catalog against a dominant incumbent, while the OS has negligible share and leadership is unstable, ignores the primary driver of tablet adoption: ecosystem density.

Steelman: The hardware quality and brand equity can overcome early software scarcity if developer momentum is ignited immediately by heavy ad spend and outreach.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High price, small catalog, heavy marketing. Cost: Extreme. Risk: Catastrophic inventory write-down if apps don't materialize. Reversibility: Low (brand damage).
2. **Do Nothing:** Wait for OS maturity or leadership stabilization. Cost: Opportunity delay. Risk: Missing the window. Reversibility: High.
3. **Subsidized Entry:** Price significantly below market leader to force adoption despite app gap. Cost: Margin erosion. Risk: Perceived as "cheap." Reversibility: Medium.

Fit: Assuming the north star is market share, pricing "in line" with the leader while offering a fraction of the value (apps) is a strategic mismatch. Users buy tablets for software, not just hardware specs.

Pre-mortem: The device launches; reviewers praise the build but pan the lack of key apps. Consumers, seeing the high price and empty store, buy the competitor. Developers, seeing low sales, refuse to port apps. The cycle reinforces itself, leaving the company with unsold inventory and a fractured leadership team unable to pivot.

Evidence: The proposal admits the app catalog is a "small fraction" and the OS has "almost no market share." History shows platforms fail when they cannot bridge the chicken-and-egg gap without aggressive pricing or killer exclusives, neither of which are guaranteed here. Leadership transition adds execution risk to an already fragile bet.

Blockers:
1. **Price-Value Mismatch:** Charging market-leading prices for a subordinate ecosystem contradicts basic adoption mechanics for platform businesses.
2. **Timing:** Launching during leadership transition increases the probability of strategic drift before the product finds traction.

Risks: Developer outreach may fail; ad spend may not convert to sustained usage.

The plan requires a price advantage or a definitive killer app to justify the switch cost for consumers. As proposed, it asks customers to pay a premium for an inferior experience.

DECISION: NO-GO
