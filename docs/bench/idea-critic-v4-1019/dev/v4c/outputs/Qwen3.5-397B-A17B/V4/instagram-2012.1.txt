First call: GO. The strategic necessity of neutralizing the primary mobile threat and acquiring its user base immediately outweighs the lack of current revenue or a defined business model.

Steelman: Acquiring the dominant mobile-native photo platform at a premium is the only viable path to secure mobile relevance, eliminate a existential competitive threat, and capture a network effect that cannot be built organically in time for the buyer's IPO.

Options analysis confirms that "do nothing" cedes the mobile future to a competitor with superior engagement. Building a rival product internally has already failed (weak mobile product) and is too slow. Acquiring a smaller competitor fails to match the target's scale and cultural momentum. The $1 billion price tag, while high relative to revenue, is low relative to the cost of losing the mobile photo category entirely.

Fit is absolute: this moves the north star of "mobile-first dominance" which the buyer currently fails to achieve. The pre-mortem suggests failure modes include cultural integration clashes and regulatory delays. However, these are manageable risks compared to the certainty of irrelevance if the acquisition is skipped. The cost of delay is catastrophic; waiting allows the target to grow larger, more expensive, or potentially partner with a different IPO-bound rival.

Evidence check: The proposal states tens of millions of users and fast growth on one platform with a fresh launch on the other. These are leading indicators of network effects that justify a revenue-agnostic valuation in a winner-take-all market. The buyer's own weak mobile product validates the "build" option as non-viable.

Blockers: NONE. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal; the high valuation is a feature of the strategic urgency, not a mathematical error.
Risks: Cultural destruction, regulatory scrutiny, and integration failure. These are mitigated by operating the app as an independent unit and leveraging the buyer's resources for scaling rather than restructuring.

Kill criteria: If user growth stalls for two consecutive quarters post-acquisition or if key engineering talent retention drops below 80% within six months, the integration strategy must pivot to full absorption or asset liquidation. Leading metric: Daily Active Users (DAU) growth rate on the newly launched platform.

DECISION: GO
