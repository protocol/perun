First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores the core risk that cola flavor is psychologically linked to color, making a clear product fundamentally mismatched with category expectations.

Steelman: A clear cola can succeed as a premium, "purer" niche extension if novelty converts to retention and cannibalization is offset by new volume.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Protects flagship margin; accepts slowing growth.
2. **Limited regional rollout:** Tests repeat rates and cannibalization without national spend.
3. **Proposed national launch:** High cost, high risk of flagship erosion, irreversible brand dilution if it fails.

Fit: Slowing growth demands innovation, but not at the expense of the cash cow. The "purer" angle is assumed to matter; evidence is only test market positivity, which often overstates demand due to novelty.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because initial trial is high, but repeat purchase collapses when consumers realize the taste does not match the visual cue of "cola." Rivals copy the gimmick instantly. The flagship loses shelf space and identity. This is a one-way door for brand perception; reversing it requires expensive rebranding. Cost of delay is low; waiting for repeat data prevents national embarrassment.

Evidence: The proposal admits shoppers link flavor with color and that test markets overstate demand. Yet it proceeds to national launch anyway. The risks (cannibalization, low repeat, rival copies) are acknowledged but unmitigated. No cap on marketing spend, no kill criterion for repeat rates, and no fallback plan if the flagship dips.

Blockers:
- **Fundamental mismatch:** The stated risk that "shoppers link cola flavor with color" contradicts the core product premise without evidence this bias can be broken.
- **Missing validation:** Test market "positive response" likely reflects novelty, not repeat intent. Launching nationally before verifying repeat rates is financially reckless.
- **Cannibalization:** No math shows new volume exceeds flagship loss.

Risks: High marketing spend on a fad; permanent brand dilution; rival copycats eroding margins.

Kill criteria (if revised): Limit to 3 regions for 6 months. Stop if repeat purchase <30% by month 3. Cap marketing at $X.

DECISION: NO-GO
