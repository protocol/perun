First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer with no developer platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors already oppose heavy capital spending.

**Steelman:** Leveraging existing spiky infrastructure to create a new high-margin revenue stream could amortize fixed costs and diversify beyond low-margin retail, turning a liability into an asset.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capability Gap:** Running internal infrastructure for spiky retail loads is fundamentally different from selling reliable, multi-tenant compute to developers. The latter requires billing systems, API stability, security isolation, and 24/7 support cultures the retailer explicitly lacks.
2.  **Capital Contradiction:** Investors are already questioning heavy capex. This plan doubles down on infrastructure spend without proven demand, directly antagonizing the capital base needed for the core retail business.
3.  **Market Risk:** The market is defined as "unproven." Entering first against established hardware/software vendors without a unique technical moat or customer base is speculative gambling, not strategy.
4.  **Distraction:** Management focus is finite. Diverting attention to build a B2B platform while margins are thin risks degrading the core retail operation, which currently funds everything.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Optimize internal costs; avoid new risk.
*   *Partner:* Rent excess capacity to an existing vendor (lower margin, zero distraction).
*   *Pilot:* Run a tiny, isolated beta before committing to a "new business line."

**Pre-mortem:** The project launches, reliability slips due to lack of platform expertise, early developer adopters churn, investors panic at continued capex burn, and the core retail business suffers from management distraction. The company is forced to shut down the division at a loss.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** No internal experience running a developer platform at scale.
*   **Fact:** Market is unproven; demand is hypothetical.
*   **Fact:** Investors already oppose the required capital intensity.
*   **Fact:** Core business has thin margins, leaving no room for error or dilution.

These are not mere risks; they are structural failures in the proposal's premise. The plan assumes success in an area where the company has zero competence, during a time of capital skepticism, in a market that may not exist.

DECISION: NO-GO
