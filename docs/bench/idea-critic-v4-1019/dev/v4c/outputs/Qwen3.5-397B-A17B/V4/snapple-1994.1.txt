First call: NO-GO. The plan to immediately migrate a cult brand with a "quirky" independent distributor network into mass-market channels and corporate advertising ignores the specific assets driving its value: exclusivity and authenticity.

Steelman: The acquisition leverages the buyer's scale to accelerate growth by fixing distribution gaps and amplifying awareness, assuming the brand's appeal is transferable to mass markets.

Attack: The proposal treats distribution and marketing as universal multipliers, but for premium cult brands, these are often the very constraints that create desire. Moving from independent, loyal distributors to a supermarket-focused mass network risks alienating the core base that fuels the "strong consumer loyalty." Simultaneously, applying mass-market advertising to a "quirky" brand frequently dilutes the unique identity that justifies its premium price, making it vulnerable to the very competition cited (large soft-drink companies). The plan assumes the brand is a smaller version of the buyer's sports drink; evidence suggests it is a different category entirely with different rules. The risks are not hypothetical; they are structural contradictions between the brand's current success factors and the buyer's proposed operating model.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids $1.7B cash outlay and integration risk; status quo growth continues.
2. Acquire but ring-fence: Maintain independent distributors and niche marketing to preserve brand equity while providing back-office support. This captures financial upside without destroying the product's market fit.
3. Proceed as proposed: High probability of destroying the "cult" value, leading to churn among loyalists and failure to convert mass consumers who see the brand as "sold out."

Blockers: The plan's core mechanism (mass distribution/advertising) directly contradicts the stated source of the target's strength (quirky network, cult image). No evidence is provided that this specific brand transcends the typical "premium-to-mass" failure mode.
Risks: Brand dilution, distributor revolt, price erosion, inability to differentiate against incumbent soft-drink teas once the "quirky" shield is removed.

The proposal fails to demonstrate how the brand survives the proposed integration. It attempts to fix what isn't broken and break what is working.

DECISION: NO-GO
