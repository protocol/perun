First call: NO-GO. The price is extreme relative to output, and key risks are unresolved facts, not mere uncertainties.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio is the only way to secure its creative pipeline, prevent a rival partnership, and revitalize the buyer's struggling animation unit by installing proven leadership.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Partnership expires; rival potentially gains exclusivity; buyer's unit continues underperforming.
2.  **Renegotiate:** Attempt a revised distribution-only deal (likely failed already).
3.  **Acquire (Proposed):** Secure assets and talent permanently but assume massive debt/equity dilution for ~1 film/year output.

**Fit:** Aligns with securing content libraries, but the cost structure threatens overall conglomerate stability.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal fails because the "one film a year" cadence cannot service the $7.4B valuation, causing stock collapse. Culture clash drives the creative leaders to leave post-vesting, leaving the buyer with high costs and no talent. The founder, now a major shareholder, blocks necessary restructuring.

**Evidence & Math:** At $7.4B for one film annually, the acquisition implies a valuation multiple that requires every future film to be a historic blockbuster just to break even on capital cost. There is no evidence provided that the studio can scale output to justify this price. The risk of dependence on specific leaders is cited but not mitigated by contract terms in the proposal.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Unit Economics:** The stated output (one film/year) mathematically contradicts the price ($7.4B) without explicit, verified plans to scale production immediately.
2.  **Key Person Risk:** The value resides entirely in creative leaders who have no binding long-term employment contracts mentioned; if they leave, the asset is worthless.
3.  **Governance:** The founder becoming a major shareholder creates a conflict of interest that could paralyze decision-making.

**Risks:** Culture clash, integration failure, stock dilution.

**Kill Criteria:** If the studio cannot commit to a minimum of two films per year for five years, or if creative leaders do not sign 5-year employment contracts, the deal must terminate.

DECISION: NO-GO
