First call: NO-GO. The product fails to deliver a compelling value proposition relative to its physical discomfort and technical limitations, risking brand damage between major generations.

**Steelman:** A low-cost ($180) bridge device leveraging existing IP to maintain market presence with novel stereoscopic 3D, accepting monochrome visuals as a trade-off for affordability.

**Attack:**
1.  **User Experience Blocker:** The requirement for an eyepiece contradicts the "tabletop" form factor, forcing an unnatural, hunched posture. Combined with red monochrome graphics, this creates a high probability of physical discomfort (eye strain, headaches), directly reducing engagement time. A gaming device that causes pain is fundamentally flawed.
2.  **Market Fit Failure:** It is neither truly portable (requires tabletop stability) nor a comfortable home console replacement. It occupies a "no-man's-land" use case.
3.  **Content Risk:** "Few games at launch" is fatal for a hardware platform relying on novelty. Without a strong library, the hardware's single gimmick (3D) cannot sustain interest.
4.  **Cannibalization & Timing:** With a true next-generation home console due within two years, resources spent here divert focus from the main revenue driver. The product risks confusing consumers just before a major launch.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital and brand reputation; focus entirely on the upcoming home console.
*   **Software Only:** Release 3D-enhanced games for the existing handheld, avoiding new hardware costs and ergonomics issues.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, sells poorly due to discomfort and lack of games, is discounted heavily within six months, and becomes a cautionary tale that distracts from the successful home console launch.

**Blockers:**
*   **Physical Discomfort:** The design inherently causes strain/headaches, violating the core requirement of a consumer entertainment device.
*   **Value Proposition:** The trade-off (monochrome, eyepiece, tabletop tether) offers less value than existing handhelds or waiting for the home console.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, developer alienation due to low install base, inventory write-downs.

The proposal attempts to solve a timing gap with a compromised product that actively degrades the user experience. The physical discomfort and lack of content are not mere risks; they are structural failures that cannot be mitigated post-launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
