First call: NO-GO. The plan relies on selling below cost to buy growth while burning cash on TV and acquisitions, a strategy that fails if capital markets tighten or shipping costs remain high.

Steelman: Aggressively capture market share and infrastructure now to establish a monopoly before competitors do, using public markets to fund temporary losses.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost, extreme risk, irreversible once capital is spent. Requires perpetual market openness.
2. **Do Nothing:** Preserve cash, fix unit economics, grow organically. Low risk, slower scale.
3. **Selective Expansion:** Raise capital but focus only on profitable niches and efficient logistics, avoiding below-cost pricing and massive TV spends. Lower cost, moderate risk, reversible.

Fit: Assumes the "north star" is market share at any cost. However, selling below cost with negative gross margins contradicts basic solvency unless dominance is guaranteed immediately, which the proposal's own risks (competitor spending, capital closure) deny.

Pre-mortem: The IPO window slams shut due to broader market correction. The company is left with massive warehouse debt, unsold inventory, and a cash burn rate it cannot cover because it priced products below cost. It cannot raise emergency funds due to negative margins. Bankruptcy follows within 18 months.

Evidence: The proposal admits to "negative gross margins," "high customer-acquisition cost," and the risk that "capital markets could close." Selling below cost requires infinite capital or immediate monopoly power; neither is guaranteed. The "cheaper" alternative (fixing margins) reaches the same upside (survival/growth) without the existential risk.

Blockers:
1. **Negative Unit Economics:** Selling below cost while shipping heavy, low-margin items creates a hole that deepens with every sale. This is a mathematical failure, not a risk.
2. **Capital Dependency:** The plan collapses entirely if "capital markets close," a stated risk. A strategy that fails completely under a plausible scenario is a blocker.
3. **Competitive Parity:** Competitors are also spending heavily; there is no guarantee this spend buys dominance rather than just inflating industry costs.

Risks: Execution difficulty, warehouse overcapacity, brand dilution.

The plan requires spending money the company may not be able to raise, on activities that lose money on every transaction, to fight competitors doing the same. This is not a bet; it is a guaranteed liquidation event if the single assumption of perpetual capital access fails.

DECISION: NO-GO
