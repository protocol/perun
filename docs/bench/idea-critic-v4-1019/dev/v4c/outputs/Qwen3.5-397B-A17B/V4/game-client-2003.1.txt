First call: GO. The core claim is that a mandatory client solves critical update and cheating failures that retail distribution cannot address, creating a foundation for a broader platform.

Steelman: A proprietary client is the only viable mechanism to enforce real-time anti-cheat integrity and deliver seamless patches, turning a fragmented retail product into a persistent service that eventually monetizes third-party titles.

Options analysis shows "do nothing" guarantees continued revenue loss from cheating and brand erosion due to broken update cycles. The alternative of patching via retailers is technically impossible for frequent updates. While a web-based approach avoids downloads, 2003 browser technology cannot support robust anti-cheat or large binary delivery. The proposed client beats these alternatives by owning the execution environment.

Fit is high; the strategy shifts from pure product sales to service retention, aligning with the north star of maximizing player lifetime value.

Pre-mortem: The initiative fails if early client instability drives players to pirated, unpatched versions before the network effect of the store takes hold. This is a two-way door; the client can be made optional for legacy titles if adoption stalls, though cheating will persist there. The cost of delay is immediate: every month without this system allows cheaters to ruin the ecosystem for legitimate paying customers, accelerating churn.

Evidence check: The proposal correctly identifies that retail boxes are static while cheating and game logic are dynamic. No internal data contradicts the necessity of an always-on verification layer for competitive integrity.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (broadband adoption, retail friction) are execution hurdles, not fundamental flaws. Broadband was already growing rapidly in 2003, and retail partners depend on the publisher's hit games more than the publisher depends on shelf space for long-tail revenue.

Risks: Initial user backlash and technical debt. These are mitigated by making the client mandatory only for *new* titles, preserving the installed base while migrating the future.

Kill criteria: If the client causes a >15% drop in new game activations within the first quarter compared to previous launches, pause mandatory enforcement for subsequent titles. Leading metric: Daily active users completing a match without reported cheating incidents.

DECISION: GO
