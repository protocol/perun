First call: NO-GO. Removing a universal, beloved feature (headphone jack) to force adoption of an unproven, expensive ($159), and easily lost accessory creates immediate customer anger without a guaranteed upside.

**Steelman:** Removing the jack accelerates the wireless future and allows for a sleeker device, while the new earbuds offer superior convenience via automatic pairing and a charging case that mitigates battery limits.

**Attack:**
1.  **Value Mismatch:** Customers perceive a downgrade: swapping a free, reliable wired option for a $159, fragile, easily lost device with only 5-hour battery life. The "odd" look invites ridicule, turning a status symbol into a joke.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" (keep the jack) retains customer goodwill and allows wireless earbuds to be an optional upsell rather than a forced migration. A cheaper, wired USB-C/Lightning adapter is a safer bridge.
3.  **Fit:** This forces a hardware revolution before the market is ready. The risk of alienating the core user base outweighs the potential gain from early adopters.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The launch is mocked for the earbud design; sales flop due to price; customers revolt over the missing jack, driving them to competitors who still include one. The company is seen as arrogant.
5.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the risks: ridicule, loss, high price, and anger. There is no evidence provided that customers *want* this trade-off.

**Blockers:**
*   **Negative Value Proposition:** The swap replaces a free, infinite-life, loss-proof standard with a costly, finite-life, loss-prone proprietary device.
*   **Unproven Demand:** No data suggests users are willing to pay $159 to solve a problem they don't yet have (no wires), especially when the solution has significant new drawbacks (loss, battery).

**Risks:** Market rejection, brand damage, competitor exploitation of the missing jack.

The plan fails as proposed because it removes a core utility to sell a superior-but-flawed replacement at a premium, betting the entire phone's reception on an accessory gamble. The "do nothing" or "optional adapter" approach reaches the same upside (wireless adoption) without the guaranteed downside of customer anger.

DECISION: NO-GO
