First call: GO. The proposal addresses a critical vulnerability: commodity trapping where buyers cannot differentiate the core value driver. By shifting demand pull to the consumer, the chip maker bypasses the computer makers' pricing power.

**Steelman:** If consumers demand the branded chip, computer makers are forced to include it to sell units, allowing the chip maker to capture margin and defend against clones despite being a component supplier.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary objection is that consumer advertising for a component is unprecedented. However, "unprecedented" is not a blocker; it is a signal of a blue-ocean opportunity where competitors are blind to the leverage point. The alternative—"do nothing"—guarantees continued erosion by clones using similar part numbers, as buyers remain ignorant. A "computer-maker led" approach fails because makers have no incentive to highlight a specific supplier over cost.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a growth stage requiring differentiation. If this fails, it will be because the consumer message was too technical or the rebate insufficient to change maker behavior. The cost of delay is high: every quarter without branding cements the "generic" perception, making future separation harder.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal facts state clones use similar part numbers and buyers are ignorant. These are the blockers for the status quo, not the proposal. The risks (resentment, budget share) are manageable execution details, not structural flaws. Resentment is irrelevant if the program drives sales; budget share is an investment, not a cost, if it secures pricing power.

**Decision:**
Blockers: NONE. The facts support the need to break buyer ignorance.
Risks: Execution complexity, maker pushback (mitigated by sales leverage).
Kill Criteria: If consumer awareness does not rise by 15% within 12 months, or if computer maker participation drops below 60%, terminate the rebate program. Leading metric: Percentage of retail ads featuring the logo.

The plan turns a hidden component into a demanded feature. It beats doing nothing.

DECISION: GO
