First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a search-ad company with no hardware expertise to spend at least $50 million on a six-person team building an OS in a market controlled by carriers and incumbent vendors, with no revenue model and a multi-year payback.

Steelman: Acquiring this team is the only way to secure a free, open mobile platform that will expand mobile web usage, thereby protecting and growing the core search-ad business against future platform封锁.

However, the blocker test fails immediately. The stated facts confirm the company has no hardware business, yet the acquisition requires building an entire mobile ecosystem (OS, handset partnerships, carrier deals) from scratch. The proposal admits carriers control distribution and handset makers have "no reason to adopt" this new platform. Without a guaranteed distribution channel, the bet that "free software" alone will drive adoption is an assumption contradicted by the stated market reality. The $50 million price tag for six people is unjustified without a clear path to overcoming the carrier/handset blockade, which the proposal explicitly lists as a risk without a mitigation strategy.

Alternatives like partnering with existing OS vendors or waiting for the market to mature offer lower cost and risk. The "do nothing" option preserves capital until the mobile web threat is more tangible or the distribution landscape shifts. The risks (no revenue model, adoption hurdles, carrier resistance) are not merely uncertainties; they are structural barriers that the current plan does not solve. A "go-if" condition cannot be met now because the fundamental distribution problem remains unsolved.

Blockers: The plan relies on handset makers and carriers adopting a new OS despite having "no reason" to do so, with no strategy to change this incentive. The $50M cost for a 6-person team with no revenue model is unsupported by the stated facts.
Risks: Multi-year payback, carrier resistance, lack of hardware expertise.

DECISION: NO-GO
