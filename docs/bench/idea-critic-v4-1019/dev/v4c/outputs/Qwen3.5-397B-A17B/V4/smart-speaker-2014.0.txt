First call: NO-GO. The company's core competency is retail logistics and e-commerce, not consumer hardware manufacturing or acoustic engineering, and the previous hardware failure indicates a systemic inability to execute in this domain.

Steelman: An always-listening speaker creates a new, high-frequency touchpoint that locks users into the retailer's ecosystem, driving recurring revenue and data advantages that a phone-based assistant cannot match due to proximity and dedicated hardware.

However, the blocker test fails on multiple fronts. First, the "always-on microphone" risk is not merely a customer sentiment issue; in 2014, this represents a fundamental privacy barrier with no established market precedent to prove demand. Second, the proposal relies on voice recognition technology that is explicitly admitted to often fail; shipping a core feature known to be unreliable is a direct path to brand erosion, compounding the damage from the poor smartphone launch. Third, the "invitation only" strategy assumes scarcity will drive desire, but without a proven product-market fit or a functioning category, this limits feedback loops necessary to fix the admitted technical flaws.

The alternative of "do nothing" or waiting for voice technology to mature (as competitors are doing with phone-based assistants) offers a safer path with zero capital expenditure on unproven hardware. The cheaper alternative is to integrate deeply with existing smartphone assistants rather than building a $199 hardware device that requires a complete new supply chain and support structure the company has already demonstrated it cannot manage effectively.

The pre-mortem is clear: The device launches, privacy concerns trigger a media backlash, voice commands fail frequently leading to user frustration, and the hardware becomes a costly inventory burden, forcing a write-down similar to the smartphone division. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a second consecutive hardware failure.

Blockers: 1) Core technology (voice recognition) admitted as unreliable. 2) No market precedent for always-on home audio privacy. 3) Proven organizational inability to launch hardware successfully.
Risks: Brand erosion, inventory write-downs, privacy backlash.

DECISION: NO-GO
