First call: NO-GO. Buying a shrinking handset division to solve an app-ecosystem deficit is a classic "owning the pipe" fallacy; developers follow users, not vertical integration.

Steelman: Acquiring the manufacturer secures the primary hardware outlet for the OS, preventing its immediate defection to the rival platform and allowing tight software-hardware optimization to improve user experience.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Risk the manufacturer defects, killing the OS immediately.
2. **Subsidize developers:** Pay for app ports directly (cheaper, targets the root cause: app gap).
3. **Acquire hardware:** High cost, integrates a declining business, distracts from software.

Fit: The stated north star is ecosystem viability. However, the proposal admits smartphone sales are falling and the rival has the apps. Hardware ownership does not create developer demand; it only captures a shrinking installed base.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition closes. Integration chaos consumes engineering bandwidth for 18 months. Sales continue to drop because the app gap widens relative to the leader. The company is left owning a legacy feature-phone business with high fixed costs and no software leverage.

Evidence: The proposal itself states sales are falling and the rival holds the developers. No evidence suggests owning the factory changes developer incentives. The €5.4B cost is a sunk bet on a dying volume stream.

Blockers:
1. **Causal Mismatch:** The core problem is the app ecosystem (software/network effect). The solution is hardware acquisition (manufacturing/supply chain). The proposal offers no mechanism by which owning the factory attracts developers faster than the rival, especially as sales fall.
2. **Declining Asset:** Buying a business with falling sales at a premium (€5.4B) destroys capital unless a specific turnaround exists; none is proven here.

Risks: Integration friction, competitor retaliation, continued developer apathy.

This is a two-way door mistake treated as a one-way save. The capital is better spent aggressively subsidizing the ecosystem or licensing the OS to other manufacturers to grow the user base, rather than nationalizing a failing partner.

DECISION: NO-GO
