First call: GO. The proposal trades a known, rapidly commoditizing cost (storage) for a high-value, defensible differentiator (searchable history) in a market where incumbents are constrained by legacy cost structures.

**Steelman:** By offering 1,000x the standard storage, the service renders the user habit of "deleting email" obsolete, creating a sticky, data-rich environment where precise ad targeting yields higher CPMs than generic banner ads, funding the infrastructure before competitors can react.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker claim is cost. However, in 2004, disk prices are plummeting. If the cost per GB is lower than the lifetime ad revenue per user, the unit economics work. The "privacy" risk is real but manageable via terms of service; the market has not yet hardened against content scanning. The "mature market" argument is weak because incumbents are trapped by low-storage norms; they cannot match this offer without rewriting their entire backend architecture. Doing nothing cedes the high-end free tier to a future competitor. A smaller pilot (e.g., 500MB) loses the psychological shock value required to disrupt user habits.

**Pre-mortem & Evidence:**
Failure mode: Ad revenue per user fails to cover the marginal cost of storage and compute, or privacy backlash triggers regulatory scrutiny. The proposal assumes text ads will be accepted; if users revolt, the revenue model collapses. However, the invitation-only launch acts as a circuit breaker, limiting blast radius while testing demand and cost basis. The "no direct revenue" is a feature, not a bug; it prioritizes scale to train the ad algorithm.

**Blockers:** NONE. The costs are hypothetical but trending favorable; the privacy risk is a reputational bet, not a technical impossibility. The incumbents' inability to pivot quickly is a verified strategic opening.
**Risks:** Privacy backlash, ad-tech maturity, storage cost volatility. These are managed via the invitation-only cap and kill criteria (churn rate vs. cost per user).

The asymmetry is clear: the downside is contained capital loss; the upside is owning the world's communication index.

DECISION: GO
