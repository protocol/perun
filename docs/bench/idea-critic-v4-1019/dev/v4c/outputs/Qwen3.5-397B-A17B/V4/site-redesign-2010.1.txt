First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines maximum engineering risk with maximum business risk (big-bang migration) while alienating the core user base that drives traffic.

Steelman: A complete rewrite with a publisher-focused algorithm and immediate full migration is the only way to reverse traffic decline and modernize the platform before competitors capture the market.

**Blockers:**
1. **Contradictory Evidence:** The proposal admits the core community values existing voting mechanics, yet the plan changes how stories surface and favors publishers. This directly attacks the primary asset (loyal community) causing the very slowdown it aims to fix.
2. **No Reversibility:** Moving all users at once eliminates rollback capability. If the new algorithm fails or the community revolts, the site cannot revert, potentially causing irreversible traffic collapse.
3. **Unproven Upside:** There is no evidence that favoring publishers over community voting will increase traffic; historically, such shifts on social news sites drive power users away.

**Risks:**
- Large engineering effort diverts resources from fixing the actual slowdown causes.
- Competitor growth accelerates if the launch fails.
- Community backlash could be immediate and permanent.

**Alternatives Considered:**
- *Do nothing:* Traffic continues slowing, but community remains intact.
- *Staged rollout:* Test new surfacing on a subset of users to validate the "publisher favor" hypothesis before full migration.
- *Iterative improvement:* Tweak the existing codebase to address specific performance bottlenecks without a full rewrite.

The proposal fails because it assumes a total replacement is necessary without validating the core hypothesis (that publisher content drives more traffic than community voting) and removes all safety nets. A cheaper, safer alternative (staged testing) exists that reaches the same upside (validating new algorithms) without the existential risk of a big-bang failure. The stated facts show the target users (core community) likely do not want the proposed change, and the plan offers no answer to this contradiction.

Kill criteria for any future attempt: Retain 90% of daily active power users after 30 days of staged rollout; if not, pivot back to community-centric mechanics.

DECISION: NO-GO
