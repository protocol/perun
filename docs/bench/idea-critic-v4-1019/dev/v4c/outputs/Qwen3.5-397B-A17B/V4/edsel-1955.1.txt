First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a massive, irreversible infrastructure build (new dealer network) and a fixed 1957 launch date despite an acknowledged recession risk and a segment that "could shrink."

**Steelman:** Capturing the growing mid-priced gap requires a distinct brand and dedicated dealers to avoid diluting existing lines, justifying the upfront spend to secure market share before competitors.

**Attack:**
1.  **Macro Mismatch:** Launching a capital-intensive, luxury-adjacent product line precisely when a recession threatens is historically fatal. Consumers trade down in downturns; this plan trades up.
2.  **Fixed Timing:** The 1957 debut is a hard constraint. If the recession hits in 1957 (as cycles suggest), the brand launches into a collapsing market with high fixed costs.
3.  **Channel Bloat:** Building a new dealer network from scratch creates enormous fixed overhead before a single unit is sold. In a downturn, underutilized dealers become liabilities, not assets.
4.  **Cannibalization:** The risk of overlap with existing low/premium lines is high. Without a recession-proof value proposition, the new brand will simply steal sales from the parent company while incurring duplicate infrastructure costs.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; let competitors fight for the shrinking mid-market.
*   **Badge Engineering:** Use existing dealers and platforms to create a "mid-priced" trim under current brands, avoiding new infrastructure.
*   **Delay:** Wait for economic clarity before committing to dealers.

**Pre-mortem:** It is 1958. The recession hit. Sales are 40% of forecast. The new dealers are bankrupt or demanding bailouts. The brand is perceived as overpriced. The company burns cash supporting a ghost network.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Timing:** Launching a high-fixed-cost venture immediately prior to a probable recession contradicts basic cyclical strategy.
*   **Infrastructure Rigidity:** Committing to a new dealer network removes the flexibility to scale down if the "growing" segment shrinks.

**Risks:** Brand overlap, marketing inefficiency, execution delay.

**Decision Logic:** The plan fails the "recession test." A sound operator delays the irreversible capex (dealers) or stages the launch until the macro environment is verified. Proceeding as proposed invites catastrophic loss.

DECISION: NO-GO
