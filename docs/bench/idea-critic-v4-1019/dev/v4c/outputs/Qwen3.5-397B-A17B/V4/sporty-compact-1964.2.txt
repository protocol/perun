First call: GO. The proposal targets a verified demographic shift (postwar boom entering market) with a capital-efficient architecture (reuse reliable compact mechanics) and a revenue-maximizing model (low entry price, high-margin options).

**Steelman:** Leverage existing low-cost reliability to deliver aspirational styling for a massive, underserved youth cohort, using customization to fund heavy marketing while minimizing upfront tooling risk.

**Attack & Rebuttal:**
1.  **Unproven Demand:** Critics argue sporty small cars are unproven at volume. *Rebuttal:* The "unproven" status is the opportunity; the demographic wave is a fact. The low base price reduces the penalty for being wrong, while the options list captures upside if right. Waiting for proof cedes the segment to competitors.
2.  **"Cheap" Perception:** Sharing parts with an economy car may tarnish the image. *Rebuttal:* The distinct "long hood, short deck" styling and two-door configuration visually sever the link to the dull donor car. Reliability is a feature, not a bug, provided the aesthetic promise is kept.
3.  **Heavy Launch Spend:** Spending before sales are known is risky. *Rebuttal:* Without a massive launch, the "dull" donor platform drags the new model down. The advertising *creates* the category. The risk is not spending enough to make the styling stick.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Cede the youth market as the boom peaks.
*   *New Platform:* Prohibitively expensive and slow; misses the demographic window.
*   *Sports Car:* Too expensive for the target volume; fails the "low price" constraint.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited are inherent to category creation, not flaws in the logic. The plan explicitly addresses the cost risk via parts sharing and the revenue risk via options.

**Kill Criteria:** If option take-rate falls below 40% within six months, the margin model fails; pivot to a single well-equipped trim. If first-year sales do not reach 100,000 units, the cultural hook failed; cease specialized marketing.

**Decision:** The proposal converts a demographic inevitability into a product advantage with capped downside and uncapped upside. The risks are manageable execution details, not strategic blockers.

DECISION: GO
