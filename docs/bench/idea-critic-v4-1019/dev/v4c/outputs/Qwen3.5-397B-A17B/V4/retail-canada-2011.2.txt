First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve three unproven variables (cross-border logistics, new inventory system, foreign regulation) with maximum capital exposure and speed, violating basic expansion hygiene.

**Steelman:** Leveraging existing leases accelerates market entry to capture price arbitrage before the entrenched competitor reacts, betting that domestic operational excellence transfers quickly to a neighboring bilingual market.

**Attack:**
1.  **Compound Unproven Dependencies:** The plan couples a *new* supply chain with a *new* inventory system in a *new* regulatory environment simultaneously. If the inventory system fails (high probability for unproven tech), the new supply chain has no brain, halting 130+ stores.
2.  **Pace vs. Learning:** Opening ~150 stores in two years allows no time for iterative learning. A single error in bilingual labeling or local tax compliance, replicated at this speed, creates a systemic liability before a fix can be deployed.
3.  **Competitive Reality:** An entrenched dominant competitor with higher prices likely maintains them due to localized cost structures or service expectations, not inefficiency. Undercutting them requires a cost advantage the retailer does not yet possess given the new logistics overhead.
4.  **No "Do Nothing" or Staged Alternative:** The proposal ignores a pilot (e.g., 5–10 stores) to validate the inventory system and supply chain. It forces a binary outcome: total success or catastrophic failure across the entire international footprint.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Core Mechanism:** The inventory management system is explicitly "not yet proven." Deploying it as the backbone for 150 stores immediately is a factual failure point.
*   **Capacity Contradiction:** The retailer must "learn" complex cross-border dynamics while executing a hyper-aggressive rollout. These are mutually exclusive; you cannot learn at the required speed without breaking the model.

**Risks:** Price perception misalignment, regulatory fines, supply chain rupture.

**Verdict:** The absence of a pilot phase and the reliance on an unproven inventory system for a massive simultaneous launch constitutes a fatal flaw. The plan must be revised to include a staged rollout validating the supply chain and software first.

DECISION: NO-GO
