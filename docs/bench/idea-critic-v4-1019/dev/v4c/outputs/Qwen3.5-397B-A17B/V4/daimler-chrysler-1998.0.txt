First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a cyclical peak and ignores fatal cultural integration risks.

**Steelman:** Combining a premium German engineer with a profitable US truck specialist creates a global scale leader with complementary portfolios, sharing platforms to cut costs while accessing the mass market.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cyclical Trap:** The US partner's profitability hinges entirely on a light-truck boom. Buying at the 1998 peak exposes the combined entity to massive downside when the cycle inevitably turns, dragging the premium brand's stability into a volume crisis.
2.  **Cultural Incompatibility:** "Merger of equals" is a fantasy here. German hierarchical engineering precision clashes fundamentally with US consensus-driven, cost-focused mass production. History shows these cultures rarely integrate; they fracture, causing decision paralysis and talent flight.
3.  **Brand Dilution:** Platform sharing between a luxury marque and a mass-market truck builder risks eroding the premium brand's exclusivity, its core value driver, without guaranteeing mass-market success against entrenched domestic rivals.
4.  **Governance Failure:** A combined board with equal representation invites deadlock. Without a clear single decision-maker, strategic pivots during a downturn will be too slow to survive.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve the German firm's high margins and brand equity; wait for the US cycle to correct before considering asset purchases.
*   **Joint Venture:** Test platform sharing on specific components without merging balance sheets or cultures.
*   **Minority Stake:** Gain market insight without assuming full liability for the US partner's cyclical exposure.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-merger, the truck market crashes. The US division requires massive bailouts. Cultural friction prevents necessary cost-cutting. The premium brand's quality reputation suffers due to rushed parts sharing. The stock collapses, and the "merger of equals" is revealed as a distressed acquisition.

**Blockers:**
*   The US partner's profitability is tied to a verified cyclical peak, making the valuation dangerous.
*   No credible plan exists to reconcile fundamentally opposed corporate cultures and labor models.
*   "Merger of equals" structure lacks a mechanism to break governance deadlocks.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, execution paralysis, cyclical downturn timing.

The risks are not merely operational; they are structural and existential. The proposal fails to address the inevitability of the cycle turning or the near-impossibility of the cultural merge.

DECISION: NO-GO
