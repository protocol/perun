First call: GO. A dying OS and two failed internal attempts create an existential blocker that only an external, modern core can solve; the alternative is liquidation.

**Steelman:** Acquiring a proven, object-oriented OS core is the only viable path to stop market share collapse, as internal development has demonstrably failed twice. The founder's expertise is essential for migration, outweighing past interpersonal friction.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Alternatives:** Licensing is a temporary fix that cedes control; continuing internal development ignores the base rate of two prior failures. "Do nothing" guarantees irrelevance. Acquisition is the only two-way door that offers a survival chance.
2.  **Risks vs. Blockers:**
    *   *Low sales of target:* Irrelevant. We are buying the technology and talent, not their revenue stream. Their failure was likely go-to-market, not engineering.
    *   *Migration risk:* Real, but inevitable with any new core. The founder's return mitigates this specific execution risk.
    *   *Founder friction:* A management challenge, not a technical blocker. Advisors can be managed; dead products cannot.
    *   *Price:* High relative to target revenue, but negligible relative to the acquirer's potential loss. The cost of inaction exceeds the acquisition price.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be due to cultural rejection of the founder or slow developer adoption, not the technology itself.
4.  **Fit:** Directly addresses the "dated OS" root cause.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented proves the plan fails as proposed. The risks are execution challenges, not disqualifiers.
**Risks:** Cultural integration, developer migration speed, brand perception. These become kill criteria: if the new OS isn't developer-ready within 18 months, pivot.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal solves the only problem that matters: the lack of a modern core. Every objection raised is a known variable that can be managed, whereas the status quo is fatal. The price is an investment in survival, not a purchase of current earnings.

DECISION: GO
