First call: NO-GO. The proposed price is prohibitive, performance is inadequate, and internal competition exists.

**Steelman:** A premium, mouse-driven office computer justifies a $10,000 price point despite slow speed and limited software by offering a revolutionary interface that boosts productivity enough to offset costs.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $10,000, the unit costs several times more than leading business PCs. Without a robust software library to demonstrate immediate ROI, office managers cannot justify the capital expenditure. The "graphical interface" is a novelty, not a proven productivity multiplier at this price differential.
2.  **Performance Bottleneck:** "Runs slowly" is a fatal flaw for business tools where throughput equals money. A slow GUI frustrates users more than a fast command line.
3.  **Internal Cannibalization:** A cheaper machine with a similar interface is already in development internally. Launching the expensive, slower version first damages credibility and sales of the upcoming superior/cheaper model.
4.  **Software Ecosystem:** Business adoption relies on applications (spreadsheets, word processors). "Little third-party software" means the hardware has no utility for core business functions upon launch.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for the cheaper internal project to mature; avoid brand damage from an overpriced, slow launch.
*   **Pivot:** Target a niche vertical willing to pay for graphics specifically, not general office work, and lower the price.
*   **Delay:** Postpone launch until software partners commit and performance is optimized.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sells fewer than 1,000 units due to price and speed, developers ignore the platform due to low install base, and the internal cheaper team loses momentum due to market confusion.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price:** $10,000 is unsupported by value proposition (speed/software).
*   **Performance:** Slow speed contradicts business efficiency needs.
*   **Strategy:** Internal cheaper alternative makes this launch self-defeating.

**Risks:** Market rejection, developer apathy, internal resource conflict.

**Conclusion:** The proposal fails on price, performance, and strategic fit. It cannot beat "do nothing" or the internal alternative.

DECISION: NO-GO
