First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and market trajectory contradict the core premise.

**Steelman:** A premium, niche service for global travelers can generate sufficient margin to service debt before terrestrial networks achieve ubiquitous coverage.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Erosion:** Ground cellular is spreading fast. The target window for "no alternative" is closing quarterly, not yearly. If coverage gaps vanish in major business hubs, the $3,000 handset becomes a hard sell.
2.  **Unit Economics Failure:** At "several dollars a minute," usage will be restricted to emergencies. Debt service on $5 billion requires massive, consistent volume, not sporadic high-margin calls. The revenue model cannot support the capital structure.
3.  **Product-Market Fit:** Bulky, indoor-incompatible hardware at luxury prices limits the total addressable market to a fraction of what is needed to break even.
4.  **Execution Risk:** Sales channels are not ready. Building a global distribution network for a niche, expensive product while burning cash on debt service is a likely failure point.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid sinking further capital into a misaligned model.
*   **Pivot:** Target government/military contracts (less price sensitive, higher volume guarantees) rather than consumer travelers.
*   **Delay:** Wait for hardware to shrink and costs to drop, though this risks obsolescence by terrestrial networks.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-launch, subscriber growth is 10% of projections. Terrestrial roaming agreements have solved the coverage gap in key markets. The company defaults on debt payments because the premium consumer market was too small to sustain the $5B burden.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Mismatch:** The projected revenue from a limited, high-price user base cannot mathematically service $5B in debt within the required timeframe given the rapid erosion of the unique value proposition (coverage).
*   **Product Limitation:** Indoor failure renders the device useless for a significant portion of business use cases, further shrinking the viable market.

**Risks:** Regulatory hurdles, hardware supply chain delays, competitor pricing wars.

**Conclusion:** The proposal relies on a shrinking window of opportunity and a revenue model that cannot support its capital structure. The risks are not merely executional; they are fundamental to the business case.

DECISION: NO-GO
