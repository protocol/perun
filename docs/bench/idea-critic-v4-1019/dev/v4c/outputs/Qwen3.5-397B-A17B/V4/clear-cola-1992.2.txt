First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores the core risk that cola flavor is psychologically tied to color, making nationwide repetition unlikely despite positive test markets.

**Steelman:** A clear cola leverages "purity" trends to capture novelty seekers and health-conscious drinkers, expanding the portfolio before competitors do.

**Attack:**
1.  **Flavor-Color Association:** Consumers equate dark color with cola taste. Removing color may break the flavor expectation, causing immediate rejection after the first sip. Test markets often fail to capture this long-term sensory disconnect.
2.  **Novelty vs. Repeat:** Positive test results likely reflect curiosity, not loyalty. Without repeat purchases, the large marketing spend yields no lifetime value.
3.  **Cannibalization:** A failed extension dilutes the flagship brand's equity without generating net new volume. If it fails, it signals innovation weakness.
4.  **Competitor Response:** Rivals can copy the clear formula instantly if it succeeds, eroding first-mover advantage while the proposer bears the education cost.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve flagship margins; avoid brand confusion.
*   **Limited Regional Rollout:** Test repeat rates in a specific demographic before national spend.
*   **Sub-Brand Launch:** Release under a different name to protect the flagship if it fails.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales have collapsed. Cause: Customers bought once, found the taste "wrong" due to lack of color cue, and never returned. Marketing spend is sunk; shelf space is lost.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Repeat Rate:** The proposal relies on test market *response*, not verified *repeat purchase data* over a sufficient period to rule out novelty decay.
*   **Psychological Mismatch:** The core premise (purity via clarity) directly conflicts with established sensory expectations for cola, a fact not addressed by the plan.

**Risks:** Cannibalization, competitor copying, brand dilution.

**Kill Criteria:** If repeat purchase rate in test markets is below 20% after 8 weeks, or if blind taste tests show a significant preference drop when color is removed, the project stops.

The proposal demands a nationwide launch based on initial interest, ignoring the critical gap between trial and habit. The psychological barrier of color-flavor association is a fundamental flaw not mitigated by the current plan.

DECISION: NO-GO
