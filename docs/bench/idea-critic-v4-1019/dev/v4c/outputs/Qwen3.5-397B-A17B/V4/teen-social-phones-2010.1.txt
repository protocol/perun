First call: NO-GO. The proposal demands full-price data plans and excludes apps/games for price-sensitive youth, a fundamental mismatch between cost structure and target market value.

**Steelman:** Launching a restricted, carrier-locked social device immediately leverages an acquired team's momentum before the main platform arrives, capturing early social engagement without waiting for a full app ecosystem.

**Attack:** The steelman collapses under basic unit economics. Requiring a "full smartphone data plan" for a device with "no app store and no games" forces the target demographic to pay a premium for utility they cannot access. Price-sensitive users will reject a high-cost plan for a feature-limited device, especially when the company's own robust platform launches later that year. The "no games" constraint removes the primary driver of mobile adoption for youth. Furthermore, internal conflict suggests execution risk; forcing a delayed project out the door with these constraints likely guarantees a public failure that damages the brand before the main launch. The "do nothing" option (waiting for the main platform) offers a superior product at a likely better price point with zero channel conflict.

**Blockers:**
1. **Economic Mismatch:** The cost of the required data plan equals a smartphone's cost, while the device offers a fraction of the utility. This pricing model is mathematically incompatible with a "price-sensitive" demographic.
2. **Value Proposition Void:** Excluding apps and games from a "social phone" for young people removes the core use case, leaving no justification for the high recurring cost.

**Risks:** Internal team conflict, cannibalization of the upcoming main platform launch, carrier relationship strain if activation targets are missed.

**Kill Criteria:** If the projected attach rate for the full data plan is below 60% within the first quarter, or if customer acquisition cost exceeds lifetime value due to churn, the project must be terminated immediately.

**Decision:** The proposal fails to address the basic economic reality of its target market. It asks the most price-sensitive users to pay the highest prices for the least functionality.

DECISION: NO-GO
