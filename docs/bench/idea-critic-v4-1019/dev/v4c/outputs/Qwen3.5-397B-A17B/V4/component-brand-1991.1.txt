First call: GO. The proposal converts an invisible commodity into a requested brand asset, shifting power from computer makers to the chip supplier.

**Steelman:** By subsidizing OEM ads that feature the chip's logo, the company creates direct consumer pull, forcing computer makers to include the chip to sell their own products, thereby securing volume and pricing power despite clone competition.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue losing margin to clones as buyers remain indifferent; computer makers switch suppliers based solely on price.
2.  **Trade discounts:** Lower prices for OEMs. Risk: Margin erosion without brand loyalty; easily matched by competitors.
3.  **Proposed Campaign:** High upfront cost but builds an economic moat via consumer preference. It beats "do nothing" because indifference guarantees commoditization.

**Fit:** Assumes the goal is long-term margin protection and market dominance rather than short-term budget preservation. This aligns with surviving the "clone" threat described.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if computer makers unite to reject the branding requirements or if consumers ignore the logo. However, the financial incentive (subsidized ads) lowers the barrier for OEM participation, making unified resistance unlikely. The "unprecedented" nature of the risk is precisely the source of potential advantage; if it were common, it would offer no differentiation.

**Evidence:** The proposal cites specific mechanics: logo usage tied to ad-cost sharing. The risk of "resentment" is mitigated by the fact that the program pays for part of the OEMs' advertising, turning a constraint into a subsidy. The risk of "no sales benefit" is inherent to all new marketing but is outweighed by the certainty of commoditization if nothing is done.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented proves the plan fails as proposed. The lack of precedent is a risk, not a blocker.
**Risks:** OEM pushback, budget drain, consumer apathy. These are managed by the program's voluntary nature (OEMs opt-in for the subsidy) and the clear alternative (price wars).

**Kill Criteria:** If, after 12 months, consumer awareness surveys show <20% recognition or OEM participation remains below 50% of key partners, terminate the subsidy. Leading metric: Percentage of OEM ads featuring the logo.

DECISION: GO
