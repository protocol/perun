First call: GO. The proposal targets an underserved segment (youth) with a focused value proposition (portability, price) by stripping non-essential features (recording, speaker) to achieve cost leadership, despite channel skepticism.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, affordable playback-only device unlocks a new usage context (mobile, personal listening) for youth, creating a volume market that multi-function hi-fi systems cannot serve due to price and size.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Channel Bias:** Dealers project existing hi-fi buyer behaviors onto a new demographic. Their doubt reflects a lack of imagination, not market reality.
2.  **Social Risk:** "Antisocial" listening is actually a feature for privacy and personal space, appealing directly to the target youth market.
3.  **Competition:** While built from existing parts, the specific integration for extreme portability and low cost creates a first-mover brand association before copycats arrive.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the emerging mobile audio category to future entrants. A smaller pilot run fails to achieve the critical price point required for mass youth adoption.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the price misses the youth budget or if the "no recording" constraint is perceived as a defect rather than a cost-saving enabler. However, the large initial run is a prerequisite for the price strategy, not a separate risk.

**Evidence & Fit:** The proposal relies on the known existence of cassette technology and the distinct economic constraints of the youth demographic. No external research is needed to validate that lower prices expand markets; the strategy fits a volume-over-margin approach to capture a new user base.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The objections raised are hypotheses about behavior, not facts proving impossibility. The technical feasibility is confirmed ("built from existing parts").
*   **Risks:** Dealer resistance, copycats, social perception. These are managed by the speed of market penetration and the aggressive pricing enabled by the large run.

The plan survives the attack because the "risks" are merely the inertia of the old business model clashing with a new market creation strategy. The lack of prior market research is acceptable because the product creates its own category; you cannot research demand for an experience that does not yet exist. The large production run is the only way to hit the price point that makes the concept viable.

DECISION: GO
