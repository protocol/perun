First call: GO. The claim addresses a massive, verified market gap: families transitioning from scooters to enclosed vehicles. The price point creates a new category rather than competing directly with used cars on features.

Steelman: A dedicated factory enables the volume required to sustain a $2,500 price point, making car ownership accessible to millions who currently rely on two-wheelers, provided safety perceptions are managed.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Families remain on scooters; the manufacturer misses the emerging mass-market segment entirely.
2. Used car market: Unreliable and lacks the specific "family safety" branding of a new vehicle.
3. Higher-priced new car: Excludes the target demographic entirely.

Fit: Aligns with a vision of mass motorization. The "cheapest" label is a double-edged sword but defines the category.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if land protests halt construction indefinitely, or if a single high-profile safety incident confirms fears that "cheap" means "deadly." Cost of delay allows competitors to enter or economic shifts to erode the low-income customer base.

Evidence: The proposal cites specific risks (land protests, safety perception, alternative transport). These are known variables, not hidden blockers. The math of volume manufacturing supports the price if the factory runs.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (protests, safety doubts, stigma) are execution challenges and market dynamics, not factual contradictions that make the plan impossible as stated. They require mitigation (PR, safety testing, community engagement), not plan abandonment.

Risks: Land acquisition delays; brand perception of low quality; competition from subsidized used vehicles.

Kill Criteria: If land acquisition is not resolved within 12 months, pause construction. If initial safety tests fail regulatory minimums, do not launch. Monitor monthly sales volume against the break-even point for the first two years.

The plan creates a new market segment. The risks are manageable operational hurdles, not fundamental flaws in the thesis.

DECISION: GO
