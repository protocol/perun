First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits irreversible capital before validating the core business model in a single market.

Steelman: Rapid nationwide scaling creates an unassailable logistics moat and captures market share before competitors can react, leveraging abundant venture capital to buy dominance.

However, the plan fails the blocker test. The proposal explicitly admits the company has "no operating track record" and has not yet "learning the unit economics of the first" market. Groceries are defined as "low-margin," meaning small operational inefficiencies destroy profitability. Building "highly automated, very large regional warehouses" in "over a dozen" areas creates massive fixed costs and capital intensity before proving that customers will adopt the service at a density high enough to support the "30-minute delivery slot" promise.

The primary blocker is the contradiction between the stated lack of unit economic knowledge and the commitment of $1 billion to a specific, rigid infrastructure. If the first city reveals that 30-minute slots are too costly to fulfill or that customer density is insufficient, the company will be locked into a dozen expensive, specialized warehouses that cannot be easily repurposed. This is a one-way door decision made with zero validated learning.

A cheaper, safer alternative exists: fully validate unit economics, customer retention, and delivery density in the initial metropolitan area using flexible, lower-cost fulfillment centers. Only after proving a profitable path in one city should capital be deployed to replicate the model. The current plan bets the entire company on unproven assumptions regarding margin, density, and automation efficacy. The risks of capital intensity and thin margins are not merely theoretical; they are guaranteed to cause failure if the underlying unit economics do not support the heavy infrastructure load.

Blockers: Committing $1B to fixed infrastructure without validated unit economics from the pilot market; scaling a low-margin model before proving operational efficiency in a single location.
Risks: Customer adoption rates, actual delivery costs per order, technology failure in automation, competitor response.

DECISION: NO-GO
