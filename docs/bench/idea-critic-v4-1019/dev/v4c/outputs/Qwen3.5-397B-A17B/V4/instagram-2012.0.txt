First call: NO-GO. The price implies a valuation with no revenue, no business model, and extreme execution risk, violating basic capital discipline.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the sole mobile-native threat neutralizes a competitor that could erode the core business's long-term dominance, buying time to fix internal mobile weaknesses.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financials:** Paying $1B for ~12 employees and zero revenue is speculative insanity without a proven monetization path. The "growth" is on a single platform with a fresh, unproven launch on the second.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows the market to validate the model first. If the app fails, the cost is zero. If it succeeds, the buyer can build a clone or acquire later at a clearer valuation.
3.  **Fit & Culture:** Integrating a tiny, culture-heavy startup into a large incumbent often destroys the very value being bought. The proposal admits this risk but offers no mitigation.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** In two years, the app's growth stalls due to platform shifts or culture clash, leaving the buyer with a $1B write-down and a distracted leadership team, while the core mobile product remains weak.
5.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no business model" and "weak mobile product." Buying a competitor to hide internal weakness is a strategy of desperation, not strength. Regulatory review adds uncertainty to a deal with no immediate financial return.

**Blockers:**
*   **Valuation Mismatch:** No revenue or business model exists to justify a $1B price tag based on current facts.
*   **Strategic Desperation:** The primary driver is fear of a "threat," not a clear path to value creation.
*   **Execution Risk:** High probability of cultural destruction and failure to integrate, admitted in the proposal itself.

**Risks:** Market shift, regulatory block, talent exodus, continued mobile weakness.

**Kill Criteria:** None proposed; the deal lacks any metric to define success post-acquisition other than "not failing immediately."

This is a panic buy, not a strategic investment. The absence of a business model and the astronomical price relative to any tangible asset constitute a factual failure of the proposal's core logic.

DECISION: NO-GO
