First call: NO-GO. The plan contradicts the brand's core identity and customer value proposition without a mechanism to overcome it.

**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can temporarily overwrite brand perception, forcing adults to trial a premium burger despite the chain's "kids/value" reputation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit:** The proposal ignores the stated north star: the chain is defined by a "child-centered image" and "value." Adults currently view it as "fast, not premium." A price increase directly attacks the "value" pillar, while the brand equity actively repels the "premium" aspiration.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves the profitable core. A "better way" would be testing a premium item in adult-heavy markets with minimal spend to validate demand before committing the "largest budget in industry history."
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The campaign launches, awareness spikes, but conversion fails. Adults perceive the high price as incongruent with the toy-driven brand, viewing it as overpriced rather than premium. Regular buyers are priced out. The massive sunk cost of the ad budget yields no repeat business, damaging the core value perception.
4.  **Evidence:** The risks listed *in the proposal itself* (adults see it as fast/not premium, price deters buyers) are treated as known facts. The plan offers no solution to these specific, stated contradictions, relying entirely on spending volume to solve a positioning problem.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Facts:** The proposal admits adults see the brand as "fast, not premium" and "value" oriented, yet proposes a "premium" price point with no strategy to bridge this gap other than ad spend.
*   **Unproven Demand:** Committing the "largest advertising budget" before validating if the target demographic wants premium food from *this specific chain* violates basic capital allocation logic.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, alienation of the core family demographic, massive financial loss on unproven demand.

**Kill Criteria:** If a small-scale pilot does not achieve a 15% repeat purchase rate among adults within 3 months, stop.

**Decision:** The plan relies on brute force spending to solve a fundamental brand-positioning mismatch that the proposal itself identifies as a critical risk. It fails to beat "do nothing" or a staged pilot.

DECISION: NO-GO
