First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a fixed public schedule for unproven core technologies without a demand signal or regulatory mandate, creating asymmetric downside.

**Steelman:** A bold, fixed-date commitment forces the organization to solve novel battery and transmission challenges faster than any competitor, securing a decade-long market lead if durability holds.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Technical & Financial):** The core claim relies on nickel-metal-hydride (NiMH) durability in volume production, which is explicitly "unproven." Committing to a hard 1997 launch date before validating battery cycle life creates a near-certain risk of massive warranty exposure or a delayed launch that destroys credibility. A fixed schedule for unproven chemistry is a factual contradiction; you cannot guarantee a date for a variable you cannot yet measure.
2.  **Market Mismatch:** With cheap fuel and no regulatory mandate, there is no near-term demand signal. The cost premium of a novel hybrid system cannot be recovered in a market indifferent to fuel efficiency. The "first mover" advantage is worthless if the product is too expensive or unreliable for the current economic reality.
3.  **Better Way:** The optimal path is a limited technical pilot or a "compliance car" lease program to gather real-world durability data without a mass-production commitment. This preserves the option value of the technology while avoiding the catastrophe of a public failure.

**Pre-mortem:** It is 1998. The car launched late, the battery packs are failing at 40,000 miles, and the warranty costs exceed the vehicle margin. The brand is associated with unreliability, and competitors who waited for the technology to mature are now entering with cheaper, proven systems.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** Unproven battery durability combined with a fixed public launch date; no economic rationale (cheap fuel, no mandate) to support the cost premium.
*   **Risks:** High warranty exposure, brand damage from delay/failure, capital misallocation.

The absence of a pilot, a validated battery lifecycle, or a demand signal constitutes a fatal flaw in a mass-production commitment. The plan fails the basic operator test of matching risk to evidence.

DECISION: NO-GO
