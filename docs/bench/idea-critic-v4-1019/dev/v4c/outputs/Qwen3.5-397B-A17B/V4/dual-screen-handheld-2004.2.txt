First call: GO. The proposal avoids a direct hardware war against a superior rival by creating a distinct value proposition: dual-screen interaction and touch input at a lower price point.

Steelman: By leveraging unique form factors and software innovation rather than raw graphical power, the company can expand the market to non-core players while maintaining legacy support, effectively neutralizing the rival's graphical advantage through differentiated gameplay.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (developer confusion, unproven touch mechanics, gimmick perception) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The proposal explicitly accepts the cost of two screens but offsets it with a lower target price, implying a viable margin structure. No evidence suggests the target price is below cost or that the installed base cannot support two SKUs.

Risks:
1.  **Developer Friction:** Unusual hardware requires new tools and mindsets.
    *   *Mitigation:* Provide robust first-party titles demonstrating the dual-screen mechanic to set the standard.
2.  **Market Perception:** Dismissal as a gimmick.
    *   *Mitigation:* Marketing must focus on specific gameplay innovations (e.g., inventory on one screen, action on the other) rather than hardware specs.
3.  **Cannibalization:** Keeping the older handheld may split the audience.
    *   *Mitigation:* Position the older unit as the entry-level budget option and the new unit as the premium innovation, clearly segmenting the price points.

Alternatives considered:
*   *Match rival's graphics:* High cost, late entry, fighting on enemy turf. (Rejected)
*   *Do nothing:* Cede the handheld market to the rival. (Rejected)
*   *Proposed path:* Differentiate on interaction and price. (Selected)

The "gimmick" risk is inherent to any innovation; the kill criterion is whether flagship software can demonstrate indispensable dual-screen utility within the first year. The cost risk is managed by the explicit lower-price strategy relative to the rival, suggesting a disciplined bill-of-materials approach. Since no factual blocker exists (e.g., impossible price point, technology unavailability), the plan proceeds as a strategic pivot to broaden the audience.

DECISION: GO
