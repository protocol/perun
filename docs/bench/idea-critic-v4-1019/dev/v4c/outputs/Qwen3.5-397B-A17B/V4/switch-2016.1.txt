First call: GO. The hybrid form factor directly addresses the eroding handheld market while leveraging existing IP, turning the "weak specs" constraint into a portability feature rather than a deficit.

**Steelman:** A differentiated hybrid device captures both home and portable play, creating a unique value proposition that powerful but stationary rivals cannot match, relying on strong first-party software to drive hardware adoption despite modest specs.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal acknowledges risks (specs, confusion, supply) but offers a coherent strategic pivot. The "confusing positioning" risk is mitigated by the clear "hybrid" utility, distinct from the previous failed console's identity crisis. The thin launch lineup is a known constraint for new hardware, not a factual contradiction of the plan's viability.

**Risks:** Third-party support may lag due to architectural differences; supply constraints could limit initial revenue; the price point may feel high relative to raw power. These are execution risks, not fundamental flaws.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the hybrid concept is marketed as a compromised home console rather than a premium portable that docks, or if first-party titles delay past launch. The "short window" is real but inherent to the industry; waiting for perfect conditions guarantees obsolescence.

**Alternatives:**
1. *Do nothing:* Handheld line continues eroding; no recovery path. (Higher risk)
2. *Powerful stationary console:* Direct competition with established rivals using weakened brand equity. (Higher cost, lower differentiation)
3. *Cheap low-end handheld:* Commoditized market, low margins, fails to solve home console absence. (Lower upside)

The proposed path beats "do nothing" by offering a unique market position. It does not need to beat rival power metrics to succeed; it needs to own the "play anywhere" niche. The financial losses from the previous console necessitate a bold pivot, not a cautious iteration. The modest chip ensures cost control and battery life, aligning with the handheld use case.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders do not exceed the previous console's launch quarter by 20% within 30 days of announcement, or if the top three first-party titles slip more than 90 days past launch, halt mass production expansion.

**Leading Metric:** Attach rate of first-party software to hardware in the first 90 days.

DECISION: GO
