First call: NO-GO. The financial exposure exceeds all historical precedents for the firm, relying on a single customer and unproven propulsion technology while facing an existential threat from supersonic competitors.

Steelman: Building a massively scalable, convertible freighter now secures long-term dominance if subsonic demand persists and supersonic transitions fail.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Preserve capital but cede future large-aircraft market to competitors if the airline shops the request elsewhere.
2. **Supersonic focus:** Pivot entirely to next-gen speed, betting the market rejects larger subsonic capacity.
3. **Proposed plan:** Bet the company on subsonic scale and freighter flexibility.

Fit: Aligns with the customer's immediate cost-reduction demand but assumes subsonic travel remains the primary growth vector, contradicting the industry expectation of supersonic replacement.

Pre-mortem: The project bankrupts the manufacturer before the first delivery due to engine delays and cost overruns; the airline cancels as supersonic tickets go on sale; the factory sits idle. This is a one-way door with catastrophic downside. The cost of delay is losing the exclusive launch customer, but the cost of failure is corporate extinction.

Evidence: The proposal admits the outlay is unprecedented, the engine is unproven, and the market direction (supersonic vs. subsonic) is contested. Only one order exists.

Blockers:
1. **Single-point failure:** Relying on one airline for a project of this magnitude creates an unacceptable dependency; if they walk, the program dies.
2. **Technology readiness:** Committing to an unproven high-bypass engine without a backup or proven prototype introduces a schedule risk that guarantees massive cost overruns.
3. **Strategic contradiction:** Investing heavily in subsonic capacity while the market anticipates supersonic replacement contradicts the stated industry trajectory without proof that supersonic will fail.

Risks: Market misreading of supersonic adoption; execution failure on the new factory; competitor response.

The lack of multiple launch customers and the unproven state of the critical engine technology are fatal flaws in the current plan. The strategic bet against supersonic travel is unsupported by the facts provided.

DECISION: NO-GO
