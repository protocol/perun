First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets $1B+ on unproven demand for premium short-form subscriptions amid known fatigue and abundant free alternatives.

Steelman: A high-fidelity, mobile-native video service with A-list talent and adaptive orientation technology can carve a profitable niche by offering superior quality that free platforms cannot match, justifying a subscription despite market saturation.

Attack:
1. **Demand Contradiction**: The proposal admits "no proven demand" and "subscription fatigue" while asking for massive capital. Free, abundant short-form video (UGC and ad-supported) already dominates mobile attention. Converting users to pay for what they currently get free, specifically in a format (short-form) associated with quick, disposable consumption, contradicts observed behavior.
2. **Cost Structure vs. Value**: High production budgets and A-list talent drive costs sky-high, requiring immense scale to break even. Short-form episodes (<10 mins) typically generate lower engagement time per session compared to long-form, making the cost-per-hour of entertainment inefficient compared to competitors.
3. **Feature Moat**: "Video looking right vertically or horizontally" is a baseline UX expectation, not a differentiator worthy of a subscription premium or a $1B valuation.
4. **Alternatives**: The "do nothing" or "license content to existing platforms" options carry significantly less risk. A cheaper, staged pilot (e.g., a single show on an existing platform) could test demand without billions in upfront capital.

Blockers:
- **Economic Mismatch**: The cost structure (A-list talent, high production) cannot be supported by the likely adoption rate given "subscription fatigue" and "no proven demand." The math fails as proposed.
- **Lack of Differentiation**: The core tech feature is table stakes, not a defensible moat against entrenched free competitors.

Risks:
- Execution difficulty in securing exclusive A-list talent for short-form.
- Rapid churn if the content library is small.

The absence of a pilot, cap, or metric proving willingness to pay for *premium* short-form is a fatal gap when combined with the explicit admission of no proven demand. This is a classic case of throwing capital at a hypothesis that contradicts current market signals.

DECISION: NO-GO
