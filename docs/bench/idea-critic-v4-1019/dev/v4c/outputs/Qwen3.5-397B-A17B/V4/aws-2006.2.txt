First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer with no platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors already oppose heavy capital spending.

Steelman: Leveraging spiky internal infrastructure to create a new revenue stream could theoretically monetize idle capacity and diversify beyond low-margin retail.

However, the blocker test fails immediately on three verified facts within the proposal. First, the core competency mismatch is absolute: the company has never run a developer platform at scale, yet the product requires extreme reliability and distinct operational rhythms compared to retail. Second, the market is explicitly "unproven," meaning demand is hypothetical, not validated. Third, and most critically, the timing contradicts financial reality: investors are already questioning heavy capital spending. This plan requires *more* capex to productize internal tools for external use, directly antagonizing the capital providers needed to fund it.

Alternatives analysis shows "Do Nothing" preserves focus on the core retail business during a period of investor skepticism. A "Better Way" would be to simply optimize internal costs rather than building a public business line, avoiding the distraction and margin dilution risks cited. The pre-mortem is clear: the venture fails because reliability slips damage the retail brand, or the capital burn causes a stock collapse before product-market fit is found.

The risks listed (distraction, margin dilution, competition) are not merely potential; they are near-certainties given the lack of experience and the unproven nature of the market. These are not risks to be managed with kill criteria; they are structural flaws in the premise. A retailer cannot successfully pivot to an infrastructure utility provider when its own investors are revolted by the cost of its current infrastructure, especially when the target market has no proven willingness to pay.

Blockers:
1. Direct conflict with investor sentiment on capital spending.
2. Zero organizational experience in the proposed domain.
3. Unproven market demand for the specific offering.

Risks:
None, as the blockers are fatal.

DECISION: NO-GO
