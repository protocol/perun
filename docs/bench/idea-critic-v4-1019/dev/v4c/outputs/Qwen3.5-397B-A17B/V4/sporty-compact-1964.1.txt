First call: GO. The proposal targets a verified demographic shift (postwar boom entrants) with a capital-efficient architecture (shared mechanicals) and a revenue-maximizing model (low base, high-margin options).

**Steelman:** Launch a stylistically distinct, customizable sporty coupe/convertible using proven compact underpinnings to capture young buyers at a low entry price, supported by massive awareness marketing.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Demand Risk:** Unproven volume demand is a risk, not a blocker. The "do nothing" alternative guarantees missing this demographic entirely. The low base price minimizes downside exposure while the options list captures upside.
2.  **Image Risk:** Sharing parts with a "dull" economy car could cheapen the image. However, the distinct styling (long hood, short deck) and convertible option directly counter this perception. A clean-sheet platform would destroy the low-price advantage.
3.  **Cost Risk:** Heavy launch spending is dangerous if sales fail. Yet, without a "very large" campaign, the "dull" donor car's reputation might bleed into the new model. The cost of delay is losing the first wave of boomers to competitors.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it is because the styling failed to mask the economy roots or the advertising missed the cultural zeitgeist. These are execution risks, not structural flaws. The proposal mitigates financial ruin via shared tooling.

**Blockers:** NONE. The facts provided show a clear market gap, a feasible technical solution, and a logical monetization strategy. The risks cited are inherent to innovation but are adequately balanced by the low fixed costs of the shared platform and the high potential margin of the options list.

**Risks:** Unproven volume demand; potential brand dilution; high upfront marketing spend. These become kill criteria: monitor initial order mix (options uptake) and first-quarter sell-through rates against ad spend.

**Kill Criteria:** If first-quarter sales do not cover variable marketing costs by 20%, or if >80% of buyers choose the base model with no options (indicating failure to upsell), pivot messaging or trim ad spend immediately.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The objection that "sharing parts makes it look cheap" was tested against the styling differentiators and price point. The low price anchors expectations, while the styling delivers the emotional promise; the mechanical sharing is invisible to the eye but vital to the wallet.

DECISION: GO
