First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines a premium price with a smaller app store, missing mainstream apps, and carrier exclusivity in a saturated two-platform market; this fails the basic test of value exchange.

Steelman: A device leveraging unique 3D and shopping features could carve a niche if the core utility (apps) is sufficient. However, the "smaller app store" explicitly excludes "mainstream apps," which are table stakes for a flagship-priced smartphone.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital; avoid entering a winner-take-most hardware war without a differentiator that matters to mass users.
2. Low-cost tablet expansion: Leverage existing success in low-priced tablets where app gaps are less fatal and price sensitivity matches the value prop.
3. Accessory strategy: Sell the 3D camera/shopping tech as an accessory for existing dominant platforms rather than building a whole phone.

Fit: This does not move the north star of "customer convenience" if customers cannot access the apps they use daily. A shopping-focused phone is useless if it cannot run the shopping apps users already have.

Pre-mortem: The device launches, early adopters try the 3D feature, but daily utility fails due to missing banking, social, and communication apps. Returns spike, carrier partnership sours, and the brand takes a reputation hit for overpricing a limited device.

Evidence & Math: Flagship price + no mainstream apps + single carrier = severely restricted total addressable market. In 2014, app ecosystem lock-in was the primary driver of smartphone choice. A "forked OS" without key apps is a non-starter at a premium price point.

Blockers:
1. **Value Mismatch:** Charging flagship prices for a device lacking mainstream apps contradicts basic market mechanics; users will not pay a premium for a downgrade in core utility.
2. **Distribution Ceiling:** Single-carrier exclusivity caps growth potential while the app gap prevents the loyalty needed to sustain a niche.

Risks: Market saturation and execution difficulty are secondary to the fundamental product-market fit failure defined by the blockers.

Kill Criteria: If the device cannot run top-50 mainstream apps, it cannot command a flagship price.

DECISION: NO-GO
