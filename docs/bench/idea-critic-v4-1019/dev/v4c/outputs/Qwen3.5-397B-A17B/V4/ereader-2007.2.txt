First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a software-centric bookseller to enter capital-intensive hardware manufacturing with no prior experience, while simultaneously disrupting publisher pricing models in an unproven market.

Steelman: A dedicated, cellular-enabled device removes friction for book buying, creating a closed ecosystem that locks customers into the store despite hardware inexperience and publisher fears.

Attack:
1. **Capability Mismatch:** Designing consumer hardware and negotiating cellular data contracts are fundamentally different competencies from retail and logistics. Failure here risks brand reputation and burns cash before software value is realized.
2. **Economic Misalignment:** Offering "free" cellular connectivity shifts massive operational costs to the retailer. Combined with "low price per title," this creates a double margin squeeze that requires unrealistic volume to sustain.
3. **Market Contradiction:** The proposal admits earlier e-readers sold poorly due to lack of demand or poor tech. Betting on a "paper-like screen" (unproven at scale in 2007) to solve this is a technological gamble, not a strategy.
4. **Channel Conflict:** Publishers fearing piracy and margin erosion will likely withhold content, rendering the device useless. Cannibalizing print sales without a guaranteed digital upside is financial suicide.

Blockers:
- **Fact:** The company has never designed consumer hardware. This is a core competency gap that cannot be bridged instantly.
- **Fact:** Publishers explicitly fear piracy and margin loss; the proposal offers them lower prices and higher risk, ensuring content starvation.
- **Fact:** Free cellular + low book prices creates a negative unit economics model with no clear path to profitability given the "poor sales" history of the category.

Risks:
- High capital expenditure on inventory and R&D with low reversal potential.
- Brand damage if the device fails (high probability given inexperience).
- Irreversible breakdown of publisher relationships.

The proposal fails the "fit" test: it moves away from the core business into high-risk operations without solving the primary adoption barrier (content availability) or the cost structure. The "do nothing" or "partner with existing hardware makers" alternatives are superior as they avoid the capability gap.

DECISION: NO-GO
