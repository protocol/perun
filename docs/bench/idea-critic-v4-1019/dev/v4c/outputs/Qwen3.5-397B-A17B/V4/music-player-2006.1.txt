First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market late, abandons a strategic standard (copy-protection) that secures partner relationships, and relies on unproven label cooperation for wireless sharing without a clear price or feature advantage over the entrenched leader.

Steelman: A well-funded entrant can disrupt the music market by leveraging superior hardware (larger screen) and a seamless ecosystem (store + subscription + wireless sharing) to override late timing and ecosystem lock-in.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost, high risk of partner alienation, uncertain label buy-in.
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoids capital burn and partner conflict; accepts status quo.
3. **Licensing/Partnership:** License software to existing hardware makers or partner with the dominant player; lower risk, preserves copy-protection standard, faster market entry.

Fit: Assuming the goal is market share growth, this approach conflicts with the reality of partner dependency. Abandoning the copy-protection standard undermines trust with existing retail and device partners who rely on it.

Pre-mortem: The project fails because record labels, fearing piracy via "wireless sharing," restrict content availability or refuse to license, rendering the store empty. Simultaneously, former partners, betrayed by the drop in copy-protection standards, actively block distribution. The large marketing budget burns cash against a loyal competitor ecosystem without a unique value proposition strong enough to switch users.

Evidence: The proposal admits the company is "late to market," the leader has "enormous share," and there is "no clear price advantage." The risks regarding label restrictions and partner alienation are stated as probable conflicts, not solved problems.

Blockers:
1. **Partner Alienation:** Dropping the promoted copy-protection standard contradicts prior commitments to partners, likely causing immediate channel conflict.
2. **Content Availability:** Wireless sharing creates a piracy vector labels are unlikely to accept without ironclad guarantees not present in the proposal.
3. **Late Entry:** Entering late without a price or definitive feature advantage against a loyal ecosystem is a fatal strategic position.

Risks: Marketing inefficiency, hardware adoption failure, subscription churn.

The plan requires resolving the label and partner conflicts before proceeding. As proposed, it guarantees channel hostility and content scarcity.

DECISION: NO-GO
