First call: NO-GO. The price implies a valuation with no revenue, no business model, and extreme execution risk, violating basic financial discipline for a public company.

**Steelman:** Buying the app instantly neutralizes the primary mobile threat, acquires tens of millions of active users, and prevents a competitor from owning them, justifying the premium as a defensive necessity rather than a revenue play.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Risk rapid user migration to the threat; own mobile product likely fails to catch up.
2.  **Build/Buy smaller:** Too slow; no other target has comparable traction or cultural cache.
3.  **Acquire (Proposed):** Immediate market dominance, but at an unproven $1B price tag with high integration risk.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of maintaining social graph dominance, assuming mobile is the future (a strong but unproven assumption in 2012).

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition fails if the app's culture is crushed by corporate process, causing founders and engineers to leave, rendering the product stagnant. Or, regulators block the deal, wasting months of focus. The "one-way door" nature means if the trend shifts away from mobile photos, the capital is permanently lost.

**Evidence & Math:** The $1B price for ~30M users is ~$33/user. With zero revenue, ROI relies entirely on future monetization or ad inventory integration. The buyer's weak mobile position confirms the threat is real, but paying 100% of perceived value upfront removes all leverage.

**Blockers:**
*   **Valuation without fundamentals:** No revenue, no path to profitability defined. Paying $1B for "potential" sets a dangerous precedent and invites shareholder lawsuits if the stock dips.
*   **Cultural destruction risk:** The proposal admits the risk of destroying the app's culture; without a concrete, binding plan to maintain autonomy, the asset's value (its team and vibe) evaporates post-close.

**Risks:** Regulatory blocking, platform dependency (iOS/Android shifts), failure to monetize.

**Kill Criteria:** If user growth slows by >20% within 6 months post-acquisition, or if founder retention drops below 12 months, the thesis is broken.

The lack of a defined business model and the sheer magnitude of the premium relative to current financials constitute a blocker. A "go-if" structure (earn-outs, strict autonomy clauses) is required, but the proposal asks for approval "as proposed."

DECISION: NO-GO
