First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on unproven film capabilities and assets currently locked away from the buyer's primary goal.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the publisher instantly grants a library of male-focused characters, solving the conglomerate's demographic gap and leveraging the target's nascent film success to build a new cinematic universe.

**Attack:** The core claim—that this purchase fixes "weak reach with boys"—fails immediately against the stated facts. The target's "most famous characters" are encumbered by long-term deals with rivals. You are paying $4 billion primarily for assets you cannot use yet. Furthermore, the target's film division is described as having a "short and unproven" slate, with only one success. Betting $4 billion on a studio that has just started self-financing, while its crown jewels are licensed to competitors, is speculation, not strategy. The "cyclical nature" risk compounds this; entering at the peak of a trend with restricted assets invites massive write-downs.

**Alternatives:** 
1. **Do nothing:** Avoids overpaying for locked IP.
2. **License specific characters:** Negotiate for the male-focused IP not yet locked or upon deal expiration, costing a fraction of $4B.
3. **Wait:** Let the target prove its film slate beyond one hit before buying.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-acquisition, the conglomerate holds a $4B debt load but cannot launch new boy-focused toys or shows because the popular characters are still legally tied to rival studios. The new film studio fails to replicate its initial success, leading to creative clashes and leadership exodus.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Asset Unavailability:** The stated fact that key characters are licensed to rivals directly contradicts the goal of immediate reach expansion.
2. **Unproven Valuation:** The price assumes a film track record that the proposal explicitly admits is "short and unproven."

**Risks:** Creative control disputes, cyclical downturn, integration failure.

**Kill Criteria:** If the top 3 male characters cannot be reclaimed or utilized within 18 months, halt further film investment in this unit. Leading metric: Revenue from newly acquired male IP (must be >$0 within 24 months).

DECISION: NO-GO
