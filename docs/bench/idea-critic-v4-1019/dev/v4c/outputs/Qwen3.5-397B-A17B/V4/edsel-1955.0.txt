First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a massive, irreversible infrastructure build (new dealer network) and a fixed 1957 launch date despite an acknowledged recession risk and a segment that "could shrink."

**Steelman:** Capturing the growing mid-priced gap requires a distinct brand to avoid cannibalization, justifying the dealer investment to compete with the market leader.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Timing & Irreversibility):** The plan hardcodes a 1957 debut while admitting a recession risk. In an economic downturn, the mid-priced segment is historically the first to collapse as consumers trade down. Building a new dealer network is a high-fixed-cost, one-way door that cannot be quickly reversed if sales miss targets due to macro conditions.
2.  **Better Way:** The "cheaper alternative" is to bridge the gap using the existing low-priced brand with a premium trim line or a badge-engineered model sold through current dealers. This captures immediate share with negligible infrastructure risk, allowing the company to test the segment's resilience during a potential recession before committing to a standalone division.
3.  **Fit:** The strategy assumes continuous segment growth. If the recession materializes, the "perceived gap" may vanish, leaving the new division with no market and stranded assets.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch coincides with a 1958 economic contraction. Sales plummet as buyers seek value. The new dealer network, lacking volume, goes bankrupt or demands expensive bailouts. The brand becomes synonymous with failure, damaging the parent company's reputation and balance sheet.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** The combination of a "one-way door" investment (dealer network) and a known, unmitigated macro threat (recession) with a fixed launch date constitutes a factual failure of the plan as proposed. The plan does not offer a staged entry or a fallback if the economy turns.
*   **Risks:** Market overlap and execution difficulty are secondary to the structural exposure to a downturn.

A sound operator does not bet the firm's capital on a fixed timeline when the primary variable (economic health) is volatile and the cost of being wrong is catastrophic. The plan must be revised to include a staged rollout or a recession-proof contingency before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
