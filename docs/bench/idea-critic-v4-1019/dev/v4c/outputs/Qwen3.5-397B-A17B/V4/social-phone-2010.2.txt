First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores fatal market mismatches: teenagers are price-sensitive, yet the data plan is expensive; they demand apps, yet there is no store; and the company abandons its existing platform for an unproven one.

Steelman: Leveraging the acquired brand's loyalty to capture the teen segment via a curated, carrier-subsidized social device, bypassing the complexity of a full app ecosystem initially.

Attack:
1.  **Economic Mismatch:** Teens have low disposable income. A mandatory data plan costing as much as a full smartphone plan removes the primary adoption driver (affordability).
2.  **Feature Deficit:** In 2010, "smartphone" implies apps. Competitors have large catalogs; launching with *no* app store makes the device functionally obsolete on day one for a demographic defined by social networking variety.
3.  **Strategic Fragmentation:** Ignoring the company's own mobile platform to build a new OS doubles development risk and fragments the user base, wasting the very synergy the acquisition sought.
4.  **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** Two years of sunk development is not a reason to launch a flawed product; it is a reason to write off the loss rather than compound it with a likely market failure.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; let the acquired brand fade or integrate it slowly.
*   **Integrate:** Port the messaging features to the company's existing mobile platform and OS, launching immediately with an app store.
*   **Subsidize:** Keep the hardware but negotiate a teen-specific low-cost data tier (unlikely to succeed without apps).

Pre-mortem: The phone launches, teens reject the high monthly cost and lack of apps, sales flatline, and the company writes off both the acquisition and the new OS development. This is a one-way door that locks the company out of the teen market for years.

Blockers:
*   **Price:** Data plan cost exceeds target demographic capacity.
*   **Utility:** No app store in an app-driven market.
*   **Strategy:** Abandoning existing platform for a new, unproven OS.

Risks: Carrier exclusivity limiting reach; brand dilution.

The plan fails on basic unit economics and product-market fit. The sunk costs are irrelevant to future success.

DECISION: NO-GO
