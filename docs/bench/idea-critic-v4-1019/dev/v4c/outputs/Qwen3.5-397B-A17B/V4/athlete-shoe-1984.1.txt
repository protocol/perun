First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets a large share of the marketing budget on an unproven rookie with high injury risk and potential league sanctions, violating standard risk management for a market leader.

**Steelman:** Secure a generational talent before competitors recognize his value, using controversy (rule-breaking colorways) to generate massive free media coverage that offsets the high upfront cost and creates a cultural phenomenon rather than just a shoe line.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain status quo; likely continue losing basketball market share to established stars.
2.  **Sign established star:** Lower risk, but prohibitively expensive and limited upside; competitors can outbid.
3.  **Proposed rookie bet:** Highest risk, but only option offering category disruption and long-term brand ownership if the player succeeds.

**Fit:** Assumes the brand needs a radical shift to enter basketball, not just incremental gain. If the goal is market leadership, safe plays fail.

**Pre-mortem:** The player suffers a career-ending injury in year one; the league bans the shoes immediately; the brand burns cash with no inventory sales. **Door:** Two-way (can stop production after year 1 if sales flop, but sunk endorsement costs remain). **Delay cost:** Waiting for the player to prove himself guarantees losing him to a rival or paying 10x the price.

**Evidence:** No data provided on the player's actual skill, only "rookie" status. Financials show "large share" of budget but no ROI projection. League rules are stated as a barrier, not a calculated fine cost.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial exposure:** Committing a "large share" of the budget to one unproven asset without a cap or insurance clause is fiscally irresponsible based *only* on the facts given.
*   **Regulatory certainty:** The plan relies on breaking rules; without a confirmed strategy to absorb fines or leverage the ban for marketing, this is a liability, not a tactic.

**Risks:** Injury, flop performance, consumer rejection of non-compliant shoes.

**Decision:** The lack of financial safeguards (caps, insurance) and a concrete plan to monetize the regulatory conflict makes this a gamble, not a strategy. A "GO" requires these missing elements to be added first.

DECISION: NO-GO
