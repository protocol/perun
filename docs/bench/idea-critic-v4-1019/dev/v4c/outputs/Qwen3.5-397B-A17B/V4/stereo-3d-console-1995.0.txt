First call: NO-GO. The proposal describes a device with severe usability flaws (discomfort, eye strain), a crippled display (red monochrome), and a confusing form factor (tabletop "portable") that fails to leverage the company's upcoming home console synergy.

Steelman: A low-cost ($180) bridge product utilizing existing tech to capture market share between generations via unique stereoscopic 3D.

Options:
1. **Proposed:** High risk of hardware failure due to discomfort; low software appeal due to visual limitations.
2. **Do Nothing:** Preserve brand reputation; wait for the confirmed next-gen home console (2 years).
3. **Software Bridge:** Release enhanced games for the current handheld/home systems. Cheaper, no hardware risk, immediate revenue.

Fit: The product contradicts the "north star" of comfortable, immersive gaming. Eye strain and headaches are direct opposites of user value. The red-only display limits genre viability, reducing the addressable market for launch titles.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, reviewers cite immediate physical discomfort and poor visibility. Sales plummet within weeks. Retailers return stock. The brand is associated with "gimmick" rather than innovation, damaging the upcoming home console launch. The $180 price point is too high for a monochrome novelty given the discomfort.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the core negatives: "neither portable nor comfortable," "eye strain or headaches," and "red-on-black visuals only." These are not risks; they are stated features that invalidate the user experience. The "few games at launch" confirms a weak ecosystem.

Blockers:
1. **Physical Harm/Discomfort:** The stated fact that users "may get eye strain or headaches" and the device is "uncomfortable" is a fundamental product failure. A gaming device that causes pain cannot succeed.
2. **Value Proposition:** A non-portable "handheld" with a inferior display (red monochrome) offers no clear advantage over existing home or handheld units, especially with "few games."

Risks: Brand damage, inventory bloat, distraction from the next-gen home console.

The stated flaws are not mitigatable via kill criteria; they are intrinsic to the proposed design. No amount of marketing fixes a device that physically hurts the user.

DECISION: NO-GO
