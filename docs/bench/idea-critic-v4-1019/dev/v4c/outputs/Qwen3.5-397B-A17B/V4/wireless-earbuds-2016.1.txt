First call: GO. Removing the jack forces a transition to wireless; owning the chip ensures pairing quality that third parties cannot match, creating a defensible ecosystem lock-in despite short-term friction.

**Steelman:** Launching proprietary wireless earbuds at $159 immediately upon removing the headphone jack captures the inevitable migration of audio accessories, turning a customer pain point into a high-margin revenue stream while establishing a new product category before competitors react.

**Attack & Analysis:**
*   **Price vs. Value:** $159 is steep compared to free wired options. However, the value proposition shifts from "audio access" to "convenience and status." The charging case mitigates the 5-hour battery limit, effectively offering all-day power.
*   **Design Risk:** "Odd" looks are a transient risk; distinct design often drives early adopter status. Ridicule frequently precedes normalization in hardware shifts.
*   **Loss Risk:** Ease of loss is real, but this creates a secondary replacement market. The friction of loss is the price of cutting the cord.
*   **Customer Anger:** Anger is guaranteed but finite. The strategic imperative is to force the market forward; delaying keeps the company dependent on legacy standards. The "do nothing" option (keeping the jack) stalls innovation and cedes the wireless future to rivals.

**Blockers:** None. The proposal's risks (price, design, battery) are execution challenges, not fatal flaws. The core logic—that removing the jack necessitates a superior wireless alternative owned by the manufacturer—is sound. The high price signals premium positioning, distinguishing it from commodity audio.

**Risks:** Adoption lag if the pairing experience glitches; brand backlash if the audio quality doesn't justify the removal of the jack. These are managed by the proprietary chip investment (ensuring integration) and the charging case (solving battery anxiety).

**Kill Criteria:** If initial pre-orders miss internal targets by >40% due to price sensitivity, pivot to a lower-cost "lite" version within 6 months. If pairing failure rates exceed 5% in first-week user reports, halt marketing spend until a firmware fix is deployed.

**Decision:** The move is a classic one-way door that secures long-term ecosystem control. The short-term pain is the cost of leadership.

DECISION: GO
