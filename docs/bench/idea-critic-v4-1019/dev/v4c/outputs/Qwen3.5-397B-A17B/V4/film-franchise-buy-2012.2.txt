First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a creative engine (the founder) that is explicitly leaving, while recent output shows declining quality, making the $4B valuation speculative rather than supported by current cash flow.

**Steelman:** Acquiring this franchise provides immediate, deep IP capable of saturating parks, products, and screens for decades, justifying a premium price to secure a generational asset before competitors do.

**Attack:** The core value proposition collapses if the "space-fantasy" appeal was tethered to the retiring founder rather than the setting itself. The "mixed reviews" of recent entries suggest the brand is already fatigued, not dormant. Paying $4B for a declining asset without the original creator is a classic "buying the peak" error. Furthermore, with significant merchandise rights already licensed to third parties, the immediate synergy upside for the "consumer products" division is capped, delaying ROI. The risk of fan rejection is not theoretical; it is indicated by the recent mixed reception. Without the founder, the new trilogy is a guess, not a guarantee.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve $4B cash; avoid integrating a potentially toxic or fading brand.
2.  **License Only:** Negotiate a revenue-share for park rights or new films without the massive upfront acquisition cost and operational burden.
3.  **Wait:** Let the founder fully exit and the market value stabilize based on the performance of the first post-founder content.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-acquisition, the new trilogy flops, fans boycott the "corporate" direction, and the parks division cannot integrate the IP due to existing licensing legal tangles. The $4B becomes a write-down.

**Blockers:**
*   **Creative Void:** The proposal admits the creative lead is retiring, yet the plan depends on producing new mainline films immediately. There is no verified replacement strategy.
*   **Valuation Mismatch:** The price is based on historical peak value, not current trajectory (mixed reviews) or immediate synergy potential (licensed merchandise).

**Risks:** Fan backlash, integration delays, trilogy failure.

**Kill Criteria:** If the first announced film script does not secure internal approval from legacy creative leadership within 6 months, terminate the production slate.

DECISION: NO-GO
