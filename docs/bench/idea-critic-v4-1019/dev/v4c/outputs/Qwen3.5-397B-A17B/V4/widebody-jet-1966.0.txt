First call: NO-GO. The financial exposure exceeds all prior precedents, the core propulsion technology is unproven, and the market premise faces an existential threat from emerging supersonic competitors.

Steelman: Building a massively scalable, convertible freighter/passenger jet now secures long-term dominance by locking in the primary customer and anticipating cargo needs, despite high upfront costs.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Capital Ruin:** The development and factory outlay is "far larger than any the company has made." A single failure or delay could bankrupt the manufacturer, as there is no diversified order book to absorb the shock.
2.  **Technological Dependency:** The design relies on an "unproven" high-bypass engine. If the supplier fails to deliver efficient power, the aircraft's economic value proposition (cost per seat) collapses.
3.  **Obsolescence Risk:** The explicit expectation that "supersonic airliners are expected to replace subsonic jets" suggests the product may be outdated before it reaches mass production. Investing heavily in subsonic capacity contradicts the projected industry trajectory.
4.  **Market Concentration:** With "only one airline" ordered, the project lacks market validation. If this single customer cancels or reduces the order, the specialized factory becomes a stranded asset.

**Risks:**
Execution complexity of a wide-body design; potential delays in the new factory construction; competitor response with supersonic models.

**Analysis:**
The proposal demands a "bet the company" commitment based on a single customer's request while ignoring a looming technological shift (supersonic travel). The absence of multiple orders indicates the broader market is not yet convinced, likely due to the supersonic threat. Proceeding without a proven engine or a broader order book violates basic capital preservation principles. The risk of building a white elephant factory for a soon-to-be-obsolete subsonic giant is too high. The "raised cockpit" feature, while clever for future freighter conversion, does not mitigate the immediate existential threats of funding, technology, and market relevance.

The plan fails the blocker test: the financial scale relative to company size, combined with unproven critical components and a threatening market shift, creates a high probability of catastrophic failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
