First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a software ecosystem deficit with a hardware acquisition, misaligning capital with the core constraint.

Steelman: Acquiring the struggling manufacturer secures the primary hardware outlet for the OS and prevents immediate defection to the rival platform, buying time to court developers.

However, the claim fails the blocker test. The proposal admits smartphone sales are falling and the rival holds the app ecosystem. Buying a declining asset at €5.4 billion consumes capital needed for developer incentives while inheriting integration risks and alienating other handset partners. Hardware ownership does not force developers to build apps; it only verticalizes a losing position. The "app-ecosystem problem" is a network effect issue, not a supply chain one. A cheaper, safer alternative exists: license the OS aggressively to multiple manufacturers to maximize install base, using the €5.4 billion to subsidize developer tools and porting services. This approach scales usage without the drag of a failing hardware division.

The pre-mortem is clear: The acquisition closes, integration stalls innovation, other manufacturers flee to the rival platform to avoid competing with the owner, and the app gap widens due to stagnant user growth. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed integration; waiting allows the market to clarify whether the OS can survive as a pure software play.

Blockers:
1. The core deficit is apps/developers; the proposal spends billions on hardware, which does not directly solve the stated root cause and diverts resources from the actual solution (ecosystem subsidies).
2. Acquiring a manufacturer with falling sales and a large workforce introduces immediate negative cash flow and integration drag, contradicting the need for agility against a dominant rival.

Risks: Partner retaliation, cultural clash, continued sales decline.

Kill criteria: If the combined entity does not achieve a 20% increase in active developer sign-ups within 18 months post-close, divest the hardware division. Leading metric: Monthly active developers targeting the OS.

DECISION: NO-GO
