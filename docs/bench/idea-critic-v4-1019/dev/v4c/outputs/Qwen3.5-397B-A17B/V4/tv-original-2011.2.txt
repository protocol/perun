First call: NO-GO. The proposal concentrates a massive share of the budget into a single unproven asset with no intermediate validation, violating basic capital preservation for a non-dominant player.

**Steelman:** Leveraging proprietary viewing data to bypass traditional pilot testing allows the service to secure exclusive prestige content that differentiates it from competitors, locking in subscribers before licensing costs become unsustainable.

**Attack:**
1.  **Data Validity:** Viewing data reflects past consumption of *licensed* content, not demand for original productions. Assuming users who watch existing dramas will watch a new, unproven original is an unsupported leap. The data cannot predict reception of a specific creative team or genre shift.
2.  **Financial Concentration:** Committing two full seasons (26 episodes) upfront removes all leverage. If the first few episodes fail to resonate, the company is contractually bound to fund the remainder, potentially crippling operations. A standard pilot or single-season order limits downside exposure.
3.  **Distribution Risk:** Releasing all episodes at once is untested for prestige drama. While it may encourage binge-watching, it eliminates the week-to-week cultural conversation and sustained subscription retention that weekly releases generate.
4.  **Talent Dependency:** Betting on a film director and star does not guarantee television success; their film appeal may not translate to the serialized format or the specific subscriber base.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Continue licensing; risk rising costs but preserve capital.
*   **Pilot First:** Commission a single episode to test creative execution and audience response using the same data hypothesis, reducing financial exposure by ~95%.
*   **Single Season Order:** Commit to one season only, with renewal contingent on specific early-viewership metrics.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The cost is a "large share" of the content budget.
*   **Fact:** No pilot is proposed.
*   **Contradiction:** The core de-risking mechanism (data) has never been validated for original production decisions. Proceeding without a pilot or a smaller initial commitment creates a single point of failure that could bankrupt the content strategy if the show underperforms.

**Risks:** Audience rejection, wasted capital, inability to pivot after poor early performance.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot or first 3 episodes do not achieve a specific retention/conversion lift within 30 days of internal screening, the project halts.

DECISION: NO-GO
