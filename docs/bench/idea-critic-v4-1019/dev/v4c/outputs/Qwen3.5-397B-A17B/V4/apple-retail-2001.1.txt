First call: NO-GO. A loss-making manufacturer with 3–5% share entering a low-margin, high-fixed-cost business during a recession contradicts basic survival logic.

**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only way to demonstrate the integrated music strategy and stop third-party staff from pushing cheaper competitors, thereby saving the core PC business.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Blocker:** The company is currently losing money. Opening prime mall stores requires massive upfront capital (leases, build-outs, inventory) and creates high fixed operating costs. In a recession, sales volatility could trigger immediate insolvency. The proposal offers no funding source for this cash burn.
2.  **Strategic Contradiction:** Management cites a "poor retail track record in the industry" as a known risk yet proposes entering that exact sector. If the industry fails at retail, why will this specific manufacturer succeed without prior experience?
3.  **Channel Suicide:** Third-party retailers already resist pushing this brand. Launching competing stores will cause them to drop the product entirely to avoid showrooming, instantly collapsing the remaining 95% of sales volume before the new stores become profitable.
4.  **Better Way:** Instead of capital-intensive stores, create "shop-in-shop" demo zones within existing retailer locations or launch a direct-to-consumer web store (lower fixed cost, no lease risk) to demonstrate the music strategy.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-launch, the company declares bankruptcy. Cause: Fixed lease obligations consumed all cash reserves during a recession-induced sales dip, while alienated retail partners refused to carry inventory, leaving the new stores with insufficient foot traffic to break even.

**Blockers:**
*   **Funding Gap:** No identified capital to fund build-outs and operating losses for a loss-making entity.
*   **Channel Collapse:** High probability of immediate revenue drop from alienated third-party partners.
*   **Recession Timing:** High fixed costs introduced precisely when consumer discretionary spending is contracting.

**Risks:** Inventory obsolescence, execution failure due to lack of retail expertise, brand dilution if stores feel empty.

The plan introduces existential financial risk to solve a marketing problem, ignoring cheaper alternatives and immediate channel retaliation. The math does not work for a company already in the red.

DECISION: NO-GO
