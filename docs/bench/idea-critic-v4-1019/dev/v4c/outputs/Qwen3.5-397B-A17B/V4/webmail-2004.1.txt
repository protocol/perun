First call: GO. The proposal trades a rapidly commoditizing cost (storage) for a defensible differentiation (searchability) in a market where incumbents are constrained by legacy cost structures.

**Steelman:** By offering 1,000x the standard storage, the service renders manual deletion obsolete, positioning "search" as the primary interaction model. This creates a sticky, high-engagement platform where contextual text ads yield higher CPMs than generic banner ads, funding the infrastructure before direct monetization is required.

**Attack & Rebuttal:**
1.  **Cost Blocker?** Critics cite the high cost of 1GB/user. *Rebuttal:* Storage costs follow a steep downward curve; buying capacity now locks in a future margin advantage while competitors remain stuck in the megabyte era. The cost is an investment in barrier-to-exit, not an expense.
2.  **Privacy Objection?** Scanning mail for ads risks user trust. *Rebuttal:* The value exchange (free massive storage + powerful search) is explicit. Users trading privacy for utility is an established, accepted pattern in free web services; the alternative is paying for storage, which limits scale.
3.  **Market Saturation?** Incumbents dominate. *Rebuttal:* Incumbents are optimized for low-storage, low-bandwidth constraints. They cannot pivot to 1GB without cannibalizing existing paid tiers or breaking their legacy architecture. This is a classic disruptor entry via over-serving on a key dimension.
4.  **No Immediate Revenue?** *Rebuttal:* Invitation-only growth creates scarcity and validates the model with high-value early adopters, delaying ad-load until engagement metrics justify it.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks are execution challenges or market assumptions, not factual contradictions. The math holds: ad revenue per engaged user with full-history context exceeds the marginal cost of commoditized storage.

**Risks:** Privacy backlash could slow adoption; storage cost curves might flatten unexpectedly. These are managed by the invitation-only rollout (kill criterion: churn rate > X% due to privacy complaints) and capped infrastructure spend.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal beats "do nothing" and "incremental improvement" by fundamentally changing the user behavior from "archive/delete" to "search/keep." It leverages a specific technological inflection point (cheap storage) to attack a structural weakness in incumbents (legacy limits).

DECISION: GO
