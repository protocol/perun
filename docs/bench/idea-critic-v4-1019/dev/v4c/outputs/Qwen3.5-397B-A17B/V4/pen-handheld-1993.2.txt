First call: NO-GO. The core value proposition (reliable handwriting recognition) is explicitly stated as unproven and unreliable in testing, while the price point is high for an unproven category.

**Steelman:** Launching a flagship device at $700 with heavy CEO backing will force the market to accept pen-input as the new standard, overcoming early technical flaws through brand momentum.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Core Function Failure):** The primary input mechanism is unreliable. A computer that cannot reliably accept user input fails its basic function. No amount of publicity fixes a broken core utility at launch.
2.  **Blocker (Unit Economics/Adoption):** At $700 (a premium price in 1993), early adopters expect polish. Selling an unreliable, bulky device at a premium risks permanent brand damage and category rejection before maturity.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "wait" option is superior. Wait for recognition accuracy to meet a minimum viability threshold before committing to a mass-market launch. Alternatively, launch a developer kit or a lower-spec prototype to gather data without risking the main brand's reputation on a flawed consumer product.
4.  **Fit:** Leading a category requires defining the *correct* experience. Defining the category experience as "frustrating and expensive" cedes the market to a future competitor who launches when the tech works.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, reviews savage the handwriting accuracy, sales stall, inventory piles up, and the "handheld computer" category is branded a gimmick for a decade.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits recognition is "unreliable in testing." This is a factual contradiction to the requirement of a functional computer.

**Blockers:**
*   Core input mechanism (handwriting) is unreliable.
*   High price ($700) contradicts the low reliability value proposition.

**Risks:**
*   Category unproven (mitigated by being first, but not if the product fails).
*   Size issues (secondary to functionality).

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed until independent testing shows handwriting recognition accuracy exceeds 95% on common tasks.

**Decision:** The plan relies on hope over engineering reality. The core feature does not work.

DECISION: NO-GO
