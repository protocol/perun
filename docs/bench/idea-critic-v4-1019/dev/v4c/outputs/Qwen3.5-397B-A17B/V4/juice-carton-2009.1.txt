First call: NO-GO. The proposal discards the brand's primary shelf cue for an untested aesthetic, risking immediate sales collapse among a loyal, older demographic without a safety net.

Steelman: A modernized identity will attract new buyers and signal quality, outweighing temporary confusion among legacy shoppers.

Attack: The claim fails the blocker test. The proposal explicitly admits the old image is the "main shelf cue" and the base is "older." Removing the dominant visual anchor for this demographic is not a risk; it is a verified failure mode. The "large advertising campaign" is an assumed fix, not evidence that recognition will survive the switch. A cheaper, safer alternative exists: A/B test the new design in a limited region to measure recognition loss before a nationwide rollout. Launching everywhere at once removes the ability to stop the bleed if the hypothesis is wrong.

Blockers:
1. The proposal states the old image is the main shelf cue, yet removes it entirely for a demographic reliant on visual cues.
2. No pilot or metric is included to verify recognition before committing to a costly, irreversible nationwide print run.

Risks:
1. Sales drop due to non-recognition.
2. High cost of reprinting and ad spend with negative ROI.

Kill Criteria:
If a regional pilot shows a >5% drop in repeat purchases within 4 weeks, halt the rollout. Leading metric: Shelf recognition speed and repeat purchase rate.

The absence of a pilot for a change this fundamental to a legacy brand's identity is a blocker. You cannot bet the brand on an assumption when the counter-evidence (reliance on the old image) is stated as fact in the proposal itself.

DECISION: NO-GO
