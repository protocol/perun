First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines maximum engineering risk with maximum deployment risk while alienating the core user base that drives traffic.

Steelman: A complete rewrite with a new algorithm and big-bang migration is the only way to reverse traffic decline and compete with rivals by prioritizing publisher content.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Contradictory Evidence:** The proposal admits the community values existing voting mechanics, yet the plan replaces them. This directly attacks the source of current loyalty without proof that "publisher-favored" content will retain them.
2.  **Irreversible Deployment:** Moving all users at once eliminates the ability to rollback if the new algorithm fails or the rewrite contains critical bugs. With a rival growing, a failed launch could be fatal.
3.  **Unproven Premise:** There is no evidence provided that the traffic slowdown is caused by the surfacing mechanism rather than market saturation or the rival's growth. Rewriting the entire product assumes the problem is technical rather than strategic.

**Risks:**
*   Alienating the core community during a rewrite.
*   Engineering delays inherent in starting from scratch.
*   No feedback loop before full commitment.

**Analysis:**
The plan fails the "do nothing" comparison. Doing nothing preserves the current community while allowing time to test surfacing changes on a small segment. The proposed "big bang" approach offers no intermediate learning state. The cost of delay (losing more traffic to the rival) is real, but the cost of a failed total rewrite is existential. A cheaper, safer alternative exists: iterate the surfacing algorithm on the current codebase for a subset of users, or launch the new design to a small beta group first. The absence of a pilot, a phased rollout, or validation that users want publisher-focused content constitutes a blocker under the stated facts. The plan relies on the hope that the new version works perfectly immediately, which contradicts the admitted engineering risk of a full rewrite.

DECISION: NO-GO
