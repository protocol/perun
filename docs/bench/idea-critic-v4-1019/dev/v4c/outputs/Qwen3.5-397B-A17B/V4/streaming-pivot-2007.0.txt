First call: GO. The proposal defends the core business against inevitable disruption by leveraging existing subscriber trust before competitors lock them in.

**Steelman:** Launch streaming immediately as a free value-add to retain subscribers and gather data, accepting short-term margin pressure to secure long-term relevance.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserves current margins but invites immediate obsolescence as broadband spreads and giants enter. High risk of total collapse.
2.  **Paid/Separate Service:** Reduces cannibalization but slows adoption and fails to build the necessary user base quickly enough to deter entrants.
3.  **Proposed Plan (Free Add-on):** Maximizes adoption speed. The "small, old catalog" is acceptable for an early MVP; the goal is habit formation, not immediate parity. Poor quality is a temporary constraint of 2007 bandwidth, not a permanent flaw.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This aligns with the north star of "home entertainment delivery," shifting the vehicle from physical to digital. If we wait for perfect quality or universal broadband, we lose. The pre-mortem failure mode is bleeding cash on licenses without retention gains. However, since the service is free for existing users, the marginal cost is primarily licensing, which is capped by the current catalog size.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal admits high licensing costs and cannibalization risks. These are *risks*, not *blockers*. Cannibalization is intentional: it is better to cannibalize our own DVD revenue than have a competitor take the customer entirely. The lack of universal broadband limits immediate scale but does not invalidate the strategic move. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal's own numbers; the "profitable" status provides the cash runway to absorb early losses.

**Decision:** The absence of a blocker confirms the path. The risks (cost, tech, competition) are mitigated by the "free add-on" model which lowers the barrier to entry and creates a defensive moat through user data and habit.

Blockers: NONE.
Risks: Licensing burn rate, slow broadband adoption, content gap.
Kill Criteria: If streaming churn exceeds DVD churn by 20% within 12 months, or if licensing costs exceed 15% of DVD profit, pause expansion.

DECISION: GO
