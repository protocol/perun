First call: GO. The proposal secures the franchise's survival against an inevitable market shift, even at the cost of short-term margin erosion.

**Steelman:** Launching a free, limited streaming bundle now converts a vulnerable physical monopoly into a hybrid model, retaining subscribers who might otherwise defect to emerging pure-play competitors, while gathering critical data on digital consumption at minimal incremental cost.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary fear is cannibalization of the high-margin DVD business. However, doing nothing is the riskier path; if broadband adoption accelerates or a giant enters (as expected), the company loses customers with zero digital footprint to defend them. A "paid add-on" alternative fails because it creates friction and slows adoption, ceding the learning curve to rivals. "Waiting for better tech" ignores that the strategic asset being built is the licensing relationships and user behavior data, not just the current video quality.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the long-term vision of home entertainment delivery. If we wait five years for perfect quality and universal broadband, we will likely be irrelevant, having lost the customer relationship. The cost of delay is total obsolescence. The small, older catalog is a feature, not a bug: it minimizes licensing costs while testing the infrastructure.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*Blockers:* NONE. The financials show the core business is profitable enough to subsidize this experiment. No fact presented proves the plan fails as proposed; the risks are executional, not fatal.
*Risks:* High licensing costs, potential margin dilution, and competition. These are managed by limiting the initial catalog and keeping the service free for existing users (capped cost).

**Decision:**
The proposal represents a classic "two-way door" experiment with a capped downside (licensing fees for a small catalog) and an uncapped upside (survival and market leadership in streaming). The absence of a pilot is mitigated by the "existing subscribers only" constraint, which naturally limits blast radius.

**Kill Criteria:**
Stop if streaming infrastructure costs exceed 5% of DVD profits within 12 months, or if subscriber churn increases despite the added benefit. Leading metric: Weekly streaming hours per user. Review date: Q4 2007.

DECISION: GO
