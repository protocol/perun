First call: NO-GO. The proposed $6 billion cash outlay for a display-ad platform, aimed at catching a search leader who just acquired a rival, ignores the fatal conflict with the buyer's existing agency customers and the extreme premium in a fast-changing market.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform is the only way to instantly own display technology and prevent the search leader from monopolizing the ad stack, despite the high cost and channel conflict.

**Attack:**
1.  **Channel Conflict (Blocker):** The proposal admits concerns about conflicts with existing agency customers. Buying an agency/platform hybrid alienates these partners, who may flee to competitors. Losing established revenue streams to chase a speculative display future destroys net value.
2.  **Market Timing & Tech Risk:** In 2007, display technology is volatile. Paying a "large premium" for assets that may depreciate rapidly due to tech shifts is financially unsound. The search leader's move does not necessitate a panicked, overpriced reaction in a different ad vertical.
3.  **Alternative:** A "do nothing" or "build/buy smaller" approach preserves cash and partner trust. The urgency is artificial; being second in display is preferable to being bankrupt or partner-less.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-acquisition, major agency clients have left due to perceived competition, the technology is already obsolete requiring another write-down, and the $6 billion cash reserve is gone, leaving the company vulnerable.

**Evidence Check:** The proposal itself cites "conflicts with existing agency customers" and "fast-changing technology" as known risks. These are not hypothetical; they are stated facts that directly undermine the core value proposition of the acquisition. No mitigation is offered.

**Blockers:**
*   Stated conflict with existing revenue-generating agency customers creates immediate net revenue loss risk.
*   Large premium paid for assets in a "fast-changing" market with no moat guarantees capital destruction.

**Risks:** Execution difficulty, cultural clash, regulatory scrutiny (noted but secondary to the financial logic failure).

The plan fails on its own stated facts: it trades certain, stable partner revenue and cash for a conflicted, overpriced asset in a volatile market.

DECISION: NO-GO
