First call: NO-GO. The proposal discards the brand's primary shelf cue—the orange with a straw—which is explicitly cited as the main recognition driver for a loyal, older demographic.

**Steelman:** A modernized, minimalist design with a cap-shaped orange and vertical branding will refresh the brand image and attract new buyers without alienating the core base, justified by a massive advertising push.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits the old image is the "main shelf cue" and the base is "older." Removing the primary visual anchor for this specific demographic contradicts the stated facts. Advertising cannot instantly retrain deep-seated visual habits at the point of sale, especially for older consumers who rely on established cues.
2.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves shelf recognition. A "staged pilot" in limited markets would test the recognition failure before a costly nationwide rollout. The proposed "all stores at once" approach maximizes downside risk.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch failed. Why? Shoppers walked past the cartons, unable to locate the brand quickly. The vertical text and minimalist glass were insufficient substitutes for the iconic straw-orange. The cost of delay (waiting for a pilot) is negligible compared to the cost of a nationwide sales collapse.
4.  **Blocker:** The plan explicitly removes the verified primary identification mechanism for its core user base while committing to an irreversible, simultaneous nationwide launch. This is a factual contradiction: you cannot claim the old image is essential for recognition and then remove it entirely without a validation step.

**Blockers:**
-   Removes the stated "main shelf cue" for a "loyal and older" base in a single high-risk step.
-   No pilot or phased rollout to verify recognition before nationwide commitment.

**Risks:**
-   Advertising may fail to overcome loss of visual cue.
-   High cost of reprinting and campaign if sales dip.

**Decision:** No-go. The plan ignores its own evidence regarding shelf cues and demographics. A capped, time-boxed pilot in a single region is required to prove recognition before any nationwide change.

DECISION: NO-GO
