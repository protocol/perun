First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a shrinking trend with a product that likely satisfies neither core segment, at a higher price, while a rival tests the same uncertain water.

**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures consumers wanting reduced sugar without the artificial taste of diet, leveraging a premium price to signal quality to young men before the low-carb trend fades.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Trend:** The core premise relies on the "low-carb" diet being a sustained shift. If this is a fad (as noted in risks), launching a permanent SKUs expansion is capital destruction. The trend was peaking in 2004; entering late with a premium product increases exposure to the crash.
2.  **Segmentation Failure:** The "uncanny valley" of calories is real. Health-conscious buyers choose zero-calorie diet drinks; taste-focused buyers choose full-sugar. A half-sugar drink risks alienating both: too many carbs for dieters, compromised taste for regular drinkers.
3.  **Pricing & Trial:** A premium price creates a barrier to trial for a product with an unproven value proposition. If the taste is compromised (likely, given 2004 sweetener tech), the high price amplifies consumer regret, killing repeat purchases.
4.  **Competitive Dynamics:** A rival is already launching a similar drink. Entering a crowded, niche market with a "me-too" product at a higher price point is strategically weak unless the brand equity is overwhelming, which is not guaranteed for a specific sub-variant.
5.  **Opportunity Cost:** Capital spent on a large campaign for a potentially transient trend diverts resources from defending core sugar soda share or investing in proven diet growth.

**Blockers:**
*   **Trend Volatility:** The proposal admits the low-carb trend may be a fad. Launching a major CPG SKU on a potentially collapsing trend is a factual mismatch between risk and commitment.
*   **Value Proposition Gap:** No evidence suggests a "half-sugar" taste profile is desirable or technically feasible without off-notes, yet the price is premium.

**Risks:** Cannibalization of existing lines; failure to convert diet users; rapid trend collapse.

**Kill Criteria:** If the low-carb trend shows >10% decline in 6 months, or if blind taste tests show <50% preference over diet/full-sugar variants at parity pricing, the project stops.

DECISION: NO-GO
