First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $36 billion to acquire a network explicitly described as incompatible and limited, creating an immediate obligation to run dual legacy systems with no stated plan or budget for the required massive integration effort.

Steelman: The buyer acquires a sticky, high-value business segment (push-to-talk) that competitors cannot easily replicate, justifying the premium if the technology can be migrated or bridged efficiently.

However, the facts provided confirm a fatal blocker: the core asset (the network) is technologically incompatible with the buyer's infrastructure and is "limited for data." In 2005, data capacity is the primary growth vector; acquiring a network that restricts this while forcing the operation of two disparate systems guarantees exorbitant integration costs and operational complexity. The proposal acknowledges these risks but offers no mitigation strategy, timeline, or specific cap on the integration spend. The "large goodwill" implies paying for value that the technical debt may erode before synergy is realized.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids $36B outlay and integration chaos; forfeits the specific push-to-talk niche.
2. Build/Bridge: Develop a gateway solution or build a compatible push-to-talk layer on the existing network. This likely costs less than $36B and avoids merging incompatible physical layers.
3. Acquire only the customer contracts (if legally feasible) and migrate them, leaving the legacy network behind.

The proposal fails because it treats the acquisition of the *network* as necessary when the value lies solely in the *customer base*. Buying the incompatible network forces the buyer to solve a hard engineering problem (merging distinct technologies) while simultaneously paying a premium for the customers. Without a verified migration path that retires the legacy tech quickly, the integration cost will exceed the lifetime value of the acquired users. The stated facts show the target's technology is a liability, not an asset, making the purchase of the infrastructure itself unjustifiable.

Blockers: The plan requires integrating incompatible networks with no defined technical path or cost cap, and the acquired network limits data growth, contradicting industry direction.
Risks: Massive integration overruns, customer churn during migration, goodwill impairment.

DECISION: NO-GO
