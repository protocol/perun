First call: NO-GO. The proposal compounds a recent 60% price hike with immediate, high-friction operational fragmentation.

**Steelman:** Separating the brands isolates the declining DVD asset, allowing the streaming unit to pivot without legacy drag.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing & Trust:** Executing a complex split (new logins, bills, brands) immediately after a massive price increase is perceived as punitive evasion. It validates customer fears that the price hike was a tactic to force a split or shed DVD users.
2.  **Friction vs. Value:** The proposal explicitly removes shared ratings and adds login/billing friction while offering no new value. In a subscription model, increasing effort while decreasing utility drives churn.
3.  **Self-Contradiction:** The rationale cites "customer confusion" as a risk, yet the solution *creates* maximum confusion (two brands, two queues) during a period of high price sensitivity.
4.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "internal separation only" options achieve management focus without alienating the remaining dual-subscriber base. A cheaper alternative is to keep the brand unified while separating backend P&Ls.

**Blockers:**
*   **Compound Shock:** The combination of a 60% price hike and immediate service fragmentation creates a single point of failure for customer retention. The stated facts confirm the price hike just occurred; adding structural friction now guarantees a churn spike that outweighs any strategic benefit of separation.
*   **Value Destruction:** Explicitly removing cross-service recommendations downgrades the product for the most loyal (dual-use) segment immediately after asking them to pay significantly more.

**Risks:** Total brand alienation, mass cancellation of the remaining DVD base, and streaming growth stalling due to negative PR.

**Kill Criteria:** If dual-subscriber churn exceeds 20% within 30 days of announcement, the split is aborted. However, given the current facts, the probability of hitting this trigger is near certain.

The proposal fails because it treats a strategic restructuring as a tactical fix for pricing backlash, ignoring that the *method* of separation destroys the remaining value proposition for the targeted customers.

DECISION: NO-GO
