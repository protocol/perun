First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a massive, irreversible infrastructure build (new dealer network) and a fixed 1957 launch date despite an explicit, unquantified risk of recession and a segment that "could shrink."

**Steelman:** Launching a distinct mid-priced brand with dedicated dealers captures the growing market gap before competitors, leveraging extensive research to ensure product-market fit.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing vs. Irreversibility:** The plan locks in high fixed costs (dealer network, tooling) for a 1957 debut while admitting a recession risk. If the economy contracts in 1957-58, demand for mid-priced cars typically collapses first, leaving the new division with empty showrooms and stranded inventory. The "massive" ad spend cannot overcome macroeconomic contraction.
2.  **Channel Conflict:** Building a *new* dealer network creates immediate friction with existing low and premium dealers who may view the new brand as cannibalization rather than expansion, potentially fracturing the company's overall sales force morale and loyalty.
3.  **Alternative:** A cheaper, reversible path exists: launch the new models through *existing* dealers (perhaps dual-franchising with the premium or low-end lines) to test demand without the capital burden of a standalone network. This preserves the option to kill the brand if the recession hits, whereas a standalone network is a sunk cost.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fixed Launch Date:** The commitment to a 1957 debut ignores the leading indicator of a potential recession. Launching into a downturn with a new, unproven brand and high overhead is a capital destruction event.
*   **Infrastructure Lock-in:** Building a new dealer network before validating sales in a potentially shrinking market violates the principle of reversible steps.

**Risks:** Market segment shrinkage, brand overlap, execution complexity. These are compounded by the blocker of fixed infrastructure costs.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal fails the "reversibility" test. It bets the farm on a specific economic outcome (no recession) while admitting the opposite is possible. The existence of a lower-cost alternative (using existing dealers) that achieves the same market test without the catastrophic downside means the current plan is unnecessarily risky. The plan must be revised to decouple the product launch from the infrastructure build and include a recession contingency.

DECISION: NO-GO
