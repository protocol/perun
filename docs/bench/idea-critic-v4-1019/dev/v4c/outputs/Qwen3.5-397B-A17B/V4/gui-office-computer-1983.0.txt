First call: NO-GO. The stated price is prohibitive, performance is inadequate, and internal cannibalization is imminent.

**Steelman:** A premium, mouse-driven workstation offers unparalleled usability for high-value office tasks, justifying a luxury price point through productivity gains.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on its own stated facts. At $10,000, the unit costs several times more than established competitors while delivering slower performance. Office workers, the target demographic, prioritize throughput and compatibility over novel interaction models when the price premium is this extreme. The lack of third-party software creates a barren ecosystem, rendering the hardware useless for standard business operations. Most critically, an internal cheaper alternative is already in development; launching this expensive, slow unit now guarantees immediate obsolescence and brand damage upon the sibling's release.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve capital and brand reputation; wait for the internal cheaper project to mature.
2.  **Pivot to niche:** Target specialized graphical work (e.g., design) where the mouse is essential, not general office work.
3.  **Delay:** Halt launch until performance matches or exceeds competitors and the software gap is closed.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sells negligible units due to price/performance mismatch, developers ignore the platform due to low install base, and the internal cheaper team inherits a tainted market segment.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price/Performance Mismatch:** Charging a premium for inferior speed is commercially non-viable.
*   **Internal Cannibalization:** A cheaper, similar internal product makes this launch strategically redundant.
*   **Ecosystem Void:** No software means no utility for the target audience.

**Risks:** Market confusion, wasted R&D, opportunity cost of delaying the superior internal option.

**Decision:** The plan cannot proceed as proposed. The combination of excessive price, poor performance, and an imminent internal superior alternative constitutes a fatal strategic error. Launching this now would be an act of commercial self-sabotage. The only sound operator move is to stop this specific configuration and price point immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
