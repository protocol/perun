First call: GO. The company leverages its dominant hardware position to solve the fragmentation and legal risk of piracy, creating a seamless, paid alternative where none exists.

**Steelman:** By integrating a legal, à la carte store directly into the best-selling portable player, the company converts pirates into paying customers through unmatched convenience, while securing exclusive content deals that rivals cannot match immediately.

**Attack & Analysis:**
*   **Free Competition:** File-sharing is ubiquitous but friction-heavy (search time, virus risk, legal fear). A 99-cent price point is low enough to impulse-buy for reliability and safety.
*   **Label Risk:** Labels are desperate for revenue amidst collapsing CD sales; they need a controlled distribution channel more than the manufacturer needs any single catalog. Mutual dependency secures deals.
*   **Margin Concerns:** Hardware sales drive the ecosystem. The store need not be highly profitable initially; its purpose is to increase the value and stickiness of the high-margin hardware.
*   **Platform Lock-in:** Restricting the store to own computers initially is a feature, not a bug. It creates a "halo effect," driving computer and player sales through a walled garden of convenience.
*   **Subscription Rivals:** In 2003, bandwidth and mobile data make streaming/subscriptions clunky. Ownership (downloading) fits the current "portable" paradigm better than renting.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if labels collude to withhold content or if the copy protection alienates users. However, the alternative (doing nothing) guarantees total erosion of music value. The "do nothing" option yields zero revenue and continued piracy.

**Blockers:** None. The risks are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The margin issue is offset by hardware synergy. The platform restriction is a strategic moat.

**Risks:** Label negotiations stall; competitors clone the model once proven. These are managed by moving fast and leveraging the existing installed base.

**Kill Criteria:** If major labels refuse licensing within 6 months, or if attachment rate (songs bought per user) remains below 10% after one year, pivot to subscription or wholesale models.

**Leading Metric:** Songs sold per active device in Q1 post-launch. Review date: 90 days after launch.

DECISION: GO
