First call: GO. The stated facts show a dominant content owner with billions in licensing revenue, a clear strategic pivot to direct-to-consumer, and a willingness to fund multi-year losses to capture market share against a single incumbent. The risks are real but manageable given the asset base.

Steelman: Leveraging an unmatched content library to disintermediate distributors, accepting short-term licensing loss and operating deficits to build a scaled, high-margin recurring revenue stream that future-proofs the business against cord-cutting.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Retain licensing fees but cede long-term customer ownership and pricing power to rivals; eventual margin compression as rivals produce more content.
2. **Hybrid/Windowing:** License content with delays. Reduces cannibalization but dilutes the "must-have" value proposition needed to drive mass migration.
3. **Proposed (Full Pull):** Maximizes subscriber growth velocity and brand control. Beats "do nothing" on strategic survival; beats "hybrid" on clarity of value.

Fit: Aligns with the industry shift from distribution-led to content-led models. Assumes the content library is sufficiently deep to sustain retention without third-party hits.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the tech platform crashes at scale, content costs spiral while licensing revenue vanishes, or the price point fails to overcome switching friction. This is a two-way door (content can be re-licensed, service sold/pivoted), though costly to reverse. Cost of delay is high: every quarter waits cedes more data and habit formation to the incumbent.

Evidence: Based on 2019 market dynamics where the incumbent had >100M subs and the proposer had billions in licensing income to fund the transition.

Blockers: NONE. No fact provided shows the math fails (licensing loss < projected LTV), that the tech is impossible to build/buy, or that the audience explicitly rejects the model. The risks cited (burn, execution, cannibalization) are inherent to the strategy, not proof of failure.

Risks: High cash burn, technical execution debt, temporary revenue dip. These are mitigated by the "multi-year operating loss" funding plan and the asset depth.

Kill Criteria: Halt expansion if subscriber growth falls below 5M/year after Year 2, or if cash burn exceeds 150% of forecast without corresponding retention uplift. Leading metric: Net Subscriber Additions per Quarter.

DECISION: GO
