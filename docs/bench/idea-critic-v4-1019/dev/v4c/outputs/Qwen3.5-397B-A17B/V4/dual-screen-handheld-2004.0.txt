First call: GO. The proposal avoids a direct hardware arms race against a well-funded rival, opting instead for asymmetric competition via unique form factor and price.

Steelman: By introducing a dual-screen device with touch input at a lower price point while maintaining the legacy product line, the company can expand the market to non-core gamers without alienating existing users, leveraging software innovation over raw graphical power.

Options analysis:
1. **Do nothing:** Cede the mass market to the rival's superior graphics; likely long-term decline as hardware becomes the sole differentiator.
2. **Match hardware:** Enter a capital-intensive war against a better-funded rival with no guarantee of winning; high risk, low margin.
3. **Proposed path:** Differentiate on gameplay mechanics (touch/dual-screen) and price. Lower hardware specs reduce unit cost, enabling a aggressive price strategy. Keeping the older model on sale captures the budget segment and maintains the installed base.

Fit: This aligns with broadening the audience beyond core players. The "gimmick" risk is mitigated by the lower price point, which lowers the barrier to entry for casual users who do not prioritize high-fidelity graphics.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if developers ignore the second screen, making the device merely a more expensive single-screen console. However, the lower price relative to the rival offsets this hardware complexity for the consumer. The "unproven" nature of touch is an opportunity to define a new category rather than follow an established one.

Blockers: NONE. The facts provided show no contradiction in the financial logic or market positioning. The risks (developer confusion, gimmick perception) are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws in the strategy. The proposal explicitly addresses the cost concern by targeting a lower price than the rival, implying the bill of materials is manageable.

Risks: Developer adoption speed; consumer perception of novelty vs. utility. These are managed by the continued sale of the older handheld (risk mitigation) and the price advantage.

The strategy successfully pivots the battlefield from "who has the best graphics" to "who has the most engaging experiences," accessible at a lower cost.

DECISION: GO
