First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve three unproven, high-complexity variables (cross-border logistics, new inventory system, foreign regulation) simultaneously with a massive capital commitment (130–150 stores) before validating unit economics in the new market.

**Steelman:** Rapid saturation prevents the entrenched competitor from retaliating locally and leverages existing lease availability to secure a dominant footprint before costs rise.

**Attack:**
1.  **Compound Risk:** The plan couples an unproven supply chain with an unproven inventory system. If the new system fails to handle bilingual labeling or cross-border nuances, the entire network of 150 stores faces stockouts or compliance fines immediately. There is no "learning phase"; the learning happens at maximum scale.
2.  **Alternative:** A "beachhead" strategy (5–10 stores) would validate the price perception and logistics model with <5% of the capital risk. The proposal rejects this implicitly by demanding "nearly all" sites open within two years.
3.  **Competitive Reality:** Entrenched competitors can sustain price wars longer than a new entrant burning cash on systemic failures. Speed only matters if the operation works; speed amplifies failure when the backend is untested.
4.  **Missing Evidence:** The proposal admits risks regarding "price perception" and "unproven systems" but offers no pilot data, kill criteria, or fallback plan. It assumes the domestic model translates directly despite admitted regulatory and logistical differences.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Core Systems:** Launching a full network on an inventory system explicitly stated as "not yet proven" in this context is a factual failure condition.
*   **All-In Commitment:** The requirement to open ~150 stores before validating the supply chain removes the ability to pivot based on early operational data.

**Risks:** Regulatory delays, bilingual labeling errors, competitor price retaliation, supply chain breakdown. These are not merely risks; without a pilot, they are probable outcomes.

**Kill Criteria Missing:** No metric defines success for the first 10 stores before committing to the remaining 140. No cap on loss if the inventory system fails.

**Verdict:** The plan conflates speed with viability. It bets the company on a "big bang" launch of untested systems in an hostile environment. A sound operator validates the supply chain and system with a small cluster before signing 150 leases.

DECISION: NO-GO
