First call: GO. The proposal targets an uncontested market segment (casual/non-gamers) with a differentiated value proposition (motion control, low cost) that rivals cannot match without cannibalizing their high-margin HD strategies.

Steelman: By abandoning the specs war, the console creates a new category where price and accessibility matter more than fidelity, turning hardware limitations into a cost advantage.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Remain third in a shrinking race for hardcore gamers; likely irrelevance.
2. Compete on power: Require massive R&D spend against entrenched leaders; high risk of failure.
3. Proposed path: Low capex, unique input method, mass-market pricing. Only this option offers a path to category leadership.

Fit: Assumes the goal is market expansion and profitability, not just technical prestige. A "toy" perception is a feature for the target demographic, not a bug.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if third-party developers refuse to build software or if the motion tech proves unreliable. However, the low hardware cost reduces the break-even point, and first-party titles can bridge the software gap initially. This is a two-way door; if it fails, losses are capped by the modest hardware investment. If it succeeds, it redefines the industry.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the verified fact that the company is currently third place. The "untested audience" is a risk, not a blocker; every new platform faces this. The skepticism of hardcore gamers is irrelevant to the success criterion of capturing non-gamers.

Blockers: NONE. No fact presented proves the casual market does not exist or that the price point is unsustainable. The thin margin is a strategic choice to drive volume, not a mathematical impossibility.

Risks: Third-party hesitation, "toy" stigma, technical novelty issues. These are managed by making the hardware cheap enough to sell at volume and controlling the software narrative internally.

Kill Criteria: If unit sales do not exceed the combined total of the top two competitors within 18 months, or if the motion controller return rate exceeds 15%, pivot or cease production. Leading metric: Weekly active non-gamer households.

DECISION: GO
