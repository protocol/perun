First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a software ecosystem deficit with a hardware acquisition, misaligning capital with the core failure mode.

Steelman: Acquiring the handset manufacturer is the only remaining lever to prevent immediate platform abandonment and force a unified hardware-software experience that might attract developers.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Misaligned Solution:** The primary threat is the lack of applications and developers on the rival platform. Buying a declining hardware division (€5.4B) does not directly create software demand or port apps. It consumes cash needed for developer incentives while adding a bleeding hardware business.
2.  **Falling Asset:** The target's sales are already falling. Acquiring a losing position in a winner-take-all market locks capital into a depreciating asset rather than funding the pivot required to compete on services.
3.  **Integration Distraction:** The stated risks (workforce integration, competitor reaction) are severe. Managing a large hardware workforce diverts executive focus from the critical software ecosystem battle during a pivotal year (2013).

**Risks:**
*   Competitor retaliation could isolate the combined entity further.
*   The "app gap" may prove insurmountable regardless of hardware ownership.
*   Culture clash between software and hardware divisions.

**Analysis:**
The proposal treats a platform problem as a supply chain problem. In a two-sided market, owning the device does not guarantee developer interest if the user base is shrinking. The €5.4 billion would be better deployed as a "kill criterion" fund: direct subsidies for top developers, porting tools, or a strategic pivot to niche enterprise markets where app count matters less. Buying the handset maker assumes hardware control fixes software adoption; history suggests it usually results in owning a larger share of a dying segment. The falling sales indicate the market has already voted; this acquisition attempts to buy time that the ecosystem dynamics may not grant. Without a concrete plan to instantly reverse the app deficit using this hardware control, the spend is a defensive panic move, not a strategic offensive.

DECISION: NO-GO
