First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and market trajectory described make rapid subscriber growth—the only path to solvency—mathematically improbable.

**Steelman:** This is the sole viable option for true global coverage, capturing high-margin business travelers before ground networks mature.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Shrinkage:** The target segment (international business travelers) is shrinking as ground cellular expands. The proposal admits this risk but relies on a window of opportunity that is closing faster than the debt clock ticks.
2.  **Price Elasticity:** At $3,000 per handset and several dollars per minute, the service is a luxury niche, not a mass utility. Debt service on $5 billion requires massive scale, which these prices preclude.
3.  **Product Deficit:** Bulky hardware that fails indoors contradicts the core value proposition of reliable mobile communication.
4.  **Channel Readiness:** Launching without ready sales channels guarantees a slow ramp, directly conflicting with the urgent need for cash flow to service borrowed capital.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid sinking further capital into a asset with rapidly depreciating utility.
*   **Pivot to Niche/Gov:** Target specific government or maritime use cases where price is no object, rather than the broader "business traveler" market.
*   **Delay:** Wait for handset miniaturization and cost reduction, though this risks ground network saturation.

**Pre-mortem:** The company launches, misses aggressive subscriber targets due to price and product friction, cannot service the massive debt load, and declares bankruptcy within two years.

**Blockers:**
*   **Math Mismatch:** The debt structure requires mass adoption; the pricing and product quality limit adoption to a tiny niche. This is a fundamental contradiction.
*   **Timing:** The competitive advantage (global coverage) is eroding daily as ground networks spread, while the product remains static and flawed.

**Risks:** Even if sales occur, the margin may not cover the cost of capital.

The plan relies on capturing a large market with a product priced and designed for a small one, while carrying debt that demands the former. This is a fatal structural flaw.

DECISION: NO-GO
