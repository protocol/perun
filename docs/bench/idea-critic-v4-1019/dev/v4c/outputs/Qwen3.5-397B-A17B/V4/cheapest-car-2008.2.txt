First call: GO. The claim addresses a massive, unserved demographic transitioning from two-wheelers to four-wheelers with an unprecedented price point.

Steelman: A dedicated high-volume factory can achieve a $2,500 price point, creating a new market segment for families upgrading from scooters, provided safety perceptions are managed and land disputes resolved.

**Blockers:** NONE. The listed risks (protests, safety perception, brand stigma, alternatives) are execution challenges, not factual impossibilities. Land protests delay but do not permanently invalidate the site unless violence escalates beyond resolution (not stated). Safety doubts are countered by meeting regulatory standards, not eliminating them. "Cheapest" stigma is a marketing pivot, not a product failure; the value proposition is mobility, not prestige. Scooters lack weather protection and safety; used cars at this price point are often unreliable. The math holds: if volume targets are met, unit costs drop to support the price.

**Risks:** High. Farmer protests could delay construction. Cost-cutting could lead to recalls if quality floors are breached. Brand positioning as "cheapest" might limit upward mobility later. Competitors might undercut with used inventory.

**Better Ways:** Doing nothing cedes the entire bottom-of-pyramid market to two-wheelers forever. A phased launch or using an existing factory reduces capital risk but likely fails to hit the $2,500 target due to lack of dedicated scale. The "cheapest" angle is the only wedge strong enough to disrupt the status quo.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if the factory is delayed so long that costs inflate beyond the $2,500 cap, or if a single high-profile accident confirms safety fears. The cost of delay is losing the first-mover advantage in a nascent segment.

**Decision Logic:** No factual blocker exists. The risks are manageable through aggressive community engagement, transparent safety testing, and reframing marketing from "cheapest" to "most accessible family car." The potential upside of mobilizing millions of families outweighs the execution risks. The proposal must proceed immediately to secure the land and lock in supply chains before costs rise.

DECISION: GO
