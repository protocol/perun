First call: NO-GO. The plan bets the entire brand equity on taste preference while ignoring the verified risk that removing the original formula triggers an emotional backlash the tests did not measure.

**Steelman:** Replacing the flagship with a scientifically superior-tasting formula maximizes immediate consumption utility and halts share loss to sweeter rivals.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on blind taste tests where participants lacked full information. Specifically, consumers were not told the original would be withdrawn. This creates a fatal data gap: the tests measured "preference in addition to" rather than "preference instead of." The stated risks (emotional attachment, heritage) are not abstract uncertainties; they are core drivers of this specific brand's value. By withdrawing the original entirely, the plan converts a product choice into a forced loss of heritage, likely triggering a psychological reactance that overrides taste preference. A "better" taste does not compensate for the perceived theft of a cultural icon.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Retain status quo; slow share erosion continues.
2.  **Line Extension:** Launch the sweeter formula as a new variant alongside the original. This captures the "sweeter" demographic without alienating loyalists or testing the "withdrawal" hypothesis destructively.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** High reward, catastrophic failure mode if the emotional bond proves stronger than the palate.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales have collapsed not because the drink tastes bad, but because loyal consumers feel betrayed by the removal of their legacy product. Boycotts form, media narratives focus on "corporate arrogance," and bottlers face hostility. The brand is no longer defined by taste but by loss.

**Blockers:** The testing methodology is fundamentally flawed for the specific action proposed (total replacement). The data claims superiority based on incomplete premises (hiding the removal), rendering the core justification invalid. The absence of a fallback product means there is no recovery path if the emotional reaction exceeds the taste gain.

**Risks:** Brand alienation, bottler revolt, irreversible loss of heritage positioning.

**Decision:** The plan fails the evidence test because the supporting data explicitly excluded the primary variable (removal) that defines the proposal's risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
