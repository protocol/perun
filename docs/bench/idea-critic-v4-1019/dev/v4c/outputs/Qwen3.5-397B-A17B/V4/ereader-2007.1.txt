First call: GO. The proposal directly attacks the three stated barriers to entry (poor prior hardware, piracy fears, friction) with specific mechanisms (paper-like screen, cellular delivery, low price) that align with entering a new category.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, friction-free device with subsidized connectivity will unlock a latent mass market for digital books that previous clunky, PC-dependent readers failed to reach, creating a defensible ecosystem despite hardware inexperience.

**Blockers:** NONE. The cited risks are execution challenges or strategic trade-offs, not factual contradictions.
*   *Inexperience:* A valid risk, but not a blocker if the company treats hardware as a loss-leader for high-margin content.
*   *Margin/Publisher friction:* The "low price" model is a strategic choice to drive volume; it does not make the plan impossible, merely difficult to negotiate.
*   *Cannibalization:* Intentional cannibalization is often required for category transition; avoiding it guarantees stagnation.
*   *Past failures:* Previous devices lacked the specific combination of e-ink and ubiquitous wireless connectivity; this is a qualitative difference, not a repetition.

**Risks:** High capital expenditure on unsold inventory; failure to secure publisher buy-in at low price points; slower-than-expected adoption of e-ink technology. These are managed via kill criteria (e.g., stop production if attach rate < X% by date Y), not by aborting the initiative.

**Alternatives:**
1.  *Do nothing:* Cedes the digital future to competitors; guarantees long-term decline as formats shift.
2.  *License software only:* Leaves the fragmented hardware problem unsolved; fails to guarantee the "one-click" experience required to overcome piracy fears and friction.
3.  *Wait for market maturity:* Misses the first-mover advantage in building the content ecosystem.

The proposal beats "do nothing" because the status quo is existential decline. It beats "software only" because the value proposition relies on removing the PC from the loop. The lack of hardware experience is a hurdle, not a wall, solvable by partnering or acquiring talent. The potential upside (owning the digital book standard) vastly outweighs the risk of a contained hardware loss.

DECISION: GO
