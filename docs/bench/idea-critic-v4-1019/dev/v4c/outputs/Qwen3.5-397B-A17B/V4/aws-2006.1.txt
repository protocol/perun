First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer with no developer platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors are already hostile to its capital spending.

**Steelman:** Leveraging spiky internal infrastructure to create a new revenue stream could turn a cost center into a profit center if the market materializes.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capability Gap:** Running a retail site is fundamentally different from operating a multi-tenant developer platform. The organization lacks the specific engineering culture, support models, and reliability SLAs required. Building this from scratch invites catastrophic failure.
2.  **Market Validation:** The prompt explicitly states the market for hourly server rental is "unproven." Entering an undefined market requires R&D spend, contradicting the need to reassure investors about capital efficiency.
3.  **Strategic Distraction:** With thin margins, the core retail business cannot absorb the focus shift or potential reliability incidents caused by a experimental external platform. A failure here could damage the core brand's trust.
4.  **Competitive Moat:** Large incumbent hardware and software vendors already exist. Without a proven unique advantage or existing developer loyalty, the retailer has no clear path to capture share.
5.  **Investor Sentiment:** Proceeding with heavy capital expansion into an unproven sector directly antagonizes current investor concerns, risking capital access for the core business.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Market:** The core revenue hypothesis relies on a market that does not yet exist.
*   **Missing Capability:** The company has zero experience in the proposed domain at scale.
*   **Capital Conflict:** The strategy exacerbates the primary friction point with investors.

**Risks:** Margin dilution, brand damage from reliability issues, total loss of invested capital, and distraction leading to core retail erosion.

**Conclusion:** This is a high-cost, high-risk bet on an unproven market by an unqualified team, directly conflicting with investor mandates. It fails the "do nothing" comparison because "do nothing" preserves capital and focus for the core business while the market validates itself.

DECISION: NO-GO
