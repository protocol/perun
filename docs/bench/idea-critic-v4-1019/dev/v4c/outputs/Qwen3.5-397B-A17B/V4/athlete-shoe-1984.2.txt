First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets a large share of the marketing budget on an unproven rookie with high injury risk and regulatory uncertainty, violating standard risk management for a market leader.

Steelman: A unique, rule-breaking brand identity centered on a singular talent can disrupt a stagnant category, turning regulatory conflict into marketing fuel and justifying the premium via exclusivity and narrative.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Maintain status quo; likely continue losing basketball share to established stars. Low risk, low reward.
2. **Sign established veteran:** Higher certainty of performance, lower narrative upside, likely higher cost, no rule-breaking hook.
3. **Proposed Rookie Bet:** Highest risk (injury, flop), highest potential reward (cultural icon, rule-breaking buzz).

Fit: Assumes the goal is category disruption, not just safe share retention. If the north star is "leadership," safe plays often fail to regain lost ground.

Pre-mortem: The player suffers a career-ending injury in game one; the league bans the shoes immediately, forcing a costly recall; the player underperforms, making the shoe unsellable. This is a one-way door for the budget spent, but reversible in brand perception if framed as "bold."

Evidence: No data provided on the player's actual skill, only "rookie." No confirmation the league *will* fine (vs. just threaten). The claim that signature shoes rarely sell beyond a season is historical data that this specific strategy (rule-breaking) aims to invalidate.

Blockers: NONE. While risks are extreme, they are known variables, not factual contradictions. The "large share of budget" is a constraint, not a blocker, if the potential upside justifies the concentration. The proposal explicitly accepts these risks.

Risks: Injury, regulatory ban, player underperformance, budget concentration.

Kill Criteria: If the league issues a permanent ban on the specific colorways within 30 days, pivot to standard colors. If the player does not start 50% of games in Year 1, cap Year 2 spending. Leading metric: Pre-order volume vs. production cost by Month 6.

The proposal is a high-stakes gamble, but in a losing position, asymmetric bets are the only path to leadership. The risks are features, not bugs, of the disruption strategy.
