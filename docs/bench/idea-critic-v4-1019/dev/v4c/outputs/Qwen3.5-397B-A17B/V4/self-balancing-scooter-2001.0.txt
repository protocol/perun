First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to high-volume manufacturing and a premium price point before resolving fundamental legal and market-fit uncertainties.

Steelman: A revolutionary personal transporter with strong backing can create a new urban mobility category if launched decisively at scale to capture early adopters and institutional buyers.

However, the "blocker test" reveals critical failures in the current plan:
1.  **Legal Blocker:** The proposal admits the legal status on sidewalks and roads is "unclear." Launching a hardware product dependent on public infrastructure access without regulatory clarity is a fatal flaw. If cities ban it post-launch, inventory becomes worthless scrap.
2.  **Market Blocker:** The price ($5,000) is prohibitive for mass adoption, yet the factory is sized for 40,000 units/year. With "unproven" uses beyond institutions and "conspicuous" ridership, demand cannot support this volume. The unit economics likely fail if sales miss targets by even 50%, which is probable given the risks.
3.  **Alternative:** A "do nothing" or "pilot first" approach is superior. Deploy 500 units to institutional partners to validate usage patterns and lobby for regulations before committing to a $5,000 price point and massive factory overhead.

The risks listed (legal ambiguity, high price, unproven utility, social stigma) are not merely "risks" to be managed; they are unresolved prerequisites for the business model to function. Proceeding as proposed ignores the "one-way door" nature of building a factory and setting a brand price before validating the regulatory environment. The cost of delay (waiting for legal clarity) is negligible compared to the cost of failure (unsold inventory and regulatory bans).

Blockers: Regulatory uncertainty makes the product illegal in key markets; volume commitment exceeds proven demand by an order of magnitude.
Risks: Social stigma limiting adoption; limited utility reducing repeat value.

Kill criteria: Do not proceed until legal status is confirmed in top 10 target cities and pre-orders from non-institutional buyers exceed 5,000 units at the target price.

DECISION: NO-GO
