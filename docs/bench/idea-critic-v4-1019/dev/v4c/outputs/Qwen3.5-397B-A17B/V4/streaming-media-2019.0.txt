First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades certain, high-margin licensing revenue for speculative subscriber growth while intentionally funding multi-year losses, creating an immediate cash-flow deficit without a verified path to profitability.

Steelman: Direct-to-consumer distribution captures full lifetime value and data, preventing rivals from building moats using this company's content, provided the audience scales fast enough to offset the lost licensing fees.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Continue licensing; safe cash flow, but cedes strategic control and long-term relevance as rivals vertically integrate.
2. **Hybrid windowing:** License content with delays before moving to own platform; balances immediate revenue with audience building, reducing cash burn.
3. **Proposed (All-in):** Immediate withdrawal and low-price penetration; highest upside but maximum exposure to execution failure and cash depletion.

Fit: Aligns with a "direct relationship" vision but assumes the market will tolerate a late entrant burning cash to undercut an incumbent with 100M+ subscribers.

Pre-mortem: The platform launch suffers technical outages (never run before), subscriber growth stalls at 20M due to content fragmentation, and the loss of licensing revenue triggers a credit downgrade before scale is reached. This is a one-way door; pulling content from rivals is hard to reverse without damaging future partnerships. The cost of delay is losing first-mover data advantages, but the cost of speed is potential insolvency.

Evidence: The proposal admits the company has "never run" such a platform and targets "tens of millions" against a leader's "hundred million+" while accepting "multi-year operating loss." No math is provided showing when lost licensing fees are recouped.

Blockers:
1. **Unit economics mismatch:** The plan explicitly funds growth with losses while eliminating a billion-dollar revenue stream. Without a concrete cap on total burn or a specific break-even date tied to the "tens of millions" target, the financial model fails as stated.
2. **Execution gap:** Building a global streaming stack from zero while simultaneously marketing to tens of millions is an unproven capability for this entity; the risk is treated as manageable rather than existential.

Risks: Cannibalization of theatrical/cable, price wars with deeper-pocketed rivals, and content library dilution.

The absence of a capped pilot, a specific break-even metric, or a hybrid transition plan constitutes a failure of the proposal's financial logic. It demands approval for a strategy where the failure mode is running out of cash before proving demand.

DECISION: NO-GO
