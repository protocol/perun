First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets the console's ecosystem on unproven broadband adoption and a mandatory paywall for a feature competitors give away, risking immediate market rejection.

**Steelman:** A unified, premium online service creates a sticky ecosystem and recurring revenue, differentiating the console through superior identity and voice features that fragmented, free alternatives cannot match.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Fit:** In 2002, broadband penetration is critically low. Mandating it excludes the vast majority of the installed base, artificially capping the addressable market for online play before it begins.
2.  **Pricing Friction:** Competitors offer free online play. Introducing a double cost (console + subscription) for a "nice-to-have" feature violates established consumer expectations. Users will reject paying for what rivals provide free, especially when the value prop (voice/friends) is unproven at scale.
3.  **Operational Risk:** The company lacks experience running global hosting and support operations. Failure here damages the brand permanently, not just the service.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "freemium" model (free basic matchmaking, paid voice/features) or deferring the mandate until broadband hits 20% adoption reduces friction while testing the waters. The current "all-or-nothing" paid approach ignores Chesterton's Fence: the competitors' free model exists for a reason (user acquisition).

**Pre-mortem:** The service launches, subscription uptake is <5%, developers ignore the small paid user base for multiplayer titles, and the high operational costs bleed cash. The console is perceived as "expensive and restrictive."

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Broadband adoption is too low to sustain a mandatory model; the math on potential subscribers vs. operational cost likely fails.
*   **Fact:** Competitor pricing (free) sets a hard anchor; no evidence suggests users will pay a premium for unified identity yet.
*   **Capability:** Zero internal experience in running this specific service infrastructure creates a high probability of operational collapse.

**Risks:** User backlash, developer abandonment, brand damage. These are severe, but the blockers (market size and pricing reality) are fatal to the *current* proposal structure.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot in a high-broadband region shows <15% attach rate within 3 months, kill the mandatory aspect immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
