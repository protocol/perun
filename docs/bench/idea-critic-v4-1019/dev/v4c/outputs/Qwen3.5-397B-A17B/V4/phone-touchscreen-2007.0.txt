First call: GO. The proposal trades initial scale for unprecedented control over the user experience and a new revenue model, directly countering the erosion of the music player business by integrating it into a superior device.

**Steelman:** Launching a premium, keyboard-less touchscreen phone via an exclusive carrier partnership allows the company to define a new category, protect its music ecosystem, and capture service revenue, rather than ceding design control to carriers or competing on specs alone.

**Attack & Options:**
Critics cite lack of phone experience and the absence of a physical keyboard as fatal. However, the "do nothing" option guarantees the music player's decline as competitors bundle mediocre players into phones. Alternative paths like licensing software to multiple carriers would surrender design control, replicating the fragmented, carrier-dominated status quo. A cheaper, keyboard-based device would fail to differentiate. The premium price is justified only by a revolutionary interface; a standard phone at a high price would fail.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the vision of integrating hardware, software, and services. If it fails, it will be because the market rejects the touchscreen interface or the price point is too high for non-business users. However, the cost of delay is the total commoditization of the music player. The "no third-party apps" limitation is a temporary constraint to ensure stability for version one, not a permanent flaw.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*Blockers:* NONE. The lack of experience is a risk, not a factual blocker; the proposed revenue share and design control mitigate the carrier risk. The market's love for keyboards is a prevailing trend, not a law of physics preventing a new input method.
*Risks:* Unproven demand for a keyboard-less phone; limited initial reach due to exclusivity; no app ecosystem at launch. These are managed by the premium positioning (targeting early adopters) and the strategic necessity of the carrier deal to fund the revolution.

The proposal correctly identifies that competing on existing terms is a losing strategy. It creates a two-way door for future iterations while establishing a beachhead for a new platform.

DECISION: GO
