First call: NO-GO. The core value proposition is contradictory: it targets calorie-conscious consumers with a product that contains significantly more calories than their current choice (diet), while offering less satisfaction than the regular version they may be avoiding.

Steelman: A mid-calorie option captures consumers who find artificial sweeteners unacceptable but still want reduced sugar, leveraging a premium price to signal quality to young men.

However, the attack on this claim reveals fatal flaws based solely on the provided facts:
1.  **Market Fit Failure**: The proposal admits risks that the target demographic already has a superior solution (diet cola) for calorie reduction. A "half-sugar" drink fails the "what would have to be true" test: it requires a massive segment of users who strictly reject artificial sweeteners yet are highly sensitive to carbohydrates, a contradiction not supported by the stated trend of "low-carb diets" driving diet cola growth.
2.  **Competitive & Trend Risk**: A rival is already launching a similar drink, suggesting first-mover advantage is lost or the space is crowded. Furthermore, if the low-carb trend is a fad, investing in a large campaign for a niche hybrid product creates high exposure to a collapsing market segment.
3.  **Pricing Barrier**: Premium pricing for a product that satisfies neither the "taste" group (regular soda) nor the "health" group (diet soda) creates a friction point that deters trial, especially when cheaper, more effective alternatives exist.

The "better way" is to double down on the growing diet segment or innovate within the regular segment, rather than creating a compromised middle ground that alienates both. The pre-mortem is clear: the product launches, consumers try it once, realize it doesn't taste as good as regular nor save as many calories as diet, and churn immediately. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed large-scale campaign.

Blockers: The stated risk that the product satisfies neither group is a fundamental contradiction of the product's existence, not a mitigatable risk. The premium price exacerbates this by demanding higher value from a lower-value proposition.

Risks: Trend volatility, competitor saturation.

DECISION: NO-GO
