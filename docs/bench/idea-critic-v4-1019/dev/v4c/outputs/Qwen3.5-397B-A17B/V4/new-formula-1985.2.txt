First call: NO-GO. The proposal conflates sip-level preference with brand-level loyalty, ignoring that the product's value is partly emotional heritage, not just taste.

**Steelman:** Replacing the formula with a objectively better-tasting version will halt share loss by satisfying the primary functional demand of consumers.

**Attack:** The evidence provided contains a fatal gap: testing did not disclose the removal of the original. This invalidates the results for a replacement strategy. Consumers may prefer the sweeter taste in a vacuum but reject it if it requires sacrificing the original. The "sweeter wins" data only supports a line extension, not a substitution.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Retain share erosion but preserve brand equity.
2.  **Line Extension:** Launch the new formula alongside the old (lower risk, captures sweet-preference segment without alienating purists).
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Total replacement.

**Fit:** This contradicts the brand's "heritage" asset. A century-old formula is a unique differentiator; swapping it for a generic "sweeter" profile makes the brand a commodity competitor on taste alone, where it cannot win permanently against a rival already defined by that trait.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch triggers a public backlash. Loyalists feel betrayed not by the taste, but by the loss of choice and the erasure of history. Sales plummet due to boycott, not flavor.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Invalidated Data:** The core evidence (taste tests) is materially misleading because the constraint (total removal) was hidden from participants. The data proves "New > Old" in isolation, not "New (only) > Old (only)."
2.  **No Fallback:** The plan explicitly withdraws the original entirely. With no safety net, the identified risk of "emotional attachment" becomes a guaranteed failure mode if the reaction is negative.

**Risks:** Bottler rebellion, media narrative of corporate arrogance, permanent loss of the "authentic" segment.

**Conclusion:** The plan relies on data that does not test the actual proposal. You cannot approve a total replacement based on tests that only measured addition. The lack of a fallback product turns a manageable risk into an existential threat.

DECISION: NO-GO
