First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to raise unit costs and divert engineering focus from a fierce core rivalry to build capacity for a market with zero committed customers and no proven demand.

Steelman: Embedding general-purpose compute capabilities now creates an uncontested moat, transforming a commodity graphics vendor into a foundational platform for scientific computing before rivals recognize the shift.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Maintain cost leadership in gaming; let academics continue hacking existing APIs. Low risk, zero incremental cost.
2. Partner-led development: Build tools only for workstation cards where margins absorb costs, testing demand without poisoning mass-market unit economics.
3. The Proposal: Subsidize a hypothetical future market by taxing every current gamer via higher prices, while fighting a two-front war against a dominant rival.

Fit: This contradicts the immediate survival need to win the graphics race. A "north star" of scientific computing is assumed, not stated, and cannot justify bleeding cash in the core business today.

Pre-mortem: The program burns capital for three years. The compiler remains buggy, academic interest fails to translate to enterprise spend, and the core gaming division loses market share due to distracted engineering and higher prices. The company becomes a niche player in both graphics and compute.

Evidence: The proposal admits "no large paying market exists" and "no committed customers." Costs are immediate; revenue is speculative.

Blockers:
1. Unit economics failure: Raising costs on every unit without a paying customer base directly undermines the cash flow required to survive the core rivalry.
2. Strategic distraction: Engineering resources are finite; splitting focus during a "core race" invites defeat in the primary revenue generator.

Risks: Niche demand remains niche; toolchain adoption lags; rival exploits the distraction.

The absence of a pilot, cap, or committed anchor tenant is fatal here. This is not a risk to be managed with kill criteria; it is a fundamental misallocation of resources in a winner-take-all core market. The cheaper, safer alternative is to wait for clear demand signals or restrict this to high-margin workstation parts.

DECISION: NO-GO
