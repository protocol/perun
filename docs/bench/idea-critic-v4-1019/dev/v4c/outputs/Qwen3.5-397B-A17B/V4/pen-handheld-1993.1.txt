First call: NO-GO. The core value proposition (reliable handwriting recognition) is explicitly stated as unproven in testing, while the price point is high and the form factor compromises portability.

Steelman: Launching now establishes category ownership and leverages CEO publicity to force software adoption despite early hardware flaws.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Avoids reputational damage from a flawed launch but cedes the emerging handheld market to competitors.
2. **Delay launch:** Wait for reliable recognition and smaller form factor; risks missing the first-mover window but ensures product quality.
3. **Proposed plan:** High-risk launch with known defects; bets on marketing overcoming technical debt.

Fit: Aligns with the vision of leading a new category, but the execution contradicts the requirement for a "personal" device if it cannot fit in a pocket or reliably understand input.

Pre-mortem: The device launches to heavy fanfare, but users immediately encounter unusable handwriting recognition and bulkiness. Returns spike, reviews turn toxic, and the brand becomes synonymous with "vaporware" or failure, killing the category before it matures. This is a one-way door for brand reputation; a two-way door for capital.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits recognition is "unreliable in testing," the price is "high," and the size is "too big for a pocket." These are not future risks but current, verified negatives. No data suggests marketing can override fundamental utility failures in a computing device.

Blockers:
1. **Core feature failure:** The primary input method (handwriting) is unreliable. A computer that cannot accept input reliably fails its basic function.
2. **Form factor mismatch:** Marketed as "handheld" but "too big for a pocket," violating the core convenience promise of the category.
3. **Economic disconnect:** High price ($700 in 1993 is significant) for a device with little software and broken input offers no compelling value over existing solutions.

Risks: Unproven category, lack of software ecosystem, execution difficulty. These are secondary to the blockers.

The plan commits real money to a product that fails its primary functional requirement and portability constraint. No kill criteria or pilot is offered to mitigate the certainty of user frustration.

DECISION: NO-GO
