First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and behavioral friction of a $699 connected juicer relying on expensive proprietary consumables create an insurmountable barrier to mass adoption in 2016.

**Steelman:** A premium, hassle-free nutrition system where freshness guarantees and automated reordering justify a high upfront cost and recurring subscription for busy, affluent consumers.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price & Value:** At $699, the device costs more than high-end blenders and competitors' cold-press machines that accept *any* produce. The subscription model likely prices per serving significantly higher than grocery store produce, violating the "cheap healthy food" mental model.
2.  **Connectivity Friction:** Requiring Wi-Fi to verify "freshness" of a physical bag introduces a single point of failure. If the server drops or the user's network changes, the device becomes useless brickware. This is a solution in search of a problem; expiration dates can be printed on bags.
3.  **Market Reality:** The "single-serve appliance" market is niche. Scaling to justify "tens of millions" in funding requires mass penetration. History shows that closed-loop hardware (proprietary pods/bags) fails when the per-use cost exceeds the generic alternative by a wide margin, unless the convenience is absolute (which juicing cleanup is not, but prep is).
4.  **Better Way:** Sell a $299 open-system press. Let users buy any fruit. Offer a optional subscription for *convenience*, not *necessity*. Remove the internet requirement for basic operation.

**Pre-mortem:** The company burns cash on inventory and customer acquisition. Users try the device, balk at the $8/bag cost versus $1 homemade juice, and the machine sits on the counter. The "smart" features annoy users when connectivity glitches. The company pivots to B2B or shuts down within 18 months.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Mismatch:** The combined cost of device amortization + subscription exceeds the value proposition of fresh juice for the vast majority of the target market.
*   **Unnecessary Dependency:** Internet connectivity for freshness checking is a technical risk that offers no compensating user value, creating a guaranteed support nightmare and return vector.

**Risks:** Supply chain complexity for proprietary bags; low repeat purchase rate after novelty fades.

**Kill Criteria:** If Month 3 repeat purchase rate is <40% or CAC exceeds $300, halt expansion.

DECISION: NO-GO
