First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a fixed public schedule for unproven core technologies (battery durability, novel transmission) without a demand signal or regulatory mandate, creating massive warranty and reputational liability.

**Steelman:** A bold, fixed-schedule launch forces the organization to solve hard engineering problems quickly, securing first-mover advantage before competitors react.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Technical/Financial):** The core value proposition relies on battery durability, which is explicitly "unproven." Committing to mass production by 1997 guarantees that early units will likely suffer catastrophic failure modes unknown at pilot scale. With fuel cheap and no regulatory push, customers will not tolerate premium pricing for unreliable vehicles. The warranty exposure alone could bankrupt the program.
2.  **Blocker (Market):** There is "no near-term demand signal." In the mid-90s, consumers prioritize cost and reliability over efficiency when fuel is cheap. A fixed schedule prevents pivoting if early market tests show zero willingness to pay the hybrid premium.
3.  **Better Way:** A "technology validator" approach. Produce a limited fleet (e.g., 500 units) for internal data and select fleets by 1997 to prove durability *before* committing to mass production tooling. This preserves the innovation lead without the existential risk of a full-scale recall.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** It is 1998. The cars are recalled due to battery cell degradation after 18 months. The brand is associated with "experimental failures," and the stock price tanks due to unexpected warranty reserves. The public schedule made retreat impossible.

**Decision Logic:**
This is a one-way door with high irreversible costs. The proposal lacks a kill criterion for battery life and ignores the absence of market pull. A fixed schedule for unproven science is not a strategy; it is a gamble with the company's balance sheet. The lack of a "cap" on exposure or a "pilot" phase renders the plan fatal as written.

Blockers: Unproven battery durability combined with fixed mass-production schedule; zero market demand signal; high warranty risk with no mitigation.
Risks: Competitor reaction, fuel price volatility.

DECISION: NO-GO
