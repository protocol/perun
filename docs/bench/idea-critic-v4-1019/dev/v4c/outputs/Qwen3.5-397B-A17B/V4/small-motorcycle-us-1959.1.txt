First call: GO. The proposal targets an unaddressed segment (commuters/students) with a product fit for that need, bypassing direct competition with established "large machine" incumbents.

**Steelman:** By ignoring the existing motorcycle market and creating a new category for light, accessible transportation sold through non-traditional channels, the company can achieve volume without battling the "rough image" of current competitors.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary blocker claim is that larger models fail at US highway speeds. However, the proposal explicitly targets *commuters and students* with a *50cc* machine, a class not intended for sustained high-speed highway travel. This objection attacks a product (large bikes) the strategy does not rely on for entry. The risk of brand obscurity is mitigated by the low price point and novelty of the step-through design, which functions as its own marketing. The "do nothing" option guarantees zero US revenue; the "large bike only" alternative risks failure due to the identified mechanical and cultural mismatches. The proposed path is the only one that aligns the product's strengths (small, easy) with a specific, unserved user need.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This moves the north star of global expansion by finding a beachhead rather than forcing a square peg into a round hole. If this fails, it will likely be due to regulatory classification of 50cc vehicles or inability to secure non-motorcycle retail partners, not product-market fit among the target demographic. The cost of delay is high: if a competitor identifies this niche first, the "easy-to-ride" positioning is lost.

**Blockers:** NONE. The highway speed issue applies to the wrong vehicle class for this strategy. The currency risk is a standard cost of entry, not a deal-breaker given the potential market size.
**Risks:** Regulatory hurdles for 50cc; cultural rejection of "scooter-like" aesthetics; supply chain strain. These are managed by the pilot nature of the subsidiary and non-traditional distribution.

**Kill Criteria:** If sales to non-motorcycle shops do not reach 1,000 units within 12 months, or if regulatory bodies ban 50cc usage on major commuter routes, pivot or exit. Leading metric: Units sold per non-motorcycle retail partner per month.

DECISION: GO
