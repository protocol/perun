First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets $1B+ on unproven demand for premium short-form subscriptions amid known fatigue and abundant free alternatives.

**Steelman:** A high-fidelity, mobile-native video platform with exclusive A-list content can carve a profitable niche by solving orientation friction and leveraging studio backing, despite market saturation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Demand Contradiction:** The proposal admits "no proven demand" for premium short-form while noting "abundant" free short video and rising "subscription fatigue." Users already consume short-form for free; asking them to pay a premium for the same format, even with A-list talent, fights established behavior without evidence of willingness to pay.
2.  **Feature Insufficiency:** Automatic vertical/horizontal adaptation is a baseline technical expectation, not a differentiator worth a billion-dollar bet. It does not solve the core value gap.
3.  **Cost Structure:** "High production budgets" for sub-10-minute episodes creates an unsustainable cost-per-minute compared to long-form SVOD or ad-supported models. The unit economics likely fail before launch.
4.  **Alternatives:** The "cheaper alternative" is licensing existing short-form hits or launching an ad-supported tier first to validate demand. Doing nothing avoids burning capital on a hypothesis that contradicts current market signals (fatigue + free abundance).

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal explicitly states "no proven demand" for the core product (premium short-form).
*   **Fact:** "Subscription fatigue" is cited as a rising concern, directly opposing the revenue model.
*   **Contradiction:** High costs are planned for a format where the primary competitor (free UGC) has near-zero marginal cost to the consumer.

**Risks:** Execution difficulty, talent retention, phone attention competition. These are secondary to the fundamental lack of product-market fit evidence.

**Conclusion:** The plan proceeds despite admitting its central premise (demand) is unproven and facing headwinds (fatigue). A cheaper, staged approach (pilot, ad-support) is required before committing $1B. The current proposal fails the "beat do nothing" test because the stated facts suggest a high probability of immediate failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
