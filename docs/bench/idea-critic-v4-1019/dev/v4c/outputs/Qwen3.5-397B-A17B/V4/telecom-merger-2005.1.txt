First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $36 billion to acquire a network explicitly described as incompatible and data-limited, requiring the buyer to run legacy equipment while absorbing massive goodwill risk without a stated plan to resolve the technical clash.

**Steelman:** The merger is justified solely by acquiring the target's high-value business customers who rely on a specific push-to-talk service, assuming these revenues outweigh the integration nightmare of merging two incompatible networks.

**Attack:**
1.  **Technical Blocker:** The core asset (the network) is incompatible with the buyer's infrastructure and "limited for data." In 2005, data growth is the primary industry trajectory. Acquiring a legacy, non-data network forces the buyer to sustain duplicate, dying infrastructure indefinitely or face immediate customer churn if the service is migrated poorly.
2.  **Financial Risk:** The $36 billion price tag likely includes immense goodwill based on current earnings from a technologically obsolete platform. If the push-to-talk service cannot be seamlessly migrated to the buyer's modern network, the revenue base evaporates, leaving only the liability of maintaining a separate legacy grid.
3.  **Missing Alternative:** The proposal lacks a "do nothing" or "partner" comparison. Could the buyer license the push-to-talk technology or build a compatible feature on their own data-capable network for a fraction of $36 billion? Paying a premium for a "walled garden" that contradicts the industry's move toward unified data/voice standards is strategically unsound.
4.  **Integration Cost:** Running two incompatible networks doubles operational complexity and capex. The proposal admits this cost but offers no evidence that the target's customer base generates enough margin to service the debt of the acquisition *plus* the cost of dual-network maintenance.

**Blockers:**
*   **Technological Obsolescence:** The target's network is "limited for data," contradicting the 2005 market direction.
*   **Incompatibility:** No plan exists to merge the technologies; the buyer must run legacy equipment, creating permanent inefficiency.
*   **Valuation Mismatch:** The $36 billion valuation assumes perpetual viability of a legacy tech stack that the buyer themselves deem incompatible.

**Risks:** Massive customer churn during migration, inability to monetize the acquired base on modern data plans, and debt overload from goodwill impairment.

DECISION: NO-GO
