First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a fixed public schedule and novel, unproven core technologies (power-split transmission, NiMH batteries) without a demand signal, proven durability, or a regulatory mandate, while fuel is cheap. This creates massive warranty and reputation risk for a product the market has not asked for.

**Steelman:** A bold, fixed-schedule launch of a novel hybrid by 1997 will secure first-mover dominance and define the segment before competitors react.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Evidence Contradiction):** The proposal admits battery durability is "unproven" and fuel is "cheap." Committing to mass production and a public deadline before solving the durability gap guarantees catastrophic warranty costs and potential recalls. The math of low fuel prices versus high hybrid premiums yields no near-term ROI.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital until regulators force the issue or fuel prices rise. A "technology probe" (limited fleet testing) validates durability without the risk of a mass-market launch. The proposed "all-in" approach beats neither alternative on risk-adjusted return.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because the battery pack degrades faster than expected in real-world cycles, leading to a flood of warranty claims that erodes brand trust and bankrupts the program before volume scales.
4.  **Fit:** Without a regulatory mandate or customer demand, this does not move the north star of profitable volume sales; it is a speculative bet masquerading as strategy.

**Blockers:**
*   Unproven battery durability combined with a fixed mass-production schedule creates an unacceptable, quantifiable liability exposure.
*   No demand signal and cheap fuel contradict the economic viability of the premium-priced vehicle.

**Risks:**
*   Competitor fast-follow once the path is cleared.
*   Technology obsolescence if a better battery chemistry emerges during development.

**Decision:** The absence of a pilot, durability data, or market pull makes this a reckless gamble. A sound operator would cap the exposure: fund a durability pilot and a limited release, not a full corporate commitment. As proposed, it fails the blocker test.

DECISION: NO-GO
