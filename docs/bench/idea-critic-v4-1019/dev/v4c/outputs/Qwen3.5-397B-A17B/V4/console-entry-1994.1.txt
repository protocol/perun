First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a hardware manufacturer with zero platform experience to enter a duopoly dominated by incumbents with loyal users, iconic IP, and imminent competing 3D launches, while relying on unproven third-party developer support in a low-margin hardware model.

**Steelman:** A CD-based console leveraging cheaper media and accessible tools can disrupt the cartridge-dominated market if developer enthusiasm outweighs the lack of first-party franchises.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Strategy:** The company lacks the core asset of this industry: must-have software. Incumbents win via exclusive titles, not just hardware specs. Without a slate of guaranteed hits, the "easier tools" argument is insufficient to lure developers away from established user bases.
2.  **Timing & Competition:** Launching simultaneously with an incumbent's own 3D console creates a direct feature war against a brand with decades of gaming trust. Being the "also-ran" in a hardware spec race is a fatal position for a newcomer.
3.  **Economics:** Selling hardware at a loss requires massive capital reserves and confidence in software recoupment. Without a proven track record of platform management, the financial risk of subsidizing hardware for uncertain software returns is extreme.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The most likely failure mode is a "ghost town" ecosystem: hardware sells poorly due to lack of games; developers refuse to port due to low install base; the platform collapses under its own weight before reaching critical mass.

**Blockers:**
*   **No First-Party IP:** The proposal admits no experience as a platform holder and relies entirely on outside developers. In a console war, reliance on third parties without a strong first-party anchor is a structural failure point.
*   **Competitive Timing:** An incumbent launching a similar 3D system concurrently neutralizes the "first mover" advantage of 3D graphics, leaving the newcomer with inferior brand equity.

**Risks:** Thin margins, developer apathy, brand dilution. These are severe, but the blockers above are fatal as proposed. The plan attempts to solve a content problem with a hardware solution.

**Kill Criteria:** None can be set because the fundamental premise (developers will flock to an unproven platform over incumbents) is unsupported by the stated facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
