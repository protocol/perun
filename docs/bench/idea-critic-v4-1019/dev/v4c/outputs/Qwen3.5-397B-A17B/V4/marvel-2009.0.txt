First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on unproven film capabilities and encumbered assets to solve a demographic gap, creating a high-cost, low-control exposure.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the publisher instantly grants a boy-focused IP library and an in-house film engine, bypassing years of organic development to balance the conglomerate's gender skew.

**Attack:**
1.  **Asset Illusion:** The core value (famous characters) is locked in long-term rival licenses. The buyer pays $4 billion for characters they cannot immediately exploit in toys or media, the primary revenue drivers for this demographic.
2.  **Unproven Engine:** The target's film division has one hit and a short slate. Paying a premium for a "studio" that lacks a track record of sustained self-financed success is speculative. The conglomerate is buying a hypothesis, not a machine.
3.  **Integration Friction:** Creative-control disputes are flagged. Integrating a rebellious comic culture into a family-friendly conglomerate often leads to talent exodus, destroying the very IP value being purchased.
4.  **Cyclical Risk:** Entering the comics-film cycle at a peak (post-hit) with heavy debt/equity mix exposes the parent to severe downside if the next slate falters.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids $4B outlay for encumbered assets.
*   **License Specific Characters:** Negotiate short-term boy-focused IP licenses without buying the whole distressed/encumbered publisher.
*   **Wait:** Let the target prove its film slate over 2-3 years before acquiring.

**Blockers:**
*   **Encumbrance:** The most valuable assets are legally unavailable for immediate monetization by the buyer.
*   **Valuation Mismatch:** The price reflects full control value, but the reality is partial, delayed control.

**Risks:** Creative clash, film slate failure, cyclical downturn.

**Kill Criteria:** If the top 3 revenue-generating characters remain under rival license for >24 months post-close, or if the next two self-financed films fail to break even, initiate a divestiture of the film unit.

The proposal pays for control it does not yet have and capabilities it has not proven.

DECISION: NO-GO
