First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $36 billion to acquire a network explicitly described as incompatible and limited, requiring the buyer to run legacy equipment while assuming massive goodwill risk without a stated integration plan.

Steelman: The merger is justified solely to capture the target's high-value business customers who rely on push-to-talk, accepting the technical debt as the price of entry.

Options:
1. **Merge as proposed:** Highest cost ($36B), highest risk (incompatible tech, legacy burden), low reversibility.
2. **Do nothing:** Zero integration cost, zero tech risk, but forfeits the specific business segment.
3. **Commercial Partnership:** License the push-to-talk service or partner commercially. Lower cost, avoids merging incompatible networks, preserves optionality.

Fit: Assuming the goal is network modernization and scale, acquiring a "limited" and "incompatible" network contradicts the objective. It adds drag rather than capacity.

Pre-mortem: Integration stalls due to fundamental technological incompatibility; the legacy network becomes a money pit draining capital from the core business while goodwill writes down due to failed synergies. This is a one-way door with a high price of delay if competitors solve push-to-talk via software instead of hardware networks.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the technologies are "different, incompatible" and the target network is "limited for data." The $36 billion price tag creates immediate goodwill risk. No evidence is presented that the customer base cannot be served via partnership or that the legacy tech has a viable migration path.

Blockers:
1. **Fatal Technical Mismatch:** The core asset (network) is incompatible with the buyer's and limited in capability, making the $36B valuation difficult to justify against the cost of running dual legacy systems.
2. **Missing Integration Path:** The proposal acknowledges the incompatibility but offers no plan to resolve it, rendering the operational model undefined.

Risks: Goodwill impairment, integration cost overruns, distraction from core network upgrades.

The plan fails the basic operator test: it buys a problem (incompatible legacy network) at a premium price without a solution. A commercial agreement for the push-to-talk service would secure the customers without the toxic technical baggage.

DECISION: NO-GO
