First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and market timing fundamentally contradict the capital structure.

**Steelman:** A premium, niche service for high-value travelers who absolutely require connectivity in remote regions, justifying high hardware and usage costs before global cellular coverage is complete.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Erosion:** The core assumption is a multi-year window where ground networks are absent. However, ground cellular is spreading *fast*. By the time this expensive network scales, the addressable market (international business travelers in non-urban areas) will likely shrink below the threshold needed to service $5 billion in debt.
2.  **Unit Economics Failure:** At "$3,000 per handset" and "several dollars a minute," the product is prohibitively expensive even for the target demographic. High prices severely limit adoption velocity.
3.  **Debt vs. Growth Mismatch:** The proposal admits debt payments require *fast* subscriber growth, yet the high price point, bulky hardware, poor indoor performance, and unready sales channels all act as severe drags on adoption speed. You cannot service high-leverage debt with a slow-rollout luxury product.
4.  **Product Deficits:** Bulky handsets and poor indoor performance are critical flaws for a premium service. Business travelers expect reliability and convenience; this offers neither.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid sinking further capital into a likely insolvent venture.
*   **Pivot to B2B/Government:** Ignore consumers; sell exclusively to governments or NGOs for remote operations where price is no object (but this doesn't match the current "business traveler" pitch).
*   **Delay Launch:** Wait for hardware to improve, but debt payments cannot wait.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-launch, the company defaults. Ground cellular covered 80% of the target routes, the handsets remained too bulky for pockets, and the subscriber base is 10% of the projection needed to cover interest payments. The asset is stranded.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial:** The debt load requires rapid mass adoption that the high price and poor product features physically prevent.
*   **Strategic:** The shrinking window of opportunity due to competing ground networks makes the business case obsolete before launch.

**Risks:** Execution delays, further hardware defects, competitor price wars.

**Conclusion:** The math does not work. The cost of capital demands volume that the product design and pricing explicitly reject.

DECISION: NO-GO
