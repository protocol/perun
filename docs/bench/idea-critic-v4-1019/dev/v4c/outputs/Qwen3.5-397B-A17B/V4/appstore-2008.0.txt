First call: GO. The proposal converts a defensive "web-only" stance into a controlled ecosystem, addressing the primary risk of irrelevance against rivals with app stores.

**Steelman:** A single, curated native app store creates a defensible moat by balancing developer monetization (70/30 split) with strict quality and security control, directly countering carrier concerns and product fragmentation better than open distribution or web apps alone.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing (Web only):** Lowest risk, but cedes the emerging platform war to rivals; likely leads to commodity hardware status.
2.  **Open sideloading:** Maximizes developer speed but invites malware, instability, and carrier backlash; threatens the brand's "polish" differentiator.
3.  **Proposed walled garden:** Captures network effects while retaining gatekeeper control over user experience and security. It beats "do nothing" by enabling native capabilities web apps cannot match.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of a superior, integrated user experience. Web apps cannot access hardware features necessary for "magic" interactions.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the review process becomes a bottleneck, stifling innovation, or if the 30% fee drives key developers to rivals. However, the "polish" concern is mitigated by the review process itself. The cost of delay is catastrophic; waiting a quarter allows rivals to lock in developers and users.

**Evidence:** Rival platforms already have ecosystems; lacking one is an existential threat. The 30% model is standard for retail distribution margins.

**Blockers:** NONE. The concerns (security, carrier objections, workload) are operational hurdles solved by the proposed review mechanism, not facts proving the plan fails. The risk of hurting product polish is the exact reason to have a store, not a reason to skip it.

**Risks:** Review backlog slowing updates; developer revolt over fees. These are managed via scaling the review team and demonstrating sales volume.

**Kill Criteria:** If developer adoption <1,000 apps within 6 months, or if security incidents from approved apps exceed 0.1%, pivot to a relaxed beta channel or revise fee structure.

**Leading Metric:** Number of approved native apps and daily active usage of non-first-party apps. Review date: 6 months post-launch.

DECISION: GO
