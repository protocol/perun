First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $7.5B to a loss-making asset with no permanent CEO, facing active community hostility and credible free alternatives, without a stated mechanism to reverse defection or prove ROI.

Steelman: Acquiring the dominant platform now prevents a competitor from locking in the developer ecosystem, betting that independence preserves value long-term despite short-term losses.

However, the facts provided reveal immediate blockers. First, the target is loss-making with no leadership; paying a premium in stock transfers value from the buyer to a sinking ship without a turnaround plan. Second, the core asset is community trust, which is explicitly eroding ("vocal distrust," "alternatives touted"). Buying a platform users are actively fleeing due to the buyer's history is buying a burning building. Third, antitrust sensitivity and regulatory scrutiny create a non-trivial risk of the deal being blocked or costly to close, wasting resources.

Alternatives: Doing nothing avoids the $7.5B outflow and leadership distraction. Partnering or building internally avoids the toxicity of the specific buyer-target combination. The "cheaper" alternative of waiting for the target's valuation to correct (given the CEO vacuum and losses) is not explored; buying at the peak of friction is financially unsound.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition closes, key developers migrate to the free competitor immediately due to distrust, the platform becomes a ghost town, and the buyer writes down the entire $7.5B. The "independent" operating model fails to assuage fears because the ownership signal is too strong.

Evidence: The proposal admits the target is loss-making, leaderless, and distrusted. No metric is offered for retention post-acquisition, no cap on the spend is defined relative to recovery, and no kill criterion exists for developer churn. The stated facts alone contradict the claim that this is a sound investment today.

Blockers: (1) No permanent CEO to execute integration or stabilization. (2) Active community defection to free alternatives with no retention strategy. (3) $7.5B price tag for a loss-making entity without proven turnaround logic.

Risks: Regulatory block, total brand contamination, stock value dilution.

DECISION: NO-GO
