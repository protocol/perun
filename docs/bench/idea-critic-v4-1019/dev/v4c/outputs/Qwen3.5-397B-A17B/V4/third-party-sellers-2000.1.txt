First call: GO. The proposal trades short-term margin erosion for long-term liquidity and selection density, a necessary pivot for an unprofitable retailer facing inventory limits.

Steelman: Transforming from a pure retailer to a hybrid marketplace creates a liquid "long tail" of inventory without capital expenditure, making the site the definitive destination for buyers even if the operator loses specific unit sales to third parties.

Options analysis:
1. Do nothing: Retain full margin control but cap growth at inventory capital limits; likely path to continued unprofitability.
2. Exclusive partnerships: Lower risk but fails to solve the "long tail" availability problem or maximize traffic utility.
3. Hybrid marketplace (Proposal): Accepts cannibalization of low-margin commodity units to capture fees on transactions the operator could not fulfill, while increasing page relevance and customer retention.

Fit: Aligns with the survival imperative of an unprofitable entity; shifts the model from inventory arbitrage to platform utility.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if third-party fraud or poor shipping destroys brand trust faster than selection builds it. This is a two-way door; bad actors can be removed, but missing selection drives users away permanently. The cost of delay is the continued burn of capital on slow-moving inventory while competitors capture the "out of stock" demand.

Evidence: The proposal admits risks (cannibalization, quality control) but identifies the fee-per-sale model as the revenue engine. The math holds: a fee on a sale you wouldn't have made is pure profit contribution. The risk of supplier objection is real but manageable via contractual segmentation (new vs. used).

Blockers: NONE. The stated risks are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. Cannibalization is a feature, not a bug: if a third party sells cheaper, the operator saves the cost of goods sold and logistics, earning a fee instead of a thin margin.

Risks: Brand dilution from bad sellers; supplier retaliation. These are mitigated by strict performance metrics and clear distinction between "sold by operator" and "sold by third party."

Kill criteria: If third-party defect rates exceed 2% within six months, suspend new seller onboarding. Leading metric: Percentage of total page views resulting in a third-party transaction.

DECISION: GO
