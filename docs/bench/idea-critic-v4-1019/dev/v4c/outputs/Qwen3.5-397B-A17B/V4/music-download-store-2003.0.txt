First call: GO. The company leverages its dominant hardware position to solve the industry's existential threat (piracy) with a legal, convenient alternative, forcing labels to cooperate out of necessity.

**Steelman:** A closed ecosystem selling cheap, protected singles converts pirates to payers by offering unmatched ease-of-use on the best-selling player, securing label buy-in through volume rather than high margins.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Skeptic:** Risks are real: low margins, platform lock-in, and label volatility. However, "free" has high friction (viruses, search time); 99 cents is a low enough barrier to tip the scale. Subscription models in 2003 suffer from poor UX and limited catalogs; owning the song appeals to collectors.
*   **Better-way:** "Do nothing" cedes the market to pirates, eventually killing hardware sales. Subscriptions are the alternative, but infrastructure and licensing for unlimited streaming are not yet viable at scale. Per-song sales are the only immediate bridge.
*   **Fit:** This aligns with the north star of selling more portable players. The store is a loss leader or low-margin utility to drive high-margin hardware adoption.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if labels withhold key content or if the copy protection alienates users. Yet, labels have no better option; without this store, their revenue hits zero from file-sharers.
*   **Evidence:** The proposal rests on the verified fact that the player is already selling well. The installed base provides the distribution channel competitors lack.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (margin, competition, platform limits) are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The margin is sufficient if it drives hardware volume. Platform exclusivity is a feature, not a bug, for a hardware vendor. Rival subscriptions are unproven and complex.

**Risks:** Label negotiations stall; copy protection frustrates users; rivals pivot successfully to subscriptions later. These are managed by moving fast to secure catalogs and using the hardware lock-in to create a seamless experience that rivals cannot match immediately.

**Kill Criteria:** If major labels refuse licensing within 6 months, or if attachment rate (songs per user) remains below 1.0 after one year, pivot to negotiation leverage or alternative models. Leading metric: Weekly song sales per active device.

DECISION: GO
