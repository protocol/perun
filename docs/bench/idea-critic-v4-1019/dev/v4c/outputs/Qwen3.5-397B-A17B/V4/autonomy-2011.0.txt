First call: NO-GO. The stated facts present a clear financial contradiction: an 11x revenue multiple for a target with questioned accounting quality and a buyer lacking software integration experience creates an unacceptable risk of capital destruction.

Steelman: The acquisition is the only viable path to instantly pivot a hardware giant into enterprise analytics, bypassing years of organic R&D failure, provided the target's data is legitimate.

**Blockers:**
1. **Valuation vs. Evidence:** Paying a 64% premium (11x revenue) while the target's financial reporting has been publicly questioned since 2009 violates basic fiduciary duty. The finance chief's recorded reservations confirm internal consensus that the numbers do not support the price.
2. **Capability Gap:** The buyer has zero software experience. Integrating a complex analytics platform without prior domain expertise typically leads to value erosion, not synergy, especially at this price point.
3. **Due Diligence Timeline:** Attempting to verify potentially fraudulent accounts and complex software IP in "a few weeks" is operationally impossible. This rush suggests pressure to close rather than prudence to verify.

**Risks:**
Massive goodwill impairment if accounting irregularities are confirmed post-close; cultural clash between hardware and software engineering teams; inability to monetize the technology without existing sales channels.

**Pre-mortem:**
The deal closes in Q4 2011. By 2013, an accounting scandal forces a multi-billion dollar write-down of the acquired assets. The hardware division remains stagnant, distracted by the integration failure, and the stock price collapses to pre-announcement levels.

**Decision Logic:**
A "GO" decision requires verified evidence that the target's financials are sound and a concrete plan for software integration. Neither exists in the proposal; instead, the proposal explicitly admits these are unknowns ("questioned," "reservations," "lack of experience"). A cheaper, safer alternative is to pause, conduct a thorough forensic audit over several months, and pursue smaller, tuck-in acquisitions to build software competency organically before attempting a transformative buy. The current plan bets the company on unverified numbers and unproven capabilities.

DECISION: NO-GO
