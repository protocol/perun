First call: NO-GO. The core value proposition (reliable handwriting recognition) is explicitly stated as unproven and unreliable in testing, while the price point is high for an unproven category.

**Steelman:** Launching a premium ($700) pen-based PDA now will define a new market category through executive-led publicity, overcoming early technical limitations via brand momentum.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Core Function Failure):** The primary input mechanism (handwriting recognition) is admitted "unreliable in testing." For a device marketed on this specific capability, shipping a broken core function is a fatal flaw, not a risk. Users cannot adopt a "personal digital assistant" that cannot reliably assist with personal data entry.
2.  **Blocker (Value Mismatch):** At $700 (a significant sum in 1993), the device competes with established, reliable tools. The proposal offers a high-cost, bulky ("too big for a pocket"), software-poor device with a broken primary feature. No amount of publicity compensates for a product that fails its main job immediately upon purchase.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A "cheaper alternative" would be to license the technology to a lower-risk partner or delay until recognition accuracy meets a minimum viable threshold. The current plan bets the brand on a malfunctioning prototype.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches with great fanfare. Early adopters buy it based on the CEO's hype. They immediately encounter unusable handwriting recognition. Reviews trash the product as a gimmick. The category is branded as "failed" for years, killing future attempts. The company loses millions in inventory and reputation.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the technology is unreliable, the size is impractical, the software ecosystem is empty, and the category is unproven. These are not external risks; they are internal facts of the current build.

**Blockers:**
*   Core feature (handwriting recognition) is unreliable.
*   Price ($700) is unjustified for a broken, bulky, software-less device.

**Risks:** Market rejection, brand damage, inventory loss. These are consequences of the blockers, not independent variables.

No amount of "kill criteria" or "pilots" can fix a product launched with a known, central defect at a premium price. The plan fails on its own stated facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
