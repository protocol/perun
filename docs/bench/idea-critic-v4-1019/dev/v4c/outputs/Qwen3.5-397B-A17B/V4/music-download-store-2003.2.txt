First call: GO. The company leverages its dominant hardware footprint to solve the industry's existential threat: legal availability.

**Steelman:** A vertically integrated ecosystem where seamless, legal music acquisition drives hardware sales, creating a defensible moat against piracy and subscription rivals.

**Blockers:** NONE. The cited risks are execution hurdles, not fatal flaws.
*   *Free sharing:* Irrelevant if the legal alternative offers superior convenience and integration.
*   *Label licenses:* High difficulty, but essential; failure here kills the concept, yet the proposal assumes acquisition.
*   *Margins:* Acceptable if the primary revenue driver remains hardware units sold, not song markup.
*   *Platform lock-in:* Initially a feature, not a bug, to secure the install base before expanding.
*   *Subscription rivals:* Unproven in 2003; ownership model aligns better with current consumer psychology.

**Risks:**
*   Labels demanding unsustainable royalties.
*   Slow adoption due to price sensitivity vs. free.
*   Technical friction in copy protection alienating users.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   If major labels refuse licensing within 6 months, pivot to independent labels or delay.
*   If attach rate (songs per device) is <0.5 after 12 months, re-evaluate pricing or model.
*   Leading Metric: Weekly song sales volume relative to active device base.

**Pre-mortem:** The store launches, but labels restrict content or raise prices, making the catalog sparse compared to peer-to-peer networks. Users return to piracy.
**Mitigation:** Secure exclusive launch windows or promotional bundles with hardware to force initial trial.

**Better Way Analysis:**
*   *Do nothing:* Hardware value erodes as piracy makes content access chaotic; rivals may capture the ecosystem layer.
*   *Subscription only:* Premature for 2003 bandwidth and consumer trust; high churn risk.
*   *Cheaper alternative:* Bundle limited songs with hardware, but this lacks the recurring engagement of a store.

The proposal correctly identifies that controlling the distribution channel is more valuable than immediate software profit. It transforms a commodity hardware maker into a platform owner. The risks are manageable through negotiation and iterative product refinement. The "walled garden" approach is the only viable path to convince labels to participate.

DECISION: GO
