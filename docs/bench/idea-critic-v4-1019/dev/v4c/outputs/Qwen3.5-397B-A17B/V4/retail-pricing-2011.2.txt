First call: NO-GO. The plan removes the primary purchase trigger for the existing customer base without evidence they will adapt, while betting the entire chain on an unproven format in a single step.

**Steelman:** Eliminating coupons clarifies value and rebuilding stores as branded shops creates a modern destination, mirroring the CEO's past electronics success to stop sales erosion.

**Attack:**
1.  **Customer Mismatch:** The proposal admits sales historically come from discounted items and customers are "coupon-driven." The electronics CEO's prior success likely relied on a different demographic (tech early adopters vs. value-seeking department store shoppers). Applying a niche luxury/discount hybrid model to a mass-market, price-sensitive base ignores established behavior.
2.  **Execution Risk:** Changing pricing *and* physical layout simultaneously across the entire chain creates a single point of failure. If the new pricing alienates customers, the costly store rebuilds become sunk costs with no fallback.
3.  **No Pilot:** Announcing without a test removes the ability to measure elasticity or layout efficacy before committing capital. A "fail fast" approach is impossible; this is "fail everything."

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Continue sales erosion but preserve cash and existing traffic.
*   *Pilot:* Test everyday pricing in 5-10 stores while keeping promotions elsewhere. This isolates pricing risk from real estate risk.
*   *Hybrid:* Reduce coupon frequency gradually while introducing branded shops in high-traffic locations only.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, traffic collapses as loyalists leave for competitors offering coupons. New customers do not arrive in sufficient numbers to offset the loss. The company faces a liquidity crisis due to unrecoverable renovation costs and inability to revert to the old model quickly.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Most sales historically come from discounts; customers are trained to wait.
*   **Contradiction:** The plan assumes these customers will accept higher effective prices immediately without a transition period or proof of concept.
*   **Missing Evidence:** No data suggests the department store demographic overlaps with the CEO's previous electronics customers.

**Risks:** Total brand alienation, cash flow collapse from renovation spend, inability to pivot.

**Kill Criteria:** Not applicable as the plan should not start. A valid plan would require a pilot showing <10% traffic drop and >5% margin increase over 90 days before chain-wide rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
