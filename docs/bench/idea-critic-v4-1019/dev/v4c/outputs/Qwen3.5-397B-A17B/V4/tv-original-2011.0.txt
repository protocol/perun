First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits a large share of the budget to an unproven format (straight-to-series, two seasons) without the industry-standard de-risking mechanism (a pilot), relying solely on internal data that has never predicted success for original prestige drama.

Steelman: The service's granular viewing data uniquely identifies a latent demand for this specific genre and talent combination, rendering the traditional pilot obsolete and justifying a massive upfront commitment to secure exclusive content as licensing costs rise.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Continue relying on licensed content while costs rise, risking long-term margin erosion.
2. Traditional approach: Commission a pilot, evaluate performance, then order a season. Lower risk, slower execution, potential loss of attached talent.
3. Proposed approach: Two-season straight-to-series order. Highest risk, highest potential reward, immediate differentiation.

Fit: Aligns with the need to reduce reliance on rising licensing costs and establish a unique brand identity, but assumes the data translates to original content appeal without evidence.

Pre-mortem: The series launches to critical indifference and low subscriber retention. The "all-at-once" model fails to sustain engagement over time. The massive sunk cost of two seasons prevents course correction, crippling the budget for other initiatives. The data misinterpreted niche viewing habits as broad appeal.

Evidence: The proposal cites "viewing data" but offers no specific correlation between past viewing of licensed content and willingness to subscribe for original content. The assumption that film talent guarantees streaming success is unverified. The "all-at-once" release has no precedent for a scripted series of this scale.

Blockers:
- The commitment of two full seasons (26 episodes) before validating audience interest with a single episode removes all financial flexibility. If the first few episodes underperform, the company is locked into paying for the remainder.
- No pilot or limited initial order exists to test the core hypothesis that data-driven insights can successfully greenlight original prestige drama.

Risks: Audience rejection of the "binge" model; data misalignment; talent not translating to streaming draw; budget exhaustion.

Kill criteria: If a single-season order or pilot was feasible, this two-season commitment is unjustifiable. The lack of a phased approach is a fatal flaw given the unprecedented nature of the venture.

DECISION: NO-GO
