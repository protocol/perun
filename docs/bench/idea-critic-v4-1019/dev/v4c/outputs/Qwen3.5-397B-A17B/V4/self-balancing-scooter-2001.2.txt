First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to massive fixed costs (40k unit factory) and a high price point before resolving fundamental legal and market-fit uncertainties.

**Steelman:** Launching a premium, capacity-constrained transporter now captures early adopter revenue and establishes brand dominance before regulations solidify.

**Attack:**
1.  **Legal Blocker:** The proposal admits legal status on sidewalks/roads is "unclear." Selling 40,000 units of a vehicle that may be immediately banned in major cities is a catastrophic inventory risk. You cannot size a factory for a product that might be illegal to operate where customers live.
2.  **Demand Mismatch:** At $5,000 (2001 dollars), the market is restricted to enthusiasts or institutions. The proposal admits non-institutional use is "unproven" and riders may feel "conspicuous." A 40,000-unit factory assumes mass appeal that the stated risks explicitly contradict.
3.  **Irreversibility:** Building a factory is a one-way door. If the legal ban hits or the "conspicuous" factor kills adoption, the capital is stranded.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for legal clarity; avoid capital exposure.
*   **Pilot:** Import/build a small batch (e.g., 500 units) to test legal reception and actual usage patterns before committing to tooling.
*   **Institutional First:** Target only verified legal environments (airports, campuses) to prove utility without consumer regulatory risk.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-launch, cities ban the device from sidewalks. The company holds 35,000 unsold units in a rented warehouse, burning cash on storage while investors flee. The high price prevented a pivot to a cheaper, compliant model because the factory was tooled for the premium version.

**Blockers:**
*   **Legal Uncertainty:** The core right-to-sell is unverified. A product requiring legal permission to operate cannot be mass-produced without it.
*   **Capacity vs. Proof:** Scaling to 40,000 units contradicts the "unproven" demand status.

**Risks:** Social stigma, limited utility, high price elasticity. These are manageable if the legal blocker is cleared, but the current plan ignores the blocker.

The plan fails the basic operator test: do not build the dam before verifying the river's legal right to flow. The factory commitment is premature given the admitted legal and market unknowns.

DECISION: NO-GO
