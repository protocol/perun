First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a tiny installed base, an unproven legal music supply, and a premium price in a market where cheaper devices have already failed to gain traction.

Steelman: A tightly integrated, high-capacity device could create a unique user experience that justifies a premium, potentially driving computer sales and creating a new ecosystem lock-in before competitors can react.

However, the blocker test fails. The stated facts present a fatal contradiction: the device requires legal downloads to function as proposed, yet the music industry has not licensed them at scale. Without a legal source of music, the 5 GB capacity is useless to the mass market, and the "walled garden" is empty. Furthermore, pricing a niche product 2-3x higher than existing failures, while restricting it to a small computer user base, ignores the basic economics of adoption. The "cheaper alternative" of waiting for the market to mature or licensing deals to close is superior because it removes the supply-chain and content blockers.

The pre-mortem is clear: the device launches with no easy way to load music legally, consumers reject the $400 price point for a peripheral that works with only a fraction of their potential library, and the inventory becomes obsolete within months as flash memory prices drop and competitors secure content deals. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of launching a broken ecosystem.

Blockers:
1. No legal music supply chain exists to utilize the 5 GB capacity effectively.
2. The addressable market (own computer owners) is too small to sustain the high unit cost and R&D investment.
3. Premium pricing contradicts the historical failure of cheaper alternatives in the same category.

Risks: Component sourcing delays, brand dilution if the product fails, opportunity cost of diverting engineering resources.

This is not a risk to be managed with kill criteria; it is a fundamental flaw in the business premise based on current facts. The plan cannot succeed as proposed because the core value proposition (easy access to lots of music) is impossible to deliver today.

DECISION: NO-GO
