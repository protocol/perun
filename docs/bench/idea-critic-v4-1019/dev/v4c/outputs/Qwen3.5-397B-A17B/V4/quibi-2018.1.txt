First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets $1B+ on unproven demand for premium short-form subscriptions amidst known fatigue and abundant free alternatives.

**Steelman:** A high-fidelity, mobile-native platform with exclusive A-list content solves the "vertical video" quality gap, creating a unique premium tier that justifies a subscription despite market fatigue.

**Attack:**
1.  **Demand Contradiction:** The proposal admits "no proven demand" for *premium* short-form while noting free short-form is "abundant." Charging for what is currently free and ubiquitous, without evidence of willingness to pay, violates basic unit economics.
2.  **Feature vs. Value:** "Video looks right vertically" is a baseline technical expectation in 2018, not a differentiated value proposition worth a subscription fee or $1B investment. Competitors already support both orientations.
3.  **Subscription Fatigue:** Launching a new siloed subscription service directly conflicts with the stated risk of "subscription fatigue." Users are consolidating, not expanding portfolios.
4.  **Cost Structure:** "High production budgets" for "under 10 minute" episodes creates an unsustainable cost-per-minute compared to long-form or user-generated content, requiring impossible conversion rates to break even.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids sinking $1B into a speculative market with negative indicators.
*   **Licensing Model:** License content to existing platforms (YouTube, Instagram) to test willingness-to-pay without infrastructure capex.
*   **AVOD First:** Launch free ad-supported to build audience data before attempting monetization.

**Pre-mortem:** The service launches, gains initial traction via marketing, but churns immediately as users realize the content library is too small to justify another monthly bill. The high burn rate from A-list talent contracts depletes capital before a pivot is possible.

**Blockers:**
*   **Core Claim Failure:** The central premise (users will pay for premium short-form) is explicitly admitted as unproven while counter-evidence (free abundance, fatigue) is confirmed.
*   **Unit Economics:** High cost production + low engagement duration + high churn risk = negative LTV/CAC ratio.

**Risks:** Execution difficulty, competition for attention. These are secondary to the fundamental lack of product-market fit evidence.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot cannot demonstrate >5% conversion from free to paid within 3 months at a CAC under $20, the model fails.

The absence of validated demand for the specific monetization strategy, combined with prohibitive costs and market headwinds, constitutes a fatal blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
