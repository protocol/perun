First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a high-end hardware segment that contradicts the stated problem of "many new home users" who typically possess lower-specification machines.

Steelman: A specialized, low-friction interface could successfully onboard novices if hardware constraints were not a barrier.

However, the facts provided reveal immediate blockers. The proposal requires a "high-end PC," yet the primary risk explicitly states this "needs more memory than most home PCs have." Since the target demographic is "many new home users," and most lack the requisite hardware, the product cannot reach its intended market. This is a fundamental distribution failure, not a mere execution risk.

Furthermore, the "better-way" analysis favors inaction. A "major new operating-system release with a new interface is coming within months." Releasing a $100 stopgap product immediately before a native, likely integrated OS update renders the product obsolete upon arrival. The cost of developing, marketing, and supporting a cartoon-house shell for a shrinking window of relevance outweighs the benefit, especially given the risk that "experienced users may find it patronizing," limiting household adoption to a single user profile.

The absence of "third-party add-ons" further stifles longevity. Without an ecosystem, the "rooms" become dead ends rather than a growing environment. The combination of hardware incompatibility with the mass market, imminent obsolescence by a native OS update, and lack of ecosystem support creates a scenario where the product fails to achieve scale or retention.

Risks regarding memory are not hypothetical; they are stated as current facts ("needs more... than most... have"). Risks regarding the upcoming OS release are also stated facts ("coming within months"). These are not uncertainties to be managed with kill criteria; they are present-tense disqualifiers. A product that most customers cannot run and that will be undercut by the platform holder in months cannot succeed.

Blockers:
1. Hardware requirement exceeds the capacity of the target market's majority.
2. Imminent native OS release makes the standalone product obsolete quickly.

Risks:
1. Patronizing tone alienating secondary household users.
2. Lack of third-party ecosystem limiting utility.

DECISION: NO-GO
