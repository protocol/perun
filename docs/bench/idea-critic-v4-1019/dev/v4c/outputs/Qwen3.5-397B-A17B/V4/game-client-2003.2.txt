First call: GO. The current distribution model fails at its core functions: delivering updates and preventing cheating. The proposed client directly solves these existential threats while creating a new revenue channel.

Steelman: A mandatory client is the only viable mechanism to ensure game integrity and timely patches in a broadband-emerging era, transforming a distribution liability into a proprietary platform.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing: Cheating escalates, ruining game balance and driving away the core user base; update friction remains high.
2. Patcher-only: Fails to address real-time anti-cheat needs or create a persistent storefront ecosystem.
3. Retail-only updates: Logistically impossible for frequent patches; too slow to react to exploits.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of maintaining game quality and player retention. While retail friction is a risk, the long-term value shifts to digital ownership and service.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if early client bugs are severe enough to prevent gameplay entirely, causing a retail revolt before the digital habit forms. However, this is an execution risk, not a strategic flaw. The cost of delay is immediate loss of control over the game environment to cheaters.

Evidence: The proposal states the current struggle with updates and cheating. No evidence suggests players prefer broken, unpatched games over a mandatory client. Home broadband adoption is growing, making this a forward-looking bet rather than an immediate universal fit; early adopters will drive the transition.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (dislike of accounts, retail pushback, broadband gaps) are adoption hurdles, not fundamental flaws that make the plan impossible. They are mitigated by the sheer necessity of solving the cheating crisis.

Risks: Retail partner churn, initial technical instability, user friction. These become kill criteria: if retail sales drop >20% in Q1 without digital uptake, or if client uptime <95%, the strategy must pivot.

Leading metric: Percentage of active users running the latest patch version within 48 hours of release. Review date: 6 months post-launch.

DECISION: GO
