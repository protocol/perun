First call: GO. The market gap for an affordable, lightweight roadster exists precisely because incumbents abandoned it for higher-margin segments, creating a low-competition niche for a focused entrant.

**Steelman:** A mid-size manufacturer leverages existing front-engine/rear-drive components to produce a low-cost, two-seat convertible, capturing unmet demand from enthusiasts ignored by mass-market safety and complexity bloat.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Skeptic:** The segment shrinkage is real, but likely due to a lack of supply, not demand. Safety costs are a barrier, but using shared platforms mitigates this. The lack of heritage is irrelevant if the price-to-fun ratio is compelling.
*   **Better-way:** "Do nothing" preserves capital but cedes the niche to future competitors. A heavy, tech-laden sports car fails the "lightweight" mandate. A four-seat conversion dilutes the driving experience and increases cost/weight, missing the core value proposition.
*   **Fit:** Aligns with utilizing existing capacity and parts bin efficiency.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the car becomes too heavy or expensive trying to meet every modern regulation perfectly, pricing it out of the "affordable" bracket.
*   **Evidence:** The proposal states mass-makers view the niche as dead; this is the opportunity signal. No external data contradicts the feasibility of using existing RWD layouts.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks (shrinking segment, safety costs, no heritage) are known industry conditions, not factual contradictions proving the plan fails as proposed. They are execution challenges, not show-stoppers.
*   **Risks:** Sales fading after early adopters; regulatory cost creep; brand perception mismatch.

**Decision Logic:**
No blocker exists. The risks are manageable through strict cost control and positioning the car as a pure driver's tool rather than a luxury grand tourer. The "shrinking segment" is a self-fulfilling prophecy caused by lack of product; introducing a compelling, affordable option validates the latent demand. The plan beats "do nothing" by opening a new revenue stream with high margin potential due to low direct competition.

**Kill Criteria:**
If prototype weight exceeds target by >15% or unit cost prevents a price point 20% below existing competitors, pivot to a limited edition or cancel. Leading metric: Pre-order conversion rate among non-enthusiast general public.

DECISION: GO
