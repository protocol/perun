First call: NO-GO. The brand's economy reputation creates an immediate ceiling on luxury pricing power that a separate nameplate cannot instantly erase, while the capital required for a ground-up flagship and exclusive dealer network exposes the firm to catastrophic loss if the line-up remains thin.

Steelman: A distinct brand identity can decouple from the parent's economy image, allowing a high-quality, under-priced flagship to disrupt European incumbents by offering superior value before expanding the line-up.

However, the risks outweigh the potential. The "separate dealer network" is a massive fixed cost that destroys unit economics when selling only a single model. Luxury buyers prioritize heritage and status over value; being "priced well below" leaders often signals inferiority rather than a bargain in this segment. The proposal relies on a psychological shift (accepting a mass-maker's luxury spin-off) that historically fails without years of brand building or racing pedigree, neither of which are present.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital and focus on dominating the profitable mid-market where the brand is strong.
2. Acquire an existing luxury marque: Instantly gains heritage, dealer networks, and customer trust, avoiding the "economy" stigma.
3. Joint venture: Share development costs and leverage a partner's luxury distribution.

The proposal fails the "fit" test for a company known for economy; it attempts to buy prestige with engineering alone, ignoring that luxury is primarily a marketing and heritage game. The single-model launch is a critical vulnerability; if the flagship misses the mark on refinement (a high bar set by Europeans), the entire brand dies immediately. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of failure.

Blockers:
- Single model cannot support the fixed costs of a new, separate dealer network.
- Brand equity in "economy" actively repels luxury buyers willing to pay full price, making the "value" proposition self-defeating.

Risks:
- Incumbent retaliation on price or features.
- Failure to match European refinement exactly on the first try.

DECISION: NO-GO
