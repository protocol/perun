First call: GO. The proposal trades initial volume for control and margin, directly countering the erosion of the core music business by integrating it into a superior communication device.

**Steelman:** Launching a premium, keyboard-less touchscreen phone via an exclusive carrier partnership allows the company to dictate user experience and capture service revenue, creating a defensible moat against competitors who merely add music features to commoditized handsets.

**Blockers:** NONE. The cited risks are strategic choices, not factual impossibilities.
*   *No phone experience:* Mitigated by owning the OS and hardware stack, leveraging existing music and interface expertise.
*   *Premium price/No keyboard:* Targets a nascent consumer smartphone segment distinct from existing keyboard-led enterprise devices; the value proposition is the full web and music integration, not just calling.
*   *Exclusivity:* A necessary concession to secure carrier subsidies and design control; limits reach initially but ensures brand integrity and revenue share.
*   *No third-party apps:* A deliberate v1 scope to ensure stability and performance; can be added later once the platform is proven.

**Risks:** Market rejection of the form factor; carrier relationship friction; slower adoption due to exclusivity. These are managed by the premium positioning and the "full web" differentiator. The cost of delay is high: every month without this product accelerates the erosion of the music player dominance by phone manufacturers.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-order commitments from the exclusive carrier do not cover initial tooling costs within 90 days of announcement, or if return rates exceed 5% in the first quarter, revisit the pricing or carrier terms. Leading metric: Activation rate of music and web features vs. voice usage in the first 30 days.

The proposal correctly identifies that controlling the entire stack is the only way to deliver the required user experience, justifying the exclusivity and premium price. The risks are inherent to disruption but do not block the path to the stated upside.

DECISION: GO
