First call: GO. The proposal trades a commodity (storage) for a defensible moat (search) in a market where users are conditioned to scarcity, creating a discontinuity that incumbents cannot match without cannibalizing revenue.

Steelman: By offering 1,000x the standard storage and indexing everything, the service eliminates the user behavior of deleting mail, turning email from a transient inbox into a permanent, searchable knowledge base, monetized via high-intent contextual ads.

Alternatives: Doing nothing cedes the data layer to competitors. Matching incumbent storage limits fails to differentiate. A paid model ignores the network effects required for a communication platform. Only the "abundant storage + search" angle breaks the status quo.

Fit: Assumes the strategic goal is data acquisition and ad inventory expansion. If the north star is immediate profit, this fails; if it is long-term dominance via user retention and data richness, this is the optimal path.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if privacy backlash over content scanning triggers regulatory bans or mass user exodus, or if storage costs per user exceed ad yield permanently. However, the invitation-only launch acts as a circuit breaker, allowing cost calibration and PR management before scale. The "mature market" argument is a fallacy; maturity often signals susceptibility to disruption via a step-change in utility.

Evidence: The proposal correctly identifies that users delete mail due to artificial scarcity, not lack of value. Removing scarcity changes the product category. Text ads matched to content have higher CPM than display ads, potentially offsetting the high storage cost of 2004.

Blockers: NONE. The risks are executional (cost control, privacy optics), not fundamental flaws in the logic. The cost of delay is high, as another actor could realize that storage costs are dropping faster than ad values are rising.

Risks: Privacy perception, unit economics if ad fill rates lag. These are managed via the phased rollout and kill criteria (e.g., stop expansion if cost-per-user exceeds ad-revenue-per-user by 20% after 6 months).

DECISION: GO
