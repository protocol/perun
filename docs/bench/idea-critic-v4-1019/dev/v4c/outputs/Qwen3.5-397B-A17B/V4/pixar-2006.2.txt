First call: NO-GO. The price is extreme relative to output, and key risks are unmitigated in the proposal.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio secures a proven hit-making engine and prevents rival alignment, justifying a premium despite the high multiple.

**Attack:**
1.  **Valuation Blocker:** At ~$7.4B for one film/year, the deal implies a valuation far exceeding historical norms for animation studios without a guaranteed pipeline expansion. The proposal offers no earn-out, retention cliff, or performance-based tranche to protect against the "dependence on a few creative leaders" risk. If those leaders leave or underperform, the asset becomes worthless debt.
2.  **Dilution Danger:** Making the studio's founder a major shareholder in the conglomerate creates a powerful internal faction that could block future strategic shifts or demand excessive creative autonomy, exacerbating culture clash.
3.  **Alternative Ignored:** The proposal ignores a cheaper, reversible alternative: a distribution-only partnership or a minority stake with first-look rights. These options secure the content flow without the massive balance sheet risk or integration trauma of a full buyout.
4.  **Strategic Fit:** While the hand-drawn unit is underperforming, replacing it entirely with a single CG studio concentrates risk. A sound operator would diversify, not swap one dependency for another at a record price.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial:** No mechanism (earn-out/retention) ties the $7.4B price to the continued performance of the specific creative leaders identified as a primary risk.
*   **Governance:** Granting major shareholder status to the counterparty's founder without explicit voting caps or governance firewalls invites immediate internal conflict.

**Risks:** Culture clash, integration failure, box-office variance. These are manageable only if the financial structure protects the buyer.

**Conclusion:** The deal as proposed transfers all risk to the buyer for a price that assumes perpetual success. Without structural protections (earn-outs, governance limits) or a cheaper alternative explored, the math does not work.

DECISION: NO-GO
