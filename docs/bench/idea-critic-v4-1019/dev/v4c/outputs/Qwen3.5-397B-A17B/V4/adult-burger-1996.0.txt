First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the brand's core identity and customer value proposition without a mechanism to overcome them.

**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can temporarily override brand perception, forcing adults to trial a premium burger despite the chain's "kids and value" reputation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit:** The strategy clashes with the "child-centered image." Adults do not visit this chain for premium experiences; they visit for convenience and low cost. A premium price point alienates the existing base while failing to attract the target demographic who associate the brand with children's entertainment, not culinary quality.
2.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves margins. A "better way" would be testing premium items in adult-demographic locations first or acquiring a different brand. The proposed "largest budget ever" ignores Chesterton's Fence: the current positioning exists for a reason.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. The campaign burns cash creating awareness, but conversion is near zero because the product-brand mismatch is too strong. Regular customers feel priced out or confused; new adult customers do not trust the quality. The company faces massive fixed costs from the ad spend with no recurring revenue.
4.  **Evidence:** The risks raised in the proposal itself (customers come for kids/value, adults see it as fast/not premium) are treated as hurdles to jump rather than fundamental blockers. No evidence suggests advertising alone can invert decades of brand conditioning.
5.  **Blockers:** The core claim—that adults will buy a premium burger at this specific chain—is contradicted by the stated brand identity. The financial risk (largest budget ever) is uncapped and precedes any demand validation. This is a one-way door with high sunk costs and unproven demand.

**Blockers:**
*   Brand identity (child-centered/value) directly contradicts the product promise (premium/adult).
*   Uncapped, massive spend prior to demand validation violates basic capital allocation logic.

**Risks:**
*   Alienation of core customer base.
*   Permanent brand dilution.

**Decision:** NO-GO. The plan requires validating demand with a small pilot before committing industry-record advertising spend. As proposed, it bets the company on an unproven hypothesis that contradicts known market realities.

DECISION: NO-GO
