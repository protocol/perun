First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores the fundamental contradiction between "cola flavor" and "clear appearance," risking brand confusion and flagship cannibalization without a mitigation plan.

Steelman: A clear cola can capture health-conscious consumers seeking purity without alienating core drinkers, leveraging positive test data to secure first-mover advantage before rivals copy.

Attack:
1.  **Flavor-Color Expectation:** Consumers associate dark color with specific cola taste profiles. A clear liquid signals "citrus" or "water," creating a sensory mismatch that drives rejection after the first trial. The proposal admits shoppers link flavor with color but proceeds anyway.
2.  **Repeat Purchase Gap:** Positive test market responses often reflect novelty, not loyalty. The proposal flags "novelty buyers may not repeat" yet plans a costly nationwide launch rather than a staged rollout to verify retention.
3.  **Cannibalization vs. Extension:** Without distinct positioning beyond "purer," the product will likely steal share from the flagship rather than expand the total category. The marketing campaign amplifies this risk by drawing existing customers to the inferior (perceived) clear option.
4.  **Competitive Response:** A "major" campaign invites immediate imitation by rivals with deeper pockets, turning the innovator into a niche player if the concept fails to stick.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve flagship margins; avoid brand dilution.
*   **Regional Staged Launch:** Limit exposure to verify repeat rates and refine positioning before national spend.
*   **Sub-Brand Launch:** Distinct branding to isolate failure risk and prevent flagship cannibalization.

Pre-mortem: The product launches nationally, initial sales spike due to curiosity, then crash as repeat rates plummet. Retailers delist due to poor velocity. The flagship suffers a permanent sales dip from confused customers. Rivals launch a refined version using our market education, capturing the remaining niche.

Blockers:
*   The proposal acknowledges the core sensory contradiction (flavor vs. color) but offers no formulation or marketing solution to overcome it.
*   It admits high cannibalization and low repeat risks but commits to a high-cost, irreversible national launch instead of a validated step-down approach.

Risks: Novelty fade, competitor copying, brand dilution.

The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test because the identified risks are inherent to the product concept and are exacerbated by the proposed scale. A staged approach is required to validate unit economics before committing to a national campaign.

DECISION: NO-GO
