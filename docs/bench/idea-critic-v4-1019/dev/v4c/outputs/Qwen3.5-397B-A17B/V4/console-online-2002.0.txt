First call: NO-GO. The proposal requires a mandatory annual subscription and broadband for a console launched in 2002, when broadband penetration is low and competitors offer free or publisher-managed online play. The company lacks any prior experience running hosting or support operations, creating a high probability of service failure and reputational damage before the user base can mature.

Steelman: A unified, high-quality paid service with voice chat and friends lists creates a superior, sticky ecosystem that differentiates the console from fragmented, free alternatives, justifying the cost through exclusivity and reliability.

Options:
1. **Do nothing/Free tier:** Match rivals by keeping online play free or publisher-managed. Low risk, no new revenue, maintains status quo.
2. **Hybrid model:** Offer basic matchmaking/free identity for free; charge only for premium features (voice, advanced stats). Reduces barrier to entry while testing willingness to pay.
3. **Proposed (Paid-only):** High revenue potential if adopted, but risks alienating the entire install base if broadband adoption lags or price sensitivity is high.

Fit: Assuming the goal is market share growth in 2002, a mandatory paywall contradicts the reality of limited broadband infrastructure and established free expectations. It fits a "premium quality" vision only if the hardware install base is already large enough to sustain a niche service, which is unproven.

Pre-mortem: The service launches, but fewer than 5% of owners subscribe due to broadband limits and price resistance. Game publishers refuse to support the paid-only ecosystem, launching titles on rival platforms with free online. The company burns cash on underutilized servers and support staff it cannot manage effectively, forcing a shutdown or a humiliating pivot to free within 12 months.

Evidence: The proposal admits "few homes have broadband" and the company has "never run" such operations. These are not risks; they are stated facts that directly contradict the feasibility of a *mandatory* broadband subscription service.

Blockers:
1. **Infrastructure mismatch:** Mandatory broadband requirement excludes the vast majority of the 2002 console market (fact stated in proposal).
2. **Operational incapacity:** The company explicitly lacks the required hosting and support experience (fact stated in proposal).
3. **Market contradiction:** Competitors offer free services; imposing a mandatory fee without a proven user base is a direct path to zero adoption.

Risks: Player backlash, publisher abandonment, financial loss on infrastructure.

DECISION: NO-GO
