First call: GO. The proposal targets an unserved mass market (casual/non-gamers) by trading raw performance for accessibility and price, a valid strategy for a third-place contender unable to win the specs war.

**Steelman:** A third-place console maker can dominate by abandoning the hardcore arms race, offering a uniquely accessible, low-cost motion-controlled experience that expands the total market rather than fighting for existing share.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Continue chasing leaders in HD/power; likely results in continued third-place stagnation or exit.
*   **High-End Pivot:** Attempt to match rivals' specs; high cost, late entry, low probability of overtaking incumbents.
*   **The Proposal:** Risks alienating core gamers and publishers. However, the "toy" perception is a feature for the target demographic, not a bug. The thin hardware margin is acceptable if software attachment rates among casuals prove high. The untested audience is the only path to growth; the tested audience is already lost.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Fits a "blue ocean" strategy for a laggard. Failure scenario: Hardcore rejection bleeds brand prestige, and casuals fail to adopt, leaving no base. This is mitigated by the low price point reducing consumer barrier. The cost of delay is ceding the casual opportunity to mobile or other entrants.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists; the hardware limits are intentional, not accidental. The skepticism is expected, not disqualifying.
*   **Risks:** Third-party support may lag; brand perception may shift. These are execution risks, not fundamental flaws.

**Decision:** The plan survives the attack because the alternative (fighting a specs war as #3) is a guaranteed loss. The risks are manageable via strong first-party software launch titles. The potential upside (market expansion) vastly outweighs the downside (continued niche status).

**Kill Criteria:** If unit sales do not exceed the combined installed base of current rivals within 18 months, or if software attachment rate per unit falls below 2.0, pivot to cost-cutting or exit hardware.

DECISION: GO
