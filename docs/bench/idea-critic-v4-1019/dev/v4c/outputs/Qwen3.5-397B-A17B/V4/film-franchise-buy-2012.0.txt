First call: NO-GO. The $4 billion price tag against a franchise with recent mixed reviews and an exiting creative lead presents an immediate financial and creative mismatch that threatens the core objective of feeding all business units.

Steelman: Acquiring this franchise provides immediate, high-value IP to unify theme parks, products, and film, but only if the new trilogy successfully reignites fan enthusiasm despite the founder's departure.

Alternatives: Doing nothing preserves capital for organic growth or cheaper acquisitions. Licensing specific rights offers lower risk exposure without the $4 billion sunk cost. Developing original IP avoids legacy baggage entirely. The proposed buy must beat "do nothing," yet the risks suggest it may drain resources rather than feed the ecosystem.

Fit: While space-fantasy aligns with a broad entertainment vision, the specific condition of this asset (retiring founder, mixed recent performance) contradicts the need for a stable, long-term revenue engine.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition fails because the new trilogy flops due to lack of the founder's vision, causing brand toxicity that hurts theme park attendance and merchandise sales. The $4 billion becomes a sunk cost with no ROI. This is a one-way door; reversing the purchase is impossible. The cost of delay is minimal compared to the cost of a failed integration.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits mixed recent reviews, the founder's retirement, high price relative to current output, potential fan rejection, and existing licensing constraints. These are not hypothetical risks but stated facts that directly undermine the value proposition.

Blockers:
1. Financial Mismatch: $4 billion price vs. mixed recent performance and existing licensing limits creates a negative expected value based on provided facts.
2. Creative Void: The founder's retirement removes the primary driver of the franchise's past success, with no identified replacement strategy.
3. Brand Risk: High probability of fan rejection could actively damage the company's existing brands rather than feeding them.

Risks: Execution difficulty in producing a hit trilogy; merchandise revenue cannibalization due to existing licenses.

The stated facts confirm the core claim (feeding all businesses) is unsupported; the asset appears degraded and overpriced.

DECISION: NO-GO
