First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores stated risks of alienating loyal customers and lacks validation.

**Steelman:** A silent, immediate global rebrand modernizes the retailer's image by shedding dated visual weight, betting that contemporary aesthetics will outweigh attachment to heritage.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit:** The goal is to look "contemporary," not to erase brand equity. The old square is the primary recognition asset; removing it without testing contradicts the goal of maintaining market presence while updating style.
2.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the design was "not tested with customers." In an era where social media amplifies instant backlash, launching untested changes to a loyal base is operationally reckless.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves equity. A "staged pilot" (testing in select channels) offers the same upside (modern look) with drastically lower risk of systemic backlash. The proposal's "all channels at once" approach fails to beat these safer alternatives.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Loyal customers interpret the silent change as a rejection of their history, triggering a viral boycott. The brand loses its distinct visual anchor (the square) before the new wordmark gains traction.
5.  **Blocker:** The explicit lack of customer testing combined with the high probability of immediate social backlash constitutes a factual blocker. You cannot approve a strategy that explicitly bypasses validation for a high-stakes, low-reversibility action (you cannot un-ring the bell once every channel changes simultaneously).

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** Unvalidated design launched globally; high risk of alienating the core base identified in the proposal itself.
*   **Risks:** Backlash, loss of recognition. (These become kill criteria if a pilot were run, but here they block the full launch).

The proposal demands a "big bang" rollout without evidence that the new design works. This violates basic operational hygiene for brand management. The plan must be revised to include customer testing and a phased rollout before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
