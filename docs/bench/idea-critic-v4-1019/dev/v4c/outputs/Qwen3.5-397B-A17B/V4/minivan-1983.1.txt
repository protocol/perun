First call: GO. The proposal leverages existing assets to solve a specific customer constraint (garage fit) with car-like driving dynamics, addressing a gap between cumbersome vans and small wagons.

**Steelman:** A front-wheel-drive minivan on a compact platform offers the only viable path to a garage-fit family vehicle, utilizing a converted factory to minimize capital expenditure while creating a defensible new category before rivals react.

**Analysis:**
The "no category exists" risk is actually the primary opportunity; current options (station wagons, full-size vans) fail the garage-and-comfort test. The lack of cash is mitigated by reusing the compact-car platform and converting an existing factory, avoiding the cost of a clean-sheet design or new assembly line. While buyers might initially perceive it as commercial, marketing can position it as a "family car with space," a narrative supported by the car-like driving experience. Rival copying is a future problem; being first allows the company to define the segment and capture early adopters. The space limitation of the compact platform is a necessary trade-off for garage compatibility, not a fatal flaw.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue selling wagons and large vans; likely loss of market relevance as families seek better utility without sacrificing drivability.
2.  **Build a new platform:** Prohibitive given the cash constraint; too slow.
3.  **Import/Partner:** Cedes control and margin; fails to utilize the idle factory.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the marketing team positions it as a work van, alienating families, or if the compact platform proves too cramped for three rows of seating. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The "one-way door" aspect is low; if it fails, the factory conversion losses are contained compared to a new plant.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's constraints (cash, platform) are addressed by its own logic (conversion, reuse).
**Risks:** Market education, competitor response. These become kill criteria: if Q1 sales do not show >60% family vs. commercial uptake, pivot marketing or discontinue.

The plan turns scarcity (cash) into focus (reuse) and creates a market where none existed.

DECISION: GO
