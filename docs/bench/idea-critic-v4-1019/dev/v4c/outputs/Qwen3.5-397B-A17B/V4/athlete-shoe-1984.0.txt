First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits a dominant running brand's capital to an unproven rookie in a different category, relying on rule-breaking and high financial concentration without evidence of demand.

**Steelman:** Secure a generational talent before rivals do, using controversy and a unique product line to instantly disrupt the basketball market hierarchy, accepting high upfront risk for monopoly-like brand association.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain status quo; safe but cedes basketball ground to established competitors.
2.  **Veteran Endorsement:** Sign a proven star; lower risk, higher cost, less novelty, likely similar sales ceiling.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Highest risk/reward. Beats "do nothing" only if the player becomes a superstar immediately and the ban generates net-positive publicity.

**Fit:** Assumes the brand can pivot from "running utility" to "basketball culture" instantly. If the north star is market share growth in basketball, this fits only if the player delivers immediate elite performance.

**Pre-mortem:** The player suffers a career-ending injury in game one; the league bans the shoes permanently before launch; the marketing budget is exhausted with zero ROI; the signature line flops as fans reject a rookie over established stars. This is a **one-way door**: once the multi-year contract is signed and production tooled, capital is unrecoverable. Cost of delay: Rivals sign the talent next month.

**Evidence:** All critical claims (player potential, fine impact, sales volume) are **assumed**. No data exists for a player who hasn't played professionally. The financial commitment is a large share of the budget, yet the ROI calculation rests entirely on speculation.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Unverified Core Asset:** The plan's entire value proposition rests on a rookie who has not played a professional game. There is no evidence he can perform at the level required to justify the "far more" than rival offers.
2.  **Regulatory Dependency:** The strategy explicitly relies on breaking league uniform rules. If the league enforces bans strictly (preventing sales) rather than generating buzz, the product cannot be sold as designed.
3.  **Budget Concentration:** Allocating a "large share" of the marketing budget to a single unproven variable violates basic portfolio risk management without a hedging mechanism.

**Risks:** Injury, flopped sales, brand dilution.

**Decision:** The absence of professional performance data constitutes a factual blocker for a deal exceeding all rival offers. You cannot validate the investment thesis.

DECISION: NO-GO
