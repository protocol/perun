First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines a premium price with a smaller app store and carrier exclusivity in a saturated two-platform market, a combination that historically fails to gain traction without a massive ecosystem subsidy or a significantly lower price point.

Steelman: A differentiated hardware experience (3D display, shopping integration) can carve a niche among loyal customers despite the app gap, justifying a flagship price through unique utility rather than app breadth.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws. The "app gap" is a blocker, not a risk: mainstream apps are a baseline requirement for flagship pricing, not a differentiator. Users will not pay flagship prices for a device that lacks daily-utility apps, regardless of 3D features which have historically failed to drive adoption. The "one-touch shopping" feature is insufficient to overcome the friction of a forked OS without Google Mobile Services or equivalent mainstream support. Furthermore, carrier exclusivity limits the addressable market to a single network's subscribers, drastically reducing the volume needed to sustain a proprietary hardware platform. The cost of developing and maintaining a forked OS, combined with marketing a niche feature to a mass market at a premium price, creates a negative unit economics trajectory before launch.

Alternatives: Licensing the shopping feature to existing Android/iOS devices reaches more users at lower cost. A low-cost tablet strategy leverages existing success without entering the hyper-competitive, carrier-dependent smartphone arena. Doing nothing preserves capital for core retail growth.

The pre-mortem shows the device launching to poor reviews citing the app gap, limited carrier reach preventing scale, and rapid price cuts that destroy brand equity. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a failed hardware bet.

Blockers:
1. Flagship price paired with a missing mainstream app ecosystem contradicts market reality.
2. Carrier exclusivity prevents the scale required to sustain a third mobile platform.
3. 3D display is a gimmick without proven mass-market demand, insufficient to offset OS limitations.

Risks: Execution complexity, inventory obsolescence, brand dilution.

DECISION: NO-GO
