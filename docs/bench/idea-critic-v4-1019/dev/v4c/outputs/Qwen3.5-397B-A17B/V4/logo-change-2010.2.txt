First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a high-cost, irreversible change with zero validation of its core premise: that the new design will make the brand appear contemporary to its actual customers rather than just alienating them.

Steelman: A simultaneous, unannounced global rebrand shocks the market into recognizing the brand's modern evolution, bypassing the "committee feel" of a slow rollout.

However, the plan fails the blocker test. The proposal explicitly admits the new design was "not tested with customers" while simultaneously acknowledging risks that "loyal customers are attached to the old logo" and "social media makes backlash instant." In the clothing sector, where brand equity is tied to identity and trust, removing the primary recognition element (the dark blue square) without evidence that the replacement resonates is not a strategy; it is a gamble with the company's primary asset.

The "no advance announcement" tactic amplifies this risk. Without a narrative prepared to explain the change or a base of validated support to absorb the shock, the brand invites a vacuum of interpretation filled by confusion or hostility. The cost of delay (waiting to test) is negligible compared to the cost of a failed rollout, which includes lost sales, reputational damage, and the expensive necessity of reverting.

A cheaper, safer alternative exists: pilot the new wordmark in a single, low-risk channel or geographic market to measure sentiment and sales impact before committing the entire enterprise. The current plan offers no mechanism to detect failure until it is global and irreversible. The absence of a pilot, metric, or fallback is not a minor gap; it is a fundamental flaw in operating logic for a mature retailer. The stated facts confirm the target users' attachment to the old identity, and the plan offers no counter-evidence that the new identity preserves that value.

Blockers: The plan proceeds despite admitted lack of customer testing for a change that relies entirely on customer perception, directly contradicting the goal of improving brand image.
Risks: Immediate social backlash, loss of loyal customers, irreversible brand damage.

DECISION: NO-GO
