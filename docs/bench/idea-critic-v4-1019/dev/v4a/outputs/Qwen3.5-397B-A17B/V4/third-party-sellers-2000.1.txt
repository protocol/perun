First call: NO-GO. An unprofitable retailer inviting competitors to undercut its own core inventory on the same page threatens its only path to margin without a verified mechanism to capture sufficient fee revenue or guarantee service quality.

Steelman: Transforming from a fragile retailer into a high-margin marketplace platform by aggregating all supply, even competitive, to dominate selection and earn fees regardless of who sells the unit.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Protect current margins but limit selection; slow growth.
2. Proposed plan: Maximum selection and fee revenue, but immediate cannibalization of own sales and brand risk.
3. Hybrid (Better Way): Allow third-party sellers only for out-of-stock items or used goods clearly segregated from new offers, protecting core new-book margins while testing the model.

Fit: Assumes the north star is "total selection" over "retail profitability." If the goal is survival via cash flow, undercutting one's own profitable units is misaligned.

Pre-mortem: The initiative fails because major suppliers delist the retailer in retaliation for hosting competitors, or customers blame the retailer for third-party shipping failures, destroying trust. The door is two-way (sellers can be removed), but the reputational damage and supplier relationships are hard to reverse.

Evidence & Blockers:
The proposal admits the retailer is "not yet profitable." The core blocker is the lack of a stated fee structure or margin analysis proving that fees from undercutting competitors exceed the lost gross margin from displaced own-inventory sales. Without this math, the plan assumes volume compensates for margin erosion, which is unproven. Furthermore, there is no stated mechanism to enforce service quality or handle supplier retaliation, treating these as mere "risks" rather than operational prerequisites.

Blockers:
1. No evidence that fee revenue > lost retail margin on cannibalized sales.
2. No operational plan to prevent supplier delisting or manage service failures.

Risks: Brand erosion, supplier retaliation, customer confusion between new/used.

Kill Criteria: If third-party sales cannibalize more than 10% of high-margin own-inventory sales within Q1 without generating net-positive contribution profit, halt expansion.

DECISION: NO-GO
