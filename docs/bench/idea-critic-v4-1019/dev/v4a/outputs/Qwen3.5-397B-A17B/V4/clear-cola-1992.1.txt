First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets the brand's core identity on an unproven category linkage without a containment strategy for cannibalization.

**Steelman:** A clear cola can capture novelty seekers and health-conscious switchers as a distinct sub-brand, growing total volume despite some flagship erosion.

**Attack:** The central failure mode is category dissonance: consumers equate "cola" with dark caramel color. If the product tastes like cola but looks like water, it creates cognitive friction that kills repeat purchases after the initial novelty spike. The proposal admits shoppers link flavor to color yet ignores this psychological barrier. Furthermore, a nationwide launch with a "large marketing campaign" maximizes exposure before proving retention. If repeat rates collapse (as hinted by the "novelty buyers" risk), the company is left with massive sunk marketing costs and a diluted brand image.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve flagship margins; avoid brand confusion.
2.  **Limited Regional Rollout:** Test repeat purchase rates in a confined geography before committing national ad spend.
3.  **Sub-Brand Architecture:** Launch under a entirely new name to prevent flagship cannibalization and isolate failure.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales have plummeted 80% from launch week. Why? Consumers bought once out of curiosity, found the visual mismatch unsettling, and never returned. Meanwhile, flagship sales dipped 5% due to internal confusion. Rivals launched cheaper copies, stealing the remaining novelty segment.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal relies on test market data which it explicitly admits "can overstate demand." There is no verified metric for repeat purchase rates, only "positive response" (likely trial). The lack of a specific kill criterion for repeat usage is a fatal gap.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Unverified Repeat Demand:** No evidence exists that the "positive response" converts to habitual consumption; the proposal admits test markets overstate this.
2.  **Cannibalization Without Upside:** No math proves the new volume exceeds the lost flagship volume.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, high marketing waste, competitor copying.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. You cannot launch nationwide based on trial data when the primary risk is lack of repetition.

DECISION: NO-GO
