First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to sell a premium subscription for a format (short-form video) that is already abundantly available for free, while ignoring the stated market constraint of "subscription fatigue."

**Steelman:** A high-fidelity, mobile-native platform leveraging A-list talent could capture users willing to pay for prestige short-form content if the technical experience (orientation adaptation) is superior to free alternatives.

**Attack:**
1.  **Value Mismatch:** The core value proposition (short-form professional video) directly competes with established, free, ad-supported ecosystems (YouTube, social platforms) and user-generated content. Consumers rarely pay subscriptions for content categories where free substitutes are ubiquitous and high-quality.
2.  **Market Timing:** The proposal explicitly acknowledges "subscription fatigue" as a rising concern in 2018. Launching a new, expensive silo against this headwind requires proof of unique demand, which the proposal admits is "no proven."
3.  **Cost Structure:** Raising over $1 billion for high production budgets and A-list talent creates an immense break-even threshold. Without a proven audience willing to pay, the burn rate is unsustainable compared to lower-cost competitors.
4.  **Feature Insufficiency:** Automatic video orientation is a standard usability expectation, not a differentiated moat capable of driving subscription conversions or justifying a premium price point against free incumbents.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids sinking $1B+ into an unproven category with negative market sentiment.
*   **Licensing Model:** License A-list short-form content to existing platforms (Netflix, Hulu, YouTube Premium) to test demand without building a costly new infrastructure.
*   **Ad-Supported Pivot:** Launch as a free, ad-supported tier to build an audience first, mitigating subscription fatigue, then upsell later.

**Pre-mortem:** The service launches, spends heavily on content, fails to convert users due to price and fatigue, and shuts down within 18 months as investors refuse further capital. The technology feature becomes irrelevant.

**Blockers:**
*   **Demand:** No evidence exists that users will pay for short-form video when free options are abundant.
*   **Economics:** The $1B+ cost structure cannot be supported without proven demand.
*   **Market Sentiment:** Explicit "subscription fatigue" contradicts the subscription-only model.

**Risks:** High competition for attention; inability to secure exclusive talent long-term.

The proposal fails to address the fundamental lack of willingness to pay in a saturated free market.

DECISION: NO-GO
