First call: NO-GO. The proposal repeats the pattern of a recent hardware failure with a device requiring even deeper home integration and trust, without evidence that the specific failures of the phone launch have been resolved.

**Steelman:** A cylindrical speaker at $199 ($99 for members) creates a new "always-listening" category, leveraging membership pricing to overcome privacy skepticism and invitation-only scarcity to drive demand, despite recent hardware struggles.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & History:** The company's last hardware launch failed. Entering a non-existent category with a complex, always-on device immediately after a poor smartphone performance contradicts a prudent recovery strategy. The "invitation only" tactic does not fix fundamental product-market fit or execution risks demonstrated in the phone launch.
2.  **Privacy & Utility:** The primary risks (always-on microphone, poor recognition, no category) are not mitigated; they are the core product definition. In 2014, "always-listening" is a feature, not a proven benefit. Existing phone assistants solve the "questions" and "lists" use cases without requiring new hardware in private spaces.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" avoids compounding a hardware reputation crisis. A cheaper alternative is improving the existing smartphone or app experience, which already reaches customers. The $99 member price suggests a subsidy strategy that increases financial risk following a poor sales quarter.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The device launches, privacy concerns trigger media backlash, voice failure rates frustrate early adopters, and the "cylindrical" form factor proves gimmicky without solving a painful problem better than the phone. Sales stall, inventory piles up, and the brand's hardware credibility is permanently destroyed.

**Blockers:**
*   **Recent Failure:** The immediate precedent of a poorly selling smartphone indicates unresolved execution or market-fit issues in hardware.
*   **Unproven Category:** No existing demand for an always-listening home device; the value proposition is hypothetical against established phone-based alternatives.
*   **Privacy Barrier:** The core mechanism (always-on mic) faces a known, high-magnitude adoption barrier with no stated mitigation beyond pricing.

**Risks:** Voice recognition accuracy, member subsidy sustainability, invitation-only scaling limits.

The proposal asks to double down on hardware risk with a radical, unproven form factor immediately after a failure. The risks are not just "open"; they are fundamental contradictions to the current market reality and the company's recent track record.

DECISION: NO-GO
