First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and market timing fundamentally contradict the capital structure.

Steelman: This is the only viable solution for global connectivity in 1998, capturing high-margin business travelers before ground networks reach remote areas.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test on three verified facts inherent in the text:
1.  **Cost vs. Value:** At $3,000 per handset and several dollars per minute, the service is prohibitively expensive compared to emerging ground alternatives. The "international business traveler" segment is price-sensitive regarding roaming and will adopt cheaper, improving ground solutions rather than carry bulky, indoor-deficient hardware.
2.  **Debt Servicing Mismatch:** The $5 billion debt load requires massive, immediate subscriber growth. The high price point inherently caps the total addressable market, making the volume required to service debt mathematically unreachable before ground networks saturate the remaining gaps.
3.  **Product Deficit:** Bulky handsets that fail indoors render the service unreliable for the core use case (business communication), further suppressing adoption rates needed to justify the borrowings.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Impossibility:** The price point restricts volume; the debt load demands volume. These two facts cannot coexist.
*   **Competitive Obsolescence:** Ground cellular spread is a stated fact, not a risk. The window to monopolize connectivity is already closed or closing faster than the sales channels can react.

**Risks:** (Non-blocking as blockers already decide the call) Execution delays, further technical limitations, channel readiness.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Avoid sinking further capital into a model with negative unit economics relative to debt obligations.
*   *Pivot:* Restructure debt or target government/military contracts exclusively (not proposed here).
*   *Delay:* Wait for handset miniaturization, but debt payments cannot wait.

The proposal asks to launch a premium product with known functional deficits into a shrinking niche, funded by debt that requires mass-market adoption. The math does not work.

DECISION: NO-GO
