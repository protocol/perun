First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a segment explicitly described as "shrunk for years" and deemed a "dead niche" by mass-market experts, while the proposer admits the company lacks relevant heritage and faces high regulatory costs.

Steelman: A lean, affordable roadster could unlock latent demand among enthusiasts ignored by current manufacturers, creating a high-margin halo product despite the niche status.

However, the attack on this claim reveals fatal flaws based solely on the provided facts. The "better-way" analysis shows "do nothing" is superior: entering a shrinking market with no heritage and high compliance costs creates a negative expected value. The risks are not merely potential; they are structural. A two-seat limit caps volume, safety rules inflate the price point (contradicting "affordable"), and the admission that sales may fade after early enthusiasts buy indicates a business model with no long-term tail. There is no evidence of a path to profitability once the initial backlog clears.

The pre-mortem is straightforward: the car launches, early adopters buy, sales collapse as predicted, and the lack of heritage prevents brand loyalty from sustaining the line. The cost of delay is negligible because the market is shrinking, not growing; waiting likely reduces competition further without losing addressable demand.

Blockers:
1. The segment is shrinking and considered dead by experts, contradicting the viability of a new entry.
2. High safety costs conflict directly with the "affordable" positioning.
3. No company heritage exists to justify the premium or sustain interest after early adopters.
4. The proposal itself admits sales may fade quickly, indicating a lack of sustainable demand.

Risks:
1. Limited buyer pool due to two-seat configuration.
2. Potential for rapid brand damage if the car fails to resonate beyond the niche.

This is a classic "one-way door" with high irreversible costs (tooling, certification) and low probability of success given the stated constraints. A cheaper alternative would be to license a design or wait for market signals to change, but even those are weak given the "dead niche" consensus. The facts provided do not support a go decision; they support a no-go.

DECISION: NO-GO
