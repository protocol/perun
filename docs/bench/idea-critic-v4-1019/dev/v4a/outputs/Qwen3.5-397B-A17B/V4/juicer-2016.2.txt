First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and behavioral friction of a $699 connected juicer relying on expensive proprietary consumables in a niche market present immediate, structural failure points that outweigh the potential upside.

**Steelman:** A premium, hassle-free nutrition ecosystem where device subsidies are recouped via high-margin recurring revenue from fresh, perfectly portioned produce, justified by convenience and waste reduction.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** At $699, the device targets early adopters, but the subscription model requires per-bag costs significantly higher than grocery produce. Consumers rarely accept a permanent premium for a commodity (fruit) unless the convenience is absolute. The "tax" per glass likely exceeds $5–$8, creating a tiny addressable market.
2.  **Connectivity:** Mandating Wi-Fi for a mechanical pressing function introduces unnecessary failure modes (network outages, setup friction) without a compelling security or functional payoff that justifies the risk of a "brick" device.
3.  **Market Size:** The intersection of users willing to pay $699 upfront, commit to a subscription, and accept limited produce variety is vanishingly small. Historical data on single-function kitchen appliances suggests rapid churn once the novelty fades.
4.  **Alternatives:** A non-connected device with a lower upfront cost and open compatibility (or cheaper pods) captures more volume. "Do nothing" allows the market to validate demand for pod-based juicing before engineering a custom, locked-in ecosystem.

**Pre-mortem:** The company burns cash on inventory and customer acquisition, only to face high churn as users realize the per-serve cost is unsustainable. The app becomes a liability when servers lag or shut down, rendering hardware useless. Retailers refuse to stock a $699 niche item.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unit Economics:** The proposed price point creates a barrier to entry too high for the unproven subscription habit.
*   **Feature Bloat:** Mandatory connectivity adds cost and risk without solving a core user pain point (pressing fruit).
*   **Market Fit:** No evidence suggests a mass market exists for this specific combination of high CAPEX and high OPEX.

**Risks:** Supply chain complexity for fresh bags; consumer fatigue with subscriptions; rapid competitive imitation with open systems.

DECISION: NO-GO
