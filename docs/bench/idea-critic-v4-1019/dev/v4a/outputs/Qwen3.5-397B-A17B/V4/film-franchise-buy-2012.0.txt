First call: NO-GO. The $4 billion price tag against a franchise with recent mixed reviews and an exiting creative lead presents an immediate financial and creative blocker that outweighs the strategic fit.

Steelman: Acquiring the franchise secures essential IP to feed parks and products, but only if new content can reignite fan enthusiasm despite the founder's departure.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Financial Disproportion:** The $4 billion cost is explicitly "high against current film output." Without guaranteed box office success, this single asset could destabilize the parent company's balance sheet, violating basic capital allocation discipline for a risky creative venture.
2.  **Creative Void:** The founder's retirement removes the primary creative engine exactly when new films are needed. Recent "mixed reviews" signal declining quality prior to this exit; replacing the visionary at this inflection point drastically increases the probability of fan rejection.
3.  **Revenue Cap:** With "much of the merchandise value already licensed out," the immediate synergy upside is artificially capped. The company is paying a premium for future potential while current cash flows are restricted by existing third-party contracts.

**Risks:**
Fan rejection of new entries could devalue the entire brand, not just the films. The reliance on a new trilogy assumes execution capability that the recent track record does not support.

**Analysis:**
The proposal relies on the assumption that new films will automatically succeed. However, the evidence provided (mixed recent reviews, leader exit) suggests the opposite. The "do nothing" or "license only" alternatives offer exposure to the IP without the $4 billion downside risk or the operational burden of fixing a struggling creative pipeline. A cheaper, staged approach (e.g., co-production deal) would test the waters without the massive upfront commitment. The current plan fails the "beat do nothing" test because the cost of failure is catastrophic relative to the constrained immediate upside.

The strategic goal of feeding all businesses is valid, but this specific vehicle is too expensive and too fragile at this specific moment. The financial exposure combined with the creative uncertainty constitutes a verified blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
