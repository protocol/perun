First call: NO-GO. The proposed $6 billion cash outlay for an unproven (for the buyer) display ad platform, at a large premium, to chase a competitor's move in a fast-changing market, presents immediate financial and strategic fragility without evidence of synergistic integration or customer retention plans.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform instantly grants critical scale and technology to compete in display ads, preventing the search leader from monopolizing the sector after their own acquisition.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Risk ceding the display market entirely to the search leader; low cost, high strategic risk.
2.  **Build/Buy Smaller:** Develop internally or acquire a niche player; lower cost, slower time-to-market, uncertain scalability.
3.  **Proposed Acquisition:** Immediate capability but massive capital risk ($6B cash), potential channel conflict with existing agency clients, and integration uncertainty in a volatile tech sector.

**Fit:** Assumes "competing with the search leader" is the current North Star. If the core business is search, diverting $6B cash into a conflicting channel (agencies) may dilute focus rather than strengthen the core.

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition fails because integrated technology becomes obsolete within 18 months due to market shifts, or because major agency clients defect due to perceived conflict of interest, leaving the buyer with a devalued asset and depleted cash reserves. This is a one-way door; reversing a $6B cash spend is impossible.

**Evidence:** No data provided on the target's retention rates, technology moat durability, or specific synergy models to justify the "large premium." The "fast-changing market" description inherently undermines the value of buying current tech at a peak price.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Financial Exposure:** $6B cash is a massive concentration risk for a single bet in a "fast-changing" sector without proven buyer expertise.
2.  **Channel Conflict:** No mitigation strategy offered for alienating existing agency customers who may view the buyer as a competitor post-acquisition.
3.  **Valuation Gap:** The "large premium" lacks justification via projected ROI or defensive value quantification in the proposal.

**Risks:** Technology obsolescence, culture clash, regulatory scrutiny, customer churn.

**Kill Criteria:** If the target cannot demonstrate >20% year-over-year organic growth independent of the buyer's network within 6 months post-close, initiate divestiture. Leading metric: Net revenue retention from existing agency clients.

DECISION: NO-GO
