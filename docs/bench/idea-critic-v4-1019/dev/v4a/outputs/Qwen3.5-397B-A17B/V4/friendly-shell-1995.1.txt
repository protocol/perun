First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a mass market of "new home users" but mandates hardware ("high-end PC") that the majority of this segment does not possess, creating an immediate addressable market failure.

**Steelman:** A cartoon-house interface could significantly lower the learning curve for novices if it ran on their existing machines.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Hardware Mismatch:** The proposal admits the target audience finds computers hard to use, implying they likely own entry-level or older machines. Requiring a "high-end PC" excludes the primary customer base. At $100 software cost plus the cost of a new computer, the total price point is prohibitive for the intended demographic.
2.  **Imminent Obsolescence:** A "major new operating-system release" is months away. If the OS includes interface updates (a safe assumption for a market leader), this standalone shell becomes redundant instantly. Developing, marketing, and distributing a $100 product with a shelf-life of less than one quarter is financially unsound.
3.  **Ecosystem Void:** "Few third-party add-ons" means the "rooms" will be empty. A shell without integrated applications offers navigation without utility, failing to solve the "hard to use" problem which often stems from task execution, not just file location.

**Risks:**
*   Experienced users (who influence household purchases) may reject the system as patronizing, creating negative word-of-mouth.
*   High development costs for graphics and animation cannot be recouped before the OS update cannibalizes the product.

**Analysis:**
The core value proposition (ease of use) is directly contradicted by the deployment requirement (expensive new hardware). The "better way" is to wait for the upcoming OS release and advocate for accessibility features within that native environment, or to develop a lightweight launcher that runs on current low-end hardware. Proceeding now guarantees a product that few can run, many will dislike, and which will be obsolete upon the next OS patch. The mismatch between the target user's assets and the product's requirements is a fatal flaw that cannot be mitigated by marketing.

DECISION: NO-GO
