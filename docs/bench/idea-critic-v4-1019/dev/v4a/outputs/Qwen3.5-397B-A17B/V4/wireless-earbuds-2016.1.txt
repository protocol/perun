First call: NO-GO. Removing a universal, beloved feature (headphone jack) to force adoption of an unproven, expensive ($159), and fragile accessory (easy to lose, odd look) creates immediate customer anger without a guaranteed upside.

Steelman: Removing the jack forces a necessary industry transition to wireless audio, allowing a premium, seamless ecosystem product that defines the future of mobile sound.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Keep the jack, sell wireless earbuds optionally. Zero anger, lower risk, but slower ecosystem lock-in.
2. Bundle cost: Include the wireless earbuds in the phone price or lower the entry point to $99 to reduce sticker shock against "free" wired ones.
3. Hybrid transition: Remove the jack but include a high-quality dongle and cheaper wireless options, delaying the full wireless push.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, media ridicules the design, customers lose the tiny earbuds within weeks, and the battery life (5 hours) is deemed insufficient for daily use. Sales flop, and the brand is seen as arrogant for removing a utility without a perfect replacement. The "tax" of $159 feels punitive compared to the previous free inclusion.

Evidence & Constraints: The proposal admits high risks: ridicule, loss, high price relative to the status quo (free), and battery limitations. There is no evidence provided that customers *want* this enough to pay a premium for it, nor that the "odd" design will be accepted. The negative utility (loss of jack) is certain; the positive utility (wireless convenience) is speculative and currently flawed by battery and form factor.

Blockers:
1. **Value Mismatch:** Charging $159 for a replacement to a feature that was previously free and universally functional creates an immediate net-negative perception.
2. **Unresolved Utility:** 5-hour battery and easy-loss factors are critical functional deficits not yet solved, making the premium price unjustifiable.
3. **Certain Backlash:** Removing a standard interface without a flawless, cost-neutral alternative guarantees customer anger, which outweighs the strategic vision at this specific execution point.

Risks: Market rejection, brand damage, low attachment rate.

The plan fails the value proposition test as proposed. The price is too high for the risk profile, and the functional deficits (battery, loss) are blockers for a premium "must-have" accessory.

DECISION: NO-GO
