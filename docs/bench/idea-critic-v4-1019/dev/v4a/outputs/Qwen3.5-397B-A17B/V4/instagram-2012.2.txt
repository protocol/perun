First call: NO-GO. The price implies a valuation with no revenue, no business model, and extreme execution risk, violating basic capital discipline.

**Steelman:** Buying the sole mobile threat neutralizes a competitor that could erode the core business before a native fix ships, acquiring talent and user momentum instantly.

**Options:**
1. **Do Nothing:** Build internally. Risk: Too slow; the threat solidifies while the buyer fumbles mobile. Cost: Low cash, high opportunity cost.
2. **Buy Competitor:** Acquire a cheaper, smaller rival. Risk: Lacks the specific network effects and cultural cachet of the target.
3. **Acquire Target:** Immediate threat removal. Cost: $1B cash/stock for zero revenue. Reversibility: Low (integration destroys the asset if it fails).

**Fit:** Aligns with defending the core monopoly, but assumes the target's growth is defensible and not a fad.

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition fails because the $1B price tag creates impossible expectations, the culture clashes destroy the very creativity being bought, or regulators block the deal, leaving the buyer with wasted resources and a strengthened narrative of monopoly abuse. The "mobile threat" might have been a bubble that bursts on its own.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits zero revenue and no business model. The valuation is purely speculative based on current hype. The buyer's own weak mobile position suggests systemic issues an acquisition cannot instantly fix.

**Blockers:**
1. **Valuation Logic:** Paying $1B for zero revenue with no path to monetization contradicts fiduciary duty. The math does not work under any standard DCF or comparable analysis.
2. **Integration Risk:** The proposal explicitly flags culture destruction; history shows acquiring "cool" startups often kills the value proposition.

**Risks:** Regulatory backlash, platform dependency (iOS/Android shift), and the possibility that the app's growth is unsustainable without the buyer's ecosystem.

**Decision:** The financial premise is unsupported by current facts. While the strategic threat is real, the price is unjustifiable without a proven business model or clearer path to ROI. The risk of overpaying for a hype cycle outweighs the defensive benefit.

DECISION: NO-GO
