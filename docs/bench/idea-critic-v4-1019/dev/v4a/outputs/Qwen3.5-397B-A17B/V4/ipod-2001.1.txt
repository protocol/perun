First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and addressable market, as stated, cannot support the required volume to justify custom component sourcing and retail distribution within one year.

**Steelman:** A premium, high-capacity device could create a new category by offering superior user experience, betting that capacity and ease-of-use will overcome the small installed base and high price.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Size:** With a small computer market share and no consumer electronics footprint, the initial compatible user base is too narrow to amortize R&D and tooling costs for a custom 5GB drive solution.
2.  **Price Sensitivity:** At $400, the device is significantly more expensive than existing players. Without a proven willingness to pay a premium in this segment (where cheaper options already struggle), adoption will likely stall.
3.  **Content Gap:** The lack of licensed legal downloads means the device relies on users ripping CDs. While feasible, this friction reduces the "instant gratification" value proposition needed to drive early adopter sales at a premium price.
4.  **Supply Chain Risk:** Sourcing custom 5GB drives on a short timeline for a low-volume entrant creates high unit costs and supply fragility compared to established competitors using standard parts.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Avoids capital risk but cedes the emerging digital music space.
*   *Software-only focus:* License the software to other hardware makers first to build the ecosystem before committing to expensive proprietary hardware.
*   *Wait for market maturity:* Enter later when flash memory drops in price or hard drive components become commoditized.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because the high price limits sales volume, preventing the company from negotiating better component costs, leading to a loss-making product that is discontinued before the ecosystem matures.

**Blockers:**
*   The stated addressable market (owners of small-share computers) is insufficient to support the economics of a custom hardware launch at the proposed price point.
*   No evidence suggests consumers will pay a 2x-3x premium for capacity alone without a bundled legal music service.

**Risks:** Supply chain delays, poor software compatibility perception, brand dilution if the product feels like a niche accessory rather than a standalone revolution.

DECISION: NO-GO
