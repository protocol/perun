First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the brand's core identity and customer value proposition without a mechanism to overcome them.

**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can temporarily overwrite brand perception, forcing adults to trial a premium product despite the chain's "kids and value" reputation.

**Attack:** The plan fails the fit test. The brand equity is explicitly "child-centered" and "value." Adults do not currently associate the chain with premium quality; they associate it with speed and low cost. A higher price point actively conflicts with the established value expectation. The proposal admits customers come for kids' meals, yet offers no strategy to decouple the adult experience from the children's image. Throwing money at advertising cannot instantly rewrite years of brand conditioning; it likely results in high awareness but low conversion, burning cash on users who reject the premise.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve margin and brand clarity.
2.  **Sub-brand or separate line:** Test premium items in specific locations without diluting the main brand or committing to an industry-record budget.
3.  **Value-upgrade:** Improve core ingredients for adults at a modest price increase, aligning with the existing value proposition.

**Pre-mortem:** The campaign launches, awareness spikes, but sales flatline. Adults visit once out of curiosity, find the price unjustified for the brand context, and never return. The massive sunk cost forces a price cut, destroying margins and confusing the remaining value-conscious base.

**Blockers:**
*   **Brand Mismatch:** The stated "child-centered image" directly contradicts the "premium adult" target. No evidence is provided that advertising alone can bridge this gap.
*   **Price Elasticity:** The core customer base is value-driven; a price increase above the core menu lacks a justified value anchor for this demographic.
*   **Unproven Demand:** Committing the "largest budget" before validating demand violates basic capital allocation logic.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, alienation of the core family demographic, massive financial loss.

**Conclusion:** The proposal relies on spending to solve a strategic positioning problem. It ignores the friction between the current brand identity and the desired market. Without a pilot or a mechanism to establish premium credibility beyond ad spend, the plan is structurally flawed.

DECISION: NO-GO
