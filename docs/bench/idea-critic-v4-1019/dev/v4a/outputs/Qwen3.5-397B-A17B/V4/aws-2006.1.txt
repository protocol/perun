First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer with no developer platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors already oppose its capital intensity.

Steelman: Leveraging spiky internal infrastructure to create a new revenue stream could theoretically improve asset utilization and diversify income beyond thin-margin retail.

However, the blocker test fails immediately. The proposal admits the market for hourly server rental is "unproven" and the company lacks the specific operational DNA to run a developer platform at scale. Entering a capital-intensive business where you have no competitive advantage, no proven demand, and active investor resistance to the required spending violates basic strategic fit. The risk of distraction from the core retail business is not hypothetical; it is guaranteed given the lack of prior experience. The "spiky" nature of internal infrastructure suggests it is optimized for specific retail workloads, not the generalized, multi-tenant reliability required by external developers.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserves capital and focus on the core retail margin struggle.
2. Optimize internal usage: Reduce the "spiky" build-out costs without taking on external customer reliability SLAs.
3. Partner: License excess capacity to an established vendor rather than building a competing platform.

The pre-mortem is clear: The company burns massive capital building a generic platform, fails to attract developers away from trusted incumbents due to lack of ecosystem and support, suffers reliability outages that damage the core retail brand, and faces a investor revolt over diluted margins. The cost of delay is negligible because the market itself is unproven; being first in an undefined market is a liability, not an asset, when you are not the category creator.

Blockers:
- No proven market demand for the specific offering.
- Zero internal experience in the required business model.
- Active investor opposition to increased capital spend.
- High probability of core business distraction.

Risks:
- Margin dilution.
- Brand damage from reliability failures.
- Failure to compete with established hardware/software vendors.

This is a one-way door with no evidence it leads anywhere but capital destruction.

DECISION: NO-GO
