First call: NO-GO. The core value proposition (reliable handwriting recognition) is explicitly stated as unreliable in testing, and the form factor fails the primary constraint for a "handheld" device (pocketability).

Steelman: Launch a pioneering $700 pen-based device to define a new category, leveraging CEO publicity to overcome early adoption hurdles despite current technical limitations.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Core Function Failure:** The primary input method (handwriting recognition) is admitted as "unreliable in testing." A device selling on this feature cannot launch when the feature does not work.
2.  **Form Factor Contradiction:** The proposal claims a "handheld" computer, yet the device is "too big for a pocket," violating the fundamental portability requirement of the category.
3.  **Ecosystem Void:** "Little software" and an "unproven category" at a "$700" price point (high for 1993) creates a fatal adoption gap without a working killer app.

**Risks:**
*   High price sensitivity in an unproven market.
*   Reputational damage from launching unreliable technology with "heavy publicity."
*   Inventory obsolescence if the category fails to materialize.

**Analysis:**
The proposal relies on marketing ("heavy publicity") to solve engineering deficits ("unreliable," "too big"). This is a one-way door with high financial exposure. The "do nothing" alternative avoids sunk costs in a likely failed product. The "cheaper alternative" is to delay launch until recognition is reliable and the form factor is corrected. The pre-mortem is clear: the device launches, users cannot reliably input data, it does not fit in their pockets, and there is no software to justify the cost, resulting in massive returns and brand erosion.

There is no evidence that the market will tolerate a broken core promise at a premium price. The risks are not merely "execution difficulty"; they are fundamental flaws in the product definition itself. A pivot to a niche industrial tool might work, but the consumer PDA proposal as stated is doomed.

DECISION: NO-GO
