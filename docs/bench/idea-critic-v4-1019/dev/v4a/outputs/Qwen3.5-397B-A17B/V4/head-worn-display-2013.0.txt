First call: NO-GO. The proposal asserts a mass-market everyday product while admitting no clear everyday use for most people, creating an immediate contradiction between the target audience and the value proposition.

Steelman: An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to refine the hardware and software with enthusiastic early adopters before attempting a general release, treating the initial phase as a paid beta to solve the "everyday use" gap iteratively.

However, the risks provided are not merely potential hurdles; they are fundamental blockers to the stated goal of an "everyday consumer product." Privacy objections regarding a always-on camera create a social barrier that price cuts cannot fix; if the public rejects the device socially, it cannot become "everyday." The admission of "no clear everyday use" for the majority means the product lacks a core utility required for mass adoption, regardless of early adopter enthusiasm. A $1,500 price point restricts the market to enthusiasts, contradicting the strategy of positioning it immediately as a general consumer good. The battery life issue further degrades the "everyday" utility claim.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids reputational damage from a high-profile failure and saves R&D capital.
2. Enterprise-only pivot: Target specific industrial use cases where the camera and hands-free operation solve defined problems, bypassing social privacy concerns and justifying the high price.
3. Delay general launch: Indefinitely postpone the "general public" phase until a clear, mass-market use case is proven and privacy norms are addressed.

Pre-mortem: The product launches to general public, faces immediate backlash over privacy ("Glassholes"), fails to retain users due to lack of utility, and is discontinued within 18 months, damaging the brand's trust. This is a one-way door for brand reputation; once the social stigma attaches, it is irreversible.

Blockers: The proposal explicitly states there is "no clear everyday use for most people" while simultaneously demanding positioning as an "everyday consumer product." This internal contradiction makes the core claim false as stated. Social acceptance risks are cited as active objections, not hypotheticals.

Risks: High price limiting adoption, battery life constraints, privacy regulation changes.

DECISION: NO-GO
