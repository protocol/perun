First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets the flagship's equity on a product whose core mechanism (clear = pure) contradicts established category norms (cola = dark) without a proven retention model.

**Steelman:** A clear cola can carve a durable, high-margin niche as a "pure" alternative, growing total portfolio volume despite some cannibalism, if novelty converts to habit before rivals copy.

**Attack:**
1.  **Category Contradiction:** Cola flavor is psychologically anchored to dark color (caramel). Removing color may signal "diet" or "fake" to core users, limiting the addressable market to novelty seekers.
2.  **Retention Cliff:** Test markets measure initial trial (novelty), not repeat purchase (habit). If the "pure" promise doesn't deliver a distinct taste advantage, repeat rates will collapse once curiosity fades.
3.  **Cannibalization vs. Growth:** Without evidence that clear cola attracts *new* users rather than shifting existing ones, the large marketing spend merely subsidizes a switch from the high-margin flagship to a risky extension.
4.  **Competitor Speed:** A "major brand extension" is slow and visible. If the concept works, rivals can launch copycats faster than a nationwide rollout scales, eroding the first-mover advantage.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Repeat Rate:** The proposal relies on test market "positive response" (likely trial) but offers no data on repeat purchase frequency, the only metric that sustains a CPG brand.
*   **Missing Differentiation:** No evidence shows the "clear" attribute delivers a superior taste experience, only a visual one. Visual novelty decays; taste keeps customers.

**Risks:** High cannibalization of the flagship; rapid competitor imitation; marketing spend wasted on non-repeat buyers.

**Decision Logic:** A nationwide launch with a "large marketing campaign" is a high-cost, low-reversibility bet on unproven repeat behavior. The cheaper, safer alternative is a staged regional rollout focused explicitly on measuring *repeat purchase rates* over 6 months, not just initial trial. The current plan fails the "cheaper alternative" test because it skips the validation step required to justify the spend.

DECISION: NO-GO
