First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a massive, irreversible infrastructure build (new dealer network) and a fixed 1957 launch date despite an explicit, unquantified risk of recession and a segment that "could shrink." In the mid-1950s auto industry, cyclicality is a known killer; launching a capital-intensive division at the peak of a cycle without a confirmed downturn buffer is financially reckless.

Steelman: Capturing the growing mid-market requires a distinct brand to avoid diluting the parent's low/premium lines, necessitating a dedicated dealer network for proper customer experience.

Attack: The plan fails the "cost of delay" and "reversibility" tests. A new dealer network is a one-way door with high sunk costs; if the predicted recession hits in 1957-58, the division becomes an immediate cash drain with no easy exit. The proposal admits the segment "could shrink" yet proceeds with maximum commitment. The "extensive market research" cited is static; it cannot predict the timing of a macroeconomic shift. The risk of cannibalization is also heightened if the economy forces consumers down-market, putting the new mid-priced brand in direct conflict with the parent's volume leader just as volume becomes critical.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids massive capex; preserves cash for the inevitable downturn.
2. Staged launch: Use existing dealers to sell a limited "special series" under the current brand to test demand without building new infrastructure.
3. Delay: Wait for the recession to clear before committing to the dealer network.

Blockers:
- The explicit "recession risk" combined with a fixed, high-capex launch date creates a high probability of launching directly into a market contraction.
- The requirement for a "new dealer network" before validating demand in a potentially shrinking segment violates basic capital preservation principles during uncertain times.

Risks: Market timing error, brand overlap, excessive fixed costs.

The proposal treats a speculative growth trend as a certainty while ignoring the cyclical nature of the industry. The commitment to a new dealer network is the fatal flaw; it locks in costs that cannot be shed if the "perceived gap" vanishes during a recession.

DECISION: NO-GO
