First call: GO. The demographic shift creates a clear opening for affordable style, and leveraging existing mechanicals minimizes development risk while maximizing margin potential through options.

**Steelman:** Launch a visually distinct, customizable sporty car using proven economy underpinnings to capture the emerging youth market at a low entry price, funded by aggressive marketing to create immediate cultural relevance.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the youth segment to competitors; brand ages with current buyers.
2.  **New platform:** High cost, long lead time, misses the immediate demographic wave.
3.  **Proposed plan:** Low R&D cost, fast time-to-market, high marketing risk but scalable revenue via options.

**Fit:** Aligns with capturing the "baby boom" volume; assumes youth prioritize style over exclusive engineering.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the "cheap parts" perception overwhelms the "sporty styling," making the car appear merely as a dressed-up economy model rather than a genuine sports car. Heavy ad spend becomes sunk cost if the product positioning fails to resonate emotionally.

**Evidence:** Based on stated facts: reliable but dull current product; unproven but large demographic desire for style; mechanical sharing reduces cost.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risk of "looking cheap" is mitigated by the explicit design mandate (long hood, short deck) and the options list allowing premium customization. The unproven demand is the very opportunity being chased; waiting for proof cedes the market.

**Risks:** Marketing overspend if adoption lags; brand dilution if quality perception drops. These are managed by the low base price (low barrier to trial) and the modular options strategy (high margin on up-sells).

**Decision:** Go. The asymmetric upside of defining a new category with low incremental engineering cost outweighs the marketing risk. The "cheap" risk is a design challenge, not a fatal flaw.

**Kill Criteria:** If first-year sales do not exceed 100,000 units or option take-rate remains below 40%, halt further marketing expansion and re-evaluate positioning.

**Leading Metric:** Weekly order volume vs. advertising spend ratio in the first 90 days.

DECISION: GO
