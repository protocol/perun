First call: GO. The proposal converts a philosophical stance ("web apps are sufficient") into a defensible economic moat, directly addressing the risk of irrelevance against rivals with existing ecosystems.

**Steelman:** A curated, single-vendor native app store creates a unique value proposition that web apps cannot match in performance or integration, while the 30% fee and review process fund security and quality control, turning potential instability into a brand differentiator.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Rely on web apps; high risk of losing developers to rivals, rendering the hardware a commodity.
2.  **Open distribution:** Match rivals by allowing sideloading; maximizes developer speed but invites malware, destabilizing the device and angering carriers.
3.  **Proposed (Curated Store):** Balances developer incentive (70% revenue, native access) with carrier/security needs (centralized review). It beats "do nothing" by capturing the ecosystem war; it beats "open distribution" by preserving the product's polished reputation.

**Fit:** Moves the north star from "hardware seller" to "platform owner." Without this, the device lacks stickiness.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the review process becomes a bottleneck, stifling innovation, or if the 30% fee drives top developers to rivals. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The "one-way door" is opening the API; closing it later is impossible without killing the product. The cost of delay is immediate loss of developer mindshare to entrenched competitors.

**Evidence:** The premise relies on the fact that native code offers superior UX to 2008 web apps (verified by contemporary performance gaps). The 30% model aligns with standard retail margins, making it economically viable.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented shows the plan fails as proposed. Carrier objections are mitigated by the review power (blocking bypass apps); security risks are mitigated by the gatekeeping mechanism.

**Risks:** Review scalability and developer friction. These become kill criteria: if review time exceeds 7 days or top 50 developers reject the terms within 6 months, the model must pivot.

**Decision:** The proposal transforms a defensive weakness (late to market, web-only stance) into an offensive strength (curated quality). It is the only path to platform viability.

DECISION: GO
