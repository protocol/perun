First call: GO. The proposal converts a philosophical stance against native apps into a controlled economic engine that directly addresses the stated risks while capturing upside the current "web-only" position forfeits.

Steelman: By restricting distribution to a single, company-reviewed store, the manufacturer mitigates security and stability fears better than rival open ecosystems, while the 30% fee creates a sustainable business model that incentivizes high-quality development without requiring internal engineering headcount.

Alternatives analysis:
1. **Do nothing (Web Apps only):** Lowest risk, but cedes the emerging "native" utility market to rivals permanently; likely results in product irrelevance as user expectations shift.
2. **Sideloading (Open Distribution):** Maximizes developer speed but guarantees the security/stability failures and carrier backlash the executive team fears; unacceptably high brand risk.
3. **Proposed Store:** Balances control with innovation. It turns the review bottleneck into a quality seal, directly countering the "polish" concern.

Fit: This moves the north star from "sufficient web utility" to "indispensable platform." Ignoring the rival ecosystems is a strategic error; matching them with a stricter, curated approach leverages the brand's reputation for polish as a competitive advantage rather than a constraint.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the review process becomes so slow that developers abandon the platform, or if the 30% fee is perceived as predatory compared to emerging alternatives. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The one-way door is closing the ecosystem to sideloading later; the two-way door is adjusting the fee or review speed. The cost of delay is immediate loss of developer mindshare to rivals.

Blockers: NONE. The concerns listed (security, carrier objection, workload) are solved by the proposed mechanism (centralized review), not reasons to reject it. The fear of "hurting polish" is paradoxically the strongest argument *for* a curated store rather than against apps entirely.

Risks: Review bottleneck slowing innovation; carrier friction. These are managed via staffing the review team and negotiating carrier revenue shares from the 30% cut.

DECISION: GO
