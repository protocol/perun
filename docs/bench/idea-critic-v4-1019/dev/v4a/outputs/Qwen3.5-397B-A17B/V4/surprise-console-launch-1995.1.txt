First call: NO-GO. Launching early with high price, scarce software, and alienated retailers contradicts the fundamental drivers of console adoption: ecosystem depth and channel support.

Steelman: An early May launch at $399 secures mindshare before the competitor's September arrival, establishing a "first-mover" premium despite limited inventory.

Options analysis reveals the flaw. "Do nothing" until September aligns development and pricing with market reality. The proposed "early launch" costs $100 more than the rival, offers fewer games, and risks permanent retailer retaliation. A cheaper alternative exists: announce the May date to spur development but delay actual shipping until software libraries are robust, or match the rival's price to reduce consumer friction. The current plan beats neither do-nothing (risk of failure) nor a staged rollout (risk mitigation).

Fit with strategy is poor. A hardware platform's north star is user engagement driven by software. Launching with "few games ready" directly undermines this. The pre-mortem is clear: the console launches, sells poorly due to high price and lack of games, developers lose confidence in the platform's viability, and retailers prioritize the cheaper, better-supported competitor. This is a one-way door; once retailers are alienated and the brand is tagged as "overpriced and empty," recovery before the competitor's full launch is unlikely. The cost of delay (waiting until September) is losing four months of potential sales, but the cost of this specific early launch is likely total market irrelevance.

Evidence from the proposal itself acts as the blocker: the $100 price disadvantage, the admitted lack of games, and the explicit risk of developer unpreparedness are not hypothetical risks; they are stated facts that guarantee a failed value proposition.

Blockers:
1. Price is $100 higher than the rival with no compensating value (software library).
2. "Few games ready" violates the core requirement for platform adoption.
3. High probability of retailer retaliation cuts off distribution channels.

Risks: Developer churn, brand damage, inventory write-downs.

DECISION: NO-GO
