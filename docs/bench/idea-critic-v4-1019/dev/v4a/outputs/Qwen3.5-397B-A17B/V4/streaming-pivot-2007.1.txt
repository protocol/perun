First call: GO. The proposal secures the company's survival against an inevitable market shift by leveraging its existing subscriber base and cash flow, despite immediate operational weaknesses.

**Steelman:** Launching a free, limited streaming service now converts a physical logistics advantage into a digital foothold, training users for a future where bandwidth and catalog depth improve, while using the profitable DVD arm to subsidize the transition before external giants dominate.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maximizes short-term DVD profit but guarantees obsolescence as broadband spreads and competitors lock up exclusive digital rights.
2.  **Paid standalone streaming:** Fails due to high licensing costs and lack of brand separation; creates friction for existing users.
3.  **Wait for technology:** Cedes the customer relationship and data advantage to tech giants who will not wait.

**Fit:** Aligns with the long-term vision of home entertainment delivery; the "north star" is access, not the medium. Staying purely physical contradicts the trajectory of consumer behavior.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if licensing costs bleed cash faster than DVD profits can cover, or if the poor quality experience drives subscribers to cancel entirely rather than supplement. However, the "two-way door" nature of a free add-on allows rapid iteration. The cost of delay is total market irrelevance within 5–7 years.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal admits high costs and small catalogs but correctly identifies the strategic imperative. No factual blocker exists: the company is profitable (funding the bet), subscribers are captive (low acquisition cost), and the risk of cannibalization is preferable to cannibalization by a competitor. The risks cited are execution hurdles, not reasons to abort.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated constraints (quality, catalog, bandwidth) are temporary industry conditions, not fatal flaws in the strategy.
**Risks:** Cash burn rate, user churn due to quality, aggressive pricing by tech entrants. These are managed via the existing profitable core.

**Kill Criteria:** If streaming operational costs exceed 15% of DVD operating profit within 18 months without a corresponding increase in total subscriber retention, pause expansion. Leading metric: Percentage of active subscribers streaming at least one title monthly. Review date: Q4 2008.

DECISION: GO
