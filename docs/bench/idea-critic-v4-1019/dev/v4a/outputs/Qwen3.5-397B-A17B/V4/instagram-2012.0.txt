First call: NO-GO. The price implies a valuation with no revenue, no business model, and extreme execution risk, violating basic capital discipline.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the sole mobile-native threat neutralizes a strategic existential risk to the buyer's core business faster than internal development, buying time to pivot the entire company to mobile.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Absurdity:** $1B for ~12 employees and zero revenue is indefensible on standalone merits. It sets a dangerous precedent for overpaying for "potential" rather than value.
2.  **Integration Failure:** The primary asset is culture and speed. History shows large incumbents destroy exactly this value upon acquisition. If the culture dies, the $1B buys only a feature set that could have been copied for pennies.
3.  **Distraction:** Integrating a chaotic, high-profile startup will consume executive bandwidth needed to fix the buyer's own weak mobile product.
4.  **Regulatory & Shareholder Backlash:** Such a premium for a non-revenue entity invites immediate regulatory scrutiny and shareholder lawsuits regarding fiduciary duty.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing/Build:** Invest the $1B internally to hire top talent and accelerate the existing mobile roadmap. Cost is lower, control is higher.
*   **Partner:** Attempt a data-sharing or API integration deal first to test synergy without full ownership.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-acquisition, the app's founders leave due to culture clash, user growth stalls as the product is merged into the buyer's bloated ecosystem, and regulators force a divestiture. The $1B is written off, and the core mobile product remains weak because leadership focused on integration rather than innovation.

**Blockers:**
*   **Valuation:** No revenue model exists to justify the multiple; the math fails completely on traditional metrics.
*   **Cultural Risk:** High probability that the acquisition mechanism itself destroys the specific value (agility/culture) being purchased.

**Risks:** Regulatory block, founder retention, shareholder revolt.

**Kill Criteria:** If the founders depart within 6 months or user growth rate drops by 20% post-integration, the thesis is dead.

The proposal relies on fear (the "threat") rather than value creation. Paying a panic premium for an unproven entity without a clear path to monetization or cultural preservation is reckless.

DECISION: NO-GO
