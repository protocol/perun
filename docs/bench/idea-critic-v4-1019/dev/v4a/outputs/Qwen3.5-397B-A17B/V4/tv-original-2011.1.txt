First call: NO-GO. The proposal abandons the industry-standard pilot de-risking mechanism for a straight-to-series order of two full seasons (26 episodes), committing a large share of the content budget to an unproven format (online-only prestige drama) based solely on internal viewing data.

Steelman: Leveraging unique data to bypass traditional gatekeepers could secure exclusive, high-value IP before competitors, establishing the service as a primary destination rather than a library.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current structure:
1.  **Catastrophic Exposure:** Ordering two full seasons without a pilot removes the primary off-ramp for failure. If the first few episodes fail to resonate, the company is contractually bound to fund 20+ more episodes of a potentially dead asset.
2.  **Data Validity Gap:** Historical viewing data reflects user behavior with *licensed* content. It cannot reliably predict demand for an original, unknown property with a different tone, cadence, or star power. Assuming parity between licensing consumption and original creation is an unsupported leap.
3.  **Format Risk:** Releasing all episodes at once is untested for prestige drama. While it may suit binge-watching, it eliminates the weekly cultural buildup that sustains subscriber retention over months, potentially causing a spike-and-churn pattern.
4.  **Cost Concentration:** Allocating a "large share" of the budget to a single unproven venture violates basic portfolio theory. A single failure here could cripple the entire content slate for the year.

The "better way" is a staged approach: commission a pilot or a limited initial order (e.g., 6 episodes) to validate the audience response, the creative execution, and the "all-at-once" release model before committing to a second season or full run. The current proposal forces a binary outcome: massive success or existential financial damage, with no middle ground.

Blockers: The commitment to 26 episodes without a pilot creates an unrecoverable financial risk if the show fails early; data used for justification does not cover original content performance.
Risks: Audience rejection of the binge model; data misinterpretation; talent mismatch.

DECISION: NO-GO
