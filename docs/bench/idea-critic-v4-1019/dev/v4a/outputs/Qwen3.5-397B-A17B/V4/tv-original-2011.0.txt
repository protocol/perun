First call: NO-GO. The proposal concentrates excessive capital on an unproven format with zero traditional de-risking mechanisms.

Steelman: Leveraging unique viewing data to bypass pilot testing and secure a prestige asset that differentiates the service from licensed-content competitors.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Safe, but leaves the service vulnerable to rising licensing costs and lack of differentiation.
2. Pilot first: Standard industry practice; reduces financial exposure but delays launch and may fail to attract top talent demanding straight-to-series commitments.
3. Proposed plan: Maximum upside for brand definition, but catastrophic financial risk if the data interpretation is flawed.

Fit: Aligns with a need for proprietary content, but the "all-at-once" release and two-season commitment are unverified assumptions about user behavior in 2011.

Pre-mortem: The show launches to critical indifference; the "binge" model confuses audiences accustomed to weekly anticipation; the massive sunk cost of two seasons prevents pivoting after episode 3. The data correlated with interest in actors, not long-form narrative retention.

Evidence: The proposal cites "viewing data" as the core justification but provides no specific metrics proving users want 13+ hour commitments or that film talent translates to streaming retention. The claim that "no online-only service has produced prestige scripted series" highlights a gap in market validation, not an opportunity.

Blockers:
1. **Unverified Data Premise**: The proposal asserts data supports a two-season, straight-to-series order, but no evidence is shown that current data predicts success for *original* long-form content versus licensed clips.
2. **Irreversible Capital Allocation**: Committing to two full seasons without a pilot violates basic capital preservation for an unproven product category. If the first three episodes fail, 75% of the budget is already wasted.

Risks: Talent mismatch for TV pacing, audience confusion over release schedule, competitor mimicry without the data advantage.

The lack of a pilot or single-season trial run creates a single point of failure for a large share of the content budget. The "all-at-once" strategy is a hypothesis, not a fact supported by the cited data. A one-season order with an option for a second is the minimum viable test; demanding two seasons upfront is unjustified speculation.

DECISION: NO-GO
