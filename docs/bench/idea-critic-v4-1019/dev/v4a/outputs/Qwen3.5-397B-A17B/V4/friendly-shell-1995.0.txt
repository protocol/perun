First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a high-end hardware niche just before a major platform shift, creating immediate obsolescence risk.

Steelman: A $100 cartoon-house shell makes PCs approachable for novices on 1995 high-end hardware, bypassing current complexity.

Attack:
1.  **Hardware Mismatch:** The proposal admits most home PCs lack the required memory. Selling a $100 add-on that requires a new computer purchase defeats the "easy entry" value proposition for the mass market.
2.  **Imminent Obsolescence:** A major OS release with a new interface is months away. Investing engineering and marketing now guarantees the product is dead-on-arrival or perceived as a stopgap, destroying brand credibility.
3.  **Ecosystem Void:** Few third-party add-ons mean the "rooms" will be empty, reducing the experience to a static demo rather than a functional environment.
4.  **Market Alienation:** The "patronizing" risk is real; experienced users (who influence household purchases) will reject it, while novices need standard compatibility, not a walled garden.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for the upcoming OS release. Cost: $0. Risk: Competitors might act, but the incoming OS likely addresses usability. Reversibility: N/A.
*   **Software Only (Low-End):** Build a lightweight launcher. Cost: Low. Risk: Limited visual fidelity. Reversibility: High. This beats the proposed high-end requirement.

Pre-mortem: Launched in six months, sales are zero because the new OS includes similar features natively, and the installed base cannot run the software. The project is written off as a misread of the market transition.

Evidence: The proposal itself states the hardware limitations and the incoming OS threat. These are internal contradictions, not external uncertainties.

Blockers:
*   **Hardware Barrier:** Target audience (new home users) likely lacks the "high-end PC" required.
*   **Timing:** Launching immediately before a major OS interface overhaul renders the product obsolete before scale.

Risks: User perception (patronizing), lack of third-party content.

The hardware requirement and imminent OS release are fatal blockers that cannot be mitigated by "kill criteria" after launch; they invalidate the business case today.

DECISION: NO-GO
