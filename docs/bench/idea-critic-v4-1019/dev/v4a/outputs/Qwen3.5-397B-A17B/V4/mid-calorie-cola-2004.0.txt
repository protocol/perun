First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a shrinking demographic with a product that likely satisfies no one, at a premium price, while a rival copies the concept and the underlying trend may vanish.

**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men wanting taste without full sugar, leveraging a premium price to signal quality before the low-carb fad ends.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Demand:** The core assumption is that young men want *fewer* calories but not *zero*. Evidence suggests the opposite: the diet segment grows because users want maximum reduction. A "half-sugar" drink risks alienating full-sugar lovers (taste compromise) and diet drinkers (insufficient calorie cut), creating a "no-man's-land" product.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows the company to double down on the growing diet segment or innovate with non-caloric sweeteners that mimic sugar taste without the carb penalty. This is cheaper and safer than launching a new SKU.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: The low-carb trend proves fleeting, leaving a premium-priced, lukewarm-tasting soda with no loyal base. The rival's entry commoditizes the niche immediately, triggering a price war the premium model cannot sustain.
4.  **Evidence Gaps:** The proposal admits the trend "may be a fad" and that a rival is already launching. Entering a potentially dying market against a copycat with a compromised product is speculative gambling, not strategy.
5.  **Blocker:** The stated risk that "people who want fewer calories already buy the diet version" directly contradicts the existence of a viable market segment large enough to justify a national launch and large campaign. Without proof that a significant cohort specifically desires *exactly* half-sugar, the revenue model collapses.

**Blockers:**
*   Market contradiction: Target audience likely already served by diet cola; no evidence provided for a distinct "half-sugar" preference large enough to sustain a premium launch.
*   Trend volatility: Basing a major launch on a trend admitted to potentially be a "fad" creates unacceptable inventory and branding risk.

**Risks:**
*   Rival duplication eroding margins.
*   Premium pricing deterring trial of an unproven flavor profile.
*   Cannibalization of existing full-sugar sales without gaining new diet users.

The plan fails the basic test of solving a confirmed user need versus a hypothetical one.

DECISION: NO-GO
