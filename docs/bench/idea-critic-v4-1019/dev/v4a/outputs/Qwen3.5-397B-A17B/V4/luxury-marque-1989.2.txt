First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets the company's reputation on a single, unproven luxury vehicle while ignoring the core barrier: prestige cannot be engineered or underpriced into existence; it is accumulated over decades.

Steelman: A distinct brand and dealer network can sufficiently decouple the luxury product from the economy parent to allow buyers to accept high refinement at a lower price point, disrupting entrenched European rivals.

Attack: The "separate dealer network" creates a massive fixed-cost burden before a single unit is sold, violating the capital efficiency required for a market entry with such high uncertainty. More critically, the "thin line-up" of a single flagship sedan exposes the venture to catastrophic failure if that one model misses the mark; luxury buyers expect breadth and heritage, not a lone value proposition. Pricing "well below" European leaders may signal "cheap luxury" rather than "value," reinforcing the very economy stigma the separate brand aims to escape. The risk that buyers refuse to pay luxury prices to an economy maker is not merely a risk; it is the central hypothesis that remains unproven by the proposal's own facts.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital and brand equity; avoid diluting the core economy business.
2. Acquire an existing prestige marque: Instantly gain heritage, dealer networks, and customer trust, bypassing the "economy" stigma entirely.
3. Joint venture: Share development costs and leverage a partner's luxury credibility.

Pre-mortem: The brand launches, but showrooms remain empty because affluent buyers view the car as a "rich man's economy car," while the massive dealer overhead bankrupts the division within three years.

Blockers:
- No proof that a separate network overcomes the "economy brand" stigma (core assumption unverified).
- Single-model launch creates unacceptable concentration risk against established multi-model rivals.
- Pricing strategy risks positioning the product as "low-tier luxury" rather than "high-value."

Risks:
- Massive capital burn on dealer infrastructure before revenue validation.
- Inability to recruit top talent to a startup luxury brand without proven success.

The proposal fails to address how prestige is generated without time or acquisition, relying instead on engineering and price alone.

DECISION: NO-GO
