First call: GO. The proposal attacks a specific, verified vulnerability: buyers cannot distinguish the product, and clones exploit this ambiguity. The mechanism (co-op advertising) directly aligns incentives by subsidizing partners to solve the manufacturer's differentiation problem.

**Steelman:** By funding partners to display a quality seal, the chip maker transforms an invisible component into a consumer demand driver, forcing computer makers to adopt the brand to remain competitive.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Clones continue to erode premium pricing; buyers remain indifferent. This guarantees slow commoditization.
2.  **Direct Consumer Ads Only:** Without partner buy-in, computer makers may resist or exclude the chip. This lacks the distribution leverage of the co-op model.
3.  **Partner-Only Push:** Without consumer pull, computer makers have no urgency to prioritize this chip over cheaper clones.

**Fit:** Moves the north star from "selling to manufacturers" to "creating consumer pull." It fits the stage of needing to escape commoditization.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if computer makers unite to reject the "branding tax" or if consumers ignore the logo. However, the financial incentive (subsidized ads) lowers the barrier for partners, making resistance costly. The risk of "unprecedented" action is mitigated because the status quo (invisible component) is already failing against clones.

**Evidence & Math:** The proposal relies on the fact that buyers currently cannot identify the chip. If the campaign makes the logo a proxy for quality, buyers will ask for it. The cost is high (marketing budget share), but the alternative is losing the entire premium segment to clones. The ROI depends on the lift in premium sales versus the ad subsidy; given the high margin of microprocessors versus generic clones, even a small shift in demand justifies the spend.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented proves the plan fails; risks are executional, not fundamental flaws.
**Risks:** Partner resentment, consumer indifference. These become kill criteria: if partner adoption <60% in Year 1 or brand awareness does not lift sales by 5%, pivot.

The plan beats "do nothing" and addresses the core threat of cloning by creating a market where none existed.

DECISION: GO
