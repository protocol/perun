First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and behavioral friction of a $699 connected juicer relying on proprietary, subscription-only inputs create an insurmountable barrier to mass adoption in 2016.

**Steelman:** A premium, hassle-free nutrition ecosystem where freshness is guaranteed by sealed, trackable packs, justifying a high upfront hardware cost through recurring revenue and convenience.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price Dissonance:** At $699, the device costs more than high-end blenders and competitors' cold-press machines that accept *any* produce. The "razor-and-blade" model fails when the razor costs more than the lifetime supply of blades from alternative methods.
2.  **Subscription Fatigue:** Consumers pay a premium for the bags plus a recurring fee. Buying fresh produce locally is cheaper, flexible, and requires no internet. The value prop (convenience) does not offset the 300%+ cost markup per serving.
3.  **Unnecessary Connectivity:** Wi-Fi for "freshness checking" is a solution in search of a problem. Expiration dates are printable; internet dependency introduces failure points (outages, server shutdowns) that render the hardware useless, violating basic durability expectations for kitchen appliances.
4.  **Market Size:** The intersection of consumers willing to pay $699 upfront, commit to a perpetual subscription, and trust a startup with their daily nutrition is vanishingly small.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Sell a non-connected press compatible with standard bags or loose produce at $299.
*   *Cheaper Path:* Launch the subscription bag service first, compatible with existing machines, to validate demand before engineering custom hardware.

**Pre-mortem:** The company burns cash on inventory and customer acquisition. Early adopters revolt when the subscription price rises or the app servers lag. The device becomes a $700 paperweight. Retailers refuse restocks due to low velocity. The brand pivots to B2B or dissolves within 18 months.

**Blocker:** The core economic model (high CapEx hardware + high OpEx subscription) offers no defensible advantage over cheap, flexible alternatives. The connectivity requirement adds cost and risk without proportional user value.

**Risks:** Supply chain complexity, low retention, regulatory scrutiny on food safety claims.

**Verdict:** The proposal relies on forced lock-in rather than superior value. It will not beat "do nothing" or generic competitors.

DECISION: NO-GO
