First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines maximum engineering risk with maximum deployment risk while alienating the core user base that drives traffic.

Steelman: A complete rewrite with a new algorithm and publisher focus is the only way to reverse traffic decline and compete with rivals, requiring an immediate, total migration to realize benefits.

**Blockers:**
1. **Community Alienation:** The core community values existing voting mechanics. Changing these to favor publishers directly attacks the value proposition for the loyal users generating the current traffic. Losing them before gaining new publisher traffic creates a "dead platform" valley of death.
2. **No Rollback Capability:** Moving all users at once on a rewritten codebase eliminates the ability to revert if critical bugs or engagement collapses occur. This violates basic risk management for a "large engineering risk."
3. **Unproven Demand:** The shift to publisher-favored content is an assumption, not a verified fact. There is no evidence provided that this specific change will attract enough new users to offset the inevitable churn of the existing community.

**Risks:**
- Total platform failure during migration due to rewrite complexity.
- Irreversible loss of community trust and network effects.
- Competitor gains market share while the team fights fire-drills on a broken launch.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "do nothing" and "cheaper alternative" tests. A "do nothing" stance preserves the current loyal base while allowing time for data-driven iteration. A staged rollout (beta testing the new algorithm with a subset of users) would validate the publisher-content hypothesis without risking the entire ecosystem. The "big bang" approach offers no unique upside that justifies the existential risk; it only accelerates potential failure.

The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed launch. Waiting to test assumptions via smaller experiments is safer and likely faster to a working solution than debugging a catastrophic full-scale failure. The plan commits real resources to an unverified strategic pivot with no safety net.

DECISION: NO-GO
