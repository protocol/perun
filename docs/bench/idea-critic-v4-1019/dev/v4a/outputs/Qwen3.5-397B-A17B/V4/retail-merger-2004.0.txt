First call: NO-GO. Two declining businesses with different customers and uncertain synergies do not create value; they compound distress.

**Steelman:** The acquisition creates immediate scale and cross-selling opportunities before rivals consolidate the market further.
**Attack:** This relies on the unproven assumption that merging two failing models yields a winning one. The proposal admits sales are declining for *both* entities, stores are underinvested, and cost savings are uncertain. With distinct customer bases, cross-selling is speculative, not strategic. The "merge corporate functions" plan ignores the massive distraction of integrating legacy systems and cultures while fighting aggressive big-box growth.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve cash, fix the discount model internally. Low risk, limited upside.
2.  **Organic growth:** Invest in existing stores. Higher control, slower scale.
3.  **Acquire (Proposed):** High cost, high integration risk, negative momentum.

**Fit:** A post-restructuring company needs stability and proof of a standalone turnaround, not a complex, capital-intensive merger. This contradicts the "north star" of sustainable profitability.

**Pre-mortem:** By 2006, integration costs have blown out, key talent has fled due to culture clash, and the combined entity faces a liquidity crisis as big-box rivals steal the remaining shared customers. The deal is a one-way door into insolvency.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself cites declining sales for both, underinvested assets, uncertain savings, and customer mismatch. These are not risks to manage; they are fundamental flaws in the thesis. No evidence suggests the combined entity can reverse these trends.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Negative Momentum:** Both targets are shrinking; no mechanism is proven to reverse this.
2.  **Customer Mismatch:** Core premise of cross-selling is unsupported by data.
3.  **Uncertain Synergies:** Cost savings are admitted as "uncertain," removing the primary financial justification.

**Risks:** Integration failure, brand dilution, liquidity crunch.

**Kill Criteria:** If same-store sales do not grow within 12 months post-close, divest immediately.

**Decision:** The plan fails the basic test of combining two weak players to create strength without a proven turnaround mechanism.

DECISION: NO-GO
