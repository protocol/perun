First call: NO-GO. The $4 billion price tag against a franchise with recent mixed reviews and an exiting creative lead presents an immediate financial and creative blocker that outweighs the strategic fit.

Steelman: Acquiring this franchise secures a multi-generational IP capable of saturating theme parks, merchandise, and film pipelines, justifying the premium through long-term ecosystem dominance rather than immediate box-office returns.

However, the attack reveals critical failures. First, the valuation relies on past glory, not current momentum; recent mixed reviews indicate eroding brand equity, making the $4 billion entry price a value trap rather than a bargain. Second, the founder's retirement removes the singular creative vision that defined the space-fantasy genre, creating a high probability of fan rejection for new trilogies—a risk compounded by the fact that much of the lucrative merchandise revenue is already locked into existing third-party licenses, limiting immediate upside. Third, the "do nothing" or "develop original IP" alternative offers better capital efficiency; $4 billion could fund dozens of original projects with full ownership and no legacy baggage.

The pre-mortem is clear: The new trilogy underperforms creatively, alienating the core fanbase, while the high acquisition cost drags down overall studio profitability for a decade. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a failed integration.

Blockers:
1. Financial: The $4 billion price cannot be justified by current revenue streams given mixed recent performance and licensed-out merchandise upside.
2. Creative: The departure of the founding visionary creates an unmitigated risk of brand collapse for future entries.

Risks:
Fan backlash to new content; integration challenges across theme parks and consumer products.

This proposal fails the blocker test. The price assumes success in a vacuum where the primary drivers of past success (the founder, consistent quality) are absent or declining. Without a mechanism to guarantee creative renewal or recapture licensed merchandise rights, the investment is speculative at best and destructive at worst.

DECISION: NO-GO
