First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets the company's hardware dominance on a low-margin software service while accepting severe platform restrictions and existential supply-chain risk.

Steelman: Leveraging the existing user base to create a legal, convenient alternative to piracy that locks customers into the hardware ecosystem through format compatibility.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Supply Chain Fragility:** The model collapses if labels withdraw licenses. Given the industry's hostility toward digital distribution in 2003, relying on "all major labels" simultaneously is a single point of failure with no fallback.
2.  **Platform Cap:** Restricting the store to "only own computers" ignores the majority of the potential market (Windows users), artificially capping adoption while rivals pursue platform-agnostic subscriptions.
3.  **Margin Math:** At 99 cents with label payouts, the unit economics likely yield negligible profit or a loss per transaction, making it a subsidy play without a confirmed mechanism to recoup costs beyond vague hardware lift.

**Risks:**
*   Free file-sharing sets the price anchor at $0; converting users to $0.99 is unproven.
*   Rivals backing subscriptions may define the future market, rendering per-song purchases obsolete.
*   Light copy protection may be cracked immediately, nullifying the licensing value.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Continue selling hardware; let piracy serve as a de facto content driver without incurring operational risk.
*   *Subscription Model:* Align with rival strategy and label preferences for recurring revenue rather than one-off sales.
*   *Platform Agnostic:* Launch on Windows immediately to maximize market share, accepting lower hardware lock-in for higher service viability.

**Pre-mortem:**
The store launches, labels demand higher fees due to low volume, the Windows restriction prevents critical mass, and the company is stuck with high operational costs and no profit. The hardware advantage erodes as competitors offer broader music access.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal fails the "beat do nothing" test because it introduces massive operational complexity and dependency on hostile partners (labels) while restricting its own reach (own computers only). The margin structure is unsustainable without guaranteed hardware uplift, which the platform restriction undermines. This is a high-cost, high-risk bet with a flawed distribution strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
