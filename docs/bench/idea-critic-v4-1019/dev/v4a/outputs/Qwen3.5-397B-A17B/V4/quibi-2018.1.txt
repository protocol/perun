First call: NO-GO. The proposal seeks to sell a premium subscription for content format (short-form) that the market currently satisfies with free, ad-supported alternatives, while ignoring explicit "subscription fatigue."

Steelman: A high-fidelity, mobile-native studio service could capture users willing to pay for exclusive, A-list short narratives if the technical viewing experience is superior.

However, the claim fails the blocker test. The proposal admits "no proven demand for premium short-form" yet plans to deploy over $1 billion before validating willingness to pay. This inverts the standard risk profile: it treats unproven demand as a solved variable and addresses it with maximum capital intensity rather than a pilot. The "abundant" free alternatives create a pricing ceiling that a subscription model must breach without evidence of desire to breach it.

The "technology feature" (orientation agnosticism) is a hygiene factor, not a value proposition sufficient to drive subscription conversion against free incumbents. The risks listed (demand, price, competition) are not merely potential downsides; they are current market realities that the proposal's own facts confirm are unresolved. Launching at this scale assumes the capital itself will manufacture demand, a strategy historically prone to catastrophic failure in media when product-market fit is unverified.

A cheaper, safer alternative exists: produce a slate of shows with a fraction of the budget to test subscription conversion and retention before raising billion-dollar infrastructure capital. The current plan commits irreversible resources to an unproven hypothesis.

Blockers:
1. Unproven demand for premium short-form paired with a $1B+ upfront commitment contradicts basic unit economics validation.
2. Market saturation with free short-form video creates an unaddressed barrier to subscription pricing.

Risks:
1. Subscription fatigue reduces conversion rates below sustainability thresholds.
2. High production costs per minute cannot be recouped without massive, unproven scale.

Kill criteria (if revised to a pilot): Stop if <5% of free-tier users convert to paid within 90 days.

DECISION: NO-GO
