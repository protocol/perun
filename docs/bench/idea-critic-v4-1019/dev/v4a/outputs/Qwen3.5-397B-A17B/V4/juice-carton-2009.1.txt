First call: NO-GO. The proposal discards the brand's primary shelf cue—the orange with a straw—which is explicitly identified as the main recognition driver for a loyal, older demographic.

**Steelman:** A modernized, minimalist design with a functional cap and strong advertising will refresh the brand image and attract new buyers without losing existing ones, leveraging the unchanged recipe and price.

**Attack:** The steelman fails because it assumes advertising can override established visual habits faster than shelf confusion can cause sales collapse. For an older, loyal base, the "orange with straw" is not decoration; it is the signal. Removing it creates a "do nothing" scenario where sales drop immediately due to non-recognition, which no ad campaign can instantly fix. The "cheaper alternative" is to retain the core visual cue (the orange/straw) while updating secondary elements like the cap or typography. This proposal bets the entire franchise on a one-way door: once printed, cartons cannot be recalled if recognition fails.

**Pre-mortem:** Three months post-launch, sales have plummeted 20%. Shoppers walked past the "minimalist" cartons, assuming they were a different product or a generic imitation, because the distinct straw cue was gone. The ad campaign was ignored or misunderstood.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the old image is the "main shelf cue" and the base is "older." These are not risks to be mitigated; they are facts that contradict the core strategy of total replacement.

**Blockers:**
1. **Fact:** The old image is the main shelf cue. **Contradiction:** The plan removes this cue entirely, guaranteeing recognition failure among the core demographic.
2. **Fact:** The buyer base is loyal and older. **Contradiction:** This demographic relies most heavily on established visual patterns; radical change alienates them first.

**Risks:** High cost of nationwide reprinting; irreversible loss of market share if the new design fails to register instantly.

**Decision:** No-go. The plan destroys the very asset (recognition) it relies on for sales. A redesign must evolve the existing cue, not erase it.

DECISION: NO-GO
