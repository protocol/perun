First call: NO-GO. The proposal launches a business-grade device without its core utility (email) unless paired with another product, directly contradicting the company's market strength and the stated goal of competing with a rival's growing tablet.

Steelman: A 7-inch device on a new OS creates a unique, portable form factor that leverages a strategic OS acquisition, differentiating from the rival's larger screen while forcing ecosystem cohesion through phone pairing.

However, the plan fails the blocker test. The primary value proposition for this specific manufacturer is "business email." Shipping a device explicitly *without* built-in email, calendar, or contacts apps unless paired undercuts the very pitch required to sell it to their core demographic. The risk that "shipping without built-in email undercuts the business pitch" is not a hypothetical risk; it is a factual contradiction of the product's intended market fit. A business user buying a tablet for productivity will not accept a device that requires a specific phone to perform basic functions, especially when the rival offers a large app store and presumably standard functionality.

Furthermore, the price matches the rival's *larger* tablet. Asking customers to pay the same price for a smaller screen, an untested operating system, no native business apps, and a limited ecosystem (due to likely developer hesitation) offers no compelling value. The cheaper alternative is to delay launch until native apps are ready or to bundle the software immediately. The current plan offers higher cost (per inch of screen), higher risk (untested OS, no apps), and lower utility (no standalone email) than the status quo competitor.

Blockers:
1. Core value mismatch: A business-email leader launching a tablet without native email apps destroys its own value proposition.
2. Price-value disconnect: Matching the price of a superior (larger, established OS) rival product with an inferior spec sheet ensures market rejection.

Risks: Developer abandonment, OS instability, brand dilution.

The plan cannot succeed as proposed because it removes the primary reason the target audience would buy the device.

DECISION: NO-GO
