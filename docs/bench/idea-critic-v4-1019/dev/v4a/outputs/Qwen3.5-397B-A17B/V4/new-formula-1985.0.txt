First call: NO-GO. The plan bets the entire brand equity on taste preference while ignoring the emotional contract with loyalists, a fatal blind spot given the testing methodology hid the removal of the original.

Steelman: Replacing the formula captures the broader market's preference for sweetness, reversing share loss by offering a objectively superior tasting product that beats the rival in blind conditions.

However, the testing protocol is fundamentally flawed for a total replacement strategy. By concealing the withdrawal of the original, the data measures preference for a new option, not acceptance of losing the legacy option. This invalidates the core assumption that taste alone drives loyalty for a century-old brand.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain share erosion but preserve brand trust and bottler stability.
2. Line extension: Launch the sweeter formula as "New Cola" alongside the original, letting the market decide without forcing a switch. This captures the sweetness preference upside with zero risk of alienating heritage loyalists.
3. The proposal: Total replacement. Highest upside if correct, but catastrophic failure mode if wrong.

Pre-mortem: The launch triggers a public backlash not about taste, but about loss and corporate arrogance. Loyalists feel betrayed because their specific choice was removed, not just outperformed. Without a fallback, there is no off-ramp, forcing a humiliating reversal or permanent brand damage. Bottlers face inventory chaos and consumer anger.

Blocker: The evidence cited (taste tests) explicitly fails to address the primary risk (emotional attachment to the specific existing product) because the test design hid the removal. You cannot use data that excludes the variable of "loss" to justify a decision defined by "loss." The plan relies on a contradiction: using blind taste data to solve an emotional brand problem.

Risks: Mass boycott, bottler revolt, permanent erosion of brand heritage value, no operational fallback.

The cheaper, safer alternative (line extension) reaches the same upside (offering the sweeter taste) without the existential risk. The current plan fails the blocker test because its own evidence contradicts the magnitude of the change proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
