First call: NO-GO. The proposal substitutes a guaranteed, high-margin licensing revenue stream for a speculative, capital-intensive venture with unproven execution capabilities and immediate cash burn.

**Steelman:** Direct-to-consumer distribution captures full lifetime value per user and data ownership, which licensing forfeits, justifying short-term losses if subscriber acquisition costs remain below long-term margins.

**Attack:**
1.  **Revenue Cliff:** Pulling content instantly eliminates billions in guaranteed licensing fees. Replacing this requires acquiring tens of millions of subscribers immediately at a price point *below* the market leader. Given the leader's entrenched position (>100M subs), achieving this volume solely through price undercutting is historically improbable without unsustainable marketing spend.
2.  **Execution Void:** The conglomerate has zero track record running a global streaming technology platform. Building this while simultaneously funding multi-year operating losses creates a dual failure mode: technical instability driving churn before scale is reached, and cash reserves depleting before profitability.
3.  **Cannibalization Math:** The proposal admits to cannibalizing cable and theatrical revenue. If the new subscription price is lower than the combined value of lost licensing fees plus eroded cable/theatrical income, the net present value is negative. The "multi-year operating loss" funding mechanism relies on debt or equity dilution in a low-margin growth phase, increasing insolvency risk if subscriber targets slip even slightly.
4.  **Alternative:** A hybrid approach (licensing non-core content while launching a smaller, premium-tier service) preserves cash flow and tests technology capacity without betting the entire library on a single, underpriced mass-market gamble.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Contradiction:** The plan relies on replacing guaranteed billions with uncertain subscription revenue while intentionally pricing below the market leader, creating a mathematical gap where lost licensing fees likely exceed projected subscription margins for the foreseeable future.
*   **Capability Gap:** No evidence exists that the organization can build the required technology stack concurrently with funding massive operating losses; this is a binary execution risk not mitigated by the proposal.

**Risks:** Mass churn due to platform instability; inability to raise capital once losses mount; permanent erosion of cable partnerships.

The proposal fails the "beat do nothing" test because the immediate destruction of guaranteed revenue streams is not offset by a credible path to equivalent profitability within the funding window.

DECISION: NO-GO
