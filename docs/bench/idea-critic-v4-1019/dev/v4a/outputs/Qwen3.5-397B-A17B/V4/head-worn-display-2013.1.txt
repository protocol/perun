First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to sell an incomplete, socially contentious prototype as a polished everyday consumer product without resolving fundamental adoption barriers.

Steelman: An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to gauge demand and refine the technology among tech enthusiasts before a wider release, mitigating financial risk while establishing market presence.

However, the identified risks are not merely hurdles; they are existential blockers for an "everyday" positioning.
1. **Social & Privacy:** A wearable camera with no obvious active indicator creates immediate hostility in public spaces. Unlike a phone camera, it is covert by default. Without a verified technical or policy solution to this "glasshole" effect, social rejection is guaranteed, not just a risk.
2. **Utility Gap:** The proposal admits "no clear everyday use for most people." Selling a solution without a problem at a premium price ($1,500 is smartphone-tier) violates basic product-market fit principles. Early adopters tolerate bugs, not uselessness.
3. **Battery Life:** Short battery life renders an "everyday" device functionally broken. If it cannot last a standard day, it fails its core value proposition regardless of other features.

Alternatives:
- **Do Nothing:** Avoids brand damage from a high-profile failure and saves R&D capital.
- **Enterprise Pivot:** Target specific industrial use cases (warehouse, medical) where the camera is a feature, not a privacy threat, and battery swapping is manageable. This beats the consumer push on feasibility.
- **Developer Kit Only:** Sell strictly as a non-consumer prototype to build software ecosystems without promising a finished daily driver.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, faces immediate public backlash over privacy, reviewers pan the battery and lack of apps, and sales stall after the initial invitation wave. The brand becomes synonymous with intrusive surveillance rather than innovation.

Blockers:
- The stated lack of "clear everyday use" contradicts the "everyday consumer product" positioning.
- Unresolved privacy mechanics make public usage socially impossible.
- Battery life insufficient for daily wear.

Risks: High price sensitivity, social stigma, regulatory scrutiny on covert recording.

Kill Criteria: If a pilot group cannot identify three daily non-optional uses within two weeks, or if public sentiment analysis shows >40% negative association with privacy, terminate the consumer track immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
