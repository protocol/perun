First call: NO-GO. The plan commits to a massive, irreversible capital outlay (130–150 stores) before proving the core hypothesis: that the retailer's domestic value proposition translates to a market with different competitors, regulations, and price sensitivities.

**Steelman:** Rapid saturation creates immediate scale to amortize the high fixed cost of a new cross-border supply chain, pre-empting the entrenched competitor.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unproven Systems:** Building a *new* inventory system and supply chain simultaneously with a massive store rollout violates the "crawl, walk, run" principle. If the system fails (high probability for unproven tech), all 150 stores stall instantly. There is no containment zone for failure.
2.  **Market Fit Assumption:** "Higher prices on some goods" does not guarantee a win. The entrenched competitor likely dominates the specific high-volume SKUs that drive discount traffic. Without a pilot, the retailer cannot verify if their assortment matches local tastes or if bilingual labeling complexities erode margins.
3.  **Pace as Blocker:** Opening ~70 stores/year requires hiring and training thousands of staff while managing complex cross-border logistics. This operational velocity almost guarantees execution errors that damage the brand before it gains footing.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "better way" exists: open 5–10 pilot stores using a simplified, manual supply bridge or existing third-party logistics. This tests price perception, regulatory hurdles, and system viability with <5% of the proposed capital risk.

**Blockers:**
*   **System Risk:** The proposal admits the supply chain and inventory systems are "not yet proven." Deploying unproven critical infrastructure at maximum scale is a factual recipe for systemic collapse.
*   **Irreversibility:** Leasing 150 sites creates a massive fixed-cost burden immediately. If the concept fails, the exit cost is catastrophic compared to a staged approach.
*   **Missing Evidence:** No data proves the domestic loyal following or pricing strategy works in the neighboring country. The plan assumes success rather than testing it.

**Verdict:** The plan conflates "speed" with "efficiency." The risk of total system failure across 150 locations outweighs the theoretical benefit of first-mover scale. The existence of a cheaper, safer pilot alternative that tests the exact same core risks (logistics, regulation, price perception) makes the "all-in" approach indefensible.

DECISION: NO-GO
