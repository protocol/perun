First call: GO. The proposal trades a rapidly commoditizing asset (storage) for a defensible moat (search) in a market where incumbents are constrained by legacy cost structures.

**Steelman:** By offering 1,000x the standard storage, we render the user habit of "deleting email" obsolete, positioning the product as a permanent archive rather than a transient inbox. This creates high switching costs and massive engagement data, which fuels superior ad targeting to offset infrastructure costs.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Cost Blocker?** Storage costs follow a steep downward curve; a fixed gigabyte today is cheaper tomorrow. Competitors offering megabytes are trapped in old economics. Doing nothing cedes the "archive" use case.
*   **Privacy Risk:** Scanning content is the price of free service in 2004. The value exchange (free massive storage for relevant ads) is explicit. Mitigation: strict internal access controls, not opt-out.
*   **Market Maturity:** Incumbents cannot match this storage without bankrupting their legacy models. They are the "better way" only for users who prefer deletion over search.
*   **Revenue Delay:** Invitation-only creates artificial scarcity, driving demand while limiting initial server load. Ad revenue scales with usage, which storage encourages.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if storage costs plateau unexpectedly or if privacy backlash becomes regulatory. However, the trend lines for hardware costs make the former unlikely, and the latter is a manageable PR challenge compared to the product advantage.

**Fit:** This aligns with a search company's core competency (indexing massive datasets) and disrupts a stagnant market by changing the fundamental unit of value from "inbox space" to "retrievable history."

**Blockers:** NONE. The cost objection relies on static hardware pricing, ignoring historical deflation. The privacy objection is a feature trade-off, not a functional blocker.
**Risks:** Public perception of privacy; slower-than-expected ad fill rates. These are managed via the invitation rollout and clear terms of service.

**Kill Criteria:** If cost-per-user exceeds ad-revenue-per-user by >20% after 12 months at scale, or if invitation demand fails to exceed supply by 10x within 3 months, pivot or shut down.

DECISION: GO
