First call: GO. The core claim is that a mandatory online client is necessary to solve critical update delivery and cheating issues that retail distribution cannot address, despite adoption friction.

**Steelman:** A proprietary client is the only viable mechanism to establish a persistent, secure channel for patching and anti-cheat enforcement, transforming a product-sales model into a service platform that secures long-term revenue against piracy and obsolescence.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue losing control over game integrity and update cycles; eventual revenue collapse due to piracy and fragmented versions.
2.  **Retail-only patches:** Rely on magazines/ISPs for patches; slow, insecure, and fails to stop cheating in real-time.
3.  **Proposed Client:** High initial friction (downloads, accounts) but secures the ecosystem, enables direct sales, and creates a future marketplace moat.
The proposal beats "do nothing" because the status quo guarantees technical decay.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of controlling the user relationship and protecting IP value. Assumes broadband adoption will rise fast enough to offset retail friction.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if early client bugs drive players away before habit formation, or if retail partners retaliate by delisting boxes before digital sales mature. This is a one-way door for the specific game SKU; once required, it cannot be undone for that title. Cost of delay is high: every month without anti-cheat erodes the competitive scene and brand trust.

**Evidence:** The premise rests on the stated fact that current methods "struggle" to deliver updates and fight cheating. No alternative is presented that solves both simultaneously.

**Blocker Test:**
*Blockers:* NONE. The risks (broadband gaps, retail pushback) are execution hurdles, not logical contradictions. The proposal acknowledges these and implies a transition period ("new games").
*Risks:* Adoption friction, partner retaliation, technical debt. These become kill criteria: if <20% of new users activate the client within 6 months, or if retail revenue drops >30% without digital offset, the strategy must pivot.

The plan addresses an existential threat (loss of control) with a structural solution. While risky, the alternative is guaranteed decline.

DECISION: GO
