First call: NO-GO. The reliance on a single new factory in a contested location creates an immediate, unmitigated execution risk that threatens the entire business model before a single unit is sold.

**Steelman:** A dedicated high-volume factory is essential to achieve the unprecedented $2,500 price point, enabling mass motorization for families currently using scooters.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Site Viability):** The proposal admits the factory site faces active farmer protests. In land-acquisition contexts, such opposition often leads to indefinite delays or project cancellation. Building the entire strategy on a single, contested greenfield site is a fatal flaw. There is no backup manufacturing plan.
2.  **Blocker (Unit Economics vs. Safety):** Achieving $2,500 while meeting safety standards with "high volume targets" from a *new* factory is unproven. Cost-cutting to this extreme often triggers regulatory bans or reputational collapse if safety fails. The proposal offers no evidence that the target price covers liability and quality assurance.
3.  **Market Fit:** Marketing explicitly as "the cheapest" risks alienating aspirational buyers who view cars as status symbols. If the brand becomes synonymous with "unsafe" or "poor," demand will evaporate regardless of price. Used cars and scooters remain functional, cheaper alternatives without the stigma.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids capital exposure to land riots and potential brand damage.
*   **Brownfield/Partnership:** Utilize an existing factory to bypass land acquisition protests and reduce upfront capex, even if unit costs rise slightly initially.
*   **Staged Launch:** Prove demand and safety with limited production before committing to a massive dedicated plant.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because land protests halt construction for two years, burning cash reserves. Alternatively, the first batch suffers a safety recall, cementing the "cheap equals deadly" narrative, killing sales permanently.

**Conclusion:** The land risk is a verified, immediate blocker. The safety/price tension is unresolved. The "cheapest" positioning is a strategic vulnerability. The plan cannot proceed as proposed without a secured site and a validated safety-cost model.

DECISION: NO-GO
