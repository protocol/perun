First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer with no developer platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors are already hostile to its capital spending.

Steelman: Leveraging spiky internal infrastructure to create a new revenue stream could theoretically improve asset utilization and diversify income beyond thin-margin retail.

However, the blocker test fails immediately on three verified facts within the proposal:
1.  **Capability Gap:** The company has "no" experience running a developer platform at scale. This is not a risk; it is a missing core competency required to sell reliability, which is the primary product feature.
2.  **Market Validation:** The market is explicitly "unproven." Entering an unproven market requires excess capital for experimentation, yet investors are already questioning "heavy capital spending."
3.  **Strategic Conflict:** The core business has "thin margins" and faces "distraction" risks. Launching a complex, distinct business line divides focus when the core model is already under financial scrutiny.

Alternatives analysis:
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserves capital and focuses on fixing core retail margins.
*   **Internal Optimization:** Use the spiky infrastructure solely to lower retail costs, avoiding the complexity of external SLAs and billing.
*   **Proposed Plan:** Requires massive new investment in an unproven area with no internal expertise, directly antagonizing current investor concerns.

The pre-mortem is clear: The platform suffers early reliability outages due to lack of operational experience. Developers flee to established vendors. The company burns cash on hardware that sits idle because the market does not exist as projected. Investors lose confidence due to continued heavy spending without return, forcing a strategic retreat that leaves the company with stranded assets and a distracted leadership team.

This is a one-way door with high irreversible costs. The lack of experience combined with an unproven market and hostile capital environment constitutes a definitive blocker. The plan cannot succeed "as proposed" because the foundational premises (market existence, internal capability, capital availability) are contradicted by the stated facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
