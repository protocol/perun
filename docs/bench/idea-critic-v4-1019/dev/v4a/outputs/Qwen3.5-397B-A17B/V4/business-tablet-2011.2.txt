First call: NO-GO. The proposal launches a premium-priced device into a competitor's strong category while deliberately removing the core feature (email) that defines the company's brand strength, creating an immediate value mismatch.

Steelman: The company aims to establish a foothold in the emerging 7-inch tablet market using an acquired OS to differentiate, betting that ecosystem synergy with their dominant phone will eventually offset the lack of native productivity apps.

However, the plan fails the blocker test. The proposal explicitly states the device will ship "initially without built-in email" despite the company's market leadership being defined by "business email." This is not a risk; it is a self-inflicted wound that contradicts the primary value proposition. Furthermore, pricing a smaller, unproven device with fewer features to match a rival's larger, established tablet with a vast app store violates basic economic logic. The "untested" operating system compounds this, offering developers no incentive to commit to a niche form factor that alienates the very business users the company claims to serve.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve brand equity rather than diluting it with a compromised product.
2. Launch with email: Prioritize the core business use case even if it delays launch.
3. Lower price: Match the rival's value proposition if features cannot match.

The cost of delay is negligible compared to the reputational damage of launching a "business" tablet that cannot check email out of the box. The pre-mortem is clear: business customers reject the device due to missing core functionality, developers ignore the platform due to low adoption, and the company is left with high inventory and a tarnished brand. The lack of native apps is a blocker because the proposal admits the target audience is business users, yet the product denies them their primary workflow.

Blockers: Shipping without email contradicts the company's core brand promise; price parity with superior rival products offers no rational purchase incentive.
Risks: Developer apathy, OS instability, market confusion.

DECISION: NO-GO
