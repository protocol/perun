First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits a painful prior transition, immediate sales freeze risk, and performance degradation for users, with no evidence that the translation layer solves the speed problem or that the brand can survive commoditization.

**Steelman:** Switching to the dominant architecture is the only path to competitive thin laptops, and a translation layer mitigates application loss enough to justify the two-year disruption.

**Attack:**
1.  **Performance & Value:** Translated software runs slower. If the new chips are only "cool and efficient" but not faster than native competitors, the user experience degrades. Why would customers pay a premium for slower apps on a "distinctive" brand that is becoming ordinary?
2.  **Sales Freeze:** Announcing early explicitly freezes current sales. With a two-year runway, this creates a massive revenue valley before the new line matures. The proposal offers no bridge product or marketing strategy to sustain cash flow during this "dead zone."
3.  **Developer Friction:** Asking developers to rebuild software while providing a slow translation layer creates the worst of both worlds: high effort for them, poor performance for users. This accelerates abandonment rather than ensuring continuity.
4.  **Historical Precedent:** The company's earlier transition was painful. Repeating a known failure mode without a fundamentally different execution plan (e.g., a phased rollout vs. "whole line") ignores empirical evidence of organizational incapacity.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Maintain niche status with warm/slow chips; risk long-term irrelevance but preserve current margins.
*   *Hybrid:* Introduce the new architecture in only one high-end line first to test the translation layer and market reaction, rather than betting the whole company.

**Blocker:** The combination of guaranteed sales freeze, slower application performance, and a history of painful transitions constitutes a factual failure of the value proposition as stated. The plan relies on hope that the translation layer is "good enough" without proof, while admitting known negative outcomes.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, developer exodus, cash flow collapse during the 2-year gap.

**Kill Criteria:** If a single prototype shows translated app performance below 80% of native speed on the target architecture, or if projected revenue drop during the announcement window exceeds cash reserves, the plan must stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
