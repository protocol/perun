First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a non-existent market gap with a product that likely satisfies no one, priced too high for a trend that may vanish.

**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men wanting taste without diet stigma, leveraging a premium price to signal quality before the low-carb trend fades.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Demand:** The core assumption—that young men want "half-sugar"—is unproven and contradicts existing behavior. Consumers seeking fewer calories already choose diet; those wanting taste choose full-sugar. A hybrid risks alienating both, creating a "worst of both worlds" product.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows the company to monitor if the low-carb trend persists or if diet cola continues its organic growth. Launching a rival's copycat product into a potentially fad market increases exposure without guaranteeing upside.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: The low-carb craze was a fad (as history showed), leaving a premium-priced, lukewarm-tasting product with no loyal base. The rival's similar launch signals a crowded, speculative space, not validation.
4.  **Evidence Gap:** The proposal cites "low-carb diets hurting sugar sodas" but offers no data that a *mid-calorie* option reverses this. It assumes a premium price won't deter trial despite the risk-averse nature of fad-driven consumers.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Value Proposition:** The product explicitly tries to serve two opposing needs (taste vs. low-calorie) without evidence that a midpoint satisfies either. This is a fundamental product-market fit failure.
*   **Fad Risk:** Betting a large campaign and premium pricing on a trend identified as a potential "fad" within the proposal itself is financially reckless without a pilot.

**Risks:**
*   Cannibalization of existing diet or regular lines.
*   Rival undercutting or out-marketing the launch.
*   Trend collapse leaving excess inventory.

The proposal fails the "beat do nothing" test. The risks of creating a confused product for a fleeting trend outweigh the speculative upside, especially when a cheaper, safer alternative (monitoring diet cola growth) exists.

DECISION: NO-GO
