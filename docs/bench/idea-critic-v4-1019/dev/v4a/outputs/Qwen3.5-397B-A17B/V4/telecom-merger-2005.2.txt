First call: NO-GO. The proposal explicitly admits the core assets (networks) are incompatible and the target is limited for data, requiring the buyer to run legacy equipment indefinitely while absorbing $36 billion in goodwill.

Steelman: The merger is justified solely to acquire the target's high-value business customers who rely on push-to-talk, assuming their loyalty outweighs the technical debt of maintaining two disparate networks.

Options:
1. **Merge as proposed:** Highest cost ($36B + integration), highest risk (technical fragmentation, goodwill impairment), low reversibility.
2. **Do nothing:** Avoids integration chaos and goodwill risk; forfeits the specific business segment.
3. **Acquire only the customer base/service layer:** Negotiate a transition of push-to-talk subscribers to the buyer's native platform over time, avoiding the purchase of incompatible network infrastructure and the associated legacy maintenance burden.

Fit: A national carrier in 2005 should be consolidating standards for data scale, not diversifying into incompatible legacy hardware. Running two networks contradicts the efficiency gains of consolidation.

Pre-mortem: Integration stalls due to technical incompatibility; the legacy network becomes a cash drain rather than a bridge; goodwill is written down when the "high-value" customers churn due to superior competitors offering modern data services on unified networks. The $36 billion price tag becomes a massive loss.

Evidence: The proposal itself states the technologies are "incompatible" and the target is "limited for data." It admits the necessity of running "legacy equipment." These are not risks; they are stated facts that define the operational model as inefficient by design.

Blockers:
1. **Incompatible Core:** The plan requires operating two incompatible networks indefinitely, creating permanent operational drag and preventing true consolidation.
2. **Goodwill vs. Reality:** The $36 billion valuation likely relies on synergies that the stated incompatibility makes impossible to realize without prohibitive conversion costs.
3. **Superior Alternative:** Acquiring the customer relationships without the incompatible infrastructure (Option 3) captures the value without the structural flaw.

Risks: Customer churn during migration, technology obsolescence of the push-to-talk standard, integration cost overruns.

The stated facts confirm the acquisition buys a structural deficit (incompatible legacy tech) at a premium price. The "high-value" customer base can be targeted without purchasing the incompatible network assets that anchor the buyer to the past.

DECISION: NO-GO
