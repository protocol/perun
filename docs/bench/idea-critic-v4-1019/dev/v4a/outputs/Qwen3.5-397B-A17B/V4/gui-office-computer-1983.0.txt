First call: NO-GO. The proposed price is an order of magnitude higher than competitors while offering inferior speed and a barren software ecosystem, making immediate market adoption impossible.

**Steelman:** The machine offers a revolutionary graphical interface that could redefine office productivity, justifying a premium for early adopters if the value proposition is clear.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on three verified facts. First, at $10,000, it costs several times more than leading business PCs, creating an insurmountable barrier to entry without a corresponding performance advantage. Second, the unit runs slowly, directly contradicting the efficiency needs of office workers. Third, the lack of third-party software renders the hardware a孤岛 (island) of limited utility. Most critically, an internal competitor is developing a cheaper alternative with similar capabilities; launching this expensive, slow version now cannibalizes resources and guarantees failure against both external incumbents and internal successors.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve capital and wait for the cheaper internal project to mature.
2.  **Pivot to niche:** Target only specific high-value design roles where the GUI is unique, but this contradicts the "office worker" mass-market goal.
3.  **Delay launch:** Halt until performance matches price or price drops to competitive levels.

**Pre-mortem:** If launched now, sales will be near zero. Enterprises will reject the slow speed and high cost. Developers will ignore the platform due to low install base. The internal cheaper team will view this as a wasted effort, causing organizational friction.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price-to-Performance Mismatch:** $10,000 price point with slow speed vs. cheaper, faster competitors.
*   **Ecosystem Void:** No third-party software eliminates utility for general office work.
*   **Internal Conflict:** A cheaper, similar internal machine makes this specific configuration redundant and strategically damaging.

**Risks:** Market confusion, brand damage from releasing an overpriced slow product, and internal resource war.

**Decision:** The plan cannot proceed as proposed. The price must be drastically reduced, performance increased, or the launch delayed until the software ecosystem exists. Launching a slow, expensive computer with no software against a cheaper internal alternative is financial suicide.

DECISION: NO-GO
