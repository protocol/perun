First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve three hard problems simultaneously (new country, new supply chain, unproven IT) at maximum speed, creating a single point of failure for the entire expansion.

**Steelman:** Rapidly securing 130–150 sites creates an immediate defensive moat against the entrenched competitor, leveraging the retailer's capital to win the location war before prices can be undercut.

**Attack:**
1.  **Coupled Risk:** The plan couples site acquisition with two unproven dependencies: a new cross-border supply chain and a new inventory system. If either system fails during the rapid rollout, 130 stores open empty or disorganized, destroying the brand's "reliable discount" reputation instantly.
2.  **Pace vs. Learning:** Opening "nearly all" stores within two years removes the ability to learn from early mistakes. Errors in bilingual labeling or logistics discovered in month 6 cannot be fixed before month 24, compounding losses across the entire fleet.
3.  **Competitive Response:** An entrenched dominant competitor can easily absorb a price war or exploit supply glitches from a newcomer stretching too thin, whereas a phased entry would limit exposure.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "beachhead" strategy (5–10 stores) would validate the supply chain and local demand with capped risk. The current plan bets the division on a "big bang" launch without a proven operational backbone.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Dependencies:** The core claim relies on a new supply chain and inventory system working perfectly at scale immediately. There is no evidence these systems function in a cross-border context, yet the plan mandates near-total rollout before validation.
*   **Irreversible Commitment:** Leasing 150 sites creates massive fixed costs before unit economics are proven in the new market.

**Risks:** Brand destruction from stockouts, regulatory fines from labeling errors, and cash flow collapse from simultaneous rent obligations on underperforming stores.

**Kill Criteria:** The plan lacks a staged gateway. It should require a successful 90-day operation of the new supply chain supporting at least 5 pilot stores before signing more than 20 leases. Without this, the risk of total systemic failure is too high.

DECISION: NO-GO
