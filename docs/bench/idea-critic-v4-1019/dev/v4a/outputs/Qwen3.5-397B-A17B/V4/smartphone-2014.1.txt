First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to enter a saturated, two-platform duopoly with a high-price, unsubsidized device that lacks essential mainstream apps and relies on a single carrier for distribution.

Steelman: A differentiated shopping phone with 3D capabilities could carve a niche among loyal retail customers willing to pay a premium for integrated commerce features.

However, the fatal blocker is the combination of price and ecosystem. In 2014, mainstream adoption requires access to dominant social and utility apps; their absence renders the device non-viable as a primary phone regardless of hardware novelty. The "forked OS" creates an immediate app gap that cannot be bridged quickly enough to justify a flagship price point. Furthermore, carrier exclusivity severely limits the addressable market for a niche device, while the unsubsidized price places it in direct competition with established leaders that offer full app ecosystems. The 3D display and one-touch shopping are incremental features, not foundational enough to overcome the friction of switching to a limited OS.

The pre-mortem is clear: The device launches to initial curiosity from brand loyalists, but daily usability frustrations regarding missing apps lead to high return rates and negative word-of-mouth. The carrier, seeing low volume and high support costs, drops support within 18 months. The cost of delay is negligible; waiting would not solve the app gap, but rushing guarantees a high-visibility failure that damages the brand's hardware credibility.

Blockers:
1. Flagship price point with a fragmented app ecosystem lacking mainstream essentials.
2. Single-carrier exclusivity restricts volume needed to sustain a third platform.
3. Hardware novelty (3D) does not compensate for the loss of core software utility.

Risks:
1. High marketing spend required to educate users on non-standard UI.
2. Potential cannibalization of existing tablet/e-reader sales without net new growth.

This plan fails the basic test of offering comparable utility at a competitive value proposition. It asks users to pay more for less functionality based on a single use case.

DECISION: NO-GO
