First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the core differentiator (styling) tested poorly with the target "youthful" demographic, while the underlying platform is aging and compromised.

Steelman: A cost-efficient, niche vehicle leveraging existing minivan assets to capture a camping demographic via accessories, accepting polarizing styling as a brand statement.

Attack: The proposal fails its own logic. It targets a "youthful" market but relies on an "aging minivan platform" with "compromised design" due to cost cuts. Youth markets prioritize aesthetics and capability; this vehicle offers neither. The "distinctive angular body" was explicitly rated poorly in clinics. You cannot market your way out of a product the target audience dislikes on sight.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Save capital, avoid launching a known flop.
2. Revise platform: Too late/costly for this cycle; defeats the low-cost premise.
3. Launch as proposed: High probability of missing the 30,000 break-even target due to poor initial uptake.

Fit: This contradicts the goal of capturing a youthful demographic. An aging, compromised product signals decline, not innovation.

Pre-mortem: Launch occurs. Early reviews highlight the cheap interior and lack of off-road utility. The "camping" accessories are seen as gimmicks on a vehicle that cannot handle rough terrain. Sales stall at 15,000/year. The brand suffers reputational damage among younger buyers.

Evidence: The proposal itself contains the fatal flaws: poor clinic ratings, aging platform, compromised design, and no off-road ability. These are not risks; they are confirmed negatives.

Blockers:
1. Target market rejection: Clinics already proved the styling fails with the intended audience.
2. Value proposition mismatch: "Camping options" cannot compensate for a vehicle with "no real off-road ability" on an "aging platform."
3. Financial viability: With known product deficits, achieving the 30,000 sales break-even is unsupported by the evidence provided.

Risks: Brand dilution, inventory write-downs, opportunity cost of diverting resources from viable projects.

The proposal asks to proceed despite knowing the product fails its primary acceptance criteria. This is not a calculated risk; it is a confirmed failure mode.

DECISION: NO-GO
