First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits the company to an existential financial gamble based on a single customer's request, while ignoring a looming technological shift (supersonic travel) that could render the product obsolete before it pays back.

**Steelman:** Building a massively larger, convertible freighter jet now secures dominant market share for decades by locking in the primary customer and creating a dual-use asset that survives passenger demand shifts.

**Attack:**
1.  **Concentration Risk:** Relying on one airline for a project exceeding all prior capital outlays creates a single point of failure. If that carrier falters or cancels, the company faces bankruptcy.
2.  **Technological Obsolescence:** The industry expectation is a shift to supersonic travel. Investing heavily in subsonic capacity just as the market pivots risks stranding the asset.
3.  **Unproven Dependencies:** The design hinges on a new, unproven high-bypass engine. Failure in engine development delays the airframe, compounding cost overruns.
4.  **Market Validation:** Only one order exists. The assumption that "air travel grows quickly" justifies *this specific size* is unverified across the broader market.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserve capital; wait for supersonic trends to clarify.
*   *Incremental Stretch:* Modify existing airframes to test demand without new factory/engine risk.
*   *Consortium:* Share development costs and risk with competitors or governments given the scale.

**Pre-mortem:** The project consumes all liquidity. The engine fails certification. Supersonic jets capture premium routes. The single customer cancels due to recession. The company collapses under debt.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Exposure:** The outlay exceeds all historical precedents with no diversified order book to cushion failure.
*   **Strategic Misalignment:** Heavy investment in subsonic tech contradicts the stated industry trajectory toward supersonic flight.
*   **Single-Customer Dependency:** No evidence of broader market demand for 2.5x capacity.

**Risks:** Engine failure, cost overrun, supersonic displacement. These are severe, but the blockers above are fatal as stated. The plan lacks a kill criterion for the supersonic threat or a minimum order book threshold before full factory commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
