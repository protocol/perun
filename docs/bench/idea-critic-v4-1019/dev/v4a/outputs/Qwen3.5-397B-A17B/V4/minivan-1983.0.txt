First call: GO. The proposal addresses a clear market gap (garage-friendly family van) using existing assets (compact platform, converted factory) to solve an immediate liquidity constraint.

Steelman: Leveraging the compact-car platform and a converted factory allows the cash-strapped carmaker to rapidly produce a unique, car-like family van that fits in home garages, creating a new category before rivals can react.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing: High risk of continued decline; fails to utilize the government guarantee effectively.
2. Traditional full-size van: Requires too much capital and fails the "home garage" differentiator.
3. New platform development: Impossible given the cash shortage; time-to-market too slow.

Fit: Aligns with survival (stage) and innovation (vision). Reusing the compact platform is a strategic necessity, not just a cost-saver.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if buyers perceive the vehicle as a commercial van. This is mitigated by marketing it strictly as a family station-wagon alternative ("drive like a car"). The one-way door risk is low; the factory conversion is reversible or repurposable.

Evidence: The government loan guarantee provides the necessary capital runway. Families already buy station wagons and full-size vans, proving demand for both comfort and capacity, but no product bridges the gap for garage storage.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (no existing category, cash shortage, copycats, platform limits) are actually arguments *for* the proposal. The lack of a category indicates a blue-ocean opportunity. Cash shortage mandates the reuse of the compact platform and converted factory. Copycats validate the potential success. Platform space limits are the trade-off for the "garage-fit" requirement.

Risks: Market education (buyers confusing it with commercial vans) and execution speed. These are managed via positioning and the urgency of the financial situation.

Kill criteria: If pre-orders from family-focused demographics do not exceed commercial fleet inquiries by 2:1 within six months of launch, pivot marketing or halt production expansion. Leading metric: Ratio of retail vs. fleet inquiries.

DECISION: GO
