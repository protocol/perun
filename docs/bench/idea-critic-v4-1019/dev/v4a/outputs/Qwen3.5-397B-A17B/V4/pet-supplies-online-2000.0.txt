First call: NO-GO. The plan relies on selling below cost to buy growth while burning cash on TV and acquisitions, a strategy that fails if capital markets tighten or shipping costs remain high.

Steelman: Aggressively capture market share and infrastructure now to establish a monopoly position before competitors do, using public markets to fund the loss-leading strategy.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost, extreme risk, irreversible once capital is deployed; requires perpetual market openness.
2. **Do Nothing:** Preserve cash, refine unit economics, grow organically; low risk, slower scale, retains optionality.
3. **Targeted Growth:** Focus on high-margin niches and regional fulfillment; moderate cost/risk, sustainable unit economics.

Fit: Assumes infinite capital availability and investor tolerance for negative gross margins, which contradicts the stated risk of closing capital markets.

Pre-mortem: The IPO window slams shut due to macro conditions or poor peer performance. The company is left with massive fixed costs (warehouses, TV contracts) and a hemorrhaging cash flow from selling below cost. Unable to raise more funds and insolvent on every sale, it collapses. This is a one-way door; once public and committed to national TV and acquisitions, retreat is impossible without catastrophic reputation loss.

Evidence: The proposal admits to "negative gross margins" and "high customer-acquisition cost." Selling below cost means every additional customer deepens the hole. The plan requires buying a competitor while simultaneously fighting price wars, doubling capital needs.

Blockers:
1. **Unit Economics:** Selling below cost creates a structural deficit that volume cannot fix without infinite cheap capital.
2. **Capital Reliance:** The strategy collapses entirely if "capital markets could close," a stated risk that is currently plausible.
3. **Cash Burn:** TV advertising and warehouse construction are high-fixed-cost commitments that amplify losses when margins are negative.

Risks: Competitor retaliation, shipping cost volatility, IPO valuation failure.

The plan fails the basic test of solvency under its own stated risks. It requires perfect conditions (open markets, cheap shipping) to survive a strategy designed to lose money on every transaction.

DECISION: NO-GO
