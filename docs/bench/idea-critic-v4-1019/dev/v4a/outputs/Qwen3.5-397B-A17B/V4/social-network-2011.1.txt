First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a distribution problem with forced integration, ignoring that the core failure mode of the three previous attempts was likely lack of organic user desire, not lack of exposure.

**Steelman:** Leveraging existing search and mail dominance to bootstrap a superior social graph via unique video and grouping features, aligning executive incentives to ensure focus.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Distortion:** Tying executive bonuses to adoption of a specific *product* rather than user *satisfaction* or *retention* guarantees the very "forced integration" identified as a risk. This prioritizes vanity metrics over product-market fit, repeating the cycle of previous failures by optimizing for the wrong variable.
2.  **Network Effect Reality:** The incumbent's hundreds of millions of users create a barrier that "better features" (circles, hangouts) rarely overcome unless they define a new category. Integrating into search/mail treats social interaction as a utility to be appended, not a destination users choose. History shows three failures; without a fundamental change in *why* users would switch (beyond features), a fourth attempt with higher coercion is likely to fail more expensively.
3.  **Cost of Delay/Alternative:** The "do nothing" or "acquire" alternative is stronger. Building internally repeats known failures. The cost of delaying this specific "company-wide push" is negative; it saves engineering cycles and reputational capital from another high-profile flop.

**Blockers:**
*   **Misaligned Incentives:** The bonus structure explicitly encourages the behavior (forced integration) cited as a primary risk, creating a logical contradiction that ensures user annoyance.
*   **Historical Precedent:** Three prior failures suggest a fundamental mismatch between the company's core competency (search/utility) and social networking, which this plan addresses only with more force, not a new strategy.

**Risks:** User backlash, fragmentation of core product experience, wasted capital on network effects that won't materialize.

**Kill Criteria:** If this were to proceed, the bonus metric must shift from "adoption" to "organic retention," and the "company-wide push" must be limited to a standalone pilot not integrated into core search/mail until retention proves superior to the incumbent. As proposed, the incentive structure guarantees failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
