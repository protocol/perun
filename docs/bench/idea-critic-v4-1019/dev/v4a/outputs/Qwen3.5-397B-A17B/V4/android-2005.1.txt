First call: NO-GO. A search-ad company with no hardware DNA spending $50M+ on a six-person secret OS project in 2005 contradicts core competency and faces insurmountable distribution barriers.

**Steelman:** Acquiring this open-source OS is the only viable hedge to prevent search monopoly erosion as mobile usage displaces desktop, betting that controlling the platform layer will secure long-term ad inventory despite high execution risk.

**Options:**
1. **Acquire ($50M+):** Highest cost, highest risk. Requires building a hardware ecosystem from scratch against entrenched carriers and handset makers. Payback is multi-year and speculative.
2. **Partner/License:** Lower cost, but offers no control over the user experience or search default. Unlikely to succeed given carrier gatekeeping.
3. **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital. Risk losing future mobile search share if the market shifts entirely to walled gardens this OS cannot penetrate.

**Fit:** The proposal assumes "open source" automatically defeats carrier/handset resistance. In 2005, carriers strictly control handsets and demand revenue shares; they have no incentive to adopt a free OS that undermines their billing models. The company lacks the relationships, supply chain knowledge, or sales force to place this OS on devices.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because handset makers refuse to adopt an unproven OS from a search company, carriers block distribution to protect existing data revenue, and the six-person team cannot scale to support a full mobile stack. The $50M is sunk with zero ad revenue lift.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "no revenue model," "handset-maker adoption" risks, and "carrier resistance." These are not unknowns; they are stated facts that directly contradict the path to upside. The cost ($50M for 6 people) implies a massive premium for unproven technology in a market where distribution is king.

**Blockers:**
1. **Distribution Impossibility:** Carriers and handset makers control the market and have stated reasons to reject a new, non-revenue-generating OS from a non-hardware vendor.
2. **Capability Mismatch:** The acquirer has no hardware business, making successful integration and scaling of an OS highly improbable.
3. **Economic Misalignment:** No clear path to monetization exists to justify the $50M+ outlay against the stated risks.

**Risks:** Multi-year payback, open-source commoditization without ad lift, talent retention of a tiny team.

The stated facts confirm the plan fails as proposed: the route to the upside (mobile search ads) is blocked by the very market dynamics the proposal acknowledges.

DECISION: NO-GO
