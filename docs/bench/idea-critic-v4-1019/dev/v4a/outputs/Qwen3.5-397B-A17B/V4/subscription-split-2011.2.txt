First call: NO-GO. The proposal doubles down on a recent 60% price hike by fracturing the user experience, ignoring the primary risk of customer backlash.

**Steelman:** Separating the brands allows streaming to pivot quickly without the drag of a declining DVD logistics model, preventing operational paralysis.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing & Trust:** Executing a complex split (new logins, bills, queues) immediately after a massive price increase is catastrophic. Customers perceive this as a "double penalty": paying significantly more for a service that is now being broken into two inconvenient fragments. This validates fears of greed over value.
2.  **Friction vs. Retention:** Forcing two accounts and bills increases churn probability precisely when price sensitivity is highest. The loss of shared ratings removes a key differentiator against emerging streaming-only competitors, making the DVD portion feel like an orphaned legacy product rather than a premium add-on.
3.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "internal separation only" options preserve the single customer view. A unified front end with distinct backend management achieves the operational goal without the customer-facing friction. The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" because it externalizes internal operational costs onto the customer as increased hassle.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-split, churn spikes 20% higher than projected. The narrative becomes "they raised prices and then made it harder to use," leading to a brand crisis that overshadows any operational gains. The DVD business collapses faster due to neglect, and streaming growth stalls due to reputational damage.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** A 60% price increase occurred just two months prior.
*   **Contradiction:** The proposal claims to manage "reaction to the price increase" by implementing a change that objectively worsens the customer value proposition (higher price + higher friction). This directly contradicts the goal of retention.
*   **Missing Evidence:** No data suggests customers want separate bills or that the brand damage from the price hike can survive a functional split.

**Risks:** Mass churn, brand irreparability, accelerated DVD decline, competitive disadvantage in recommendation quality.

**Decision:** The plan as proposed guarantees a customer revolt. It must be revised to decouple the operational split from the customer experience, or delayed until price sensitivity stabilizes.

DECISION: NO-GO
