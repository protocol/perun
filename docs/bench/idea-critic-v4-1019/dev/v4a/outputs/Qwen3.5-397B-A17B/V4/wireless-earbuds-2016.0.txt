First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets the company's audio future on an unproven form factor at a premium price point while simultaneously removing a beloved, universal standard, creating a high probability of customer backlash without a guaranteed replacement value.

**Steelman:** Removing the jack forces a necessary industry evolution toward wireless convenience; launching a seamless, proprietary ecosystem at $159 captures high-margin revenue and justifies the hardware change by offering a superior, albeit expensive, user experience.

**Attack:**
1.  **Value Mismatch:** Customers perceive the $159 price as a "tax" for losing free wired earphones, not as an upgrade. The "odd" appearance invites ridicule before utility is proven.
2.  **Functional Regression:** Five-hour battery life is a downgrade from unlimited wired listening. Easy loss creates a recurring friction point absent in wired models.
3.  **Strategic Risk:** Angering the core user base to push an accessory is a one-way door that damages brand trust if the accessory fails to delight immediately.
4.  **Alternatives:** A lower-cost adapter included in the box, or a cheaper wireless option, mitigates anger while testing the market. Doing nothing preserves the status quo until the technology matures.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch is mocked as arrogant; sales lag due to price and fear of loss; the narrative becomes "they broke our phones to sell junk." The removal of the jack is blamed for the failure, not the earbuds themselves.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price Perception:** No evidence provided that customers value the convenience enough to pay $159 *plus* the cost of losing the jack.
*   **Battery/Utility:** Five hours is insufficient for heavy users, creating a functional deficit compared to the removed feature.
*   **Adoption Risk:** High likelihood of ridicule undermining the "premium" positioning before market education occurs.

**Risks:** Loss of customer goodwill, low attach rate, negative press cycle focusing on "lost" features rather than new ones.

**Conclusion:** The plan relies on customers accepting a functional downgrade (battery, loss risk) and a significant price increase simultaneously with the removal of a standard feature. The anger is a near-certainty; the delight is speculative. The cheaper alternative (adapter + later wireless iteration) achieves the transition with less brand damage.

DECISION: NO-GO
