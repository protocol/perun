First call: NO-GO. The $7.4 billion price tag for a studio producing one film annually represents an extreme valuation multiple with no immediate path to ROI, creating a massive financial blocker regardless of strategic fit.

Steelman: Acquiring the studio is the only way to secure its creative talent, prevent a rival partnership, and revitalize the conglomerate's failing hand-drawn unit by installing proven leadership.

However, the financial arithmetic fails the blocker test. A single annual film cannot service the opportunity cost of $7.4 billion in equity, even with significant backend synergies. The proposal relies entirely on future hits to justify a present premium that assumes perfection. If the creative leaders depart or the next film underperforms, the conglomerate faces catastrophic write-downs.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain cash, accept the partnership expiry, and attempt internal turnaround. Cost: Low. Risk: Continued underperformance.
2. Renewed negotiation: Offer a profit-participation extension rather than an outright buyout. Cost: Variable. Risk: Studio rejects.
3. Acquisition (Proposed): Cost: $7.4B. Risk: Extreme financial exposure, culture clash, founder leverage.

The "dependence on a few creative leaders" risk is amplified, not solved, by this deal; it concentrates the conglomerate's entire animation future on individuals who now hold significant equity leverage over the buyer. The famous founder becoming a major shareholder creates a governance conflict where one individual can block or dictate strategy.

While the strategic fit (saving the animation division) is sound, the specific mechanism (all-stock purchase at this price) is financially indefensible based on current output. The deal bets the entire company's liquidity on a "hit streak" continuing indefinitely. A cheaper, staged alternative—such as a multi-picture distribution deal with an option to acquire later at a performance-adjusted price—reaches the same upside (securing content) without the immediate $7.4 billion downside.

Blockers: The valuation ($7.4B for ~1 film/year) creates a negative expected value scenario unless unrealistic growth assumptions are met immediately. The governance risk of the founder becoming a major shareholder is unmitigated.

Risks: Culture clash, key person dependency, box office volatility.

DECISION: NO-GO
