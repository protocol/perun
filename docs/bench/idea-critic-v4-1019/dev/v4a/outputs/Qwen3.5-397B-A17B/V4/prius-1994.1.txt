First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a fixed public schedule for unproven core technologies without a demand signal or regulatory mandate, creating asymmetric downside.

**Steelman:** A bold, first-mover commitment forces internal alignment and secures IP leadership in hybrid tech before competitors react, accepting high initial losses for long-term dominance.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Technical & Financial):** The proposal admits battery durability is "unproven" and the transmission is "novel." Committing to mass production by 1997 implies tooling and supply chain lock-in before validating these components in real-world conditions. A single failure mode in the NiMH pack or transmission under warranty exposure could bankrupt the program. The math fails: high R&D + unproven yield + no premium pricing power (cheap fuel) = negative ROI with no defined kill switch.
2.  **Better Way:** A "stealth" prototype program with a flexible timeline allows technical de-risking without public schedule pressure. Alternatively, license the tech to a niche luxury brand first to prove durability before volume commitment. "Do nothing" preserves capital until regulators force the issue or a competitor absorbs the initial risk.
3.  **Fit:** With cheap fuel and no mandates, the market signal is absent. This contradicts the north star of profitable volume production.

**Pre-mortem:** By 1998, the cars are recalled due to battery degradation after 30,000 miles. Warranty costs exceed revenue, the brand reputation for reliability collapses, and the stock price tanks. The public schedule prevented delaying the launch to fix the root cause.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** Unproven battery durability + fixed mass-production date + no demand signal. The plan assumes success before evidence exists.
*   **Risks:** Cost overruns, warranty explosion, brand damage.
*   **Kill Criteria Missing:** No metric defines when to stop if batteries fail testing. No pilot phase is defined.

This is a one-way door with high irreversibility. The lack of a verified durability baseline is a factual blocker to a "mass production" commitment. The plan must be revised to include a rigorous, private validation phase with go/no-go gates tied to battery cycle life data, decoupled from a public launch date.

DECISION: NO-GO
