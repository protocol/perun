First call: NO-GO. The proposed price is prohibitively high for the target market, and superior internal alternatives exist.

**Steelman:** Launching a premium, mouse-driven office computer at $10,000 establishes a high-end category leader before competitors can react, leveraging unique hardware to secure early adopters despite limited software.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At several times the cost of leading business PCs, the unit economics fail for typical office deployment. Without a killer app library (which is currently absent), the ROI for customers is negative.
2.  **Internal Cannibalization:** A cheaper, similar machine is already in development internally. Launching the expensive version first destroys the market for the upcoming cost-effective model and confuses the sales channel.
3.  **Performance:** Slow execution undermines productivity claims essential for business tools, making the high price indefensible.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows the internal cheaper project to mature and launch with a stronger software ecosystem at a viable price point. This is strictly superior to forcing a premature, overpriced launch.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price:** $10,000 exceeds market tolerance for a machine with little software and slow performance.
*   **Internal Conflict:** The existence of a cheaper, similar internal project makes this launch strategically redundant and financially destructive.

**Risks:**
*   Market rejection due to cost.
*   Brand damage from releasing an underpowered, expensive product.
*   Wasted R&D if the internal cheaper project renders this obsolete immediately.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal fails the basic test of beating "do nothing." The internal alternative offers the same interface benefits at a lower cost, likely with better timing for software availability. Launching the expensive, slow unit now blocks the better internal option and alienates the target audience. There is no unique upside that justifies the massive price premium and performance deficit. The plan commits real money to a losing position when a stronger hand is being held internally.

DECISION: NO-GO
