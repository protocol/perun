First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a distribution problem with forced integration, ignoring that the core failure mode of the previous three attempts was likely lack of genuine user desire, not lack of exposure.

**Steelman:** Leveraging existing search and mail dominance to bootstrap a superior social graph via unique video and grouping features, aligning incentives to ensure execution focus.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & History:** The company has already failed three times. A "company-wide push" and bonus ties address execution effort, not product-market fit. If users rejected previous iterations, forcing integration into core utilities (search, mail) risks degrading the value of those profitable products to subsidize a failing experiment.
2.  **Alternatives:** The "cheaper" alternative is *not* a company-wide mandate. It is a contained experiment within a single vertical or a standalone acquisition. The proposal rejects "do nothing" but fails to prove why *this* specific integrated approach succeeds where three isolated attempts failed.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: Users actively disable the integration or abandon the platform because the "forced" nature creates resentment, while the network effect of the incumbent (hundreds of millions of users) renders the new features (circles, hangouts) insufficient to trigger migration. The "invite-only" beta contradicts the "company-wide push" scale; you cannot force-feed a viral product.
4.  **Evidence Gap:** The proposal cites no evidence that "friend-grouping" or "video hangouts" are sufficient switchers against established network effects. It assumes integration equals adoption.

**Blockers:**
*   **Historical Precedent:** Three prior failures suggest a fundamental mismatch between this company's identity and social networking, which a "push" strategy does not resolve.
*   **Cannibalization Risk:** Forced integration into core revenue drivers (search, mail) without proven demand threatens existing user trust and utility.
*   **Incentive Misalignment:** Tying bonuses to adoption metrics encourages gaming the system (forced sign-ups) rather than building genuine value, potentially accelerating user churn.

**Risks:** High capital burn, brand damage to core products, executive distraction.

**Decision:** The plan relies on coercion rather than organic value creation to overcome network effects, repeating past strategic errors with higher stakes.

DECISION: NO-GO
