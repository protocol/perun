First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores stated, fundamental blockers that prevent the product from functioning as an "everyday consumer product" at the proposed scale.

Steelman: An invitation-only launch at $1,500 validates the technology and gathers early feedback before a wider release.

However, the attack reveals critical failures in the core claim:
1.  **Contradictory Positioning:** The plan targets "early adopters" via an "invitation-only program" but positions the device immediately as an "everyday consumer product." These are mutually exclusive strategies. Everyday products require mass availability and utility; invitation-only exclusivity at $1,500 (4x the contemporary flagship smartphone price) explicitly rejects the mass market.
2.  **Unresolved Blockers:** The raised risks are not merely "risks" but active blockers for the stated goal. "Privacy objections" and "social acceptance" are binary adoption gates for a wearable camera; without a solution, the product is socially unusable regardless of technical capability. "No clear everyday use" directly contradicts the "everyday consumer product" positioning. A product without a defined daily use case cannot be sold as one.
3.  **Economic Failure:** At $1,500, the device is a luxury niche item, not an everyday tool. The proposal offers no path to bridge the gap between this price point and the volume required for an "everyday" classification.

The proposal fails because it asserts a mass-market identity while employing a niche strategy and ignoring fatal usability constraints. It attempts to skip the necessary phase of defining utility and solving social friction before claiming "everyday" status.

Blockers:
- Stated lack of "clear everyday use" contradicts the core "everyday product" claim.
- "Privacy objections" and "social acceptance" issues remain unaddressed, preventing public usage.
- $1,500 price point + invitation-only access contradicts "everyday consumer product" positioning.

Risks:
- Battery life limitations.
- Potential negative brand impact from failed social integration.

DECISION: NO-GO
