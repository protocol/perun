First call: GO. The proposal solves the core friction of digital books (need a PC, slow downloads) and aligns with the company's catalog strength, despite hardware inexperience.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled device removes purchase friction and piracy fears (via controlled ecosystem), unlocking the company's vast catalog for immediate consumption at a mass-market price point.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the emerging digital market to competitors; print sales eventually decline without a digital substitute.
2.  **Software-only app:** Requires users to own PCs/laptops (rare in 2007 for casual reading) and lacks the "paper-like" experience; piracy risks higher.
3.  **Proposed Hardware:** High upfront cost/risk, but creates a closed, premium ecosystem controlling distribution and pricing.

**Fit:** Moves the north star from "selling print units" to "selling reading moments." Reuses the existing catalog and publisher relationships, though it requires new hardware capabilities.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if hardware costs force a high retail price, if publishers withhold titles due to low pricing, or if the cellular model proves too expensive to subsidize. It is a one-way door regarding capital expenditure but reversible in market presence if sales flop. Cost of delay is high; first-mover advantage in a dedicated reader market is critical.

**Evidence:** Assumed: Cellular data costs can be subsidized by book margins; publishers will accept lower per-unit revenue for volume and piracy protection. The proposal lacks specific unit economics proving the subsidy model works.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (inexperience, margin squeeze, cannibalization) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. Cannibalization is acceptable if it secures the digital future. Inexperience is mitigated by partnering with manufacturers (implied necessity).

**Risks:** Hardware supply chain failures; publisher revolt on pricing; subsidy model bleeding cash.

**Kill Criteria:** If device BOM cost exceeds $150 (making subsidy impossible) or if <3 major publishers sign on by launch, halt production. Leading metric: Devices sold per week vs. attach rate of books per device. Review in 6 months.

DECISION: GO
