First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a tiny installed base, an unproven legal music supply, and a premium price in a market where cheaper devices have already failed to gain traction.

Steelman: A high-capacity, tightly integrated device could create a superior user experience that justifies a premium, eventually forcing the music industry to license content and expanding the ecosystem beyond the current small computer base.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoid capital risk but cede the emerging digital music category to competitors.
2. License software broadly: Immediately expand the addressable market to Windows users, reducing dependency on the small computer base.
3. Proposed plan: Bet the brand on a closed ecosystem with no existing content pipeline.

Fit: This assumes a "north star" of ecosystem lock-in and vertical integration. However, without legal music downloads, the hardware is a solution without a viable fuel source. The stated constraint of selling only to existing computer owners severely caps the initial upside.

Pre-mortem: The device launches to critical acclaim for design but fails commercially because users cannot easily load legal music. The $400 price point is unjustified without a seamless software store, which does not exist. The company runs out of cash trying to negotiate industry deals while inventory sits unsold. The one-way door is the massive sunk cost in custom hard drive sourcing and marketing for a niche product.

Evidence: The proposal admits the music industry has not licensed downloads at scale and that cheaper competitors have sold poorly. There is no evidence presented that a higher price will overcome the lack of content or the small compatible user base.

Blockers:
1. No legal content pipeline: The core value proposition (easy music management) is broken without licensed downloads, making the premium price indefensible.
2. Addressable market too small: Restricting sales to a small-share computer owner base cannot support the economics of a new hardware category launch.

Risks: Component sourcing delays, industry resistance to licensing, consumer rejection of high price.

DECISION: NO-GO
