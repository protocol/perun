First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a computer and music player maker with zero phone experience to enter a carrier-dominated market with an unproven, keyboard-less, premium device, relying on a single carrier for revenue sharing and design control—a massive deviation from industry norms with no evidence of demand for such a hybrid.

**Steelman:** A converged device leveraging existing music dominance and web capabilities can disrupt the fragmented, carrier-controlled phone market if one partner cedes control for exclusivity, creating a premium category that justifies the lack of a physical keyboard.

**Attack:**
1.  **Experience Gap:** The company has never manufactured phones, navigated carrier certification, or built cellular radios. Failure here is catastrophic, not just a delay.
2.  **Market Fit:** Business users (the premium segment) demand physical keyboards. Removing it for a touchscreen is a bet against established user behavior without pilot data.
3.  **Carrier Leverage:** Carriers dictate terms. Expecting one to pay revenue shares and surrender design control for an unproven vendor is historically unprecedented and likely a negotiation non-starter.
4.  **Ecosystem Void:** Launching without third-party apps limits utility compared to emerging smartphone competitors, making the "premium" claim hard to sustain on hardware alone.
5.  **Cannibalization vs. Defense:** While music players are at risk, jumping into the complex phone arena to defend them may destroy capital faster than the erosion would.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Protect margins on existing devices; license music software to existing phone makers instead of building hardware.
*   *Partnership:* Collaborate with an established phone maker (e.g., Motorola, Nokia) to handle cellular hardware while providing the OS and music interface, reducing execution risk.
*   *Keyboard Hybrid:* Launch a device with both a touchscreen and a physical keyboard to mitigate the input risk.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** No prior phone manufacturing or carrier relationship exists.
*   **Fact:** Premium business market currently mandates physical keyboards.
*   **Fact:** No carrier has ever agreed to revenue sharing + design surrender for a new entrant.

**Risks:** Total product failure, brand damage, massive R&D sunk cost, carrier rejection.

The proposal relies on multiple unprecedented conditions aligning perfectly (carrier behavior, user acceptance of no keyboard, successful entry into a new manufacturing domain) with no validating evidence. The risks are existential, and the "do nothing" or "partnership" options offer safer paths to the same strategic end.

DECISION: NO-GO
