First call: NO-GO. A loss-making manufacturer with low margins proposing high-fixed-cost retail leases during a recession violates basic capital preservation logic.

Steelman: Direct retail is the only viable channel to demonstrate the integrated hardware-software music strategy, as third-party staff actively undermine the premium value proposition, making the investment a necessary survival pivot rather than optional expansion.

Options analysis reveals "Do Nothing" preserves cash but cedes brand narrative to competitors pushing cheaper units. The cheapest alternative—leasing small kiosks or "shop-in-shop" zones within existing department stores—offers demonstration capability with a fraction of the lease liability and inventory risk. The proposed full-chain rollout carries massive reversibility costs; once signed, mall leases are difficult to exit without severe penalties.

Fit with strategy is high regarding the "digital-music" vision, but the execution method contradicts the company's current financial stage. A company running losses cannot absorb the operating leverage of prime real estate without a proven retail operating model, which the proposal admits the industry lacks.

Pre-mortem: The plan fails because a mild recession reduces foot traffic while fixed rents remain due. Inventory of specialized components becomes obsolete before sale. Channel conflict causes major resellers to de-prioritize the brand entirely, collapsing the remaining 95% of revenue before the new stores reach profitability.

Evidence check: The proposal cites "management argument" but offers no corrected unit economics showing how high leases become profitable given the stated 3-5% market share and recent losses. The assumption that customers will travel to prime malls specifically for this brand during an economic downturn is unproven.

Blockers:
1. Financial mismatch: Recent losses and low margins cannot support high-fixed-cost leases without a guaranteed volume that is not evidenced.
2. Macro timing: Entering a high-lease sector during a recession creates an immediate cash-flow trap.
3. Lack of capability: Acknowledged poor industry track record suggests execution risk is critical, not manageable.

Risks: Channel retaliation, inventory obsolescence, brand dilution if stores feel empty.

The plan requires a pilot (kiosks) to prove the unit economics before committing to a full chain. As proposed, it is a bet-the-company gamble on unproven assumptions during a downturn.

DECISION: NO-GO
