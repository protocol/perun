First call: NO-GO. The proposal explicitly admits the core assets (networks) are incompatible and the target is data-limited, creating an immediate integration burden that threatens the $36B valuation without a stated plan to resolve the technical clash.

**Steelman:** The merger acquires a sticky, high-margin business segment (push-to-talk) that the buyer cannot organically replicate, justifying the premium if the network can be unified.

**Attack:** The claim fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests. The proposal identifies "technology incompatibility" as a primary risk but offers no mechanism to bridge two different, incompatible network standards while running legacy equipment. In 2005, data capability is the growth vector; acquiring a "data-limited" network creates a technological dead zone. The $36B price tag likely consists largely of goodwill based on current earnings, which are at risk if the service degrades during a complex, un-planned integration. There is no "cheaper alternative" analyzed, such as a roaming agreement or MVNO deal to access those customers without assuming the legacy network liability.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume the merger failed in 2008. Cause: Integration costs ballooned as engineers could not merge the incompatible protocols, forcing the carrier to run two parallel networks indefinitely. Margins collapsed under the weight of maintaining legacy hardware, while data customers fled to competitors with native high-speed networks. The "high-value" customers left when service reliability dipped during the transition.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Technical Incompatibility:** The proposal states the networks are "incompatible" and the target is "data-limited" but provides no engineering path to unify them. This contradicts the goal of a combined network.
2.  **Valuation vs. Reality:** The $36B value relies on goodwill from a business model the buyer explicitly cannot support technically without massive, undefined reconstruction.

**Risks:** Integration cost overruns, customer churn during migration, stranded legacy assets.

**Decision:** No-go. The proposal identifies a fatal structural flaw (incompatible technologies) and a strategic weakness (data limitations) but treats them as manageable risks rather than blockers. Without a verified technical integration plan and a revised valuation that accounts for the cost of replacing the legacy network, the deal destroys value.

DECISION: NO-GO
