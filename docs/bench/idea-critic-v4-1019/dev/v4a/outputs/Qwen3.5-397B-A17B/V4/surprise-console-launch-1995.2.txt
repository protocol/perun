First call: NO-GO. Launching early with high price, scarce software, and alienated retailers contradicts the fundamental requirements for a successful console ecosystem launch.

**Steelman:** An early May launch at $399 secures a four-month head start to establish brand presence before the competitor arrives, capturing early adopters despite limited inventory.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economic Failure:** At $100 above the rival, the value proposition collapses when paired with "few games ready." Early adopters pay a premium for content depth, not just hardware availability. The high price creates an immediate ceiling for adoption that the thin software library cannot justify.
2.  **Distribution Suicide:** Shipping only to "a few retailers" while excluding others guarantees retaliation. In a hardware business dependent on shelf space and promotional support, alienating the channel partner network before the main holiday season is catastrophic. Competitors will exploit this rift to secure exclusive placement later.
3.  **Developer Erosion:** Developers have not planned for the early date. Forcing a launch without their support results in a barren library, reinforcing the "hard to program" reputation. This creates a negative feedback loop: no games mean no sales, which means less developer interest for the full September window.
4.  **Strategic Mismatch:** The "head start" is illusory. Without mass market availability (retailers) and compelling software (developers), the early window generates negative word-of-mouth rather than momentum. The competitor launches in September with a lower price, full retail support, and a stronger lineup, effectively rendering the May launch a costly beta test that damaged brand equity.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price/Content Mismatch:** $399 price point with insufficient software inventory guarantees poor sell-through.
*   **Channel Alienation:** Excluding major retailers invites active sabotage or freezing out during the critical Q4 holiday season.
*   **Developer Readiness:** The technical difficulty and unplanned timeline ensure the software library remains critically thin.

**Risks:** Long-term brand damage as a "premium but empty" system; loss of retailer trust permanently.

**Kill Criteria:** If sell-through at selected retailers is below 50% in the first month, or if major excluded retailers publicly commit to the rival, the early launch strategy has failed.

DECISION: NO-GO
