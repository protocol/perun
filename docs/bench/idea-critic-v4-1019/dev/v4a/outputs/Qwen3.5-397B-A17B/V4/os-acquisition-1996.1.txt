First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on acquiring a commercially unproven asset at a premium to solve a problem the buyer has already failed to fix internally twice, introducing massive integration risk and personnel volatility without guaranteed market fit.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the small firm provides the only remaining viable modern core to halt market share collapse, leveraging the founder's unique vision despite past friction, whereas licensing dilutes differentiation and internal development has proven impossible.

**Options:**
1. **Acquire:** High cost, high integration risk, potential cultural toxicity, but offers full control and a modern foundation.
2. **License:** Lower upfront risk, faster deployment, but cedes strategic control and long-term margins to a competitor.
3. **Do Nothing:** Continued market share erosion leading to likely insolvency or fire-sale breakup.

**Fit:** Assumes the primary north star is survival via a differentiated product, not short-term cost containment.

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition fails because the technical debt of migrating the existing ecosystem overwhelms the small team, the founder's return triggers internal rebellion, and the "modern" OS lacks critical drivers, causing a delayed launch that misses the market window entirely. This is a one-way door; once bought, the capital is gone and the internal team demoralized.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal admits the target has "sold few units," meaning there is no market validation for the core asset. The price is "high against small revenue," indicating a speculative bet rather than an earnings-accretive move. The "difficult history" of the founder is a stated negative fact that the plan does not mitigate, posing a direct threat to execution. The alternative of licensing exists and reduces the specific risk of integration failure, even if it sacrifices some control.

**Blockers:**
1. **Commercial Validation:** The target has no proven market traction; buying it is buying a hypothesis, not a product.
2. **Execution Risk:** The buyer has failed twice internally; adding a tiny, unproven team and a volatile leader increases complexity without guaranteeing success.
3. **Cost/Benefit:** The premium price for negligible revenue violates basic capital allocation discipline without a concrete turnaround plan.

**Risks:** Migration failure, cultural implosion, delayed timeline, competitor licensing improvements.

The plan commits significant capital to an unproven asset with known personnel risks and no clear path to overcoming the buyer's historical inability to ship. The existence of a licensing alternative that avoids the integration and personnel minefield makes this specific acquisition path indefensible as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
