First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits a large share of the budget to an unproven format (full-season drop) and medium (online-only prestige) without the industry-standard pilot de-risking step, relying solely on internal data that has never predicted success in this specific category.

Steelman: The service's unique viewing data reveals a latent demand for binge-watched prestige content that traditional pilots cannot detect, justifying a massive bet to secure exclusive ownership and define the brand before licensing costs become unsustainable.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Continue rising licensing costs; eventual margin collapse.
2. **Pilot first:** Commission a single episode to test audience reaction and production quality before ordering full seasons; standard industry practice, lower upfront risk.
3. **Proposed plan:** 26 episodes, no pilot, all-at-once release; highest risk, highest potential reward.

Fit: Aligns with the need for owned IP but ignores the "startup" reality of the platform having zero track record in original production.

Pre-mortem: The show launches to critical indifference; the "all-at-once" model confuses users accustomed to weekly anticipation; the massive sunk cost of two seasons prevents cancellation or course correction, bankrupting the original content initiative. The data misinterpreted niche interest as mass appeal.

Evidence: The proposal cites "viewing data" but provides no specific correlation between past viewing habits and the willingness to pay for *original* drama. No online service has ever succeeded at this; the assumption that digital users want TV-quality drama is unproven. The cost is a "large share" of the budget, meaning failure is existential.

Blockers:
1. **No pilot:** The proposal explicitly removes the primary industry mechanism for de-risking high-cost drama. Without a pilot, there is no verified evidence of quality or audience retention for this specific asset.
2. **Unproven medium:** No online-only service has produced a prestige hit; assuming the medium supports the content is a guess, not a fact.
3. **Budget concentration:** Investing a "large share" of the budget in a single unproven hit violates basic portfolio theory for a company with no prior production success.

Risks: Audience rejection of the release model; star/director mismatch for serial TV; data misinterpretation.

The lack of a pilot is a fatal flaw for a first-time producer entering a high-cost category. The data may suggest interest, but it cannot validate execution quality or the viability of the release model without a tangible test.

DECISION: NO-GO
