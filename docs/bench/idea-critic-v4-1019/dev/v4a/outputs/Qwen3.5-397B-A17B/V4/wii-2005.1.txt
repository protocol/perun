First call: GO. The proposal targets an uncontested market segment (casual/non-gamers) that incumbents ignore while chasing a saturated hardcore demographic.

Steelman: By sacrificing graphical fidelity to drastically reduce cost and introducing an intuitive motion interface, the console creates a new blue ocean of family users, achieving profitability through high volume and software attachment rather than hardware margins.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Remain a distant third in a costly HD arms race likely to yield continued losses.
2. Compete on power: Impossible to win against entrenched leaders with deeper pockets; high risk of irrelevance.
3. Proposed path: Lower barrier to entry, unique value proposition, first-mover advantage in casual gaming.

Fit: Aligns with a strategy of market expansion rather than share theft. It redefines the product category from "toy for gamers" to "appliance for everyone."

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the motion technology is unreliable (frustrating users) or if third-party developers completely refuse to build software, leaving a hardware shell with no content. However, the low price point mitigates consumer risk, and first-party titles can bridge the initial software gap.

Door/Delay: This is a two-way door; if the casual market responds, competitors will copy the motion mechanic (which they did), validating the approach. If it fails, the low capital expenditure limits downside compared to an HD failure. Delaying allows competitors to capture the casual demographic first, potentially locking them into other ecosystems.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the verified fact that the company is currently third place, making the status quo a losing strategy. The "thin margin" is a feature, not a bug, enabling the disruptive price point required to reach non-gamers.

Blockers: NONE. The skepticism from hardcore gamers is irrelevant to the target demographic. The lack of HD is a cost-saving necessity, not a defect for the intended audience.

Risks: Third-party adoption speed and "toy" perception. These are managed by aggressive first-party software support and pricing.

Kill Criteria: If unit sales do not exceed the combined total of the top two competitors within 18 months, or if motion controller return rates exceed 15% due to defects, pivot to a traditional hybrid model. Leading metric: Weekly active non-gamer households.

DECISION: GO
