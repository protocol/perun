First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets a large share of the marketing budget on an unproven rookie with high injury risk and regulatory uncertainty, violating basic capital preservation principles for a public company.

**Steelman:** Secure a generational talent early to dominate basketball culture before rivals react, using controversy to drive demand.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain status quo; likely continue losing share safely.
2.  **Sign established star:** Higher cost, proven performance, lower injury/regulatory risk.
3.  **Proposed Rookie Deal:** Highest upside, but catastrophic downside if the player fails, gets injured, or the league bans the shoes immediately.

**Fit:** Assumes the company can absorb a total loss of this budget segment. Without verified cash reserves or board approval for "speculative gambling," this misaligns with fiduciary duty.

**Pre-mortem:** The player tears an ACL in preseason; the league fines the company daily for uniform violations, forcing a product recall; inventory sits unsold because the player never gains fame. The brand looks reckless, not revolutionary.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the player is "unproven," injury is a real threat, and rule-breaking invites fines. No data exists to prove this specific rookie will be an exception to the historical failure rate of signature shoes for unknowns. The financial exposure ("large share of marketing budget") is a verified blocker when weighed against the "unproven" status.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Capital Allocation:** Committing a "large share" of the budget to an asset with unquantified, high-probability failure modes (injury, obscurity) is financially irresponsible without a hedging strategy.
2.  **Regulatory Halt:** Building a line around rules the league explicitly forbids creates an immediate operational stopper; if the league enforces fines on day one, the product cannot be sold as designed.

**Risks:** Player underperformance, career-ending injury, brand reputation damage from regulatory fights.

**Decision:** The reliance on a single unproven variable for a dominant budget portion constitutes a blocker. The plan requires a capped pilot (e.g., a single regional release) or a reduced financial commitment before a full five-year, budget-dominating deal can be considered. As proposed, it is too brittle.

DECISION: NO-GO
