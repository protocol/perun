First call: NO-GO. Launching early with high price, scarce software, and alienated retailers contradicts the fundamental requirements for a successful console platform launch.

**Steelman:** An early May launch at $399 secures a four-month head start to establish mindshare before the competitor arrives, betting that being first outweighs having a complete ecosystem.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The proposal fails the "do nothing" (wait for September) test. Waiting allows time to secure game libraries, optimize the difficult 3D architecture, and align pricing with the rival's $299 expectation. The proposed alternative (early, expensive, sparse) offers a worse user experience at a higher cost.
*   **Cost/Risk:** $399 is a 33% premium over the rival. With few games and a hard-to-program machine, early adopters will face buyer's remorse.
*   **Channel Damage:** Shipping only to "a few retailers" guarantees retaliation from excluded major partners, poisoning distribution channels right when broad availability is needed most.
*   **Developer Reality:** Developers cannot magically produce titles for a hard-to-code machine four months early; this guarantees a software drought.

**Pre-mortem:** By Christmas 1995, the console is labeled an overpriced failure with no games. Major retailers refuse to stock it during the critical holiday season, handing the market entirely to the competitor who launched later with a better library and lower price.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Price Disadvantage:** The $100 premium over the rival is a factual blocker for mass adoption, especially without a superior game library to justify it.
2.  **Software Void:** The admission that "few games will be ready" and developers "did not plan" for this date means the core value proposition (games) is missing.
3.  **Channel Hostility:** Intentionally excluding retailers creates an immediate, solvable problem (distribution) that becomes a permanent barrier.

**Risks:** Brand reputation damage, developer churn, and irreversible loss of retail shelf space.

**Decision:** The plan relies on a "first mover" advantage that does not exist in hardware platforms without software support. The blockers are fatal to the launch's viability.

DECISION: NO-GO
