First call: NO-GO. The proposal demands high costs from price-sensitive users while stripping away the primary value drivers (apps, games) and forcing a costly data plan, creating an immediate economic mismatch.

Steelman: Launching a locked-down, carrier-exclusive social device leverages the acquired team's expertise to capture the youth market quickly before the main platform arrives, avoiding app-store complexity.

However, the attack on this claim reveals fatal blockers. First, the economic model is inverted: requiring a full smartphone data plan for a device costing as much as a smartphone itself, yet offering fewer features (no apps, no games), alienates the stated price-sensitive demographic. They will not pay a premium for a restricted experience. Second, the "no app store" constraint removes the primary utility of modern phones for this demographic, making the device a single-purpose tool at a multi-purpose price. Third, internal conflict between teams combined with existing delays suggests execution risk is high; launching two devices simultaneously (this and the main platform) splits focus and cannibalizes the upcoming major launch.

Alternatives: Doing nothing preserves capital and avoids brand confusion until the main platform launches. A cheaper alternative would be a software-only social layer on existing low-cost devices, removing hardware risk entirely.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, carriers refuse to subsidize the high data plan requirement, youth reject the high monthly cost for a "dumb" smartphone, and the internal conflict causes missed marketing windows. The result is unsold inventory and a damaged carrier relationship.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the target is price-sensitive, the data plan is expensive, and there are no apps. These facts contradict the core value proposition.

Blockers:
1. Economic mismatch: Price-sensitive users cannot/will not pay smartphone data prices for a non-smartphone experience.
2. Value deficit: No apps/games removes the primary reason teens buy phones.
3. Strategic conflict: Cannibalizes the main platform launching later same year.

Risks: Internal team conflict causing further delays; carrier rejection of restrictive plans.

Kill criteria: If pre-orders do not cover 50% of manufacturing costs within 30 days of announcement, halt production. Leading metric: Carrier commitment level.

DECISION: NO-GO
