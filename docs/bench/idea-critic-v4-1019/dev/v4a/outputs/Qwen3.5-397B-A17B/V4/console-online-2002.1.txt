First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates broadband and a paid subscription in 2002, when broadband penetration is critically low and the market expects free online play, creating an immediate adoption barrier that contradicts the goal of building a player base.

**Steelman:** A unified, premium online service creates a sticky ecosystem and recurring revenue, differentiating the console through superior reliability and social features that fragmented, free alternatives cannot match.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Rely on publishers; zero cost, zero differentiation, high risk of fragmented user experience.
2.  **Free tier + Paid premium:** Offer basic matchmaking/free identity to build scale, charge for voice/storage; balances adoption with revenue.
3.  **Proposed (Paid-only):** Maximum revenue per user, but severely caps total addressable market given 2002 infrastructure constraints.

**Fit:** Assumes the strategy is premium differentiation over mass market share. If the north star is install base growth, this fails.

**Pre-mortem:** The service launches to empty servers because the install base is too small to sustain matchmaking queues, causing early adopters to churn. The company burns cash on underutilized hosting operations it lacks experience running, forcing a price cut later that alienates early payers.

**Evidence Check:** The proposal admits "few homes have broadband" and "players expect online play to be free." These are not risks; they are current market facts that directly contradict the mandatory nature of the proposal. The cost of delay is losing first-mover advantage on infrastructure, but the cost of premature launch is brand damage and operational failure.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Infrastructure Mismatch:** Mandatory broadband in 2002 excludes the vast majority of the console's potential owners (fact stated in proposal).
2.  **Pricing Friction:** Charging for a utility competitors offer free, while the user base is price-sensitive and infrastructure-limited, prevents the network effects required for matchmaking to function.

**Risks:** Operational inexperience in hosting; high support costs; slow adoption.

**Decision:** The plan fails as proposed because its mandatory requirements (broadband, payment) exclude the critical mass needed to make the service viable. It must be revised to a hybrid model or delayed until broadband penetration reaches a viable threshold.

DECISION: NO-GO
