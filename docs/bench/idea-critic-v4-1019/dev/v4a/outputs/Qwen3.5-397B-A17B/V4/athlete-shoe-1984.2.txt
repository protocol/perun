First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets a large share of the marketing budget on an unproven rookie with no professional track record, violating standard risk management for a market leader.

**Steelman:** Secure a generational talent before competitors recognize his value, using controversy (rule-breaking colorways) to generate massive free media attention that outweighs the cost of fines and the risk of injury.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain status quo; likely continue losing basketball market share safely.
2.  **Veteran endorsement:** Sign an established star; higher upfront cost, proven performance, lower risk, less viral potential.
3.  **Proposed Rookie Deal:** High risk of total loss (injury/flop), but unique upside in brand transformation and cultural relevance.

**Fit:** Assumes the brand needs a radical identity shift to win in basketball, not just incremental share. This aligns with "losing ground" but assumes a rookie can drive this shift immediately.

**Pre-mortem:** The player suffers a career-ending injury in the first season. The company is left with $500k+ in sunk costs (1984 dollars), unsellable inventory, and no spokesperson. Alternatively, the league bans the shoes entirely, and the novelty fades without sales volume to sustain the line.

**Evidence:** No data exists on this specific player's professional viability (he hasn't played). The claim that "signature shoes rarely sell beyond a season" is a stated fact in the proposal that directly contradicts the long-term revenue model required to justify a five-year deal.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Contradictory Premise:** The proposal admits signature shoes rarely last a season, yet commits to a five-year term. Without evidence this specific player breaks that historical trend *before* signing, the math fails.
2.  **Unverified Capacity:** Betting a "large share" of the budget on an unproven asset lacks a kill switch for injury or underperformance.

**Risks:** Injury, league ban, consumer rejection of an unknown player.

**Decision:** The historical failure rate of signature lines combined with the total lack of professional data creates an unjustifiable blocker. The five-year commitment cannot be supported by the "rookie" status.

DECISION: NO-GO
