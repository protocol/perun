First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines maximum engineering risk with maximum business risk (alienating the core community) while eliminating the safety net of a rollback.

Steelman: A complete rewrite is necessary to modernize the architecture and pivot toward publisher content to reverse traffic decline, requiring an immediate, total migration to realize value.

Attack:
1.  **Community Alienation:** The core asset is the loyal community that values existing voting mechanics. Changing these mechanics while simultaneously favoring publishers (potentially at the expense of user-submitted content) directly attacks the community's identity. In 2010, social news sites lived or died by network effects; alienating the base invites immediate migration to the growing rival.
2.  **Engineering Risk vs. Traffic Reality:** Traffic is already slowing. A "from scratch" rewrite introduces a long period of zero feature development and high instability risk. If the rewrite slips or bugs emerge, the declining traffic trajectory accelerates into a death spiral before the new version even launches.
3.  **The Rollback Trap:** Moving "all users at once" with "no rollback" is a single point of failure. If the new algorithm depresses engagement or the rewrite contains critical performance bugs, there is no escape hatch. The site dies on launch day.
4.  **Better Way:** The proposal ignores staged deployment. A/B testing the new surfacing logic on a subset of traffic, or launching the new design to new users only while keeping legacy users on the stable platform, would validate the "publisher favor" hypothesis without betting the entire company.

Blockers:
-   **No Rollback:** The explicit removal of the ability to revert makes this a suicide pact if the new logic fails.
-   **Contradictory Incentives:** The plan relies on the community that values the old mechanics to adopt a system designed to de-prioritize their preferred content style in favor of publishers.
-   **Timing:** Executing a high-risk rewrite while traffic is already falling and a rival is growing violates basic crisis management; speed and stability are required, not a ground-up rebuild.

Risks: Total community exodus to the rival; engineering timeline slippage causing further traffic decay; unproven assumption that publisher-focused content will drive growth for this specific user base.

DECISION: NO-GO
