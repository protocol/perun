First call: NO-GO. The price is extreme, the target's accounts are disputed, and the buyer lacks the core competency to integrate it.

Steelman: This acquisition is the only viable path to instantly pivot a hardware giant into enterprise analytics, bypassing years of slow organic build-out.

Options:
1. **Acquire:** Highest cost ($11.7B), highest risk (integration failure, accounting fraud), lowest reversibility (divestiture takes years).
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, maintains status quo, misses the software shift window.
3. **Partner/Build:** Lower cost, slower entry, fully reversible, avoids buying unknown liabilities.

Fit: While the strategic direction (software pivot) aligns with the new CEO's vision, the specific mechanism (buying this distressed target at a 64% premium) contradicts basic financial prudence and the company's current capabilities.

Pre-mortem: The deal closes. Six months later, an internal audit reveals the accounting irregularities suspected since 2009 were material. The hardware culture clashes with the software team, causing mass exodus. The $11.7B becomes a massive write-down, destroying shareholder value and forcing a fire sale. This is a one-way door; once closed, the capital is gone.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the finance chief has reservations, the press has questioned reporting since 2009, and the buyer has no software experience. The premium (11x revenue) is unjustified without verified, clean books.

Blockers:
1. **Unverified Financials:** Buying a company with known, long-standing accounting questions at a massive premium is a factual failure of due diligence. The "few weeks" timeline is insufficient to cleanse these risks.
2. **Capability Gap:** A hardware-only organization cannot successfully integrate a complex analytics firm without a proven leadership bridge, which is absent.
3. **Price vs. Value:** The 64% premium assumes perfect execution and clean books; both are actively disputed.

Risks: Cultural rejection, technology mismatch, regulatory scrutiny, overpayment even if books are clean.

The combination of disputed financial integrity, an extreme premium, and zero buyer experience creates an insurmountable barrier. The plan fails as proposed because it requires believing the very numbers under suspicion.

DECISION: NO-GO
