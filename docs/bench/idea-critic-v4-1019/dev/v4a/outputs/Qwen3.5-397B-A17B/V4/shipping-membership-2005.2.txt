First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits unproven economics and a large short-term margin hit without defining the capital runway or the specific unit economics required to sustain the loss leader strategy.

**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee creates immediate cash flow and locks in customer loyalty, increasing lifetime value enough to offset per-order shipping losses through volume and reduced marketing spend.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** The core admission is that "economics are unproven." Launching a structural cost change without a validated model where Lifetime Value (LTV) exceeds Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) plus shipping subsidies is financial negligence. If heavy buyers increase frequency such that shipping costs exceed the $79 fee, every additional order deepens the loss.
2.  **Irreversibility:** The proposal correctly identifies the offer is "hard to reverse." Unlike a temporary promotion, selling an annual contract creates a legal and reputational lock-in. If the model fails in month two, the company cannot stop the bleeding without refunding members and destroying trust.
3.  **Margin Hit:** A "large short-term margin hit" requires verified cash reserves. Without evidence of available capital to absorb months of negative unit economics while waiting for retention benefits to materialize, this threatens solvency.
4.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option preserves current margins. A "pilot" option (e.g., invite-only or regional test) would validate the "heavy buyer" behavior hypothesis before a full launch, directly addressing the "unproven" status. The proposal skips this validation step.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Economics:** The proposal explicitly states the financial model is unproven. Launching a company-wide pricing structure on an unproven model is a definitive failure condition.
*   **Missing Kill Criteria:** There is no defined metric (e.g., "membership uptake > X%") or date to stop the program if losses exceed a specific cap.

**Risks:**
*   Cash flow exhaustion due to higher-than-anticipated order frequency.
*   Inability to pivot quickly due to annual contractual obligations.

**Decision:**
The combination of unproven economics, high irreversibility, and admitted large margin hits constitutes a blocker. The plan must first prove the unit economics via a constrained pilot with pre-defined stop-loss metrics before a general launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
