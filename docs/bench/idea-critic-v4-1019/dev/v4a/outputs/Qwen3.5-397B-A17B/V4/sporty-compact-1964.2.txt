First call: GO. The demographic tailwind (postwar boom entering market) combined with an unmet desire (sporty styling at low cost) creates a clear opportunity. The proposed solution leverages existing assets (compact car mechanics) to minimize development risk and cost, while the long options list protects margins and allows customization.

**Steelman:** Launch a visually distinct, customizable sporty car using proven economy underpinnings to capture a new demographic segment at a low entry price, supported by massive awareness marketing.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Skeptic:** The primary risk is brand dilution ("looking cheap") and unproven demand. However, the "long hood, short deck" styling specifically counters the economy aesthetic, creating a psychological break from the donor car. The unproven demand is mitigated by the low base price; if volume fails, the financial exposure is capped by shared tooling.
2.  **Better Way:** Doing nothing cedes the emerging youth segment to competitors. Building a new platform from scratch would destroy the low-price advantage and delay launch. The proposed "parts bin" approach is the only viable path to the target price point.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because the marketing failed to separate the car's image from its economy roots, or the options pricing alienated buyers. The heavy ad spend is a two-way door; it can be paused if early indicators sour, but cannot be effective if delayed (cost of delay is losing the cultural moment).

**Blockers:** None. The risks cited are inherent to innovation but are structurally managed by the low-cost foundation and high-margin options strategy. The "unproven demand" is a hypothesis worth testing given the low downside of reusing mechanical parts.

**Kill Criteria:** If first-year sales do not cover the marginal marketing spend, or if the option take-rate falls below 40% (indicating buyers want a cheap car, not a customized one), the program should pivot to a standard trim model or cease production.

**Decision:** The proposal aligns perfectly with the demographic shift and utilizes a capital-efficient strategy. The risks are acceptable given the potential to define a new market category.

DECISION: GO
