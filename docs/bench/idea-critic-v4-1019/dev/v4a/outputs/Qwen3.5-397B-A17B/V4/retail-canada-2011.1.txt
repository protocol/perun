First call: NO-GO. The plan attempts to solve three hard problems simultaneously (new country, new supply chain, new IT system) at maximum speed, creating a single point of failure for the entire expansion.

**Steelman:** Rapidly securing 130+ sites creates an immediate scale advantage against the entrenched competitor, leveraging the retailer's domestic loyalty before prices adjust.

**Attack:**
1.  **Compound Risk:** The proposal couples unproven cross-border logistics with a brand-new inventory system. If the IT system fails to handle bilingual labeling or cross-border tax rules, the physical stores cannot open or will stock incorrectly. Testing this system in a live, high-velocity rollout is reckless; a bug could halt 150 locations simultaneously.
2.  **Pace vs. Learning:** Opening "nearly all" stores within two years eliminates the ability to learn from early mistakes. The first 10 stores will reveal critical flaws in logistics and price perception. Rolling out the remaining 140 before fixing these flaws locks the retailer into expensive leases for underperforming assets.
3.  **Price Perception:** The market has higher prices on some goods, but the retailer's "discount" model relies on volume and specific supply chains. Without a proven local supply chain, they cannot guarantee the low prices required to dislodge the incumbent, risking a brand mismatch where they are perceived as "cheap but expensive."
4.  **Alternative:** A phased approach (10–15 stores) allows validation of the new inventory system and supply chain with limited lease liability. This cheaper alternative reaches the same strategic upside (market entry) with significantly lower ruin risk.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven System at Scale:** Deploying a new, untested inventory management system across 130+ sites on day one is a factual recipe for operational paralysis.
*   **Irreversible Lease Commitment:** Signing leases for 150 sites before validating the unit economics in the new regulatory environment creates massive sunk costs before product-market fit is confirmed.

**Risks:** Competitor price war, supply chain delays, regulatory fines on labeling.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. The dependence on an unproven IT system and the refusal to phase the rollout creates an unacceptable probability of total expansion failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
