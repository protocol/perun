First call: NO-GO. Launching a premium-priced tablet with a tiny app catalog against a dominant incumbent, while the OS has negligible share and leadership is unstable, ignores the primary driver of tablet adoption: ecosystem depth.

**Steelman:** The brand's hardware prestige can temporarily offset OS weakness if marketing convinces users the device is essential, buying time for developers to catch up.

**Attack:**
1.  **Ecosystem Gap:** Tablets are consumption devices driven by available software. A "small fraction" of the leader's catalog means missing critical apps users expect. Price parity removes the only logical reason to accept this deficit.
2.  **Developer Chicken-and-Egg:** Heavy outreach fails without installed base; installed base fails without apps. Paying developers to port apps to a niche OS with transitioning leadership is a capital incinerator with low conversion probability.
3.  **Leadership Void:** Internal transition creates strategic drift. Executing a complex two-sided market launch (users + devs) requires absolute executive focus, which is currently absent.
4.  **Perception:** "Unproven" status at a premium price point signals high risk to consumers. Without a clear, cheaper entry point or a killer exclusive feature, the value proposition is negative.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserve capital, wait for OS maturity or leadership stabilization.
*   *Low-Cost Entry:* Undercut the leader significantly to force adoption, accepting hardware losses to build user base for developers.
*   *Niche Focus:* Target a specific vertical where the small catalog is sufficient, rather than a general consumer launch.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, marketing spends heavily, early adopters buy, but churn immediately upon realizing key apps are missing. Developers see low engagement and stop porting. The product becomes a shelf-ware failure, damaging the brand's premium reputation.

**Blockers:**
*   Price parity with a superior ecosystem makes the product irrational for consumers.
*   Leadership transition prevents the sustained focus required to solve the ecosystem gap.

**Risks:** Brand damage, wasted marketing spend, developer disillusionment.

**Conclusion:** The proposal asks to fight a fortress war with a skirmish force at full price. The mismatch between price, ecosystem size, and market reality is fatal.

DECISION: NO-GO
