First call: GO. The proposal converts an invisible commodity into a demanded brand asset, shifting power from computer makers to the chip supplier.

**Steelman:** By subsidizing OEM ads that feature the chip's logo, the company creates consumer pull that forces computer makers to adopt the chip to sell their own units, overcoming the "clone" problem.

**Alternatives:**
1.  *Do nothing:* Continue competing on price/specs alone; clones erode margins as buyers remain ignorant.
2.  *Direct B2B sales:* Fails because buyers don't know what they are buying; sales teams cannot reach the end-user.
3.  *Solo advertising:* Wastes budget without OEM channel integration; lacks the "seal of approval" context.
The proposed co-op model beats these by leveraging existing OEM spend to educate the market.

**Fit:** Assumes the goal is margin protection and differentiation in a commoditizing market. If the north star is purely short-term profit, this fails; if it is long-term pricing power, it fits perfectly.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if OEMs refuse the branding guidelines or if consumers ignore the logo. However, the financial incentive (subsidy) aligns OEM interests, making refusal costly. The risk of consumer indifference is real but untestable without launch; the cost of delay is ceding the "quality" narrative to clones.

**Evidence & Math:** The proposal cites "unprecedented" risk, but unprecedented moves often capture category definition. The budget share is high, but the alternative is losing the entire premium segment to clones. No specific numbers contradict the logic; the mechanism (subsidy for compliance) is sound.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (resentment, unproven channel) are execution hurdles, not factual contradictions. The "unprecedented" nature is a feature, not a bug, in a stagnating commodity market.
**Risks:** OEM pushback on creative control; consumer apathy. These become kill criteria: if OEM adoption <60% in Year 1 or brand recall <20%, pivot.

**Decision:** The plan addresses the root cause (buyer ignorance) with a mechanism (financial alignment) that bypasses the blocker (OEM gatekeeping).

DECISION: GO
