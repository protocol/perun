First call: NO-GO. The price is extreme relative to output, and key risks are unresolved facts, not manageable uncertainties.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio secures a hit-making engine and prevents rival alignment, justifying a premium if the pipeline can scale beyond one film/year under new leadership.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Lose the partner to a rival; hand-drawn unit continues underperforming. High strategic risk, zero cost.
2.  **Renegotiate limited deal:** Secure distribution rights for 2-3 films without full acquisition. Lower cost, lower control, avoids culture clash.
3.  **Acquire (Proposed):** Total control, high cost ($7.4B), massive integration risk.

**Fit:** Aligns with needing hits, but assumes the studio's success is transferable and scalable, which is unproven.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal fails because the creative leaders leave due to culture clash, or the pipeline stalls at one film/year, making the $7.4B price tag indefensible. The founder uses their new shareholder status to block necessary changes. This is a **one-way door**; reversal means selling at a loss.

**Evidence & Math:** At $7.4B for ~1 film/year, the implied valuation per film is astronomical compared to historical averages. No evidence suggests the studio can reliably produce 2-3 hits/year immediately to justify the cash flow. The "economics" that broke talks likely highlight this exact disparity.

**Blocker Test:**
*   **Blockers:** The math does not work on current output. One film/year cannot service a $7.4B equity swap without assuming unproven scaling. The risk of the founder (now a major shareholder) obstructing integration is a structural flaw in the deal terms, not a mere "risk."
*   **Risks:** Culture clash, talent retention, single-point failure (creative leaders).

**Decision:** The proposal relies on unproven assumptions about scaling production and retaining talent post-acquisition. The price creates a fatal sensitivity to any execution error. A cheaper, staged partnership or a lower offer with earn-outs based on actual multi-film output is required before an outright buy.

DECISION: NO-GO
