First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a computer and music player maker with zero phone experience to enter a carrier-dominated market with an unproven, keyboard-less, premium device, relying on an unprecedented revenue-share deal and exclusivity that limits reach.

Steelman: A converged device leveraging existing music dominance can redefine the category if carriers cede control, but the lack of industry experience, absence of a physical keyboard, and high price point create immediate adoption barriers.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Protect current player sales but risk erosion as competitors add music to phones.
2. License OS to multiple carriers: Faster reach but loses design control and revenue share.
3. Proposed plan: High risk/high reward; requires breaking carrier norms and consumer habits.

Fit: Assumes the "north star" is converging communication and media under user control, not just selling hardware. However, the assumption that a single carrier will accept a revenue-share model without proven phone sales is unverified.

Pre-mortem: The device fails because business users reject the lack of a keyboard, the premium price limits volume, and the exclusive carrier deal prevents critical mass. The company burns capital trying to educate a market not ready for a touchscreen-only interface.

Evidence: All claims regarding "no experience," "keyboard-led market," and "carrier dictate" are stated as facts in the proposal. The revenue-share model is an assumption with no precedent cited.

Blockers:
1. **Unproven Economic Model:** The proposal hinges on a carrier accepting a revenue-share deal for a company with no phone track record. No evidence suggests a carrier will agree to these terms for an unproven form factor.
2. **Market Mismatch:** The primary market (business) demands physical keyboards; the proposed device explicitly removes this. Entering a market against its dominant feature without a bridge strategy is a fatal flaw.
3. **Zero Ecosystem:** Launching without third-party applications limits utility compared to emerging smart devices, making the premium price harder to justify.

Risks: Consumer confusion over touch-only input; exclusivity stifling growth; rapid competitor response with hybrid devices.

The lack of phone experience combined with a requirement to overturn established carrier economics and consumer hardware preferences presents insurmountable blockers as proposed. The revenue-share assumption is unsupported, and the keyboard omission ignores the stated market leader's core feature.

DECISION: NO-GO
