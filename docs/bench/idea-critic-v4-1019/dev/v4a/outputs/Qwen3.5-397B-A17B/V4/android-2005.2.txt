First call: NO-GO. A search-ad firm with no hardware DNA paying $50M+ for a 6-person secret OS project in 2005 contradicts core competency and market reality.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the only viable open-source mobile OS early secures the future gateway to search, preventing a competitor from locking down mobile traffic before it exists.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Capability:** The acquirer sells ads, not operating systems. They lack the distribution, carrier relationships, and hardware partnerships required to make an OS succeed. Building or buying an OS is a decade-long infrastructure play, not a quick search expansion.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" and wait for the market to mature is cheaper and safer. Partnering with existing handset makers to pre-install search bars costs fractions of this amount and reaches users immediately without the burden of OS maintenance.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 2010. Cause: Carriers rejected the unknown platform; handset makers refused to adopt a rival's OS; the team burned cash building features no one used while the core search business stagnated. The "open source" angle fails if no major player adopts it as a standard.
4.  **Evidence Gap:** The proposal admits *no revenue model*, *no adoption*, and *carrier resistance*. These are not risks; they are current facts that invalidate the business case. The $50M price tag for six people is unjustified without a single commercial contract or clear path to distribution.
5.  **Blocker:** The core claim relies on "free software pushing web use," but without a distribution mechanism (which the acquirer lacks), the OS remains invisible. You cannot monetize search on a phone nobody has.

**Blockers:**
*   No distribution channel (carriers/handset makers) exists or is willing to adopt.
*   Zero revenue model or path to profitability defined.
*   Strategic mismatch: Ad company cannot operate an OS platform effectively.

**Risks:** Multi-year cash burn, distraction from core search business, total loss of $50M if the platform fails to gain traction (likely).

The proposal asks to bet the farm on a hypothesis that contradicts the company's strengths and the market's gatekeepers. The lack of a distribution strategy is fatal.

DECISION: NO-GO
