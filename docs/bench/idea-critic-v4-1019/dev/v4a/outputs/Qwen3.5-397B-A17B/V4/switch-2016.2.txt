First call: GO. The hybrid concept directly addresses the erosion of the handheld line by phones and the failure of the previous home-only console by merging both markets into a single device, leveraging unique first-party IP to offset hardware deficits.

**Steelman:** A hybrid device leverages existing strong IP to create a unique market category where portability and home play are not mutually exclusive, allowing the company to dominate a niche rivals cannot touch without cannibalizing their own high-power lines.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Handheld line dies to phones; home console reputation remains toxic. Certain decline.
2.  **High-power home console only:** Repeats previous failure; ignores mobile erosion; high financial risk.
3.  **Hybrid (Proposed):** Captures both use cases. Risks: weak specs, supply issues. Upside: Market creation.
The proposal beats "do nothing" because the status quo is existential decay. It beats a pure home console by solving the specific cause of the previous loss (market mismatch) while mitigating mobile erosion.

**Fit:** Aligns with the necessity to stop financial bleeding and reclaim relevance. The "confusing positioning" risk is mitigated by the clear value proposition: "play anywhere."

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if third-party support collapses entirely, leaving only a thin first-party lineup that cannot sustain hardware sales. However, the unique form factor creates a "must-have" for the installed base regardless of third-party breadth. This is a two-way door; if it fails, the company retreats to software-only or licensed hardware, but the current trajectory guarantees failure anyway. Cost of delay is total loss of the handheld segment.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on the verified fact that the previous home console failed and phones are eroding handhelds. The "modest-power" constraint is a feature, not a bug, enabling the hybrid form factor and price parity.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks (specs, supply, third-party) are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The alternative (status quo) is proven failure. The "thin lineup" is a known starting condition, not a blocker, provided the first-party titles are strong (assumed given the company's history).

**Risks:** Supply constraints at launch; developer hesitation due to non-standard architecture. These become kill criteria: if supply cannot meet pre-order demand within 3 months, or if key third-party partners do not commit by launch, the strategy requires immediate pivot.

**Decision:** The unique value proposition outweighs the hardware deficit. The market needs differentiation, not another power war.

DECISION: GO
