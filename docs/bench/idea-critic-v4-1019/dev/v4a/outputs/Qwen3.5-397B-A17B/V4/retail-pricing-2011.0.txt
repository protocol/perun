First call: NO-GO. The proposal eliminates the primary purchasing trigger (discounts) for the core demographic without evidence they will accept everyday pricing, while simultaneously imposing massive operational friction via store rebuilds.

Steelman: Eliminating coupon complexity and modernizing the store layout will attract new, higher-margin shoppers and restore brand dignity, offsetting the loss of promo-driven volume.

Attack: The plan fails the "fit" and "evidence" checks. The customer base is explicitly defined as "older" and "coupon-driven," with most sales historically occurring at a discount. Removing discounts removes the reason this specific cohort shops. The proposed replacement—branded in-store shops—is a capital-intensive strategy that worked for electronics but assumes department store shoppers value boutique experiences over price. There is zero evidence provided that this demographic desires this shift. Furthermore, executing a total chain-wide transformation without a pilot ignores the massive execution risk. If the pricing strategy fails, the company has already burned cash on physical renovations, leaving no runway to pivot.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retains current bleeding but preserves cash.
2. Pilot program: Test everyday pricing in 5-10 stores to validate demand elasticity before chain-wide rollout.
3. Hybrid model: Reduce coupon frequency gradually while introducing limited branded sections, measuring churn.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-launch, sales collapse as loyal customers defect to competitors offering coupons, while new target shoppers do not arrive in sufficient numbers to cover the margin loss. The company faces liquidity crisis due to unrecoverable renovation costs.

Blockers:
1. Core value proposition mismatch: The plan removes the sole known driver of sales for the existing base without verified demand from a new base.
2. No pilot: The scale of change (pricing + physical layout) creates an irreversible bet without data.

Risks: Brand alienation, cash flow exhaustion, competitor exploitation of the price gap.

Kill Criteria: If a 3-store pilot shows a >15% drop in transaction volume within 90 days, halt expansion and revert pricing.

The lack of a pilot and the direct contradiction of established customer behavior constitute a fatal flaw.

DECISION: NO-GO
