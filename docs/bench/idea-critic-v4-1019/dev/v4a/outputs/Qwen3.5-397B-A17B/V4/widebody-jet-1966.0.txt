First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a capital outlay exceeding all prior experience based on a single customer order, while facing existential technological displacement from supersonic rivals and unproven propulsion.

Steelman: Building a massive, convertible wide-body jet secures the dominant market position against cost pressures and future freight needs before competitors react.

However, the blocker test fails immediately. The financial risk is absolute: the development and factory costs are unprecedented for the firm, creating a single-point failure mode where any delay or cancellation bankrupts the company. Relying on one airline for such scale violates basic portfolio theory; if that carrier falters or cancels, the project collapses with no other buyers lined up.

The technological risks are equally fatal. The high-bypass engine is unproven; betting the company's future on a supplier's experimental powerplant introduces a critical dependency outside direct control. Furthermore, the strategic timing is suspect. With the industry expecting supersonic travel to replace subsonic jets, investing billions in a larger version of "obsolete" technology could render the asset worthless before it pays back. The "convertible to freighter" feature is a hedge, not a guarantee of viability if passenger demand shifts entirely to speed.

Alternatives like upgrading current models or waiting for supersonic trends to clarify offer lower risk. The "do nothing" option preserves capital until the engine technology matures or supersonic viability is proven. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of failure.

Blockers:
1. Financial exposure exceeds all historical precedents with only one customer committed.
2. Critical dependency on an unproven engine technology from a third party.
3. Strategic misalignment if supersonic travel renders subsonic giants obsolete.

Risks:
1. Market shift to supersonic travel.
2. Single-customer dependency.
3. Execution failure on unprecedented factory scale.

No kill criteria or phased approach is offered to mitigate these existential threats. The proposal demands an irreversible, all-in bet on uncertain technology and demand.

DECISION: NO-GO
