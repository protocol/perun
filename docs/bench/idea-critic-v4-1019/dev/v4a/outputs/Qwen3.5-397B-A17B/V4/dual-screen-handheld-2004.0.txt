First call: GO. The proposal avoids a direct hardware arms race against a well-funded rival by leveraging asymmetric innovation (dual screens, touch) to access an untapped market segment (broad audience) rather than fighting for core gamers on graphics alone.

**Steelman:** Differentiate via unique interaction models and price to expand the total addressable market, using the legacy device to maintain core gamer satisfaction while the new device courts casual users.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Cedes the narrative to the rival's superior specs; risks long-term irrelevance as graphics become the sole differentiator.
2.  **Match Specs:** Likely fails due to cost constraints and entering a "me-too" battle against a stronger financial position.
3.  **The Gimmick Risk:** While developers may struggle initially, the lower price point and novel input method create a "blue ocean" where software novelty outweighs graphical fidelity. The risk of confusion is mitigated by keeping the older handheld on sale for traditionalists.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with a strategy of market expansion over specification dominance. If this fails, it will be because first-party software fails to demonstrate the touch screen's utility, not because the hardware is weak. The cost of delay is high; waiting allows the rival to solidify the definition of "next-gen" around graphics alone.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The facts provided show no contradiction in the plan's logic; the lower price is achievable by not matching the rival's expensive graphics silicon.
*   **Risks:** Developer adoption, unproven touch mechanics, and potential perception as a toy. These are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. They are addressed by the dual-device strategy (keeping the old unit) and the focus on a broader audience less concerned with raw polygon counts.

The proposal successfully identifies that winning requires changing the game, not playing the rival's game better. The risks are manageable trade-offs for the potential of market expansion.

DECISION: GO
