First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a scale deficit by acquiring a second declining asset, compounding execution risk without addressing the core lack of capability.

The steelman is that combining two struggling media properties creates sufficient scale to challenge duopoly leaders. However, this ignores the fundamental mismatch: the buyer is a wireless carrier with zero history in consumer media, attempting to integrate a target with falling traffic, shrinking ad revenue, and unstable management. Scale in a declining market does not create competitiveness; it merely aggregates decay.

Alternatives: Doing nothing preserves capital for organic mobile initiatives or assets with growth trajectories. Investing in first-party data or specific technology stacks offers a cheaper, more reversible path than a $4.8 billion integration nightmare. The "cheaper" alternative here is avoiding the purchase entirely, as the integration costs of merging two distressed cultures and tech stacks will likely exceed the purchase price.

Fit is poor. The strategy relies on becoming a media giant through acquisition while lacking the operational DNA to run media businesses. The target's repeated management changes signal deep structural issues that a carrier is ill-equipped to fix.

Pre-mortem: One year post-close, the combined entity reports a $2 billion write-down. Ad revenue continues to slide as key talent flees the cultural clash between telecom engineering and media creativity. The integration distracts leadership from the core wireless business, causing subscriber churn. This is a one-way door; exiting requires selling at a massive loss.

Evidence: The proposal admits the target's revenue and traffic are falling for years. Buying a falling knife to fix a lack of experience is a logical contradiction. The $4.8 billion price tag assumes stability that the facts explicitly deny.

Blockers: The buyer has no history in consumer media, and the target is in structural decline with unstable leadership. These facts guarantee integration failure and continued revenue erosion.
Risks: Massive cultural clash, accelerated talent departure, and inability to monetize mobile effectively.

DECISION: NO-GO
