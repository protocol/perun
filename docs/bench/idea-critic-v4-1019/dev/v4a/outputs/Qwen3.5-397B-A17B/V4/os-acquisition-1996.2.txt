First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on acquiring an unproven, low-revenue asset to solve a critical, existential failure where two prior internal attempts have already collapsed, introducing massive integration risk and personnel conflict for a high price.

Steelman: Acquiring a modern, object-oriented core is the only viable shortcut to replace a dated OS after two failed internal attempts, leveraging external innovation to halt market share loss despite integration challenges.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Financial Mismatch:** The target has "few units" sold and "small revenue," yet the cost is "a few hundred million dollars." This valuation lacks fundamental justification based on current traction, representing a massive overpayment for unproven technology.
2.  **Execution Risk:** Migration of existing software and developers to a new core is explicitly identified as risky. Combined with the target's lack of market success, the technical viability at scale is unproven.
3.  **Personnel Liability:** The founder's "difficult history" with the company introduces a high-probability cultural and operational conflict that could derail the advisory role essential for success.

**Risks:**
*   Licensing another vendor's system remains a cheaper, lower-risk alternative that avoids integration debt and personnel drama.
*   The acquired technology may not scale to the hardware maker's volume, repeating previous failures.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" test. Licensing exists as a stated alternative that likely offers a modern core without the acquisition premium, integration burden, or toxic personnel dynamics. The "do nothing" option is fatal due to falling market share, but this specific path bets the company on an overpriced, unproven asset managed by a contentious figure. The financial premise (high price vs. small revenue) is a factual blocker; the plan does not explain how the value is created post-acquisition to justify the multiple. The personnel risk is a known negative that the plan attempts to mitigate only with an "advisor" title, which is insufficient given the history.

A sound plan must either justify the valuation through proprietary technical advantage not available via licensing or remove the personnel blocker. As proposed, it exchanges a slow decline for a high-cost, high-conflict gamble with unproven technology.

DECISION: NO-GO
