First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market late, abandons a strategic standard (copy-protection) that alienates existing partners (labels, device makers), and offers no price advantage against a dominant incumbent with high switching costs.

Steelman: A well-funded entrant can disrupt a complacent leader by offering superior hardware (screen, wireless sharing) and a seamless ecosystem, betting that user experience trumps legacy DRM and market share.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Avoids massive capex and partner backlash; preserves status quo but cedes the digital music future to the incumbent.
2. **Licensing/Software only:** License the software to existing device makers; lower risk, no hardware cost, but less control over UX and revenue.
3. **The Proposal:** High cost, high risk of partner revolt, requires building hardware, store, and content deals from scratch.

Fit: Assuming the goal is market leadership, this conflicts with the reality of partner dependency. Dropping the industry-standard DRM unilaterally risks label boycotts, leaving the store empty.

Pre-mortem: The device launches, but major labels refuse to license content due to the lack of copy-protection, citing fear of unchecked sharing. The "wireless sharing" feature becomes a liability, not a feature. The marketing budget burns cash acquiring users who find an empty or limited store. The company is stuck with unsold hardware and no content pipeline.

Evidence: The prompt states the company is "late to market," the leader has an "enormous share," and there is "no clear price advantage." Crucially, the proposal explicitly drops the copy-protection standard partners rely on.

Blockers:
1. **Content Availability:** Dropping the industry copy-protection standard without a guaranteed alternative creates a high probability that record labels will restrict or deny content licensing, rendering the store non-viable.
2. **Late Entry:** Entering late with no price advantage and no unique content exclusivity against a loyal ecosystem offers no clear path to critical mass.

Risks: Marketing inefficacy, hardware supply chain failures, rapid incumbent retaliation.

The lack of a content strategy that satisfies label security requirements is a fatal flaw. Without music, the player is useless.

DECISION: NO-GO
