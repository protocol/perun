First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits irreversible capital before validating the core business model in a single market.

Steelman: Aggressive infrastructure deployment creates an unassailable moat by locking in capacity and geography before competitors can react, leveraging cheap venture capital to buy speed.

However, this claim fails the blocker test. The proposal explicitly admits the company has "no operating track record" and has not yet "learning the unit economics of the first" market. Groceries are defined as "low-margin," meaning errors in density or logistics efficiency are fatal, not merely costly. Building "highly automated, very large regional warehouses" in "over a dozen" areas creates massive fixed costs and operational complexity before proving that customers in even one city will adopt the service at a profitable rate.

The "do nothing" alternative (optimize the first city, prove unit economics, then expand) offers the same upside potential with drastically lower risk. A cheaper, staged alternative involves using third-party logistics or smaller, manual fulfillment centers in the next 2-3 cities to validate density requirements without billion-dollar sunk costs. The current plan violates the basic operational principle of verifying demand before scaling supply, especially in a capital-intensive, low-margin sector.

The pre-mortem is clear: The company runs out of cash because the automated warehouses in new cities sit underutilized due to unforeseen local adoption barriers or logistical inefficiencies that could have been identified in a pilot. The cost of delay (waiting to prove economics) is negligible compared to the cost of failure (billions in stranded assets).

Blockers:
1. No proven unit economics in the initial market before scaling to dozens.
2. Irreversible commitment of $1B+ to untested operational models in low-margin industry.
3. Lack of track record makes multi-city execution highly probable to fail.

Risks: Customer density mismatches, automation failures, margin erosion, capital exhaustion.

Kill criteria: Do not proceed until the first metropolitan area demonstrates positive unit economics for three consecutive months.

DECISION: NO-GO
