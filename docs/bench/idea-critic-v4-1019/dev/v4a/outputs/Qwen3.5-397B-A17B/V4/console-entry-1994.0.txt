First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to enter a saturated, two-player market with no existing platform leverage, relying entirely on unproven third-party developer adoption while facing a direct, simultaneous launch from an entrenched incumbent with superior first-party content.

Steelman: A standalone CD console leveraging lower media costs and easier development tools can disrupt the cartridge-dominated duopoly by attracting cost-sensitive developers and offering a superior 3D experience before incumbents fully transition.

However, the attack reveals fatal blockers. First, the "easier tools" advantage is theoretical; without a proven install base, developers face high porting risks for a platform with zero users. Second, the timing is catastrophic: launching 3D hardware simultaneously with an incumbent's own 3D system invites a direct feature war the proposer cannot win given their lack of gaming pedigree. Third, the hardware margin trap is acute; selling at a loss requires massive capital reserves to subsidize units until software royalties kick in, a cash-flow dynamic untested by this consumer electronics firm.

The pre-mortem is clear: the console launches, fails to secure exclusive "killer apps" away from the incumbents' loyal franchises, ships with a weak library, and becomes a costly niche product. The "cheaper disc" argument is a cost benefit, not a demand driver; gamers buy systems for games, not media format economics.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids massive R&D and marketing sinkhole; preserves capital for core electronics business.
2. Partner with existing software houses for a non-console peripheral: Lower risk, leverages existing ecosystems.
3. Delay launch until a unique software lineup is secured: Mitigates the "empty shelf" risk but misses the 3D window.

Blockers:
- No proven route to secure critical mass of third-party developers against incumbent loyalty.
- Direct head-to-head launch timing with a dominant competitor's next-gen hardware.
- Lack of internal experience in platform holder economics (subsidized hardware, royalty management).

Risks:
- Hardware subsidy bleeding cash faster than software revenue can replenish it.
- Brand damage from entering a market as a "also-ran."

The proposal relies on assumptions of developer behavior that contradict current market inertia. Without a guaranteed "killer app" or a distinct distribution advantage, the unit economics will not sustain the platform long enough to reach critical mass.

DECISION: NO-GO
