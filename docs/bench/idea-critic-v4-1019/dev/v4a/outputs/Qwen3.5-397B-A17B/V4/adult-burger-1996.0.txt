First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the brand's core identity and customer value proposition without a mechanism to overcome them.

**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can temporarily overwrite brand perception, forcing adults to trial a premium product despite the chain's "kids/value" reputation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Brand Incompatibility:** The chain's equity is built on affordability and child-centricity. Adults seeking "premium" experiences actively avoid brands associated with children's toys and low-cost meals. Advertising cannot easily sever this deep-seated association; it may instead confuse the existing base.
2.  **Price-Value Mismatch:** The core customer base visits for value. A price point "above the core menu" alienates regulars without guaranteeing new adult traffic. The proposal admits adults see the chain as "fast, not premium," yet offers no operational or experiential changes (e.g., separate ordering, upgraded ambiance) to justify the premium other than advertising.
3.  **Financial Exposure:** Spending the "largest advertising budget the industry has seen" before validating demand is a one-way door with catastrophic downside. If the brand perception barrier holds (likely), the sunk cost is total.
4.  **Better Way:** Test the product in a limited market with modest promotion to measure actual adult uptake before committing industry-record budgets. Alternatively, acquire or launch a distinct sub-brand to house premium offerings, avoiding brand dilution.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Premise:** The proposal acknowledges adults view the chain as "fast, not premium" but assumes advertising alone can invert this perception instantly. History shows brand repositioning of this magnitude rarely succeeds via spend alone without fundamental operational shifts.
*   **Unproven Demand:** Committing maximum capital before demonstrating that the target demographic will overcome their brand bias is a fatal planning error.

**Risks:**
*   Alienating the core family demographic.
*   Wasting record marketing spend on a product the brand image actively repels.
*   Failure to achieve premium status despite high pricing, resulting in zero volume.

The plan relies on hope rather than a validated mechanism to change deep-rooted consumer behavior. The risks are existential, and the "do nothing" or "test small" alternatives offer safer paths to the same insight.

DECISION: NO-GO
