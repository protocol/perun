First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on unverified assumptions about US consumer behavior and product durability that directly contradict the stated market reality.

**Steelman:** Entering the US with small bikes via non-traditional channels captures an untapped commuter niche before competitors react, leveraging home-market dominance.

**Attack:**
1.  **Product Fit:** The proposal admits larger models fail at US highway speeds. If the brand is known for motorcycles generally, introducing a vehicle perceived as a "toy" or "moped" (50cc) into a culture valuing power and distance could cement a weak brand image permanently.
2.  **Channel Mismatch:** Selling through sporting-goods stores bypasses expert dealers who provide service and legitimacy. If the bikes require maintenance (likely given the "rough use" context), this channel creates a warranty nightmare and customer dissatisfaction loop.
3.  **Financial Exposure:** Tying up scarce foreign currency on a venture where the core product (small bike) addresses a market segment the proposal itself defines as "small" and "non-existent" (commuters don't currently buy motorcycles) is speculative gambling, not strategy.
4.  **Cultural Blindness:** The assumption that Americans will shift from "large machines" to step-through scooters ignores the entrenched cultural association of motorcycles with rebellion and power.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserve capital and reputation; wait for US market evolution.
*   *Export Large Bikes Only:* Leverages existing brand perception, though technical durability risks remain.
*   *Licensing:* Partner with a US manufacturer to test waters without currency exposure.

**Pre-mortem:** The subsidiary opens, sporting-goods stores refuse to service broken units, consumers reject the "uncool" small bikes, and the parent company drains its reserves with zero market share gained.

**Blockers:**
*   **Product Viability:** No evidence the 50cc engine survives US distances or speeds; the proposal admits larger bikes fail here, casting doubt on overall engineering suitability for US conditions.
*   **Demand Validation:** Zero data supports the existence of a "commuter" motorcycle market in the US; the proposal assumes creation of a category rather than serving one.
*   **Channel Risk:** Non-specialist retailers cannot support complex mechanical goods, creating a high probability of brand-damaging service failures.

**Risks:** Currency loss, brand dilution, operational collapse.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot in one city does not achieve 10% repeat purchase rate within 6 months, exit immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
