First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a software ecosystem deficit with a hardware acquisition, a strategic mismatch given the target's declining sales.

Steelman: Buying the handset maker is the only way to prevent immediate defection to the rival platform and secure a dedicated vessel for the operating system.

However, the claim fails the blocker test. The core problem is identified as a lack of apps and developers on the market-leading rival platform. Hardware ownership does not compel developers to write software; it only controls the distribution channel. Acquiring a division with falling sales for €5.4 billion burns cash on a depreciating asset without addressing the root cause: developer incentives. The "integration of a large workforce" is not merely a risk but a certainty that will distract leadership from the actual work of courting developers. Furthermore, other handset makers, seeing the OS owner become a direct competitor, will likely accelerate their shift to the rival platform, shrinking the total addressable market for the OS further.

The alternative of "do nothing" or licensing the OS more aggressively to other manufacturers without owning the hardware preserves cash and avoids alienating partners. A cheaper, safer alternative is to invest the €5.4 billion directly into developer funds, subsidies, and tools to solve the app gap, rather than subsidizing failing hardware manufacturing.

Blockers:
1. The proposal spends massive capital on a declining hardware asset to solve a software ecosystem problem, a mechanism mismatch that fails to address the stated root cause (lack of apps).
2. The action likely accelerates the very threat it seeks to prevent by alienating other handset manufacturers, pushing them toward the rival platform.

Risks:
1. Integration drag distracts from platform development.
2. Continued sales erosion makes the acquisition a sunk cost quickly.

This is a one-way door with a high probability of compounding the original failure. The cost of delay in fixing the app ecosystem is high, but this specific path actively increases that cost by draining resources and alienating partners.

DECISION: NO-GO
