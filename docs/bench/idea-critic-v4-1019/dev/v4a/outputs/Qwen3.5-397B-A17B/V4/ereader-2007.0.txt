First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a software-first retailer to enter capital-intensive hardware manufacturing without experience, while simultaneously disrupting publisher pricing models in a market with historically low adoption.

Steelman: A dedicated, cellular-enabled device removes friction for book buyers and locks them into the store ecosystem, creating a defensible moat despite high upfront costs and channel conflict.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoids hardware risk but cedes the digital future to competitors who solve the distribution problem.
2. Software-only app: Lower cost, but depends on users owning computers or early smartphones; fails the "instant gratification" test.
3. Proposed Hardware: Highest risk/cost, but offers the only path to control the entire reading experience and supply chain.

Fit: Aligns with the vision of "any book, anywhere," but contradicts the current capability set (no hardware DNA).

Pre-mortem: The device fails because the hardware subsidy burns too much cash before adoption critical mass is reached, or publishers withhold key titles due to the $9.99 price point, leaving the store empty. The door is one-way; once tooling is paid for, retreat is expensive.

Evidence: The prompt states earlier e-readers sold poorly and the company has zero hardware experience. These are facts, not assumptions. The margin squeeze on publishers is a stated risk, not a hypothetical.

Blockers:
1. Capability Gap: The company has never designed consumer hardware. This is a factual mismatch between the task and the team's verified history.
2. Supply Chain Risk: Publishers fearing piracy may refuse the low-price model, rendering the device's content library weak at launch.

Risks: Cannibalization of print sales (accepted trade-off), high capital burn, potential for low adoption repeating history.

The lack of hardware experience is a structural blocker for a "design and sell" mandate. While the strategic logic is sound, executing this specific plan with the current team is a high-probability failure mode. The proposal must be revised to include a hardware acquisition strategy or a partnered manufacturing approach before approval. However, per the strict constraint to judge the proposal *as proposed*, the capability gap forces a rejection.

DECISION: NO-GO
