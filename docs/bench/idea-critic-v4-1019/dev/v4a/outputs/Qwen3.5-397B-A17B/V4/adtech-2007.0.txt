First call: NO-GO. The proposed $6 billion cash outlay for a display-ad platform in May 2007 represents a massive, premature pivot away from the company's core search dominance into a fragmented, lower-margin market where it lacks operational expertise, while simultaneously alienating existing agency customers who view the target as a competitor.

Steelman: Acquiring the target instantly provides the scale and technology to block the search leader's competing acquisition, creating a full-funnel duopoly. However, this relies on the assumption that display advertising will grow fast enough to justify a premium valuation unrelated to current earnings, and that channel conflicts can be managed without losing major agency partners.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve cash and focus on organic search growth; risk ceding display to the competitor.
2. Build internally: Slower, but avoids cultural clash and customer conflict; likely too slow to match the competitor's move.
3. Acquire: Highest cost, highest integration risk, immediate channel conflict.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition fails because the buyer cannot integrate the distinct sales cultures, causing key agency clients to defect to neutral platforms. The technology becomes obsolete as real-time bidding emerges, leaving the buyer with a $6 billion sunk cost and a distracted management team. The "conflict" concern is not a risk but a near-certainty: agencies will not trust their data to a platform owned by a rival media buyer.

Evidence: The proposal admits the price is a "large premium" and the market is "fast-changing." In 2007, display ad yields are significantly lower than search. Paying a search-multiple for display assets assumes a convergence of value that has not yet occurred. The stated goal is to "compete," but the cost creates a financial drag that could undermine the core search business if the display bet does not pay off immediately.

Blockers:
1. Channel Conflict: The target's business model (serving agencies) fundamentally conflicts with the buyer's existing customers (agencies), creating an unsolvable trust barrier that threatens the target's primary revenue stream.
2. Valuation Mismatch: $6 billion cash for a "fast-changing" display platform in 2007 assumes a market maturity and yield parity with search that does not exist, making the ROI mathematically unsound based on current facts.

Risks: Technology obsolescence, cultural integration failure, regulatory scrutiny.

DECISION: NO-GO
