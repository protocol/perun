First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer with no developer platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors already oppose heavy capital spending.

Steelman: Leveraging spiky internal infrastructure to create a new revenue stream could theoretically amortize fixed costs and diversify income beyond thin-margin retail.

However, the blocker test fails immediately. The proposal admits the market for hourly server rental is "unproven" and the company lacks any experience running a developer platform at scale. Entering a capital-intensive business with unverified demand, directly contradicting investor concerns about heavy spending, constitutes a factual failure of the core premise. The risks of distraction and margin dilution are not hypothetical; they are guaranteed outcomes of executing a plan where the primary asset (infrastructure) is being repurposed for a customer base (developers) the company does not understand, served by a business model (platform) it has never operated.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserves capital, avoids distraction, and respects investor constraints.
2. Optimize internal retail usage: Reduces spikiness without external market risk.
3. Partner with existing vendors: Tests demand without building a platform.

The pre-mortem is clear: The initiative fails because reliability expectations for external developers exceed internal retail tolerances, causing brand damage to the core retail business while burning cash on an unproven product. The cost of delay is negligible because the market itself is defined as unproven; waiting for validation costs nothing compared to the cost of being the validation experiment.

Evidence: The proposal itself states the market is unproven, the team lacks experience, and investors question capital spending. These are not risks to be managed; they are current facts that invalidate the go decision.

Blockers:
1. Unproven market demand for the specific offering.
2. Zero organizational experience in the target business model.
3. Direct conflict with active investor constraints on capital expenditure.

Risks:
1. Core retail distraction.
2. Margin dilution.
3. Reliability failures damaging the retail brand.

No kill criteria can save a plan where the fundamental premises (market existence, capability, capital availability) are explicitly stated as absent or negative.

DECISION: NO-GO
