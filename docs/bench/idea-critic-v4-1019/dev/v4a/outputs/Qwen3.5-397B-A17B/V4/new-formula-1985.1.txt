First call: NO-GO. Replacing a heritage asset based solely on sensory data ignores the brand's emotional equity.

**Steelman:** The data proves the new formula is sensorially superior; swapping it maximizes immediate taste satisfaction and halts share erosion by beating the rival on flavor.

**Attack:** The proposal conflates "preferred taste" with "preferred product." Blind tests isolate flavor but strip away brand identity, heritage, and the psychological ownership loyalists feel. By withholding the fact that the original would vanish, the research measured addition, not substitution. The moment consumers realize the choice is binary (new or nothing), the metric shifts from "which tastes better?" to "what are you taking away from me?"

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Retain status quo; slow decline continues but brand trust remains intact.
2.  **Line Extension:** Launch the sweeter formula as a new variant (e.g., "New Coke") while keeping the original. This captures the taste-preference segment without alienating the core.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Total replacement. Highest risk of backlash due to perceived betrayal.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** The strategy violates the brand's north star of heritage and continuity. Assuming this fails: loyalists view the removal as an arrogant erasure of their history, triggering a backlash that outweighs any gain from switchers. Unlike a tech pivot, this is a one-way door with massive reputational damage; you cannot easily "re-launch" a deleted century-old icon without admitting fault.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fatal Flaw in Evidence:** The testing methodology is invalid for the proposed action. It explicitly failed to disclose the withdrawal of the original, meaning the "preference" data does not apply to a replacement scenario. The core claim (consumers want the swap) is unsupported by the provided facts.
*   **No Fallback:** Withdrawing the original entirely leaves no safety net if the reaction turns negative, which the emotional attachment risk suggests is probable.

**Risks:** Mass boycott, bottler revolt, permanent brand damage, media narrative of corporate arrogance.

**Decision:** The plan relies on data that explicitly excludes the primary variable (loss of the original). You cannot approve a total replacement based on tests that hid the replacement nature of the decision.

DECISION: NO-GO
