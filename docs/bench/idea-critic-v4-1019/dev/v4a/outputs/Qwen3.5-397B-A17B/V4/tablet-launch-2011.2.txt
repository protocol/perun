First call: NO-GO. Launching a premium-priced tablet with a tiny app catalog against a dominant incumbent, while the OS has negligible share and leadership is unstable, ignores the primary driver of tablet adoption: ecosystem depth.

Steelman: The brand's hardware quality and marketing spend can overcome early app scarcity to gain foothold.
Attack: Hardware alone rarely sustains a tablet platform; users buy ecosystems. A "small catalog" at "market-leading price" offers no value proposition versus the incumbent. Developer outreach takes years to mature; advertising cannot fake utility.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids burning capital on a likely failure during leadership transition.
2. Low-price entry: Undercut the leader significantly to force trial despite app gaps (high risk, but better than parity pricing).
3. Delay: Wait for leadership stability and a critical mass of apps before launching.

Fit: Assuming the goal is profitable market share, this fails. If the goal is merely "having a tablet," it succeeds but wastes resources.

Pre-mortem: The device launches, reviewers praise the build but pan the app selection. Sales stall immediately. Marketing spend yields low conversion due to lack of killer apps. The project is cancelled in 18 months, damaging the brand. This is a one-way door with high sunk costs.

Evidence: The proposal admits the app catalog is a "small fraction" and OS share is "almost none." History shows premium hardware fails without software parity.

Blockers:
1. Price-to-value mismatch: Premium price + tiny catalog = no rational purchase reason.
2. Timing: Leadership transition creates strategic drift risk during critical launch phase.
3. Ecosystem gap: Cannot be solved quickly by outreach; creates a permanent disadvantage vs. incumbent.

Risks: Developer churn, brand damage, inventory write-offs.

Kill criteria: If app count does not triple in 6 months, halt marketing. Leading metric: App usage hours per device.

The plan commits real money to a flawed value proposition. It must be revised to either price aggressively low or delay until the ecosystem matures. As proposed, it is financially unsound.

DECISION: NO-GO
