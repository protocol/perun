First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a high-risk, irreversible change to a core brand asset without validation, ignoring explicit risks regarding customer attachment and backlash.

Steelman: A simultaneous, unannounced rollout maximizes shock value to signal a decisive break from the past, forcing immediate market attention.

Attack: The "no advance announcement" strategy removes the safety valve for customer sentiment. In 2010, social media could amplify perceived arrogance instantly. Replacing a decades-old identifier with an untested design violates the principle that brand equity is earned, not declared. The proposal admits the new design was not tested; proceeding anyway assumes the executive team's taste supersedes customer loyalty.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Retains equity but fails the "contemporary" goal.
2. Phased rollout with testing: Validates the new design with loyalists first, allowing adjustment before full commitment.
3. Proposed approach: Highest cost of failure (brand alienation) with zero learning loop.

Fit: Moving to "contemporary" requires relevance, not just aesthetic minimalism. Alienating the core base to chase a vague modernity contradicts sustainable growth.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-launch, sales dip as loyal customers feel ignored; social channels flood with comparisons favoring the old logo; the company is forced to revert, looking indecisive. This is a one-way door; once the old logo is removed everywhere, the signal of instability is permanent.

Evidence: The proposal itself cites "loyal customers are attached" and "not tested" as facts. These are not hypothetical risks but stated conditions that guarantee friction.

Blockers:
1. Lack of testing: A core mechanism (customer acceptance) is unverified despite known attachment.
2. No communication plan: "No advance announcement" guarantees the narrative is controlled by critics, not the brand.

Risks: Backlash intensity, loss of heritage value, confusion in channel recognition.

The plan fails because it treats a relationship-based asset as a purely visual one. You cannot force a contemporary perception by discarding history without consent. The absence of a pilot or feedback loop is a fatal flaw.

DECISION: NO-GO
