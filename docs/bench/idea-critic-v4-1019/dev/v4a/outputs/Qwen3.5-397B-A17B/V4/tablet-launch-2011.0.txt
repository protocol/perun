First call: NO-GO. Launching a premium-priced tablet with a tiny app catalog against a dominant incumbent, while the OS has negligible share and leadership is in flux, ignores the primary driver of tablet adoption: ecosystem depth.

**Steelman:** The brand's hardware prestige can temporarily offset OS weakness if developer momentum is ignited immediately by heavy ad spend, forcing a two-sided market tip before competitors react.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed:** High price, small catalog, heavy ad spend. (High cost, high risk of inventory glut).
2.  **Do Nothing:** Wait for OS maturity or leadership stability. (Low cost, missed window).
3.  **Alternative:** Subsidize hardware to loss-leading levels to force user acquisition, betting on volume to attract developers. (High cash burn, but addresses the catalog chicken-and-egg problem directly).

**Fit:** Assumes the brand can override category norms. If the "unproven" perception is tied to the OS, not the hardware, marketing cannot fix the utility gap.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches; reviewers praise the build but pan the app selection. Consumers, unwilling to pay a premium for a limited experience, buy the market leader instead. Developers, seeing low sales, deprioritize the platform. The company is left with expensive inventory and a stalled OS.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the app catalog is a "small fraction" and the OS has "almost no market share." In two-sided markets, content drives hardware sales, not vice versa. Pricing "in line with the market leader" removes the only logical incentive (cost) for a consumer to tolerate a deficient ecosystem.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Price-Value Mismatch:** Charging parity pricing for a fraction of the utility (apps) is a factual barrier to adoption.
2.  **Leadership Transition:** Heavy strategic bets require stable execution; transition introduces execution risk that compounds the market risk.

**Risks:** Developer outreach may fail regardless of spend; shoppers may remain skeptical.

**Decision:** The plan fails the basic economic test of value proposition. You cannot charge a premium for a discounted experience without a unique, non-app differentiator, which is not stated.

DECISION: NO-GO
