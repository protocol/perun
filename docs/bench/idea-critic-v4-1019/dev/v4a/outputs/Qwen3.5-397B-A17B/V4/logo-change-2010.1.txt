First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a high-risk, irreversible change to a core brand asset without validation, ignoring explicit risks regarding customer attachment and lack of testing.

Steelman: A simultaneous, unannounced global rebrand to a minimalist aesthetic will instantly modernize the retailer's perception, signaling a decisive break from tradition to attract contemporary consumers before competitors react.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost, extreme risk of alienating the core base, zero reversibility once public.
2. **Do Nothing:** Maintain current equity; risk appearing dated but safe.
3. **Phased Pilot:** Test the new mark in select digital channels or a single region to measure sentiment before full rollout. This offers the same upside potential with drastically lower risk and high reversibility.

Fit: While "looking contemporary" is a valid goal, destroying existing brand recognition (the blue square) contradicts the need for sustained equity unless the new design is proven superior. The proposal assumes the new look works without evidence.

Pre-mortem: The launch triggers immediate social media backlash from loyalists who feel the brand identity was stolen. Sales dip as confusion rises. Because there was no announcement or testing, the company cannot pivot quickly without appearing incompetent. The cost of delay (waiting to test) is negligible compared to the cost of a botched global launch.

Evidence: The proposal explicitly admits the design was "not tested with customers" and acknowledges "loyal customers are attached to the old logo." These are facts, not assumptions. Proceeding ignores direct evidence of potential failure.

Blockers:
1. **Lack of Validation:** The core claim (that this will make the brand look contemporary *without* losing customers) is unsupported by data. The proposal admits no testing occurred.
2. **Contradictory Strategy:** Removing the primary recognition element (the square) while trying to modernize creates a high probability of brand erosion, directly conflicting with the goal of sustainable growth.

Risks: Instant backlash, loss of loyal customer base, confusion in the market, irreversible damage to brand equity.

A cheaper, safer alternative (phased pilot) reaches the same upside (modernization) without the existential risk. The stated facts show the target users (loyal customers) may reject this, and the plan offers no answer to this threat.

DECISION: NO-GO
