First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets the company's hardware dominance on a low-margin software service that is initially incompatible with the majority of potential customers' computers, while competitors pursue a subscription model that better addresses the "free" alternative.

**Steelman:** Selling individual songs creates a legal, convenient ecosystem that locks users into the company's hardware, securing long-term device sales despite thin immediate software margins.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Strategy:** The store works *only* on the company's own computers. In 2003, this excludes the vast Windows market, severely limiting the user base needed to pressure record labels or achieve scale.
2.  **Alternatives:** Rivals are backing subscriptions. A per-song model at 99 cents leaves little margin after label payments and fails to compete with the "all-you-can-eat" psychology of free file-sharing or emerging subscription services.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: Labels withdraw licenses due to low volume; users reject the platform lock-in; the thin margin cannot sustain operations against free alternatives.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "free file-sharing is everywhere" and "rivals are backing subscriptions." These are not minor risks; they are existential market forces. The "light copy protection" may be easily circumvented, negating the value proposition.

**Blockers:**
*   **Platform Limitation:** Restricting the store to only the company's computers caps the addressable market immediately, making it impossible to achieve the scale required to satisfy labels or compete with free.
*   **Margin Structure:** 99 cents with label payments leaves insufficient margin to operate a robust service, especially without scale.
*   **Market Direction:** The industry is moving toward subscriptions; betting on per-song sales contradicts the emerging consumer preference for access over ownership.

**Risks:** Label withdrawal, inability to compete with free, slow adoption due to platform lock-in.

**Decision:** The platform restriction is a fatal flaw that prevents the necessary scale. The margin structure is unsustainable without that scale. The market is shifting to subscriptions.

DECISION: NO-GO
