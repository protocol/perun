First call: NO-GO. The proposal eliminates the core purchasing trigger (coupons) for the existing customer base without evidence they will accept everyday low pricing, while simultaneously undertaking a massive, untested physical reconstruction.

**Steelman:** The current discount model erodes brand value and margins; a clean everyday pricing strategy combined with curated branded shops will modernize the experience, attract higher-value shoppers, and restore profitability, replicating the success seen in the electronics sector.

**Attack:**
1.  **Customer Mismatch:** The electronics chain succeeded with a different demographic and product lifecycle. Department store customers here are explicitly "trained to wait for coupons." Removing this incentive instantly alienates the revenue base before new customers are acquired.
2.  **Execution Risk:** Rebuilding all stores into branded shops while changing pricing is a "one-way door" executed at maximum velocity. Doing both simultaneously creates compound failure modes: operational chaos from construction plus revenue collapse from pricing confusion.
3.  **Lack of Evidence:** No pilot exists. The plan assumes the electronics CEO's past success transfers directly to a struggling department store context without validation. The historical reliance on discounts suggests price elasticity is high; ignoring this is fatal.
4.  **Alternatives:** A phased approach (pilot in 5-10 stores) or a hybrid pricing model would test the hypothesis without betting the entire chain. "Do nothing" preserves current cash flow while a safer transition is designed.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Most sales historically come from discounted items.
*   **Contradiction:** The plan removes discounts immediately chain-wide, directly attacking the proven revenue engine without a verified replacement demand source.
*   **Risk vs. Blocker:** The lack of a pilot is a blocker here because the change is irreversible at scale and contradicts established customer behavior data.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It bets the company on an unproven behavioral shift for a specific, resistant demographic, executed via a high-risk, all-at-once rollout. The cost of delay (waiting for a pilot) is negligible compared to the cost of immediate, chain-wide failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
