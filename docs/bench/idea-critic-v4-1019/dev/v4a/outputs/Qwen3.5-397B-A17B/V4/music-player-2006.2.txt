First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market as a late follower, abandons a strategic standard (copy-protection) that secured partner alliances, and relies on unproven wireless sharing that faces likely label restrictions, all without a price advantage to disrupt the incumbent's loyal ecosystem.

**Steelman:** The company leverages its brand and marketing budget to overcome lateness, betting that superior hardware (screen) and frictionless sharing will break user loyalty where price could not.

**Attack:**
1.  **Strategic Contradiction:** Dropping the copy-protection standard alienates existing partners (labels, device makers) whose content is essential for the store. This burns bridges to secure a foothold in a content-controlled industry.
2.  **Ecosystem Moat:** The dominant player's lock-in is driven by library size and habit, not just hardware specs. A "larger screen" and "wireless sharing" are insufficient differentiators against an entrenched network effect, especially when sharing faces legal/regulatory headwinds from labels.
3.  **Cost vs. Return:** A "large marketing budget" spent on a me-too product with higher friction (no content deals due to DRM abandonment) yields negative ROI. The "late to market" status means acquiring users costs significantly more than retaining them did for the leader.
4.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "partner" option is superior. Licensing the software to existing device makers or integrating with the dominant store preserves the copy-protection standard and avoids direct competition where the company has no asymmetric advantage.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal admits dropping the copy-protection standard previously promoted to partners.
*   **Consequence:** This creates an immediate conflict of interest with content providers, likely resulting in catalog restrictions that make the store non-viable at launch.
*   **Fact:** No price advantage exists.
*   **Consequence:** Without lower cost or exclusive content, there is no rational switch trigger for consumers.

**Risks:** Label restrictions on sharing could render the key feature useless; marketing spend may simply educate users on the competitor's superiority.

**Kill Criteria:** If label agreements for wireless sharing are not secured prior to hardware tooling, or if the marketing budget cannot guarantee 5% market share within 18 months given the late entry, the project must terminate.

**Decision:** The plan fails on strategic coherence (alienating partners) and economic logic (no switch trigger).

DECISION: NO-GO
