First call: NO-GO. The proposed price is an order of magnitude higher than competitors while offering inferior speed and software support.

Steelman: This machine offers a revolutionary graphical interface that could redefine office productivity, justifying a premium for early adopters seeking a competitive edge.

Attack: The steelman collapses under the specific facts provided. A price of $10,000 is several times that of leading business PCs, yet the unit runs "slowly" and lacks third-party software. In the 1983 business context, software availability and processing speed are primary drivers of value, not just interface novelty. Furthermore, the existence of a cheaper, similar machine in development within the same company creates a fatal conflict. Launching the expensive, slower model now would cannibalize the future cheaper model or force an immediate, embarrassing price cut.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital and brand reputation; wait for the internal cheaper model.
2. Pivot to niche: Target only specific high-end design firms (unlikely to sustain volume at $10k with slow performance).
3. Delay launch: Halt this specific configuration until costs drop or software library grows.

Fit: The proposal fails to move the north star of "business adoption" because the total cost of ownership (high price + low utility due to speed/software) is negative compared to established text-based competitors.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, sales are negligible due to price/performance ratio, developers ignore the platform due to low install base, and the internal cheaper team is demoralized by competing against their own overpriced predecessor.

Blockers:
1. Price mismatch: $10,000 is unsupported by performance or software ecosystem relative to competitors.
2. Internal conflict: A cheaper, similar internal alternative makes this specific SKU redundant and strategically damaging.
3. Value deficit: "Slow" performance negates the efficiency gains of the graphical interface for office workers.

Risks: Market confusion, developer abandonment, inventory write-downs.

The combination of excessive price, poor performance, lack of software, and an imminent internal substitute creates an insurmountable barrier to success as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
