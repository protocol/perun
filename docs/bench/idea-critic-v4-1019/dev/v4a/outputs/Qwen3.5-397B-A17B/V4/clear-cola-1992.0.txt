First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on unproven behavioral assumptions and ignores severe cannibalization risks without a mitigation plan.

**Steelman:** A clear cola can capture a distinct "purity" segment and reverse growth stagnation if flavor-color association is broken and repeat purchase exceeds novelty decay.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fundamental Contradiction:** The core risk—that consumers link cola flavor to dark color—is not a minor hurdle; it is a category definition. If the product tastes like cola but looks like water, it creates cognitive dissonance that likely suppresses repeat purchases after the initial trial. Test markets often inflate novelty; they rarely predict long-term habit formation for counter-intuitive products.
2.  **Cannibalization vs. Growth:** The proposal admits the flagship may be cannibalized but offers no math showing net growth. If the clear cola merely shifts existing volume while diluting brand equity, the massive marketing spend yields negative ROI.
3.  **Competitive Response:** A "large marketing campaign" signals the move to rivals immediately. If the concept is easily copied (as noted), first-mover advantage is fleeting unless the brand lock-in is instant, which contradicts the "repeat purchase" uncertainty.
4.  **Missing Kill Criteria:** There is no defined threshold for repeat purchase rates or cannibalization limits to trigger a stop. Launching nationwide without these guards turns a testable hypothesis into an existential bet.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserve flagship margins; explore flavor innovations within the traditional color spectrum.
*   *Staged Rollout:* Limit to specific regions with strict repeat-purchase tracking before national spend.
*   *Sub-Brand:* Launch as a distinct brand to protect the flagship from direct association failure.

**Verdict:**
The proposal fails the blocker test. The risk that the product violates the fundamental sensory expectation of the category (color = flavor) is a structural flaw, not just an execution risk. Without evidence that repeat purchase rates in test markets exceeded novelty decay (not just initial trial), and without a cap on cannibalization, a nationwide launch is premature. The "positive response" cited is likely noise from novelty seekers.

Blockers: Unproven resolution of flavor-color cognitive dissonance; missing net-growth math accounting for cannibalization.
Risks: Rapid competitive copying; brand dilution; high marketing burn with low retention.

DECISION: NO-GO
