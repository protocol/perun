First call: GO. The core failure modes (updates, cheating) are existential; the proposed cure directly addresses them despite friction.

Steelman: A mandatory client is the only viable mechanism to serialize updates, enforce integrity, and monetize post-launch, securing the franchise's lifespan against piracy and obsolescence.

Alternatives analysis shows "do nothing" guarantees continued revenue leakage and unpatched vulnerabilities. Patching via retailers or mail is too slow for 2003's threat landscape. A voluntary client fails to capture the cheaters and laggards who necessitate the system. The "cheaper" alternative of ignoring the problem costs the product its competitive life.

Fit is absolute: the strategy shifts from selling static goods to managing live services. Without this infrastructure, the "popular PC games" become unsupported legacy code within months of release.

Pre-mortem: The initiative fails if the client crashes on launch or if broadband adoption lags so severely that the install base fractures. However, these are execution risks, not strategic blockers. The cost of delay is immediate: every month without anti-cheat erodes the multiplayer community, the primary driver of longevity for popular titles.

Evidence check: The proposal correctly identifies that retail boxes cannot deliver binary updates or real-time heuristic analysis. The risks cited (user friction, retail pushback) are valid but secondary to the primary threat of a broken, cheated ecosystem. Home broadband was growing rapidly in 2003; targeting new games allows a natural cutoff for early adopters, leaving older titles on boxes if necessary.

Blockers: NONE. No fact presented proves the plan fails as proposed; the risks are manageable trade-offs for survival.
Risks: User churn due to friction, retail channel conflict, initial stability issues. These are mitigated by the "mandatory" nature applying only to *new* games, allowing a transition period.

Kill criteria: If the client causes >15% drop in active users within 90 days of a new title launch, or if uptime falls below 98% in the first month, the mandatory requirement must be relaxed to optional for single-player modes. Leading metric: Daily Active Users (DAU) vs. Cheat Reports ratio.

DECISION: GO
