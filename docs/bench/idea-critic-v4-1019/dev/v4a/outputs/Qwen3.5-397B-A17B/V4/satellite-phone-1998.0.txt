First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and market timing fundamentally contradict the capital structure.

**Steelman:** A premium, niche service for global travelers who value ubiquity over cost, capturing early adopters before terrestrial networks mature.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Shrinkage:** The core premise relies on a lack of terrestrial coverage. However, ground cellular is spreading rapidly in 1998. The window to capture high-value users is closing faster than the debt schedule allows.
2.  **Price vs. Product:** At $3,000 per handset and several dollars per minute, the service is inaccessible to all but the most desperate users. Yet, the product is bulky and fails indoors—the two places business travelers most need reliability. This is a premium price for an inferior user experience.
3.  **Financial Suicide:** With $5 billion in borrowed capital, the company requires massive, immediate subscriber growth to service debt. The high price point and product limitations severely cap the total addressable market, making the required growth trajectory mathematically improbable.
4.  **Channel Risk:** Sales channels are not ready, delaying revenue recognition while interest accrues.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; wait for satellite tech to mature and costs to drop.
*   **Pivot to B2B/Government:** Ignore consumers; sell exclusively to maritime, aviation, and defense sectors where indoor use is less critical and price sensitivity is lower.
*   **Delay Launch:** Postpone commercial service to reduce handset costs and improve indoor penetration, though this risks conflicting with debt covenants.

**Pre-mortem:** The company launches, misses subscriber targets due to high costs and poor indoor signal, defaults on loan payments within 18 months, and declares bankruptcy while possessing a functional but unusable network.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Debt payments require fast growth; **Fact:** High price and poor indoor performance limit market size. These two facts cannot coexist successfully.
*   **Fact:** Terrestrial networks are expanding, eroding the unique value proposition daily.

**Risks:** Execution delays, further handset bulk issues, regulatory hurdles.

**Decision:** The financial model relies on capturing a mass premium market that the product cannot serve and the competition is rendering obsolete.

DECISION: NO-GO
