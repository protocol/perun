First call: NO-GO. The proposal doubles down on a recent 60% price hike by adding severe friction (separate logins, bills, queues) and destroying value (no shared ratings), likely triggering massive churn rather than clarifying strategy.

**Steelman:** Separating the declining DVD unit allows streaming to pivot faster without legacy drag.
**Attack:** This steelman ignores the immediate context: customers are already reeling from a 60% price increase. Splitting the services now looks not like strategic clarity, but like a deceptive unbundling to justify the hike while degrading the product. The loss of cross-service recommendations and the inconvenience of two accounts provide zero upside to the user, only downsides.

**Options:**
1. **Do nothing:** Keep the combined plan; absorb the complexity to retain customers post-hike.
2. **Reverse the hike:** Restore pricing stability to calm the base before any structural changes.
3. **Proposed split:** Maximizes customer anger by combining higher costs with fragmented UX.

**Fit:** If the north star is streaming growth, alienating the existing dual-subscriber base via a clumsy split contradicts retention goals. A "declining business" still generates cash flow; strangling it with a separate brand and broken data synergy accelerates collapse without guaranteeing streaming success.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Customers view the split as a "junk fee" tactic, cancel both services in protest, and competitors capture the displaced viewers. The DVD brand dies instantly without the streaming halo; streaming loses its curated data advantage.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits risks of "customer confusion" and "reaction to the price increase." These are not mere risks; they are certainties given the timing. No evidence suggests users want two logins or disjointed queues.

**Blockers:**
1. **Timing:** Executing a complex, high-friction split immediately after a 60% price hike is a factual recipe for mass cancellation.
2. **Value Destruction:** Explicitly removing shared ratings and recommendations reduces the product's core utility without a compensating benefit.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, operational overhead of two sites, permanent loss of cross-sell data.

**Decision:** The plan fails the basic test of not making a bad situation (price hike) catastrophic (fragmented service). It must be revised to prioritize customer retention and data synergy before considering structural separation.

DECISION: NO-GO
