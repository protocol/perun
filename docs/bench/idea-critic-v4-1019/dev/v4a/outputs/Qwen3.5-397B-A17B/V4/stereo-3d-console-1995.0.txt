First call: NO-GO. The proposal describes a device with severe usability flaws (discomfort, eye strain), a crippled visual presentation (red monochrome), and a weak value proposition (neither portable nor a home console) at a premium price point, all while cannibalizing attention from a superior home console arriving shortly.

Steelman: A low-cost bridge product leveraging existing tech to capture market share between generations via novel 3D gameplay.

Attack: The "novelty" of stereoscopic 3D is negated by the requirement of an eyepiece, which destroys the "tabletop" social aspect and causes physical discomfort. Red monochrome graphics are a significant regression from contemporary color standards, making the $180 price hard to justify against full-color competitors. The lack of launch games creates a dead-on-arrival ecosystem. Crucially, the impending next-gen home console renders this stopgap obsolete before it can establish a library.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve brand reputation, focus resources on the upcoming home console. Cost: $0. Risk: Competitor fills the gap. Reversibility: N/A.
2. Accelerate home console: Bring the next-gen system forward or lower its price. Cost: High R&D. Risk: Supply chain strain. Reversibility: Low.
3. Proposed device: Cost: High manufacturing/marketing. Risk: Brand damage, hardware failure, user health issues. Reversibility: Low (hardware inventory).

Fit: Does not move the north star of "quality entertainment experiences"; the described experience is physically painful and visually inferior.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, reviewers cite headaches and poor graphics, sales flatline, and the brand is associated with a failed gimmick just as the real next-gen console arrives, confusing consumers.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits to red-only visuals, discomfort, eye strain, few games, and a competing internal release window. These are not risks; they are stated facts of the design.

Blockers:
1. Stated fact: Device causes eye strain/headaches (unacceptable for consumer hardware).
2. Stated fact: Red monochrome only (inferior to market standard).
3. Stated fact: Neither portable nor comfortable (fails core form-factor utility).
4. Stated fact: Next home console due in 2 years (cannibalization/obsolescence).

Risks: None needed; blockers are sufficient.

DECISION: NO-GO
