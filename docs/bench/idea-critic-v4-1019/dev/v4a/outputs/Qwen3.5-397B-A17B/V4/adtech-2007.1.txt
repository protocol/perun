First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $6 billion cash, the company's largest acquisition ever, at a large premium in a fast-changing market, without evidence that owning an agency resolves the stated conflict with existing agency customers or guarantees technological parity against a competitor who has already moved.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform immediately secures critical display-ad technology to compete with the search leader before the window closes, accepting the premium as the cost of speed.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Blocker:** The price ($6B cash) represents a massive, irreversible capital outlay at a "large premium" in a "fast-changing" sector. If the technology shifts again within 18 months, the asset becomes stranded while the cash reserve is gone. The proposal offers no kill criteria or valuation model to justify this specific multiple.
2.  **Strategic Contradiction:** The buyer is a software company; the target includes a "digital advertising agency." Acquiring a service business creates direct conflicts with the buyer's existing agency customers, who may flee to competitors to avoid funding a rival's infrastructure. The proposal identifies this risk but offers no mitigation plan (e.g., divesting the agency arm).
3.  **Timing & Alternatives:** The competitor "just agreed" to buy a platform, meaning the defensive urgency is reactive, not proactive. "Do nothing" or building/buying only the tech stack (excluding the agency) are cheaper alternatives that avoid the customer conflict. The proposal fails to demonstrate why this specific bundled asset is the only path.

**Blockers:**
*   **Conflict of Interest:** No plan to resolve the tension between owning an agency and serving other agencies.
*   **Valuation Void:** No evidence provided that the "large premium" yields a positive ROI given the market volatility.
*   **Asset Mismatch:** The inclusion of the agency service layer introduces liability without clear strategic value for a software vendor.

**Risks:** Technology obsolescence, customer churn due to conflict of interest, cash depletion limiting future R&D.

**Conclusion:** The plan fails the "cheaper alternative" test (buy tech only) and introduces a fatal channel conflict without a resolution strategy. The financial exposure is too high for an unproven integration model in a volatile market.

DECISION: NO-GO
