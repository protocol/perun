First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets a dominant running brand's core marketing budget on an unproven rookie, violating the principle of preserving cash flow in the primary category to fund expansion in a secondary one.

**Steelman:** Secure a generational talent before market pricing reflects their value, using controversy (rule-breaking colors) to manufacture demand that overrides the risk of inexperience.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain status quo; likely cede further basketball share to established stars with proven sales.
2.  **Veteran Strategy:** Sign a proven All-Star for a standard fee; lower risk, higher cost, less cultural disruption.
3.  **Proposal:** High-risk, high-reward bet on potential and controversy.

**Fit:** Assumes the brand can absorb a total loss of this budget without impacting running shoe dominance. Assumes "rebellion" resonates more than "performance" in 1984 basketball culture.

**Pre-mortem:** The player suffers a career-ending injury in preseason; the brand is left with dead inventory and no spokesperson. The league bans the shoes immediately, forcing a costly redesign and killing the "banned" marketing hook before launch. The rookie averages low minutes, failing to capture public imagination.

**Evidence:** No data exists on this specific player's professional viability (he has not played). Historical data suggests signature lines for rookies rarely sustain beyond one season. The financial commitment is disproportionately large relative to the current basketball division revenue.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Unverified Core Asset:** The entire ROI depends on a player with zero professional track record. Unlike a veteran, there is no baseline performance data to model sales against.
2.  **Catastrophic Single Point of Failure:** An injury wipes out 100% of the investment immediately.
3.  **Regulatory Dependency:** The marketing strategy explicitly relies on the league *allowing* the rule-breaking narrative to persist long enough to generate sales, yet acknowledges the league may fine/ban it. If the league acts swiftly, the primary differentiator vanishes.

**Risks:** Budget cannibalization from the core running business; brand dilution if the player fails; inventory obsolescence.

**Decision:** The lack of professional data creates an uninsurable risk profile. A "go-if" is impossible without a game played. The potential for total capital loss on a non-core product line constitutes a blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
