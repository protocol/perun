First call: NO-GO. The core value proposition (reliable handwriting recognition) is explicitly stated as unproven in testing, while the price point ($700) and form factor (too big for a pocket) violate basic usability and market fit for a "handheld" device.

Steelman: Launching now establishes category dominance through first-mover advantage and CEO-led publicity, betting that software updates will fix recognition and economies of scale will lower prices before competitors arrive.

However, the blockers are fatal based on the provided facts. A pen-based PDA without reliable handwriting recognition has no functional primary input; this is not a risk to be managed but a broken core mechanism. The device is described as "too big for a pocket," directly contradicting the requirement for a "handheld" computer intended for personal, mobile use. At $700, with little software and an unproven category, the value equation fails completely. The "heavy publicity" cannot compensate for a product that does not work as advertised (unreliable input) and is physically inconvenient (too large).

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids reputational damage from launching a flawed flagship product and preserves capital until the technology matures.
2. Delay and refine: Wait until recognition is reliable and the form factor fits a pocket, even if it means losing first-mover status.
3. Pivot: Rebrand as a specialized stationary terminal rather than a personal handheld, though this abandons the stated vision.

Pre-mortem: The product launches to massive hype, users immediately discover the handwriting recognition fails in real-world scenarios, the device is too cumbersome to carry daily, and the lack of software renders it a costly novelty. Sales collapse, the brand suffers long-term association with failure, and the "new category" is defined by this flop, delaying genuine adoption for years.

Blockers:
1. Core mechanism failure: Handwriting recognition is unreliable in testing; without this, the device has no viable primary input.
2. Form factor violation: Device is too big for a pocket, failing the fundamental "handheld" criterion.
3. Value mismatch: High price ($700) combined with little software and unproven utility offers no compelling reason to buy.

Risks: Market education costs, competitor entry, technology maturation timeline. These are secondary to the immediate functional failures.

DECISION: NO-GO
