First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on unproven film production capabilities and overpays for assets whose primary value is currently locked away by rival contracts.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the publisher instantly grants a deep library of male-oriented characters, fixing the conglomerate's demographic gap and leveraging its merchandising machine better than the target can alone.

**Attack:**
1.  **Valuation Mismatch:** Paying $4 billion for a company with a "short and unproven" film slate and cash-flow constraints from long-term rival licenses is speculative. The price assumes future film success that the target has not yet demonstrated independently.
2.  **Asset Encumbrance:** The "most famous characters" are licensed to rivals. The immediate upside is capped until these deals expire, meaning the buyer pays a premium today for revenue streams they cannot access for years.
3.  **Execution Risk:** The target just started self-financing films. Transitioning from licensing to production is capital-intensive and risky. Adding "creative-control disputes" suggests the integration could stall, leaving the buyer with a costly, dysfunctional subsidiary.
4.  **Cyclicality:** Comics-based films are cyclical. Buying at a peak or during a transition without a proven slate increases exposure to a downturn.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid overpaying for unproven assets; wait for the target to prove its film model or for licenses to revert.
*   **License Only:** Negotiate specific character licenses for toys/games without the burden of film production risks and creative disputes.
*   **Staged Buy:** Acquire a minority stake or option to buy later, contingent on the success of the next 1-2 self-produced films.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial:** The $4B price tag is not supported by current proven cash flows given the licensing encumbrances.
*   **Strategic:** The core value (famous characters) is currently inaccessible due to rival deals.

**Risks:** Creative clashes, film flops, cyclical downturns.

**Decision:** The proposal fails the value test as proposed. The price is too high for assets that are currently legally restricted and operationally unproven in their new strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
