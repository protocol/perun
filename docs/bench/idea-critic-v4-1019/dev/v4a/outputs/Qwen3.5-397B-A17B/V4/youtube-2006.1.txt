First call: NO-GO. The price is astronomical for an asset with zero revenue, massive liabilities, and no proven monetization path.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant market leader instantly solves distribution scale and eliminates a competitor, betting that ad technology and legal frameworks will mature fast enough to monetize the traffic before litigation destroys the company.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Suicide:** Paying $1.65 billion for a company with "no meaningful revenue" implies a multiple of infinity. The bandwidth costs alone could bankrupt the combined entity before ad rates stabilize.
2.  **Existential Legal Risk:** The core library consists largely of infringing content. Media companies are already threatening lawsuits. There is no "fair use" shield strong enough to cover a commercial entity built entirely on stolen TV and film clips at this scale. The liability is not a risk; it is a near-certainty that could result in statutory damages exceeding the purchase price.
3.  **Unproven Model:** Video advertising in 2006 is nascent. Betting the company's stock on an unproven ad model for long-form content is speculation, not strategy.
4.  **Alternative:** The buyer already runs a video service. Organic growth or licensing legitimate content is slower but avoids inheriting a lawsuit bomb and infinite burn rate.

**Blockers:**
*   **Liability:** The proposal admits the site hosts copyrighted material and faces threatened lawsuits. This is a factual blocker; the potential damages exceed the acquisition cost.
*   **Economics:** No revenue pathway exists to justify $1.65B. The bandwidth cost creates a negative margin on every view.

**Risks:** Total loss of capital, destruction of buyer's stock value, precedent-setting legal loss for user-generated content.

**Decision Method:**
This is a one-way door with catastrophic downside. The "upside" relies on assumptions (legal immunity, ad adoption) that are currently contradicted by the stated facts (threatened lawsuits, no revenue). A cheaper alternative (organic growth) exists, even if slower. The plan fails the basic test of solvency and legal compliance as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
