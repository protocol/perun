First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a highly profitable incumbent to voluntarily introduce an inferior, costly product that threatens its core revenue stream while inviting competition from capital-rich giants, all without a clear path to offsetting the inevitable margin erosion.

Steelman: Launching a free, limited streaming service now is a defensive necessity to retain subscribers and gather data before the market shifts entirely digital, preventing total obsolescence later.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the 2007 context. First, the unit economics are broken: high licensing fees for a small catalog combined with zero incremental revenue guarantee immediate margin compression on the existing profitable base. Second, the "cannibalization" risk is not theoretical; with no price differentiation, users will migrate to the lower-friction digital option even if quality is poor, accelerating revenue decay before the technology or broadband infrastructure can support a viable standalone business. Third, competing against incoming tech giants with deep pockets and superior engineering talent using a "small and mostly older" catalog is a losing strategy; it signals weakness rather than establishing a moat.

The proposal lacks a kill criterion or a phased approach. It offers no mechanism to test demand without exposing the entire subscriber base to a degraded experience or inflated costs. A "do nothing" stance preserves the cash cow while the company negotiates better licensing terms or waits for broadband penetration to mature. A "paid add-on" or "separate beta" would be superior alternatives to validate the model without cannibalizing the core.

Blockers:
1. Negative unit economics: High fixed licensing costs with zero marginal revenue guarantee immediate profit loss.
2. Unchecked cannibalization: Free access to an inferior product will still erode the high-margin DVD business prematurely.
3. Strategic mismatch: Entering a capital-intensive war against larger foes with an inferior product suite invites destruction.

Risks:
1. Accelerated subscriber churn if the streaming experience frustrates users.
2. Inability to secure future content rights if the initial launch fails to gain traction.

This plan sacrifices a proven profit engine for an unproven, loss-leading distraction without guardrails.

DECISION: NO-GO
