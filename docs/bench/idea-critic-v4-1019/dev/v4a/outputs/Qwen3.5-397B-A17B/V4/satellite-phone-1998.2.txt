First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and market trajectory described make success impossible under the proposed terms.

**Steelman:** A premium, niche service for global travelers who absolutely require connectivity where terrestrial networks do not exist, accepting high costs for guaranteed coverage.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Erosion:** The core value proposition (global coverage) is being rapidly commoditized by spreading ground cellular networks. As terrestrial coverage expands, the addressable market for this expensive alternative shrinks daily.
2.  **Price vs. Debt:** The $3,000 handset and high per-minute costs restrict the market to a tiny elite. However, $5 billion in borrowed capital requires massive, rapid subscriber growth to service debt. The pricing strategy directly contradicts the volume needed to avoid default. You cannot sell luxury goods at mass-market volumes.
3.  **Product Deficits:** Bulky handsets that fail indoors severely limit utility. Business travelers, the target demographic, spend significant time indoors (offices, hotels, airports). A phone that doesn't work where the customer is located is unsellable at any price.
4.  **Channel Readiness:** Launching without ready sales channels guarantees a slow start, which is fatal given the immediate debt service requirements.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid sinking further capital into a failing model; write off the investment rather than compounding it with operational losses.
*   **Pivot to B2B/Government:** Ignore consumers entirely; sell dedicated terminals to maritime, aviation, and defense sectors where price sensitivity is lower and indoor use is less critical.
*   **Delay Launch:** Wait to refine handsets and lower costs, though this risks running out of cash before readiness.

**Pre-mortem:** The company launches, misses subscriber targets due to price and poor indoor performance, ground networks expand further, revenue fails to cover debt service, and the company declares bankruptcy within two years.

**Blockers:**
*   The math does not work: Debt service requires mass adoption; pricing limits adoption to a niche.
*   Product performance (indoor failure) negates the primary use case for the target demographic.

**Risks:** Rapid terrestrial expansion rendering the network obsolete before ROI.

**Decision:** The financial structure demands volume that the product and pricing explicitly prevent.

DECISION: NO-GO
