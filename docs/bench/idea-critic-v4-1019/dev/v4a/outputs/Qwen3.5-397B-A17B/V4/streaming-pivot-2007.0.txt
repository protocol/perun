First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a highly profitable incumbent to voluntarily introduce an inferior, costly product that directly threatens its core revenue stream during a period of limited market readiness.

**Steelman:** Launching a free, limited streaming service now is a defensive necessity to retain subscribers and gather data before dominant tech giants lock up exclusive rights, even if it temporarily depresses margins.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economic Suicide:** The unit economics are inverted. High licensing costs paired with a "no extra charge" model destroy margin on the very users generating all current profit. With a small, old catalogue and poor quality, the value add is negligible compared to the cost.
2.  **Cannibalization without Upside:** Broadband penetration in 2007 is insufficient to support a full transition. Encouraging users to switch to an inferior experience accelerates churn to future competitors without securing loyalty to the new format.
3.  **Timing Mismatch:** Entering a nascent market against well-funded tech entrants (who control the pipes and devices) while hamstrung by high content costs and a legacy infrastructure is a strategic overreach. The company lacks the capital structure to fight a price war with Google or Apple.
4.  **Failure of Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option preserves the cash cow. A "paid add-on" or "delayed launch" would test demand without gifting the core business away. This proposal beats neither.

**Blockers:**
*   **Negative Unit Economics:** The plan explicitly pairs high costs (licensing) with zero incremental revenue, guaranteeing immediate profit erosion.
*   **Strategic Contradiction:** It deploys capital to build a product that is objectively worse (quality/catalog) than what competitors will soon offer, while undermining the only product currently working.

**Risks:**
*   Accelerated subscriber loss to better-funded entrants.
*   Irreversible damage to studio relationships due to low-price expectations.
*   Technology debt from premature scaling.

The proposal fails the basic test of preserving the engine that funds any future transition. It sacrifices a certain, massive profit for an uncertain, inferior foothold in a market not yet ready to sustain it. The correct move is to wait for bandwidth/cost curves to improve or to license the tech rather than build it at a loss.

DECISION: NO-GO
