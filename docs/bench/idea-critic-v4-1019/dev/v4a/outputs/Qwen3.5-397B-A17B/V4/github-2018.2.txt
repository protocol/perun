First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $7.5 billion to a loss-making asset with no permanent leadership, facing active hostility from its core user base and credible free alternatives, while the buyer has a historical reputation for antagonism toward the very community being acquired.

Steelman: Acquiring the dominant platform now secures the developer ecosystem before a competitor does, betting that independent operation and the buyer's resources can reverse losses and win over distrust faster than organic decline or a rival acquisition.

However, the facts presented reveal immediate blockers. The target is loss-making with no CEO, meaning the buyer inherits a leadership vacuum and negative cash flow at a premium valuation. The "vocal distrust" and existence of a "free competitor" create a high probability of immediate developer defection upon announcement, directly eroding the asset's primary value (network effects) before integration benefits can materialize. The buyer's history of hostility makes the promise of "independent" operation inherently non-credible to the community, rendering the mitigation strategy ineffective by default.

Alternatives: Doing nothing avoids burning $7.5 billion on a deteriorating asset. Waiting for the target to appoint a CEO and stabilize leadership reduces execution risk. Investing in organic growth or smaller acquisitions avoids the antitrust scrutiny and cultural backlash inherent in this specific merger.

Pre-mortem: The deal closes, key developers migrate to the free alternative en masse due to distrust, the platform loses its dominance within 18 months, and the buyer is left with a $7.5 billion write-down and heightened regulatory scrutiny for no gain.

Blockers: 
1. No leadership in place to execute the turnaround immediately post-acquisition.
2. Active community hostility combined with a viable free alternative creates an immediate, unmitigatable risk of value destruction (defection) that contradicts the thesis of maintaining dominance.
3. The buyer's historical stance makes the "independent operation" promise unbelievable to the stakeholders whose retention is critical.

Risks: Regulatory block, integration failure, continued losses.

The combination of a leadership vacuum, active user rebellion, and a credible free exit route means the core value proposition cannot be secured at the proposed price or terms.

DECISION: NO-GO
