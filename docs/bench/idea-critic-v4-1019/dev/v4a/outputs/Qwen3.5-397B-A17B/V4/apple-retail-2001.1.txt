First call: NO-GO. A loss-making manufacturer with single-digit share proposing high-fixed-cost retail in a low-margin, recession-prone industry contradicts basic unit economics.

**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only viable channel to demonstrate a complex digital-music strategy that third-party retailers refuse to support, potentially saving the core hardware business.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Suicide:** The company is already losing money. Adding prime-mall leases (high fixed costs) to a low-margin hardware business creates massive operating leverage against a recession. One quarter of missed sales targets triggers insolvency.
2.  **Channel Warfare:** Alienating existing resellers who currently provide the bulk of volume will crash immediate revenue before new stores can mature. The company cannot afford the transition gap.
3.  **Historical Precedent:** The prompt cites a "poor retail track record in the industry." Ignoring sector-wide failure patterns while lacking capital is negligence, not innovation.
4.  **Better Way:** The "digital-music strategy" demonstration does not require owning the real estate. Dedicated "shop-in-shop" zones within existing partner stores, or small experience kiosks, achieve the demo goal without the lease liability.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years later, the company is bankrupt. The stores drew foot traffic but conversion was too low to cover rent. Resellers dropped the brand in retaliation. The recession emptied the malls. The cash reserved for R&D was burned on build-outs.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capital Mismatch:** Loss-making status + high lease costs = immediate cash burn threat.
*   **Strategic Contradiction:** You cannot fight a recession by increasing fixed costs.
*   **Channel Dependency:** Current volume relies on the very partners this plan undermines.

**Risks:** Inventory obsolescence, inability to negotiate mall leases without creditworthiness, execution distraction from core product fixes.

**Conclusion:** The proposal bets the entire company on a high-cost channel shift during a downturn without a bridge to profitability. The "demo" requirement can be met via lower-cost alternatives (shop-in-shop, pop-ups) that do not threaten solvency. The fixed-cost structure is a fatal flaw given the current loss position.

DECISION: NO-GO
