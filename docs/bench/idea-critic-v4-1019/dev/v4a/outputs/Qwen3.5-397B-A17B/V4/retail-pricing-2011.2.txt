First call: NO-GO. The proposal eliminates the primary purchasing trigger (discounts) for the core demographic without evidence they will accept everyday pricing, while simultaneously imposing massive operational disruption via store rebuilds.

**Steelman:** Eliminating coupon clutter simplifies the brand and improves margins, while branded shops modernize the experience to attract younger shoppers, replicating a successful electronics retail model.

**Attack:** The steelman fails on fit and evidence. The customer base is explicitly "older" and "coupon-driven," with most sales historically occurring at a discount. This indicates deep behavioral conditioning, not mere preference. Removing discounts entirely ignores the "what would have to be true" condition: that these customers value simplicity over savings enough to change lifelong habits. The cited success in electronics does not transfer; electronics buyers seek tech authority and fixed specs, whereas department store shoppers in this segment seek bargain hunting as a recreational activity.

**Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option preserves current cash flow while testing small pricing adjustments. A "cheaper alternative" involves piloting the everyday pricing model in 5–10 stores to measure churn before a chain-wide rollout. The current plan chooses the most expensive, highest-risk path (full rebuild + total pricing shift) with zero validation.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in Q4 2012. Cause: Loyal customers stop visiting immediately upon seeing full prices; new customers do not arrive fast enough to offset the loss because the store rebuilds cause inventory confusion and reduced foot traffic during construction. The company runs out of cash before the brand perception shifts.

**Blockers:**
1. **Contradictory Evidence:** The proposal admits most sales come from discounted items yet removes discounts chain-wide without a pilot. This mathematically guarantees an immediate revenue cliff.
2. **Missing Validation:** No pilot exists for either the pricing model or the store layout. Executing both simultaneously creates unisolated variables; if sales drop, leadership cannot know if the cause was price or construction disruption.

**Risks:** Customer alienation, cash flow collapse during rebuilds, competitor exploitation of the price gap.

**Decision:** The plan lacks a survival mechanism for the transition period. It bets the entire company on an unproven hypothesis regarding a resistant demographic.

DECISION: NO-GO
