First call: NO-GO. Removing a universal, beloved feature (headphone jack) to force adoption of an unproven, expensive ($159), and fragile accessory (easy to lose, odd look) creates immediate customer anger without a guaranteed upside.

**Steelman:** Removing the jack accelerates a wireless future, and premium earbuds with a charging case solve the battery and pairing friction, justifying the cost through convenience and ecosystem lock-in.

**Attack:**
1.  **Value Mismatch:** Customers perceive $159 as a massive price hike compared to "free" wired included earphones. The "odd" design invites ridicule, undermining the premium positioning.
2.  **Functional Regression:** Five hours of battery life is insufficient for heavy users, and the high risk of loss makes them a liability compared to durable wired options.
3.  **Customer Backlash:** Removing the jack is a one-way door that alienates the existing user base before the replacement is proven desirable. The anger is immediate; the adoption is speculative.
4.  **Alternatives:** A cheaper, transitional dongle or retaining the jack while offering wireless as an optional upgrade avoids the backlash while testing the market. The proposal forces a binary choice that favors the company's roadmap over user readiness.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch is mocked for the design; sales stall due to price; customers revolt over the missing jack, driving them to competitors who retain it. The company is seen as arrogant, not innovative.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal admits removing the jack "may anger customers" and the product looks "odd." These are stated negatives that remove the route to upside (customer delight) without a mitigation plan.
*   **Fact:** $159 is a high barrier against a free alternative currently in the box.
*   **Fact:** No evidence is provided that customers *want* this specific form factor or price point; it is assumed.

**Risks:** Battery anxiety, loss frequency, market rejection of the design.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It trades a certain negative (anger, loss of utility) for an uncertain positive based on unproven assumptions about design acceptance and price elasticity. The "cheaper, safer alternative" (transitionary approach) reaches the same upside (wireless adoption) without the immediate backlash.

DECISION: NO-GO
