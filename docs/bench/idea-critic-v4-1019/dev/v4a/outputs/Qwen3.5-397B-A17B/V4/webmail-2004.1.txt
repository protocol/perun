First call: GO. The proposal trades a rapidly commoditizing cost (storage) for a defensible differentiation (searchability and permanence) in a market where incumbents are constrained by legacy cost structures.

Steelman: By offering 1,000x the standard storage, the service renders manual deletion obsolete, positioning "search" as the primary interface rather than "file management." This creates a sticky user base whose data trains better ad targeting, justifying the infrastructure spend via high-LTV text ads.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Cede the shift from local clients to cloud storage to competitors.
2. Match incumbents (few MBs): Guarantees irrelevance; users already hate deleting mail.
3. Paid model only: Fails to achieve the network effects required for ad-data scale.

Fit: Aligns with a search company's core competency (indexing massive datasets) and north star (organizing information). The assumption is that storage costs will drop faster than ad yields decline.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if privacy backlash over content scanning triggers regulatory bans or mass user exodus before ad revenue scales. However, the "invitation-only" launch gates this risk, allowing controlled exposure. The cost of delay is high; waiting allows another player to define "cloud email" first.

Evidence: The proposal correctly identifies that "routine deletion" is a pain point, not a preference. Storage costs in 2004 are trending down sharply (Moore's Law applied to disks), making 1GB sustainable where it was previously prohibitive. The ad model relies on the premise that users trade privacy for utility, a behavior already established in search.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (cost, privacy, competition) are structural industry challenges, not factual contradictions. The cost argument assumes static pricing, ignoring historical trends. Privacy is a reputational risk, not a functional blocker, manageable via terms of service and opt-in growth. Competition is entrenched only in low-storage paradigms; this proposal changes the paradigm.

Risks: Privacy backlash could slow adoption; ad relevance may initially be low without sufficient data volume. These are mitigated by the invitation-only rollout and the inherent value of never deleting email.

Kill criteria: If user retention drops below 40% after 6 months due to privacy concerns, or if cost-per-user exceeds ad-revenue-per-user by 2x after 12 months, pivot to a tiered paid model. Leading metric: Daily active users performing search queries.

DECISION: GO
