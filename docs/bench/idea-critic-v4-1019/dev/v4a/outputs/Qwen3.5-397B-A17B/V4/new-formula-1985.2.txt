First call: NO-GO. Replacing a heritage asset based solely on blind taste data ignores the brand's emotional equity.

**Steelman:** The data proves a sweeter formula wins on immediate sensory preference; swapping it should maximize volume by satisfying the primary use case (taste).

**Attack:** The proposal confuses "preferred in isolation" with "preferred as a replacement." Blind tests explicitly removed the variable of *loss*. By withdrawing the original entirely, the company transforms a product launch into a forced subtraction of a cultural icon. The risks listed (emotional attachment, heritage) are not merely "risks" but the core value driver of the brand, which the taste tests failed to measure because participants were unaware the original would vanish.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain share erosion but preserve brand trust.
2.  **Line Extension:** Launch the sweeter formula as "New Cola" alongside the original. This captures the sweet-preference segment without alienating loyalists or triggering a backlash over removal.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** High risk of consumer revolt due to the "endowment effect"—people value what they have more than what they are offered, especially when the existing option is revoked.

**Fit:** A strategy that ignores "brand heritage" while selling a heritage product is a strategic mismatch. The north star is total brand loyalty, not just sip-test scores.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch triggers a public relations crisis. Loyal consumers feel betrayed, not upgraded. Media narratives shift from "taste" to "corporate arrogance." Sales plummet not because the new drink tastes bad, but because the old one is gone.

**Blocker:** The testing methodology is fundamentally flawed for this specific decision. It measured *addition* preference but is being used to justify *subtraction*. The stated fact that "testing did not tell participants the original would be removed" invalidates the data for a replacement strategy. The plan fails its own evidence test because the evidence does not support the specific action of withdrawal.

**Decision:** NO-GO. The plan relies on data that explicitly excludes the primary driver of the predicted failure (loss aversion).

**Kill Criteria:** If proceeding with a modified plan, stop immediately if net promoter score drops by >5 points in week one or if social sentiment regarding "removal" exceeds 20% negative.

DECISION: NO-GO
