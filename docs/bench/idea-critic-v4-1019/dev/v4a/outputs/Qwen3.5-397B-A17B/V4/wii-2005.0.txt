First call: GO. The proposal targets an unserved mass market (casual/non-gamers) with a distinct value proposition (motion control, low cost) that rivals ignoring.

**Steelman:** By abandoning the specs war, the console creates a new category where it is the only player, monetizing volume over hardware margin via software attachment among families.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Remain a distant third in a saturated hardcore market; likely eventual exit.
*   *High-Def Race:* Compete directly with entrenched leaders on their terms; requires massive R&D, high retail price, and offers no differentiator. High risk of failure.
*   *The Blocker Test:* Skeptics cite "no HD" and "toy perception" as fatal. However, these are features, not bugs, for the target demographic who find complex controllers barriers. The "untested audience" is the entire opportunity; waiting for proof means missing the window. The thin hardware margin is acceptable if the attach rate (software sales per unit) is high, which simple, addictive games drive.
*   *Pre-mortem:* Failure occurs if third-party support collapses entirely or the motion tech is unreliable. Mitigation: First-party titles must prove the concept immediately at launch to drive install base, forcing third parties to follow later.

**Fit:** This moves the north star from "best graphics" to "biggest audience." It fits a stage where catching up in power is impossible, so changing the game is necessary.

**Decision Logic:**
*   *Blockers:* NONE. The stated risks are uncertainties inherent to innovation, not facts proving failure. The lack of HD does not prevent the core value prop (motion gaming) from working.
*   *Risks:* Third-party hesitation, brand perception. These become kill criteria: if software attachment rates do not exceed industry average within 12 months, pivot.
*   *Cheaper Alternative:* No cheaper path exists to capture this specific demographic without new hardware.

The proposal survives the attack because the "flaws" are actually the strategic wedge. The market does not need another black box under the TV; it needs a living room activity.

DECISION: GO
