First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to high-volume production ($5,000 price, 40,000 units/year capacity) despite unproven consumer demand, unclear legal status, and significant usability limitations.

Steelman: A revolutionary personal transporter with strong backing can create a new urban mobility category if launched decisively at scale to capture early adopters and establish brand dominance before regulations solidify.

However, the facts provided reveal fatal blockers. The legal status on sidewalks and roads is "unclear." Launching 40,000 units into a regulatory void risks immediate impoundment, bans, or liability suits that could bankrupt the venture before market fit is found. Furthermore, the price ($5,000) is high, and uses beyond institutional buyers are "unproven." Committing factory capacity for 40,000 units assumes mass consumer adoption that the proposal explicitly admits is unverified. The identified risks—conspicuousness, low utility (cannot carry much), and high cost—directly attack mass-market viability.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids catastrophic capital loss if the product fails legally or commercially.
2. Pilot launch: Produce a limited run (e.g., 500 units) for institutional buyers and select cities to test legal frameworks and user behavior before scaling factory size. This matches the "unproven" nature of the use cases.
3. Revise proposal: Delay factory sizing until legal clarity and unit economics are validated.

The proposal fails the blocker test: it commits to irreversible capital expenditure (factory sizing) and mass production while admitting the core premises (legal clarity, consumer demand) are unknown. A "go" decision here ignores the pre-mortem reality that unsold inventory and legal battles are the likely outcome of scaling an unproven, legally ambiguous product. The risks are not merely execution difficulties; they are fundamental threats to the business model's existence. The correct path is a staged approach, not the proposed immediate scale.

DECISION: NO-GO
