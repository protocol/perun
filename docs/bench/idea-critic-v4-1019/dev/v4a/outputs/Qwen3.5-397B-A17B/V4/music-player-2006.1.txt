First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market as a late follower, abandoning the very copy-protection standard the company previously championed to partners, which guarantees immediate ecosystem hostility and legal friction without a compensating price or feature advantage.

Steelman: The company can leverage its brand and a superior screen to disrupt the market by offering unrestricted wireless sharing, betting that convenience outweighs label restrictions and late entry.

Options:
1. **Do Nothing:** Avoid massive CAPEX/OPEX; maintain status quo while the dominant player solidifies its lock-in.
2. **Licensing:** License the dominant player's technology or content store instead of building a duplicate infrastructure.
3. **The Proposal:** Build hardware, store, and subscription from scratch. This option has the highest cost, highest reversibility friction (hardware inventory), and lowest probability of label cooperation given the abandoned DRM standard.

Fit: Entering a "winner-take-most" network effect business years late contradicts prudent capital allocation unless a unique, defensible moat exists. Wireless sharing is a feature, not a moat, especially if labels restrict it.

Pre-mortem: The device launches, but labels refuse content due to the lack of DRM, rendering the store empty. The marketing budget burns cash acquiring users who find no music, forcing a price war the company cannot win against the incumbent's economies of scale. The hardware becomes inventory write-downs.

Evidence: The proposal admits "no clear price advantage," "late to market," and "labels may restrict sharing." These are not risks; they are stated facts that dismantle the core value proposition. Abandoning the company's own promoted DRM standard alienates existing partners without a guaranteed replacement.

Blockers:
1. **Content Unavailability:** The stated likelihood of label restrictions on sharing, combined with the removal of the industry-standard DRM, creates a high probability of an empty or legally constrained store at launch.
2. **Late Entry Cost:** Entering a mature ecosystem without a price or exclusive content advantage violates basic market entry logic; the marketing budget cannot buy a network effect that the product cannot sustain.

Risks: Hardware failure, subscription churn, brand dilution.

The plan fails on its own stated facts: it offers no advantage to overcome the barriers it explicitly acknowledges.

DECISION: NO-GO
