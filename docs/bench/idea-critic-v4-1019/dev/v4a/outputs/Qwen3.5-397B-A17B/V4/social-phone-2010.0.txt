First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a price-sensitive demographic with a premium-priced data plan and a product missing the primary value driver (apps) that defines the competitor's success.

Steelman: Leveraging an acquired brand to launch a closed, carrier-exclusive ecosystem could succeed if the social experience is so superior it outweighs the lack of third-party apps and high cost.

However, the attack reveals fatal blockers. First, the target market (teenagers) is explicitly mismatched with the pricing strategy; a data plan costing as much as a full smartphone plan is prohibitive for users with low disposable income, especially when rivals offer comparable hardware with established app stores. Second, launching with "no application store" in 2010 is a category error; the market has already shifted where the OS value is the ecosystem, not just the device. A new, unproven OS without apps cannot compete against entrenched rivals with large catalogs, regardless of the acquired brand's popularity.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids sinking further capital into a misaligned product.
2. Port the acquired brand's experience to existing, popular platforms (iOS/Android) to capture the user base immediately without hardware risk.
3. Launch the hardware later, only after the app ecosystem is viable, rather than forcing a premature launch due to sunk costs.

The "two years of development sunk" is a fallacy; continuing to invest in a flawed go-to-market strategy compounds the loss. The pre-mortem is clear: the phones launch, teens reject the high monthly cost and lack of apps, sales flatline, and the brand equity is wasted. The cost of delay is lower than the cost of a failed launch that alienates the core demographic.

Blockers:
1. Price mismatch: Target demographic cannot afford the mandatory data plan relative to competitors.
2. Feature gap: No app store at launch contradicts the primary purchase driver for smartphones in 2010.

Risks: Brand dilution, carrier dependency, total loss of sunk R&D if pivoted.

The plan fails on basic unit economics and product-market fit for the stated year and demographic.

DECISION: NO-GO
