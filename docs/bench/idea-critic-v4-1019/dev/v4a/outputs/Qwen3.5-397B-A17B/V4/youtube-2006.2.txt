First call: NO-GO. The price is astronomical for an asset with zero revenue and existential legal threats.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant platform instantly secures the category leader, neutralizes a future competitor, and captures massive user growth that organic efforts cannot match, betting that legal liabilities can be managed or monetized later.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Continue building the smaller internal service; risk being displaced as the market standardizes around the target.
2.  **Build/Buy Smaller:** Acquire a cheaper, compliant competitor; forfeit the network effect of the market leader.
3.  **Acquire Target:** Pay $1.65B for immediate dominance but inherit massive debt-like liabilities (lawsuits, bandwidth).

**Fit:** Aligns with organizing the world's information, but the financial exposure threatens the entire corporation's stability if the ad model fails or lawsuits succeed.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, but media giants win injunctions forcing the removal of all premium content. Users leave due to sterile content library. Bandwidth costs continue to bleed cash while ad revenue remains negligible. The stock price collapses under the weight of wasted capital and legal fees. This is a one-way door; the stock spent cannot be recovered.

**Evidence & Calculation:** The target has no meaningful revenue. At $1.65B, the buyer is paying purely for potential. The "very small staff" cannot manage the "large volume" of copyrighted material legally. The threats are not hypothetical; they are active. The math does not work on current fundamentals; it requires a miraculous shift in copyright law or advertiser behavior.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Financial Suicide Risk:** Paying $1.65B for negative cash flow and unquantified legal liability violates basic fiduciary duty without a guaranteed path to profitability.
2.  **Existential Legal Threat:** Active lawsuits from media companies could result in the platform being shut down entirely, rendering the asset worthless.

**Risks:** Ad model failure, bandwidth costs exceeding revenue, cultural integration failure.

The potential upside does not justify the immediate risk of corporate collapse via litigation or cash burn. The plan fails the basic test of solvency and legal viability as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
