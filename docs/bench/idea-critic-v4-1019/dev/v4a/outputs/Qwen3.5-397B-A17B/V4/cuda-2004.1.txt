First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits massive, certain costs to every unit sold for a market that does not yet exist, directly threatening the company's survival in its core business.

**Steelman:** The company bets its entire product line on creating a new general-purpose computing category before competitors do, accepting short-term margin destruction to secure a monopoly in a future high-value market.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain dominance in gaming/workstation graphics; zero added cost; risk being disrupted later if programmable graphics become standard.
2.  **Specialized SKU:** Build programmable features only for expensive workstation cards; limits cost exposure but slows ecosystem adoption.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Subsidize R&D and tooling via every consumer chip; highest cost, highest risk, potential total market transformation.

**Fit:** This contradicts the immediate north star of winning the core gaming race. It assumes a future scientific market will materialize quickly enough to offset current competitive losses.

**Pre-mortem:** The program fails because the added silicon area reduces gaming performance or raises prices, causing gamers to switch to the rival's cheaper/faster chips. The scientific market remains niche academic experimentation, never generating enough revenue to cover the billions in lost gaming market share. The company goes bankrupt funding a future that arrives too late.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "no large paying market exists yet" and relies on "academic users experimenting." There are no committed customers to validate demand. The cost is immediate and universal; the revenue is hypothetical and distant.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Suicide:** Raising unit costs and diverting engineering resources in a "core race with its main rival" without a guaranteed offsetting revenue stream creates an immediate existential threat.
*   **Missing Demand:** No committed customers means the upside is purely speculative.

**Risks:** Engineering distraction, niche demand remaining niche, competitor undercutting on price/performance.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. You cannot bet the entire company on a non-existent market while actively losing the current one. A safer path is required (e.g., specialized SKUs or waiting for clearer demand signals).

DECISION: NO-GO
