First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to massive fixed costs (40k unit factory) and a premium price point before resolving fundamental regulatory and market-fit uncertainties.

**Steelman:** Launching a premium, high-capacity transporter now capitalizes on first-mover advantage and investor momentum to define a new category before competitors emerge.

**Attack:**
1.  **Regulatory Blocker:** The legal status on sidewalks and roads is explicitly "unclear." Deploying a 40,000-unit supply chain before legality is established risks creating stranded inventory if governments ban the device from primary use cases. This is a fatal mismatch between capacity and permission.
2.  **Market Fit vs. Price:** At $5,000, the device is inaccessible to most consumers, yet "uses beyond institutional buyers are unproven." Relying solely on institutional sales to fill a 40,000-unit factory is an unverified assumption with no evidence of demand volume.
3.  **Social Friction:** The risk that riders "may feel conspicuous" directly threatens mass adoption, which is required to justify the factory size. High visibility without clear utility leads to ridicule, not trendsetting.
4.  **Better Way:** A "do nothing" regarding the factory is superior. The alternative is a pilot program with contract manufacturing to test regulatory reception and actual usage patterns without sinking capital into fixed infrastructure. This preserves reversibility.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because cities ban the device six months post-launch due to safety concerns, leaving the company with a specialized factory, unsold inventory, and no legal place to use the product. The high price prevents a pivot to niche hobbyists quickly enough to save cash flow.

**Conclusion:** The proposal treats unproven regulatory acceptance and unverified mass-market demand as facts to justify irreversible capital expenditure. The "unclear" legal status is a hard blocker for a launch of this magnitude. The plan must be revised to a low-capacity pilot to validate legality and usage before scaling.

Blockers: Regulatory uncertainty creates a risk of total inventory stranding; unproven demand cannot justify 40k unit capacity.
Risks: Social stigma, high price limiting adoption, limited utility.

DECISION: NO-GO
