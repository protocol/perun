First call: NO-GO. Two declining businesses with different customers and uncertain synergies do not create a winning whole; they compound distress.

**Steelman:** The acquisition creates immediate scale and cross-selling opportunities that stabilize the restructured discounter before rivals dominate the market.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on merging two failing trajectories. Both entities suffer from declining sales and underinvested, aging stores. Combining them merges two weak balance sheets and two distinct, eroding customer bases without a proven mechanism to reverse the trend. The "synergy" of selling older brands in discount stores assumes brand equity exists despite the decline, which is unproven. Meanwhile, agile big-box rivals are growing, suggesting the market is shifting away from this specific department/discount hybrid model. The cost savings from merging corporate functions are uncertain and likely insufficient to offset the capital drain of renovating old stores and integrating disparate systems.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Focus capital on restructuring the existing discounter chain to compete directly with big-box rivals.
2.  **Organic Growth:** Invest in modernizing current stores rather than acquiring legacy debt and real estate.
3.  **Targeted Acquisition:** Buy specific assets (locations or brands) rather than the entire failing entity.

**Pre-mortem:** By 2007, the combined entity faces liquidity crises as integration costs overrun estimates, and the distinct customer bases fail to cross-pollinate. The older stores become anchors dragging down the profitable discount units, leading to a second restructuring or bankruptcy.

**Blockers:**
*   **Compounding Decline:** Both targets have negative momentum; no evidence suggests the combination reverses this.
*   **Customer Mismatch:** Distinct customer bases reduce cross-selling viability.
*   **Capital Misallocation:** Investing in old, underperforming stores while rivals grow creates a competitive disadvantage.

**Risks:** Integration failure, brand dilution, continued sales erosion.

**Kill Criteria:** If post-merger sales do not stabilize within 12 months, halt conversions and divest non-core assets.

The proposal lacks a credible path to growth and ignores the fundamental weakness of both parties.

DECISION: NO-GO
