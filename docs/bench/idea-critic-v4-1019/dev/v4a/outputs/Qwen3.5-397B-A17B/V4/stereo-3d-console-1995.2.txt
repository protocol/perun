First call: NO-GO. The product sacrifices core usability (comfort, visual fidelity) for a gimmick (stereoscopic 3D) that causes physical harm (eye strain), violating the primary mandate of entertainment hardware.

Steelman: A low-cost ($180) bridge device leveraging existing tech to capture market share between generations.
Attack: The "low cost" advantage is negated by a value proposition that actively degrades the user experience. Red monochrome displays limit game design and visual appeal compared to contemporaries. The form factor fails both portability (tabletop only) and ergonomics (eyepiece discomfort), alienating the handheld audience while offering inferior graphics to the home audience.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Preserve brand reputation; wait for the true next-gen home console.
2. True portable: Refine handheld tech for better battery/screen, ignoring 3D.
3. Proposed: High risk of brand damage due to physical discomfort and poor visuals.
The proposal loses to "do nothing" because the risks (health effects, poor software library) directly attack the core utility of a game console.

Fit: A bridge product must extend the brand's promise of quality play. This proposal contradicts that by introducing physical pain and visual limitations. It does not move the north star of "engaging entertainment"; it creates a barrier to it.

Pre-mortem: The device launches, reviewers cite headaches and poor graphics, sales stall, and the brand is associated with a failed gimmick rather than innovation. The two-year wait for the real home console makes this a distracting failure. One-way door: Manufacturing tooling and inventory write-downs are irreversible; brand trust takes years to rebuild.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits to red-only visuals, discomfort, and health risks. These are not hypotheticals; they are stated features/flaws. No evidence suggests consumers want headaches or poor graphics at a discount.

Blockers:
1. Stated health risk (eye strain/headaches) contradicts the fundamental requirement of safe consumer electronics.
2. Poor ergonomics and visual fidelity guarantee negative reception, rendering the $180 price point irrelevant.

Risks: Limited launch library; brand dilution.

Kill criteria: If user testing shows >10% report discomfort within 15 minutes, terminate immediately. Leading metric: Return rate due to "discomfort" vs. industry average.

DECISION: NO-GO
