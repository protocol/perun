First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on acquiring an unproven, low-revenue asset to solve a critical, existential failure where two prior internal attempts have already collapsed, introducing massive integration risk and personnel conflict for a high price.

Steelman: Acquiring a modern, object-oriented core is the only viable shortcut to replace a dated OS after repeated internal failures, despite the target's low sales, because the technology itself is sound and the founder's return secures essential architectural knowledge.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing: Market share continues to fall; eventual irrelevance.
2. License another vendor: Loss of platform control; perpetual royalties; potential strategic misalignment.
3. Third internal attempt: High probability of repeating past failures given the track record.

Fit: Assumes the company's survival depends on regaining technical relevance quickly. A modern core aligns with this, but the execution path is fraught.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition fails because the technical debt of migrating existing software proves insurmountable, the founder's return creates internal toxicity that stalls development, and the "modern" OS lacks the driver ecosystem needed for hardware compatibility, leading to a delayed, buggy release that accelerates market exit. The door is one-way; the capital is spent, and the old OS is abandoned before the new one is viable.

Evidence: The proposal admits the target sold few units (market validation missing), the price is high against revenue (financial strain), and the founder has a difficult history (execution risk). These are not hypothetical risks; they are stated facts that directly contradict the likelihood of success.

Blockers:
1. The target's lack of market success suggests the technology may not be as robust or ready as claimed, despite being "modern."
2. The founder's difficult history poses a direct threat to team cohesion and project leadership.
3. The high price relative to revenue creates a financial burden with no immediate ROI, risky for a company already losing share.

Risks: Migration complexity, ecosystem gaps, cultural clash.

The stated facts reveal fundamental flaws in the target's viability and the strategic fit of the founder. Without evidence that the technology works at scale or that the personnel risks can be mitigated, the plan fails the blocker test.

DECISION: NO-GO
