First call: GO. The proposal targets a clear gap (garage-friendly family van) using existing assets (compact platform, converted factory) to solve an immediate survival need post-bailout.

Steelman: Launching a front-wheel-drive, car-like minivan on a compact platform leverages current infrastructure to create a new consumer category, offering a capital-efficient path to profitability for a cash-strapped manufacturer.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing: High risk of continued decline; fails to utilize the bailout for transformation.
2. Traditional full-size van: Ignores the garage constraint and car-like driving demand; higher material costs.
3. New platform development: Prohibitive given the "little cash" constraint; too slow.

Fit: Aligns with survival (stage) and efficient mobility (vision). Reuses bailouts to pivot rather than sustain legacy failure.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if buyers strictly perceive it as a commercial vehicle or if the compact platform compromises cargo utility too severely. This is a two-way door; production can be scaled or halted based on early sales. Delay costs first-mover advantage in a nascent segment.

Evidence: The bailout provides the window; the compact platform exists; garage sizing is a physical constraint families face. No contradictory data provided.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (no existing category, cash shortage, copycats, platform limits) are actually arguments *for* this specific approach. "No category" implies blue-ocean opportunity; "little cash" mandates reusing the compact platform and converted factory; "copycats" validates the market potential; "space limits" are the trade-off for garage fit, which is the core value proposition. None of these facts prevent execution; they define the strategy.

Risks: Market education (commercial vs. family perception) and platform capacity constraints. These become kill criteria: if early marketing tests show >60% commercial intent, pivot messaging; if cargo volume falls below a viable threshold for a family of four, redesign the rear overhang.

The proposal turns constraints into features. The lack of a category is the opportunity. The cash shortage forces the efficient reuse of the compact platform. The decision is to proceed immediately to capture the undefined market before rivals react.

DECISION: GO
