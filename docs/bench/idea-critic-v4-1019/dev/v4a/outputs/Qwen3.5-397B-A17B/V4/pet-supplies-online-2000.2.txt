First call: NO-GO. The plan relies on selling goods below cost to acquire customers while simultaneously undertaking massive, cash-intensive expansions (IPO, TV ads, acquisition, warehouses) in a fragile capital market environment.

Steelman: Aggressively capture market share and infrastructure now to establish a monopoly position before competitors, using public markets to fund temporary losses.

The proposal fails the blocker test immediately. The core mechanism is selling products below the cost of goods sold (negative gross margin). In the pet supply sector, where shipping heavy, low-margin items is already costly, every additional unit sold increases the total loss. This is not a path to profitability; it is a recipe for rapid insolvency unless capital is infinite and permanent.

The plan requires four massive cash outflows simultaneously: IPO costs, national TV advertising (especially major sports broadcasts), acquiring a competitor, and building warehouses. These must be funded by the IPO proceeds. However, the proposal acknowledges that "capital markets could close." If markets tighten, the company cannot raise the funds needed to cover the operating losses generated by its below-cost pricing strategy. The business model collapses without continuous external financing.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve cash, fix unit economics, and grow organically.
2. Niche focus: Target high-margin segments instead of heavy, low-margin goods.
3. Controlled expansion: Build one warehouse and test local advertising before national scaling.

Pre-mortem: The IPO launches, but market sentiment shifts. The stock price drops. The company burns through cash on TV ads and below-cost sales. Unable to raise more debt or equity due to negative margins and market closure, it runs out of liquidity. The warehouses sit underutilized, and the acquired competitor becomes a dead weight.

Blockers:
1. Negative gross margins on core items combined with high shipping costs mean revenue does not cover variable costs.
2. Simultaneous execution of four capital-intensive initiatives exceeds likely IPO proceeds, especially given the risk of capital market closure.
3. No path to profitability is demonstrated; growth is purchased at a loss that scales with volume.

Risks: Competitor spending, customer acquisition costs, market timing.

DECISION: NO-GO
