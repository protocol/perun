First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits irreversible capital before validating the core business model in a single market.

Steelman: The company aims to secure an unassailable first-mover advantage by building massive, automated infrastructure across dozens of cities immediately, betting that speed of expansion outweighs the risk of unproven unit economics.

However, this claim fails the blocker test. The proposal explicitly admits the startup has "no operating track record" and has not yet "learning the unit economics of the first" market. Groceries are defined as "low-margin," making the business extremely sensitive to the very "customer-density needs" and operational efficiencies that can only be proven through live operation, not theoretical modeling.

Committing $1 billion to highly automated warehouses in over a dozen cities creates a rigid cost structure before the variable costs and revenue per order are known. If the first city reveals that 30-minute slots are unsustainable or that density targets are missed, the company is left with a dozen expensive, specialized white elephants and a massive fleet, with no ability to pivot. The "rapid pace" strategy directly contradicts the need to learn; you cannot learn if you bet the entire farm on the first lesson being perfect.

The risks listed (capital intensity, thin margins, density needs) are not merely potential downsides; they are active blockers when combined with zero data. A cheaper, safer alternative exists: prove profitability and repeatable unit economics in the initial metropolitan area, then expand sequentially using cash flow or staged capital raises tied to actual performance metrics. Building twelve more problems before solving the first one is not strategy; it is gambling with other people's money on a known failure mode of retail expansion.

Blockers: Commitment of $1B to fixed assets before validating unit economics in the pilot market; expansion plan prevents learning from the initial launch.
Risks: Low margins cannot support debt service on unused capacity; customer density may not materialize; automation may not handle real-world grocery variability.

DECISION: NO-GO
