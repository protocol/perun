First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the segment has shrunk for years, safety rules raise costs, and the company lacks heritage, while relying on a finite pool of enthusiasts before fading.

Steelman: A low-volume, lightweight roadster can profit by targeting underserved enthusiasts if unit economics remain positive despite low volume and high regulatory costs.

Options:
1. **Proposed:** High risk; relies on niche revival without heritage or cost advantage.
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoids sunk costs in a declining, regulation-heavy segment.
3. **Derivative:** Add a convertible trim to an existing high-volume model to share tooling and compliance costs, reducing risk.

Fit: Assuming the north star is profitable growth, entering a shrinking niche with no heritage contradicts market momentum. The "affordable" claim clashes with rising safety compliance costs for low-volume convertibles.

Pre-mortem: The car launches to press acclaim but fails to hit volume targets; per-unit costs skyrocket due to low absorption of fixed compliance/tooling costs; the brand dilutes without gaining performance credibility; the project is canceled after two years with heavy losses. This is a one-way door for capital; delay costs little as the niche is not expanding.

Evidence: The proposal itself states the segment has shrunk, safety rules increase costs, and sales may fade post-enthusiast wave. No data supports a reversal of these trends or a path to profitability at low volume.

Blockers:
1. **Economic Viability:** The proposal admits rising costs (safety) and falling demand (shrunk segment) without a mechanism to achieve profitability.
2. **Strategic Mismatch:** Entering a heritage-dependent category with no brand equity guarantees a discount price point, further crushing margins.
3. **Finite Demand:** Reliance on "early enthusiasts" with an expected fade confirms a lack of sustainable mass-market appeal.

Risks: Brand confusion, regulatory cost overruns, rapid sales collapse.

The plan fails the basic test of economic logic: it proposes high fixed costs in a low-volume, declining market without a unique cost advantage or brand premium. It must be revised to demonstrate positive unit economics or a shared-platform strategy before consideration.

DECISION: NO-GO
