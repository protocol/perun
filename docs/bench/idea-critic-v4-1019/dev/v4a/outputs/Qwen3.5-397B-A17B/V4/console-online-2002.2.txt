First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates broadband and a paid subscription in 2002, directly contradicting the market reality where few homes have broadband and players expect free online play.

Steelman: A unified, premium online service creates a sticky ecosystem and recurring revenue, differentiating the console through superior voice chat and identity management that fragmented publisher-led solutions cannot match.

However, the blocker is fatal. The proposal requires broadband connectivity as a hard constraint. In 2002, broadband penetration is too low to support a "console-wide" mandatory service; this artificially caps the total addressable market to a fraction of early adopters, preventing the network effects required for matchmaking and friends lists to function. Furthermore, the company lacks any operational history in running hosting and support infrastructure. Launching a mandatory paid service without this expertise invites catastrophic failure in reliability and customer trust.

Alternatives:
1. **Do nothing:** Allow publishers to handle online play. Risk: Fragmented experience, no recurring revenue. Upside: Zero operational risk, no barrier to entry for users.
2. **Hybrid/Optional:** Offer the service as an optional premium layer while supporting free, publisher-led online play for dial-up and broadband. Risk: Complex implementation. Upside: Captures early adopters without alienating the majority.
3. **Proposed (Paid + Broadband Only):** Risk: Market rejection, operational collapse. Upside: High if successful, but success is improbable given the constraints.

The cost of delay is low; waiting for broadband penetration to grow reduces the risk of a barren matchmaking pool. The cost of proceeding now is high: building an operations center from scratch while selling a service the majority of the installed base physically cannot use.

Blockers:
1. Mandatory broadband requirement exceeds 2002 household adoption rates, rendering the "console-wide" claim false.
2. Lack of internal operational capability for hosting/support creates an unmitigated execution risk.
3. Paid model conflicts with established user expectation of free online play, compounding the adoption barrier.

Risks:
1. Insufficient subscriber count to cover infrastructure costs.
2. Competitor free models capturing the entire market.
3. Service outages damaging the new console's brand.

The plan fails as proposed because its core constraints (broadband, paid) exclude the vast majority of the 2002 market, and the company is operationally unprepared to deliver the service.

DECISION: NO-GO
