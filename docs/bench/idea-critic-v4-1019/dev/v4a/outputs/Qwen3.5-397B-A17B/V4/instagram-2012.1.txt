First call: NO-GO. The price implies a valuation disconnected from any verified revenue or proven business model, creating an immediate destroy-of-value risk for shareholders if the standalone economics cannot be justified within a reasonable horizon.

**Steelman:** The acquisition is a defensive necessity to neutralize the primary mobile threat before the IPO locks in the buyer's legacy positioning, buying immediate scale and talent that cannot be built internally fast enough.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financials:** Paying $1B for zero revenue and an unproven model assumes future dominance without a clear path to monetization. If the "threat" proves fleeting or the model fails, this is a total write-down.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or "build/buy smaller" allows the market to validate the model first. Buying now prices in perfection.
3.  **Fit & Culture:** Integrating a dozen-person culture into a giant entity often destroys the very agility being purchased. The risk of cultural collapse is high, rendering the asset worthless.
4.  **Regulatory:** A dominant player buying the top competitor invites immediate antitrust scrutiny, potentially blocking the deal or forcing costly divestitures post-closing.
5.  **Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, the app's growth stalls due to integration friction, regulators sue, and the $1B is viewed as a panic premium paid for a feature, not a business.

**Blockers:**
*   **Valuation Mismatch:** No revenue multiple exists to justify $1B; the core claim rests entirely on speculative future market share defense.
*   **Strategic Panic:** The urgency stems from the buyer's own weak mobile product, suggesting a reactive rather than proactive strategy.

**Risks:** Regulatory block, cultural implosion, monetization failure, shareholder backlash pre-IPO.

**Decision Logic:** While the strategic threat is real, the financial terms lack any anchor in current reality. A "go" requires a capped, structured deal with earn-outs tied to specific retention and revenue milestones, not a flat $1B cash/stock dump. As proposed, the risk of capital destruction outweighs the defensive benefit.

DECISION: NO-GO
