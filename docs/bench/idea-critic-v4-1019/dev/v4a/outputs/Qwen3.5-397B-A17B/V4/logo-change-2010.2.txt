First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a high-risk, irreversible change to the brand's primary asset without validation, relying on a "big bang" rollout that eliminates the ability to retreat if the market reacts negatively.

Steelman: A simultaneous, unannounced global rebrand creates a shocking, unified signal of modernity that prevents the brand from appearing hesitant or fragmented during the transition.

However, the claim fails the blocker test. The proposal explicitly admits the new design was "not tested with customers" while acknowledging that "loyal customers are attached to the old logo." In the context of 2010, where social media can amplify dissatisfaction instantly, launching an untested visual identity to every channel simultaneously is not a strategy; it is a gamble with the company's core recognition. The "small blue square" risks diluting the very asset (the dark blue square) that drives recognition.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retains equity but fails the contemporary goal.
2. Phased rollout with testing: Allows measurement of sentiment and adjustment before full commitment.
3. Hybrid approach: Modernize typography while retaining the iconic square prominently.

The proposed plan beats none of these. It carries the highest cost of failure (total brand alienation) with zero evidence of upside. The "no advance announcement" tactic removes the ability to manage the narrative, turning potential confusion into immediate backlash. The lack of customer testing is a fatal flaw when the specific risk cited is customer attachment to the current logo.

Blockers:
1. Zero validation: Launching an untested design against known customer attachment guarantees avoidable risk.
2. Irreversibility: Rolling out to "every channel at once" makes correction slow and costly once negative feedback loops begin on social media.

Risks:
1. Immediate sales drop due to customer confusion or perceived loss of heritage.
2. Social media backlash framing the brand as out of touch with its own history.

This is a one-way door decision made without a map. The cost of delay (waiting for testing) is negligible compared to the cost of a failed rebrand.

DECISION: NO-GO
