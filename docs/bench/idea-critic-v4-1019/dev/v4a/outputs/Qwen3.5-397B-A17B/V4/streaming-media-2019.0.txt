First call: NO-GO. The proposal substitutes a guaranteed, high-margin licensing revenue stream for a speculative, capital-intensive venture with unproven execution capabilities and a clear path to massive cash burn.

Steelman: Direct-to-consumer distribution captures full lifetime value per user and secures strategic independence, justifying short-term losses if subscriber acquisition costs remain low and churn is minimal.

Options analysis reveals the "do nothing" stance preserves billions in annual licensing fees with zero execution risk. The proposed "low price" strategy commoditizes the asset, requiring unsustainable volume to offset the immediate loss of rival payments. Building a global technology platform from scratch introduces a high probability of technical failure or delay, a domain where the conglomerate has no prior success.

Pre-mortem: The initiative fails because the operating loss exceeds capital reserves before reaching the tens of millions of subscribers needed for unit economics to work. Simultaneously, cannibalization of existing cable and theatrical windows erodes the core business faster than the new service grows, creating a "valley of death" the company cannot cross. The one-way door nature of pulling content from rivals means一旦 the bridge is burned, re-licensing at previous rates becomes impossible if the new venture stalls.

Evidence check: The proposal relies entirely on assumed future subscriber growth without citing current market penetration data or a verified customer acquisition strategy. The "multi-year operating loss" is treated as a feature rather than a fatal flaw given the lack of existing tech infrastructure.

Blockers:
1. **Revenue Contradiction**: The plan requires destroying a known, profitable revenue stream (licensing) to fund a hypothetical one, with no mathematical bridge showing the net present value of the new service exceeds the lost licensing fees within a safe timeframe.
2. **Capability Gap**: The organization has zero verified experience running a global streaming technology platform, making the execution risk a near-certain failure point without external acquisition or partnership, which is not proposed.

Risks: Massive cash burn, brand dilution via low pricing, and irreversible loss of distribution partners.

DECISION: NO-GO
