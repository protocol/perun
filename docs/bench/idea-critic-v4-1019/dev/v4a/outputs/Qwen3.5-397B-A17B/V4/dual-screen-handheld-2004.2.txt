First call: GO. The proposal avoids a direct hardware war against a superior rival by creating a distinct market category (broad audience, lower price) rather than competing on raw graphics power.

**Steelman:** By leveraging unique dual-screen/touch interaction, the company can offer novel gameplay experiences that the rival's superior graphics cannot replicate, effectively neutralizing the competitor's hardware advantage while retaining the installed base via the legacy device.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Ceding the high-end to the rival while relying solely on aging tech guarantees eventual obsolescence as developers chase the rival's graphics.
2.  **Match Hardware:** Entering a specs war against a "well-funded" rival with stronger media features is a financial trap; winning requires unsustainable R&D spend for a "me-too" product.
3.  **Risks:** While developer confusion and "gimmick" perceptions are valid, they are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The "unproven" nature of touch is precisely the opportunity to define a new standard before the rival adapts. The cost of two screens is mitigated by targeting a lower price point than the rival's premium device, suggesting a strategic cost structure rather than a bloated one.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This fits a strategy of market expansion (beyond core players). If this fails, it will likely be due to a lack of compelling "killer apps" that utilize the second screen, not the hardware concept itself. The delay cost is high; waiting allows the rival to cement developer loyalty.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. No fact provided proves the plan fails as proposed. The lower price target contradicts the fear that two screens make it too expensive to compete.
*   **Risks:** Developer adoption, consumer perception of gimmickry, unproven touch mechanics. These become kill criteria: if no major titles utilize the touch screen within 12 months, or if sales do not exceed the legacy unit within two quarters, the strategy must pivot.

The proposal successfully identifies a path to victory that does not require beating the rival at their own game. It turns a hardware disadvantage into a software differentiator.

DECISION: GO
