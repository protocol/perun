First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a massive, irreversible capital expenditure (new brand, new dealer network) based on a market segment that is explicitly acknowledged as potentially shrinking due to recession risk, without a staged entry mechanism.

**Steelman:** Launching a dedicated mid-priced division with unique dealerships captures the growing "step-up" market before competitors, leveraging extensive consumer research to ensure product-market fit upon the 1957 debut.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing & Macro Risk:** The plan targets a 1957 debut despite admitted recession risks and a potential segment shrinkage. Committing to fixed infrastructure (dealerships) before the economic cycle confirms demand is a classic value-destruction error. If the segment contracts, the new dealer network becomes an immediate, unrecoverable liability.
2.  **Irreversibility:** Building a separate dealer network is a "one-way door." Unlike using existing showrooms or a sub-brand, this creates high fixed costs that cannot be shed quickly if the launch coincides with a downturn.
3.  **Cannibalization:** The proposal admits risks of overlap with the firm's own low and premium lines. Without a confirmed mechanism to prevent internal competition, the new division may simply shuffle sales rather than generate net growth, while doubling overhead.
4.  **Better Way:** A "go-if" approach using existing dealerships to sell the new models would test demand with fraction of the capital risk. The current plan rejects this cheaper alternative in favor of a full-scale rollout.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The segment "could shrink" and a "recession risk exists."
*   **Fact:** The plan requires a "new dealer network" (high fixed cost).
*   **Conflict:** Locking in high fixed costs immediately when the primary revenue driver (segment growth) is uncertain and potentially negative violates basic capital allocation logic for a cyclical industry. The proposal offers no pilot, no phased rollout, and no kill criteria tied to pre-launch economic indicators.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, dealer bankruptcy during downturn, failure to differentiate from the market leader's established mid-priced offerings.

**Conclusion:** The proposal fails the reversibility and timing test. It bets the division's liquidity on a specific economic outcome (continued growth) that the proposal itself flags as doubtful. A staged entry via existing channels is mandatory before committing to independent infrastructure.

DECISION: NO-GO
