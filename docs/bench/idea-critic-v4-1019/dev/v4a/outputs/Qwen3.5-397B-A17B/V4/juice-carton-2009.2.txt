First call: NO-GO. The proposal removes the brand's primary shelf cue—the orange with a straw—which is explicitly identified as the main recognition driver for a loyal, older demographic.

Steelman: A modernized design with a cap-shaped orange and vertical text will attract new buyers while retaining existing ones through aggressive advertising and recipe consistency.

Attack: The steelman fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests. The proposal admits the old image is the "main shelf cue" yet plans to remove it entirely. For an older, loyal base, visual continuity is the product identity; the straw is the heuristic for "drinkable orange." Replacing this with a "minimalist glass" and a "cap shaped like an orange" introduces high cognitive friction. The vertical rotation further degrades legibility on crowded shelves.

Alternatives: "Do nothing" preserves shelf share at zero redesign cost. A "staged pilot" in a limited region would test recognition failure before committing to a costly nationwide print run and ad campaign. The proposal rejects staging for a "all stores at once" launch, maximizing downside risk.

Pre-mortem: Assume the launch failed badly. Why? Shoppers walked past the cartons, unable to locate the brand they have bought for years. The advertising campaign was insufficient to retrain decades of muscle memory instantly. The cost of delay (waiting a quarter) is negligible compared to the cost of a nationwide recall or permanent market share loss to competitors who maintain clear shelf cues.

Blockers: The proposal contains an internal contradiction: it cites the loss of the main shelf cue as a risk but proceeds with a design that eliminates that cue entirely without a verified migration path (like a transition period showing both images). The evidence provided (loyal, older base) directly contradicts the strategy (radical minimalism and vertical text).

Risks: High execution difficulty in retraining an older demographic; massive sunk cost in printed cartons if recognition fails; irreversible brand damage if customers switch brands during confusion.

Kill criteria: If a blind shelf test shows >15% increase in time-to-locate, stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
