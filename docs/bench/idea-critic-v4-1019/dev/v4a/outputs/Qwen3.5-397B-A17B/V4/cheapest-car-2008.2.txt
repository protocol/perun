First call: NO-GO. The "cheapest" positioning invites a safety stigma that could be fatal for a family vehicle, while land protests threaten the dedicated factory model required to hit the $2,500 price point.

Steelman: A dedicated high-volume factory enables a breakthrough $2,500 price, successfully migrating scooter families to four-wheel safety despite "cheapest" stigma and land disputes.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** High risk. Land protests delay the only path to the target cost; "cheapest" branding may repel the very families seeking safety upgrades.
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoids capital exposure but cedes the low-end market to used cars and scooters.
3. **Alternative (Staged):** Launch via existing contract manufacturing to bypass land protests and validate demand before committing to a dedicated plant. This sacrifices some margin for speed and risk reduction.

Fit: Moving families to cars aligns with the vision, but the execution strategy (dedicated factory + "cheapest" label) conflicts with the stated risks of land acquisition and quality perception.

Pre-mortem: The project fails because farmer protests halt construction indefinitely, causing costs to spiral above $2,500. Simultaneously, the "cheapest" marketing triggers safety concerns, leading buyers to choose used cars instead. The door is one-way; the factory investment is sunk before demand is proven.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the factory site faces protests and that buyers may reject the "cheapest" label. These are not hypotheticals but stated facts within the plan that directly undermine the core cost and revenue assumptions.

Blockers:
1. **Land Risk:** The dedicated factory is essential for the $2,500 price, yet the site faces active protests. This is a factual contradiction in the current plan.
2. **Positioning Paradox:** Marketing as "the cheapest" directly fuels the stated risk that buyers will doubt safety, undermining the primary value prop for families.

Risks: Execution complexity, supply chain scaling, and competitor response.

The plan relies on a factory that may not be built and a brand message that may repel customers. A staged approach using existing capacity would test the market without the immediate blocker of land acquisition.

DECISION: NO-GO
