First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the core brand identity and value proposition with unverified assumptions about adult demand.

**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can temporarily override brand perception, forcing adults to trial a premium burger before competitors react.

**Attack:** The plan fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests. The chain's equity is built on value and child-centricity; a premium price point directly alienates the existing base without guaranteeing adult adoption. The proposal admits adults view the brand as "fast, not premium," yet offers no mechanism to shift this deep-seated perception other than spending money. The "largest advertising budget" is a blocker, not an asset, because it scales a potentially flawed premise. If the product-market fit is weak, maximum spend only accelerates capital destruction.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve margins and brand clarity.
2.  **Pilot:** Test the premium burger in adult-heavy markets with minimal marketing to validate demand before scaling.
3.  **Reposition:** Launch a "better" value meal for adults rather than a "premium" one, aligning with the existing value perception.

**Pre-mortem:** The campaign launches, costs skyrocket, and adults reject the food as "overpriced fast food" while loyal families feel priced out or ignored. Same-store sales drop due to traffic loss, and the stock price collapses under the weight of wasted marketing spend.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Positioning:** The plan explicitly targets a demographic that currently perceives the brand as incompatible with "premium," with no evidence that advertising alone can bridge this gap.
*   **Unverified Spend:** Committing the "largest budget" before proving demand violates basic capital allocation logic. The risk of deterring regular buyers is stated as a fact, yet the plan proceeds anyway.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, loss of core family demographic, massive sunk costs.

**Conclusion:** The proposal relies on a "build it and they will come" fantasy supported only by budget size. It ignores the stated reality of customer perception and price sensitivity. Without a pilot to prove adults will pay a premium at this specific brand, the full-scale launch is reckless.

DECISION: NO-GO
