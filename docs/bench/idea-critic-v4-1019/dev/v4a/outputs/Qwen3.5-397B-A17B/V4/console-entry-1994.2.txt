First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to enter a duopoly as a novice platform holder without a killer application, facing a direct 3D launch from an incumbent.

Steelman: A new entrant can disrupt the console market by leveraging superior CD manufacturing economics and developer tools to bypass cartridge costs, enticing third-party studios to build a library that compensates for a lack of first-party hits.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Platform Inexperience:** The proposer has zero history as a platform holder. Managing an ecosystem, certifying games, and supporting developers is fundamentally different from manufacturing consumer electronics.
2.  **Incumbent Moat:** Two established competitors control loyal customer bases and possess iconic first-party franchises that drive hardware sales. A new box without equivalent "system sellers" is a hard sell.
3.  **Timing Collision:** An incumbent is launching a competing 3D console simultaneously. Competing head-to-head with a veteran during launch window is a high-probability failure mode.
4.  **Economic Trap:** Hardware margins are negative. Without guaranteed software volume to recoup losses, the company bleeds cash on every unit sold.

**Risks:**
*   Third-party developers may refuse to bet on an unproven platform despite cheaper tools.
*   The market may not distinguish the new 3D capabilities from the incumbent's offering.

**Better Ways:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid the massive capital burn of a platform war.
*   **Partner:** Instead of building a standalone console, attempt to become the CD-drive peripheral for an existing non-competitor or retry the cancelled partnership with different terms.
*   **Software Only:** Become a third-party publisher for the existing duopoly rather than a hardware rival.

**Pre-mortem:**
The console launches, developers hesitate to port hits due to low install base projections, sales miss targets, and the company cannot subsidize the hardware loss, leading to a rapid exit and brand damage.

**Evidence:**
The proposal itself admits "no experience," "loyal customers" for rivals, and "thin margins." These are not hypothetical risks; they are stated facts that constitute a fatal barrier to entry under the current plan. The cancellation by the partner already signals external validation failure.

**Kill Criteria:**
If the company cannot secure binding commitments from three major third-party publishers before tooling begins, the project must stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
