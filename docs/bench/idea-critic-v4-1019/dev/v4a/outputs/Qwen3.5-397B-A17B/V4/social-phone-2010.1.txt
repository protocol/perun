First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores fundamental market mismatch: teenagers require apps and low costs, yet this plan offers neither.

**Steelman:** Leverage the acquired brand's social cachet to lock teens into a carrier-exclusive ecosystem, using a custom OS to differentiate from rivals while recouping sunk development costs.

**Attack:**
1.  **Value Mismatch:** Teens live on social networks, which function as applications. Launching with "no application store" renders the primary user need unmet. Rivals offer vast catalogs; this offers a walled garden with no plants.
2.  **Price Barrier:** A mandatory data plan costing as much as a full smartphone plan is prohibitive for the target demographic, who are price-sensitive and often dependent on parental subsidies.
3.  **Strategic Fragmentation:** Building a new in-house OS instead of using the company's existing mobile platform doubles down on risk. It fragments the ecosystem, alienates existing developers, and ignores the "app gap" that kills non-mainstream OSs.
4.  **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** Two years of sunk development is a reason to cut losses, not a justification for a flawed go-to-market strategy. Throwing good money after bad to "use" the OS ignores that the product, as defined, cannot compete.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing/Port:** Port the acquired messaging features to the company's existing, supported mobile platform. Cost: Low. Risk: Low. Reversibility: High.
*   **Cheaper Entry:** Launch on an existing OS with a prepaid, no-contract model. Cost: Medium. Risk: Medium. Reversibility: Medium.

**Pre-mortem:** The phones launch, teens realize they cannot install specific social apps their peers use, the monthly cost is rejected by parents, and sales flatline. The carrier, seeing no uptake, drops the exclusive deal. The sunk cost becomes a total write-off.

**Blockers:**
*   **No App Store:** Directly contradicts the "live on social networks" user behavior.
*   **High Cost:** Pricing exceeds the demographic's willingness/ability to pay.
*   **Wrong OS:** Fragmentation ensures no developer support, guaranteeing the app gap remains permanent.

These are not risks; they are factual contradictions of the proposal's own premises. The plan fails on day one.

DECISION: NO-GO
