First call: NO-GO. The combination of a historically hostile buyer, a loss-making target, active community distrust, and a vocal free alternative presents an immediate, unmitigated risk of asset destruction where the core value (the community) flees before synergies can be realized.

**Steelman:** The acquisition secures the dominant development platform to prevent competitor lock-in, betting that operational independence and the buyer's resources will stabilize the target and monetize the user base faster than organic growth or a free rival can erode it.

**Attack:** The primary failure mode is not regulatory or financial, but cultural. The target's value is entirely network-dependent; if the "vocal distrust" triggers a mass migration to the free alternative upon deal announcement or closing, the $7.5 billion price tag purchases a hollow shell. The absence of a permanent CEO at the target indicates internal instability, making the integration of two disparate cultures even more volatile. The proposal relies on the assumption that "running independently" is sufficient to assuage a community that fundamentally distrusts the buyer's historical stance. There is no evidence presented that the buyer has successfully managed a similar community-led turnaround before.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids the $7.5B outlay and the risk of triggering a community exodus that validates the free competitor.
2.  **Partnership:** Secure integration without ownership to test cultural fit and community reaction at a lower cost and reversibility.

**Blockers:** The proposal lacks a concrete, verified mechanism to prevent immediate developer defection given the stated "vocal distrust." Without a pre-committed kill criterion or a specific, proven strategy to convert hostility into trust (beyond the vague promise of independence), the risk of buying a declining asset at a peak price is a factual blocker based on the provided premises. The "free competitor" offers a zero-cost exit ramp for the very users being purchased.

**Risks:** Regulatory scrutiny, integration failure, continued losses.

**Conclusion:** The plan fails the "do no harm" test for the core asset (the community) because the mitigation strategy (independence) is unproven against the specific, stated threat (historical hostility).

DECISION: NO-GO
