First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on unproven demand for a device that removes core functionality (recording) and social utility (speakers), while requiring a massive, risky upfront investment in a market where dealers are already skeptical.

**Steelman:** A low-cost, portable, personal music device leveraging existing components could unlock a new youth market if the convenience and price outweigh the lack of recording and social sharing.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Contradictory Evidence:** Dealers and staff, the primary channel to the customer, explicitly doubt the value proposition of a non-recording player. Ignoring this frontline intelligence without counter-evidence is fatal.
2.  **Unproven Demand:** The proposal admits "no market research shows demand." Launching a large production run based solely on assumption violates basic inventory risk management, especially for a product with no differentiating moat against competitors who can copy the "existing parts."
3.  **Strategic Misfit:** Removing the speaker and recording function creates a "worse" product by traditional metrics. Without data proving users prefer "antisocial" listening, this is a solution in search of a problem.

**Risks:**
*   Competitors copy the design immediately using the same off-the-shelf parts.
*   Large initial run leads to massive unsold inventory if the "youth" segment does not materialize.
*   Brand damage if the product is perceived as a crippled, cheap version of their core hi-fi offerings.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "do nothing" test. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed large-scale launch. The "cheaper alternative" is a small pilot run to validate the "antisocial" hypothesis and dealer resistance before committing to mass production. The current plan demands high capital expenditure for a product that experts believe has no market. The lack of a recording function is not a feature; it is a deficit unless proven otherwise. The assumption that "lightweight" equals "desirable" is unverified.

A go decision requires a blocker-free path. The lack of demand data combined with active channel resistance constitutes a factual blocker. The plan cannot proceed as proposed without first validating the core premise: that young people want a playback-only device enough to overcome dealer skepticism and competitor copying.

DECISION: NO-GO
