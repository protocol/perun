First call: NO-GO. The proposal demands full smartphone data pricing for a device explicitly stripped of smartphone utility (no apps, no games), creating an immediate value mismatch for price-sensitive youth.

Steelman: Launching a curated, carrier-exclusive social phone now captures the youth market before the main platform arrives, leveraging the acquired team's expertise despite internal friction.

Attack:
1. **Economic Failure:** The core constraint is fatal. Requiring a full data plan for a "walled garden" with no third-party apps forces users to pay smartphone rates for a feature-phone experience. Competitors offering actual smartphones at similar price points will obliterate this value proposition.
2. **Strategic Cannibalization:** Launching this limited device months before the company's main new platform creates internal competition. It risks confusing carriers and consumers, potentially depressing demand for the flagship launch later in the year.
3. **Operational Risk:** The history of conflict between the core team and the acquired unit, combined with previous delays due to software switches, suggests high execution risk. Adding a complex carrier-exclusive launch model increases the likelihood of further delays or a botched rollout.
4. **Market Fit:** Youth demographics are notoriously volatile and value-driven. A high-cost, low-utility device ignores the primary drivers of adoption in this segment: versatility and affordability.

Alternatives:
- **Do Nothing:** Preserve resources for the main platform launch later this year, avoiding brand dilution and channel conflict.
- **Pilot Only:** Run a limited technical trial without a commercial launch to validate the software switch, decoupling it from a risky go-to-market strategy.
- **Reprice/Rebundle:** Negotiate a subsidized data tier specifically for this device, though this undermines the "full plan" requirement and carrier economics.

Blockers:
- **Value Mismatch:** Price of data plan equals smartphone cost, but utility is strictly limited to social networking with no extensibility.
- **Strategic Conflict:** Launch timing directly precedes and competes with the company's primary platform release.

Risks:
- Internal team conflict causing further delays.
- Carrier rejection due to poor consumer value.
- Brand damage among youth demographic.

The proposal fails the basic economic test for its target audience and jeopardizes the broader portfolio strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
