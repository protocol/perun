First call: GO. The proposal trades short-term control for the only viable long-term defense against rivals who already possess app ecosystems; without native apps, the device becomes a feature phone in a smartphone market.

Steelman: A curated, single-store model mitigates the stated security and stability risks while capturing revenue and ensuring quality, directly addressing the CEO's previous hesitation by proving native apps enhance rather than degrade product polish.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Guarantees obsolescence as rivals leverage existing ecosystems; zero revenue share; high churn.
2. Open sideloading: Maximizes developer joy but invites immediate carrier rebellion and security incidents that could brick the brand's reputation for polish.
3. Proposed store: Balances carrier safety (via review), security (via sandboxing/approval), and monetization (30% cut). It beats "do nothing" by creating a moat and beats "open distribution" by preserving the premium user experience.

Fit: Moves the north star from "web-only sufficiency" to "best mobile platform." The 30% cut is standard for assuming distribution and payment risk.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the review process becomes a bottleneck, stifling innovation, or if carriers block the device entirely. However, the "one-way door" of not entering the app market is far more dangerous than the "two-way door" of adjusting review speed later. The cost of delay is total market irrelevance within 12 months.

Evidence: Rivals already have ecosystems; staying web-only contradicts market trajectory. The 30% model aligns incentives without upfront developer costs.

Blockers: NONE. The concerns (security, carrier objection, workload) are operational hurdles managed by the proposed review process, not fundamental flaws. The risk of "hurting polish" is mitigated by the curation itself.

Risks: Review backlog slowing developer adoption; carrier pushback on specific app categories. These are managed via kill criteria: if app count < 500 by launch + 6 months, relax specific non-core restrictions; if a carrier blocks the device, negotiate exclusivity on their services within the store.

The proposal converts a defensive weakness (no apps) into an offensive strength (curated quality + revenue).

DECISION: GO
