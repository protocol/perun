First call: NO-GO. The plan relies on a factory site currently blocked by protests and a value proposition ("cheapest") that actively repels the target demographic while ignoring safer, cheaper alternatives.

**Steelman:** Launch a safe, dignified $2,500 car to upgrade families from scooters, leveraging a new high-volume factory to achieve unbeatable scale, provided land access is secured and safety perception is managed.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Land):** The proposal admits the factory site faces active farmer protests. Without land, there is no factory; without a factory, there is no car. This is a binary failure condition, not a risk.
2.  **Blocker (Positioning):** Marketing explicitly as the "cheapest car in the world" creates a stigma of inferiority and danger. Families upgrading from scooters prioritize safety and status; this label signals the opposite.
3.  **Better Way:** Used cars and scooters are proven, cheaper alternatives. A "cheap new car" with safety doubts loses to a reliable used car. The "do nothing" option (wait for land resolution, rebrand as "smart family car" rather than "cheapest") offers higher survival odds.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The project fails because production never starts due to legal injunctions on land, or sales flatline because buyers fear the vehicle is a death trap due to the "cheapest" branding.

**Evidence Check:** The proposal itself cites the land protests and safety doubts as existing facts. These are not hypothetical risks; they are stated barriers that invalidate the current execution plan.

**Blockers:**
*   Factory site unavailable due to active protests (stated fact).
*   Brand positioning ("cheapest") directly contradicts the safety requirements of family buyers (stated risk).

**Risks:**
*   Quality perception issues.
*   Competition from used market.

**Decision:** The plan cannot proceed as proposed because the primary production asset (land) is contested and the core marketing strategy undermines the product's value proposition.

DECISION: NO-GO
