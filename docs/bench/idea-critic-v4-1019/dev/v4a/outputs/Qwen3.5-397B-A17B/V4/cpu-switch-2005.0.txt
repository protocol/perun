First call: GO. The current roadmap fails the core requirement for thin, efficient laptops, and the supplier has demonstrated an inability to correct this over a decade. Staying the course guarantees product irrelevance in the emerging mobile form factor.

Steelman: Migrating to the dominant architecture is the only viable path to compete on performance-per-watt for thin devices, provided a translation layer mitigates immediate software incompatibility.

Alternatives:
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue shipping thermally constrained, lagging devices. Result: Market share erosion as competitors release superior thin laptops.
2.  **Dual sourcing:** Maintain old chips for legacy, new for thin. Result: Increased BOM costs, fragmented user experience, and continued reliance on a failing supplier roadmap.
3.  **Wait for supplier:** Hope for a miracle chip. Result: High risk of missing the market window entirely given the ten-year failure streak.

Fit: Aligns with the necessity to produce competitive thin laptops, which is impossible with the current silicon.

Pre-mortem: The transition fails if the translation layer performance is unacceptable to pro users or if the sales freeze from early announcement outweighs the long-term gain. However, these are execution risks, not strategic blockers. The cost of delay is losing the "thin laptop" category definition to competitors.

Evidence: The proposal states the supplier has failed for ten years and lags the dominant maker. This is a factual blocker for the status quo.

Blockers: NONE. The stated risks (sales freeze, developer friction, slower translated apps, brand dilution, past pain) are known execution challenges, not facts that prove the plan fails. They require mitigation, not abandonment. The alternative (staying) has a guaranteed negative outcome based on the supplier's track record.

Risks: Execution complexity of the translation layer; temporary brand confusion; short-term sales dip. These become kill criteria: if translation performance drops below 80% of native or if sales do not recover within two quarters of launch, the strategy must be re-evaluated.

The proposal addresses the only existential threat: hardware incapacity. The risks are manageable compared to the certainty of failure if the company remains on the current path.

DECISION: GO
