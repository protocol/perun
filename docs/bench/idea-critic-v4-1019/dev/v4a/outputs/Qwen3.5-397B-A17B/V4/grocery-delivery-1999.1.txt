First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits irreversible capital before validating the core business model in a single market.

**Steelman:** Secure dominant market share immediately by building insurmountable infrastructure moats before competitors react, accepting early losses as the cost of speed.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unit Economics Unknown:** The proposal explicitly admits the unit economics of the first city are unlearned. Groceries have thin margins; without proven density and pick-pack efficiency, scaling losses linearly with expansion guarantees bankruptcy.
2.  **Irreversible Commitment:** $1 billion in specialized, highly automated warehouses is a one-way door. If the first city reveals that customers prefer different slots, product mixes, or that automation fails at grocery scale, the capital is stranded across a dozen cities.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" alternative regarding new cities is superior. The cheaper, safer path is to maximize density and prove profitability in the initial metro area. Only then should capital be deployed to replicate a proven model. Expanding to a dozen cities while the first is unproven multiplies risk without increasing the probability of success.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs when cash runs out because the cost to acquire customers and fulfill orders in new, low-density cities exceeds the lifetime value, while fixed costs of empty warehouses burn capital daily. The 30-minute slot promise likely drives fulfillment costs above the gross margin.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Unit economics are unproven in the only operating market.
*   **Fact:** The plan requires $1 billion commitment before learning if the model works.
*   **Contradiction:** Rapid expansion contradicts the need to learn unit economics; you cannot learn if you are too busy putting out fires in 12 new markets.

**Risks:** Capital intensity, low margins, unproven density, no track record. These are not just risks; they are active blockers when combined with irreversible spending.

**Decision Logic:** A sound plan must prove the engine works before adding fuel. This plan builds the entire refinery before confirming the engine runs. The lack of verified unit economics is a fatal blocker for a $1 billion, multi-city expansion.

DECISION: NO-GO
