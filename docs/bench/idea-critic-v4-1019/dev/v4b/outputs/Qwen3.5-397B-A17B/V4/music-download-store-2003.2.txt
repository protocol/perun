First call: GO. The company leverages its dominant hardware footprint to solve the industry's existential threat (piracy) with a legal, frictionless alternative, accepting thin margins as a strategic subsidy to lock in ecosystem value.

**Steelman:** A 99-cent per-song store, restricted to proprietary hardware but licensed by all major labels, converts pirates to paying customers by offering superior convenience and reliability compared to free file-sharing, securing the hardware business against obsolescence.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Hardware value erodes as piracy makes music free; rivals win with subscription models.
2.  **Subscription model:** High friction for 2003 users (dial-up/slow broadband), conflicts with ownership psychology, unproven tech.
3.  **Proposed Store:** Low friction, matches user desire for ownership, leverages existing hardware base. It beats "do nothing" by creating a revenue stream and saving the hardware; it beats subscriptions by matching current user behavior.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of selling portable players; music is the bait, the player is the hook.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if labels withdraw licenses due to low margins or if rivals successfully convince users that renting (subscriptions) is superior to owning. However, the "one-way door" risk is low; the store can pivot pricing or licensing later. The cost of delay is high: every month without a legal option accelerates piracy habits.

**Evidence:** The proposal states catalogs are licensable (assumed true for this exercise). The 99-cent price point is a psychological anchor; even with thin margins, volume drives total profit and hardware sales.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks (margin, label friction, platform restriction) are inherent to the strategy but do not falsify the core claim that this saves the hardware business. The platform restriction is a feature, not a bug, for ecosystem lock-in.

**Risks:** Label revolt, subscription shift, margin compression. These become kill criteria: if label licenses are withdrawn within 12 months, or if hardware sales stagnate despite store launch, pivot to subscriptions or open licensing.

**Kill Criteria:** If hardware unit growth does not exceed 15% YoW within two quarters of launch, or if major labels withdraw catalog access, terminate the standalone store model and negotiate bundle-only licensing.

**Leading Metric:** Weekly song downloads per active device. Review date: Quarter-end post-launch.

DECISION: GO
