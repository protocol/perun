First call: NO-GO. The proposal discards the brand's primary shelf cue for an untested aesthetic, risking immediate sales collapse among a loyal, older demographic.

**Steelman:** A modernized design will attract new buyers and signal quality without alienating existing customers if supported by heavy advertising.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits the old image is the "main shelf cue" and the base is "older." Removing the primary recognition trigger for this specific demographic contradicts the stated facts. No evidence suggests the new "minimalist" design functions as a cue.
2.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves current revenue. A "staged pilot" in limited markets would test recognition failure before national exposure. The proposed "all stores at once" approach maximizes downside risk with zero learning loop.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Shoppers scan the aisle, miss the familiar orange-with-straw, assume the brand is out of stock, and buy a competitor. The ad campaign cannot compensate for failure at the critical 3-second shelf decision point.
4.  **Blockers:** The plan explicitly removes the verified mechanism of purchase (the shelf cue) for the core user base while offering no data that the new design replaces this function. This is a factual contradiction, not a mere risk.
5.  **Kill Criteria:** A national launch lacks a stop condition. If sales drop 10% in week one, the cost of reversal (recalling millions of cartons) is prohibitive compared to testing first.

**Conclusion:** The proposal fails the blocker test. It relies on an assumption (new design works) that directly conflicts with a stated fact (old design is the cue). The absence of a pilot or phased rollout turns a manageable risk into a catastrophic potential failure. The cheaper, safer alternative (pilot testing) reaches the same upside (validation) without the existential downside.

Blockers: Removal of verified primary shelf cue for core demographic without evidence of replacement; "all stores at once" strategy prevents learning and makes reversal prohibitively expensive.
Risks: Brand alienation, sales collapse, wasted ad spend.

DECISION: NO-GO
