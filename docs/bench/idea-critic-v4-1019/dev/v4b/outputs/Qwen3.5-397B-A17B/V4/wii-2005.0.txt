First call: GO. The market leader and runner-up are fighting a costly war for hardcore gamers and graphical fidelity, leaving the casual and non-gamer segment entirely unserved. A low-cost, novel input method directly targets this blue ocean.

**Steelman:** By abandoning the specs race, the console captures a massive, untapped demographic (families, seniors, casuals) with a lower price point and intuitive motion controls, creating a new market category rather than competing in an existing one.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue fighting for third place in the shrinking hardcore market; likely financial failure.
2.  **High-Definition Race:** Attempt to match rivals; requires massive R&D, higher price, and still leaves us as a distant third with no exclusive pull.
3.  **Proposed Strategy:** Low cost, motion control, casual focus. High risk of publisher skepticism, but offers the only path to market leadership via volume and new users.

**Fit:** This moves the north star from "best graphics" to "biggest audience." It fits a stage where survival requires differentiation, not imitation.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if third-party publishers refuse to support the platform due to non-HD specs, leaving a content drought. However, the low hardware cost reduces the barrier for first-party hits to carry the system. The door is two-way; if the casual market rejects motion controls, the hardware loss is minimized by low component costs compared to a failed HD console.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on the assumption that "non-gamers" are a large enough cohort to sustain the business. Given the saturation of the hardcore market, this is a sound strategic pivot. The skepticism from hardcore gamers is irrelevant; they are not the target. The thin margin is acceptable if volume compensates, which the lower price point enables.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks (publisher support, toy perception) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The lack of HD is a feature, not a bug, for the cost target.

**Risks:** Third-party abandonment; perception as a fad. These are mitigated by strong first-party software launches and the sheer price advantage.

**Kill Criteria:** If unit sales do not exceed the combined installed base of rivals within 18 months, or if software attach rate falls below 4 titles per console by Q4 2006, pivot to cost-cutting or licensing the controller tech.

DECISION: GO
