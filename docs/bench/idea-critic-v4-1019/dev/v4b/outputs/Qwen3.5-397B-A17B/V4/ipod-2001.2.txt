First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a tiny installed base, an unproven legal music supply, and a premium price in a market where cheaper devices have already failed to gain traction.

Steelman: A tightly integrated, high-capacity device could create a superior user experience that justifies a premium, eventually forcing the music industry to license content and expanding the ecosystem beyond the current small computer base.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoid capital risk but cede the emerging digital music category to incumbents.
2. License software to Windows: Immediately expands the addressable market but dilutes the hardware-software integration strategy and requires partnerships not yet in place.
3. Wait for legal downloads: Reduces product risk but misses the first-mover window and allows competitors to establish habits.
The proposal must beat "do nothing" on current facts; it currently relies on future industry shifts that are not guaranteed.

Fit: Assumes the goal is to dominate digital music, not just sell hardware to existing users. However, the current strategy restricts the product to the very users least likely to adopt a niche, expensive peripheral without a broader ecosystem.

Pre-mortem: The device launches to poor sales because the limited computer user base is insufficient to sustain volume manufacturing, component costs remain high, and the lack of legal music sources makes the device a piracy tool, attracting industry lawsuits. This is a one-way door with high sunk costs. Delaying six months to secure music licenses would likely kill the hardware momentum but reduce legal risk.

Evidence: All claims regarding market share, pricing, and industry status are assumed based on the prompt. The math of 5GB at $400 implies a high cost-per-GB that the market has not yet accepted for portable devices.

Blockers: The addressable market (owners of this specific small-share computer) is likely too small to achieve the economies of scale required to profitably source custom hard drives and components within a one-year timeline at the $400 price point. The business case fails without an immediate expansion to other operating systems or a guaranteed music catalog, neither of which are present.

Risks: Component sourcing delays, industry litigation, consumer rejection of high price.

Kill criteria: If pre-orders from the existing computer base do not cover 40% of the tooling costs within 90 days of announcement, cancel the project.

DECISION: NO-GO
