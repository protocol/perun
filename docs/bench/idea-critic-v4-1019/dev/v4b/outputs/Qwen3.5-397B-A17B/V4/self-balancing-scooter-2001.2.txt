First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to massive fixed costs (40k unit factory) before validating demand, legal viability, or product-market fit beyond niche institutional use.

**Steelman:** Launching immediately at scale captures first-mover advantage in a revolutionary category before competitors emerge, leveraging high-profile backing to define the market.

**Attack:**
1.  **Legal Blocker:** The proposal admits legal status on sidewalks and roads is "unclear." Selling a vehicle that may be illegal to operate in primary use cases (commuting) is a fatal flaw. Regulatory bans would render inventory worthless.
2.  **Demand Mismatch:** At $5,000, the price excludes mass adoption. With "unproven" uses beyond institutions and high social conspicuousness, consumer demand is speculative. Building for 40,000 units assumes mass market success without evidence.
3.  **Utility Constraints:** Limited cargo capacity and range restrictions further narrow the addressable market to early adopters, not the volume required to justify the proposed factory size.
4.  **Reversibility:** This is a one-way door. Sizing a factory for 40,000 units creates immense fixed costs and inventory risk. If the legal or market assumptions fail, the capital is sunk.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for regulatory clarity and lower component costs.
*   **Pilot:** Produce a limited run (e.g., 500 units) for institutional trials to prove utility and lobby for regulations before committing to mass manufacturing.
*   **Price Down:** Redesign for a lower price point to reduce consumer friction, though this delays launch.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, regulators ban it from sidewalks in major cities, consumers refuse to pay $5,000 for a novelty they feel silly riding, and the company collapses under the weight of an underutilized factory and unsold inventory.

**Blockers:**
*   Unclear legal status prevents guaranteed utility for the core user base.
*   Factory scale (40k) vastly exceeds validated demand for an unproven, high-cost niche product.

**Risks:** Social stigma, high price elasticity, limited utility. These are secondary to the legal and capacity blockers.

**Conclusion:** The plan fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests. You cannot build a mass-production factory for a product that might be illegal to use and has no proven mass market. The correct path is a capped pilot to resolve legal and demand uncertainties.

DECISION: NO-GO
