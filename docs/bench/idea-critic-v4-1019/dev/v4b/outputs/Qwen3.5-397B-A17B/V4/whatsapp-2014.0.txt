First call: NO-GO. The price is 12x the prior valuation with no revenue, no monetization plan, and explicit refusal to use the buyer's core monetization engine (ads), making financial justification impossible on stated facts.

Steelman: Acquiring the target is the only viable defense against mobile displacement, securing hundreds of millions of users who are abandoning the buyer's platform, where the strategic value of network ownership outweighs the lack of immediate financial returns.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Risk continued erosion of the core user base as messaging shifts to mobile-native apps.
2. Build competitor: Too slow; network effects favor the incumbent target.
3. Acquire: Secures the user base immediately but at an unprecedented, unproven financial multiple.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of preventing displacement, but assumes the target's anti-ad stance can coexist with the buyer's economic model without destroying value.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition fails because the target's founders leave due to cultural clash over privacy/ads, the user base migrates to a new ephemeral app anyway, or regulators block data integration, leaving the buyer with a $19B loss and no synergy. This is a one-way door; delay costs market share daily.

Evidence: The proposal states the target has minimal revenue, refuses ads, and was valued at $1.5B one year ago. The $19B price tag relies entirely on the assumption that future monetization (undefined) will justify a 12,600% increase in value in 12 months.

Blockers:
1. Financial Incoherence: The proposal explicitly states "no monetisation plan" and "refused to show advertising," yet commits $19B. There is no path to ROI described or even hypothesized in the facts provided.
2. Valuation Contradiction: The jump from $1.5B to $19B in one year without revenue growth contradicts basic investment logic unless a specific, verified synergy is defined, which is absent.

Risks: Founder retention, regulatory scrutiny, cultural incompatibility, and the possibility that the "displacement" fear is overblown.

The absence of a monetization strategy for an asset costing $19B is not a risk to be managed; it is a fundamental failure of the proposal's core premise. You cannot approve a spend of this magnitude on a "hope" that contradicts the target's stated operating principles.

DECISION: NO-GO
