First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a massive, irreversible infrastructure build (new dealer network) and a fixed 1957 launch date despite an acknowledged recession risk and a segment that "could shrink." An operator facing a potential downturn does not lock in high fixed costs before verifying demand resilience.

Steelman: Launching a distinct mid-priced brand with dedicated dealers captures the growing market gap before competitors solidify, leveraging extensive research to ensure product-market fit.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost, high risk, low reversibility (dealer contracts, tooling). Fails if recession hits in 1957.
2. **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; risk losing share if the segment grows and the leader dominates.
3. **Staged Entry (Better Way):** Launch the car line immediately using the *existing* low-priced dealer network to test demand and brand perception. Delay the capital-intensive separate dealer network until sales volume proves the recession won't crush the segment. This captures upside with significantly lower fixed costs and higher reversibility.

Fit: Assumes the goal is market share growth; however, survival during a downturn is a prerequisite. The proposed plan jeopardizes the firm's financial stability if the "perceived gap" closes due to macroeconomics.

Pre-mortem: The 1957 launch coincides with a sharp economic contraction. Consumers trade down to low-priced cars or delay purchases. The new division, burdened by unused dealer infrastructure and high marketing spend, bleeds cash. The parent company cannot subsidize it without hurting its core lines. The brand becomes a costly failure.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits "recession risk exists" and the segment "could shrink." It offers no contingency for this specific downside, only a fixed commitment to a 1957 debut.

Blockers:
1. **Fixed Cost vs. Variable Risk:** The plan mandates a new dealer network (high fixed cost) before confirming the segment can withstand a recession. This mismatch threatens solvency if the acknowledged risk materializes.
2. **Timing Rigidity:** A hard 1957 launch ignores the cyclical nature of auto sales; a delay or soft launch would mitigate the recession impact.

Risks: Brand overlap, marketing inefficiency, execution complexity.

The proposal fails the operator test because it treats a contingent opportunity as a certainty, ignoring its own cited risks in the structural design. It must be revised to decouple product launch from infrastructure commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
