First call: NO-GO. The proposal discards the brand's primary shelf cue for an unproven aesthetic, risking immediate sales collapse among a loyal, older demographic.

Steelman: A modernized design with functional cues (cap shape) and heavy advertising can refresh the brand without losing recognition.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Zero cost, zero recognition risk, but stagnates brand image.
2. **Staged rollout:** Test new design in limited markets to measure recognition failure before national spend.
3. **Proposed (Big Bang):** Maximum cost, maximum risk of alienating the core base instantly.

Fit: While modernization aligns with a "fresh" vision, it contradicts the reality of an older, habit-driven buyer base. The proposal assumes advertising can override deep-seated visual habits instantly, which is unproven.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-launch, sales have plummeted. Cause: Older shoppers scanned shelves for the familiar orange-with-straw icon, missed the minimalist glass, and switched to competitors. The advertising budget was insufficient to retrain millions of habitual buyers simultaneously. The "cap shaped like an orange" is too subtle a cue to replace the dominant graphic.

Evidence: The proposal admits the old image is the "main shelf cue" and the base is "older." These are facts, not risks. Removing the main cue for this demographic is a direct attack on the purchase mechanism. No evidence is offered that the new design works better or equally well for this specific group.

Blockers:
1. **Contradictory Facts:** The plan relies on an older demographic that depends on the "main shelf cue" (the orange with straw), yet the proposal explicitly removes this cue nationwide. This guarantees recognition failure.
2. **No Validation:** A "large advertising campaign" is not evidence that recognition will transfer; it is a costly hope. There is no pilot data showing the new minimalist design works for older shoppers.

Risks: High cost of reprinting nationwide; irreversible loss of market share if shoppers defect during confusion.

The proposal fails because it removes the single most important driver of sales for its core demographic without testing if the replacement works. It bets the entire brand on an unverified assumption.

DECISION: NO-GO
