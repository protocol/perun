First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a search-advertising company with no hardware experience to spend at least $50 million on a six-person team building an OS in a market controlled by carriers and entrenched vendors, with no revenue model and a multi-year payback.

Steelman: Acquiring this team allows the company to own the underlying platform of mobile internet, ensuring search dominance as users shift from desktop to mobile, effectively buying future ad inventory before the market exists.

However, the blockers are fatal based on the stated facts. First, the distribution channel is sealed: carriers control distribution, and handset makers have zero incentive to adopt a new, unproven OS from a non-hardware player. Without a path to installation, the "free" software generates no value. Second, the financial mismatch is extreme. A $50 million price tag for six people implies a valuation disconnected from current reality, demanding a perfect execution of a multi-year strategy where the company has no core competency (hardware/OS management). Third, the "open-source" lever does not solve the adoption problem; incumbents can fork or ignore it, and carriers will block it if it threatens their control.

The risks listed (no revenue model, carrier resistance) are not merely uncertainties; they are structural barriers confirmed by the current market state described. A cheaper alternative is to wait for the market to mature or partner with an existing handset maker, rather than building an entire stack from scratch. The cost of delay is low because the mobile ad market does not yet exist at scale; rushing in now burns capital on infrastructure that cannot be deployed.

This is a one-way door with a high probability of total loss. The plan fails because it assumes adoption will follow availability, ignoring the gatekeepers (carriers) who decide availability. There is no evidence provided that these gatekeepers will cooperate.

Blockers: 1) No distribution path due to carrier/handset maker control. 2) $50M cost for 6 people with no revenue model violates capital discipline. 3) Lack of hardware competency makes execution impossible.
Risks: Multi-year payback, open-source fragmentation.

DECISION: NO-GO
