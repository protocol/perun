First call: GO. Moving families from exposed two-wheelers to an enclosed vehicle at a disruptive price point addresses a massive, unmet need in an emerging market.

**Steelman:** A no-frills, safe-enough vehicle priced near motorcycle levels will unlock car ownership for millions of upgraders, relying on volume to offset razor-thin margins.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Families remain on dangerous scooters; market ceded to used cars or competitors.
2.  **Used cars:** Cheaper but unreliable, unsafe, and lack warranty support.
3.  **Premium compact:** Safer but priced out of reach for the target demographic.

**Fit:** Aligns with the vision of mass motorization. The "cheapest" label is a double-edged sword: it drives volume but risks brand stigma.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if land protests halt construction indefinitely, or if a single high-profile safety incident confirms fears that "cheap" equals "deadly." Cost of delay allows competitors to capture the first-mover advantage in the entry segment.

**Evidence & Constraints:** The proposal relies on unverified assumptions: that farmer protests can be resolved without major cost/schedule slippage, and that safety perceptions can be managed despite extreme cost-cutting. The $2,500 price point leaves almost no room for error in manufacturing or liability.

**Blockers:** NONE explicitly stated in the text that definitively kills the project *today*, provided the land issue is treated as an execution risk rather than a showstopper. The safety concern is a risk to be managed via rigorous testing and marketing, not a factual blocker yet.

**Risks:** Land acquisition delays; brand perception of "unsafe"; inability to maintain quality at target price; competition from subsidized used vehicles.

**Kill Criteria:** If the factory site cannot be secured within 12 months, or if initial crash tests fail to meet minimum regulatory standards, the project must pivot or stop. Leading metric: Pre-order volume vs. production capacity in the first quarter.

The potential social impact and market size outweigh the execution risks, provided the land dispute is resolved and safety is not compromised.

DECISION: GO
