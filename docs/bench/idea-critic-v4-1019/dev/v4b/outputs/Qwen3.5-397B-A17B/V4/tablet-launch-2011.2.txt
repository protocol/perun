First call: NO-GO. Launching a premium-priced tablet with a tiny app catalog against a dominant incumbent, while the OS has negligible share and leadership is in flux, ignores the primary driver of tablet adoption: ecosystem depth.

**Steelman:** The brand's hardware prestige and heavy marketing can temporarily overcome the app deficit, buying time for developers to flock to the new platform before consumers notice the gap.

**Attack:**
1.  **Ecosystem Moat:** Tablets are utility devices, not status symbols alone. A "small fraction" of apps means missing critical use cases (media, productivity, niche tools). Consumers comparing devices side-by-side will reject the inferior utility regardless of price parity.
2.  **Chicken-and-Egg:** Developer outreach fails without an installed base; an installed base fails without apps. Heavy ad spend burns capital accelerating awareness of a product users cannot fully utilize, accelerating negative word-of-mouth.
3.  **Leadership Vacuum:** Transitioning leadership creates strategic drift. Executing a complex platform play requires absolute clarity and long-term commitment, which is impossible during a power vacuum.
4.  **Price Sensitivity:** Pricing "in line with the market leader" removes the only viable alternative strategy: undercutting on price to force trial. Without a price advantage or an app advantage, the value proposition is zero.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Preserve capital, wait for OS maturity or leadership stabilization.
*   *Low-cost entry:* Launch at a loss-leader price to force unit volume, accepting hardware margins hit to build user base for developers.
*   *Niche focus:* Target a specific vertical where the small app catalog suffices, rather than a general consumer launch.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to fanfare, early reviewers praise the hardware but pan the app selection, sales stall after the initial brand-loyal burst, developers abandon the platform seeing low activation, and the company writes off the inventory and marketing spend within 18 months.

**Blockers:**
*   **Value Gap:** Price parity + App deficit = Negative consumer value.
*   **Strategic Instability:** Leadership transition prevents the sustained focus required for platform turnaround.
*   **Market Reality:** The "small catalog" is a fatal flaw for a general-purpose tablet, not a temporary hurdle solvable by ads.

**Risks:** Brand damage from a failed flagship; wasted R&D; developer alienation.

**Kill Criteria:** If the app count does not reach 40% of the leader's core top-100 apps within 6 months, halt general launch and pivot to niche or price-cut strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
