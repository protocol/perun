First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer with no developer platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors are already hostile to its capital spending model.

**Steelman:** Leveraging existing spiky infrastructure to create a high-margin revenue stream that diversifies beyond retail, turning a cost center into a profit center if the market materializes.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Focus on core retail; avoid distraction and capital scrutiny.
2.  **Internal optimization:** Use excess capacity solely to stabilize internal retail spikes, reducing costs without external risk.
3.  **Proposed plan:** Build a public cloud platform. Requires massive new investment, cultural shift, and creates direct competition with established hardware vendors.

**Fit:** This contradicts the company's stated identity as a retailer. It moves into a completely different category (infrastructure services) where the company has zero proven competency. The "north star" of retail efficiency is diluted by the need to serve external, unpredictable developer demands.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because reliability expectations for external developers exceed the retailer's internal tolerance, leading to reputational damage. Capital spending triggers investor revolt before revenue proves the model. The team gets distracted, causing retail operations to suffer during peak seasons.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the market is "unproven" and the company has "no experience." Investors are already questioning heavy capital spending. There is no data showing external demand exists or that the company can compete with incumbent vendors.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capital Constraint:** Investors are already hostile to heavy capital spending; this plan requires significantly more without guaranteed returns.
*   **Competency Gap:** No experience running a developer platform at scale; high risk of failure against established vendors.
*   **Market Validation:** The core premise (renting servers by the hour) is explicitly stated as "unproven."

**Risks:** Distraction from core retail business, margin dilution, reliability failures, and competitive defeat.

**Decision:** The combination of unproven market demand, lack of core competency, active investor hostility to capital expenditure, and high distraction risk creates an insurmountable barrier. The plan fails the basic test of leveraging existing strengths without jeopardizing the core business.

DECISION: NO-GO
