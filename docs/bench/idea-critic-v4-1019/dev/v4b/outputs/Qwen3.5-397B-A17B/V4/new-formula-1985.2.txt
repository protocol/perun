First call: NO-GO. The plan bets the entire brand equity on taste preference alone, ignoring the verified risk that removing the original formula will trigger an emotional backlash that taste tests cannot measure.

**Steelman:** Replacing the flagship with a demonstrably superior-tasting formula maximizes immediate consumption satisfaction and halts share loss to sweeter rivals.

**Attack:** The testing methodology is fatally flawed. By withholding the fact that the original would be withdrawn, the tests measured "addition" (a new sweet option) rather than "substitution" (loss of a heritage icon). Consumers often prefer a sweeter drink in a blind sip test but reject it as a total replacement for a product tied to identity and memory. The proposal explicitly acknowledges "emotional attachment" and "brand heritage" as risks yet offers no data quantifying them, no fallback product to retain loyalists, and no strategy for bottlers facing consumer revolt. A one-way door decision (permanent withdrawal) is being made based on two-way door data (preference ranking).

**Alternatives:**
1. **Do nothing:** Retain share loss but preserve brand trust.
2. **Line extension:** Launch the new formula alongside the original (co-existence). This captures the "sweeter" preference segment without alienating heritage loyalists or triggering the specific risk of forced removal.
3. **Proposed plan:** High risk of catastrophic brand damage due to the "endowment effect" where loss of the original is valued higher than the gain of the new taste.

**Blockers:** The absence of a fallback product combined with the unquantified risk of emotional backlash creates a single point of failure. If the backlash occurs (as the risk log admits is possible), there is no mechanism to retain the core customer base. The testing did not validate the core claim that users accept the *removal* of the original, only that they like the *taste* of the new one.

**Decision:** The plan fails because it treats a psychological/brand decision as a purely sensory one. The lack of a "co-existence" pilot or a fallback option makes the risk unmanageable.

Blockers: Testing methodology ignored the variable of permanent removal; no fallback product for heritage loyalists.
Risks: Consumer boycott, bottler rebellion, irreversible brand erosion.

DECISION: NO-GO
