First call: GO. The proposal leverages existing assets to solve a clear market gap (garage-friendly family transport) with a plausible path to profitability given the government backing.

**Steelman:** A front-wheel-drive minivan on a compact platform offers car-like handling and garage fitment, creating a new category that blends station wagon utility with van capacity, utilizing a converted factory to minimize capital expenditure.

**Alternatives:**
1. *Do nothing:* Risk stagnation as families stick to cumbersome full-size vans or inadequate station wagons; competitors may eventually fill the gap.
2. *Full-size van update:* High cost, fails the "garage fit" requirement, retains poor handling.
3. *New platform development:* Prohibitive cost given cash constraints; too slow.
The proposed path beats "do nothing" by capturing an unserved demographic with lower risk via platform reuse.

**Fit:** Aligns with the survival stage; the north star is cash-flow positivity through high-volume, differentiated consumer sales. Reusing the compact platform is a strategic necessity, not just a cost saver.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if marketing fails to distinguish the vehicle from commercial vans, leading to low consumer adoption. However, the "station wagon" buyer profile suggests a latent demand for more space without losing car dynamics. The door is two-way; if the category fails, the platform can revert to standard car production. Delay costs first-mover advantage in a nascent segment.

**Evidence:** The government guarantee mitigates the cash risk. The "no existing category" is an opportunity, not a blocker, evidenced by families compromising with ill-fitting vehicles. Platform space limits are a known trade-off but likely sufficient for the target "garage" constraint.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (category creation, perception, copying) are inherent to innovation, not factual contradictions. The cash constraint is addressed by the loan and platform reuse.
**Risks:** Consumer confusion (mitigated by marketing); competitor response (mitigated by speed to market).

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders or initial sales reviews indicate >60% of buyers perceive it strictly as a commercial vehicle within 6 months, pivot marketing or halt expansion. Leading metric: Unit sales vs. station wagon cannibalization rate.

DECISION: GO
