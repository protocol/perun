First call: GO. The proposal targets an unserved segment (commuters/students) with a product fit (small, easy-to-ride) that bypasses the entrenched "rough image" of the incumbent market via novel distribution channels (sporting-goods stores).

**Steelman:** By ignoring the existing large-bike market and creating a new category for light transportation sold outside traditional dealerships, the company can achieve volume without direct competition or brand baggage.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Safe on currency, but cedes the potential mass market to future entrants.
2.  **Export Large Models:** High risk of mechanical failure (highway speeds) and brand rejection; requires scarce currency for low-probability success.
3.  **Wait for Brand Fame:** Circular logic; fame comes from presence, not absence.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** The strategy fits a "blue ocean" approach: making the competition irrelevant by changing the rules (vehicle class and sales channel).
*Pre-mortem:* Failure occurs if the small bikes mechanically fail on US roads or if cultural stigma prevents adoption. However, the proposal specifically avoids highway use cases (commuters/students) and bypasses the "biker" stigma by selling in sporting-goods stores. The currency risk is real but is the cost of entry for any global expansion; limiting it to a subsidiary focused on high-volume, low-unit-cost goods mitigates exposure compared to heavy infrastructure investment.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal relies on the factual mismatch between US market offerings (large, rough) and the product (small, easy). There is no evidence that Americans *cannot* use small bikes, only that they currently *favor* large ones for recreation. The "unknown brand" is neutralized by the non-traditional channel strategy.
*Blockers:* None. The risks cited (image, size, currency) are addressed by the specific tactical choices in the proposal (target demographic, channel selection, focused rollout). The mechanical concern regarding larger models is irrelevant as the proposal focuses on the 50cc unit.

**Decision:**
The plan converts weaknesses (small size, unknown brand) into advantages (maneuverability, no baggage) by attacking a non-consumer base. The currency risk is acceptable given the low unit cost and potential volume.

DECISION: GO
