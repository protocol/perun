First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a factory site currently blocked by active farmer protests, making the high-volume production target impossible to meet on the proposed timeline.

Steelman: A dedicated factory can mass-produce a safe, ultra-low-cost car that successfully displaces scooters if land acquisition is resolved and safety perceptions are managed.

Options:
1. **Proposed:** New dedicated factory at contested site. High risk of total delay due to protests; lowest unit cost if successful.
2. **Do Nothing:** Maintain status quo. Zero capital risk, but forfeits the emerging family-market segment to competitors.
3. **Alternative (Contract Manufacturing):** Utilize existing idle capacity in partner plants. Higher unit cost, but bypasses land protests entirely and allows immediate market testing of the "cheapest" positioning.

Fit: Aligns with the vision of motorizing families but fails the operational reality of the chosen execution path. The strategy depends on volume, which the contested site prevents.

Pre-mortem: The project fails because land protests delay construction by two years, burning cash while competitors launch similar models. The "cheapest" label triggers a safety backlash after a single high-profile accident, destroying brand trust. This is a one-way door; reputational damage is hard to reverse. Cost of delay is high, as first-mover advantage in the entry-level segment is critical.

Evidence: The proposal itself states the factory site "faces protests from farmers." This is a current, active blocker, not a future risk. Without land, there is no factory; without a factory, there is no volume; without volume, the $2,500 price point is unreachable.

Blockers:
1. **Land Acquisition:** Active protests at the specific proposed site prevent the construction of the dedicated factory required for the business model.
2. **Price-Volume Link:** The $2,500 price is contingent on high-volume production from the new factory, which cannot be built at the contested location.

Risks: Safety perception, brand stigma of "cheapest," competition from used cars. These are manageable with marketing and engineering but are irrelevant if the car cannot be built.

The plan fails as proposed because the primary execution vehicle (the new factory) is currently non-viable due to the stated land protests. A pivot to contract manufacturing or a new site is required before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
