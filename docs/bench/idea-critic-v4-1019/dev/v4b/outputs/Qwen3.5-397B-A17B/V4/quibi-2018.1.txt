First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets $1B+ on unproven demand for premium short-form subscriptions amid known fatigue and abundant free alternatives.

Steelman: A high-fidelity, mobile-native studio service could capture high-value users if orientation-agnostic tech and A-list talent overcome subscription fatigue. However, the claim rests on assumptions contradicted by current market facts.

Alternatives: Doing nothing avoids massive capital destruction. Licensing content to existing platforms or launching a free ad-supported tier first offers lower risk and validates demand without a $1B upfront burn. The proposed "all-in" subscription model fails to beat these safer, cheaper paths on the stated facts.

Fit: The strategy ignores the "north star" of current consumer behavior: migration toward free, algorithmic short-form video. Betting on a paid reversal of this trend without a pilot is misaligned.

Pre-mortem: The service launches, users try it for the free trial, then cancel because the content library is too small to justify another monthly fee, or they simply watch free competitors. The "orientation" feature is a novelty, not a retention driver. The cost of delay is low; waiting for clearer demand signals saves billions.

Evidence: The proposal admits "no proven demand" and cites "subscription fatigue" as a rising concern. These are not risks to be managed; they are blockers to the core revenue hypothesis. High production budgets and A-list talent increase the burn rate without solving the fundamental lack of willingness to pay.

Blockers:
1. The core claim (users will pay for premium short-form) is explicitly admitted as unproven while contradictory evidence (fatigue, free abundance) is stated as fact.
2. No pilot, cap, or metric is offered to test demand before raising $1B; the plan demands full scale before validation.

Risks: Competition for attention, price sensitivity, execution difficulty.

The absence of a validated demand signal for the specific value proposition, combined with the massive irreversible capital requirement, constitutes a fatal flaw. A capped pilot could be viable, but the proposal as stated is a blind bet.

DECISION: NO-GO
