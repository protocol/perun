First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a factory site currently blocked by farmer protests, making the high-volume production target impossible to start as proposed.

**Steelman:** A dedicated factory enables the scale required to hit the 100,000 rupee price point, successfully transitioning families from scooters to safe four-seat vehicles before competitors react.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** High risk due to land protests halting construction; "cheapest" branding may alienate aspirational buyers; safety doubts could kill demand.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain status quo; families remain on scooters; competitors may launch safer, slightly more expensive alternatives first.
3.  **Alternative (Staged):** Launch via existing contract manufacturing or a smaller pilot line to prove demand and safety ratings before committing to the contested greenfield site. This reduces capital exposure and allows time to resolve land issues or rebrand away from "cheapest."

**Fit:** Aligns with the vision of motorizing families but fails the operational reality of the stated land conflict.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because land protests delay the factory for years, burning cash while competitors launch superior used car financing or slightly pricier new models that don't carry the "unsafe cheap car" stigma.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the factory site "faces protests from farmers." This is a current, stated fact, not a future risk. Without land, there is no factory; without a factory, there is no volume; without volume, the $2,500 price point collapses.

**Blockers:**
*   **Land Access:** The stated farmer protests prevent the construction of the "new dedicated factory" required for the business model.
*   **Value Proposition:** The "cheapest" positioning combined with explicit "safety doubts" creates a fatal perception gap that cheaper scooters and used cars exploit.

**Risks:** Brand stigma, regulatory safety bans, cost overruns if forced to move sites.

**Decision:** The plan cannot execute as proposed because the primary production asset (the factory) is blocked by a known, active conflict. The pricing model depends entirely on a volume that cannot be achieved at this specific site right now.

DECISION: NO-GO
