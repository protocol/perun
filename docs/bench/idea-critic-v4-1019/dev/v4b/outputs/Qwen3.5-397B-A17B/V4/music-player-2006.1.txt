First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market late, abandons a strategic standard (copy-protection) that secures partner relationships, and relies on unproven wireless sharing that labels may block, all without a price advantage to dislodge a loyal ecosystem.

**Steelman:** A superior hardware form factor (larger screen) combined with a seamless ecosystem (store + subscription + wireless sharing) can overcome lateness by offering a user experience the incumbent cannot match due to legacy constraints.

**Attack:**
1.  **Strategic Contradiction:** Dropping the copy-protection standard alienates current partners (labels, device makers) whose content is essential for the store. Without their buy-in, the store fails; with their restrictions, "wireless sharing" is impossible.
2.  **Ecosystem Moat:** The incumbent's "enormous share" creates a network effect. A late entrant requires a massive price discount or exclusive content to switch users. The proposal explicitly states there is "no clear price advantage."
3.  **Dependency Risk:** Wireless sharing depends entirely on label approval. If labels restrict this (a stated risk), the primary differentiator vanishes, leaving only a "larger screen" against an entrenched giant.
4.  **Cost vs. Return:** A "large marketing budget" burned against a dominant leader with no unique value proposition (once sharing is restricted) leads to catastrophic cash burn with low conversion.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Preserve partner relationships and avoid cash burn.
*   *License software:* Put the player on the incumbent's device rather than building new hardware.
*   *Niche focus:* Target a specific segment ignored by the leader rather than a head-on assault.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, labels block song sharing due to piracy fears, the store lacks key catalog due to the dropped protection standard, and the high marketing spend yields negligible market share against the incumbent. The project is canceled after draining reserves.

**Blockers:**
*   The proposal removes the very standard (copy-protection) required to secure content deals, yet relies on that content to succeed.
*   No mechanism exists to force label approval for wireless sharing; if denied, the product value collapses.
*   Late entry with no price advantage and no guaranteed exclusive features cannot break a loyal ecosystem.

**Risks:** Label retaliation, partner alienation, marketing inefficacy.

DECISION: NO-GO
