First call: GO. The market collapse is a distribution and perception failure, not a demand extinction; the proposed re-positioning directly addresses the stated blocker (retailer refusal) by masking the product category.

Steelman: By rebranding the console as an "Entertainment System" bundled with non-game toys (robot, light gun) and assuming retailer inventory risk via buy-back guarantees, the company can bypass the "video game" stigma, secure shelf space, and control software quality to prevent another market crash.

Alternatives:
1. **Do nothing:** Cede the North American market entirely; zero revenue, zero risk, but guaranteed irrelevance in the region.
2. **Direct launch as console:** High probability of immediate retailer rejection based on current sentiment; likely total failure before traction.
3. **Licensing to existing US publisher:** Lower capital risk but surrenders control over quality and branding, risking a repeat of the 1983 saturation with cheap software.

Fit: Assumes the goal is long-term market dominance rather than short-term unit sales. The strategy aligns with overcoming the specific "passed fad" narrative.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the toy bundles fail to distract retailers from the console's core function, or if the lockout chip triggers legal challenges or developer boycotts that starve the system of software. The buy-back promise could bankrupt the division if the product flops, though this is the cost of entry.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the assumption that "toy" categorization bypasses the "video game" blacklist. While unproven in this specific context, the logic holds: retailers reject *games*, not necessarily *electronics* or *toys*. The lockout chip addresses the historical cause of the crash (software oversaturation), turning a risk into a feature.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (retailer refusal, no track record, developer anger) are precisely what the proposal's mechanisms (rebranding, buy-back, lockout) are designed to neutralize. No internal contradiction exists.

Risks: High capital exposure from buy-backs; potential developer friction. These are managed risks, not showstoppers.

Kill Criteria: If retailer acceptance remains below 20% of targeted chains after the "toy" pitch, or if software library growth stalls due to lockout disputes within 6 months, pivot or exit. Leading metric: Number of retail doors opened under the "Entertainment System" classification.

DECISION: GO
