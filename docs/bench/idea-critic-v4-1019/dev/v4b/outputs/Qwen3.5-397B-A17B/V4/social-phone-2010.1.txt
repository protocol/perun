First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores basic market fit: teenagers need apps, not just messaging, and cannot afford premium data plans without app utility to justify the cost.

Steelman: Leverage the acquired brand's social cachet to lock in teens via a curated, carrier-subsidized device, avoiding app store fragmentation by bundling essential social features directly into the OS.

Options analysis:
1. **Do nothing:** Retain the acquired brand's value without risking capital on an unproven OS and expensive hardware subsidy.
2. **License existing OS:** Port the messaging experience to Android/iOS to access existing app ecosystems immediately, reducing development risk and cost.
3. **Proposed plan:** High cost, high risk, limited utility (no apps), and high barrier to entry (expensive data) for the target demographic.

Fit: The strategy contradicts the "social network" premise. Teens live on platforms (apps); a walled garden with no third-party apps in 2010 is a non-starter against rivals with thousands of apps.

Pre-mortem: The phones launch, teens realize they cannot run popular games or emerging social apps, the data plan feels like a tax on a broken toy, and sales flatline. The sunk cost of two years becomes four as the company tries to recruit developers to a dead platform.

Evidence & Math:
- **Cost:** Data plan ≈ full smartphone plan. Teen budget < adult budget. Value prop fails.
- **Market:** 2010 context implies App Store/Android Market dominance. "No app store" is a disqualifier for a "social" phone.
- **Sunk Cost:** Two years sunk is irrelevant to future viability; continuing doubles the loss if the premise is flawed.

Blockers:
1. **No App Ecosystem:** A "social networking phone" in 2010 without an app store cannot compete with rivals offering thousands of apps. This removes the route to the upside (social engagement).
2. **Price Mismatch:** Mandatory high-cost data plan exceeds typical teen budget constraints, especially for a device with limited utility.

Risks: Carrier exclusivity limits reach; new OS lacks developer support.

The plan fails on its own stated facts: it targets social-native users but denies them the primary mechanism of social interaction (apps) while pricing them out of the market.

DECISION: NO-GO
