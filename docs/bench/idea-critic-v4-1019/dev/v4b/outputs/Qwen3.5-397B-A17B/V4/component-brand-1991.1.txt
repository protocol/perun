First call: GO. The claim rests on shifting power from OEMs to end-users in a commoditizing market where differentiation is invisible.

Steelman: By subsidizing OEM ads that feature the chip brand, the company creates consumer pull that forces OEMs to prioritize its processors, turning a hidden component into a required feature.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Risk becoming a low-margin commodity as clones proliferate and OEMs switch suppliers based solely on price.
2. Direct B2B sales push: Ineffective against clone makers who undercut pricing and confuse buyers with similar part numbers.
3. This campaign: High cost but creates a moat by making the chip the primary purchase criterion.

Fit: Aligns with the need to escape price wars and secure long-term OEM contracts through brand loyalty.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if consumers ignore the logo or if major OEMs refuse participation, leaving the budget spent with no leverage. However, the "Intel Inside" model proves that co-op advertising can align incentives; OEMs resent control less when their own ad costs are subsidized. The risk of consumer ignorance is mitigated by the sheer volume of exposed impressions across all partner ads.

Evidence: The proposal correctly identifies that buyers currently cannot distinguish chips. The unprecedented nature of consumer advertising for components is a barrier to entry for competitors, not a flaw. The budget share is high, but the cost of losing the market to clones is higher.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (unprecedented approach, budget share, OEM resentment, lagging sales metrics) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. They do not prove the plan fails as proposed; they define the difficulty of the task. The plan addresses the core problem: lack of buyer awareness enabling clone competition.

Risks: High cash burn before sales lift; potential OEM pushback on creative control. These are managed by setting clear co-op terms and monitoring brand recall as a leading metric.

Kill Criteria: If brand recall among consumers does not rise by 15% within 12 months, or if three major OEMs opt out, pivot to direct incentives.

DECISION: GO
