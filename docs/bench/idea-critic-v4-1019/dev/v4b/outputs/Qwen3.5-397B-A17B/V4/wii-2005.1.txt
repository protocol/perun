First call: GO. The market leader and runner-up are fighting a costly war for hardcore gamers with high-definition hardware; entering that race as a distant third with inferior specs is a losing strategy. This proposal avoids direct competition by creating a new category (motion-controlled, casual gaming) and competing on price and accessibility rather than raw power.

Steelman: By targeting non-gamers and families with a novel, low-cost interface, the console can achieve massive install volume that outweighs thin per-unit margins and attracts developers seeking scale, effectively making the "toy" perception a feature for the target demographic.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Remain a distant third in a shrinking niche; likely eventual exit from hardware.
2. Compete on power: Requires massive R&D spend against entrenched rivals with superior tech; high risk of failure.
3. This plan: Lowers entry barrier, expands the total addressable market, and leverages novelty.

Fit: Assumes the goal is market relevance and profitability, not winning the specs war. The "north star" is expanding the user base, which this directly addresses.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the motion controller is seen as a gimmick with no lasting software support, or if third-party publishers refuse to build games despite the install base. However, the low price point reduces consumer risk, and first-party titles can bridge the gap.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the fact that the current market ignores casual users. The skepticism from hardcore gamers is irrelevant if the target is non-gamers. The thin margin is acceptable if volume compensates (razor-and-blades model).

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (perception, third-party support) are inherent to disrupting a market, not factual contradictions. The lack of HD is a deliberate cost-saving choice, not a defect.

Risks: Software drought if third parties don't buy in; controller novelty wearing off. These are managed by strong first-party launch titles and proving the install base quickly.

Decision logic: The plan beats "do nothing" and the "power race" alternative by changing the rules of engagement. The potential upside (mass market dominance) far exceeds the cost of failure (limited loss vs. guaranteed irrelevance in the status quo).

DECISION: GO
