First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits a large share of the budget to an unproven format (full seasons, no pilot) in an unproven channel (online-only), relying entirely on internal data that has never predicted success for original prestige drama.

**Steelman:** The service's granular viewing data reveals a specific, underserved appetite for this director-star combination, making a traditional pilot redundant and allowing a disruptive "all-at-once" release to capture immediate market share before competitors react.

**Attack:**
1.  **Data Validity:** Historical data reflects user behavior with *licensed* content. There is zero evidence this data predicts demand for *original* productions. Users may binge old sitcoms but reject new dramas. The core premise conflates consumption of library assets with appetite for new IP.
2.  **Financial Ruin:** Committing two full seasons (26 episodes) without a pilot removes the industry's primary kill-switch. If the first three episodes fail to resonate, the company is locked into funding the remaining 23, potentially bankrupting the venture before it gains traction.
3.  **Channel Risk:** No online-only service has ever succeeded with prestige scripted content. The assumption that subscribers will tolerate a purely digital release for high-budget drama is untested. If the platform infrastructure or user习惯 cannot support this shift, the content investment yields no return.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Continue licensing; costs rise but survival is proven.
*   **Pilot First:** Commission a single episode to test the "data hypothesis" and the "online audience" assumption. This preserves capital if the data proves misleading.
*   **Shorter Order:** Commit to one season of 10 episodes, not two.

**Pre-mortem:** The show launches to critical indifference. Subscribers do not sign up in projected numbers because the "data" failed to account for the quality gap between licensed library content and original production. The company burns its entire content budget on 26 episodes of a show nobody watches, leaving no funds for recovery or alternative programming.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The data is derived solely from licensed content, not originals.
*   **Fact:** No pilot exists to validate the creative execution or audience reception.
*   **Fact:** The commitment covers two full seasons, eliminating mid-course correction.

**Risks:** Audience rejection of online-only prestige; cost overruns; star/director underperformance.

**Kill Criteria:** If a single pilot episode does not achieve a completion rate of X% within 48 hours, cancel the series.

The lack of a pilot and the double-season commitment transform a calculated risk into an existential gamble based on unverified data extrapolation.

DECISION: NO-GO
