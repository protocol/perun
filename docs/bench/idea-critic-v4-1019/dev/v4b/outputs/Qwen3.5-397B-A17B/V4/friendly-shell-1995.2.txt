First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a mass market ("many new home users") with a product that requires hardware ("high-end PC") the majority of that market does not possess, while facing imminent obsolescence from the company's own roadmap.

Steelman: A transitional, high-margin niche product ($100) could generate immediate revenue and ease the learning curve for early adopters before the major OS release integrates these features natively.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Preserve engineering capacity for the upcoming major OS release; avoid fragmenting the user base.
2. **Integrate into next OS:** Delay the feature set until the native release, ensuring broad compatibility and zero marginal cost for users.
3. **Proceed as proposed:** Capture a tiny, high-end niche now but risk brand confusion and cannibalization of the upcoming flagship.

Fit: The strategy conflicts with the stated goal of serving "many" users when the hardware requirement excludes most homes. It also misaligns with the company's North Star of platform dominance if it fractures the experience just before a major unified launch.

Pre-mortem: The product launches to poor sales because the install base is too small. Six months later, the major OS release includes similar features for free, rendering the $100 shell worthless and angering early adopters. The engineering effort diverted delays the critical major release.

Evidence & Calculation: The proposal admits "needs more memory than most home PCs have." If "most" cannot run it, the addressable market is a minority of a minority. The "major new operating-system release... coming within months" creates a near-certain cancellation event for this standalone product. The $100 price point is a barrier for the remaining compatible users who can wait for the native solution.

Blockers:
1. **Market Mismatch:** The core target ("many new home users") cannot run the software due to hardware constraints.
2. **Self-Cannibalization:** The company's own upcoming major release invalidates the product's unique value proposition within months.
3. **Opportunity Cost:** Engineering resources spent here delay the critical platform update.

Risks: Brand perception damage ("patronizing"), lack of ecosystem ("few third-party add-ons").

Decision: The hardware mismatch and imminent internal replacement make this a guaranteed failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
