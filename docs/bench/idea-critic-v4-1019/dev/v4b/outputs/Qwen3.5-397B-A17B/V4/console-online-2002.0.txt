First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a paid subscription and broadband requirement in a 2002 market where competitors offer free online play and broadband penetration is minimal, creating an immediate barrier to adoption without proven demand.

**Steelman:** A unified, premium online service creates a sticky ecosystem and recurring revenue stream that differentiates the console through superior social features and reliability, justifying the cost if early adopters value quality over price.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Match rivals with free, publisher-led online play; zero infrastructure cost, but no ecosystem lock-in.
2.  **Hybrid model:** Free basic matchmaking with optional paid premium features (voice, storage); lowers entry barrier while testing willingness to pay.
3.  **Proposed:** Mandatory paid subscription + broadband only. Highest risk, highest potential revenue, but ignores current market constraints.

**Fit:** The strategy assumes a "premium quality" north star, but in 2002, the primary constraint is market readiness, not feature depth. Forcing broadband excludes the vast majority of the installed base.

**Pre-mortem:** The service launches to a tiny fraction of users due to broadband scarcity. Publishers refuse to support a paid-only ecosystem, leading to a barren game library. The company bleeds money on unused server capacity and support staff, forcing a shutdown within 18 months.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "few homes have broadband" and "players expect online play to be free." These are not risks; they are current market facts that directly contradict the mandatory nature of the proposal. The company also lacks operational experience in running such a service, increasing execution risk.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Market Mismatch:** Mandatory broadband in 2002 excludes >90% of potential users (fact stated in proposal).
2.  **Pricing Contradiction:** Charging for a service competitors give away, without a proven unique value proposition that outweighs the cost, violates basic adoption logic for a new console.
3.  **Operational Void:** The company has no history of running hosting/support operations, making the scale of this proposal unverified and dangerous.

**Risks:** High churn, publisher alienation, brand damage if service fails.

The proposal fails because it forces a future-state business model (paid, broadband-only) onto a present-state market that cannot support it. The lack of a pilot, a free tier, or a phased rollout makes this a binary bet on an unproven assumption.

DECISION: NO-GO
