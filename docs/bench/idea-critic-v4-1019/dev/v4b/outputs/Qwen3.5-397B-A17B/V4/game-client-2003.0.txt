First call: GO. The current distribution model fails at its core functions: delivering updates and preventing cheating. The proposed client directly solves these existential threats while creating a new revenue channel.

Steelman: A mandatory client is the only viable mechanism to ensure game integrity and seamless patching in a digital era, transforming a distribution liability into a proprietary platform ecosystem.

Alternatives analysis:
1. **Do nothing:** Continued erosion of game integrity and inability to patch leads to franchise decay.
2. **Retail-only patches:** Logistically impossible at scale; too slow for anti-cheat arms races.
3. **Third-party platforms:** Cedes customer ownership and revenue share; relies on external roadmaps.
The proposal beats "do nothing" by solving the immediate crisis and beats alternatives by capturing long-term platform value.

Fit: Aligns with the shift toward digital services and recurring engagement, moving beyond one-time box sales.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the client launches with critical stability bugs or if the install barrier exceeds the value of the game for casual users. However, for a developer with "very popular" titles, the content leverage outweighs the friction of a new client. The risk of retail pushback is real but acceptable; boxed sales are already struggling with updates. Competitors copying the idea validates the strategy, not invalidates it.

Evidence: The premise rests on the stated failure of current update/anti-cheat methods. No data contradicts the need for a controlled environment.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (broadband adoption, user friction) are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. In 2003, broadband is growing, and dedicated gamers will tolerate friction for fair play. These are managed via kill criteria (e.g., abort if adoption < X% after Y months), not reasons to reject the strategic pivot.

Risks: High initial development cost, potential retail channel conflict, user backlash. These are mitigated by the "very popular" status of existing games, which provides the necessary leverage to force adoption.

The proposal addresses a critical operational failure with a scalable solution that opens a larger market. Hesitation guarantees obsolescence.

DECISION: GO
