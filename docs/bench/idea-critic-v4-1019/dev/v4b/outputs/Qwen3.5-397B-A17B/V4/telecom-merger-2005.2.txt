First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $36 billion to acquire a network explicitly described as incompatible and limited, requiring the buyer to run legacy equipment while absorbing massive goodwill risk without a stated plan to resolve the technological clash.

**Steelman:** The merger secures a high-value business customer base loyal to a unique push-to-talk service, justifying the $36 billion cost and integration headache as the only way to capture this revenue stream before a competitor does.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "build" options are ignored. Could the buyer license the push-to-talk technology or build a compatible application layer on their own superior data network for a fraction of $36 billion? Acquiring the whole network to get the software feature is inefficient.
2.  **Fit & Evidence:** The target's network is "limited for data." In 2005, data growth is the primary value driver. Buying a legacy, incompatible network anchors the combined entity to old tech, slowing the transition to modern data services. The "high-value" customers are tied to a dying tech stack.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 3 years. Cause: Integration costs balloon as two incompatible networks must be run in parallel; the legacy network becomes a drain rather than an asset; the goodwill is written down as the specialized hardware becomes obsolete. The "large goodwill" is a ticking time bomb if the specific hardware users abandon the platform during migration.
4.  **Blocker:** The core claim relies on the target's value being in its *network*. However, the network is incompatible and data-limited. If the value is only the *customers*, paying for the incompatible infrastructure is a strategic error. There is no evidence that the customers cannot be migrated to a software-based solution on the buyer's existing network. The plan fails to separate the valuable asset (customers) from the liability (incompatible network).

**Decision:** The proposal lacks a credible path to monetize the incompatible technology or a cheaper alternative to acquire the customers. The risk of stranded assets and integration failure outweighs the unproven synergy.

DECISION: NO-GO
