First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a fixed public schedule for unproven core technologies without a demand signal or regulatory mandate, creating asymmetric downside.

**Steelman:** A bold, fixed-date commitment forces the organization to solve novel battery and transmission challenges rapidly, securing first-mover status before competitors react.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital while regulators and fuel prices clarify. A "technology probe" (limited fleet, no public date) validates durability without warranty catastrophe. The proposal beats neither; it merely adds schedule risk to technical risk.
2.  **Fit:** With cheap fuel and no mandates, the market north star is cost/reliability, not efficiency. This misaligns with immediate customer value.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume 1998: Mass recall due to battery degradation. The brand is associated with unreliable "future tech" rather than dependable transport. Warranty costs erase decades of profit.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits battery durability is "unproven" and demand is absent. Committing to mass production before proving these is a violation of basic engineering economics. The "bold schedule" is a liability, not an asset, when the critical path (chemistry) is unknown.
5.  **Blocker:** The core claim relies on a "novel power-split transmission" and "NiMH pack" working at volume by 1997. Since durability is explicitly unproven, the plan fails its own feasibility test. A fixed date for an unproven chemical process is not a plan; it is a gamble with corporate solvency.

**Blockers:**
*   Unproven battery durability combined with a fixed mass-production date creates a guaranteed high-probability failure mode (warranty collapse) that the plan does not mitigate.
*   Zero near-term demand signal means the upside is speculative while the downside (cost/reliability) is certain if the tech fails.

**Risks:**
*   Competitors might enter later with mature tech (mitigated by waiting).
*   Fuel prices might rise (uncertain, not a blocker for today's decision).

**Decision:** No-go. The absence of a pilot, the unproven core mechanism, and the lack of market pull make the fixed-date mass-production commitment irrational. De-risk via a limited fleet trial first; remove the public deadline until durability is proven.

DECISION: NO-GO
