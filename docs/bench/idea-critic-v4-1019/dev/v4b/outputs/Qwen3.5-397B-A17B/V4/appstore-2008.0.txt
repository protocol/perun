First call: GO. A closed, curated store transforms the security and stability risks of native code into a defensible product differentiator while capturing revenue from a nascent ecosystem.

**Steelman:** Launching a single, company-controlled app store in 2008 allows the phone maker to pivot from its previous "web-only" stance, offering high-performance native experiences that web apps cannot match, while the 30% fee and strict review process directly mitigate security, stability, and brand-polish concerns.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Stick to web apps; likely cedes the emerging native ecosystem to rivals and limits device utility.
2.  **Open sideloading:** Matches rival openness but invites immediate security failures, carrier backlash, and brand damage from low-quality apps.
3.  **Proposed Walled Garden:** Balances developer excitement with strict quality control, turning the review bottleneck into a trust signal for users and carriers.

**Fit:** This moves the north star from "sufficient web access" to "premium integrated experience," essential for a second-generation device to compete with established rivals.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the review process becomes so slow that developers abandon the platform, or if the 30% fee triggers a developer revolt. However, given the lack of alternative distribution for native iOS-like experiences at this specific historical moment, developer demand will likely outweigh friction. The "one-way door" of opening the API is irreversible, but the cost of delay is losing the entire third-party innovation wave to competitors.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated concerns (security, stability, carrier relations) are features of the proposed solution (review process, single store), not bugs. The existence of rival open ecosystems does not block this; it validates the need for apps while leaving room for a premium, safe alternative.

**Risks:** Review bottlenecks slowing initial growth; potential developer friction over the 30% cut. These are managed via the proposed kill criteria: if app approval times exceed 7 days or if top-tier developers publicly reject the terms within 6 months, the process must be streamlined or fees reconsidered.

**Kill Criteria:** If the store launches with fewer than 500 quality apps in the first quarter, or if a major security breach occurs due to a reviewed app, the review policy must be immediately audited and potentially paused.

DECISION: GO
