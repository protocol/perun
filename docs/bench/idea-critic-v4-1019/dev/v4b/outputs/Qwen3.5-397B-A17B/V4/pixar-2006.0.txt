First call: NO-GO. The price is extreme relative to output, and the structural risks threaten the very asset being purchased.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio is the only way to secure its future output and revitalize the buyer's struggling animation division by installing proven creative leadership.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Lose the partner to a rival; continue internal underperformance.
2.  **Joint Venture/First-Look Deal:** Secure distribution rights without full balance-sheet exposure or culture assimilation.
3.  **Acquisition (Proposed):** Total control, but at $7.4B for ~1 film/year, the valuation assumes perpetual perfection.

**Fit:** While aligning with a content-first strategy, the "all-stock" nature makes the conglomerate's existing shareholders highly vulnerable to the studio's specific execution risk.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal fails because the creative leaders chafe under public-company scrutiny and leave, or the founder uses their massive new equity stake to block strategic shifts. The "culture clash" isn't a risk; it's a near-certainty given the size disparity and the founder's new leverage as a top shareholder.

**Evidence & Math:** At $7.4B for one film a year, even a massive hit ($1B box office) yields a thin margin after production/marketing costs. The payback period is dangerously long. The reliance on "a few creative leaders" creates a key-person risk that no amount of equity can fully mitigate.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Valuation Mismatch:** The price implies a growth trajectory and output volume that the historical record (1 film/year) does not support.
2.  **Governance Hazard:** Making the founder a major shareholder of the parent company creates a conflicted power center that can hold the conglomerate hostage.

**Risks:** Culture clash, key-person dependency, integration failure.

**Decision:** The financial terms are unjustifiable based on current output, and the governance structure invites internal conflict. A less risky commercial partnership should be pursued instead of a total buyout at this premium.

DECISION: NO-GO
