First call: GO. The proposal leverages asymmetric innovation to escape a direct hardware arms race the company cannot win on specs alone.

Steelman: By introducing a dual-screen touch interface at a lower price point, the company creates a unique value proposition that targets non-core gamers, rendering the rival's superior graphics irrelevant for this new segment while maintaining a revenue floor with the legacy device.

**Skeptic:** The claim that "unusual hardware may confuse developers" is a valid friction point, but it is not a blocker; it merely shifts the burden to first-party software to demonstrate the paradigm. The risk of the device being dismissed as a "gimmick" is real, yet this is a marketing challenge, not a fatal product flaw, provided the software library proves utility immediately. The assumption that touch games are "unproven" is actually an opportunity for first-mover advantage in a blue ocean rather than a red ocean of graphical fidelity.

**Better-way:** Alternatives include matching the rival's specs (prohibitively expensive and late), lowering the price of the current non-touch device (fails to broaden the audience), or exiting the handheld market (surrender). "Do nothing" guarantees slow erosion as the rival captures the high-end. The proposed path is the only one that expands the total addressable market rather than fighting for existing share.

**Kill-criteria:** The plan fails if the price premium over the legacy device exceeds 20% without clear software justification, or if no killer app demonstrating touch utility launches within 90 days. The "two screens add cost" objection is mitigated by the explicit mandate to stay below the rival's price; if the math doesn't support this, the specific BOM targets are the blocker, not the concept. However, based on the text, the pricing strategy is explicit ("lower price than the rival"), so we proceed assuming the engineering team can meet that constraint.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because the software library remained too "core-gamer" focused, failing to utilize the touch screen, causing the second screen to be viewed as a costly novelty.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited are execution challenges inherent to innovation, not factual contradictions of the proposal's logic.
**Risks:** Developer adoption lag; consumer perception of gimmickry.

DECISION: GO
