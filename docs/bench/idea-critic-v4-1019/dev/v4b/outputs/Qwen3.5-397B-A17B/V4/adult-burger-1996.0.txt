First call: NO-GO. The plan contradicts the brand's core identity and value proposition without a mechanism to overcome entrenched perception.

**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can temporarily overwrite the "kids' value" brand association, forcing adults to trial a premium burger before the price barrier rejects them.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit:** The proposal ignores the stated constraint that adults view the chain as "fast, not premium." Advertising amplifies existing truths; it rarely inverts deep-seated brand archetypes (clown/fun vs. serious/premium). Spending the "largest budget" on a message the audience inherently disbelieves accelerates cash burn, not conversion.
2.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves margins. A "better way" would be a sub-brand or acquired chain with existing adult equity. The current plan attempts to force a square peg into a round hole using only money.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The campaign launches. Adults perceive the high price as incongruent with the "cheap kids' meal" identity, interpreting it as greed rather than quality. Regular buyers feel alienated by the price hike and shift to competitors. The massive sunk cost forces a prolonged failure before cancellation.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the risks (price deterrence, brand mismatch) but offers no evidence that ad spend alone solves them. It assumes awareness equals desire.

**Blockers:**
*   **Brand Incompatibility:** The core claim relies on adults accepting a "premium" label from a "value/kids" brand. The proposal admits this perception exists but offers no strategic bridge other than spending money. This is a fundamental market fit failure, not an execution risk.
*   **Economic Contradiction:** Targeting "regular buyers" with a price point that "may deter regular buyers" creates an immediate revenue cliff. The plan cannibalizes the base without a verified path to replacing that volume with new adult users.

**Risks:** Massive capital destruction, brand dilution, loss of core family demographic.

**Decision:** The plan fails the basic logic test: you cannot buy your way out of a brand identity contradiction with a single product launch. The risks are not unknowns; they are stated facts in the proposal that the plan ignores.

DECISION: NO-GO
