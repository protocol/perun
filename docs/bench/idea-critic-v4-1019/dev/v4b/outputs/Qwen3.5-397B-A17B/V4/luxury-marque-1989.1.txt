First call: GO. A separate brand with a clean-sheet flagship priced below incumbents directly attacks the prestige barrier while leveraging underlying engineering efficiency.

**Steelman:** Creating a distinct luxury marque with its own retail channel and an all-new flagship allows the manufacturer to bypass economy-brand stigma, offering European-level refinement at a disruptive price point to capture aspirational buyers.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Remain capped at the mid-market; margin growth limited.
2.  **Badge engineering:** Upscale existing models. High risk of brand dilution; unlikely to convince luxury buyers.
3.  **Proposed path:** High upfront cost and execution risk, but offers the only viable route to high-margin segment penetration and brand elevation.

**Fit:** Moves the north star from "volume reliability" to "profitable prestige." Assumes the market values objective quality over heritage if the price delta is sufficient.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the separate dealer network fails to deliver a luxury experience distinct from the mass-market showrooms, or if the single flagship cannot sustain showroom traffic. This is a two-way door regarding future model expansion, but a one-way door on the initial infrastructure spend. Delay costs first-mover advantage in the emerging "value-luxury" niche.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits heavy sunk costs and a thin lineup. However, these are execution hurdles, not fundamental flaws. The core claim—that a separate brand solves the reputation barrier—is historically sound in automotive strategy. The risk of buyers rejecting the brand is mitigated by the price advantage and physical separation of dealers.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (cost, prestige, thin lineup) are inherent to the strategy but do not invalidate the logic; they are managed by the "separate brand" structure and pricing strategy.
**Risks:** Dealer network profitability before volume scales; brand perception lag. These become kill criteria: monitor dealer satisfaction and repeat purchase rates within 24 months.

The plan survives the attack. The separation of brand and channel is the critical success factor that neutralizes the "economy car" stigma.

DECISION: GO
