First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets a dominant running brand's core marketing budget on an unproven rookie, violating the principle of protecting the cash cow while entering a new category.

**Steelman:** Secure a generational talent early to own the basketball narrative before competitors react, using controversy to generate free media coverage that offsets the high upfront cost.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve budget, but cede basketball growth to established rivals.
2.  **Sign a veteran:** Lower risk, proven sales, but higher cost and less "hype" potential.
3.  **Proposed Rookie Deal:** Highest risk/reward. Creates a unique asset but exposes the firm to total loss if the player fails physically or the league bans the product immediately.

**Fit:** Assumes the company can survive a total loss of this budget without hurting the running shoe dominance. This is unverified.

**Pre-mortem:** The player tears an ACL in preseason; the league fines the company daily until compliance; inventory sits unsold because fans reject a rookie's shoe. The brand looks desperate, not innovative.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the player is "unproven," injury is a real risk, and league fines are probable. There is no data showing signature rookie shoes sell long-term. The "colorway rule-break" relies on the league allowing the fines to continue rather than forcing an immediate ban or suspension.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Budget Concentration:** Spending a "large share" of the marketing budget on a single unproven asset creates an existential risk to the broader brand if it fails. No cap or fallback is defined.
2.  **Regulatory Stopper:** If the league enforces uniform rules by banning the shoe entirely (not just fining), the product cannot be sold, rendering the inventory worthless. The proposal treats fines as a cost, not a potential ban.

**Risks:** Player injury, lack of consumer adoption, competitor retaliation.

**Decision:** The lack of a capped pilot (e.g., a one-year option, a smaller initial line) and the reliance on regulatory leniency constitute fatal flaws. The plan fails the "protect the core" test required for a market leader diversifying.

DECISION: NO-GO
