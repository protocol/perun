First call: NO-GO. The core claim—that a mass-market economy brand can successfully launch a standalone luxury division with a single flagship sedan against entrenched European incumbents—ignores the fatal mismatch between brand heritage and luxury perception.

**Steelman:** A separate brand and dealer network can sufficiently insulate the parent's economy reputation, allowing a high-quality, under-priced flagship to disrupt the luxury market before line-up expansion.

**Attack:**
1.  **Brand Gravity:** "Economy" is not just a price point; it is a psychological ceiling. Buyers paying luxury premiums seek status and heritage, not value. A "cheap luxury" car is an oxymoron to the target demographic. The separate name may not overcome the known parentage quickly enough to justify the investment.
2.  **The Single-Model Trap:** Luxury buyers expect a range (sedan, coupe, wagon, SUV). Launching with one sedan forces customers to compromise on body style or wait years for updates, driving them back to incumbents with full showrooms.
3.  **Capital Incineration:** Building a dealer network from scratch requires massive fixed costs before a single unit sells. With only one model to amortize these costs, the break-even volume is likely unattainable in the short term. The "heavy development cost" cited is a sunk cost fallacy; throwing more capital at a flawed go-to-market strategy compounds the loss.
4.  **Incumbent Moat:** European rivals compete on prestige, not just refinement. Undercutting them on price signals "inferior," not "value," in the luxury segment.

**Better Way:** Acquire an existing struggling luxury marque to inherit its heritage and dealer network, or upgrade the parent brand's top trim gradually (though this caps price potential). Do not build a castle on sand.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders from current owner-clients do not exceed 20% of capacity within six months of reveal, the brand transition has failed. If the cost per dealer setup exceeds the projected 3-year margin per outlet, the network strategy is insolvent.

**Blockers:** The reliance on a single model to sustain a standalone luxury dealer network is a mathematical failure; the unit economics cannot support the fixed overhead without a full range. The brand perception gap is not solvable by engineering quality alone.

DECISION: NO-GO
