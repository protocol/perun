First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets the brand's equity on a product that contradicts the core sensory identity of the category (cola flavor equals dark color) without a proven retention mechanism.

**Steelman:** A clear cola captures a permanent niche of "purity-seeking" drinkers who currently avoid traditional cola, generating incremental revenue that outweighs short-term novelty decay and manageable cannibalization.

**Attack:**
1.  **Category Contradiction:** The primary risk—that shoppers link cola flavor to color—is not a minor hurdle but a fundamental cognitive dissonance. If the taste is authentic cola, the clear appearance creates distrust; if the taste is altered to match the look, it ceases to be a cola competitor. Test markets often mask this by attracting curiosity seekers rather than repeat buyers.
2.  **Cannibalization vs. Extension:** Without evidence that "purity" drives *new* volume rather than shifting existing flagship sales, a nationwide launch with a "large marketing campaign" risks diluting the flagship's shelf presence and marketing share for a product with high churn.
3.  **Missing Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks a specific metric to distinguish novelty trials from repeat purchases before scaling. Launching nationwide based solely on "positive response" ignores the high probability of a steep drop-off after the first purchase.
4.  **Better Way:** A staged regional rollout with a strict "repeat purchase rate" gate is superior. If the product cannot sustain >20% repeat usage in a controlled test after the novelty window (e.g., 90 days), a nationwide launch is financially reckless. The current plan commits to full scale before validating the core hypothesis of retention.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal admits "novelty buyers may not repeat" and "test markets can overstate demand," yet proceeds to a nationwide launch anyway. This is a contradiction between identified risk and proposed action.
*   **Fact:** No mechanism is provided to verify if the "positive response" includes repeat intent or just trial.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, wasted marketing spend on a fad, flagship cannibalization without net growth.

The plan fails because it treats a high-uncertainty hypothesis (clear cola retention) as a confirmed fact suitable for immediate nationwide scaling. It must prove repeat behavior before committing to a large campaign.

DECISION: NO-GO
