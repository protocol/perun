First call: GO. The current distribution model fails at its core functions: delivering updates and preventing cheating. The proposed client directly solves these existential threats while creating a new revenue channel.

Steelman: A mandatory client is the only viable mechanism to ensure game integrity and seamless patching in a digital era, transforming a distribution liability into a platform asset.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing: Cheating erodes the player base; update failures increase support costs and churn. Result: slow decline.
2. Voluntary patcher: Fails to stop cheaters who simply opt out; fragmented user experience.
3. Retail-only updates: Logistically impossible for frequent balance patches; too slow for anti-cheat arms races.

Fit: Aligns with the industry shift toward digital services and recurring revenue, moving beyond one-time box sales.

Pre-mortem: The initiative fails if the client launches with critical bugs that prevent gameplay, causing immediate retail backlash and player revolt. However, this is an execution risk, not a strategic flaw. The cost of delay is high: every month without effective anti-cheat accelerates community decay. Broadband adoption is rising; being early secures the foothold before competitors.

Evidence: The proposal cites specific operational failures (updates, cheating) that the current box-model cannot address. No data suggests players prefer broken, cheated games over a mandatory client.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (dislike of accounts, broadband gaps, retail friction) are adoption hurdles, not logical contradictions. They mitigate via phased rollout and improved client stability, not by abandoning the strategy. The "competitors may copy" risk is actually a validation of the strategy's potential value.

Risks: Initial player friction, retail partner churn, technical debt. These are managed by making the client robust and valuable (store features) rather than optional.

The absence of universal broadband in 2003 is a constraint, but the trajectory is clear. Waiting for 100% penetration cedes the anti-cheat and update advantage to pirates and competitors. The upside of a unified, secure platform outweighs the transitional friction of mandatory adoption.

DECISION: GO
