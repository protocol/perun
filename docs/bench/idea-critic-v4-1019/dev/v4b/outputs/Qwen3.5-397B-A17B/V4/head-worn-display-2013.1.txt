First call: NO-GO. The stated facts describe a product with unresolved fundamental barriers to mass-market adoption: invasive privacy implications, prohibitive cost, undefined utility for the average user, and social friction.

Steelman: An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to validate demand and refine the technology among tech enthusiasts before a general release, treating early risks as manageable growing pains.

Attack:
1.  **Fit & Utility:** The proposal positions this as an "everyday consumer product," yet admits there is "no clear everyday use for most people." A product cannot be everyday if it lacks daily utility. The high price ($1,500) further contradicts mass-market positioning, limiting the pool to early adopters who do not represent the "general public" target.
2.  **Privacy & Social Acceptance:** The privacy objections and social acceptance risks are not merely technical hurdles; they are existential threats to the product category. A wearable camera creates immediate hostility in public spaces, potentially leading to bans in venues and social ostracization, which kills the "everyday" use case regardless of battery life.
3.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "enterprise-only" alternative is superior. Selling to enterprises (manufacturing, logistics, medicine) where the camera provides clear utility, the cost is justifiable, and privacy is controlled avoids the consumer backlash entirely. The proposed path attacks the hardest market (privacy-sensitive consumers) with the weakest value proposition (no clear use).
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume the general launch failed. Why? Because the public rejected the device as a privacy violation and a social liability, not because of battery life. The invitation-only program validates interest among hobbyists but fails to prove viability for the stated goal of an "everyday consumer product."

Blockers:
-   **Contradictory Positioning:** The plan targets the "general public" as an "everyday product" while explicitly acknowledging "no clear everyday use" and "privacy objections." This is a strategic contradiction that guarantees market rejection.
-   **Price vs. Value:** $1,500 is unjustifiable without a defined everyday utility, making the general release financially non-viable.

Risks: Social backlash, regulatory scrutiny on wearable recording, brand damage.

The proposal attempts to bypass fundamental product-market fit issues with an exclusive launch, but the core defects (privacy, utility, price) prevent it from ever becoming the proposed "everyday consumer product."

DECISION: NO-GO
