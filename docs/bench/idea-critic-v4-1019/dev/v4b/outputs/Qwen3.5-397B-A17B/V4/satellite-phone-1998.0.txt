First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and market timing fundamentally contradict the capital structure.

**Steelman:** A premium, niche service for global travelers who value ubiquitous coverage over cost and device ergonomics, generating immediate cash flow to service debt before terrestrial networks saturate.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Shrinkage:** The target demographic (international business travelers) is precisely the group most likely to benefit from rapidly expanding ground cellular roaming agreements, which are becoming cheaper and more convenient. The "ubiquity" advantage shrinks daily.
2.  **Unit Economics Failure:** At $3,000 per handset and several dollars per minute, the addressable market is too small to support $5 billion in debt. Debt service requires massive, immediate scale, but the high price point inherently restricts volume. You cannot solve a $5 billion balance sheet problem with a luxury niche product.
3.  **Product-Market Friction:** Bulky handsets that fail indoors severely limit utility. Business travelers need reliability in airports, hotels, and conference centers—exactly where this system underperforms compared to ground networks.
4.  **Channel Risk:** Without ready sales channels, subscriber acquisition will lag. Debt payments do not wait for channel development.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing/Wait:** Delay launch until handsets are smaller/cheaper and a clearer gap in terrestrial coverage exists. (Risk: Sunk costs, debt interest).
*   **Pivot to B2B/Government:** Ignore consumers; sell exclusively to maritime, aviation, and defense sectors where indoor use is less critical and price sensitivity is lower.
*   **Restructure:** Negotiate debt relief before launching a product likely to fail commercially.

**Pre-mortem:** The company launches, misses subscriber targets by 40% due to price and device bulk, cannot service debt, and declares bankruptcy within two years while terrestrial networks achieve 90% global population coverage.

**Blockers:**
*   The math does not work: The projected revenue from a price-constrained niche cannot service $5 billion in borrowed capital.
*   The core value proposition (global coverage) is being rapidly commoditized by the very trend cited as a risk (spreading ground cellular).

**Risks:** Execution delays, further handset bulk issues, slower than expected terrestrial rollout (the only hope for this plan).

The proposal attempts to force a mass-market financial solution onto a luxury-product reality. The debt load demands success; the product specs and pricing guarantee failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
