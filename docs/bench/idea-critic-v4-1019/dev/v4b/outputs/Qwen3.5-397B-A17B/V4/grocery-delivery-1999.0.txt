First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits irreversible capital to an unproven model in a low-margin industry before validating unit economics.

**Steelman:** Rapidly building a national moat via automated warehouses prevents competitors from securing density, accepting early losses as the cost of market dominance.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" beyond the current metro preserves cash while validating the core hypothesis: can anyone profitably deliver groceries in 30 minutes? A "cheaper alternative" involves leasing existing cold storage and using third-party logistics to test density requirements without billion-dollar fixed costs. The proposal ignores these, betting the entire company on a single untested architecture.
2.  **Fit & Evidence:** The startup has zero operating track record. The claim that rapid expansion is necessary contradicts the stated risk of "customer-density needs." Density is local, not national; failing in one city does not require failing in twelve simultaneously. There is no evidence that 1999 infrastructure or consumer behavior supports this scale.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 24 months. Cause: The $1 billion is spent on warehouses in cities where order volume is 10% of projections. Fixed costs crush thin margins before density is achieved. Cash runs out. The assets are too specialized to sell.
4.  **Blocker:** The proposal explicitly admits to acting "before learning the unit economics." In a low-margin business, unit economics are the only determinant of viability. Committing $1 billion before knowing if the fundamental math works is not a strategy; it is a gamble with a known negative expected value based on current facts. The lack of a pilot or capped experiment is a fatal flaw.

**Risks:** Capital intensity, unproven demand, operational complexity, and technological obsolescence. These are not mere risks here; they are certainties of failure without validation.

**Kill Criteria:** None exist in the proposal. A valid plan would cap spending at a level sufficient to prove profitability in the first city before expanding.

The absence of a learning loop before massive capital deployment is a blocker. The plan fails as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
