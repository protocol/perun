First call: GO. The company leverages its dominant hardware position to solve the industry's existential threat: piracy.

**Steelman:** A legal, frictionless alternative to piracy, integrated with the market-leading player, will convert casual pirates into paying customers despite a higher marginal cost than free.

**Attack & Analysis:**
*   **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the ecosystem to chaos, eventually harming hardware sales. Subscription models (the rival approach) fail in this era due to poor connectivity and consumer preference for ownership. The proposed per-song model fits current user behavior better.
*   **Fit:** This moves the North Star from "selling boxes" to "owning the music ecosystem." Without content, the hardware advantage erodes.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if labels withdraw licenses or if the proprietary format frustrates users. However, labels are desperate for revenue and have no better alternative; their leverage is temporary. The "walled garden" risk is mitigated by the hardware's market share.
*   **Evidence:** The premise relies on the hardware's existing install base. If the player is truly "selling well," users are a captive audience seeking convenience over perfect freedom. The 99-cent price point is a psychological anchor; marginal margin matters less than ecosystem lock-in.
*   **Blockers vs. Risks:** The risks (label revolt, low margin, proprietary lock-in) are execution hurdles, not factual blockers. No evidence suggests labels *will* withdraw; conversely, their need for a solution is acute. The lack of Windows support initially is a strategic choice to drive hardware adoption, not a fatal flaw.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal addresses the core threat (piracy) with the only viable monetization model available in 2003 (à la carte). Rivals betting on subscriptions are likely premature. The "walled garden" creates a competitive moat. While margins are thin, the strategic value of controlling the distribution channel outweighs immediate per-unit profit. The absence of a pilot is acceptable given the urgency and the clarity of the market failure (piracy).

**Kill Criteria:** If major labels refuse licensing within 6 months, or if attach rate (songs per device) remains below 1.0 after one year, the strategy must pivot to subscription or licensing the OS.

DECISION: GO
