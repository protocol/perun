First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and behavioral friction of a proprietary, internet-dependent juicer at a luxury price point in a commoditized market suggest a fundamental mismatch between cost and value.

**Steelman:** A premium, hassle-free nutrition ecosystem where freshness and convenience justify a high upfront cost and recurring subscription, locked via hardware/software integration.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** At $699, the device targets early adopters, but the recurring cost of proprietary bags likely exceeds fresh produce without offering proportional value (taste/nutrition). Consumers resist "razor-and-blade" models when the "blade" (juice) is cheaper to make manually.
2.  **Friction:** Mandatory Wi-Fi for a countertop appliance introduces unnecessary failure points (connectivity loss, server shutdowns) and privacy concerns for a simple mechanical task. This creates a "brick" risk if the startup fails.
3.  **Market Fit:** The juicer market is saturated with cheap, effective mechanical alternatives. The specific niche of "internet-connected pressing" solves no urgent pain point that justifies a 10x price premium over standard blenders or juicers.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The company runs out of cash trying to subsidize hardware to gain subscription users who churn due to high per-serving costs. The app becomes a liability when servers are deprecated.

**Blockers:**
*   **Value Proposition Failure:** The proposed subscription model almost certainly results in a higher cost-per-ounce than fresh produce, violating basic consumer rationality for a commodity good.
*   **Unnecessary Complexity:** Internet connectivity is a solution in search of a problem here, adding cost and risk without clear upside.

**Risks:** High churn, brand damage from "dumb" smart devices, limited total addressable market due to price.

**Kill Criteria:** If customer acquisition cost exceeds 3x lifetime value within 6 months, or if churn exceeds 10% monthly due to bag pricing, pivot or cease hardware production.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The only potential defense is extreme convenience for a wealthy niche, but the requirement for internet connectivity undermines reliability, making even that segment vulnerable to competitors offering offline, cheaper premium options.

DECISION: NO-GO
