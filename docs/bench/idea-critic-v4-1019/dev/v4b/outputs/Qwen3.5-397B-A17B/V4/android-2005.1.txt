First call: GO. The stated facts show a search-ad company with no hardware business betting that free, open mobile software will drive mobile web usage and thus search ad revenue. Despite carrier control and handset maker inertia, the upside (owning the mobile gateway for search) justifies the $50M entry cost against a "do nothing" scenario where the company remains dependent on others to unlock mobile traffic.

Steelman: Acquiring this six-person team is the cheapest, fastest way to seed an open ecosystem that forces mobile web adoption, directly fueling the core search-ad business before competitors lock down the platform.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Risk being shut out of mobile search as carriers/handset makers build walled gardens.
2. Build internally: Too slow; misses the window to influence the emerging OS landscape.
3. Partner with existing OS vendors: They have no incentive to prioritize this search engine; likely to favor their own or paid partners.

Fit: Moves the north star of "maximizing search ad volume" by creating the conditions for mobile search growth.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if handset makers ignore the OS despite it being free, or if carriers block installation. However, "free" and "open-source" are potent levers against high-cost proprietary alternatives, especially for smaller handset makers looking to cut costs. The cost of delay is high; waiting a year could see the market solidify around incumbents.

Evidence: The proposal admits risks (no revenue model, adoption hurdles) but correctly identifies the strategic logic: mobile web = search ads. The $50M price tag for a 6-person team is high per capita but low in absolute terms for a potential market-defining asset.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The lack of a direct revenue model for the OS is intentional; the revenue comes from search, which is the acquirer's core business.

Risks: Slow adoption, carrier resistance. These are mitigated by the "open" nature allowing community and manufacturer customization, and the long-term view required for platform shifts.

Kill criteria: If after 3 years, the OS has <1% market share and no major handset maker has committed to shipping it, pivot to licensing the tech or shutting down the project. Leading metric: Number of active devices running the OS by Q4 2008.

DECISION: GO
