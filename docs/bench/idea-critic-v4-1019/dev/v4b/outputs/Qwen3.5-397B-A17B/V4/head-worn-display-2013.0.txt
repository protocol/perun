First call: NO-GO. The stated facts describe a product with active, unresolved blockers in privacy, utility, and social acceptance, priced at a premium that demands mass-market appeal which the proposal explicitly admits is missing.

**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to validate privacy norms and use-cases with a controlled group before a general consumer release, mitigating risk through staged exposure.

**Attack:** The steelman collapses under the weight of the stated risks. Privacy objections to a wearable camera are not merely "risks" but potential deal-breakers that an invitation-only program cannot solve; if the device is socially unacceptable or legally restricted in key markets due to privacy, a small early-adopter cohort does not create a path to "everyday consumer product" status. The high price ($1,500 in 2013) contradicts the "everyday" positioning, as everyday items require mass adoption to justify infrastructure, yet the use case remains undefined for most people. You cannot sell an undefined utility at a luxury price point and expect it to become everyday. The "social acceptance" risk is fatal for a product designed to be worn in public; if users feel awkward or are asked to remove the device, the value proposition vanishes instantly.

**Alternatives:** Doing nothing preserves capital and brand reputation. A B2B or enterprise-focused pilot (e.g., for surgeons, mechanics, or logistics) would address specific utility needs and bypass general public privacy backlash, offering a clearer route to viability than a confused consumer push.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, faces immediate public backlash and legal challenges regarding surreptitious recording, fails to gain traction due to lack of clear utility, and is discontinued within 18 months, damaging the parent brand's trust.

**Blockers:** 
1. No clear everyday use case for the target mass market, making the $1,500 price unjustifiable.
2. Active privacy objections and social acceptance issues that directly contradict the "everyday consumer" positioning and cannot be solved by an invitation-only tier.

**Risks:** Short battery life, high manufacturing costs, potential regulatory bans.

The proposal attempts to bypass fundamental product-market fit questions with a staged rollout, but the core value proposition is undefined and the risks are existential to the category.

DECISION: NO-GO
