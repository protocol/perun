First call: NO-GO. The plan commits to a massive, irreversible capital outlay (130–150 stores) before proving the core hypothesis: that the retailer's domestic value proposition translates to a market with different competitors, regulations, and supply chain realities.

Steelman: Rapid saturation creates immediate scale to amortize the high fixed costs of a new cross-border supply chain, preempting the entrenched competitor.

Attack:
1.  **Fit & Strategy:** The proposal admits the need to "learn" logistics, regulation, and labeling. Learning requires iteration. Opening ~150 stores in two years is an execution sprint, not a learning loop. It maximizes exposure to unproven systems before they are stabilized.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A "beachhead" strategy (5–10 stores) tests price perception and supply chain viability with capped downside. The proposed "all-in" approach lacks a reversible off-ramp; failing at store 140 is catastrophic compared to failing at store 5.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in Year 3. Cause: The new inventory system glitches under real-world cross-border complexity, causing stockouts in 100+ locations simultaneously. The brand's "loyal following" does not exist here; the "higher prices" of incumbents reflect local cost structures the discounter underestimated. The entrenched competitor lowers prices temporarily to crush the newcomer.
4.  **Evidence Gap:** No pilot data exists. The claim that "nearly all" stores should open quickly relies on the assumption that domestic operations map 1:1 to international, which the proposal itself contradicts by listing unique hurdles (bilingual labeling, new regulations).

Blockers:
-   **Scale vs. Learning:** The velocity (150 stores/2 years) directly contradicts the stated need to "learn" new complex systems. You cannot learn safely at this scale.
-   **Unproven Systems:** Deploying a new inventory management system across a vast, untested network simultaneously is a single point of failure with no fallback.

Risks:
-   Price perception mismatch.
-   Entrenched competitor retaliation.
-   Regulatory delays stalling specific sites while leases burn cash.

The plan fails the "reversibility" and "learning" test. It bets the company on a hypothesis that requires a pilot to validate. A capped, time-boxed pilot of 5–10 stores is the only rational first step.

DECISION: NO-GO
