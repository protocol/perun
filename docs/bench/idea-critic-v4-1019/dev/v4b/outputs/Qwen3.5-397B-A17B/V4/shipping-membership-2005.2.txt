First call: GO. Removing the largest friction for repeat customers aligns with standard retail growth logic, despite the cited risks.

**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee creates immediate cash flow and locks in loyalty, increasing order frequency enough to offset per-unit shipping losses through volume efficiencies and higher lifetime value.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary fear is that heavy buyers will cause margins to collapse. However, "do nothing" guarantees continued friction and lost repeat business. A "pay-per-use" alternative fails to solve the friction problem. The only viable alternative is a lower fee with stricter limits, but that weakens the psychological commitment and marketing hook.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
Assuming the goal is market share dominance and frequency, this fits. If it fails, it will be because the uptake rate among low-frequency shoppers was too high (subsidized by the retailer) or the operational cost of two-day shipping was underestimated. This is a one-way door; once sold, refunds damage trust. The cost of delay is ceding the "loyalty" mindshare to competitors.

**Evidence & Verification:**
No external data exists to verify the 2005 economics. We must rely on the internal assertion that shipping is the *biggest* friction. If this premise is false, the solution solves nothing. The proposal admits economics are unproven; therefore, the financial model is an assumption, not a fact.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*Blockers:* NONE. The lack of proven economics is a risk, not a blocker, provided the plan includes a mechanism to learn quickly. The difficulty of reversal is a feature of the commitment, not a flaw, unless the loss per member is catastrophic immediately.
*Risks:* Margin erosion if frequency doesn't scale; operational strain; inability to pivot if the model fails.

**Decision:**
The proposal identifies a critical friction point and offers a bold mechanism to remove it. While the risks of margin hit and irreversibility are real, they are inherent to any disruptive loyalty strategy. The absence of a pilot or cap in the text is a gap, but per protocol, we add the kill criterion rather than block the sound strategic direction. The potential upside of locking in customer behavior outweighs the unproven short-term margin risk.

**Kill Criterion:** Halt expansion if average shipping cost per member exceeds $90 within the first 6 months.

DECISION: GO
