First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines a premium price, a tiny compatible installed base, and a missing legal content ecosystem, while cheaper competitors have already failed to gain traction.

Steelman: A tightly integrated, high-capacity device could create a superior user experience that justifies a premium, eventually expanding beyond the current small computer base.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoid capital risk in a proven difficult market.
2. License software broadly: Immediate addressable market expansion, but delays launch and dilutes integration control.
3. Proposed plan: High risk, high reward if the ecosystem can be forced open later.

Fit: Assumes the company can pivot from a niche computer maker to a consumer electronics leader without prior experience or current distribution. This contradicts the stated lack of consumer experience.

Pre-mortem: The device launches to critical acclaim for design but fails commercially because the $400 price excludes the mass market, the 5GB capacity is overkill for the tiny user base, and the lack of legal downloads makes the device a complex file-transfer tool rather than a music solution. Component sourcing delays push launch past the holiday window. This is a one-way door with high sunk costs.

Evidence: The proposal admits the computer market share is small, consumer experience is non-existent, cheaper players have failed, and legal downloads are unavailable. These are stated facts, not risks.

Blockers:
1. **Addressable Market:** The stated facts show the immediate compatible user base is too small to support the development and sourcing costs of a custom hardware project at a $400 price point.
2. **Content Ecosystem:** The core value proposition (digital music) relies on a legal download infrastructure that the proposal admits does not yet exist.
3. **Capability Gap:** The company explicitly lacks consumer electronics experience, making the "under a year" timeline for a complex hardware/software integration highly improbable.

Risks: Component sourcing delays, price sensitivity, brand dilution.

The plan fails on its own stated facts: the market is too small, the content is missing, and the builder is unproven. A cheaper, staged alternative (software licensing first) reaches the same upside potential with less capital risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
