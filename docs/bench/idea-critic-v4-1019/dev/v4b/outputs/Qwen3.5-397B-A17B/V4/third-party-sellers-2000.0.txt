First call: GO. A marketplace model leverages existing traffic to generate high-margin fee revenue without inventory risk, addressing the core profitability gap.

**Steelman:** Transforming from a pure retailer to a hybrid platform maximizes selection and liquidity, turning competitor listings into incremental revenue rather than lost sales, while shifting fulfillment risk to third parties.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Cannibalization vs. Conversion:** While used copies may undercut new sales, losing a sale to a third-party seller on-site is superior to losing the customer entirely to an external competitor. The fee recoups margin on transactions otherwise lost.
2.  **Trust & Quality:** The risk of seller misbehavior is real but manageable via performance bonds, strict suspension policies, and customer guarantees funded by fees. This is an operational cost, not a strategic blocker.
3.  **Supplier Relations:** Publishers may object, but the expanded long-tail inventory (out-of-print, used) creates a destination hub that increases overall traffic, potentially boosting new book visibility.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" guarantees continued losses as inventory costs rise. Building a separate storefront for third parties fragments the user experience and reduces liquidity. The integrated page is the only path to critical mass.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if fee structures are too low to cover support costs for bad actors, or if quality control is so lax that brand trust collapses. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The "one-way door" risk is low; sellers can be delisted if the model fails.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's stated risks are inherent to the marketplace model but do not invalidate the economic logic. The fear of undercutting ignores the alternative: zero revenue on those units.
**Risks:** Brand dilution from poor seller service; supplier retaliation. These become kill criteria: suspend sellers exceeding a 2% complaint rate; pause category expansion if top-tier suppliers terminate contracts.

The economics of taking a cut on infinite shelf-space outweigh the risks of managing it, provided strict governance is enacted immediately.

DECISION: GO
