First call: NO-GO. Launching a premium-priced tablet with a tiny app catalog against a dominant incumbent, while the OS has negligible share and leadership is in flux, ignores the primary driver of tablet adoption: ecosystem depth.

Steelman: The hardware quality and brand trust can overcome a thin app catalog if marketing convinces developers and users that this is the inevitable next platform, creating a self-fulfilling prophecy before the leadership transition stalls momentum.

Options:
1. **Proposed:** Premium price, small catalog, heavy ad spend. High cost, extreme risk, low reversibility once inventory is built.
2. **Do Nothing:** Wait for OS maturity and leadership stability. Zero cost, risk of missing the window, fully reversible.
3. **Subsidized Entry:** Low price to force adoption, accepting hardware losses to build app density. High cost, moderate risk, hard to reverse pricing later.

Fit: Assuming the north star is market share growth, this plan misaligns price with value. Users pay for apps, not just hardware. A premium price requires a premium ecosystem, which does not exist.

Pre-mortem: The device launches; reviewers praise the build but pan the "app gap." Developers ignore outreach because install numbers are trivial. Sales miss targets by 80%. The company is stuck with unsold inventory and a demoralized developer relations team just as new leadership arrives, forcing a fire sale that brands the OS as a failure.

Evidence: The proposal admits the app catalog is a "small fraction" and OS share is "almost none." History shows platforms with <10% share struggle to attract premium developers without massive subsidies or killer exclusive apps (not mentioned).

Blockers:
1. **Price-Value Mismatch:** Charging market-leading prices for a fraction of the utility (apps) contradicts basic consumer behavior in this category.
2. **Timing:** Launching during leadership transition invites internal paralysis and external perception of instability.

Risks: Developer apathy, consumer confusion, inventory write-downs.

The plan fails because it asks consumers to pay a premium for an incomplete product in a winner-take-most market. The heavy ad spend will only accelerate the realization of the app gap.

DECISION: NO-GO
