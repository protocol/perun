First call: NO-GO. The proposal compounds a recent 60% price hike with immediate, high-friction operational fragmentation.

**Steelman:** Separating the brands isolates the declining DVD asset to prevent it from dragging down streaming's valuation and strategic agility.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing & Trust:** Executing a complex brand split immediately after a massive price increase is catastrophic for customer sentiment. It signals instability and punishes loyal users twice: first with higher costs, then with broken convenience (two logins, two bills).
2.  **Value Destruction:** Severing data sharing eliminates cross-service recommendations, degrading the product experience for both segments before the new brands can establish independent value.
3.  **Cost vs. Benefit:** The operational cost of building a separate brand, site, and billing system diverts critical resources from streaming growth during a pivotal moment. The "declining" DVD business still generates cash; strangling it via confusion accelerates revenue loss without guaranteeing streaming gains.
4.  **Alternatives:** A internal management split achieves the strategic separation without the customer-facing chaos. A phased approach would wait for price-hike backlash to subside.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, churn spikes as customers overwhelmed by the dual-billing hassle cancel both services. The stock tanks due to perceived mismanagement, not market fundamentals. The "clean" streaming brand is tainted by the association.

**Blockers:**
*   **Compounded Shock:** The combination of a 60% price hike and immediate service fragmentation creates an uninsurable retention risk.
*   **Data Silos:** Deliberately breaking recommendation engines reduces immediate unit economics for no proven gain.
*   **Execution Risk:** Building duplicate infrastructure while managing PR fallout from the price hike exceeds reasonable operational capacity.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, customer confusion, accelerated DVD collapse, streaming growth stall.

**Conclusion:** The proposal solves an internal accounting preference by creating an external customer nightmare. It fails the "do no harm" test during a volatile pricing transition. The strategic goal (separate management) does not require a customer-facing brand split at this specific moment.

DECISION: NO-GO
