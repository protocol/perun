First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer with no developer platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors are already hostile to its capital spending, creating a high probability of catastrophic distraction and margin dilution.

**Steelman:** Leveraging spiky internal infrastructure to create a new revenue stream could theoretically amortize fixed costs and diversify income if the market matures rapidly.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capability Gap:** Running a reliable, multi-tenant developer platform requires distinct engineering, sales, and support competencies absent in a retail organization. Building these from scratch while managing "spiky" internal loads invites reliability failures that damage both brands.
2.  **Market Timing:** The market is explicitly "unproven." Being first requires subsidizing education and adoption, burning cash the company cannot afford given investor skepticism.
3.  **Strategic Conflict:** Investors question heavy capex; this plan doubles down on infrastructure spend for a speculative venture, directly antagonizing capital providers.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves focus on the core retail margin. A "wait and see" approach allows incumbents to validate the market before entering.

**Pre-mortem:** One year later, the platform suffers outages due to conflicting internal/external load priorities. Retail margins slip due to management distraction. Investors revolt over continued capex without ROI, forcing a fire sale of the new unit.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capability:** Zero internal experience running a developer platform at scale.
*   **Capital:** Investors are already hostile to the required heavy spending; this exacerbates the conflict.
*   **Market:** The core value proposition (hourly server rental) is unproven; no evidence exists that external developers will trust a retailer over hardware vendors.

**Risks:** Margin dilution, brand damage from reliability issues, loss of strategic focus. These are not mere risks; combined with the blockers, they form a fatal thesis. The plan fails the "fit" test: it does not move the north star of retail profitability and introduces existential financial risk. A cheaper alternative is to optimize internal usage or wait for market validation.

DECISION: NO-GO
