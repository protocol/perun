First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a company dependent on gaming revenue to raise unit costs and divert engineering focus from a fierce competitive race to build a market that does not yet exist and has no committed customers.

Steelman: Embedding general-purpose compute capabilities in every consumer chip now creates an indispensable moat and captures the future of scientific computing before rivals react, turning a commodity graphics market into a high-value platform.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve margins and focus on winning the core gaming war; enter computing later if a market proves viable.
2. Software-only layer: Enable limited compute via existing APIs without expensive silicon changes or full compiler toolchains.
3. Targeted high-end SKU: Add features only to expensive workstation cards where scientific users might pay, avoiding mass-market cost penalties.

Fit: This contradicts the immediate survival need to win the graphics race. It assumes a future scientific market will materialize to justify current costs, but the proposal admits no large paying market exists.

Pre-mortem: The program burns cash for years; the core gaming business loses share to the rival due to distracted engineering and higher prices; the scientific feature set remains unused by consumers and too niche to attract enterprise buyers; the company runs out of capital or is acquired.

Evidence: The proposal itself states revenue is "almost entirely" from gaming, there are "no committed customers," and demand is "niche." Raising unit costs in a competitive mass market without immediate demand is financially fatal.

Blockers:
1. Financial suicide: Increasing unit costs on every chip while fighting a primary rival for core revenue, with zero committed customers to offset the expense.
2. Strategic distraction: Diverting engineering from the "core race" threatens the existing cash cow required to fund any future venture.

Risks: Niche demand never scales; academic experimentation does not convert to enterprise spend; competitor undercuts price while this firm adds costly unused silicon.

The plan fails as proposed because it jeopardizes the company's current survival for a speculative future without a bridge mechanism (like a high-end SKU) to validate demand or protect margins.

DECISION: NO-GO
