First call: NO-GO. Launching early with high price, scarce software, and alienated retailers contradicts the fundamental requirements for a successful console ecosystem.

**Steelman:** An early May launch at $399 secures a four-month head start to establish market presence before the competitor arrives, betting that being first outweighs the lack of games and higher cost.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing (September Launch):** Aligns with developer schedules, ensures a robust game library, matches competitor timing, and maintains retailer relationships. Risk: Losing the "first to market" narrative.
2.  **Proposed Plan (May Launch):** Gains four months of exclusivity but risks immediate backlash due to $100 price premium, empty shelves (few games), and angry excluded retailers.
3.  **Limited Technical Demo:** Showcase the hardware at the trade show without shipping units, building hype while waiting for the September window.

**Fit:** A console's success depends on software availability and developer support. Launching when the machine is "hard to program" and developers "did not plan" guarantees a software drought. A console without games fails regardless of launch date.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because early adopters pay a premium for a device with nothing to play, leading to negative reviews and returns. Excluded retailers prioritize the competitor's cheaper, better-stocked console. Developers, rushed by the date change, deliver poor quality ports, damaging the brand's reputation long-term.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the critical failures: price disadvantage ($399 vs $299 implied), software scarcity, developer unreadiness, and retailer hostility. These are not risks; they are stated facts that guarantee a poor customer experience.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Software Void:** Shipping with "few games ready" for a game console is a fatal flaw; the product offers no core value.
2.  **Price Disadvantage:** Being $100 more expensive than the rival at launch creates an immediate barrier to entry with no compensating benefit (like a game library).
3.  **Channel Alienation:** Retaliatory behavior from excluded retailers can permanently shelf the product in key markets.

**Risks:** Long-term brand damage, developer churn, and inventory glut.

**Kill Criteria:** If a full game library is not available by launch day, or if the price cannot match the competitor, the launch must be delayed.

DECISION: NO-GO
