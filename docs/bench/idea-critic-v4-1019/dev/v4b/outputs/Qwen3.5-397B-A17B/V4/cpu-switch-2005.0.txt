First call: GO. The current roadmap fails the core requirement for thin, efficient laptops, and the supplier lags the market leader; staying put guarantees product irrelevance.

**Steelman:** Migrating to the dominant architecture is the only viable path to competitive hardware, with a translation layer mitigating immediate software incompatibility.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Guarantees continued thermal inefficiency and performance lag, ceding the thin-laptop segment to competitors.
*   **Dual Architecture:** Increases engineering complexity and cost without solving the immediate thermal deficit of the current supplier.
*   **Wait for Supplier:** High risk; their roadmap already lags, and "cool enough" chips are not imminent.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of delivering superior personal computing hardware; distinctiveness must shift from unique silicon to superior industrial design and user experience on better silicon.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the translation layer is too slow for pro users or if early announcement halts current sales before new units ship. However, these are execution risks, not strategic flaws. The cost of delay is losing the next two years of laptop evolution.

**Evidence:** The proposal states the current supplier cannot meet thermal/efficiency needs and lags the dominant maker. This factual deficit blocks the current strategy.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated facts confirm the current path is broken.
**Risks:** Sales freeze during transition, developer friction, performance overhead in translation, brand dilution, and organizational trauma from a prior painful transition. These are managed via kill criteria, not by rejecting the move.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal addresses a fundamental supply-chain failure. The risks are real but manageable compared to the certainty of product stagnation. The translation layer is a necessary bridge, not a permanent crutch. The two-year timeline allows for phased mitigation of sales and developer concerns.

**Kill Criteria:** If translated app performance drops below 70% of native speed for key workflows by month 18, or if current model sales drop >40% post-announcement without new unit uptake, trigger a pivot to aggressive native-rewrite incentives.

**Leading Metric:** Percentage of top 100 apps running natively vs. translated by end of Year 1. Review date: 12 months.

DECISION: GO
