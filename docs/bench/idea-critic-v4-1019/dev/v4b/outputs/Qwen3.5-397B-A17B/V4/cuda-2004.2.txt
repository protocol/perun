First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a company dependent on gaming revenue to increase unit costs and divert engineering focus toward a non-existent market while fighting a fierce rival in its core business.

Steelman: Embedding general-purpose compute capabilities in every consumer chip now creates an uncontested monopoly in scientific acceleration before rivals can react, turning a commodity graphics market into a high-margin platform business.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Protect margins and win the gaming race; risk future disruption if programmable graphics become standard later.
2. **High-end only:** Add features only to expensive workstation cards; limits market reach but contains cost risk.
3. **Proposed plan:** Subsidize a new ecosystem via mass-market units; highest cost, highest strategic risk, potential total market transformation.

Fit: This contradicts the immediate survival need to win the graphics race. It assumes a future market that currently has zero willingness to pay, betting the company's core margin on a speculative "if you build it, they will come" strategy without a bridge to revenue.

Pre-mortem: The program burns cash for three years. The gaming division loses the performance war due to diverted engineering resources, causing market share collapse. The scientific tools remain unused by mainstream buyers because no software exists, and the added chip cost makes the GPUs uncompetitive on price/performance for gamers. The company runs out of cash before the scientific market matures.

Evidence: The proposal admits "no large paying market exists" and users are currently "hacking" APIs. Raising unit costs on a commodity product without immediate demand is financially fatal when core revenues are under threat.

Blockers:
1. **Negative Unit Economics:** Increasing cost on every unit for a feature with no current buyers directly undermines the primary revenue stream needed to fund any future pivot.
2. **Strategic Distraction:** Diverting engineering from a "core race" with a main rival to build a compiler and toolchain for a non-market invites immediate defeat in the only market that currently pays.

Risks: Niche demand remains niche; academic interest fails to convert to enterprise spend; competitor captures gaming market while this company chases a ghost.

The lack of a committed customer base and the direct threat to the core business make this an unacceptable bet at this stage.

DECISION: NO-GO
