First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to high-volume manufacturing and a premium price point before resolving fundamental regulatory and market-fit uncertainties.

**Steelman:** Launching a premium, niche transporter at scale now secures first-mover advantage and leverages current hype before competitors emerge.

**Attack:**
1.  **Regulatory Blocker:** The legal status on sidewalks and roads is explicitly "unclear." Deploying 40,000 units creates an immediate liability avalanche if municipalities ban them post-launch. A factory-sized commitment assumes a regulatory environment that does not yet exist.
2.  **Market Mismatch:** At $5,000, the device is inaccessible to the mass market, yet "uses beyond institutional buyers are unproven." Institutional sales alone cannot sustain a 40,000-unit factory. The "conspicuous" nature of the device further limits organic adoption.
3.  **Better Way:** A "do nothing" stance regarding mass production is superior. The alternative is a limited pilot (e.g., 500 units) to test regulatory reception and actual usage patterns without sinking capital into fixed infrastructure. A cheaper, staged approach reaches the same learning upside with negligible downside.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: Cities ban the device due to safety incidents involving untrained riders on sidewalks, leaving the company with unsellable inventory and a brand associated with legal chaos. The high price prevents early adopters from absorbing the risk of ownership.
5.  **Evidence Gap:** The proposal offers no data on regulatory clearance or confirmed purchase orders from institutions to justify the factory size. The risks listed are not mitigated; they are ignored by the scale of the ask.

**Blockers:**
*   **Regulatory Uncertainty:** Operating at scale without defined legal status is a fatal flaw.
*   **Unproven Demand vs. Fixed Cost:** Committing to 40k units/year without proven non-institutional demand or institutional volume contracts contradicts basic operational hygiene.

**Risks:** High price sensitivity, social stigma, limited utility. These become kill criteria for a pilot, but the current plan ignores them entirely.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It bets the company on assumptions that are explicitly flagged as unknown. A capped pilot is required before any factory tooling.

DECISION: NO-GO
