First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a hardware manufacturer with zero platform experience to enter a duopoly dominated by entrenched competitors with loyal user bases and iconic software, while simultaneously fighting a direct rival launching similar 3D technology.

Steelman: A CD-based console leveraging cheaper media and accessible tools can disrupt the cartridge monopoly if third-party developers flock to the platform faster than incumbents can react.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids massive sunk costs in a low-margin hardware war; preserves capital for core electronics business.
2. Partner with existing holders: Leverages their distribution and software libraries without bearing full platform risk (already failed per proposal facts).
3. License technology only: Supply CD drives to existing console makers rather than building a full system.

Fit: Entering a software-driven entertainment market contradicts the core competency of a consumer electronics manufacturer lacking first-party game studios.

Pre-mortem: The console fails because flagship games remain exclusive to incumbents, developers hesitate to bet on an unproven user base, and the company bleeds cash subsidizing hardware units it cannot sell at profit. This is a one-way door with high capital expenditure and low reversibility once manufacturing begins.

Evidence: The proposal admits no experience as a platform holder, reliance on uncommitted outside developers, and immediate competition from an incumbent's 3D launch. Hardware margins are negative, requiring volume the company cannot guarantee without hit software.

Blockers:
1. No proven route to secure essential third-party software against entrenched incumbent loyalty.
2. Direct competition from an incumbent's simultaneous 3D launch negates the "first mover" advantage in 3D graphics.
3. Negative hardware margin model requires sales volume impossible without a library of hit games, which requires sales volume (circular dependency).

Risks: Developer apathy, price wars with incumbents, inventory obsolescence, brand damage in core electronics sector.

The absence of a proprietary hit franchise or a signed exclusive developer lineup is a fatal gap. Cheaper discs do not compensate for a lack of desire among consumers or creators. The circular dependency of needing games to sell consoles and consoles to attract games cannot be broken by "easier tools" alone against established ecosystems.

DECISION: NO-GO
