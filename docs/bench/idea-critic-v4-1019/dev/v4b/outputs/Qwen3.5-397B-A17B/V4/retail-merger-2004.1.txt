First call: NO-GO. Two declining businesses with different customer bases and uncertain synergies do not create value; they compound distress.

**Steelman:** The acquisition creates immediate scale and cross-selling opportunities, using the discount chain's liquidity to stabilize the department store's assets before competitors seize them.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Strategy:** The proposal admits customers differ and sales are declining for both. Merging distinct, weakening models rarely fixes underlying obsolescence; it usually distracts management from fixing the core discount business just emerged from restructuring.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves the fragile post-restructuring stability. A "cheaper alternative" would be piloting specific brand sales in existing stores without the massive capital outlay and integration risk of a full merger.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 2006. Cause: Integration costs overwhelmed thin margins; the older stores' lease obligations and underinvestment drained cash; big-box rivals undercut the combined entity on price while offering better selection. The "synergy" of selling brands in discount stores is a low-upside tactic that does not justify a corporate merger.
4.  **Evidence:** The facts state sales are declining for *both*, stores are *old/underinvested*, and savings are *uncertain*. There is no evidence of a turnaround mechanism, only a hope that combining losses reduces them. The "different customers" point suggests cross-selling is speculative, not proven.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Logic:** Acquiring a declining asset with uncertain savings using a balance sheet just emerging from restructuring violates basic turnaround discipline. The proposal offers no proof that the combined entity generates positive cash flow superior to the status quo.
*   **Strategic Contradiction:** The plan relies on merging functions and converting stores despite admitting the customer bases differ. This risks alienating the existing discount shoppers while failing to attract the department store shoppers who are already fleeing to big-box rivals.

**Risks:** Execution distraction, cash burn from underinvested stores, brand dilution, and accelerated decline due to integration friction.

**Kill Criteria:** If the discount chain cannot demonstrate a standalone profit increase within 6 months post-restructuring, adding a second declining chain is fatal.

**Decision:** The proposal lacks a credible path to profitability and ignores the clear signal of two failing models. It bets on uncertain synergies to fix structural decline.

DECISION: NO-GO
