First call: NO-GO. The combination of a historically hostile buyer, a loss-making target without leadership, active community distrust, and a viable free alternative creates an immediate integration failure risk that outweighs strategic alignment.

**Steelman:** The acquisition secures the dominant developer platform before a competitor does, betting that operational independence and the buyer's resources can stabilize the target and monetize it faster than organic growth or a free rival can erode its market share.

**Options:**
1.  **Acquire:** High cost ($7.5B), high risk of community exodus to free alternatives, regulatory scrutiny likely. Potential upside is controlling the ecosystem.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids integration disaster and antitrust heat. Risks losing the platform to a competitor or watching it stagnate, but preserves capital.
3.  **Partner/Invest:** Lower cost, less control. Misses the "dominant" upside but tests the water without triggering immediate community backlash.

**Fit:** Assumes the buyer's pivot to open source is credible enough to overcome historical hostility. If the community perceives this as an acquisition rather than an embrace, the north star of "developer trust" is violated immediately.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, developers migrate en masse to the free competitor citing distrust, the target remains loss-making without its core user base, and regulators force a breakup or impose costly constraints. The $7.5B becomes a write-down.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the target is loss-making, leaderless, and distrusted. The existence of a "free competitor" touted by users is a verified alternative that directly threatens the acquisition's value proposition (network effects).

**Blockers:**
1.  **Value Destruction:** Paying a premium ($7.5B) for a loss-making asset with no CEO and an active exodus risk lacks a clear path to ROI. The stated facts show the core asset (community trust) is actively deteriorating.
2.  **Alternative Viability:** A free competitor is already gaining traction due to this specific distrust. Acquiring the target now accelerates the very defection it aims to prevent.

**Risks:** Regulatory blocks, cultural clash, failure to integrate independently.

**Kill Criteria:** If developer activity drops >10% within 6 months post-announcement, halt integration spending.

The absence of a permanent CEO and the active community backlash against a historically hostile buyer constitute a fundamental flaw in the timing and logic of the purchase. The plan fails as proposed because it ignores the immediate catalyst for value destruction: the community's reaction.

DECISION: NO-GO
