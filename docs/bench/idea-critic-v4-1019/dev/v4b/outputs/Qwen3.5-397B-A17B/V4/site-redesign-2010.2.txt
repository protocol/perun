First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines maximum engineering risk with maximum deployment risk while alienating the core user base that drives traffic.

Steelman: A complete rewrite with a new algorithm and forced migration is the only way to reverse traffic decline and compete with rivals by prioritizing publisher content.

Options analysis:
1. **Do nothing:** Traffic continues to slow, but community stability remains.
2. **Proposed plan:** High cost, high risk of community revolt, zero rollback capability.
3. **Better way:** A/B test the new surfacing logic on a traffic segment; iterate based on data. If successful, migrate users gradually. This preserves the community while validating the "publisher favor" hypothesis.

Fit: The proposal assumes the community values publisher content over their own voting mechanics. This contradicts the stated fact that the community values existing voting mechanics. Alienating the loyal base to chase a rival's growth strategy is a strategic mismatch.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because the core community rejects the new mechanics, perceiving the site as "corporate" or "broken." Simultaneously, the rewrite introduces critical bugs. Because the migration is "all at once," there is no technical rollback. The site loses its loyal users before gaining new ones, accelerating the traffic decline.

Evidence & Verification: The proposal offers no data proving users want publisher-favored content. It treats a hypothesis (publisher content drives growth) as a fact requiring a total rewrite. The risk of a full rewrite is acknowledged but ignored.

Blockers:
1. **No Rollback:** Moving all users at once to an unproven rewrite eliminates the ability to recover from failure. This is a fatal operational flaw.
2. **Contradictory Premise:** The plan changes the exact mechanic the community values, risking immediate churn without validated demand for the replacement.
3. **Unvalidated Scope:** A "scratch rewrite" is unjustified without prior validation that the new surfacing algorithm actually improves metrics.

Risks: Engineering delays, community exodus, rival gaining more ground during the rewrite.

Kill Criteria: Do not proceed without a segmented pilot of the new algorithm and a phased migration plan with a verified rollback path.

DECISION: NO-GO
