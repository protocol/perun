First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market late, abandons the company's own established copy-protection standard (alienating existing partners), and offers no price advantage against a dominant incumbent with high switching costs.

Steelman: A superior hardware device with wireless sharing and a unified store can disrupt the market by offering a significantly better user experience that overcomes ecosystem lock-in.

However, the attack reveals critical blockers. First, the strategic contradiction is fatal: dropping the copy-protection standard the company previously forced on partners destroys trust and invites immediate retaliation from both labels and device makers, potentially isolating the new store. Second, "wireless sharing" directly conflicts with the likely restriction from record labels, creating a product promise that may be legally impossible to fulfill at launch. Third, without a price advantage or clear exclusive content, the "large marketing budget" attempts to buy share in a category where network effects and ecosystem loyalty are the primary moats; history shows this rarely works for late entrants without a paradigm shift (which this is not, merely an iteration).

The "better way" analysis suggests that entering via software only or licensing the existing standard would be safer, but the proposal insists on a full hardware/store ecosystem drop-in. The pre-mortem is clear: the device launches, labels block the sharing feature due to DRM concerns, partners refuse to stock a player from a company that undermined their previous DRM agreement, and the marketing spend burns out against the incumbent's inertia.

The risks are not merely executional; they are structural. The proposal relies on the company reversing its own previous strategic stance (DRM promotion) without a plan to manage the fallout, while simultaneously fighting a market leader on hardware specs alone. The absence of a negotiated path for the new sharing model with labels is a blocker, not a risk, because the product's core differentiator depends on it.

Blockers: 1. Abandoning own DRM standard alienates key partners without a mitigation plan. 2. Core feature (sharing) likely blocked by labels, making the value proposition void.
Risks: High marketing spend inefficiency against entrenched loyalty; late market entry.

DECISION: NO-GO
