First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to pay a premium ($4B) for a film studio with an unproven slate and restricted assets, contradicting the goal of immediate reach expansion.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the publisher secures long-term IP ownership to fix the boy-demographic gap, betting that internal film production will eventually outperform third-party licensing despite current constraints.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Timing:** The target's most valuable assets (famous characters) are locked in long-term deals with rivals. Buying now acquires the liability of creative disputes without the immediate revenue upside of those characters, failing the "immediate reach" need.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or licensing specific boy-focused characters is cheaper and avoids the $4B cash/stock drain. The target's own film slate is "short and unproven"; paying for a studio before it proves scale is speculative.
3.  **Evidence & Math:** The $4B price assumes future film success to justify the premium. However, the proposal admits the slate is unproven and the sector is cyclical. Paying cash plus stock dilutes current shareholders for an asset that cannot yet generate its projected value due to licensing encumbrances.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** If the first few self-produced films flop (high probability given the short slate), the conglomerate is left with $4B in debt/equity dilution, angry creative partners, and no immediate boy-audience growth because the best characters are still earning money for rivals.

**Blockers:**
*   **Valuation Mismatch:** The price reflects a fully realized film studio, but the asset is a licensor with a nascent, unproven film arm and restricted top-tier IP. The numbers do not work as proposed.
*   **Strategic Delay:** The core value (boy reach via famous characters) is legally inaccessible for the duration of existing rival deals.

**Risks:** Creative control disputes, cyclical box office failure, integration friction.

**Decision:** The plan fails the basic test of buying capacity you cannot immediately use at a price predicated on that capacity working. Wait for the licensing deals to expire or acquire specific IP rights cheaper.

DECISION: NO-GO
