First call: NO-GO. A search-ad company with no hardware expertise proposing a $50M acquisition of a pre-revenue, six-person OS startup in 2005 contradicts core competency and market reality.

Steelman: Acquiring the only viable open-source mobile OS early secures the future mobile search interface before incumbents lock it down, turning a defensive moat into an ad-revenue engine.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital, wait for market clarity, partner later with established players. Cost: $0. Risk: Missing the platform shift. Reversibility: N/A.
2. Partnership/Licensing: Fund the startup without acquisition. Cost: <$10M. Risk: Low control, competitor poaching. Reversibility: High.
3. The Proposal: Full acquisition. Cost: ≥$50M + massive operational drag. Risk: Total failure of adoption. Reversibility: Low (cultural/financial sink).

Fit: Theoretically aligns with "organizing world's information," but operationally violates the "no hardware business" constraint. Entering a carrier-dominated, handset-maker-controlled market requires capabilities the acquirer lacks.

Pre-mortem: Two years post-acquisition, the OS remains a niche project. Handset makers reject it to protect existing licensing deals; carriers block it to control user experience. The $50M is sunk, the team is demoralized within a large bureaucracy, and the open-source community forks the project due to perceived corporate capture. Mobile search revenue grows via mobile-web adaptations on *other* OSs, rendering this specific asset worthless.

Evidence Check: The proposal admits "no revenue model," "no handset adoption," and "carrier resistance." These are not risks; they are current facts. The $50M price tag for six people with no product-market fit is unjustifiable by standard ROI metrics of the era.

Blockers:
1. Competency Mismatch: Acquirer has zero hardware/OS distribution experience; the market requires deep carrier/handset relationships which money alone cannot buy quickly.
2. Economic Reality: $50M for a pre-revenue concept in a hostile distribution environment fails basic capital allocation tests against core search business opportunities.

Risks: Multi-year payback, open-source governance conflicts, inability to scale a tiny team into a platform vendor.

The plan relies on a hypothetical future (mobile web dominance via open OS) that requires overcoming entrenched incumbents without a distribution mechanism. The cost of failure exceeds the strategic option value given the acquirer's profile.

DECISION: NO-GO
