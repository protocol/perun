First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a narrow demographic with a compromised product at a premium price during a volatile trend, facing immediate competition and clear channel conflict.

**Steelman:** A mid-calorie option captures young men who reject the "diet" label but want fewer carbs than regular soda, leveraging a first-mover advantage before the rival launches.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Fit & Demand:** The core assumption—that young men want *half*-sugar—is unproven. The "low-carb" craze drives consumers toward *zero* carbs, not reduced ones. If the trend is a fad (as noted), inventory becomes stranded.
2.  **Cannibalization:** The product sits in a "no-man's-land." Regular soda drinkers get less value for higher cost; diet drinkers get too many carbs. It risks stealing share from the high-margin diet segment without converting enough regular drinkers.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves current margins while the low-carb trend clarifies. A better way is to extend the existing diet brand with masculine marketing rather than fragmenting the portfolio with a new SKU.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because the rival undercuts the premium price, and the target audience views the drink as "watered down" rather than "balanced." The trend collapses in 18 months, leaving unsold stock.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Value Proposition:** The proposal admits the target market already buys diet cola, yet offers a product with *more* calories at a *higher* price. There is no logical bridge explaining why this specific demographic would switch to a worse deal on both taste and cost.
*   **Unverified Trend Longevity:** Betting a major campaign and new production line on a trend explicitly flagged as a potential "fad" without a pilot test is fiscally irresponsible.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, channel confusion, and rapid obsolescence if the low-carb diet shifts or vanishes.

**Kill Criteria:** If a 3-month pilot in a test market does not show >5% net new volume (excluding cannibalization) within 60 days, terminate immediately. However, given the fundamental logic gap, the plan should not proceed to pilot in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
