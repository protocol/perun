First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a hardware manufacturer with zero platform experience to enter a duopoly dominated by entrenched competitors with loyal user bases and first-party software dominance, while simultaneously launching against a rival's next-generation 3D system.

Steelman: A CD-based console could disrupt the market by offering significantly lower manufacturing costs and easier development tools than cartridges, attracting third-party developers ignored by incumbents focused on first-party titles.

However, the attack reveals fatal blockers. First, the "no experience" constraint is critical; platform holding requires distinct ecosystem management, developer relations, and loss-leading hardware strategies unknown to a peripheral maker. Second, the timing is catastrophic: launching a new architecture while a dominant incumbent releases a competing 3D system fragments the developer base and confuses consumers. Third, the financial model relies on sustaining hardware losses to build install base, a strategy risky for a company without an existing software revenue engine to subsidize it.

Alternatives analysis:
1. Do nothing: Avoids massive R&D sink and brand damage from likely failure.
2. Partner with existing PC manufacturers: Leverage CD tech in a non-console market where competition is less winner-take-all.
3. Acquire a struggling software studio: Build content capability before attempting hardware.

The proposal fails the "fit" test; it does not move a north star of profitability or market leadership given the stated risks. The pre-mortem is clear: the device launches with weak third-party support, loses the price war against established players, and becomes a costly footnote. The risk of being squeezed between two powerful incumbents while lacking exclusive content is not a manageable variable; it is a structural barrier to entry.

Blockers: Zero platform experience combined with immediate head-to-head competition against an incumbent's new 3D launch; no proven path to secure essential third-party software without an existing install base.
Risks: Hardware margin erosion, developer apathy, brand dilution.

This plan attempts to solve a distribution problem (cartridge cost) by creating a vastly larger execution problem (platform war) without the necessary assets (IP, experience, capital reserves for long-term losses).

DECISION: NO-GO
