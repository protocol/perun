First call: NO-GO. The price is extreme relative to revenue, the core user base hates the proposed monetization model, and the buyer has a proven inability to integrate acquisitions without destroying value.

Steelman: Acquiring the platform at $1.1B preserves its independence to capture growth, betting that ad models can be gently introduced later once scale is undeniable.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Avoids massive capital risk but cedes the youth demographic to competitors.
2. **Build organic:** Slower, cheaper, avoids integration trauma, but may miss the immediate window.
3. **Acquire (Proposal):** Highest cost, highest risk of cultural clash and value destruction given the buyer's history.

Fit: Assumes the portal can succeed where it previously failed. The strategy contradicts the verified evidence of past integration failures.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition closes, the founder leaves within six months due to cultural friction, the user base revolts against new ad formats, and the portal is forced to write down the asset. This is a one-way door; the cash is gone forever. The cost of delay is lower than the cost of a failed billion-dollar integration.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the buyer has "struggled to integrate past acquisitions." The price is "high against revenue." Users "dislike ads." These are not risks; they are stated facts that directly contradict the success criteria.

Blockers:
1. **Historical Incompetence:** The buyer's verified track record of failed integrations makes success statistically improbable without a miracle change in management capability.
2. **Economic Mismatch:** Paying a premium for an asset whose users reject the only planned revenue stream (ads) creates a negative feedback loop immediately upon monetization attempts.
3. **Key Person Risk:** The founder's potential departure removes the primary value driver of an "independent" strategy.

Risks: Adult content limiting ad inventory; cultural clash accelerating user churn.

The stated facts confirm the buyer lacks the capability to execute this specific strategy. A cheaper, smaller, staged alternative (organic growth or smaller tuck-in) reaches the same upside with significantly less downside. The absence of a pilot or cap does not save this; the fundamental premises are broken.

DECISION: NO-GO
