First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores the fundamental risk that the product's core attribute (clarity) contradicts consumer expectations of cola flavor, threatening the flagship brand without proven repeat purchase.

**Steelman:** A clear cola can capture a niche seeking "purity" and generate short-term buzz, justifying a national launch if test market positivity indicates sustainable demand rather than mere novelty.

**Attack:**
1.  **Flavor-Color Association:** Consumers strongly associate dark color with specific cola taste. Removing color may break the flavor promise, leading to immediate rejection after the first trial. The proposal admits shoppers may link flavor with color but proceeds anyway.
2.  **Novelty vs. Repeat:** Test markets often spike on novelty. The proposal explicitly flags that novelty buyers may not repeat. Launching nationwide with a "large marketing campaign" before confirming repeat rates risks massive sunk costs on a fad.
3.  **Cannibalization:** As a "major brand extension," it directly competes with the flagship. If the clear version fails to retain users, it merely shifts volume from the high-margin core to a potentially lower-retention variant, diluting overall brand equity.
4.  **Competitive Response:** A national launch signals the category to rivals. If the concept is easily copied (as noted), first-mover advantage is minimal unless the brand loyalty is absolute, which is unproven for a modified core product.
5.  **Test Market Validity:** The proposal admits test markets can overstate demand. Proceeding to national scale based solely on these figures without a mechanism to verify repeat purchase behavior is premature.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Protect flagship margin; wait for clearer data on repeat rates.
*   **Regional Rollout:** Limit exposure to specific demographics to measure repeat purchase before national spend.
*   **Sub-brand Launch:** Distinct branding to isolate failure from the flagship.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Repeat Purchase:** The proposal acknowledges the risk of non-repeat buyers but offers no data proving retention exceeds the threshold required to justify national marketing spend.
*   **Flagship Risk:** No mitigation plan exists for potential cannibalization or brand dilution if the flavor profile is rejected.

**Risks:** High waste of marketing capital; permanent damage to core brand perception; rapid competitive copycatting.

**Conclusion:** The known risks (flavor mismatch, novelty drop-off, cannibalization) outweigh the unproven upside. National launch is premature without verified repeat purchase data.

DECISION: NO-GO
