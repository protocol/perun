First call: NO-GO. The market has demonstrably collapsed, retailers refuse to stock the category, and the proposal relies on unproven assumptions that rebranding alone can overcome a total loss of consumer confidence and channel support.

Steelman: Repositioning the console as a toy robot system with a lockout chip and risk-reversal for retailers can bypass the "video game" stigma, rebuild channel trust, and ensure software quality to prevent a repeat crash.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost (robot bundle, returns), high risk (channel rejection), low reversibility (inventory commitments).
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, zero risk, preserves capital but cedes future market potential.
3. **Direct Licensing:** License the technology to an established US toy manufacturer (e.g., Coleco, Mattel). Lower cost, shared risk, leverages existing distribution.

Fit: Assumes the "entertainment system" distinction is meaningful to retailers who have just been burned by video games. This is unverified.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because retailers reject the "toy" framing as a transparent gimmick, the robot bundle is seen as a costly novelty that distracts from gameplay, and the take-back promise triggers a cash crisis when the stock inevitably returns unsold. The lockout chip creates developer hostility, resulting in a barren library.

Evidence: The proposal states retailers are clearing stock and refusing new video games. It admits the company has no track record outside Japan. The claim that a robot bundle changes this dynamic is an assumption, not a fact. The financial exposure of the take-back promise against a collapsed market is a mathematical mismatch; if the market is dead, returns will be 100%, bankrupting the venture.

Blockers:
1. **Channel Refusal:** The stated fact that retailers refuse to stock video games extends to anything perceived as one. Rebranding is an unproven countermeasure to a total channel strike.
2. **Financial Exposure:** The take-back promise in a collapsed market guarantees massive liability with no offsetting revenue, creating an immediate solvency risk.

Risks: Developer alienation due to lockout; robot novelty wearing off; home computer competition.

The plan relies on magic (rebranding) to solve a structural collapse and introduces unlimited downside via the return policy without a verified path to sales.

DECISION: NO-GO
