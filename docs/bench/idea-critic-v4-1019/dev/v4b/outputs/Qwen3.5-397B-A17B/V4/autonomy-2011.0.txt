First call: NO-GO. The price premium and accounting red flags alone make this a bet on hope, not evidence.

**Steelman:** This acquisition is the only viable path to instantly gain enterprise analytics capability and market share, justifying a premium to accelerate the CEO's software transition before competitors lock in the sector.

**Options:**
1. **Buy:** Costs $11.7B (11x revenue). Risk: Catastrophic if accounting is flawed or integration fails. Irreversible capital deployment.
2. **Do Nothing:** Cost: $0. Risk: Slower transition, but preserves capital for smaller, verifiable targets. Fully reversible.
3. **Partner/Smaller Buy:** Cost: < $1B. Risk: Slower scale. High reversibility. Beats "Buy" on risk-adjusted return given the buyer's zero software experience.

**Fit:** Moves the "software transition" north star, but the magnitude of the bet threatens the entire company's stability if the hardware core falters during integration.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes. Six months later, an audit reveals revenue recognition errors from 2009–2011. The $11.7B value drops 40%. Integration stalls because the hardware culture rejects the software team. The CEO is ousted; the company writes down the asset.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the target's financial reporting has been questioned since 2009 and the buyer's own CFO has reservations. The 64% premium is paid over a potentially inflated share price. Due diligence is rushed ("a few weeks") for a complex forensic accounting review.

**Blockers:**
1. **Financial Integrity:** The core asset value relies on financial statements explicitly flagged as questionable. Paying a 64% premium on unverified numbers is fiduciary negligence.
2. **Capability Mismatch:** A hardware company with no software experience cannot realistically integrate a massive, complex analytics firm quickly enough to justify the premium, especially under a rushed timeline.
3. **Price vs. Reality:** 11x revenue is defensible only for flawless growth; the accounting doubts negate this premise.

**Risks:** Culture clash, customer churn, technology obsolescence. These are manageable only if the price and accounting are sound, which they are not.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed unless an independent, extended forensic audit (3+ months) clears the 2009–2011 accounts and the price is reduced to reflect a "damaged goods" discount.

DECISION: NO-GO
