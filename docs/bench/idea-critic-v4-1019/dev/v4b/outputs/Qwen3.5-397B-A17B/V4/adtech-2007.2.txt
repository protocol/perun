First call: NO-GO. A $6 billion cash outlay at a large premium for a fast-moving technology, while risking alienation of existing agency customers, fails the basic operator test of capital preservation and strategic fit without verified synergies.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform is the only way to rapidly close the gap with the search leader who is securing the same supply, making the premium a necessary cost of entry to avoid permanent irrelevance in display ads.

**Options:**
1. **Do nothing:** Remain behind in display, focus on search dominance; risk long-term ad market consolidation against us.
2. **Build/Buy Smaller:** Develop internally or acquire smaller tech; slower, lower risk, avoids customer conflict, but may miss the window.
3. **Proposed Acquisition:** Immediate scale, but massive financial risk, channel conflict, and integration uncertainty in a volatile market.

**Fit:** Assumes display ads are critical to the core mission; unverified if this specific premium asset is the only path.

**Pre-mortem:** Integration fails due to culture clash; agency customers flee to competitors due to perceived conflict; the technology becomes obsolete quickly, leaving a $6B write-down. This is a one-way door with extreme delay cost if wrong, but high opportunity cost if right.

**Evidence:** No internal data provided on synergy value, customer churn modeling, or technology durability. The "large premium" and "fast-changing market" are stated facts that weigh heavily against the purchase without proof of sustained advantage.

**Blockers:**
1. **Capital Misallocation:** $6B cash is a massive concentration risk for an unproven (for the buyer) sector with no guaranteed ROI stated.
2. **Channel Conflict:** Existing agency customers are a known revenue stream; risking them for a speculative display play lacks a mitigation plan.
3. **Market Volatility:** Buying a "fast-changing" asset at a peak premium violates prudent investment hygiene without a clear moat.

**Risks:** Integration failure, technology obsolescence, regulatory scrutiny, cultural mismatch.

**Kill Criteria:** If customer churn exceeds 5% within 12 months or if the platform's market share does not grow by 10% in 18 months, divest.

The stated risks (price, conflict, volatility) are severe and untreated. The proposal asks to bet the company's cash reserves on a defensive move in a volatile market without proving the target is the only solution.

DECISION: NO-GO
