First call: NO-GO. The price is prohibitive, performance is inadequate, and internal competition exists.

**Steelman:** This machine pioneers the graphical interface for business, offering unmatched usability that justifies a premium despite low speed and scarce software.

**Options:**
1.  **Launch at $10k:** High risk of market rejection due to cost/speed; cannibalizes internal cheaper projects.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; wait for hardware costs to drop or the internal cheaper project to mature.
3.  **Pivot to Niche/Dev:** Target early adopters willing to pay for innovation, not general office workers.

**Fit:** Assuming the goal is mass office adoption, this fails. High price and low speed contradict the needs of general clerical work. It fits only if the strategy is "technology showcase," not "business volume."

**Pre-mortem:** Launched at $10k, sales flatline. Office managers reject it as too slow for spreadsheets and too expensive compared to established terminals. The internal cheaper project launches a year later, capturing the market this unit missed. The brand suffers from releasing an overpriced, underpowered device.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the price is "several times" competitors, speed is "slow," and software is "little." These are fatal for a general office launch. The existence of an internal cheaper alternative with similar features makes the $10k price point indefensible for the same value proposition.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Price/Value Mismatch:** $10,000 for a slow machine with no software is economically unviable for the target "office worker" demographic compared to existing alternatives.
2.  **Internal Cannibalization:** A cheaper, similar internal project renders this high-cost launch strategically redundant.

**Risks:** Market confusion, brand damage, sunk costs in inventory.

**Decision:** The facts provided describe a product that is too expensive, too slow, and poorly supported for its intended mass market, especially with a better internal alternative pending. Launching now guarantees failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
