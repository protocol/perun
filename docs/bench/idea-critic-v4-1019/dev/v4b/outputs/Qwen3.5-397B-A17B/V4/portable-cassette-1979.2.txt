First call: GO. The proposal targets an unserved demographic (youth) with a portable, affordable music experience, leveraging existing components to minimize R&D risk despite high initial tooling costs.

**Steelman:** A dedicated playback-only device creates a new "personal music" category for youth, trading recording utility for portability and price, rendering dealer skepticism irrelevant if consumer demand is proven through adoption.

**Alternatives:**
1.  *Do nothing:* Cede the emerging portable market to competitors; rely on declining stationary hi-fi sales.
2.  *Full-featured recorder:* Higher cost excludes the target youth demographic; bulkier form factor reduces portability.
3.  *Wait for research:* Delays entry; market research cannot validate a product category that does not yet exist.

**Fit:** Assumes the strategic goal is market expansion among younger consumers. The "antisocial" risk is actually a feature: privacy and personal immersion.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the large production run creates unsellable inventory because the price point wasn't low enough to trigger impulse buys, or if competitors release a cheaper copy before brand loyalty forms. This is a two-way door; if it fails, the brand retreats to core hi-fi, though financial loss from inventory is the penalty. Cost of delay is high: first-mover advantage in defining "personal stereo."

**Evidence:** Facts provided are internal constraints (existing parts, large run needed) and external doubts (dealers, no research). No data contradicts the core hypothesis that youth want portable music. Dealer doubt is expected when disrupting established categories.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of market research is not a blocker for a novel category; it is a condition of innovation. The risk of copying is mitigated by speed to market and brand strength. The "no recording" feature is a deliberate cost/size trade-off, not a defect.

**Risks:** Inventory overhang if sales miss targets; rapid commoditization by competitors. These are managed by the aggressive pricing strategy and first-mover positioning.

**Kill Criteria:** If sell-through to end-users is <60% of the first production run within 6 months, halt second run and discount remaining stock. Leading metric: Units sold per capita in target age demographic vs. stationary units.

DECISION: GO
