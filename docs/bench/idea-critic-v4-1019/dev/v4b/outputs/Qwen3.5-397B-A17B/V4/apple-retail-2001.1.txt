First call: NO-GO. A loss-making manufacturer with single-digit share entering a low-margin, high-fixed-cost channel during a recession contradicts basic survival logic.

**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only viable path to demonstrate the integrated music strategy and stop third-party staff from sabotaging sales, justifying the massive fixed costs.

**Attack:**
The proposal fails the "do nothing" and "cheaper alternative" tests. Opening prime mall stores creates enormous operating leverage (high leases) exactly when the company reports losses and faces a recession. This is a binary bet on immediate, massive volume to cover fixed costs, which the current market share (3-5%) does not support.

Channel conflict is a secondary symptom; the primary blocker is the capital intensity. The company lacks the cash flow cushion to sustain the "learning curve" of a new retail operation while bleeding money on hardware. A poor industry track record suggests the unit economics are structurally broken for PC makers, not just executionally difficult.

The "digital music strategy" is a marketing hook, not a retail justification. Demonstration can be achieved via lower-cost kiosks within existing partner stores, dedicated zones, or a direct-to-consumer web channel with loaner units, avoiding the multi-year lease liabilities. The proposal treats retail real estate as a strategic necessity rather than a massive financial distraction.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Financial Mismatch:** The company is currently loss-making. Adding high-fixed-cost leases creates an immediate liquidity crisis if sales do not spike instantly, which is unlikely given the recession and low market share.
2.  **Strategic Distraction:** Turning a hardware manufacturer into a property manager and retailer diverts critical management focus from fixing the core product and supply chain issues causing the losses.
3.  **Alternative Existence:** The stated goal (product demonstration) can be met via targeted, low-capital pilots or partner renovations without the existential risk of a national store rollout.

**Risks:**
Recession deepening, inventory obsolescence, reseller retaliation, brand damage from empty stores.

**Conclusion:**
This is a one-way door with a high probability of bankruptcy. The company must return to profitability and validate the music strategy through lower-risk channels before committing to physical retail expansion. The costs outweigh the potential upside given the current financial fragility.

DECISION: NO-GO
