First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a shrinking demographic (low-carb dieters) with a compromised product (half-sugar) at a premium price, while ignoring the established growth in diet cola.

Steelman: A mid-calorie option captures young men who reject artificial sweeteners but want fewer carbs than regular soda, leveraging a first-mover advantage before a rival launches.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost (large campaign), high risk (trend fatigue, price sensitivity), low reversibility (brand damage if it fails).
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, zero risk, preserves capital for core diet/regular growth.
3. **Diet Extension:** Reformulate existing diet line with natural sweeteners; lower cost, leverages existing trust, higher reversibility.

Fit: The low-carb trend shows signs of being a fad; betting a major campaign on a potentially dying trend contradicts sustainable growth. The "young men" segment is not validated as wanting *some* sugar; they may prefer zero or full flavor.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, costs double to produce, and sits on shelves. Diet drinkers stick to zero-calorie; regular drinkers find it too weak. The premium price prevents trial. The rival launches cheaper, stealing the tiny remaining market. The low-carb craze fades, leaving a obsolete SKU.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the trend "may be a fad" and that a rival is already entering. It offers no data proving young men want half-sugar specifically, only assumptions. The risk that it "satisfies neither group" is a fundamental product-market fit failure, not a manageable risk.

Blockers:
1. **Contradictory Value Proposition:** The plan admits the product may satisfy neither existing segment (diet vs. regular). A product that fails to solve a clear need for any specific group cannot succeed at a premium price.
2. **Trend Dependency:** The entire thesis rests on the low-carb trend continuing, which the proposal explicitly flags as uncertain.
3. **Price Barrier:** Premium pricing for an unproven, compromised product deters the very trial needed to validate the concept.

Risks: Brand dilution, wasted campaign spend, inventory write-offs.

The fundamental flaw is creating a solution for a problem that may not exist (wanting *some* sugar) in a market that is already served by two extremes (zero vs. full). Without evidence that "half-sugar" is a desired attribute rather than a compromise, this is a speculative bet with negative expected value.

DECISION: NO-GO
