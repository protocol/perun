First call: GO. The proposal trades short-term margin and control for immediate inventory breadth and a high-margin fee stream, which is critical for a non-profitable retailer needing to become the "everything store" without holding capital.

**Steelman:** Transform the site from a retailer into a liquid marketplace where selection drives traffic, and traffic monetizes via fees, even if internal sales dip.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Cannibalization vs. Selection:** Yes, used copies undercut new ones. But if the customer wants the cheap used book and you don't offer it, they leave entirely. Capturing the fee on a competitor's sale is better than zero revenue.
2.  **Service Quality:** Real risk. Mitigation requires strict performance metrics (shipping time, defect rate) with immediate suspension for violations, not just a fee structure.
3.  **Supplier Backlash:** Publishers may object to used sales hurting new releases. However, long-tail inventory (out-of-print, older editions) creates value suppliers cannot, reducing direct conflict.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" limits growth to capital-intensive inventory purchases. "Curated partners only" slows liquidity. The open model is the only path to rapid scale.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if trust erodes due to counterfeit goods or chronic late shipments from third parties, causing core customers to flee. The "one-way door" risk is brand damage; the "two-way door" is suspending bad actors.

**Evidence & Logic:** The math works if the take-rate (fee) exceeds the marginal cost of hosting the listing and the lost margin on displaced internal sales is less than the customer lifetime value gained from increased selection. The proposal acknowledges the risks but lacks specific kill criteria.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks are manageable operational challenges, not fundamental flaws. The strategic fit for a non-profitable retailer needing scale over margin is perfect.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, supplier disputes, quality control overhead. These become kill criteria: if customer complaint rates regarding third-party items exceed internal items by >20% for two consecutive quarters, or if top-5 suppliers delist, the program pauses.

DECISION: GO
