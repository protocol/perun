First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a scale deficit by acquiring a second declining asset, creating a larger entity with falling revenue rather than a growing competitor.

Steelman: Combining two legacy media portfolios creates immediate scale in mobile impressions, allowing the carrier to negotiate better rates and offer a unified alternative to the dominant duopoly before the market shifts entirely to closed ecosystems.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Preserve capital but remain irrelevant in mobile advertising, relying on connectivity revenue alone.
2. **Build organically:** Develop a proprietary ad stack; slower time-to-market but avoids integrating toxic, declining assets.
3. **Acquire target (Proposal):** Instant scale at $4.8B; highest integration risk and immediate revenue headwinds.
The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" because it accelerates exposure to a structurally declining ad model without a proven turnaround mechanism.

Fit: The strategy assumes "scale" equals "competitiveness." However, the dominant platforms win on data and algorithms, not just inventory volume. A carrier with no consumer media history acquiring a mismanaged portal contradicts the need for specialized operational excellence.

Pre-mortem: Twelve months post-close, integration chaos distracts the carrier's core leadership while ad revenues from both legacy units drop faster than projected due to platform dependency changes. The $4.8B price tag becomes a sunk cost anchor, preventing necessary pivots. This is a one-way door; reversing the merger would realize massive losses. The cost of delay is low; the ad market is shifting to walled gardens regardless of this combination.

Evidence: The proposal admits the target's traffic and revenue are falling, management is unstable, and the buyer lacks relevant experience. These are not risks to be managed; they are the core characteristics of the asset being purchased. No evidence suggests the carrier possesses the specific magic to reverse these trends where others failed.

Blockers:
1. **Negative Synergy Logic:** Combining two declining businesses does not create growth; it aggregates decline. The math of "falling + falling = competitive scale" is flawed without a specific, proven turnaround engine which is absent here.
2. **Capability Gap:** The buyer has zero history in consumer media; paying a premium for a complex operational turnaround they are unqualified to execute is a fundamental strategic error.

Risks: Continued ad market contraction, culture clash, inability to integrate tech stacks, overpayment for a melting ice cube.

DECISION: NO-GO
