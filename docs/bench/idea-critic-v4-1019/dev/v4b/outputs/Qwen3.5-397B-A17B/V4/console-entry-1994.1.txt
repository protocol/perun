First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a hardware manufacturer with zero platform experience to enter a duopoly dominated by incumbents with loyal users and first-party software, while simultaneously fighting a direct 3D competitor and accepting negative hardware margins.

Steelman: A CD-based console could succeed by leveraging cheaper media and superior developer tools to attract third-party support, bypassing the cartridge cost barrier.

However, the attack reveals fatal blockers under the stated facts. First, the "no experience" constraint is critical; platform holding requires ecosystem management, not just manufacturing. Second, the market is locked by two incumbents with famous in-house series; without a comparable first-party hit, courting outside developers is insufficient, as developers follow users, not just cheap tools. Third, an incumbent is launching a competing 3D console simultaneously, neutralizing the technical advantage. Finally, selling hardware at a loss requires massive capital reserves to subsidize adoption until software sales recoup costs; the proposal acknowledges this risk but offers no evidence of the financial capacity to sustain it against established rivals.

The "cheaper disc manufacturing" advantage is a commodity benefit, easily matched by incumbents once the format shifts, and does not overcome the switching costs for consumers loyal to existing franchises. The pre-mortem is clear: the device launches, fails to secure exclusive hits, developers see low attach rates, and the company bleeds money on every unit sold without gaining market share.

Blockers:
1. Zero platform experience combined with immediate competition from an incumbent's 3D launch.
2. Inability to compete with incumbents' first-party franchises to drive hardware adoption.
3. Negative margin model requires unproven financial endurance against deep-pocketed rivals.

Risks:
Developer apathy, consumer confusion, rapid obsolescence if the format war is lost.

This plan fails as proposed because it relies on external developers to solve a first-party content problem while ignoring the capital intensity required to survive a price war with incumbents.

DECISION: NO-GO
