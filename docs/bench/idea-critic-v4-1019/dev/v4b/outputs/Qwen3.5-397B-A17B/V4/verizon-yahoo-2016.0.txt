First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a scale deficit by acquiring a second declining asset, creating a larger entity with falling revenue rather than a growing competitor.

Steelman: Combining two legacy media portfolios creates immediate scale in mobile impressions, allowing the carrier to negotiate better rates and offer a unified alternative to the dominant duopoly before the market shifts entirely to closed ecosystems.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoids integration risk but cedes the mobile ad market to the two dominant platforms; the carrier remains a dumb pipe.
2. Build organically: Too slow; the window to establish a third ecosystem is closing as mobile usage consolidates.
3. Acquire target: Immediate scale but inherits structural decline and cultural mismatch; highest cost, highest integration risk.

Fit: Assumes the carrier's North Star is becoming a media competitor rather than a connectivity provider. This contradicts the carrier's lack of consumer media history and the target's repeated management failures.

Pre-mortem: The integration fails because the carrier's telecom culture clashes with media creativity, accelerating talent flight. The "scale" achieved is in depreciating assets (desktop display) rather than growing mobile video. The combined entity misses revenue targets within 12 months, forcing a write-down. This is a one-way door; once integrated, separating the assets is costly and disruptive. The cost of delay is losing the chance to be #3, but the cost of error is billions in stranded capital.

Evidence: The proposal admits the target's traffic and revenue are falling for years and management is unstable. The carrier has no relevant track record. There is no evidence that combining two negative growth trajectories yields positive synergy; mathematically, it often yields accelerated decline due to distraction.

Blockers:
1. The core claim relies on "scale" to compete, but the scale being purchased is in a shrinking category with no proven path to reversal.
2. The buyer has zero capability in the target domain, and the target has proven unable to self-correct despite multiple management changes.

Risks: Cultural collision, accelerated revenue decline, inability to monetize mobile effectively.

The plan fails the basic test of buying growth; it buys decline hoping for a miracle turnaround by an inexperienced owner.

DECISION: NO-GO
