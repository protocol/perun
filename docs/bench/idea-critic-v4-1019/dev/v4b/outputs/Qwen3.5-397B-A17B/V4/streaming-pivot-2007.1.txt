First call: GO. The proposal secures the company's survival against an inevitable market shift to digital, even at the cost of short-term DVD profitability.

**Steelman:** Launching a free, limited streaming service now retains subscribers who will eventually demand digital access, preventing them from defecting to future competitors before the company can build its own capability.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Retain DVD margins temporarily but guarantee obsolescence as broadband adoption grows and competitors capture the digital mindset.
2.  **Paid standalone streaming:** Fails because the catalog is too weak to justify a new fee; bundling leverages existing loyalty.
3.  **Wait for better tech/contracts:** Cedes the customer relationship and data advantage to incoming giants (tech/retail) who will not wait.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of "home entertainment delivery" rather than the temporary mechanism of "physical discs." It treats the DVD business as a cash cow to fund the transition, not the end state.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if licensing costs bleed cash faster than DVD profits decline, or if poor video quality brands the service as inferior. However, the cost of delay is total market irrelevance; waiting for perfect conditions ensures a competitor defines the standard.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The facts state the company is currently *profitable* with 7M subscribers. This cash flow funds the experiment. The small catalog and poor quality are constraints, but not blockers, because the offer is *free* for existing users, lowering expectation thresholds. Cannibalization is a feature, not a bug: it is better to cannibalize your own revenue than let a competitor do it. No factual blocker exists; the risks (cost, tech, competition) are inherent to the industry shift, not specific flaws in this defensive maneuver.

**Decision:** The plan beats "do nothing" because it preserves the customer relationship during a paradigm shift. The lack of a pilot is acceptable given the "free add-on" structure limits downside exposure while maximizing learning.

Blockers: NONE.
Risks: High licensing burn rate, brand dilution from poor quality (mitigated by free tier).
Kill Criteria: Halt if streaming operational costs exceed 15% of DVD operating profit within 12 months.

DECISION: GO
