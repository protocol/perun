First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the segment has shrunk for years, faces prohibitive safety costs, and lacks a sustainable buyer base beyond a one-time enthusiast pulse, all while the company has no heritage to command a premium or absorb development risk.

**Steelman:** A lightweight, affordable roadster can revive a dead niche by targeting underserved enthusiasts with a simple, fun car that mass-market players ignore due to perceived low volume.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Reality:** The premise admits the segment is "dead" and "shrinking." Entering a contracting market with a niche product (two seats) contradicts basic volume requirements for a mid-size maker.
2.  **Cost vs. Price:** "Affordable" conflicts directly with "safety rules make convertibles costly." Without economies of scale or luxury margins, the unit economics likely fail.
3.  **Demand Cliff:** The proposal explicitly predicts sales will fade after early adopters. A platform investment requires a multi-year lifecycle, not a short spike.
4.  **Brand Mismatch:** Lacking sports-car heritage means the car must compete purely on price/performance against established rivals, which is impossible given the cost constraints.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Avoids sinking capital into a predicted decline.
*   *Collaborate:* Badge-engineer an existing sports car to test waters without full R&D.
*   *Limited Run:* A true low-volume special project, not a mainstream launch.

**Pre-mortem:** The car launches to critical acclaim from journalists but sells 40% of forecast. Safety compliance eats margins. Production ends in two years, leaving stranded customers and a write-down.

**Blockers:**
*   The proposal's own evidence states the segment is shrinking and demand will fade quickly.
*   "Affordable" pricing is mathematically incompatible with high safety compliance costs for low-volume convertibles.
*   No path to sustained volume beyond the initial enthusiast wave.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, regulatory changes, supply chain inefficiency for low-volume parts.

**Conclusion:** The plan relies on reversing a long-term market decline with a product that admits it cannot sustain sales volume. The internal contradictions between "affordable," "safe," and "low-volume niche" create an unsolvable business case.

DECISION: NO-GO
