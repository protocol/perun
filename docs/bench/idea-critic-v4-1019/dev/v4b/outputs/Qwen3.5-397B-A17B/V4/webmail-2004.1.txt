First call: GO. The proposal leverages a core competency (search) to solve a verified pain point (storage limits) with a defensible monetization model, despite apparent cost risks.

**Steelman:** Offering 1,000x the standard storage transforms email from a filing cabinet requiring maintenance into a permanent, searchable archive, creating a unique value proposition that justifies ad-supported funding and drives viral adoption via invitations.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the strategic high ground to incumbents; remain a search-only company with limited user engagement data.
2.  **Match status quo (few MB):** Non-starter; indistinguishable from competitors, no viral hook.
3.  **Proposal (1GB):** High initial cost per user, but creates a "sticky" ecosystem where users store data indefinitely, increasing ad inventory surface area and switching costs.

**Fit:** Aligns perfectly with organizing the world's information. Email is the ultimate unstructured dataset; indexing it improves core search algorithms while locking users into the ecosystem.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if privacy backlash over content scanning becomes regulatory or reputational, or if storage costs decline slower than user growth, burning cash before ad revenue scales. However, this is a two-way door; caps can be adjusted or features paused if unit economics fail. The cost of delay is high: whoever owns the inbox owns the user's primary digital identity.

**Evidence & Math:** Storage costs are falling (Moore's Law applied to disks). Even at 2004 prices, the lifetime value of a user with full-text search capability and high engagement exceeds the marginal cost of 1GB. The "free" model removes adoption friction, while invitation-only creates artificial scarcity and demand.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (cost, privacy, maturity) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The market is mature *because* incumbents refuse to innovate on storage; this is the disruption vector.
**Risks:** Privacy perception and initial cash burn. These are managed via transparent terms and capped rollout.

**Kill Criteria:** If cost per active user exceeds ad revenue yield by >30% after 12 months, or if legal injunctions halt content scanning, pivot to paid tiers or reduce quotas. Leading metric: Daily Active Users storing >500MB.

DECISION: GO
