First call: NO-GO. The plan commits irreversible capital before validating the core hypothesis that unit economics can work in a single location.

**Steelman:** Rapid nationwide warehouse construction creates an unassailable logistics moat, securing market share before competitors can react to the 1999 online grocery opportunity.

**Attack:**
1.  **Evidence & Math:** The proposal admits "no operating track record" and "unknown unit economics." In a low-margin business, scaling unproven operations multiplies losses, not just revenue. A $1 billion commitment across a dozen cities based on zero validated data is speculation, not strategy.
2.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" alternative regarding expansion (focusing solely on the first city) costs nothing in new capital and provides the data needed to build a valid model. A "better way" is a single pilot warehouse to prove the 30-minute slot is profitable before replicating it.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume it is 2001 and the company is bankrupt. Why? Cash burned on empty warehouses in cities where customer density never reached the break-even point required to support thin margins. The 30-minute promise drove up costs while adoption lagged.
4.  **Door Type:** This is a one-way door. Once $1 billion is spent on specialized automation in the wrong locations, it cannot be recovered or easily repurposed.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fatal Flaw:** The plan explicitly rejects learning the unit economics of the first market before scaling. Without knowing if a single order is profitable, scaling guarantees catastrophic capital destruction.
*   **Contradiction:** You cannot manage "capital intensity" and "thin margins" by increasing fixed costs before proving variable profit.

**Risks:** (These are secondary to the blocker) Customer adoption rates, density thresholds, operational complexity of 30-minute windows.

**Decision Logic:** A sound operator never scales a low-margin, capital-intensive model without a proven positive unit contribution in at least one cohort. The absence of a pilot is not a gap to fill later; it is a current state that makes the $1 billion bet mathematically indefensible. The proposal fails the basic test of verifying the engine works before building the highway.

DECISION: NO-GO
