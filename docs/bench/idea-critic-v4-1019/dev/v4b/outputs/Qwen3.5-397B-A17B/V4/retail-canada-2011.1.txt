First call: NO-GO. The plan commits to a massive, irreversible capital expenditure (130–150 stores) before proving the core hypothesis: that the retailer's domestic value proposition translates to a foreign market with different competitors, regulations, and supply chain realities.

Steelman: Rapid saturation creates immediate scale to deter the entrenched competitor and amortize new supply chain costs quickly.

Attack:
1.  **Capability Gap:** The proposal admits the supply chain and inventory systems are "new" and "not yet proven." Deploying unproven operational infrastructure to support 150 simultaneous openings is a classic failure mode. If the system breaks, the entire network starves simultaneously.
2.  **Market Fit:** "Higher prices on some goods" and a "larger, entrenched competitor" suggest the price advantage is not absolute. Without a pilot, there is no evidence the loyal domestic following or value perception exists across the border.
3.  **Reversibility:** Leasing 150 sites creates a massive fixed-cost burden. If the concept fails, exiting hundreds of leases in a foreign legal jurisdiction is slow and costly. This is a one-way door executed at full speed.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "do nothing" or "pilot first" approach (e.g., 5–10 stores) allows the team to learn cross-border logistics and validate price perception with limited downside. The current plan rejects learning in favor of betting the company.

Blockers:
*   **Unproven Critical Path:** The core enabler (supply chain/inventory system) is explicitly admitted as unproven while being tasked with supporting maximum load immediately.
*   **No Validation:** Zero evidence exists that the specific assortment/pricing works in the target market against the specific incumbent.

Risks:
*   Regulatory delays on bilingual labeling halting openings while leases burn cash.
*   Cultural mismatch in product selection leading to inventory bloat.

The plan confuses speed with strategy. Scaling a broken or unvalidated model 150 times faster only accelerates failure. The absence of a pilot phase to de-risk the supply chain and market fit is a fatal flaw in an otherwise aggressive growth story.

DECISION: NO-GO
