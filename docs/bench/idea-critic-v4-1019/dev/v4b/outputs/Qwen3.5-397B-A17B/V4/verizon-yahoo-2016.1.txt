First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a scale deficit by acquiring a second declining asset, compounding execution risk without addressing the core lack of carrier expertise in consumer media.

**Steelman:** Combining two distressed media portfolios creates immediate scale to negotiate better rates and cross-sell to the carrier's mobile base, theoretically catching up to dominant platforms before the market shifts further.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Capability:** The acquirer is a wireless carrier with zero history in consumer media. Buying a second failing portal does not create talent or product sense; it merely adds integration complexity to an unfamiliar domain.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A cheaper alternative is partnering with existing successful platforms or building niche mobile-first products internally, rather than inheriting legacy desktop debt.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 24 months. Cause: The combined entity continues to lose traffic because the underlying products are obsolete, while management attention is consumed by integrating two distinct legacy cultures and tech stacks. The carrier's core business suffers distraction.
4.  **Evidence:** The target's traffic and revenue are explicitly falling for years. The acquirer already owns one such failing company from the previous year. Adding a second negative trend does not create a positive one; it doubles the integration burden.
5.  **Blocker:** The proposal relies on the assumption that scale alone solves the problem, yet the stated facts show the assets are structurally declining and the buyer lacks the specific operational DNA to reverse them. There is no mechanism described to stop the revenue bleed or replace the repeated management failures.

**Risks:** Continued ad-revenue decline, culture clash, massive integration costs, distraction from core telecom business.

**Kill Criteria:** If the combined mobile ad revenue does not grow within 12 months, or if integration costs exceed 20% of the purchase price, the project must be wound down.

**Verdict:** This is a classic "throwing good money after bad" scenario. The buyer is attempting to buy relevance in a sector where they have already failed once, using an asset that is objectively deteriorating. The lack of relevant experience makes the integration risk fatal.

DECISION: NO-GO
