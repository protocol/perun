First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer with no developer platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors already oppose heavy capital spending.

Steelman: Leveraging spiky internal infrastructure to create a new revenue stream could theoretically improve asset utilization and diversify income beyond thin-margin retail.

However, the claim fails the blocker test. The proposal admits the market for hourly server rental is "unproven" and the company lacks the specific operational expertise to run a developer platform at scale. Unlike a core retail expansion, this requires a completely different sales channel, support model, and reliability SLA that conflicts with the existing cost-focused culture.

The "spiky infrastructure" argument is weak; internal burst capacity rarely maps cleanly to external, predictable commercial SLAs without massive over-provisioning, directly exacerbating the investor concerns about capital spending. The risk of distraction is critical: failing at reliability in a new B2B line could damage the brand trust required for the core retail business.

Alternatives like optimizing internal costs or partnering with existing vendors offer safer paths to efficiency without the existential risk of building a competitor to established hardware giants from a standing start. The absence of a pilot, a defined target customer who has asked for this, or a path to profitability that addresses the "unproven" market status constitutes a fatal gap. The proposal relies on hope rather than evidence that the company can execute a complex platform business it has never attempted.

Blockers: No evidence of market demand (admitted "unproven"); no internal capability (admitted "no one has run..."); direct conflict with investor sentiment on capex; high probability of core business distraction.
Risks: Margin dilution, brand damage from reliability failures, capital waste.

DECISION: NO-GO
