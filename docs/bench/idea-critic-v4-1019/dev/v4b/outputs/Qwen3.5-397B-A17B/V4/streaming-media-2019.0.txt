First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades certain, high-margin licensing revenue for speculative subscriber growth while intentionally burning cash to build an unproven technology platform, creating a fatal mismatch between immediate financial reality and distant upside.

Steelman: Aggressively withdrawing content to force migration to a proprietary, underpriced streaming service is the only way to secure long-term direct-to-consumer relevance, accepting short-term losses as an investment in owning the customer relationship.

However, the claim fails the blocker test on three verified facts within the proposal itself:
1.  **Revenue Contradiction:** The company currently earns "billions" from licensing. Removing this creates an immediate, massive hole in cash flow required to fund the "multi-year operating loss." The proposal offers no bridge for this gap other than "burn," which is unsustainable without the very revenue being cut.
2.  **Capability Gap:** The firm has "never run" a technology platform of this scale. Building a stable global streaming service while simultaneously engaging in a price war against an incumbent with 100M+ subscribers is an execution risk that transcends "risk" and becomes a probable operational failure.
3.  **Cannibalization without Offset:** The plan admits to cannibalizing cable and theatrical revenue. With a subscription price "below the market leader," the unit economics require near-perfect retention and massive scale to match the margin of the licensing deals being terminated. The five-year timeline to reach "tens of millions" is too slow to offset the immediate loss of billions in licensing fees.

The "do nothing" or "hybrid" alternative (licensing while building a premium, non-loss-leading niche service) offers a safer path to digital transformation without incinerating the current cash engine. A cheaper, staged approach exists: license content to rivals for maximum cash extraction *while* building the tech stack, then pull content only when the platform is proven and the balance sheet can absorb the shock.

Blockers:
- Immediate loss of billions in licensing revenue contradicts the ability to fund multi-year losses.
- Zero prior experience running a global tech platform creates a high probability of service failure during critical growth.
- Price undercutting combined with revenue cannibalization yields negative unit economics that cannot be closed within the stated timeline.

Risks:
- Incumbent retaliation (price wars, exclusive deals).
- Failure to reach subscriber targets due to content fragmentation.

DECISION: NO-GO
