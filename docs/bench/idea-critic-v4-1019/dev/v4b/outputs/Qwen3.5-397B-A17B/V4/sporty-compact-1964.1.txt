First call: GO. The demographic tailwind of postwar baby boomers entering the market creates a verified, time-sensitive opportunity that outweighs the unproven demand risk.

**Steelman:** Leverage existing low-cost mechanicals to deliver high-perceived value sporty styling to a massive, underserved youth demographic, using customization to mask economy origins and advertising to define the category.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Demand Risk:** While volume demand is unproven, the "do nothing" alternative cedes this emerging segment to competitors. The cost of delay is losing the defining generation of buyers. A smaller pilot is inferior because the product's viability depends on cultural momentum generated only by a massive launch.
2.  **Cheap Perception:** Sharing parts risks a "cheap" label. However, the "long hood, short deck" proportion and two-door body style are visual differentiators that psychologically separate it from the utilitarian compact. The extensive options list allows buyers to self-select premium features, further distancing the final product from the base economy model.
3.  **Spending Risk:** Heavy ad spend before sales validation is dangerous. Yet, for a lifestyle product targeting a specific cultural moment, visibility *is* the product. Without the campaign, the car remains just another compact. The spend is the primary mechanism to prove the demand hypothesis.

**Blockers:** None. The proposal's risks are inherent to the opportunity, not fatal flaws. The mechanical sharing is a feature (low price), not a bug, provided the styling successfully masks it.

**Risks:** If styling fails to convince buyers of its sportiness, the parts sharing will indeed look cheap. If the ad campaign misses the cultural tone, the spend is wasted.

**Kill Criteria:** Halt production expansion if first-year sales do not reach 100,000 units. Monitor the ratio of options-added revenue per unit as a leading metric for brand perception; if buyers stick strictly to the base model, the "premium sporty" positioning has failed.

The plan beats "do nothing" by capturing a generational shift. The risks are manageable through the proposed styling and marketing levers.

DECISION: GO
