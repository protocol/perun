First call: NO-GO. The proposal sacrifices the company's core differentiator (business email) on an untested OS while matching a competitor's price for an inferior hardware size and ecosystem.

**Steelman:** A 7-inch device targets a portable, media-focused niche distinct from the 10-inch productivity market, using the new OS to establish a long-term ecosystem independent of the phone, with email added later via updates or pairing.

**Attack:**
1.  **Contradictory Positioning:** The company leads in business email, yet the device ships without native email, calendar, or contacts unless paired. This alienates the standalone tablet user and undermines the "business" brand promise.
2.  **Price/Value Mismatch:** Matching the rival's price for a smaller screen, no native apps, and a tiny app store offers negative value. Customers will buy the larger, established rival tablet for the same money.
3.  **Untested Foundation:** Basing a new hardware category on an untested OS introduces massive execution risk. If the OS stutters or lacks APIs, the hardware fails regardless of size.
4.  **Developer Cold Start:** Developers prioritize the rival's large install base. Without a killer app or unique utility (which "no email" negates), the app gap will widen, not close.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Protect the brand reputation; wait until the new OS has traction or native apps are ready.
*   *Differentiate:* Price significantly lower to compensate for the smaller screen and app gap, targeting a budget segment rather than competing head-on.
*   *Bundle:* Make the phone pairing mandatory and market it strictly as a "companion device," not a standalone tablet, adjusting price and expectations accordingly.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, reviewers praise the build but pan the lack of apps and native email. Sales flatline because business users stick to laptops/rivals, and consumers buy the rival's larger tablet. The new OS gains no traction, and the project is cancelled within a year, wasting the acquisition cost.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Value Deficit:** Same price as a superior competitor (larger screen, established apps) with fewer features (no native email).
2.  **Brand Erosion:** Launching a "business" device without core business tools contradicts the company's market leadership claim.

Risks (app adoption, OS stability) are secondary to the fundamental flaw in the value proposition.

DECISION: NO-GO
