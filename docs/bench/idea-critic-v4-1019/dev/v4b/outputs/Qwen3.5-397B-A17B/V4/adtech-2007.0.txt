First call: NO-GO. A $6 billion cash outlay at a large premium for a fast-moving technology, while alienating existing customers, fails basic capital discipline without proof of durable advantage.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform is the only way to rapidly close the gap with the search leader who is already securing similar assets, making speed more valuable than perfect fit.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financials:** $6 billion cash is massive for 2007. Paying a "large premium" for a company in a "fast-changing" market creates immediate impairment risk if technology shifts.
2.  **Channel Conflict:** The buyer is a software company; the target is an agency. Buying a competitor to your own customers (agencies) destroys trust and revenue in the core business. You cannot serve both the platform and the agencies using it without inherent conflict.
3.  **Strategic Reactivity:** The move is framed as a response to a competitor's deal. Chasing a rival into a complex, low-margin services business (agency) to solve a technology gap is a classic diversification trap.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves cash and customer trust. Building or licensing ad-serving tech internally avoids the agency conflict and likely costs far less than $6B plus integration chaos.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-acquisition, the technology is obsolete due to market shifts, major agency clients leave for neutral vendors, and the $6B write-down cripples R&D for the core search product.

**Blockers:**
*   **Channel Conflict:** The proposal admits conflicts with existing agency customers but offers no mitigation. Alienating the distribution base to buy a tool is fatal.
*   **Valuation vs. Volatility:** A large premium on cash for "fast-changing" tech lacks a safety margin. The math only works if the market freezes, which the proposal explicitly says it won't.
*   **Strategic Fit:** Entering the agency business contradicts the software model.

**Risks:** Integration failure, culture clash, regulatory scrutiny, technology obsolescence.

**Kill Criteria:** If the combined entity loses >5% of existing agency partners within 6 months, or if the ad-serving market share does not grow by 10% in 12 months, divest immediately.

The conflict with existing customers and the excessive price for volatile tech make this a value-destroying reaction, not a strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
