First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve three hard problems simultaneously (new country, new supply chain, new IT system) at maximum speed, creating a single point of failure across the entire venture.

**Steelman:** Rapidly securing 130+ sites creates an unassailable footprint before the entrenched competitor can react, leveraging existing leases to bypass construction delays.

**Attack:**
1.  **Coupled Risk:** Launching a new inventory system *and* a cross-border supply chain *while* opening ~70 stores/year creates a "perfect storm." If the unproven IT system fails to handle bilingual labeling or cross-border logic, every new store opens empty or non-compliant. There is no learning loop; the first error scales to 130 locations immediately.
2.  **Competitive Reality:** The market is dominated by a larger, entrenched rival with higher prices. While price gaps suggest opportunity, the incumbent knows the local regulatory and logistics landscape. A slow, complex rollout gives them time to lower prices selectively or lobby for regulatory friction against the newcomer's novel supply chain.
3.  **Operational Overload:** Learning cross-border logistics and regulation while simultaneously debugging a new inventory system exceeds reasonable organizational bandwidth. The proposal assumes flawless execution in areas where the retailer has zero prior experience.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital and domestic focus; avoid catastrophic foreign failure.
*   **Staged Pilot:** Open 5–10 stores using manual or interim logistics processes to validate the supply chain and IT system before signing 130 leases. This isolates the technology risk from the real estate commitment.
*   **Acquisition:** Buy a small local chain to inherit compliant systems and local knowledge, rather than building from scratch.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months in, the new inventory system cannot process bilingual labels correctly, causing customs delays. Simultaneously, the entrenched competitor drops prices on key goods. With 100 stores opening simultaneously into this bottleneck, the retailer faces massive stockouts, regulatory fines, and brand damage, forcing a total retreat.

**Blockers:** The plan couples unproven technology and logistics with an irreversible, high-speed real estate commitment. There is no mechanism to learn or adapt before full exposure. The risk is not just failure, but catastrophic, company-threatening loss.

**Risks:** Price perception misalignment, regulatory hurdles, supply chain breakdown.

DECISION: NO-GO
