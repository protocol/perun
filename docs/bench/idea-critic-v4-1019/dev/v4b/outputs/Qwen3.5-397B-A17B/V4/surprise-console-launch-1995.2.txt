First call: NO-GO. Launching early with high price, scarce software, and alienated retailers contradicts basic market entry logic for a hardware platform dependent on ecosystem support.

**Steelman:** An early, limited launch secures mindshare and tests the channel before the competitor's September arrival, accepting short-term friction for long-term positioning.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** At $399 vs. the rival's $299, the console is 33% more expensive. In 1995, price sensitivity is extreme; without a killer app, this is a non-starter for mass adoption.
2.  **Supply Chain:** Shipping to "a few retailers" guarantees retaliation from excluded major chains (e.g., Electronics Boutique, Walmart). Once burned, these gatekeepers may refuse future stock, killing distribution before the real launch.
3.  **Software:** Developers explicitly did not plan for May. A console without games is a brick. Hard-to-program 3D hardware exacerbates this; rushing yields broken demos, damaging the brand's technical reputation permanently.
4.  **Strategic Fit:** The goal is to compete with the September rival. Arriving four months early with an inferior product lineup and hostile retail relationships hands the competitor a narrative of "failed hype" to exploit in their own launch.

**Pre-mortem:** By December 1995, the system is labeled a "premium failure." Retailers prioritize the cheaper, better-supported rival. Developers, burned by the rushed May deadline, deprioritize the platform. The early launch didn't steal thunder; it detonated the own goal.

**Blockers:**
*   **Retailer Hostility:** Excluding major partners creates an irreversible distribution blockade.
*   **Software Void:** Releasing without developer alignment ensures a barren library at launch.
*   **Price Disadvantage:** $100 premium with no content justification guarantees low sell-through.

**Risks:** Brand damage, developer exodus, inventory glut.

**Conclusion:** The proposal sacrifices the entire holiday season and retail relationships for a hollow headline. The "early" advantage is nullified by the lack of product-market fit (games/price). This is not a pilot; it is a self-sabotage event.

DECISION: NO-GO
