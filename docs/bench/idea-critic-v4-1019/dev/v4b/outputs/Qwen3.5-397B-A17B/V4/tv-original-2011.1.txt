First call: GO. The data-backed hypothesis that a full-season drop of prestige content drives subscriptions outweighs the pilot-less risk for a challenger brand needing differentiation.

**Steelman:** Leveraging proprietary viewing data to bypass the pilot system and commission a high-quality, full-season original exclusively for online release will create a must-have asset that accelerates subscriber growth and validates the streaming model faster than incremental licensing.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Continue rising licensing costs with no unique IP; eventual margin collapse or service irrelevance.
*   **Pilot First:** Delays launch, signals lack of confidence to top talent, and fails to test the "binge" consumption hypothesis.
*   **Cheaper Originals:** Low-budget attempts likely fail to attract the film star/director needed to legitimize the platform.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of becoming a primary destination, not just a content aggregator. The "all-at-once" release is a strategic differentiator, not just a distribution choice.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the data misinterpreted niche interest as mass appeal, or if the "all-at-once" model cannibalizes long-term engagement without driving enough new subs to cover the massive upfront cost. However, the cost of delay allows competitors to lock up talent and market share.

**Evidence Check:** The proposal relies on internal viewing data (assumed accurate). The attachment of a film star and director mitigates the "no pilot" quality risk. The 2011 context means no direct competitor has proven this model, making first-mover advantage critical.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks (audience uncertainty, cost) are inherent to any major content bet, not specific blockers invalidating the approach. The lack of a pilot is a feature of the strategy, not a flaw, provided the data supports the creative choice.

**Risks:** High financial exposure if the show flops; potential brand damage if quality suffers without pilot testing. These are managed by the kill criteria.

**Kill Criteria:** If Season 1 does not drive a net new subscriber increase of >5% within 90 days of release, halt Season 2 production and pivot to lower-cost originals. Leading metric: New subscriber acquisition cost (CAC) vs. Lifetime Value (LTV) of cohorts exposed to the show.

DECISION: GO
