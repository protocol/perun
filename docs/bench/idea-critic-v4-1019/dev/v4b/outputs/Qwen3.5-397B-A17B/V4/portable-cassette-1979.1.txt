First call: GO. The proposal targets an unmet need for portable, affordable personal audio using existing technology, despite internal skepticism.

**Steelman:** A low-cost, portable cassette player focused exclusively on playback via headphones creates a new "personal music" category for youth, leveraging existing components to achieve a price point competitors cannot match without redesigning their recorder-centric models.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain status quo; cede the emerging portable market to future entrants.
2.  **Full-featured portable:** Add recording/speaker; higher cost, bulkier, misses the "personal" angle, likely too expensive for the target demographic.
3.  **Proposed approach:** High risk due to lack of research, but offers first-mover advantage in a new niche with lower unit costs.

**Fit:** Aligns with a vision of democratizing music consumption, moving from stationary hi-fi to personal mobility.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if dealers refuse to stock it due to "no recording" bias, or if youth reject headphones as isolating. However, the "antisocial" argument may actually be the product's core appeal (privacy/immersion). The large production run is a financial risk if demand is misjudged, but necessary for the price strategy.

**Evidence:** No market research exists (stated). Competitor copying is possible but delayed by their commitment to recorder functionality. The "existing parts" claim reduces R&D risk.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of market research is a *risk*, not a blocker; the proposal's logic (price + portability + existing tech) creates its own market hypothesis. Dealer skepticism is a sales challenge, not a product flaw.

**Risks:** Misreading the "antisocial" aspect; inventory overhang from large initial run; fast follower competition.

**Kill Criteria:** If first-month sell-through to youth is <50% of forecast, halt second run. If dealer return rate exceeds 15% due to "no recording" complaints within 90 days, re-evaluate marketing or features. Leading metric: Units sold to customers under 25 in first quarter.

The objections (no recording, antisocial, no research) are precisely the assumptions that, if proven wrong by the market, define the opportunity. The plan is sound enough to test with a capped, time-boxed launch.

DECISION: GO
