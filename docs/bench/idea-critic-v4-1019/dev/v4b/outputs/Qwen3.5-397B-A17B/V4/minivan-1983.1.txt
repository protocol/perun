First call: GO. The proposal leverages existing assets to solve an immediate survival need with a product that fits a clear, unmet consumer gap (garage-friendly family transport).

**Steelman:** Reusing the compact-car platform and a converted factory minimizes capital expenditure while creating a unique "car-like" van category that rivals cannot immediately copy due to the specific platform conversion and timing advantage.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** High risk of continued decline; no new revenue stream.
*   **New Platform:** Impossible given the "little cash" constraint.
*   **Commercial Van Perception:** Mitigated by marketing the vehicle strictly as a family wagon alternative, leveraging the "drives like a car" attribute.
*   **Space Constraints:** Acceptable trade-off for the garage-fit requirement; the target demographic (station wagon buyers) prioritizes maneuverability over maximum cargo volume.
*   **Rival Copying:** First-mover advantage in a non-existent category creates a defensive moat; copying takes time the company needs to stabilize.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the brand cannot shake its commercial image or if the compact platform proves too weak for family loads. However, the government loan guarantee provides the runway to iterate. The cost of delay is catastrophic given the recent near-collapse.

**Evidence & Fit:** The facts state families currently buy station wagons or full-size vans, leaving a void for a mid-size option. The proposal directly addresses this using available resources (existing platform, converted factory). The risks cited are speculative market reactions, not factual blockers like lack of technology or insurmountable debt.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of cash argues *for* this low-capital approach, not against it. The non-existence of the category is the opportunity, not a risk.

**Risks:** Market education costs, potential perception issues. These are managed via positioning and the inherent uniqueness of the product.

**Decision:** The plan turns constraints (low cash, existing platform) into strategic advantages (low cost, unique size). It addresses the immediate need for a viable product without requiring unavailable capital.

DECISION: GO
