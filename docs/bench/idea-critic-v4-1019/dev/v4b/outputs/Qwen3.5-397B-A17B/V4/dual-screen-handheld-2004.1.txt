First call: GO. The proposal avoids a direct hardware war against a superior graphical opponent by pivoting to a distinct value proposition: dual-screen interaction and touch input at a lower price point.

Steelman: By leveraging unique form-factor innovation rather than raw power, the company can unlock new gameplay mechanics that attract non-core users while retaining legacy support via the older model, creating a differentiated market segment the rival cannot immediately copy.

Options analysis: Matching the rival's graphics requires a costly arms race with no guarantee of overtaking their lead. Doing nothing cedes the high-end market without a defense. The dual-screen approach beats "do nothing" by offering a unique selling price and feature set that redefines the competition rather than participating in it.

Fit: This aligns with a strategy of broadening the audience beyond core players. It assumes the market values novel interaction over fidelity, a reasonable bet given the price differential and the untapped potential of touch interfaces.

Pre-mortem: Failure would occur if developers ignore the second screen, producing only split-view ports that confuse users, or if the touch interface proves too resistive or inaccurate for mainstream appeal. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The cost of delay is high; waiting allows the rival to solidify their graphical dominance as the industry standard.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the stated fact that the rival dominates on graphics/media. The counter-strategy relies on the unproven but plausible premise that unique interaction models can drive hardware sales independently of graphical fidelity.

Blockers: NONE. No stated fact contradicts the core claim that a lower-price, feature-differentiated device can coexist with a high-end rival. The risks cited (developer confusion, unproven touch) are manageable execution hurdles, not show-stoppers.

Risks: Developer adoption friction and consumer perception of the device as a "gimmick." These are mitigated by keeping the older handheld on sale to serve the core base while the new device courts the mass market.

Kill criteria: If developer kits do not yield at least three flagship titles demonstrating unique dual-screen mechanics within 12 months, or if launch sales fail to exceed the installed base of the legacy handheld by 20% in the first quarter, the strategy must pivot.

DECISION: GO
