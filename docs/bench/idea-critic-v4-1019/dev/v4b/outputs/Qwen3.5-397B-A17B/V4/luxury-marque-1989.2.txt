First call: GO. The proposal targets an unmet need for luxury quality at a disruptive price, leveraging existing engineering competence while isolating brand risk through a separate identity.

Steelman: A dedicated luxury brand with a distinct dealer network and a flagship sedan engineered to match European quality but priced lower can successfully penetrate the luxury segment by offering superior value without diluting the parent brand's economy reputation.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Remain confined to the mid-price segment, ceding luxury margins to Europeans forever.
2. Badge engineering: Sell a slightly upgraded version of an existing car under the main brand. Risk: Brand dilution and failure to convince luxury buyers; low upside.
3. The Proposal: High upfront cost and execution risk, but offers a clear path to a new, high-margin market segment with brand protection. It beats "do nothing" by creating a new revenue stream and beats badge engineering by offering a credible luxury proposition.

Fit: Assumes the strategic goal is market expansion and margin growth. A sound idea that moves the north star of profitability and market share.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the separate dealer network fails to attract traffic due to lack of brand heritage, or if the single flagship model cannot sustain showroom interest. This is a two-way door; the brand can be paused or the network scaled back if metrics fail. The cost of delay is losing the first-mover advantage in the "value luxury" niche to competitors.

Evidence: The proposal states development costs are already sunk. The core claim rests on the ability to engineer quality matching Europeans at a lower price point, which is plausible given the parent company's reputation for reliability and economies of scale.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (brand perception, dealer cost, prestige, thin lineup) are valid but do not constitute factual blockers. They are execution challenges mitigated by the separate brand strategy and the value proposition. The "thin line-up" is a standard launch strategy for new luxury marques.

Risks: Brand acceptance, dealer network profitability, competitive response. These become kill criteria: monitor sales volume vs. targets and dealer profitability in the first 18 months.

DECISION: GO
