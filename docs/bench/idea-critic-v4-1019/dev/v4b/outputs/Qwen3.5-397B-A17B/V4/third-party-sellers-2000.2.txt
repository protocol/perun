First call: GO. The proposal trades short-term margin pressure for immediate inventory expansion and fee revenue without capital expenditure, a critical move for an unprofitable retailer needing scale.

**Steelman:** Transforming the site from a single retailer into a hybrid marketplace creates a "long tail" inventory instantly, turning competitors into revenue sources via fees while increasing page utility for customers, outweighing the risk of internal cannibalization.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain control but cap growth at own inventory limits; likely path to continued unprofitability.
2.  **Exclusive partnerships:** Lower risk but slower scale and higher negotiation costs.
3.  **Proposal (Open Marketplace):** Highest immediate risk to price control and brand trust, but offers the only path to rapid scale and fixed-cost revenue (fees) that decouples profit from inventory ownership.
The proposal beats "do nothing" because the current trajectory is unsustainable; it beats exclusives on speed and breadth.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of becoming the "everything store" and achieving profitability through scale and high-margin fee income rather than low-margin retail arbitrage.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if bad seller experiences drive customers away permanently or if suppliers boycott the platform. However, these are manageable via strict performance metrics and banning non-compliant sellers. The door is two-way: sellers can be removed if trust erodes. Cost of delay is high: every month without expanded selection is a month further from profitability.

**Evidence:** The logic relies on the assumption that customers value selection and price over the guarantee of a single seller, and that fee income offsets lost retail margin. While specific numbers aren't provided, the economic model of taking a cut of infinite inventory is superior to owning finite inventory for an unprofitable entity.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (undercutting, cannibalization, trust) are inherent to the model but do not invalidate the core thesis; they are operational hurdles, not fatal flaws. Cannibalization is acceptable if the fee on the used sale exceeds the lost margin on the new sale, or if the new sale would not have happened otherwise.

**Risks:** Supplier retaliation and quality control. These become kill criteria: monitor supplier churn and customer complaint rates regarding third-party items.

DECISION: GO
