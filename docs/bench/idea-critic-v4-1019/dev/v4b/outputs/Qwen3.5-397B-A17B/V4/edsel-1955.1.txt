First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a massive, irreversible infrastructure build (new dealer network) and a fixed 1957 launch date despite an explicit, unmitigated risk of recession and a potentially shrinking segment.

**Steelman:** Launching a distinct mid-priced brand with dedicated dealers captures the growing "step-up" market before competitors, leveraging extensive research to ensure product-market fit.

**Attack:**
1.  **Macro Mismatch:** The plan relies on a "growing" segment but admits a recession risk. In a downturn, the mid-priced sector is historically the first to collapse as buyers trade down to economy cars or delay purchases. Launching a premium-adjacent brand into a contraction is capital suicide.
2.  **Infrastructure Rigidity:** Building a new dealer network is a high-fixed-cost, one-way door. If the segment shrinks or the recession hits, these assets become immediate liabilities with no salvage value, unlike shared infrastructure which can be scaled back.
3.  **Timing Fallacy:** The 1957 debut is driven by marketing momentum ("teaser campaign"), not economic indicators. A rigid launch date ignores the possibility that delaying entry until the economic outlook clears would save hundreds of millions in sunk costs.
4.  **Cannibalization:** The risk of overlap with existing lines is acknowledged but not solved. In a tight market, internal competition dilutes sales further without gaining net share.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; wait for recession clarity.
*   **Shared Network:** Launch the brand using existing dealerships to test demand without the infrastructure bet.
*   **Delay:** Push launch to 1958/59 based on economic data, not marketing calendars.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal admits a recession risk and segment shrinkage possibility.
*   **Fact:** The plan mandates a new dealer network (high fixed cost) and a fixed 1957 launch.
*   **Conflict:** Committing to high fixed costs and a rigid timeline while acknowledging a high probability of market contraction makes the financial model untenable. The plan fails to offer a conditional launch or a flexible infrastructure strategy.

**Risks:** Market timing error, brand cannibalization, stranded dealer assets.

**Conclusion:** The proposal demands an irreversible, high-cost commitment in the face of a known, material threat (recession) without a contingency plan. It bets on marketing momentum over economic reality.

DECISION: NO-GO
