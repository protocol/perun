First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a paid subscription for a feature competitors offer free, while the primary user base lacks the required broadband infrastructure.

Steelman: A unified, high-quality online ecosystem creates a defensible moat and recurring revenue, distinguishing the console through superior reliability and social features that fragmented, free alternatives cannot match.

However, the execution plan fails the blocker test on three fronts derived directly from the stated facts:
1.  **Market Readiness:** The proposal "requiring broadband" contradicts the fact that "few homes have broadband." Mandating hardware/connectivity the customer does not possess shrinks the addressable market to near zero at launch.
2.  **Pricing Mismatch:** Charging an annual fee when "players expect online play to be free" and rivals charge nothing creates immediate friction. Without a proven willingness to pay, this pricing model blocks adoption before it starts.
3.  **Operational Void:** The company "has never run" hosting or support operations. Launching a service dependent on these unproven capabilities introduces a high probability of catastrophic failure (downtime, poor support) that would damage the core console brand.

While the vision of a unified service is strategically sound, the specific proposal to make it **mandatory, paid, and broadband-only** in 2002 is unsupported by the current environment. The risks are not merely "execution difficulty"; they are fundamental mismatches between the offer and the market's current state (connectivity, price expectation) and the company's current state (operations).

A viable path would require a phased approach: optional adoption, freemium tiers, or dial-up support, none of which are in this proposal. As written, the plan commits the company to high fixed costs for a service the majority of users cannot access and do not want to pay for.

Blockers: Mandatory broadband in a low-penetration market; paid model against free expectations and zero operational history.
Risks: Brand damage from service failure; low attach rate.

DECISION: NO-GO
