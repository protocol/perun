First call: NO-GO. A 60% price hike followed immediately by a forced brand split signals punishment, not strategy, and invites massive churn.

**Steelman:** Separating a declining legacy unit (DVD) from a growth unit (Streaming) allows distinct management focus and prevents the legacy cost structure from dragging down the growth valuation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing & Trust:** Executing a structural split two months after a 60% price increase looks like a bait-and-switch. Customers perceive they are paying more for less (the unified brand value). The "confusion" risk is understated; this is perceived hostility.
2.  **Value Destruction:** Siloing data (ratings/queues) destroys the primary competitive advantage: a unified understanding of user taste across physical and digital media. This reduces engagement for both services.
3.  **Friction:** Forcing existing users to manage two accounts, two passwords, and two bills creates immediate friction, prompting cancellation of the higher-friction component (likely the DVD side, but potentially both out of spite).
4.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "gradual transition" options were not adequately weighed against the shock of immediate separation. A softer decoupling (unbundled billing without brand schism) could achieve financial clarity without the reputational nuclear option.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, subscriber churn spikes 20% above projections. Social sentiment turns toxic, framing the company as greedy. The separate DVD brand fails to attract new users due to stigma, while streaming growth stalls due to the backlash. The stock price halves.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal ignores the compounded negative sentiment from the recent 60% price hike. The market has not absorbed the first shock; adding a second structural shock is fatal.
*   **Fact:** Destroying cross-service data synergy removes a unique moat for no immediate operational gain that couldn't be achieved internally without customer-facing separation.

**Risks:** High probability of coordinated customer boycott; loss of cross-selling data; brand fragmentation.

**Kill Criteria:** If customer support ticket volume regarding "billing confusion" or "cancellation" exceeds 2x the baseline within 48 hours of announcement, halt the split and revert to unified billing immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
