First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $36 billion to acquire a network explicitly described as incompatible and limited, requiring the buyer to run legacy equipment while absorbing massive goodwill risk without a stated plan to resolve the technical clash.

**Steelman:** The merger is justified solely by acquiring the target's high-value business customers who rely on a specific push-to-talk service, assuming their retention outweighs the integration nightmare of merging two incompatible networks.

**Attack:**
1.  **Technical Incompatibility:** The core asset (push-to-talk) runs on technology incompatible with the buyer's network. Running parallel legacy networks indefinitely destroys the synergies required to justify a $36 billion price tag. There is no stated path to migrate these users to the buyer's standard without losing the very service they pay for.
2.  **Financial Risk:** The "large goodwill" implies paying a premium over tangible assets. If the integration fails or customers churn due to service degradation during the complex migration of incompatible tech, this goodwill becomes an immediate write-down.
3.  **Missing Alternative:** The proposal ignores the "do nothing" or "partner" option. Could the buyer license the push-to-talk technology or form a roaming agreement to serve these business customers without assuming the liability of an incompatible network and $36 billion in debt?

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-merger, the integration stalls. Business customers flee because the push-to-talk service is degraded on the legacy network, and the cost to rebuild the feature on the buyer's network exceeds projections. The company is left with bloated debt, stranded legacy assets, and a massive accounting charge for failed goodwill.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The networks are incompatible and the target's tech is "limited for data."
*   **Fact:** The deal requires running legacy equipment.
*   **Missing:** No technical migration plan is offered to unify these incompatible systems, yet the valuation assumes a unified future entity. Buying a broken, incompatible half of a whole for $36 billion is a failure of arithmetic.

**Risks:** Customer churn during migration, inflation of integration costs, regulatory scrutiny on market consolidation.

**Kill Criteria:** If a third-party audit cannot certify a migration path for the push-to-talk users to the buyer's network within 6 months at a cost under $2 billion, the deal terminates.

DECISION: NO-GO
