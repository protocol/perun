First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a software ecosystem deficit with a hardware acquisition in a declining business, a mismatch that historically destroys value.

Steelman: Acquiring the handset division secures the primary distribution channel for the operating system and prevents its defection to the rival platform, buying time to cultivate the app ecosystem.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Risk the manufacturer switching to the rival, causing immediate OS irrelevance.
2. **Acquire (Proposed):** Gain control but inherit a bleeding asset with falling sales and high integration costs.
3. **Strategic Partnership:** Pay the manufacturer to stay exclusive without owning the liability of their hardware division. This offers similar defensive value at lower cost and risk.

Fit: While vertical integration aligns with controlling the user experience, it does not address the core "north star" deficiency: the lack of applications. Hardware ownership cannot force developers to build apps if the user base is shrinking.

Pre-mortem: Two years post-acquisition, the company is burdened with massive write-downs from unsold inventory and an inflated workforce. The app gap remains because developers follow active users, not corporate ownership structures. Competitors react by lowering prices, squeezing margins further. This is a one-way door with a high cost of delay; waiting allows the market to clarify whether the OS can survive independently.

Evidence & Blockers: The proposal admits smartphone sales are falling and the rival holds the app advantage. The core claim—that hardware ownership fixes an app-ecosystem problem—contradicts the stated fact that developers flock to the market leader. Owning a shrinking pipeline does not solve the lack of water (apps). The €5.4 billion price tag for a declining asset represents a definitive financial blocker; the ROI logic fails because the asset's value is tied to an OS that is already losing the platform war.

Blockers:
1. **Strategic Mismatch:** Hardware acquisition cannot resolve a software ecosystem deficit when sales are falling.
2. **Financial Liability:** Buying a declining business at a premium diverts capital from the actual problem (developer incentives).

Risks: Integration friction, competitor retaliation, continued developer apathy.

DECISION: NO-GO
