First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores the fundamental contradiction between "cola flavor" and "clear appearance," risking brand confusion and flagship cannibalization without a proven retention strategy.

**Steelman:** A clear cola can successfully carve a niche as a "purer" alternative, leveraging novelty to capture health-conscious users without eroding the core brand, provided the marketing campaign is massive enough to define the category before rivals react.

**Attack:**
1.  **Flavor-Color Expectation:** Consumers strongly associate dark color with specific cola taste profiles. A clear liquid signals "citrus" or "water," creating a sensory mismatch that leads to immediate rejection after the first trial.
2.  **Novelty Trap:** Test markets measure initial curiosity, not repeat purchase. If the product is perceived as a gimmick rather than a superior taste experience, sales will collapse post-launch once the novelty fades.
3.  **Cannibalization:** Without distinct differentiation beyond color, the new product will likely steal share from the flagship rather than expanding the total market, diluting marketing spend across two similar SKUs.
4.  **Competitive Response:** A "large marketing campaign" invites immediate copycats. If the underlying product advantage is weak (just color), rivals can launch me-too products quickly, fragmenting the niche before profitability is reached.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Protect flagship margins; avoid brand dilution.
*   *Limited Regional Rollout:* Test repeat purchase rates and flavor acceptance over 6 months before national spend.
*   *Sub-Brand Launch:* Launch under a different name to isolate failure risk and prevent flagship association.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because consumers buy once, dislike the taste expectation mismatch, and never return. The flagship loses 5% volume to the new SKU. Rivals launch clear versions within 3 months, turning the category into a low-margin commodity.

**Blockers:**
*   No evidence provided that "clear" delivers a *better* taste, only a different visual.
*   No data on repeat purchase rates in test markets (only "positive response" which often means trial).
*   High risk of flagship cannibalization without a mitigation plan.

**Risks:** Novelty fade, flavor confusion, competitive copying, marketing inefficiency.

**Kill Criteria:** If repeat purchase rate in test markets is <20% after 90 days, or if flagship sales drop >2% in test regions, halt national launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
