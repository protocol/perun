First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets price-sensitive teenagers with a high-cost mandatory data plan and no app ecosystem, directly contradicting market realities where rivals dominate via app catalogs.

Steelman: Leveraging a popular messaging brand to lock in teens via a carrier-exclusive, OS-controlled device could secure high-margin recurring revenue if network effects outweigh app absence.

Options analysis:
1. **Do nothing:** Preserve capital; avoid entering a saturated market with a disadvantaged product.
2. **Current Plan:** High risk. Teens reject high data costs; lack of an app store is a fatal utility gap compared to rivals. Sunk costs in a custom OS are a trap, not an asset.
3. **Alternative:** Port the messaging strength to existing platforms (iOS/Android) or launch a low-cost device on the company's established mobile platform with immediate app support. This reaches the same upside (user acquisition) with lower friction and cost.

Fit: The strategy ignores the "north star" of teen adoption, which relies on peer apps and affordability, not carrier exclusivity.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because teens cannot afford the data plan and refuse to abandon apps for a "walled garden" with zero third-party support. The carrier exclusivity limits reach just as social proof is needed most. This is a one-way door with massive inventory write-down risk.

Evidence & Calculation: The proposal admits rival smartphones have large app catalogs while this launch has none. The data plan cost equals a full smartphone plan, pricing out the target demographic. Two years of sunk cost on a custom OS creates an incentive to persist with a flawed product rather than pivot to the company's own proven mobile platform.

Blockers:
1. **Economic Mismatch:** Mandatory high-cost data plan exceeds the budget of the target teenage demographic.
2. **Utility Gap:** No application store at launch renders the device non-competitive against rivals with large catalogs, a fact admitted in the proposal.
3. **Strategic Error:** Using a new in-house OS instead of the company's existing mobile platform increases risk and development time without offering compensatory value to the user.

Risks: Carrier dependency, brand dilution if the device fails, and total loss of sunk development costs if the OS is abandoned.

The plan fails on basic unit economics and product-market fit for the stated audience. The sunk cost fallacy is driving the OS choice.

DECISION: NO-GO
