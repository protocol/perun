First call: GO. The proposal trades short-term control for long-term platform dominance, directly addressing the CEO's prior skepticism by proving native apps offer value web apps cannot.

Steelman: A curated, revenue-sharing native app store creates a defensible ecosystem that enhances device value while mitigating security risks through centralized review, turning potential chaos into a premium feature.

Options:
1. **Proposed Store:** High initial operational cost (review team), 30% revenue capture, creates a "walled garden" moat. Reversibility is low once developers commit.
2. **Web Apps Only (Status Quo):** Zero review cost, no revenue share, but cedes high-performance use cases to rivals. Irreversible loss of developer mindshare.
3. **Open Sideloading:** Maximizes developer freedom, maximizes security/stability risk, alienates carriers. Likely leads to fragmentation and support nightmares.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of creating a superior, integrated user experience that rivals cannot match via open but fragmented distribution.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the review process becomes a bottleneck, stifling innovation, or if the 30% cut drives key developers to rival platforms. However, the "polish" concern is mitigated by the very review process proposed. The cost of delay is catastrophic; waiting allows rivals to lock in users and developers permanently.

Evidence: The existence of rival ecosystems proves demand. The CEO's previous stance is the primary internal barrier, but the market reality (rivals succeeding) invalidates the "web apps are sufficient" hypothesis for high-end use cases.

Blockers: NONE. The concerns (security, carrier objection, workload) are operational hurdles inherent to the strategy, not facts proving the plan fails. They are managed by the proposed review process and revenue model.
Risks: Review backlog, developer revolt over fees, carrier pushback. These are managed risks, not blockers.

Kill Criteria: If within 12 months, the store lacks 5,000 quality apps or developer satisfaction drops below 70%, pivot to a hybrid web-native model. Leading metric: Number of approved apps and daily active usage of third-party apps.

DECISION: GO
