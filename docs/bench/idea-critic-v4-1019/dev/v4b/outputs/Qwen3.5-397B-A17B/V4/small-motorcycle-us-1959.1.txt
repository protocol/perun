First call: GO. The proposal targets an unserved niche (commuters/students) with a product fitting that need, bypassing direct competition with established "large machine" manufacturers.

**Steelman:** Entering the US market via non-traditional channels with a small, accessible vehicle creates a new category rather than fighting for share in the existing heavy motorcycle segment.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Fit:** The US market for large bikes is small and saturated; the "small bike" segment is undefined but potentially vast among non-riders. This aligns with expanding the total addressable market.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the potential future small-vehicle market to competitors. Exporting only large models risks mechanical failure (as noted in risks) and brand damage. The proposed path is the only one leveraging the company's core strength (50cc reliability).
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the "rough image" of motorcycling taints the small bike or if highway attempts destroy the engines.
    *   *Mitigation:* Selling through sporting-goods stores dissociates the product from the "biker" culture. Strictly marketing for city use prevents highway abuse.
4.  **Evidence/Constraints:** The risk regarding foreign currency is real but constitutes an investment cost, not a structural flaw. The risk of large models failing is a reason *not* to push large bikes, reinforcing the strategy to push small ones.
5.  **Blockers vs. Risks:**
    *   *Blockers:* None. The facts do not prove Americans *won't* buy small bikes; they only state current preference for large ones. Preferences shift when new options appear.
    *   *Risks:* Brand unknown (solved by niche positioning), currency cost (investment decision), mechanical limits of large bikes (avoided by not selling them initially).

**Decision Logic:** The proposal correctly identifies that competing head-on with large bikes is a losing strategy. By changing the vehicle type, the target audience, and the distribution channel, it circumvents the stated barriers. The risks are manageable execution details, not fundamental reasons to abort. The potential to create a mass market outweighs the currency cost.

DECISION: GO
