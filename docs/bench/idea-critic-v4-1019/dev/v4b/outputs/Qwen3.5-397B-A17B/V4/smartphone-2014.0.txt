First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines a premium price with a fragmented ecosystem and a gimmicky differentiator in a saturated two-horse race, a recipe for immediate irrelevance.

**Steelman:** A niche device leveraging unique 3D hardware and shopping integration to capture high-value customers despite app limitations.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economic Mismatch:** Charging flagship prices for a device lacking flagship apps (social, messaging, utility) violates basic consumer utility theory. Users pay for the ecosystem, not just hardware.
2.  **Differentiation Failure:** "3D-like display" via head tracking is a novelty, not a utility. History shows such features (e.g., parallax barriers) fail to drive mass adoption compared to battery life, camera quality, or app availability.
3.  **Distribution Bottleneck:** Exclusive carrier availability at an unsubsidized premium price severely limits reach. In 2014, carrier subsidies drove volume; ignoring this while lacking apps is suicidal.
4.  **Opportunity Cost:** The "one-touch shopping" feature does not require a dedicated phone; it can be a software layer on existing dominant platforms, reaching 100% of the market instead of a tiny fraction.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to poor reviews citing the app gap. Early adopters return it due to battery drain from constant camera-based head tracking. The carrier, seeing zero activation traction, refuses marketing support. Inventory piles up, written off as a total loss within 12 months.

**Blocker:** The core value proposition (shopping) is software-native and platform-agnostic, yet the proposal forces a hardware-heavy, platform-restricted solution at a premium price point. The math does not work: Premium Price + Limited Apps + Limited Distribution = Zero Volume.

**Risks:** High R&D sunk cost, brand damage from a failed "me-too" attempt, and distraction from the successful e-reader/tablet lines.

**Kill Criteria:** If the projected attach rate of the shopping feature does not exceed 50% of daily active users within 30 days of a limited pilot, the hardware program must terminate immediately. However, given the structural flaws, no pilot should proceed.

DECISION: NO-GO
