First call: NO-GO. The price implies a valuation with no revenue, no business model, and extreme execution risk, violating basic capital discipline.

**Steelman:** Buying the only credible mobile threat neutralizes a strategic existential risk and acquires a dominant user base instantly, justifying a premium for speed over build.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financials:** $1B for ~12 employees and zero revenue is indefensible by standard metrics. There is no path to ROI shown.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows organic competition; building a better mobile product is slower but cheaper. Copying features costs fractions of this sum.
3.  **Fit:** While it solves the mobile gap, it relies on the assumption that the threat cannot be survived organically.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Integration fails, culture dies, users flee to the next competitor, and regulators block the deal due to monopoly concerns. The $1B is written off.
5.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no revenue" and "no business model." The valuation is purely speculative based on "threat" status, not cash flow.
6.  **Blocker Test:**
    *   *Blocker:* The core claim (strategic necessity) rests on an unproven assumption that the threat is fatal if not bought. However, the *financial* reality is a hard blocker: paying $1B for an asset with no monetization mechanism and high integration risk violates the duty of capital preservation. No alternative reaches the same upside, but the cost of failure (total loss) outweighs the speculative benefit of removing a competitor that hasn't yet monetized.
    *   *Risk:* Regulatory block, culture clash, platform dependency.

**Decision:** The absence of a business model and the astronomical multiple create a factual blocker: the numbers do not work as proposed. A capped pilot or earn-out based on future revenue would be logical; a flat $1B cash/stock buy now is reckless.

**Kill Criteria:** None applicable as the deal is rejected. If pursued, a metric would be user retention post-integration, but the entry price is the failure point.

DECISION: NO-GO
