First call: GO. The strategic logic of owning the customer relationship and capturing full lifetime value outweighs the near-term loss of licensing revenue, provided the content library is sufficiently deep to drive acquisition.

**Steelman:** Leveraging a unique, irreplaceable content moat to disrupt an incumbent by subsidizing user acquisition, accepting short-term P&L degradation to secure long-term recurring revenue and data ownership.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary failure mode is underestimating the "churn wall." Low prices attract deal-seekers, not loyalists. If the content pipeline gaps, subscribers leave before profitability. The "do nothing" option preserves cash flow but cedes direct-to-consumer relevance, eventually reducing the library's licensing value as rivals build their own. A "hybrid" model (licensing some, keeping some) dilutes the exclusive value proposition required to move the needle.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a "platform owner" north star, shifting from B2B wholesale to B2C retail. Pre-mortem: The tech platform crashes during launch spikes, or content production delays create dry spells, causing mass churn. The cost of delay is high; waiting allows competitors to lock in users and produce similar genre content.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal relies on the assumption that the library is "must-have." Without file access to verify catalog depth or engagement metrics, we must accept the premise that the conglomerate's IP is uniquely valuable. The stated risks (cash burn, tech execution) are real but manageable with hiring and capital; they are not factual blockers proving the model fails. The loss of licensing revenue is a known cost of goods sold for the new strategy, not a contradiction.

**Decision:**
No factual blocker exists in the text. The risks are executional, not structural. The alternative (licensing forever) leads to commodity status. The plan requires a capped pilot for tech stability before global marketing spend, but the strategic direction is sound.

**Kill Criteria:**
Stop if subscriber growth falls below 40% of target trajectory by Month 18, or if churn exceeds 5% monthly after the first year.

DECISION: GO
