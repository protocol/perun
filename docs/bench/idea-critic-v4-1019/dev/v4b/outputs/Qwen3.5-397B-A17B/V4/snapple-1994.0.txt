First call: NO-GO. The plan to force a cult brand into a mass-market distribution and advertising model ignores the specific assets that create its value: quirky independence and loyal, niche distributors.

Steelman: The acquisition only works if the brand's growth is driven by untapped mass-market potential that the current independent distributors cannot reach, and if its "cult" status is a temporary phase rather than its permanent ceiling.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Avoids destroying brand equity but cedes growth to competitors.
2. **Hybrid approach:** Acquire but maintain separate distribution and limited, targeted marketing to preserve exclusivity while scaling slowly.
3. **Proposed plan:** Full integration. Highest risk of alienating the core base; if the "cult" image breaks, the premium pricing power collapses, leaving a generic tea in a crowded market against soft-drink giants.

Fit: The strategy assumes the buyer's mass-market playbook is universally applicable. This contradicts the premise that the target's value lies in its *difference* from mass-market norms. Applying a sports drink model to a premium tea ignores category nuances.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-acquisition, independent distributors defect to competitors due to contract changes. Mass-market advertising dilutes the brand's exclusive image, causing core loyalists to abandon it. Sales flatten as the brand becomes just another option on the shelf, unable to compete on price with Coke or Pepsi's tea lines. The $1.7 billion purchase price is never recovered.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits risks regarding distributor loyalty and image survival but offers no mitigation other than "doing what worked for the sports drink." This is an assumption, not evidence. No data suggests this specific consumer base responds well to mass marketing.

Blockers:
- **Strategic Contradiction:** The plan explicitly targets the brand's unique differentiators (independent distribution, cult image) for removal. This invalidates the core reason for the brand's premium valuation.
- **Alternative Superiority:** A hybrid approach offers growth potential with significantly lower risk of brand destruction. The proposed "all-in" mass-market shift is not required to capture value.

Risks: Competitor saturation, price sensitivity, integration failure.

The plan fails the "do no harm" test for the asset being purchased. It attempts to fix what isn't broken while breaking what is valuable.

DECISION: NO-GO
