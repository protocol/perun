First call: GO. The proposal solves the core friction of early e-reading (need for a PC) and addresses publisher piracy fears via a closed, cellular-connected system, creating a unique value proposition despite hardware inexperience.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled device with a paper-like screen removes the need for a computer, directly addressing the primary adoption barrier of 2007, while a unified low-price store secures publisher buy-in against piracy.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Cedes the emerging digital market to future competitors; print sales will eventually plateau.
2.  **Software-Only App:** Fails in 2007 due to lack of ubiquitous smartphones and persistent piracy concerns from publishers.
3.  **Partner with Hardware Maker:** Dilutes the integrated user experience and store control essential for the "one-click" model.

**Fit:** Aligns with the vision of becoming the primary destination for all book consumption, moving beyond physical logistics.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if hardware defects plague the launch or if publishers refuse the low-price model, starving the catalog. However, the cellular connection is a decisive differentiator that earlier failed devices lacked.

**Evidence:** The proposal correctly identifies that previous failures stemmed from requiring a PC connection and lacking content integration. The "paper-like" screen addresses eye-strain objections common with LCDs of the era.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (inexperience, margin squeeze, cannibalization) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. Hardware inexperience is mitigated by contracting manufacturing; margin squeeze is a strategic choice to drive ecosystem lock-in; cannibalization is preferable to losing customers to competitors.

**Risks:** High upfront capital expenditure; potential supply chain delays; publisher resistance to pricing. These are managed by treating the device as a loss leader to drive high-margin content sales.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders do not cover 20% of initial tooling costs within 30 days of announcement, or if fewer than 3 major publishers sign on by the beta phase, pivot to a software-only strategy or delay launch.

**Leading Metric:** Attach rate (books purchased per device) within the first 90 days. Review date: 6 months post-launch.

DECISION: GO
