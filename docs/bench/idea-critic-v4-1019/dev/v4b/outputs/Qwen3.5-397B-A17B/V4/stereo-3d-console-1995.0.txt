First call: NO-GO. The proposal describes a device with severe usability flaws (discomfort, eye strain), a crippled visual experience (red monochrome only), and a weak value proposition (neither portable nor a true home console) at a non-trivial price point, all while cannibalizing attention from a superior home console arriving within two years.

Steelman: This device creates a unique "social tabletop" 3D niche that leverages existing IP to bridge the hardware gap, offering an affordable entry into stereoscopic gaming before full home 3D is viable.

Attack: The steelman collapses under the stated facts. A "tabletop" device requiring an eyepiece contradicts the social nature of tabletop gaming; users cannot share the experience easily. The red monochrome display severely limits game design and appeal compared to contemporary color handhelds. Most critically, the physical discomfort (eye strain, headaches) is a fundamental blocker for a consumer entertainment product; if the core mechanism causes pain, the product fails regardless of price or library size. The "bridge" strategy is also flawed: launching a compromised product just two years before a true next-gen home console risks brand damage and consumer confusion rather than building momentum.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve brand reputation and wait for the true next-gen home console in two years. Cost: $0. Risk: Gap in lineup, but no active harm.
2. Focus on software for existing handhelds: Leverage the current installed base with new titles. Cost: Low. Risk: Lower hardware revenue, but safer.
3. Accelerate the home console: If the gap is critical, pull forward the true successor. Cost: High. Risk: Execution pressure, but delivers a superior product.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, reviewers pan the discomfort and visuals, sales stall at 10% of forecast, and the company is forced to slash prices within six months, tarnishing the brand before the real next-gen launch.

Blockers:
- Fundamental usability failure: Stated risk of eye strain and headaches makes the core interaction painful.
- Contradictory form factor: "Tabletop" + "eyepiece" prevents the social sharing inherent to tabletop play.
- Cannibalization/Confusion: Launching a stopgap 2 years before a true successor dilutes the roadmap.

Risks: Limited game library, consumer rejection of monochrome 3D.

DECISION: NO-GO
