First call: NO-GO. The $6 billion cash outlay for a fast-moving technology stack, absent verified synergy math or customer retention guarantees, represents an unjustifiable concentration of risk against a reactive motive.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform instantly grants the necessary infrastructure to challenge the search leader's display dominance, preventing permanent market exclusion.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Motive:** The proposal is reactive ("competitor just agreed") rather than driven by a verified north star. Chasing a rival's move without proven unit economics often leads to overpayment for assets whose value is eroding due to the very "fast-changing" nature cited.
2.  **Evidence Gap:** The "large premium" and "$6 billion cash" are stated facts, but the return on investment is entirely assumed. There is no evidence provided that the buyer's existing agency customers will remain post-acquisition despite stated "conflicts." If those customers churn, the revenue base collapses, turning the asset into a liability.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or build/buy smaller technologies preserves capital. The proposal fails to demonstrate why this specific massive outlay beats a staged approach, especially given the technology volatility.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 2009. The cause: Integration friction drove key agency clients to competitors, while the underlying ad-serving tech shifted again, rendering the $6 billion purchase obsolete before payback. The cash reserve depletion left the company vulnerable to the 2008 financial crisis (imminent in this timeline).

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Contradiction:** The plan commits 100% cash for a volatile asset without a stated kill criterion or cap on customer churn. The stated "conflicts" with existing customers directly threaten the revenue needed to justify the price, yet no mitigation is offered.
*   **Missing Verification:** The core claim that this enables competition relies on the assumption that the technology won't shift again immediately. In a "fast-changing" market, buying at a peak premium is a fatal strategic error without a verified moat.

**Risks:** Total capital loss, customer exodus, technological obsolescence.

**Kill Criteria:** None proposed. A deal this size requires a strict churn threshold and a technology relevance review within 12 months. Without these pre-committed stop-losses, the bet is unmanaged.

**Decision:** The lack of verified customer retention plans and the reactive nature of the purchase create an unmitigated financial blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
