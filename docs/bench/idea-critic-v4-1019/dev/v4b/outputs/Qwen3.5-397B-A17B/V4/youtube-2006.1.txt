First call: NO-GO. The price is astronomical for an asset with zero revenue, active legal threats, and unproven economics.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant platform instantly secures the category leader, neutralizing a competitor and capturing the entire market shift to video before it fully matures.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Continue building the smaller internal service; risk being displaced as the user base migrates entirely to the target.
2.  **Buy:** Pay $1.65B for immediate dominance, inheriting massive liability and costs.
3.  **Partner/License:** Attempt a commercial deal; likely insufficient to stop the network effect or litigation.
The "Buy" option only beats "Do Nothing" if the growth trajectory is exponential and legal risks are manageable.

**Fit:** Aligns with organizing the world's information, but the cost diverts resources from core search innovation for a decade.

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition closes, but media lawsuits force the removal of all popular content. Users leave immediately because the library is empty. The $1.65B stock payment dilutes shareholders while the asset becomes a liability with huge bandwidth bills and no ad revenue to offset them. The internal video service was starved of attention during integration and fails too.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "no meaningful revenue," "high bandwidth bills," and "threatened lawsuits." The price represents a massive multiple of non-existent earnings. The copyright risk is not theoretical; it is an active threat from deep-pocketed incumbents capable of shutting the site down.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Financial Suicide:** Paying $1.65B for negative cash flow and undefined liability violates basic capital allocation principles. The math does not work without assuming impossible ad monetization speeds.
2.  **Existential Legal Risk:** If the core value proposition (user-uploaded copyrighted clips) is ruled illegal, the asset value drops to zero instantly, leaving only debt (bandwidth) and a hollow shell.

**Risks:** Ad model failure, bandwidth costs scaling faster than revenue, cultural clash, integration distraction.

**Decision:** The combination of zero revenue, massive cost, and active existential legal threats creates a factual blocker. The plan fails as proposed because the liability likely exceeds the total enterprise value.

DECISION: NO-GO
