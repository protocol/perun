First call: NO-GO. The price implies a valuation with no revenue, no business model, and extreme execution risk, violating basic capital discipline.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the sole mobile-native threat neutralizes a strategic existential risk faster than building, securing the buyer's core ad business for the next decade.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Build internally. Risk: Too slow; the threat captures the mobile shift entirely. Cost: Low cash, high opportunity cost.
2.  **Buy Competitor:** Acquire a smaller player. Risk: Does not remove the market leader; fragmentation continues. Cost: Lower cash, moderate strategic gain.
3.  **The Proposal:** Buy the leader. Risk: Total loss of $1B if culture breaks or regulation blocks it. Cost: Extreme cash burn, massive dilution.

**Fit:** Aligns with the "mobile-first" survival imperative, assuming the buyer cannot replicate the network effect internally.

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition fails because the buyer's legacy culture suffocates the startup's innovation engine, causing key talent to flee and users to migrate to the next new platform. Regulatory bodies block the deal as anti-competitive, wasting months of focus. The $1B creates a massive goodwill impairment charge when growth stalls.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits zero revenue and no business model. The valuation is purely speculative based on user count. The buyer's own mobile weakness confirms the urgency but not the solution's validity.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Valuation Math:** Paying $1B for zero revenue requires a future monopoly profit stream that is unproven and assumes no further competition. The numbers do not work under standard investment criteria.
2.  **Strategic Contradiction:** Buying a "threat" because your own product is weak admits a fundamental capability gap that an acquisition may not fix if the culture clashes.

**Risks:** Regulatory block, culture clash, talent flight, integration failure, overpayment.

**Decision:** The absence of revenue and a business model, combined with a price tag that defies traditional valuation metrics, constitutes a financial blocker. While the strategic threat is real, paying a premium for an unproven asset without a clear path to monetization is reckless. The safer path is to accelerate internal development or seek a cheaper, albeit less dominant, alternative.

DECISION: NO-GO
