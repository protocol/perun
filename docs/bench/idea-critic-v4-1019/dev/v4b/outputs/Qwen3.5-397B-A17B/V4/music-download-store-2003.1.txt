First call: GO. The device sells well; selling the fuel (songs) legally captures value free sharing ignores.

Steelman: Leverage existing hardware dominance to create a legal, convenient alternative to piracy, forcing label cooperation through demonstrated consumer demand.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the market to pirates and subscription rivals. Subscriptions (the rival path) require high bandwidth and catalog depth not yet viable in 2003. Per-song sales match current user behavior (hoarding files) better than renting access.
2.  **Fit:** Aligns with the north star of selling more portable players. A legal store removes the legal risk barrier for mainstream users.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if labels withdraw licenses or margins vanish. However, labels are desperate for revenue due to piracy; they need this platform more than the hardware maker needs any single label. The 99-cent price point is a psychological anchor; margin is secondary to ecosystem lock-in.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal states the player is "selling well." This proves a installed base hungry for content. Free sharing proves demand but offers poor UX (viruses, bad tags, search friction).
5.  **Blockers:** None found in the stated facts. The restriction to "own computers" is a feature, not a bug, driving hardware sales. Rival subscription models are unproven and technically heavier for 2003.
6.  **Risks:** Label collusion is a risk, but their alternative (piracy) is worse. Low margin is a strategic choice to subsidize hardware.

The "free" alternative lacks reliability and legality. The subscription alternative is premature for the era's bandwidth. The per-song model fits the current technical and behavioral context. The hardware lock-in ensures profitability even with thin song margins.

Blockers: NONE.
Risks: Label renegotiation, margin compression (mitigated by hardware upsell).
Kill Criteria: If major labels refuse licensing within 6 months, or if attach rate (songs per device) remains below 1.0 after one quarter.

DECISION: GO
