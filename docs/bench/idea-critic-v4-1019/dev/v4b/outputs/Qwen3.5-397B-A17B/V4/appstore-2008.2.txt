First call: GO. The proposal converts a philosophical stance ("web apps are sufficient") into a defensible market position by addressing the clear failure of that stance to support a thriving ecosystem, while the 30% cut and review process directly mitigate the stated risks of security, stability, and carrier friction.

Steelman: A curated, revenue-sharing native app store is the only viable path to match rival ecosystems without sacrificing the device's signature polish or violating carrier constraints, turning potential chaos into a controlled value engine.

Alternatives analysis:
1.  **Do nothing (Web apps only):** Lowest cost, but guarantees obsolescence as rivals offer superior native experiences; fails to move the north star of market relevance.
2.  **Open distribution (No review):** Maximizes developer speed but invites malware and instability, validating carrier objections and destroying brand trust; high irreversibility risk.
3.  **Proposed Plan (Curated Store):** Balances developer incentive (70% revenue) with quality control (review), directly addressing security and polish concerns while creating a new revenue stream. It beats "do nothing" on ecosystem growth and beats "open distribution" on brand safety.

Fit: Aligns with the stage of needing to catch up to rivals while maintaining a premium brand identity. The review process is the "fence" protecting the user experience.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the review bottleneck becomes so severe that developers abandon the platform, or if the 30% cut is perceived as predatory compared to emerging alternatives. However, the "single company-run" model allows for scalable process adjustments. This is a two-way door; the store rules and cut can be adjusted post-launch.

Evidence: The existence of rival ecosystems proves demand; the CEO's previous stance proves the need for a pivot. The 30% model is a standard assumption for platform risk/reward balance.

Blockers: NONE. The concerns (security, carrier, workload) are features of the solution (review process, curation), not flaws in the concept.
Risks: Review scalability and developer sentiment. These are managed via the kill criteria: if app approval time exceeds 7 days or developer churn exceeds 10% in Q1, the review process must be streamlined or the revenue share revisited.

DECISION: GO
