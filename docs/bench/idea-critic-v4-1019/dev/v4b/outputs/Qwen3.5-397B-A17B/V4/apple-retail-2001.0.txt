First call: NO-GO. A loss-making manufacturer with single-digit share entering a low-margin, high-fixed-cost channel during a recession contradicts basic survival logic.

**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only way to demonstrate the integrated music strategy and stop third-party staff from sabotaging sales, justifying the burn rate as a marketing expense rather than a profit center.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve cash, accept continued channel sabotage, likely fade into irrelevance.
2.  **Shop-in-shop kiosks:** Lower capex and lease risk within existing retailers; weaker brand control but avoids direct channel war.
3.  **Proposed stores:** Maximum brand control and demonstration capability; maximum financial exposure and channel conflict.

**Fit:** Aligns with a "digital hub" vision but assumes the company can survive the cash burn required to prove it. The strategy relies on a future ecosystem that doesn't yet exist to justify present losses.

**Pre-mortem:** A mild recession extends, mall foot traffic drops, and lease obligations become untenable. Resellers, feeling threatened, deprioritize the brand further. The company runs out of cash before the stores prove their marketing value, accelerating bankruptcy. This is a one-way door; once leases are signed and channels alienated, retreat is costly and reputationally damaging.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits to recent losses, low margins in retail, and a poor industry track record for manufacturer-owned stores. The "digital-music strategy" is an assumption; no data proves customers will visit a store solely for this demonstration.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Solvency:** The company is already losing money. Adding high-fixed-cost leases without a guaranteed immediate revenue spike creates a fatal cash-flow mismatch.
*   **Channel Suicide:** Alienating the only current sales force (resellers) before own-stores are operational leaves a revenue vacuum.

**Risks:** Recession depth, inventory obsolescence, execution failure.

**Decision:** The financial risk outweighs the strategic benefit. The company cannot afford to bet its remaining liquidity on an unproven retail model while bleeding cash. The "marketing expense" argument fails when the core business is not stable enough to absorb the hit.

DECISION: NO-GO
