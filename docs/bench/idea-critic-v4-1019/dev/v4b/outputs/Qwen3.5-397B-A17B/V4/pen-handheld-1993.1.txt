First call: NO-GO. The core value proposition (reliable handwriting recognition) is explicitly stated as unproven and unreliable in testing, while the price point is high for an unproven category.

**Steelman:** Launching now establishes first-mover advantage in a new category, leveraging CEO publicity to define the market before competitors, betting that software and hardware refinements can catch up post-launch.

**Options:**
1.  **Launch now:** High cost ($700), high risk (unreliable tech), low reversibility (brand damage if failed).
2.  **Delay/Refine:** Wait for recognition accuracy to improve and size to shrink; reduces user frustration risk but loses first-mover hype.
3.  **Do nothing:** Avoid capital loss; cede category creation to others later.

**Fit:** Assumes the vision is "lead a new category." However, leading with a broken core mechanism contradicts the goal of defining the category positively.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, reviewers demolish the handwriting accuracy, the high price alienates early adopters, and the "handheld" claim is mocked due to size. The category becomes synonymous with failure, delaying adoption for years.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits recognition is "unreliable in testing." No evidence suggests this will be fixed before launch. The price ($700 in 1993) is a barrier without proven utility.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Core Function Failure:** The primary input method is admitted unreliable. A product cannot succeed if its main feature does not work as advertised.
2.  **Price-Value Mismatch:** High price combined with unproven utility and limited software creates a negative value proposition.

**Risks:** Size, software ecosystem, category uncertainty. These are secondary to the functional blocker.

**Decision Logic:** A plan relying on a core technology that is known to be unreliable at launch fails the basic viability test. The "first mover" benefit does not outweigh the reputational and financial risk of shipping a defective core experience. The cheaper, safer alternative is to delay until the recognition engine meets a minimum viability threshold.

DECISION: NO-GO
