First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a fixed public schedule for unproven core technologies (battery durability, novel transmission) without a mandated regulatory driver, a cost advantage, or a verified demand signal, while facing massive warranty exposure in a low-fuel-cost environment.

**Steelman:** A bold, first-mover commitment forces the organization to solve durability and cost issues faster than a cautious approach, securing a decade-long lead before competitors react.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Technical/Economic):** The core value proposition (fuel savings) is negated by cheap fuel, while the cost premium and unproven battery life create a negative ROI for both the manufacturer (warranty claims) and the consumer. No competitor has solved this because the physics and economics currently do not support volume production.
2.  **Blocker (Strategic):** Committing to a "bold public schedule" before proving battery durability invites catastrophic reputational damage and financial loss if the technology fails in the field. A "first for volume production" with unproven durability is not a product launch; it is an unpaid public beta test with lethal liability risks.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "limited pilot" option preserves capital while allowing competitors to bear the cost of proving the market. A staged approach (limited fleet, non-public) tests durability without exposing the entire corporation to warranty collapse.

**Pre-mortem:** By 1998, the cars are recalled due to battery degradation after 30,000 miles. The warranty costs exceed the program's total budget, the brand is associated with unreliability, and the stock price collapses. The "bold schedule" prevented the engineering team from delaying launch to fix the root cause.

**Fit:** This does not move a stated north star of profitability or reliability; it assumes a future regulatory or fuel-price shift that is not currently present.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** Unproven battery durability combined with a fixed public launch date creates an unavoidable failure mode (warranty insolvency). Cheap fuel removes the customer incentive to tolerate higher costs or lower reliability.
*   **Risks:** High execution difficulty, negative near-term demand.

The plan fails as proposed because it treats unproven science as a scheduling problem. The absence of a pilot, a capped exposure, or a proven durability metric is a fatal flaw when the cost of failure is corporate solvency.

DECISION: NO-GO
