First call: GO. The friction of per-order fees directly suppresses repeat purchase frequency, which is the primary lever for lifetime value in retail. Removing this friction via a fixed upfront fee aligns customer incentives with volume, even if per-unit margins dip.

**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee converts variable shipping costs into sunk revenue, psychologically unlocking unlimited ordering behavior that increases total basket share and locks out competitors, outweighing the marginal cost of expedited logistics.

**Alternatives:**
1.  *Do nothing:* Retain margin per order but cap growth; competitors offering frictionless shipping will steal high-frequency shoppers.
2.  *Free shipping thresholds:* Still creates friction at the margin and fails to secure upfront cash flow or total loyalty.
3.  *Lower membership fee:* Reduces the buffer against heavy users without significantly increasing adoption among the target "heavy buyer" segment.

**Fit:** Assumes the goal is market dominance and repeat frequency over immediate per-order profitability. This moves the north star from transactional margin to customer lifetime value.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the "heavy buyers" are so heavy that their shipping costs exceed the $79 fee plus product margin before they churn. However, the upfront cash flow improves working capital immediately. The door is two-way regarding price adjustments for future renewals, though difficult mid-cycle. The cost of delay is ceding the "loyal" segment to a first-mover.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits economics are unproven, but the core mechanic (fixed fee for variable service) relies on the law of large numbers: not every member will be a heavy user. The risk of margin hit is real but is the cost of purchasing market share.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (margin hit, reversal difficulty) are inherent to the strategy, not flaws in the logic. They are managed by the upfront cash collection and the expectation of increased volume.
**Risks:** Heavy user concentration; inability to pivot quickly if unit economics break.

**Kill Criteria:** If the average shipping cost per active member exceeds $90 within the first 6 months, pause new signups and re-price. Leading metric: Repeat purchase frequency of members vs. non-members.

DECISION: GO
