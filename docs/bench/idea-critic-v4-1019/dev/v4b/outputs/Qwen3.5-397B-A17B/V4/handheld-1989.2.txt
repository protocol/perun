First call: GO. The price point and battery life create a distinct market segment that the superior, expensive rival cannot address.

**Steelman:** Win by offering a portable, affordable, long-lasting device with a killer app, rather than competing on raw graphical fidelity.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the portable market to the rival; risk brand irrelevance in non-home settings.
2.  **High-end color handheld:** Match the rival. Result: Higher cost, shorter battery life, higher retail price, likely lower volume.
3.  **Proposed monochrome device:** Lower hardware cost enables aggressive pricing ($89.95) and 10+ hour battery life. The bundled puzzle game provides immediate value.

**Fit:** Expands the "home console" leadership into the portable space by targeting a different use case (on-the-go, battery-constrained) rather than duplicating the home experience.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if third-party developers ignore the platform due to perceived hardware limitations, or if the monochrome screen is rejected by consumers as "obsolete." However, the low price creates a low barrier to entry that mitigates hardware skepticism.

**Evidence & Math:**
*   **Price:** $89.95 is significantly below typical console launch prices, targeting impulse buys and gifts.
*   **Marketing:** $20M is substantial for the era, ensuring high visibility.
*   **Margins:** While thin per unit, the low BOM (Bill of Materials) from monochrome/modest specs allows profitability at high volume, unlike a costly color competitor.
*   **History:** Past single-game devices are a risk, but bundling a deep puzzle game and opening the platform to cartridges changes the model.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks (monochrome, modest specs) are intentional trade-offs for price and battery, not oversights.
**Risks:** Developer adoption; consumer perception of "inferior" tech. These are managed via the killer app bundle and marketing spend.

**Decision:** The proposal successfully identifies an uncontested space (low cost, high endurance) rather than fighting a losing battle on specs. The bundled game solves the "single-game" stigma.

DECISION: GO
