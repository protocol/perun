First call: GO. The demographic shift is a verified fact; the product leverages existing assets to capture it cheaply.

Steelman: Reuse proven mechanicals to minimize risk while styling a "cheap" chassis as a premium sporty icon, funding mass awareness to create a new category before competitors react.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Cede the emerging youth market to rivals; existing lineup ages poorly with new buyers.
2. New platform: Prohibitive cost and time; misses the immediate demographic window.
3. Upgrade current compact: Fails to deliver the distinct "sporty" image required; looks like an economy car.
The proposal beats "do nothing" by creating high-margin volume where none exists.

Fit: Aligns with capturing the postwar boom's entry-level buyers who value style over pure utility. Assumes the styling can sufficiently mask the economy underpinnings.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the "cheap" parts perception overwhelms the styling, making it a joke rather than an icon. However, the long hood/short deck proportion specifically counters this visual cue. It is a two-way door; if sales lag, production scales down easily since parts are shared. Delay costs the entire generational cohort shift.

Evidence: The buyer demographic is stated fact. The mechanical reuse is a cost advantage, not a defect, provided the styling sells the dream. The risks (unproven demand, cheap perception) are inherent to category creation but mitigated by the low capital exposure (shared parts) and the aggressive marketing push to define the narrative.

Blockers: NONE. No stated fact contradicts the core logic; the risks are manageable uncertainties, not fatal flaws. The "cheap parts" risk is addressed by the specific design directive (long hood/short deck) and the options list allowing premium customization.

Risks: Demand volume uncertainty; brand dilution if marketing fails to elevate the image. These become kill criteria: if initial pre-orders or first-quarter sales do not meet a specific threshold relative to ad spend, halt the expanded options rollout.

DECISION: GO
