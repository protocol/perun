First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines a premium price with a smaller app store and carrier exclusivity in a saturated two-platform market, a combination that historically fails to gain traction without a compelling ecosystem or subsidy.

Steelman: A differentiated hardware experience (3D display, visual search) can carve a niche among early adopters despite app gaps if the price matches perceived value.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Preserve capital; avoid entering a low-margin, high-churn market without leverage.
2. **Low-cost entry:** Subsidize the device or price it significantly below flagships to offset the app gap, targeting value seekers rather than premium users.
3. **Proposed plan:** Premium pricing, exclusive carrier, forked OS.

Fit: While visual search aligns with retail goals, the hardware strategy contradicts the reality of a platform-dominated market where app availability drives premium pricing. Users rarely pay flagship prices for incomplete ecosystems.

Pre-mortem: The device launches to initial curiosity but suffers rapid churn as users realize mainstream apps are missing. The high price prevents volume sales needed to attract developers, creating a death spiral. Carrier exclusivity limits reach just as negative reviews spread.

Evidence: The proposal admits the market is saturated and dominated by two platforms. It acknowledges the "app gap" and "price versus differentiation" risks but offers no mitigation other than unproven hardware features. Historical data (implied by "two platforms") suggests hardware differentiation alone cannot overcome network effects at a premium price point without a subsidy.

Blockers:
1. **Economic mismatch:** Premium pricing requires a complete ecosystem; the proposal explicitly lacks leading apps.
2. **Distribution constraint:** Carrier exclusivity + high price + no subsidy = negligible volume in a saturated market.

Risks: Hardware novelty (3D) may be a gimmick; developer interest will remain low without volume.

The plan fails the basic economic test of the smartphone market: you cannot charge flagship prices for a non-flagship ecosystem. The app gap is a structural blocker, not a risk, when paired with unsubsidized pricing.

DECISION: NO-GO
