First call: GO. The proposal trades a rapidly commoditizing cost (storage) for a high-margin, scalable revenue stream (search ads) while solving the user's primary pain point: deleting mail.

Steelman: By offering 1,000x the standard storage and leveraging existing search infrastructure, the service creates an indispensable archive that locks users in, allowing precise ad targeting that incumbents cannot match without rebuilding their backend.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Cede the "archive" use-case to future competitors; remain limited to transient communication.
2. Incremental increase (e.g., 100MB): Fails to change user behavior or generate headlines; easily matched by incumbents.
3. Paid model: Loses the network effects and data volume required to train better ad-matching algorithms.

Fit: Aligns perfectly with a search company's core competency (indexing massive datasets) and north star (organizing information). Email is the ultimate unstructured data source for improving ad relevance.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if privacy backlash forces a policy reversal before ad revenue scales, or if storage costs remain static while user growth explodes. However, storage costs historically drop faster than linear growth, making the "gigabyte" a moving target that becomes cheaper monthly. This is a two-way door; storage limits can be adjusted, and ad algorithms refined. The cost of delay is high: allowing another search player to index personal communications first creates an insurmountable data moat.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the known trajectory of hardware costs (Kryder's Law) and the proven efficacy of text-based ad matching in search results. The "invitation-only" gate controls the burn rate during the initial proof-of-concept.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (cost, privacy, maturity) are manageable variables, not factual contradictions. Storage costs are falling; privacy concerns are mitigated by the free value exchange; market maturity implies users are desperate for differentiation.

Risks: Public relations friction regarding privacy; slower-than-expected ad yield per user. These become kill criteria: if ad revenue per user does not exceed storage cost within 18 months, or if opt-out rates exceed 5%, the model must pivot.

DECISION: GO
