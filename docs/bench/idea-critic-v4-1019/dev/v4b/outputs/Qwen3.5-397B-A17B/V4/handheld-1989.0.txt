First call: GO. The proposal targets a distinct market segment (portability, battery life, low price) ignored by the high-end color rival, leveraging a strong software bundle to drive hardware adoption.

**Steelman:** Win by offering superior battery life and a significantly lower price point ($89.95) than the color competitor, using a killer puzzle app to prove the monochrome screen is a feature, not a bug.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Cedes the portable market entirely to the rival; relies solely on home consoles.
*   **High-End Handheld:** Matches rival specs. Result: Higher cost, shorter battery life, likely higher retail price, direct feature war against an established player.
*   **Fit:** Aligns with mass-market penetration. The "single-game" history is a process flaw, not a hardware one; the bundled puzzle game breaks this pattern.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if third-party developers ignore the platform due to modest specs or if the monochrome screen is perceived as obsolete rather than pragmatic.
*   **Evidence:** The $20M marketing spend is substantial but necessary to reframe "monochrome" as "long battery life." The $89.95 price point creates a low barrier to entry, essential for a secondary device.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (monochrome, modest hardware, thin margins) are strategic trade-offs, not factual contradictions. Thin margins are acceptable if hardware volume drives software attachment rates. The "single-game" legacy is addressed by the bundle.

**Risks:** Developer adoption; perception of being "inferior" rather than "different." These are mitigated by the low price and the proven appeal of the bundled puzzle genre.

**Kill Criteria:** If initial sell-through does not reach 500k units in the first quarter despite the marketing push, or if the attach rate for the bundled game is below 80%, reconsider the hardware strategy.

**Decision:** The proposal successfully identifies an uncontested space (battery life/price) against a superior but compromised rival. The trade-offs are intentional and defensible.

DECISION: GO
