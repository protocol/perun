First call: GO. The proposal solves the core friction of early e-reading (need for a PC) and aligns with the company's asset (large catalog), despite hardware inexperience.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled device removes purchase friction, unlocking latent demand for digital books that previous PC-tethered devices failed to capture.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Cedes the digital future to competitors; print sales will eventually plateau.
2.  **Software Only:** Fails in 2007 due to poor battery life and screen quality on existing mobile devices; requires a PC, killing impulse buys.
3.  **Hardware Risk:** Valid, but outsourcing manufacturing mitigates the lack of internal experience. The "cannibalization" fear is a fallacy; digital reaches customers print cannot (instant delivery, travel).
4.  **Publisher/Pricing:** Low prices are a feature, not a bug, to build market share. Piracy fears are addressed by a seamless, legal alternative that is easier than stealing.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the device is too expensive, the screen is hard to read, or the cellular deal makes unit economics impossible. However, the "paper-like" screen and subsidized connectivity directly address the first two. The third is a calculated bet on volume over margin per unit.

**Blockers:** None. The facts state earlier readers sold poorly because they were tethered and inconvenient. This proposal specifically removes those constraints. The risks are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws in the thesis.

**Risks:** Hardware supply chain complexity; publisher resistance. These are managed via kill criteria: halt if unit costs exceed target by 20% or if <50% of top publishers sign on before launch.

**Decision:** The proposal attacks the specific reasons for past failures while leveraging the company's unique inventory advantage. The risks are manageable compared to the cost of missing the digital shift.

DECISION: GO
