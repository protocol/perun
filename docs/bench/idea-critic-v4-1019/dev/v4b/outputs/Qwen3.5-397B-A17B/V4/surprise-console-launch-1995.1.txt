First call: NO-GO. Launching early with high price, scarce software, and alienated retailers contradicts the fundamental requirements for a successful console ecosystem launch.

**Steelman:** An early May launch at $399 secures a four-month head start to establish brand presence before the competitor arrives, capturing early adopters despite limited inventory.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing (September Launch):** Aligns with developer schedules, ensures a robust game library, matches competitor pricing ($299), and maintains full retailer support. Risk: Direct head-to-head competition on day one.
2.  **Proposed Early Launch:** Gains four months of shelf space but at a catastrophic cost. The $100 price premium is unjustifiable without a killer app library, which is explicitly missing due to developers not planning for the early date. Hardware difficulty exacerbates the software shortage.
3.  **Alternative (Limited Demo):** Show the unit at the trade show for pre-orders only, keeping the September ship date. This builds hype without burning retailer relationships or selling an undercooked product.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
The strategy fails the "ecosystem" test. Consoles live or die by software. Launching with "few games" ensures negative word-of-mouth that will poison the well before the competitor even arrives. Assuming a one-way door: once retailers are slighted and early buyers are disappointed by the lack of games and high price, trust cannot be bought back in time for the holiday season. The cost of delay (waiting until September) is negligible compared to the cost of a botched launch that cedes the mass market to the rival immediately upon their arrival.

**Blockers:**
*   **Software Void:** Developers have not planned for May; the value proposition ($399 for few games) is broken.
*   **Channel Hostility:** Excluding retailers guarantees retaliation, shrinking distribution precisely when broad reach is needed to combat the competitor.
*   **Price Disadvantage:** Being $100 higher than the rival with inferior software content makes the unit unsellable beyond a tiny niche.

**Risks:** Competitor dominance if we wait; however, a failed early launch ensures competitor dominance anyway.

**Decision:** The proposal creates a self-inflicted wound. The head start is illusory without the content to sustain it.

DECISION: NO-GO
