First call: GO. The market void creates a first-mover advantage for an affordable, lightweight roadster, and the low capital intensity of a niche launch justifies the risk despite the shrinking segment.

**Steelman:** A focused, low-overhead launch targeting unmet enthusiast demand can succeed by prioritizing driving purity over mass-market features, accepting low volume as a constraint rather than a failure.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary objection is the shrinking segment and lack of heritage. However, "dead" niches often rebound when incumbents abandon them, leaving pent-up demand. The alternative—doing nothing—guarantees zero revenue and cedes the entire remaining enthusiast base to used markets or exotic imports. A heavier, safer grand tourer would miss the "lightweight" value proposition and increase costs, failing to capture the specific roadster demographic. The risk of safety rules increasing cost is mitigated by the proposal's emphasis on "lightweight" and "affordable," implying a design that works within, not against, regulations.

**Pre-mortem & Evidence:**
If this fails, it will be because the car was too heavy or too expensive, alienating the core enthusiasts who value simplicity. The proposal explicitly counters this by mandating "affordable" and "lightweight." The lack of heritage is irrelevant if the product delivers the promised driving experience; enthusiasts buy the drive, not just the badge. The risk of sales fading after the initial wave is real but acceptable for a niche product; the goal is profitable sustainability, not mass-market dominance.

**Blockers & Risks:**
Blockers: NONE. The facts provided do not contradict the feasibility of an affordable, lightweight design.
Risks: Market size misjudgment, regulatory cost creep, brand dilution. These are managed by the strict constraints of the proposal (affordable, lightweight) and the nature of a niche launch.

**Decision:**
The proposal addresses the core market gap with a disciplined approach to cost and weight. The risks are inherent to any new product but are not fatal given the specific constraints. The potential to revive a dormant segment with a focused product outweighs the caution of a shrinking historical trend.

DECISION: GO
