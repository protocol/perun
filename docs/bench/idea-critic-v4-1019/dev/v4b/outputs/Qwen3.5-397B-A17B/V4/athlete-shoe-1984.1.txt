First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets a dominant market leader's substantial marketing budget on an unproven rookie, violating basic risk management for a publicly traded entity.

**Steelman:** Secure a generational talent early to dominate basketball culture before rivals react, using controversy to generate free media coverage that offsets the high upfront cost.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** High cost, extreme risk, potential cultural monopoly. Irreversible once signed.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain status quo in basketball; preserve budget for proven runners. Low risk, guarantees continued market share loss in basketball.
3.  **Veteran Strategy:** Sign an established All-Star. Higher guaranteed performance, lower cultural upside, similar cost but less "story."

**Fit:** Assumes the brand needs a radical cultural pivot to win basketball, not just better product. If the north star is "running dominance," this distracts. If it is "total athletic leadership," the fit is plausible but dangerous.

**Pre-mortem:** The player suffers a career-ending injury in game 1; the brand is left with dead inventory and a massive financial hole. The league bans the shoes immediately, removing the controversy engine. The player fails to meet hype, making the signature line a discount-bin failure within two years.

**Evidence:** Zero professional data exists for the player. The claim that "signature shoes rarely sell beyond a season" is stated as fact; this proposal relies on breaking that historical trend without proof the market conditions have changed. The budget impact is described as "large share," implying a single point of failure for the marketing division.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Unverified Core Asset:** The entire ROI depends on a player who has not played a professional minute. No data supports the valuation.
2.  **Regulatory Threat:** The plan explicitly relies on breaking league rules. If the league enforces bans strictly (a high probability), the primary differentiator (colorways) and marketing hook (rebellion) vanish instantly.
3.  **Historical Precedent:** The proposal admits signature lines usually fail after one season; no evidence is offered why this specific case defies the rule.

**Risks:** Injury, budget cannibalization, brand dilution if the player flops.

**Kill Criteria:** If the player does not make the All-Rookie team or the league issues a formal ban before the first game, terminate the campaign and liquidate inventory.

DECISION: NO-GO
