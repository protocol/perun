First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and behavioral friction described make mass adoption unlikely.

**Steelman:** A premium, closed-loop appliance creates recurring revenue by guaranteeing freshness and convenience, justifying a high subscription margin against the hassle of traditional juicing.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** At $699, the device targets luxury early adopters, yet the value proposition relies on a subscription model that must compete with fresh produce costing a fraction of the price. Unless the "convenience premium" exceeds 300%, the lifetime value (LTV) will not support the customer acquisition cost (CAC) required for a niche hardware product.
2.  **Friction:** Mandatory Wi-Fi for a mechanical pressing function introduces a single point of failure. If the cloud service lags or the startup pivots, the $699 hardware becomes e-waste. This erodes trust immediately.
3.  **Market:** The "countertop real estate" war is fierce. A single-purpose device requiring proprietary consumables faces the "razor-and-blade" fatigue consumers felt toward single-serve coffee systems, but with a much higher entry barrier ($699 vs. ~$100) and a perishable good (fruit) that users prefer selecting themselves.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "bring your own produce" model or a lower-cost device without mandatory connectivity captures the same convenience market with significantly lower risk and higher addressable volume.

**Pre-mortem:** The company runs out of cash trying to subsidize hardware costs while churn remains high because users reject the per-bag markup after the novelty wears off. The mandatory connectivity becomes a PR liability when servers are scaled down.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unit Economics Mismatch:** The proposal admits concerns about subscription price vs. raw produce cost but offers no data showing consumers will pay the necessary premium. Without proven willingness to pay a 3x+ markup on commodities, the revenue model fails.
*   **Unnecessary Dependency:** Mandatory internet for a core mechanical function creates a avoidable reliability risk that contradicts the "premium" positioning.

**Risks:** Market size overestimation; supply chain complexity for proprietary bags.

**Conclusion:** The combination of high upfront cost, mandatory connectivity friction, and an unproven commodity markup creates a fatal flaw in the adoption curve. The plan does not beat "do nothing" or a simpler, open-system alternative.

DECISION: NO-GO
