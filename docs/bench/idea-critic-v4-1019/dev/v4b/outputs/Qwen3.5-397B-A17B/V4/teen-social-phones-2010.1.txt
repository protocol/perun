First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks price-sensitive youth to pay a premium data plan for a device explicitly stripped of the primary value drivers for that demographic (apps and games), while cannibalizing attention from the company's own flagship platform launching months later.

**Steelman:** This is a controlled, carrier-subsidized experiment to capture early social-graph data from youth via a walled garden, using hardware constraints to force specific usage patterns before the main platform matures.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economic Mismatch:** Requiring a full smartphone data plan for a non-smartphone experience creates a price parity with superior competitors. Price-sensitive users will reject high fixed costs for limited utility.
2.  **Value Void:** Excluding apps and games removes the core retention mechanisms for young users. A "social-only" phone cannot justify smartphone-tier recurring revenue.
3.  **Internal Cannibalization:** Launching this shortly before the main platform splits marketing focus and confuses the carrier partner, likely depressing pre-orders for the higher-margin flagship.
4.  **Execution Risk:** Historical conflict between teams suggests the delayed project will suffer further integration issues, missing the narrow window before the flagship launch.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital and marketing oxygen for the upcoming flagship platform.
*   **Software Only:** Launch the social features as an application on existing cheap handsets, removing the hardware risk and data plan barrier.
*   **Delay:** Push this concept until after the flagship establishes market presence, avoiding internal competition.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because carriers refuse to subsidize a device with no app ecosystem, teens refuse the high monthly bill, and internal sabotage delays the release until it overlaps directly with the flagship, causing both to underperform.

**Blockers:**
*   The unit economics are inverted: the cost to the user (data plan) exceeds the perceived value (no apps/games) for the target demographic.
*   Strategic timing guarantees self-cannibalization of the primary revenue driver later in the year.

**Risks:** Carrier rejection, brand confusion, total project write-off due to team conflict.

**Conclusion:** The proposal demands high expenditure for a product that lacks market fit and strategically undermines the company's primary asset. It fails the basic test of offering value commensurate with cost.

DECISION: NO-GO
