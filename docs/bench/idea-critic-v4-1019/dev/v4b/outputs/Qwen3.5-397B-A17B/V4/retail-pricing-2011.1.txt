First call: NO-GO. The plan eliminates the primary purchase trigger for the existing customer base without evidence they will adapt, while simultaneously undertaking a massive, untested operational overhaul.

**Steelman:** Eliminating coupons clarifies value and rebuilding stores creates a modern destination, potentially attracting new shoppers and restoring margins eroded by constant discounting.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "do nothing" and "pilot" alternatives. The core assumption—that customers value "everyday low price" over "perceived savings via coupons"—contradicts the historical data that most sales come from discounted items. In 2011-2012, mid-market shoppers were becoming *more* price-sensitive, not less. Removing the coupon mechanism removes the reason the current base visits.

Furthermore, the "branded in-store shops" concept requires significant capital expenditure and operational change. Executing this chain-wide, immediately, alongside a pricing strategy that alienates the current revenue source, creates a single point of failure. If the pricing strategy fails (high probability), the store rebuild costs become sunk losses with no new customer base to offset the loss of the old one.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain sales, preserve margin via selective couponing. Low risk, low upside.
2.  **Pilot Program:** Test everyday pricing in 5-10 diverse stores. Measure traffic and basket size changes before chain-wide rollout. Reversibility is high.
3.  **Phased Rebuild:** Update stores only after the pricing model proves stable in pilots.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Evidence:** The proposal states most sales come from discounts, yet the plan removes discounts without a transition mechanism or verified demand for full-price goods from the current demographic.
*   **No Pilot:** The absence of a test for a strategy that fundamentally alters customer behavior is a fatal flaw when the existing behavior is the only known revenue driver.

**Risks:** Total alienation of the core customer base before new customers are acquired; cash flow collapse due to combined drop in sales and high CapEx for rebuilding.

**Kill Criteria:** If a 3-store pilot shows a >15% drop in transaction count within 60 days, the pricing model is aborted.

The lack of a pilot and the direct conflict with established customer behavior constitute a blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
