First call: NO-GO. The financial exposure and technological uncertainty described exceed prudent limits for a single customer request without broader market validation.

**Steelman:** Building a massively larger, convertible wide-body jet now secures market leadership against supersonic threats and locks in the largest customer, leveraging economies of scale to dominate future freight and passenger markets.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Ruin:** The development and factory costs are "far larger than any before." A single failure or delay bankrupts the firm. Relying on one airline for such a massive bet is catastrophic if that carrier falters or cancels.
2.  **Technological Gamble:** The high-bypass engine is unproven. If it fails, the aircraft is grounded, and the company has no fallback.
3.  **Strategic Obsolescence:** If supersonic transport (SST) rapidly replaces subsonic jets as predicted, this massive subsonic investment becomes a stranded asset before paying back.
4.  **Market Narrowness:** Only one airline has ordered. The assumption that "air travel grows quickly" justifies this specific size is unverified across the broader market.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserve capital; let competitors attempt the risky leap first.
*   *Smaller Wide-body:* Develop a twin-engine or smaller wide-body with less financial exposure and proven engines.
*   *Wait for SST:* Focus resources on supersonic technology if that is the inevitable future.

**Pre-mortem:** The project consumes all cash reserves. The engine supplier misses performance targets. The SST market takes off faster than expected, leaving the new giant jet unwanted. The single customer cancels due to economic downturn. The company collapses under debt.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial:** The outlay exceeds all prior experience with only one customer committed. This creates an existential single-point-of-failure risk.
*   **Technological:** Dependence on an unproven engine for a flagship product is unacceptable without a backup plan.
*   **Strategic:** The potential for rapid obsolescence by SST renders the massive investment highly risky without proof that subsonic demand will persist at this scale.

**Risks:** Market misreading on size; SST adoption speed; execution capability on unprecedented scale.

The proposal lacks multiple launch customers to share the financial burden, a proven engine, and a definitive analysis disproving the SST threat. The "raised cockpit" feature is a nice-to-have, not a justification for this level of risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
