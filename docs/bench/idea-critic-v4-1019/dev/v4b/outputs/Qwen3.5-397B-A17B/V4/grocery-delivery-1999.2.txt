First call: NO-GO. The plan commits irreversible capital before validating the core hypothesis that unit economics can work in a single location.

**Steelman:** Rapidly building a nationwide moat of automated warehouses prevents competitors from securing real estate and achieves scale before margins erode.

**Attack:**
1.  **Options:** The "do nothing" alternative (optimize the first city) costs near zero and validates demand. The proposed path costs $1B+ with unproven density. A cheaper alternative is leasing existing space in 2-3 cities to test automation limits. The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" on risk-adjusted return.
2.  **Fit:** Expanding to a dozen cities without a proven model in the first violates basic operational stage-gating. The north star should be positive unit economics, not geographic footprint.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: Warehouses sit half-empty because customer density never reached the threshold required to make 30-minute slots profitable in low-margin grocery. The $1B sunk cost prevents pivoting to a sustainable model.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no operating track record" and "risks raised" regarding margins and density. There is zero evidence that the first city works, let alone twelve. The math of low margins × high fixed costs × unproven volume equals immediate insolvency.
5.  **Blocker:** The core claim (rapid expansion creates value) contradicts the stated fact (unit economics are unknown). You cannot scale a broken or unproven model; you only scale losses. The lack of a pilot in the first city is a fatal flaw when the cost of error is $1 billion.

**Blockers:**
-   Scaling to 12+ cities before proving unit economics in the first city guarantees capital destruction if the model fails (which is likely given "no track record").
-   $1B commitment is irreversible; a failure here ends the company.

**Risks:**
-   Customer density insufficient to support 30-minute slots profitably.
-   Automation complexity exceeds current technical capability.

**Decision:** NO-GO. The plan must be revised to prove unit economics in the initial metropolitan area with a capped, reversible investment before any national expansion.
