First call: GO. The core problems (update delivery, cheating) are unsolvable at scale via physical media; a mandatory client is the only viable technical path, despite friction.

**Steelman:** A proprietary client is the minimum viable infrastructure to secure revenue and gameplay integrity in a shifting broadband landscape, transforming a distribution liability into a future platform.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cheating escalates, updates fail, popularity decays. High risk, zero upside.
2.  **Patch discs:** Slow, costly, fails against real-time cheating. Moderate cost, low reversibility, insufficient upside.
3.  **Mandatory Client (Proposed):** High initial friction (downloads, accounts), but solves core defects and opens a high-margin store ecosystem. Beats "do nothing" on survival and upside.

**Fit:** Assumes the north star is long-term ecosystem control and service reliability over short-term retail ease. Fits the industry shift from product to service.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if early bugs alienate the core base before broadband adoption critical mass, causing a retail revolt that starves the pipeline. However, waiting guarantees decay. This is a one-way door for the *method*, but a two-way door for the *scope* (can scale back features if needed). Cost of delay is total loss of anti-cheat viability.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits risks (broadband gaps, retail pushback) but correctly identifies that physical media cannot support the required update frequency or security checks. No external data contradicts the necessity of software-based delivery for these specific goals.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (user dislike, retail friction, bugs) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. They do not prove the plan fails as proposed; they define the difficulty curve. The alternative (physical-only) functionally cannot achieve the stated goals of real-time updates and cheating prevention.

**Risks:** Retail channel contraction, early adopter churn due to bugs, slower uptake in low-bandwidth regions. These become kill criteria: if retail sales drop >20% without digital compensation within 12 months, or if MAU stagnates due to install friction, pivot to hybrid optional client.

**Kill Criteria:** Monitor monthly active users (MAU) vs. boxed sales ratio. If MAU growth <5% quarter-over-quarter post-launch due to installation barriers, trigger a "lite" offline mode review. Leading metric: Client install completion rate. Review date: 6 months post-launch.

DECISION: GO
