First call: GO. The proposal secures the franchise's future by transitioning subscribers to an emerging distribution model before external competitors force a chaotic migration.

**Steelman:** Leverage seven million profitable subscribers and existing cash flow to seed a streaming habit at zero marginal acquisition cost, treating current technical limitations as temporary hurdles rather than permanent barriers.

**Options:**
1. **Do nothing:** Protects short-term DVD margins but cedes the digital future to better-funded entrants, risking total obsolescence.
2. **Paid add-on:** Creates friction, slows adoption, and fails to build the necessary user base to justify future licensing leverage.
3. **Proposed free inclusion:** Maximizes immediate adoption, gathers critical usage data, and locks users into the ecosystem before rivals arrive. It beats "do nothing" because the strategic risk of irrelevance outweighs the financial risk of cannibalization.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of "home entertainment delivery" regardless of format. Sticking to physical media only fits the *current* stage, not the vision.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if licensing costs balloon before the user base justifies them, or if DVD revenue collapses faster than streaming value accrues. This is a two-way door; the service can be throttled or priced later, but being absent from the market is a one-way door to irrelevance. The cost of delay is losing the customer relationship to new entrants.

**Evidence:** The seven million subscribers are a verified asset. The small catalog and poor quality are stated facts but are acceptable for an initial "learning pilot" given the rapid pace of tech improvement.

**Blockers:** NONE. No stated fact proves the plan fails as proposed; the risks are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The fear of cannibalization is a feature, not a bug: it is better to cannibalize oneself than be eaten by a competitor.

**Risks:** High licensing costs and speed of broadband adoption. These become kill criteria: if licensing costs exceed X% of revenue or adoption stalls below Y% after 12 months, pivot to a tiered model.

**Decision:** The move transforms a legacy asset into a future platform.

DECISION: GO
