First call: NO-GO. The proposed price is prohibitively high for the target market, and superior internal alternatives exist.

**Steelman:** Launching a premium, mouse-driven interface at $10,000 establishes a high-end category leader before competitors can react, targeting executives who value innovation over cost.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $10,000, this unit costs several times more than leading business PCs. Office workers (the stated target) do not control capital budgets of this magnitude for individual stations without proven productivity gains. The "slowness" further erodes the value proposition at this price point.
2.  **Internal Cannibalization:** A cheaper machine with a similar interface is already in development internally. Launching the expensive version first risks brand confusion and immediate obsolescence once the cheaper unit arrives. The "better-way" is to accelerate the cheaper internal project or position this $10k unit as a niche developer kit, not a general office tool.
3.  **Ecosystem Risk:** Little third-party software exists. Without applications, the graphical interface is a novelty, not a business tool. High price + low utility + slow performance = market rejection.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. The product launches, sales are negligible because CFOs reject the $10k price tag for slow hardware, and the internal cheaper team feels undermined. The company loses development costs and momentum.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
*   **Blocker 1 (Price):** The proposal targets "office workers" at ~$10,000. This exceeds the standard capital expenditure limit for individual workstation tools in 1983 by a factor of several, especially given the existence of cheaper leading PCs.
*   **Blocker 2 (Internal Alternative):** An internal project offers similar benefits at a lower cost. Launching the premium version first violates the principle of offering the most efficient solution to the stated user base.
*   **Blocker 3 (Performance):** "Runs slowly" is a fatal flaw for a productivity machine at any price, but unacceptable at a 5x-10x premium.

**Decision Logic:**
The plan fails on its own stated facts: the target user cannot justify the cost for slow hardware with no software, especially when a better internal option is imminent. This is not a risk to be managed; it is a fundamental mismatch of product, price, and audience.

Blockers: Price point excludes target market; internal superior alternative exists; performance inadequate for cost.
Risks: Software ecosystem adoption, market timing.

DECISION: NO-GO
