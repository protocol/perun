First call: NO-GO. The proposal eliminates the primary purchasing trigger for the existing customer base without evidence they will adapt, while executing a high-cost physical renovation simultaneously.

**Steelman:** Eliminating coupons clarifies brand value and reduces margin erosion, while branded shops modernize the experience to attract new demographics, replicating a successful electronics model.

**Attack:**
1.  **Customer Incentive Mismatch:** The core base is "coupon-driven." Removing discounts removes their reason to visit. The electronics success relied on a different customer psychology (tech early adopters vs. value-seeking department store shoppers). There is no evidence this specific demographic accepts "everyday pricing."
2.  **Compound Risk:** The plan combines a pricing strategy shift (behavioral change) with a physical rebuild (capital intensive) across the entire chain. If the pricing fails, the company is left with renovated stores and no revenue.
3.  **No Reversibility:** Rolling out to all stores immediately creates massive sunk costs. If the model fails, the chain cannot easily revert without admitting failure and re-printing all marketing materials, likely accelerating a death spiral.
4.  **Missing Validation:** Announcing without a pilot ignores the specific risk that "existing customers trained to wait" will simply leave rather than adapt. A single store test could validate retention rates before committing capital to renovations.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Continue margin erosion but maintain traffic.
*   *Pilot First:* Test everyday pricing in 5-10 stores without renovation to isolate the pricing variable.
*   *Hybrid:* Reduce coupon frequency gradually while introducing branded sections in high-traffic areas only.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Most sales historically come from discounted items.
*   **Contradiction:** The plan removes discounts immediately for the entire chain, directly attacking the only proven revenue driver without a verified replacement mechanism.
*   **Fact:** No pilot conducted.
*   **Risk:** Total chain exposure to unproven behavioral shifts in a declining sales environment.

**Decision:**
The proposal fails the blocker test. It bets the entire company on an unproven behavioral shift for a specific, price-sensitive demographic, compounded by irreversible capital expenditure, with no safety valve. The lack of a pilot is a fatal flaw given the historical dependence on discounts.

DECISION: NO-GO
