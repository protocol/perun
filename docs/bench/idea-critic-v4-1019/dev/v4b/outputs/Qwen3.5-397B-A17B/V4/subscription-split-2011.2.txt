First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a pricing backlash by fracturing the customer experience, compounding confusion with fragmentation.

**Steelman:** Separating the brands allows streaming to pivot aggressively without being anchored by a declining DVD logistics model, clarifying distinct unit economics for investors.

**Attack:**
1.  **Compounded Friction:** Customers reeling from a 60% price hike are now asked to manage two websites, two accounts, and two bills. This transforms a pricing complaint into a usability crisis, likely triggering mass churn rather than acceptance.
2.  **Data Silos:** Severing rating and recommendation sharing destroys the primary competitive moat: a unified view of user taste. This weakens both services' ability to retain users through personalization.
3.  **Timing:** Executing a complex brand split immediately after a controversial price increase signals instability and greed, not strategic clarity. It validates customer fears that the company is abandoning them.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "do nothing" approach regarding branding, coupled with a transparent communication strategy about the cost drivers of each service, carries less execution risk. A bundled discount for early adopters would soften the price shock without operational fragmentation.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-split, churn spikes 20% higher than projected because users cancel both services rather than navigate the new friction. The "new" DVD brand is perceived as a junk drawer, accelerating its death faster than if it remained attached to the premium streaming brand.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal introduces high-friction barriers (dual login/billing) immediately after a 60% price increase.
*   **Contradiction:** The rationale cites "customer confusion" as a risk, yet the solution (two brands, two queues) inherently creates that confusion.
*   **Strategic Failure:** The plan sacrifices cross-service data synergy, which is critical for long-term retention, to solve a short-term messaging problem.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, operational overhead of maintaining two stacks, and irreversible loss of unified user data.

**Verdict:** The proposal solves an internal management desire at the expense of the user experience during their most vulnerable moment. It creates a "two-door" exit for customers.

DECISION: NO-GO
