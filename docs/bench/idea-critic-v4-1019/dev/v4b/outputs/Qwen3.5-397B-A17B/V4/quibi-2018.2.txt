First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets $1B+ on unproven demand for premium short-form subscriptions amid known fatigue and abundant free alternatives.

Steelman: A high-fidelity, orientation-agnostic mobile platform could capture elite users willing to pay for exclusive, cinematic short stories, leveraging studio backing to outclass user-generated content.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost, extreme risk (unproven model), low reversibility (capital intensive).
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, no risk, preserves capital for proven formats.
3. **Pilot/Lean Launch:** Test content viability with existing infrastructure or lower budget before raising $1B; moderate cost, validated risk, high reversibility.
The proposed plan fails to beat "Do Nothing" or a "Pilot" on risk-adjusted returns given the stated lack of proven demand.

Fit: Assumes a north star of "premium mobile engagement" without evidence that users equate "premium" with "short-form subscription" rather than "free/ads."

Pre-mortem: The service launches with high-profile shows but fails to convert subscribers due to price sensitivity and content format mismatch (users prefer free short video or long-form subscriptions). The $1B burn rate becomes unsustainable before product-market fit is found. This is a one-way door for the capital; delay costs only opportunity on an unproven bet.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits "no proven demand," "subscription fatigue," and "abundant free" competitors. The core revenue hypothesis contradicts current market behavior described in the text.

Blockers:
1. **Contradictory Premise:** The plan requires raising $1B for a model the proposal explicitly states has "no proven demand" while competing against "abundant" free alternatives. The math of customer acquisition vs. lifetime value cannot work without assuming a behavioral shift the text says is currently negative (fatigue).
2. **Superior Alternative Exists:** A capped, time-boxed pilot to validate demand for *premium* short-form is possible and strictly superior to an immediate $1B launch. The failure to propose this staged approach blocks the full launch.

Risks: Talent costs, orientation technology adoption, studio politics.

The absence of a pilot phase and the reliance on unproven demand for a high-cost subscription model in a saturated free market constitute a fatal flaw.

DECISION: NO-GO
