First call: NO-GO. The plan removes the primary purchase trigger (coupons) for the core demographic without evidence they value "everyday low price" over perceived discounts, while simultaneously imposing massive operational friction via store rebuilds.

**Steelman:** Eliminating coupon clutter simplifies the brand and rebuilds margin, while branded shops modernize the experience to attract higher-value traffic, leveraging the CEO's prior electronics success.

**Attack:**
1.  **Behavioral Mismatch:** The customer base is explicitly "coupon-driven." In retail, removing the mechanism that drives 80% of transactions (discounts) to chase a theoretical margin improvement is fatal without proof of elasticity. The CEO's success in *electronics* (high-consideration, feature-driven) does not transfer to *department stores* (habit-driven, price-sensitive) without adaptation.
2.  **Execution Risk:** Rebuilding the entire chain at once creates a single point of failure. If the pricing model fails, the brand has no fallback position and has already incurred the cost of physical reconstruction.
3.  **Missing Evidence:** There is zero data provided that this specific demographic trusts "everyday pricing" more than "sales." The proposal assumes the problem is the *mechanism* of discounting, not the *merchandise* or *relevance*.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Continue bleeding sales but maintain cash flow from the known coupon model.
*   **Pilot:** Test everyday pricing in 5-10 stores while keeping the rest on the coupon model. This isolates pricing risk from construction risk.
*   **Hybrid:** Simplify promotions without eliminating them entirely; phase the store rebuilds.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, same-store sales collapse 20% as loyal customers defect to competitors' weekly flyers. The chain cannot revert quickly because the physical stores are already gutted for the new layout, and the brand identity is confused. Cash flow dries up before the new concept gains traction.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Most sales historically come from discounted items.
*   **Contradiction:** The plan eliminates discounts chain-wide without a pilot to prove the core base will buy at full price.
*   **Risk vs. Reward:** The pace (all stores at once) magnifies the unproven pricing hypothesis into an existential threat.

**Decision:** The absence of a pilot for a strategy that directly contradicts established customer behavior is a fatal flaw. The plan must be tested before total commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
