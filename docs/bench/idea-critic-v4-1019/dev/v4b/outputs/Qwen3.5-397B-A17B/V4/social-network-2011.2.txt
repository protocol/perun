First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a distribution problem with forced integration, ignoring that the core failure mode of the three previous attempts was likely product-market fit, not lack of exposure.

**Steelman:** Integrating social layers into dominant utility products (search, mail) leverages existing traffic to overcome network effects, while unique features (circles, hangouts) provide a wedge the incumbent cannot easily copy without breaking its own model.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Misalignment:** Tying executive bonuses to adoption of a specific *feature* rather than user satisfaction or retention invites "dark patterns" and forced usage that degrades the core utility products (search/mail). This risks driving users away from the company's revenue engine to chase a vanity metric.
2.  **Historical Evidence:** The proposal admits three prior failures. Without a stated reason why *this* specific approach succeeds where others failed (beyond "integration"), it repeats the sunk cost fallacy. Integration is a distribution channel, not a value proposition. If the social layer offers no standalone value, forcing it into mail/search creates friction, not adoption.
3.  **Risk of Backlash:** Forced integration into essential tools (mail, search) poses an existential risk to the core business if users perceive privacy violations or UI clutter. The "invite-only" beta contradicts the "company-wide push" and forced integration strategy; you cannot force an invite-only product.
4.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "acquire" alternative is stronger. Building from scratch against a hundreds-of-millions-user incumbent with a history of internal failure has a near-zero probability of success. A smaller, standalone pilot without bonus-linked coercion would test actual demand before risking the core brand.

**Blocker:** The plan lacks a mechanism to prevent the degradation of core products (search/mail) while chasing social metrics. The incentive structure (bonuses tied to adoption) guarantees this degradation. The contradiction between "company-wide forced push" and "invite-only beta" renders the launch strategy incoherent.

**Risks:** User backlash, loss of trust in core utilities, wasted engineering cycles on a third failed attempt.

**Kill Criteria:** If the beta does not achieve 20% organic return usage (excluding forced prompts) within 3 months, or if core product engagement (search/mail) drops by >1% post-integration, the project must terminate immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
