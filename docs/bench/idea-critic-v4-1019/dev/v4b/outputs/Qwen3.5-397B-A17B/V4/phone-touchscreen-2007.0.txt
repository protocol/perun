First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a computer and music player maker with zero phone experience to enter a carrier-dominated market with an unproven form factor (no keyboard), a premium price, and limited distribution, while lacking a critical ecosystem (third-party apps).

Steelman: A converged device leveraging existing music dominance and design control can disrupt carrier hegemony and create a new premium category, offsetting hardware inexperience with superior user experience.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Safe, but risks music player erosion as competitors add phones.
2. License OS to carriers: Faster reach, but cedes design control and margin.
3. Proposed path: High risk/reward; bets everything on design differentiation and carrier concession.

Fit: Aligns with protecting the music franchise and expanding into personal computing, but assumes carriers will break decades of precedent for an unproven vendor.

Pre-mortem: The device fails because business users reject the lack of a physical keyboard, the premium price limits adoption to early adopters only, and the lack of apps makes it a toy compared to competitors. The exclusive carrier deal caps growth, and the company burns cash trying to manufacture and support a complex phone without industry relationships.

Evidence: The proposal admits no phone experience, no keyboard, no apps, and limited reach. These are not assumptions; they are stated deficits. The claim that a carrier will pay revenue share for design control contradicts the stated norm that carriers dictate design.

Blockers:
1. **Contradictory Premise:** The plan requires a carrier to cede design control and pay revenue share to a company with no phone track record. The stated fact is that carriers normally dictate design; no evidence is provided that this specific carrier will break this rule for a novice entrant.
2. **Missing Core Functionality:** A "leading" business phone in 2007 requires third-party applications. Launching without them while charging a premium for a keyboard-less device targets a non-existent market segment.

Risks: Execution failure due to inexperience; market rejection of touch-only input; ecosystem failure.

The plan relies on a carrier behaving against their historical self-interest for an unproven partner and launches a premium product missing features (keyboard, apps) that define the current premium segment.

DECISION: NO-GO
