First call: NO-GO. Replacing a heritage asset entirely based on taste physics ignores the brand's emotional equity.

**Steelman:** The data proves consumers prefer the sweeter taste profile; swapping formulas maximizes immediate sensory satisfaction and halts share loss to rivals.

**Attack:** The proposal conflates "preferred taste in a sip" with "preferred product for a lifetime." Blind tests isolated flavor from identity, creating a false positive. By withholding the critical fact that the original would vanish, the research measured incremental preference, not replacement acceptance. This triggers a catastrophic backlash: loyalists perceive the removal as a loss of heritage, not an upgrade. Without a fallback, the company forces a binary choice that alienates its core base while failing to guarantee rival drinkers will switch to a "new" cola rather than their established favorite.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Retain share erosion but preserve brand trust.
2.  **Line Extension:** Launch the sweeter formula as "New Cola" alongside the original. This captures the taste-preference segment without provoking the heritage backlash.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** High risk of permanent brand damage and bottler revolt for uncertain gain.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales collapse not because the drink tastes bad, but because consumers feel betrayed. Media narratives focus on "corporate arrogance" and "lost history." Bottlers face angry protests. The company is forced to reintroduce the old formula at great cost, admitting the strategic error publicly.

**Evidence Check:** The proposal admits testing did not disclose the removal of the original. This invalidates the data for a replacement decision. The risks (emotional attachment, no fallback) are confirmed by the methodology gap.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Fatal Data Flaw:** Research methodology hid the core consequence (removal of original), rendering the "preference" finding inapplicable to a replacement strategy.
2.  **No Fallback:** Eliminating the only safety net against consumer rejection violates basic risk management for a flagship product.

**Risks:** Emotional backlash, bottler friction, brand heritage erosion.

**Decision:** The plan relies on incomplete data and removes all safeguards. It must be revised to include the original formula as a permanent option.

DECISION: NO-GO
