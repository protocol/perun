First call: NO-GO. The proposal discards the brand's primary shelf cue—the orange with a straw—in a single, irreversible nationwide launch, directly contradicting the stated risk that shoppers rely on this image for recognition.

Steelman: A modernized design with a functional cap and clear product imagery will attract new buyers while retaining loyalists through aggressive advertising.

Attack: The steelman fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests. The proposal admits the buyer base is "loyal and older" and that the old image is the "main shelf cue." Removing this cue guarantees recognition failure for the core demographic. Advertising cannot instantly retrain millions of habitual shoppers to find a minimalist box among competitors, especially when the visual anchor is gone. The "vertically rotated brand name" further reduces readability for older eyes.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Retains shelf presence, zero risk.
2. Staged pilot: Test new design in limited markets to measure recognition drop before national spend.
3. Proposed plan: High cost, high risk of alienating the entire base immediately.

Pre-mortem: Three months post-launch, sales collapse because loyal customers walk past the unrecognizable cartons, assuming the brand is out of stock or discontinued. The "large advertising campaign" burns cash trying to solve a visibility problem created by the design itself.

Blockers:
1. The proposal explicitly states the old image is the "main shelf cue" yet plans to remove it entirely without a transition period or validation.
2. Launching "in all stores at once" removes the ability to detect the predicted recognition failure before total revenue impact.
3. No evidence is provided that the "cap shaped like an orange" compensates for the loss of the straw graphic for an older demographic.

Risks: High execution cost, brand equity erosion, competitor exploitation of shelf confusion.

This is a one-way door with a confirmed negative predictor (loss of main cue) and no safety valve. A sound operator would demand a market pilot to quantify the recognition loss before committing to a nationwide print run. As proposed, the plan ignores its own stated risks.

DECISION: NO-GO
