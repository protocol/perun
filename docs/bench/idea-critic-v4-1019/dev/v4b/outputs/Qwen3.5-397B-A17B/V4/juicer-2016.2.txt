First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and behavioral friction of a $699 connected juicer relying on expensive proprietary consumables create an insurmountable barrier to mass adoption in a commodity market.

**Steelman:** A premium, hassle-free nutrition ecosystem where hardware subsidies are irrelevant due to deep capital reserves, and users pay a significant premium for freshness verification and convenience.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** At $699, the device targets early adopters, but the subscription model requires recurring value. Proprietary bags of chopped produce carry massive logistical costs (cold chain, packaging, waste) compared to raw produce. Unless the per-serve cost is competitive with fresh juice bars, churn will be catastrophic. The proposal admits concern over price versus buying produce; without data showing users will pay a 300%+ premium for convenience, the lifetime value (LTV) cannot justify the customer acquisition cost (CAC) for a niche appliance.
2.  **Connectivity Friction:** Mandating Wi-Fi for a basic mechanical function (pressing fruit) introduces a single point of failure. If the cloud service lags or the API changes, the hardware becomes a brick. This "phone home" requirement for freshness checking is a solution in search of a problem; expiration dates on packaging suffice. This adds cost and risk without proportional value.
3.  **Market Size:** The intersection of users willing to buy a $700 appliance, commit to a subscription, and accept limited SKU choices (proprietary bags) is vanishingly small. History shows that high-friction, closed-loop food systems fail against open markets unless the quality delta is undeniable, which is unproven here.

**Pre-mortem:** The company burns cash on inventory and hardware manufacturing. Users try the device, find the bag replacement cost exorbitant compared to grocery store produce, and cancel subscriptions. The device sits unused. The "smart" features become a liability when servers are deprecated.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unit Economics:** No evidence provided that the subscription margin covers the high logistics cost of pre-chopped, packed produce while remaining palatable to consumers.
*   **Value Proposition:** The "freshness check" via app does not solve a painful enough problem to justify the connectivity risk and cost.

**Risks:** Supply chain complexity for perishable proprietary goods; consumer resistance to closed ecosystems in food.

**Kill Criteria:** If pilot data shows subscription retention <60% after 90 days or CAC >$400, terminate hardware production immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
