First call: NO-GO. The proposal asserts a mass-market everyday product while admitting no clear everyday use for most people, creating an immediate contradiction between positioning and utility.

Steelman: An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to refine the hardware and software with enthusiastic early adopters before attempting a general consumer release, mitigating some initial risk.

However, the fatal flaw lies in the value proposition. A $1,500 price point is prohibitive for a device lacking a defined "everyday use." Early adopters tolerate bugs, but they do not tolerate uselessness. Without a killer application beyond novelty, the device becomes an expensive curiosity rather than a necessary tool. The raised risks are not merely potential hurdles; they are fundamental market barriers. Privacy objections and social acceptance issues directly limit where and how often the device can be used, further eroding the "everyday" claim. Short battery life exacerbates this by limiting utility windows.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids reputational damage from a high-profile failure and saves R&D capital.
2. Enterprise/Industrial focus: Target specific workflows (logistics, manufacturing, medical) where the hands-free camera and display solve expensive, defined problems, justifying the cost and bypassing social/privacy hurdles.
3. Developer kit only: Sell strictly to builders to cultivate an ecosystem of apps before promising a consumer product.

The proposal to position this immediately as an "everyday consumer product" fails the basic test of product-market fit. You cannot sell a solution to a problem most people do not have, especially at a premium price point with known social friction. The "invitation-only" tactic does not fix the core lack of utility; it only delays the inevitable realization that the mass market has no need for this form factor in its current state. Proceeding as proposed invites a costly public failure, branding the technology as a privacy-violating toy rather than a serious platform. The strategy must pivot to finding a specific, high-value use case before claiming the "everyday" mantle.

DECISION: NO-GO
