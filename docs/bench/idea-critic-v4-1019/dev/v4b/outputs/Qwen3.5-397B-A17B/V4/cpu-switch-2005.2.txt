First call: GO. The current roadmap fails the core requirement for thin laptops; staying the course guarantees product irrelevance.

**Steelman:** Migrating to the dominant architecture is the only viable path to competitive thermal performance and efficiency, with a translation layer mitigating immediate software obsolescence.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Retain distinctiveness but ship thermally constrained, lagging products; likely market exit.
2.  **Partial transition:** Fragment the line, confusing users and splitting developer focus; highest support cost.
3.  **Full migration (Proposed):** High execution risk and short-term sales freeze, but secures long-term viability and performance parity.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of delivering superior personal computing hardware; distinctiveness must derive from design and integration, not an inferior instruction set.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the translation layer performance is unacceptable to pro users or if the sales freeze during the announcement window depletes cash reserves before new units ship. This is a one-way door; delay costs market share quarterly as competitors leverage superior chips.

**Evidence:** The proposal states the supplier roadmap "lags" and lacks "cool and efficient" chips for thin form factors. This is a hard physical constraint, not a preference.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (sales freeze, rebuild effort, speed penalty, brand dilution, past pain) are execution challenges and market dynamics, not factual contradictions. None prove the plan fails as proposed; they define the difficulty of the necessary pivot. The "painful" prior transition is a caution for project management, not a veto on the strategic necessity.

**Risks:** Developer friction, temporary revenue dip, brand identity confusion. These become kill criteria: if translation speed drops below 70% of native within 6 months of beta, or if pre-orders miss targets by >20% post-announcement, the rollout pauses.

**Decision:** The physical limitation of the current supply chain forces this move. The risks are manageable compared to the certainty of product failure if the status quo remains.

DECISION: GO
