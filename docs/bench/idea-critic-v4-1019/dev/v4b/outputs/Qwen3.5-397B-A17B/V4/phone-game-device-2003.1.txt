First call: NO-GO. The proposal conflates two distinct device categories, creating a product that excels at neither while costing significantly more than the category leader.

Steelman: A converged device leverages existing mobile distribution to bypass the dedicated console's walled garden, offering casual gamers a "good enough" library in a device they already carry.

However, the specific execution fails on fundamental usability and economics. The requirement to remove the battery to change games is a catastrophic friction point that breaks the core loop of gaming (instant swap/play). This is not a minor inconvenience; it renders the physical media model non-viable compared to the leader's integrated cartridge slot. Furthermore, the ergonomic compromise ("awkward to hold") degrades the primary value proposition: playability.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Maintain phone dominance; let the dedicated console own gaming. Cost: $0. Risk: Low. Reversibility: N/A.
2. Software-only strategy: License games to the existing market leader or build a digital storefront for future smartphones. Cost: Low. Risk: Medium. Reversibility: High.
3. Proposed hybrid: High cost ($300+), high risk of rejection due to clunky UX, and low reversibility once hardware is tooled.

The proposal does not beat "do nothing" or a software-first approach. The price premium over the market leader cannot be justified when the user experience is objectively inferior (battery removal, poor ergonomics). The lack of publisher commitment is a symptom, not the root cause; publishers will not commit to a platform with such high user friction.

Blockers:
1. Physical Design Flaw: Removing the battery to swap games makes the device functionally broken for its secondary purpose.
2. Price/Value Mismatch: $300 for a worse experience than the $100 market leader is unsellable.
3. Ergonomics: "Awkward to hold" invalidates the handheld claim.

Risks:
1. Publisher apathy (mitigated only by fixing the hardware).
2. Brand dilution if the device fails publicly.

The hardware constraints described create a product that fails its own definition as a "handheld game console." No amount of marketing can overcome the friction of removing a battery to play a different game.

DECISION: NO-GO
