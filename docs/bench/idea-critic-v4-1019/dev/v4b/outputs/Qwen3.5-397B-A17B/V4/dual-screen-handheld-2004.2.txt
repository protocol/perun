First call: GO. Differentiation via novel interaction and price undercuts a spec-war against a stronger incumbent.

**Steelman:** Win by expanding the market to non-core players with unique dual-screen/touch gameplay at a lower price, rather than losing a graphics arms race.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposal:** Dual-screen/touch, lower cost. High novelty, moderate dev friction, accesses new demographic.
2.  **Match Rival:** Higher specs, higher price. Likely loss against entrenched rival; ignores core strength.
3.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain old hardware. Slow erosion as rival captures media/core mindshare.

**Fit:** Assumes strategy prioritizes market expansion over spec parity. Reuses existing IP library while creating new genres.

**Pre-mortem:** Fails if developers ignore the second screen (making it a costly gimmick) or if the price premium over the old model is too high for casuals. It is a two-way door; software lineup can pivot quickly if initial titles flop. Delay costs first-mover advantage in "touch" perception.

**Evidence:** Based on stated facts: rival has better graphics/media; proposal offers lower price and unique form factor. No internal data contradicts the assumption that "broadening audience" offsets "developer confusion."

**Blocker Test:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. No stated fact proves the plan fails as proposed. The risk of developer confusion is an execution hurdle, not a factual impossibility. The cost of two screens is acknowledged but mitigated by the "lower price than rival" constraint (implying the rival's premium allows room).
*   **Risks:** Developer adoption speed; consumer perception of "gimmick." These become kill criteria: if E3 developer sign-ups are <50% of legacy partners, or if pre-orders from non-core demos miss targets, pivot marketing or subsidize dev kits.

**Decision:** The proposal beats "do nothing" and "match rival" on the specific goal of audience expansion. The risks are manageable via software strategy.

**Kill Criteria:**
1.  **Metric:** 30% of launch window titles must utilize both screens meaningfully.
2.  **Date:** 6 months post-launch.
3.  **Action:** If missed, halt first-party dual-screen experiments and focus on backward compatibility marketing.

DECISION: GO
