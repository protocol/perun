First call: GO. The data-driven bet on a full-season drop with top talent aligns with emerging on-demand behaviors, despite the lack of a pilot.

**Steelman:** Leveraging proprietary viewing data to bypass the traditional pilot system allows the service to secure exclusive prestige content that directly satisfies proven user demand, differentiating it from licensed-content competitors.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue relying on rising licensed costs; risk stagnation as competitors exclusive rights.
2.  **Traditional Pilot:** Test one episode; slower time-to-market, risks talent availability, mimics broadcast models users may be outgrowing.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** High upfront cost but secures exclusive IP, locks talent, and capitalizes on binge-watching potential indicated by data.

**Fit:** Assumes the north star is transitioning from a content aggregator to a primary destination. If the data shows users consume long-form content in bursts, this fits perfectly.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the specific data signals were misinterpreted (correlation vs. causation) or if the "all-at-once" release cannibalizes subscription retention after the initial binge. However, the cost of delay is high; waiting for market validation cedes first-mover advantage in original streaming prestige.

**Evidence:** The proposal cites internal viewing data as the core justification. While external benchmarks for "online-only prestige" do not exist yet, the internal metrics are the only relevant leading indicator. The cost is high, but necessary to break the licensing cost spiral.

**Blockers:** NONE. The absence of a pilot is a strategic choice, not a factual failure, provided the data supports the genre/director/star combination. No evidence suggests the target audience rejects the format; the risk is inherent but calculated.

**Risks:** Audience mismatch despite data; budget overrun; single-point failure if the show flops. These are managed by making this the *first* major original, allowing future calibration.

**Decision:** The proposal correctly identifies that traditional de-risking (pilots) may not apply to a new distribution paradigm. The data provides sufficient cover to proceed.

**Kill Criteria:** If Season 1 completion rates fall below 60% within 30 days of release, halt Season 2 production and pivot to shorter-form originals. Monitor daily new subscriptions attributed directly to the title.

DECISION: GO
