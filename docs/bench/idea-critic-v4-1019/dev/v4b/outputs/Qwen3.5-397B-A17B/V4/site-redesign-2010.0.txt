First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines maximum engineering risk (full rewrite) with maximum deployment risk (big-bang migration) while alienating the core user base that drives traffic, all without a safety net.

Steelman: A complete rebuild is the only way to fix structural debt and pivot to publisher-focused content to arrest traffic decline before a competitor dominates.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Traffic continues slowing; competitor gains share. Low cost, high strategic risk.
2. Iterative refactor: Update surfacing algorithms and UI incrementally on the existing stack. Moderate cost, reversible, preserves community trust.
3. Proposed rewrite/migration: Highest cost, irreversible upon launch, high probability of community revolt due to changed voting mechanics.

Fit: The goal is arresting traffic decline. However, changing the core mechanic (voting) that the loyal community values contradicts the asset (community) needed to solve the problem. Shifting focus to publishers may further alienate the existing base before new users arrive.

Pre-mortem: The new site launches; the core community rejects the altered voting mechanics and reduced visibility for user-submitted content; traffic plummets immediately; the inability to rollback forces a prolonged crisis while engineering attempts fixes on an unproven codebase. The competitor captures the displaced users.

Evidence: The proposal admits the community values existing mechanics and that the launch leaves no rollback. These are internal contradictions: you cannot change what users value most and expect them to stay, nor can you claim a high-risk strategy is safe without a rollback plan.

Blockers:
1. No rollback path for a "move all users at once" deployment. This is a fatal operational flaw.
2. The plan explicitly changes the specific mechanic (voting) identified as core to the loyal community, guaranteeing friction with no mitigation strategy.
3. A full rewrite delays value delivery compared to iterative testing, increasing the window for the rival site to grow.

Risks: Community exodus, engineering delays, publisher content not filling the gap left by angry users.

Kill criteria: If a phased rollout or A/B test of the new surfacing logic is not added to the plan, the risk of total community loss is unacceptable. The proposal must include a rollback mechanism and a phased migration strategy. As written, it fails the basic safety test.

DECISION: NO-GO
