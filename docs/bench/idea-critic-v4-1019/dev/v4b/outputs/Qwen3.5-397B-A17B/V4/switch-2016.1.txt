First call: GO. The hybrid form factor directly addresses the erosion of the handheld line by phones while leveraging the maker's strong first-party IP to overcome weak hardware specs, turning a financial loss into a unique market position.

**Steelman:** A hybrid device creates a new category where portability and home play coexist, allowing the maker to dominate a niche rivals cannot touch due to their focus on raw power, relying on exclusive titles to drive hardware sales despite a thin launch lineup.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue losing handheld share to phones; brand relevance fades.
2.  **High-power home console:** Repeat previous failure; ignore handheld erosion; high financial risk.
3.  **Hybrid (Proposed):** Captures both markets; differentiates from rivals; moderate risk if execution fails.
The proposal beats "do nothing" by stopping the bleed and creating a new revenue stream.

**Fit:** Aligns with the need to stop financial losses and counter phone erosion. It assumes the brand's IP is strong enough to sell hardware, which is consistent with their history despite recent losses.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if third-party support remains non-existent and the "confusing positioning" narrative sticks, leading to poor software attach rates. However, this is a two-way door; the maker can pivot software strategy or price cuts quickly. The cost of delay is another year of handheld erosion and competitor entrenchment.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits weak specs and a thin lineup but relies on the maker's own studios. This is the only verifiable asset; rivals cannot copy the IP.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks (weak specs, confusion, supply) are known variables, not factual contradictions. The "confusing positioning" is a marketing challenge, not a product flaw, solvable by clear messaging about the hybrid nature.

**Risks:** Third-party abandonment, supply chain bottlenecks, consumer confusion. These become kill criteria: if pre-orders miss targets or key third parties drop out by launch, the project pauses.

**Decision:** The unique value proposition outweighs the hardware deficit. The maker's IP is the moat.

DECISION: GO
