First call: NO-GO. The proposal abandons the company's core strength (business email) in its most vulnerable segment (enterprise) while charging a premium price for an untested OS with a smaller screen and no native productivity apps.

**Steelman:** Launch a niche, phone-tethered 7-inch device to seed a new OS ecosystem without cannibalizing the flagship phone, betting developers will follow later.

**Attack:**
1.  **Value Mismatch:** The target audience (business users) prioritizes email/calendar. Shipping without these built-in renders the device useless for its intended pitch unless the user already owns the company phone, drastically shrinking the addressable market.
2.  **Price/Value Failure:** Matching the price of a larger, established rival tablet with a superior app store, while offering fewer features (no native email) and a smaller screen, is economically indefensible.
3.  **Platform Risk:** An untested OS with no killer apps (email missing) offers no incentive for developers to commit, creating a death spiral: no apps mean no users, which means no developers.
4.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "wait" option is stronger. Rushing an incomplete product damages the brand's enterprise reputation. A viable alternative would be porting the existing successful phone OS to tablets immediately, leveraging the existing app ecosystem and email strength.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, reviewers slam the lack of email and high price, enterprises reject it, developers ignore the barren OS, and the project is cancelled within a year, wasting the OS acquisition cost.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fatal Feature Gap:** No built-in email/calendar contradicts the "business leader" positioning and renders the device non-functional for standalone business use.
*   **Pricing Error:** Price parity with a superior, larger competitor ensures immediate sales failure.
*   **Ecosystem Void:** No proven developer commitment for an untested OS makes the platform unsustainable.

**Risks:** Brand erosion, sunk cost of OS acquisition, loss of enterprise market share to the rival.

The proposal fails on basic product-market fit and pricing logic. It attacks the company's stronghold with a weaker product at a higher relative cost.

DECISION: NO-GO
