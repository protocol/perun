First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market late, discards established partner trust by dropping copy-protection, and relies on uncertain label cooperation for sharing without a clear price or feature advantage sufficient to dislodge a dominant ecosystem.

Steelman: A superior hardware experience (screen, wireless sharing) combined with an integrated store can overcome lateness if the ecosystem lock-in is broken by usability rather than price.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids channel conflict and R&D waste; preserves partner relations.
2. License software only: Reduces hardware risk but forfeits the integrated margin and control.
3. Acquire a smaller player: Faster entry but inherits technical debt and still faces label hurdles.
The proposal must beat "do nothing," which currently carries zero execution risk.

Fit: Assumes the company's north star is direct consumer hardware ownership. If the strategy is platform licensing, this contradicts it.

Pre-mortem: The device fails because labels block wireless sharing due to piracy fears, rendering the key differentiator useless. The store fails because the catalog is inferior to the incumbent's due to partner retaliation for dropping copy-protection. The marketing budget burns without converting loyal users.

Evidence: The proposal admits "no clear price advantage" and "late to market." The claim that wireless sharing will work is marked `assumed` regarding label restrictions; the proposal itself cites this as a risk without mitigation. Dropping copy-protection contradicts the stated history of promoting it to partners, creating immediate channel friction.

Blockers:
1. The core differentiator (wireless sharing) relies on label approval, which the proposal explicitly flags as a risk with no mitigation plan. Without this, the device is just a late, expensive MP3 player.
2. Abandoning the copy-protection standard alienates existing retail and device partners, potentially blocking distribution channels needed to compete with the incumbent's reach.

Risks: High marketing spend with low conversion; ecosystem lock-in proves stronger than hardware tweaks.

The absence of a plan to secure label buy-in for sharing or to manage partner retaliation constitutes a failure of the core business logic. The risks are not merely executional; they are structural to the proposed approach.

DECISION: NO-GO
