First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines a premium price with a fragmented ecosystem and a niche hardware feature, ignoring the primary driver of smartphone adoption: app availability.

Steelman: A device leveraging unique 3D interaction and seamless shopping could create a high-margin niche for loyal customers, bypassing platform taxes.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Preserve capital; avoid entering a saturated two-horse race without leverage.
2. **Low-cost hardware:** Subsidize the device to drive retail volume, accepting hardware losses (contradicts "flagship price" premise).
3. **Proposed plan:** High price, exclusive carrier, forked OS, missing mainstream apps.

Fit: While aligning with retail integration, it contradicts the market reality that smartphones are general-purpose platforms. Users prioritize ecosystem over single-use features.

Pre-mortem: The device launches to initial curiosity but fails within 18 months. Developers ignore the tiny install base, refusing to port mainstream apps. The 3D feature is viewed as a gimmick. The high unsubsidized price makes it non-competitive against established flagships that offer full app stores. Carrier exclusivity caps total addressable market below critical mass.

Evidence: The proposal admits the OS is a fork with a smaller store and lacks leading mainstream apps. History shows that without parity in core utility (apps), hardware differentiation (3D, shopping button) cannot sustain a premium price point in a saturated market.

Blockers:
1. **Ecosystem Deficit:** Selling a flagship-priced phone without mainstream apps is a factual contradiction of user demand; the "app gap" is not a risk, it is a fatal flaw at this price tier.
2. **Price/Value Mismatch:** An unsubsidized flagship price requires flagship utility. A forked OS with limited apps provides sub-flagship utility. The math does not work.

Risks: Carrier exclusivity limiting reach; 3D technology failing to gain developer traction.

The absence of mainstream apps is a hard blocker for a premium consumer device. No amount of shopping integration compensates for the inability to run standard social or productivity tools expected at this price point. The plan requires a fundamental pivot to a loss-leader hardware strategy or a full OS license to succeed, neither of which is proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
