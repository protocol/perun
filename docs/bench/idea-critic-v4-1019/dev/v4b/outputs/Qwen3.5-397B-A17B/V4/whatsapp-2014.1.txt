First call: NO-GO. The price is 12x the prior valuation with no revenue path, violating basic capital discipline.

**Steelman:** Paying a historic premium is the only way to neutralize an existential mobile threat that internal efforts cannot stop fast enough.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Risk slow displacement by the messaging protocol; preserve cash.
2.  **Build/Buy Small:** Acquire a cheaper competitor or accelerate internal clones; lower cost, unproven scale.
3.  **Proposed Deal:** Immediate scale and user migration, but at a valuation requiring perfect execution and massive future monetization to justify.

**Fit:** Assumes the "north star" is retaining mobile relevance at any cost. If the core business is truly being displaced, fit is high; if displacement is overstated, this is panic.

**Pre-mortem:** Integration fails due to culture clash; founders leave immediately; privacy regulators block data sharing; the $1 fee alienates users if removed; the $19B never earns a return, becoming a permanent goodwill impairment. This is a one-way door; delay costs market share but saves $19B.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "no monetization plan" and "minimal revenue." The math fails: to justify $19B, the app needs billions in annual profit, yet it explicitly refuses ads and charges $1. The stated facts (refusal of ads, tiny team, huge price) contradict the claim of a sound financial investment.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Contradiction:** The target's explicit refusal of advertising and minimal revenue model makes the $19B price tag mathematically indefensible based on current facts. There is no bridge from $1/user/year to a $19B exit without changing the very product users love.
*   **Valuation Spike:** A 12x increase in one year without proportional revenue growth indicates a bubble, not a fundamental shift in value.

**Risks:** Founder retention, regulatory scrutiny, cultural integration, monetization failure.

The absence of a monetization strategy for an asset costing $19B is not a "risk" to be managed later; it is a current factual blocker. You cannot approve a spend this large on a hypothesis that contradicts the target's own operating principles.

DECISION: NO-GO
