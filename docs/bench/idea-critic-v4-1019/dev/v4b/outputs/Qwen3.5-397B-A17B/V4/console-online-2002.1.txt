First call: NO-GO. The proposal imposes high fixed costs (broadband, subscription) on a market with low infrastructure readiness and entrenched free expectations, creating an immediate adoption barrier without proven demand.

Steelman: A unified, premium online service creates a sticky ecosystem and superior user experience that fragmented, free alternatives cannot match, potentially locking in high-value users early.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoid operational risk but cede the online segment to rivals.
2. Free/Publisher-led model: Matches market expectations, lowers entry barrier, shifts hosting costs to publishers.
3. Hybrid launch: Free basic matchmaking/identity, paid premium features (voice, storage).

Fit: Assumes the goal is ecosystem lock-in via quality. However, the stated fact that "few homes have broadband" directly contradicts the requirement for broadband, severely limiting the addressable market at launch.

Pre-mortem: The service launches to a tiny user base due to broadband scarcity and subscription resistance. High fixed operational costs for hosting/support drain resources. The console is perceived as "expensive to play," damaging brand sentiment. Competitors capture the mass market with free, accessible options. This is a one-way door with high sunk costs; delay costs are low given the immature infrastructure.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits "few homes have broadband" and "players expect online play to be free." These are not risks; they are stated market realities that invalidate the core distribution model. The company lacks operational experience, adding execution risk to a flawed business case.

Blockers:
1. Infrastructure mismatch: Requiring broadband when "few homes have it" caps the potential user base below viability thresholds.
2. Pricing contradiction: Charging for a service the market expects to be free, without a proven value differential strong enough to overcome this friction.
3. Operational gap: No existing capability for the required hosting/support, increasing failure probability.

Risks: High churn, brand damage, financial loss from unused capacity.

The plan fails on its own stated facts. The infrastructure and pricing models are misaligned with 2002 market realities. A revised approach (e.g., free tier, publisher partnerships) is required before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
