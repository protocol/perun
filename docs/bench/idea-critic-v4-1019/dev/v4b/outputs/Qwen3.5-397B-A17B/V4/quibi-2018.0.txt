First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets $1B+ on unproven demand for premium short-form subscriptions amidst known fatigue and abundant free alternatives.

**Steelman:** A high-budget, mobile-native service with superior tech and A-list talent can carve a profitable niche in short-form video by offering quality that free platforms cannot match, justifying a subscription despite market saturation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Demand Mismatch:** The core claim assumes users will pay for short-form content when "free ad-supported short video... is abundant." Historically, short-form consumption is driven by algorithmic discovery and low friction, not premium curation. Subscription fatigue directly contradicts the willingness to add another fee for a format users already consume for free.
2.  **Economic Fragility:** High production budgets and A-list talent require massive scale to amortize. Short-form episodes (<10 mins) generate lower engagement time per session compared to long-form, making the cost-per-hour of entertainment prohibitively high compared to established long-form streamers or free ad-supported tiers.
3.  **Feature Moat:** "Video looking right vertically or horizontally" is a baseline expectation, not a differentiator worthy of a subscription premium. Competitors can replicate this instantly.
4.  **Competition:** The proposal admits risks are "price" and "competition for phone attention." With no proven demand, entering a red ocean against entrenched giants (who offer short-form for free) with a premium price point is financially reckless.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Avoids $1B loss; status quo remains.
*   *Ad-Supported Model:* Leverages existing behavior (free short-form) rather than fighting it; lowers barrier to entry.
*   *Long-Form Premium:* Proven willingness to pay exists here; short-form premium does not.

**Pre-mortem:** The service launches, acquires few subscribers due to price and fatigue, burns cash on A-list talent for low-retention content, and shuts down within 18 months as studios pull funding. The "tech feature" fails to retain users who leave due to lack of content volume or high cost.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Proven Demand:** The proposal explicitly states "no proven demand for premium short-form." Betting $1B on an unproven category while ignoring abundant free substitutes is a factual failure of the business case.
*   **Economic Contradiction:** High costs (A-list, high budget) vs. Low value perception (short-form, free alternatives available).

**Risks:** Execution difficulty, talent retention, rapid competitor copying of tech features.

The absence of validated demand for the specific monetization model (subscription) in this format, combined with explicit market headwinds (fatigue, free abundance), constitutes a fatal blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
