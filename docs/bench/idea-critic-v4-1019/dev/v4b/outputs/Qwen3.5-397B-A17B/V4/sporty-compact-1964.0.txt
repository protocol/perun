First call: GO. The demographic tailwind (postwar boom entering market) combined with an unmet desire (sporty styling at low price) creates a clear opportunity. Using existing mechanicals drastically reduces development cost and time, enabling a low base price while the options list protects margins.

**Steelman:** Leverage proven, cheap compact architecture to deliver a stylistically sporty vehicle at an entry-level price point, targeting a specific, growing demographic, using high-visibility marketing to define a new category before competitors react.

**Alternatives:**
1.  *Do nothing:* Cede the emerging youth segment to competitors; risk brand stagnation as the economy car remains perceived as "dull."
2.  *New platform:* Develop unique sports car mechanics. Result: Prohibitive cost, higher price point, missed demographic target, delayed launch.
3.  *Upgrade existing compact:* Add styling cues to the current dull model. Result: Fails to create the distinct "sporty" identity required to shift perception; likely insufficient to trigger trial.

**Fit:** Directly addresses the stated gap between "reliable but dull" and "sporty at low price." Aligns with capturing the high-volume baby boom cohort.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the "cheap parts" perception overwhelms the styling, making the car look like a patched-up economy model rather than a genuine sports car. The heavy ad spend is wasted if the product visual identity doesn't instantly communicate "sport." However, the long hood/short deck proportion is a specific, low-cost styling cue that historically signals performance, mitigating the "cheap" risk.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on stated demographic facts (boom generation aging) and known mechanical reuse strategies. The risk of "unproven demand" is inherent to any new category but is mitigated by the low capital exposure (shared parts) and the ability to test via the options list.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (unproven demand, cheap perception, ad spend) are execution risks, not factual blockers. The shared parts are a feature (low cost), not a bug, provided the styling successfully masks their origin. The kill criterion is clear: if initial styling clinics show the car is perceived as "cheap economy" rather than "affordable sport," pivot the design before tooling.

**Risks:** Marketing miss (fails to define category); styling execution (looks too much like the donor car). These are managed by the proposed large ad campaign and specific styling directives.

DECISION: GO
