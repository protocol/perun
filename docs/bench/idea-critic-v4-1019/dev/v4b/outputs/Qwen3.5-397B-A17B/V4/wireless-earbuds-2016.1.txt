First call: GO. The removal of the headphone jack creates an immediate, forced market for a wireless alternative; delaying this launch cedes the newly created demand to third parties.

**Steelman:** Launching a proprietary, seamlessly pairing wireless solution at $159 captures the entire installed base forced into wireless by the jack removal, turning a customer pain point into a high-margin revenue stream while establishing a new product category.

**Attack & Analysis:**
*   **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" leaves customers with no first-party audio solution, driving them to competitors or cheap third-party dongles, eroding ecosystem loyalty. A cheaper wired alternative contradicts the strategic move to remove the jack.
*   **Fit:** This aligns with a vision of a port-less, seamless future. The "odd look" is a temporary adoption hurdle outweighed by the convenience of automatic pairing.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if battery anxiety (5 hours) or loss frequency triggers mass returns. However, the charging case mitigates battery concerns, and the high price anchors the product as premium rather than disposable.
*   **Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the jack removal *will* anger customers. This anger is the catalyst. Without a ready, high-quality replacement, that anger becomes churn. With this product, it becomes a forced upgrade cycle.
*   **Blockers:** None. The risks (ridicule, loss, price, battery) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The price is high, but justified by the monopoly on the user's immediate need. The battery life is standard for the era.
*   **Risks:** Public ridicule of the design; high attachment loss rate; price resistance compared to "free" wired ears. These are managed by marketing the convenience and the case solution.

The absence of a pilot is not a blocker; the "pilot" is the phone launch itself. The kill criterion is clear: if attach rate falls below 15% in Q1, reconsider pricing or bundle strategy. The leading metric is the attach rate of earbuds to new phone units sold.

DECISION: GO
