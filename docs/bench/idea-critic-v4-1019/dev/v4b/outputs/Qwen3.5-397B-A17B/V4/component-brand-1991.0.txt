First call: GO. The proposal addresses a critical vulnerability: commoditization. If buyers cannot distinguish the component, price becomes the only differentiator, inviting clone makers to erode margins. The "Intel Inside" model (implied by the 1991 context and description) shifts power from the computer maker to the component supplier by creating end-user pull.

Steelman: By subsidizing partners' ads only when they feature the chip's logo, the company aligns incentives, turning reluctant computer makers into distribution channels for brand equity while training consumers to demand a specific, premium component.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing: Accept margin compression as clones proliferate; high risk of long-term irrelevance.
2. Price cuts: Triggers a race to the bottom with clone makers; destroys value immediately.
3. Direct-to-consumer sales: Logistically impossible given the current B2B-only channel structure.

The proposed path is the only viable mechanism to decouple the product from pure price competition.

Pre-mortem: The campaign fails if computer makers universally reject the co-op terms, viewing them as intrusive. However, the financial incentive (subsidized advertising) is strong enough to overcome resentment for most partners seeking to lower their own marketing costs. The risk of "unprecedented" consumer advertising is mitigated by the fact that the cost is shared and the message is simple: "Ask for this chip."

Evidence check: The proposal relies on the assumption that consumers care about internal components once educated. While unproven in this specific sector, the mechanism of creating brand preference for an invisible ingredient is sound logic against the stated threat of clones. The budget share is high, but the cost of inaction (total commoditization) is higher.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (resentment, unprecedented nature, budget share) are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. They are managed by the financial leverage of the co-op program.
Risks: Partner pushback, slow consumer adoption. These are monitored via quarterly sell-through data and partner participation rates.

Kill criteria: If after 12 months, consumer awareness remains below 20% or partner participation drops below 50%, pivot to trade-only incentives. Leading metric: Percentage of partner ads carrying the logo.

DECISION: GO
