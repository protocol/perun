First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines maximum engineering risk (full rewrite) with maximum deployment risk (big-bang migration) while alienating the core user base that drives traffic, all without a safety net.

Steelman: A complete rewrite is necessary to modernize the architecture and pivot toward publisher content to reverse traffic decline, requiring an immediate, total switchover to realize benefits.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retains community goodwill but accepts slowing traffic; reversible.
2. Iterative refactor: Update surfacing algorithms on the existing stack; lower risk, reversible.
3. Staged rollout: Launch new design to a small segment first; allows rollback and learning.

Fit: Assuming the goal is traffic growth, alienating the "loyal community" via mechanic changes contradicts the asset needed to achieve it.

Pre-mortem: The new voting mechanics anger power users, causing a mass exodus. Simultaneously, the rewrite introduces critical bugs. Because the migration is "all at once," there is no rollback path. Traffic collapses, and the rival site absorbs the displaced community permanently.

Evidence: The proposal admits the community values existing mechanics yet plans to change them. It acknowledges the engineering risk but proceeds with a full rewrite. It explicitly states "no rollback."

Blockers:
1. No rollback capability for a "large engineering risk" deployment violates basic operational safety.
2. Changing the core mechanic valued by the loyal community without testing guarantees churn among the primary user base.
3. The "all at once" approach prevents validating the "publisher favor" hypothesis before total commitment.

Risks: Rival growth accelerates during the transition; rewrite delays extend the traffic slump.

Decision: The lack of a rollback plan for a high-risk rewrite targeting the core value proposition is a fatal flaw. The plan must be revised to include a staged rollout, a fallback mechanism, and validation of the new mechanics with a subset of users before full migration.

DECISION: NO-GO
