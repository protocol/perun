First call: NO-GO. The price is astronomical for an asset with zero revenue, active legal threats, and unproven economics.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant cultural platform immediately secures the future of video search and distribution, neutralizing a competitor before they monetize.

**Options:**
1. **Buy:** Pays $1.65B for massive liability and operating losses; bets on ad-model viability.
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoids immediate cash burn and litigation; risks ceding the video market long-term.
3. **Build/Organic:** Leverages existing infrastructure; slower growth but avoids copyright debt.
Buying fails to beat "Do Nothing" on financial facts; it only wins on a speculative strategic monopoly.

**Fit:** Assumes video is the core future of search (plausible), but the cost threatens the entire company's stability if the bet fails.

**Pre-mortem:** Media lawsuits force the platform offline or extract settlement costs exceeding the purchase price. Bandwidth costs scale linearly while ad revenue lags, creating a bottomless pit. The "stock" payment dilutes existing shareholders massively for a company that might be worthless in two years. This is a one-way door; once bought, the liability and culture cannot be easily shed.

**Evidence & Math:** The target has *no meaningful revenue*. At $1.65B, the price-to-sales ratio is undefined/infinite. Bandwidth bills are "high" with no offsetting income. The "copyrighted clips" are not an asset but a pending lawsuit trigger. The buyer's own smaller service proves the technology is replicable; the only unique asset is the user base, which is fickle and legally toxic.

**Blockers:**
1. **Financial Suicide:** Paying a premium valuation for negative cash flow and undefined revenue violates basic acquisition logic.
2. **Existential Legal Risk:** The core content library is primarily infringing material flagged by rights holders; this is not a "risk" but a confirmed liability waiting for judgment.
3. **Unproven Unit Economics:** No evidence exists that video ads can cover bandwidth costs, let alone justify the acquisition premium.

**Risks:** Culture clash, technical scaling failure, advertiser boycotts.

**Decision:** The math does not work. The legal exposure is immediate and potentially fatal. The revenue model is a hypothesis, not a fact. No kill criteria can save a deal where the foundational assets are illegal and the price is detached from reality.

DECISION: NO-GO
