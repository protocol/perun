First call: GO. The proposal addresses a critical vulnerability: commoditization. If buyers cannot distinguish the product, price becomes the only differentiator, inviting clone makers to erode margins. The "Intel Inside" model (implied by the 1991 context and description) shifts power from the computer maker to the component supplier by creating end-user pull.

**Steelman:** By co-funding ads that feature a specific logo, the chip maker bypasses the computer manufacturer's indifference, forcing brand recognition at the point of sale and making the component a primary purchase criterion.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Status quo leads to price wars with clones. Market share may remain, but profitability collapses.
2.  **Trade Discounts:** Lowering prices for manufacturers improves short-term volume but reinforces the "invisible part" mentality and hurts margins without building brand equity.
3.  **Direct Consumer Education:** Too slow and disconnected from the purchase moment compared to embedding the message in existing manufacturer ads.

**Pre-mortem:** The campaign fails if computer makers refuse to display the logo or if consumers ignore the mark. However, the financial incentive (subsidized advertising) aligns interests: manufacturers get cheaper media buys; the chip maker gets branding. Resentment is unlikely to outweigh the economic benefit of reduced ad spend.

**Evidence & Logic:** The proposal correctly identifies that "buyers do not know which chip is in their computer." This information asymmetry is the root cause of the threat. Breaking this asymmetry is the only path to pricing power. The risk of "unprecedented" action is the price of leadership; waiting for a precedent ensures followership in a dying margin structure.

**Blockers:** None. The facts presented show a clear causal link between consumer ignorance and competitive threat. The proposed solution directly attacks the root cause with a mechanism (co-op advertising) that incentivizes the necessary behavior change in partners.

**Risks:** High budget consumption is a feature, not a bug; it creates a barrier to entry for clones who cannot match the brand noise. Sales lag is expected in brand building but is preferable to immediate margin erosion.

**Kill Criteria:** If, after two quarters, consumer awareness surveys show no lift in logo recognition among laptop shoppers, or if major partners opt out despite subsidies, the program must pivot.

DECISION: GO
