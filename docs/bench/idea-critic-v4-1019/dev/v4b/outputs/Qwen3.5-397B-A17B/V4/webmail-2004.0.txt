First call: GO. The proposal trades a rapidly commoditizing asset (storage) for a defensible moat (search) and a scalable revenue model (contextual ads), directly attacking the primary pain point of incumbents (storage scarcity).

**Steelman:** By offering 1,000x the standard storage, the service renders manual deletion obsolete, positioning "search" as the primary interaction model rather than "file management." This creates a sticky ecosystem where ad relevance improves with usage, funding the infrastructure before direct monetization is required.

**Alternatives:**
1.  *Do nothing:* Cede the search-based communication layer to competitors; remain solely a search engine with limited user retention.
2.  *Paid tier only:* Fails to achieve the network effect and data volume necessary to train superior ad-matching algorithms.
3.  *Standard storage:* Indistinguishable from entrenched incumbents; guarantees failure in a mature market.

**Fit:** Assumes the strategic north star is organizing world information; email is the most structured, high-value personal dataset available.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if privacy backlash over content scanning triggers regulatory bans or mass user exodus, or if storage costs decline slower than projected, making the per-user unit economics unsustainable. However, these are risks, not blockers. The "invitation-only" launch acts as a natural throttle to manage infrastructure load and create scarcity-driven demand.

**Evidence & Math:** Storage costs are falling exponentially (Moore's Law equivalent for drives), while ad CPMs for high-intent text contexts remain premium. The math holds: even at low fill rates, the lifetime value of a user with full historical data exceeds the marginal cost of gigabyte-scale storage in 2004.

**Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal; the costs are real but manageable via the ad model, and the market maturity is precisely why a step-change (1GB vs MB) is the only viable entry strategy.

**Risks:** Privacy perception, ad-relevance accuracy, infrastructure scaling speed. These become kill criteria: if privacy complaints exceed a specific threshold or ad yield fails to cover 80% of marginal storage costs within 12 months, the model must pivot.

**Kill Criteria:** Pause expansion if cost-per-user exceeds ad-revenue-per-user by >20% after the first 100k users, or if legal injunctions halt content scanning in two major jurisdictions.

DECISION: GO
