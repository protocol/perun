First call: NO-GO. The plan trades a functional product for an emotional asset based on incomplete data.

**Steelman:** Replacing the formula maximizes immediate taste preference scores, which are the primary driver of repeat purchases in the blind-test environment.

**Attack:** The evidence is fatally flawed. Blind taste tests isolate flavor from brand identity, measuring only immediate sensory gratification (sweeter is often preferred in sips) while ignoring the emotional utility of the brand. The proposal admits testing did not disclose the removal of the original; this is a critical omission. Consumers may prefer the rival in a sip test but reject the loss of their heritage brand in a real-world purchase scenario. The "sweeter" win likely reflects a "sip test" bias, not "case buy" behavior.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Retain share erosion but preserve brand equity and loyalist revenue.
2.  **Line Extension:** Launch the sweeter formula as a new variant (e.g., "New Coke") while keeping the original. This captures the taste-preference segment without alienating the core base.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** High risk of total brand rebellion.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch triggers a public backlash. Loyalists feel betrayed, not consulted. The narrative shifts from "taste" to "corporate arrogance." Sales plummet not because the drink tastes bad, but because the *original* is gone. Bottlers face riots.

**Blocker:** The testing methodology explicitly excluded the core variable: the emotional reaction to discontinuation. A plan to delete a century-old cultural artifact cannot rely on data that hides the deletion. The risk of "emotional attachment" is not a speculation; it is a known variable that the current data fails to quantify. Without a fallback or a test that includes the "loss" variable, the financial exposure is unbounded.

**Risks:** Irreversible brand damage, bottler revolt, permanent loss of market share to rivals who position themselves as the "original."

**Kill Criteria:** If a blind test cannot simulate the purchase decision (which includes brand loyalty), it is invalid for a replacement strategy. The absence of a "keep both" option in the proposal is a strategic failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
