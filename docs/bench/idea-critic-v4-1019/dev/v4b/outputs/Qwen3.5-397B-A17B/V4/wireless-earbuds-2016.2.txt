First call: GO. Removing the jack forces a wireless future; owning the chip and case solves pairing and battery anxiety, creating a new high-margin category despite price shock.

Steelman: Trading short-term customer irritation for long-term ecosystem lock-in and accessory revenue by making wireless the only convenient option.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Keep the jack, miss the wireless shift, remain commoditized.
2. Bundle cheap wireless: Lowers margin, fails to establish premium perception.
3. Proposed launch: High risk, but captures maximum upside and defines the standard.
The proposal beats "do nothing" because wired audio is a dying differentiator; it beats cheap bundling by establishing a premium profit center.

Fit: Assumes a strategy of vertical integration and ecosystem control. If the north star is user convenience via seamless connectivity, this fits perfectly.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if battery life degrades rapidly or pairing reliability fractures trust, causing users to cling to adapters forever. This is a one-way door for hardware design; delaying cedes the "wireless future" narrative to competitors. Cost of delay is losing the chance to frame the removal of the jack as an innovation rather than a cost-cut.

Evidence: The proposal admits risks (price, loss, battery) but offers a proprietary technical solution (own chip) for the critical friction point: pairing. The $159 price is high but justifiable against the "free" wired alternative only if the wireless experience is demonstrably superior, which the custom chip enables.

Blockers: NONE. No fact presented proves the plan fails; the risks are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The anger over jack removal is mitigated only by making the alternative (wireless) irresistible, which this product attempts.

Risks: Consumer ridicule, loss frequency, battery anxiety. These become kill criteria: if return rates exceed 15% due to fit/loss, or if pairing fails >5% of the time, the product must be re-engineered or bundled differently in the next revision.

Leading metric: Attachment rate (earbuds sold per phone) within the first quarter. Review date: 90 days post-launch.

DECISION: GO
