First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to high-volume production and a premium price point despite unverified legal status and unproven consumer demand.

**Steelman:** A breakthrough personal transporter with strong backing can create a new urban mobility category if launched decisively at scale to capture early adopters before regulations solidify.

**Attack:**
1.  **Legal Blocker:** The proposal admits legal status on sidewalks and roads is "unclear." Selling 40,000 units of a vehicle that may be illegal to operate in major markets is a fundamental failure mode. If cities ban them post-launch, inventory becomes worthless, and liability exposure is massive.
2.  **Demand Mismatch:** The price ($5,000) is prohibitive for mass adoption, yet the factory is sized for 40,000 units/year. This assumes institutional buyers will absorb volume despite "unproven" use cases beyond them. High visibility ("conspicuous") and low utility ("cannot carry much") further restrict the addressable market to niche enthusiasts, unlikely to sustain the proposed volume.
3.  **Better Way:** A staged launch is superior. Produce a limited batch (e.g., 2,000 units) to test regulatory reception and actual usage patterns. This reduces capital risk and allows pivoting based on real-world legal rulings and user feedback before committing to a 40,000-unit capacity.

**Pre-mortem:** One year later, major cities have banned the device from sidewalks due to safety concerns, and consumers refuse to pay $5,000 for a gadget they cannot legally ride. The company is stuck with 30,000 unsold units and massive fixed costs from the oversized factory.

**Blockers:**
*   **Legal Uncertainty:** The core utility depends on public right-of-way access, which is currently undefined. Scaling production before resolving this is financially reckless.
*   **Volume/Demand Disconnect:** The proposed production volume assumes a market size that contradicts the high price and limited utility described.

**Risks:** Social stigma, limited cargo capacity, high price sensitivity. These are secondary to the legal and volume blockers.

**Conclusion:** The plan fails the basic test of product-market-fit verification and regulatory clearance. Committing to 40,000 units/year before resolving whether the device can legally be ridden is not an operational option. The proposal must be revised to include a pilot program and regulatory strategy before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
