First call: GO. The strategic logic of owning the customer relationship and data outweighs the short-term loss of licensing revenue in a shifting distribution landscape.

Steelman: Direct-to-consumer distribution is essential to capture full lifetime value and control destiny, even if it requires subsidizing growth with lost licensing fees and operating losses for several years.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Retain licensing revenue but cede customer ownership and pricing power to rivals, risking long-term irrelevance.
2. Partner/JV: Share risk but dilute control and brand identity; unlikely to achieve the same scale or data integration.
3. Proposed path: High cash burn and execution risk, but offers the highest ceiling for valuation and strategic autonomy.

Fit: Aligns with the industry shift from linear/cable to on-demand streaming. Assumes the content library is strong enough to drive initial sign-ups.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if content depth is insufficient to retain users after the initial hype, or if the technology platform suffers outages that drive churn. The door is one-way regarding the lost licensing contracts; once pulled, they are hard to restore at previous rates. Delay costs market share to entrenched competitors.

Evidence: Based on stated facts: billions in current licensing, target of tens of millions vs. competitor's hundreds of millions, and explicit acceptance of multi-year losses. No external data available to verify technical capability or specific churn projections.

Blockers: NONE. The proposal explicitly acknowledges the primary risks (cash burn, execution, cannibalization) and accepts them as the cost of entry. The financial model (funding losses) is stated as a feature, not a bug.

Risks: Execution failure on the tech platform; slower-than-expected subscriber growth extending the loss period; inability to replace licensing revenue with subscription revenue in time.

Kill criteria: If subscriber growth falls below 50% of the targeted trajectory within the first 18 months, or if the technology platform experiences >4 hours of cumulative downtime in the first year, the strategy must be pivoted to a hybrid licensing model. Leading metric: Net subscriber additions per quarter.

The plan survives the attack because it correctly identifies that the greater risk is inaction. The "blocker" of high cost is internalized in the strategy.

DECISION: GO
