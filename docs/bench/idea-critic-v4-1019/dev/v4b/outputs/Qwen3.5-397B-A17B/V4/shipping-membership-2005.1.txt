First call: GO. The friction of per-order fees directly suppresses repeat purchase frequency, which is the primary lever for lifetime value in retail.

**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee creates a psychological "sunk cost" for the customer, incentivizing them to consolidate all spending with this retailer to maximize the membership's value, thereby locking out competitors and increasing order frequency enough to offset shipping losses through higher gross merchandise volume and improved unit economics on fulfillment.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue losing repeat customers to fee friction; status quo margin safety but stagnant growth.
2.  **Free shipping threshold:** Requires larger basket sizes, potentially deterring small, frequent needs; less effective at locking in loyalty than an upfront commitment.
3.  **Membership (Proposed):** High upfront risk, but creates an immediate cash infusion ($79/user) and the strongest behavioral lock-in.

**Fit:** Assumes the strategic north star is market share dominance and lifetime value over immediate quarterly net margin. This aligns with a growth-stage mindset.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the increase in order frequency among heavy buyers is insufficient to cover the marginal cost of shipping, turning every loyal customer into a loss leader. However, the $79 upfront payment acts as a buffer and immediate working capital. This is a two-way door; while hard to revoke for existing members, the program can be paused for new sign-ups or the fee adjusted annually. The cost of delay is ceding the "loyalty" mindshare to future competitors.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits economics are unproven, but the mechanism (subscription model reducing friction) is sound in theory. The risk of margin hit is real but is the cost of buying dominance.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (margin hit, unproven economics) are inherent to the strategy, not factual contradictions preventing execution. They are risks to be managed, not blockers to starting.
**Risks:** Heavy buyers utilizing the service beyond the $79 break-even point immediately; operational strain from increased volume.

**Kill Criteria:** If the average shipping cost per member exceeds the $79 fee plus the incremental gross profit from increased order frequency within the first 12 months, the program must be restructured or fees increased. Leading metric: Repeat purchase frequency of members vs. non-members.

DECISION: GO
