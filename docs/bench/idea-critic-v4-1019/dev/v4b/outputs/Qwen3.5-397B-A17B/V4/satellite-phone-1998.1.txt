First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and market trajectory contradict the core claim of viability.

**Steelman:** A premium, niche service for global travelers who value ubiquitous coverage over cost and device size, capturing early adopters before ground networks saturate remote regions.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Shrinkage:** The primary risk is not hypothetical; ground cellular expansion is an observable, accelerating trend in 1998. The target "international business traveler" increasingly finds coverage in major hubs, eroding the unique value proposition precisely where high-volume usage occurs.
2.  **Price vs. Debt:** $5 billion in debt requires massive, immediate scale. A $3,000 handset plus high per-minute rates restricts the addressable market to a tiny fraction of travelers. The math fails: the niche is too small to service the debt load within the required timeframe.
3.  **Product Deficits:** Bulky, indoor-incompatible hardware is a non-starter for a premium business tool. Executives will not carry a brick that fails inside airports or offices.
4.  **Channel Readiness:** Launching without ready sales channels guarantees a slow ramp-up, directly conflicting with the urgent need for cash flow to service borrowed capital.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing/Wait:** Avoid sinking further capital into a misaligned model; let ground networks mature or pivot to government/defense contracts where price sensitivity is lower.
*   **Pivot:** Target maritime, aviation, or government sectors exclusively, bypassing the consumer handset limitations and price sensitivity.

**Pre-mortem:** The company launches, misses subscriber targets by 40% in year one due to high costs and poor indoor performance, cannot service debt, and declares bankruptcy within two years while ground networks achieve near-global coverage.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Mismatch:** Debt service requirements exceed the realistic revenue cap of the ultra-premium niche defined by the $3,000 price point.
*   **Product-Market Fit:** Hardware limitations (indoor failure) disqualify the product for the stated target demographic (business travelers).

**Risks:** Rapid ground network expansion rendering the service obsolete before profitability.

**Decision:** The proposal relies on a shrinking window of opportunity and a market size insufficient to cover its capital structure. The hardware flaws further invalidate the business case for the primary demographic.

DECISION: NO-GO
