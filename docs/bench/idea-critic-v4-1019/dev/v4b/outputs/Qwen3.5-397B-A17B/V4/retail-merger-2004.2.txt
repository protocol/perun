First call: NO-GO. Two declining businesses with different customers and uncertain synergies do not create value; they compound distress.

**Steelman:** The acquisition creates immediate scale and cross-selling opportunities that stabilize the restructured chain before rivals dominate the market.

**Attack:**
1.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves cash for modernizing existing stores. Buying a larger, failing competitor consumes the restructuring gains on an asset with deteriorating fundamentals. The "cheaper alternative" is organic growth or smaller tuck-ins, not a massive merger of weak entities.
2.  **Fit:** A post-restructuring strategy requires stability and proof of concept, not high-leverage expansion into a different customer segment with old infrastructure.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The merger fails because integration costs exceed uncertain savings, the distinct customer bases reject the combined format, and cash flow collapses under the weight of two declining top lines while big-box rivals capture the remainder.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits declining sales for *both* parties, underinvested stores, uncertain savings, and customer mismatch. These are not risks to manage; they are factual blockers proving the core thesis (growth via combination) is false.
5.  **Blockers:** The stated fact that "customers differ" invalidates the cross-selling revenue model. The stated fact that "cost savings are uncertain" removes the only logical profit lever. Acquiring a larger, declining entity with weak assets violates basic turnaround logic.

**Blockers:**
-   Revenue synergy is impossible as customer bases differ.
-   Profit synergy is unproven ("uncertain") while costs are guaranteed.
-   Both entities have negative momentum; combining them accelerates cash burn.

**Risks:**
-   Integration distraction during fragile post-restructuring phase.
-   Capital misallocation away from store modernization.

This is a "greater fool" strategy relying on financial engineering rather than operational improvement. The proposal offers no evidence that the combined entity can reverse the decline of either chain. It bets on uncertain savings to offset guaranteed revenue mismatches.

DECISION: NO-GO
