First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a highly profitable monopoly to voluntarily dilute its revenue with an inferior, costly product while inviting well-funded giants into its market, violating the core operator instinct to protect the cash cow until the substitute is superior.

Steelman: Launching a free, limited streaming service now acts as a defensive "learning lab" to retain subscribers and gather data before competitors force obsolescence, treating the DVD business as a finite runway rather than a permanent asset.

Alternatives:
1. **Do Nothing:** Maximize short-term DVD profits but risk total irrelevance if broadband adoption accelerates faster than expected.
2. **Paid Tier:** Launch streaming as a premium add-on to test willingness-to-pay and offset licensing costs without cannibalizing the base.
3. **Delay:** Wait for broadband penetration to reach a critical mass and video quality to improve before investing heavily.

Fit: This moves the north star from "maximizing current rental profit" to "surviving the format shift," but the proposed method (free, small catalog) undermines the value proposition.

Pre-mortem: The initiative fails because high licensing costs drain cash while the poor catalog drives users to new entrants who offer better selection and quality, accelerating DVD cancellations without securing digital loyalty. This is a two-way door (can shut down streaming), but the cost of delay is low given the current technical limitations.

Evidence: The proposal admits the catalog is small, quality is poor, and broadband is not universal. These are factual blockers to user adoption. Licensing costs are stated as high, creating negative unit economics.

Blockers:
1. **Negative Unit Economics:** High licensing costs + no extra revenue = immediate margin destruction on every stream.
2. **Inferior Product:** Poor quality and small catalog cannot compete with expected entrants, making the "defensive" move ineffective.
3. **Cannibalization without Migration:** Users may cancel DVD plans due to perceived digital availability but churn entirely when the streaming experience disappoints.

Risks: Accelerated decline of the core business; brand damage from poor video quality.

The plan fails the operator test: it spends scarce capital on a product that is objectively worse than what competitors will soon offer, destroying profitability without securing a foothold. A paid pilot or a delay until technology matures is required.

DECISION: NO-GO
