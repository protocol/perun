First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a high-risk, irreversible brand change with zero validation, ignoring explicit risks regarding customer attachment and backlash potential.

**Steelman:** A simultaneous, unannounced global rebrand maximizes shock value to signal a definitive break from the past, forcing immediate market recognition of the "contemporary" shift.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves current equity. A "staged pilot" (e.g., digital-only first) tests reception with minimal downside. The proposal rejects these, demanding an "all-at-once" rollout that eliminates any chance of course correction.
2.  **Fit:** While the goal (looking contemporary) is valid, the method contradicts basic brand stewardship. Alienating the core loyal base to chase a vague "contemporary" aesthetic without testing is speculative gambling, not strategy.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Loyal customers interpret the unannounced change as erasure of heritage, triggering immediate social media backlash. The brand loses its primary differentiator (the blue square) before proving the new identity has value. Recovery is impossible because the rollout was total; there is no "old" state to retreat to quietly.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the design was "not tested with customers." In 2010, social media velocity makes untested, unannounced changes catastrophic. The risks raised (backlash, loss of recognition) are not hypothetical; they are direct consequences of the proposed execution.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The design is untested.
*   **Fact:** The rollout is "all at once" with "no advance announcement."
*   **Contradiction:** The plan seeks to strengthen the brand while intentionally removing its most recognized asset (the blue square) without verifying if the new asset works. This guarantees the stated risk of alienating loyal customers.

**Risks:** Social media backlash, loss of brand equity, confusion in channel partners. These are not mere risks here; they are near-certainties given the lack of testing and the aggressive rollout style.

**Kill Criteria:** Any rebrand must include customer validation before full deployment. A "big bang" unannounced launch fails this basic safety check. The absence of a pilot or testing phase is a fatal flaw in the plan itself, not a missing detail to add later. The cost of delay (waiting to test) is negligible compared to the cost of a failed global launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
