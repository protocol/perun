First call: GO. The shift from desktop to web, combined with satellite imagery and routing, addresses a massive latent demand for accessible, visual navigation that desktop-bound software cannot satisfy.

**Steelman:** A free, browser-based mapping platform with satellite views and routing will become the global standard for location services by removing friction (downloads) and offering unprecedented data utility, creating a defensible moat through user scale and data accumulation despite high initial licensing costs.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Retain desktop niche; eventual obsolescence as web capabilities grow. High risk of irrelevance.
2.  **License only:** Sell data to existing portals. Low margin, no brand control, dependent on others' UX.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** High upfront cost and technical risk (browser performance), but offers total ownership of the user relationship, data flywheel, and platform potential. It beats "do nothing" by capturing the emerging web-first behavior.

**Fit:** Aligns with the acquirer's vision of organizing the world's information and making it universally accessible. Assumes browser performance will improve rapidly (a safe bet given historical trends).

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if browser rendering remains too slow for smooth panning/zooming, or if licensing costs bankrupt the project before ad revenue scales. However, these are execution hurdles, not fundamental flaws. The "one-way door" is significant capital expenditure, but the cost of delay is losing the category to competitors who might attempt similar feats.

**Evidence:** The acquisition itself proves the value of the underlying map data. The lack of a direct revenue model is a known pattern for platform plays (monetize later via ads/local business). Browser limitations are temporary; the strategic advantage of being first-to-scale with satellite imagery outweighs current technical constraints.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented proves the plan fails as proposed; risks are manageable execution challenges.
**Risks:** High burn rate before monetization; technical lag in browser graphics; competitive response. These become kill criteria: if user growth stalls after 12 months despite marketing, or if licensing costs exceed 200% of projections without path to ad coverage, pivot or halt.

DECISION: GO
