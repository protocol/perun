First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on unverified consumer acceptance and ignores immediate regulatory hostility toward health implications.

**Steelman:** A heated tobacco product offers reduced smoke and potential harm reduction compared to combustion, justifying a launch to capture health-conscious smokers despite higher complexity.

**Attack:**
1.  **Consumer Fit:** The proposal admits smokers may dislike the taste and lighting mechanism. Unlike a "better way," this introduces friction (complexity) without a guaranteed superior sensory experience. If the core ritual is altered negatively, adoption will fail regardless of "cleaner" marketing.
2.  **Regulatory Blocker:** Promoting it as a "cleaner way to smoke" in 1988 invites immediate regulatory scrutiny. Authorities are likely to reject implied health claims without longitudinal data, potentially banning the marketing angle entirely or delaying launch indefinitely.
3.  **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** The "very large sum" already spent is irrelevant to future viability. Launching a flawed product to recoup costs risks greater reputational and financial damage if it fails publicly.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital and avoids regulatory battle until taste/mechanics are proven. A limited technical pilot (without health claims) is safer than a wide promotional launch.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches widely; smokers reject the taste and complicated lighting; regulators sue over "cleaner" claims; the brand suffers a high-profile failure, wasting the remaining budget.

**Evidence Check:** The proposal states risks (taste, complexity, regulation) as possibilities but proceeds anyway. These are not mere risks; they are fundamental flaws in the value proposition and go-to-market strategy. The claim that it can be "promoted widely" contradicts the likelihood of regulatory objection to health implications.

**Blockers:**
*   **Regulatory:** Promoting health benefits ("cleaner") without approved data is a legal blocker in most jurisdictions.
*   **Product-Market Fit:** Acknowledged potential for consumer rejection of taste and usage mechanics invalidates a "wide launch."

**Risks:** High development costs (sunk), brand damage, competitor response.

**Conclusion:** The plan fails the basic test of selling a product users might hate via channels that may be illegal. The sunk cost does not justify the launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
