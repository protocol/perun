First call: NO-GO. The plan trades high-trust utility (email) for low-trust social engagement using coercion rather than consent, violating the core value proposition of the service.

**Steelman:** Leveraging the massive existing user base and default visibility creates instant network effects, bypassing the cold-start problem that kills most social networks, while integrating communication directly into the daily workflow.

**Attack & Options:**
The claim relies on automatic enrollment to succeed. However, email is a private utility; users expect strict boundaries. Forced socialization treats user trust as expendable inventory.
*   **Do Nothing:** Maintain high trust and regulatory safety; forego social upsell.
*   **Opt-In Launch:** Slower growth, but respects user agency and reduces regulatory risk.
*   **Proposed (Auto-Enroll):** Maximizes initial metrics but invites immediate backlash, regulatory scrutiny, and brand erosion.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
If this fails (likely scenario), it is because users feel violated by the exposure of their contact graphs. Unlike a two-way door, exposing private connections creates a "privacy debt" that cannot be fully repaid by later adding opt-out switches. The cost of delay is low; the cost of a trust breach is existential.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal admits risks regarding privacy disregard and regulatory attention but proceeds anyway. This is a fatal flaw. A product strategy that explicitly anticipates user alienation and regulatory hostility as "risks" rather than "blockers" is fundamentally unsound. The "hard to reverse" nature of exposed connections confirms this is a one-way door with negative expected value. The assumption that users want a social network inside their inbox is unproven and contradicted by the distinct mental models of "communication" vs. "broadcasting."

**Blockers:**
1.  **Trust Violation:** Automatic enrollment with visible connections contradicts the privacy expectations of an email service, creating an immediate reputational liability.
2.  **Regulatory Exposure:** The plan invites antitrust and privacy investigations by leveraging dominance in one market (search/mail) to force adoption in another (social).

**Risks:** Low adoption, brand damage, user churn.

**Kill Criteria:** If user opt-out rates exceed 20% in week one, or if any regulatory body issues a formal inquiry, the project must terminate immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
