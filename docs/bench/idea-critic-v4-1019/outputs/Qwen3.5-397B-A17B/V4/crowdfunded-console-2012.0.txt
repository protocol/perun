First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on unverified assumptions about developer adoption and hardware execution without a credible path to overcome the "chicken-and-egg" problem of platform viability.

**Steelman:** A crowdfunded, open-software console can succeed by targeting a niche underserved by major players, leveraging community enthusiasm to bootstrap game development and mitigate marketing costs, while accepting hardware delays as a manageable risk.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** High risk of failure due to lack of developer interest and hardware inexperience; competes directly with entrenched giants.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids capital loss; team remains small but safe.
3.  **Software-First Alternative:** Build the open software platform for existing hardware (PC/Raspberry Pi) to prove developer traction before committing to custom silicon. This reaches the same upside (community platform) with negligible hardware risk and lower cost.

**Fit:** Assumes the goal is "successful consumer hardware launch." If the north star is "viable open gaming ecosystem," the hardware-first approach misaligns by putting the cart before the horse.

**Pre-mortem:** The campaign succeeds in funding, but the team misses the one-year ship date due to supply chain issues. Backers demand refunds. Developers, seeing the delay and small install base, refuse to port games. The device launches with no software, becoming a brick.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "hardware delays are common" and "team has no marketing budget" yet plans a complex hardware launch. No evidence is provided that developers want this specific platform over existing ones. The risk of "developers may not build games" is treated as a possibility, not a fatal flaw, despite being the primary determinant of console success.

**Blockers:**
*   **Chicken-and-Egg:** No mechanism to secure game developers before hardware exists; stated risk "developers may not build games" directly contradicts the requirement for a viable platform.
*   **Execution Gap:** Team admits "hardware delays are common" but sets a hard "within a year" deadline with no buffer or experienced hardware partners cited.

**Risks:** Competition from phones/consoles; zero marketing budget.

**Decision:** The cheaper, safer alternative (software-first on existing hardware) reaches the same upside without the fatal hardware execution risk. The current plan fails the blocker test on developer adoption and timeline feasibility.

DECISION: NO-GO
