First call: GO. The market shift toward small computers is an existential threat to a mainframe-only strategy; waiting for perfect margins or full vertical integration cedes the emerging category to competitors.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, isolated team can bypass corporate inertia to rapidly deploy a standardized, open-architecture PC via retail channels, sacrificing short-term margin for immediate market presence and ecosystem growth.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary objection is that open standards invite copying and erode the company's traditional high-margin, controlled-service model. However, in a nascent mass market, speed and distribution outweigh proprietary lock-in. "Do nothing" preserves margins but guarantees irrelevance as the hardware base shifts. Building a custom, closed system internally would take too long, missing the critical adoption window. The proposed "open + retail" approach is the only viable path to scale quickly enough to matter.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This contradicts the current "integrated widget" north star but is necessary to survive the platform shift. If we wait to build retail expertise or custom silicon, competitors will own the user base. The cost of delay is total market exclusion. The reliance on outside software is actually a feature, not a bug, as it accelerates application availability compared to internal development.

**Blockers & Risks:**
*Blockers:* NONE. The stated risks (copying, lower margins, supplier dependence, no retail experience) are inherent characteristics of the PC business model, not factual contradictions proving failure. They are risks to be managed, not showstoppers.
*Risks:* Margin dilution and channel conflict are real but acceptable trade-offs for survival.

**Decision:**
The proposal addresses the strategic imperative with the only feasible tactical approach. The risks are known variables of the new market, not flaws in the plan. The isolation of the team mitigates cultural contagion.

**Kill Criteria:**
If the prototype cannot reach production-ready status within 12 months, or if initial retail partners refuse to stock the unit due to design flaws, the project halts. Leading metric: Units sold through independent retailers in Q1 post-launch.

DECISION: GO
