First call: NO-GO. A $1,000 device with an unclear value proposition and limited content library cannot survive against cheaper, more versatile alternatives in the current market.

**Steelman:** This player offers a unique, high-fidelity interactive experience unattainable by current consoles or PCs, creating a new premium category.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $1,000, the unit costs significantly more than game consoles and competes with entry-level PCs that offer broader utility (productivity + emerging multimedia). Without a "killer app," the price is unjustifiable.
2.  **Content Chicken-and-Egg:** A small library of interactive titles fails to drive hardware adoption; without install base, developers won't create content. This deadlock is fatal at launch.
3.  **Identity Crisis:** Positioned "between" a console and a PC, the device risks confusing consumers who already understand those two categories. If buyers don't understand what it is for, they won't spend $1,000.
4.  **Substitution Threat:** PCs are rapidly gaining multimedia capabilities. A dedicated single-function device becomes obsolete faster than a general-purpose computer at a similar price point.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Avoid sunk costs in a likely failed format war.
*   *License Tech:* Embed the disc technology into existing PC or console ecosystems rather than building a standalone $1,000 box.
*   *Lower Price/Scope:* Launch a cheaper audio/photo-only viewer first to build install base before attempting interactive video.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sells poorly due to price and confusion, developers abandon the platform due to low sales, and the company is left with unsold inventory and a damaged brand.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price:** $1,000 exceeds the psychological barrier for an unproven peripheral without clear superiority over existing $300-$800 options.
*   **Clarity:** The proposal admits buyers may not understand the product; marketing cannot fix a fundamental positioning flaw at this price.
*   **Content:** Small library directly contradicts the requirement for a successful media platform launch.

**Risks:** Rapid PC commoditization, format wars, consumer resistance to new media formats.

The combination of prohibitive cost, consumer confusion, and lack of content creates an insurmountable barrier to adoption.

DECISION: NO-GO
