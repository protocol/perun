First call: NO-GO. Simultaneous transatlantic expansion in 1998 for unproven online fashion buying overextends limited venture capital against unvalidated logistics.

**Steelman:** Launching everywhere immediately captures global mindshare before incumbents react, leveraging first-mover advantage in a nascent category.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposal:** Global launch. Cost: Extreme. Risk: Catastrophic cash burn. Reversibility: Low (leases/hires stick).
2.  **Do Nothing:** Wait for market maturity. Cost: Opportunity loss. Risk: Competitors take lead. Reversibility: High.
3.  **Better Way:** Single-market pilot (e.g., UK or Germany). Cost: Controlled. Risk: Localized failure. Reversibility: High. Validates logistics and demand before scaling.

**Fit:** Assumes "largest in the world" requires speed, but survival is a prerequisite for size. The strategy conflicts with the constraint of finite venture capital against unproven unit economics.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, cash is depleted. Warehouses in three countries are underutilized, marketing spend is fragmented across cultures, and supply chain failures in one region damage the brand globally. The company cannot pivot because fixed costs are too high. This is a one-way door; once capital is burned on failed infrastructure, recovery is impossible.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits online fashion buying is "unproven" and venture money "may run out." Launching in multiple continents multiplies the burn rate while the core value proposition remains hypothetical. The risks cited (logistics complexity, delay, cash exhaustion) are not mitigated by the plan; they are inherent to it.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Capital Mismatch:** Finite venture funding cannot sustain simultaneous multi-country operations for an unproven business model. The math fails: burn rate > runway before product-market fit is established.
2.  **Unproven Demand:** Scaling an unvalidated behavior (online fashion buying) across diverse markets amplifies uncertainty rather than reducing it.

**Risks:** Execution complexity, cultural misalignment, supply chain fragmentation. These become kill criteria if a pilot fails, but the current plan offers no pilot to measure.

**Decision:** The plan lacks a validated learning loop. It bets the company on a hypothesis without a safe-to-fail experiment. A single-market entry provides the same upside potential with a fraction of the downside risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
