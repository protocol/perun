First call: NO-GO. The plan trades core utility (private communication) for speculative engagement using coercive distribution, violating the fundamental trust required for an email service.

**Steelman:** Leveraging the existing user graph via automatic enrollment is the only way to overcome the cold-start problem quickly enough to compete with dedicated social networks, making default visibility a necessary friction to unlock network effects.

**Attack:**
1.  **Context Mismatch:** Email is a private utility; social networking is public/semi-public performance. Merging them inside the inbox degrades the primary product's value proposition (trust and privacy) to subsidize a secondary feature.
2.  **Coercion vs. Consent:** Automatic enrollment with visible connections treats user data as an asset to be spent without explicit consent. In 2010, regulatory frameworks and user sensitivity regarding digital privacy are already tightening; this invites immediate backlash and scrutiny.
3.  **Reversibility Failure:** The proposal admits the launch is "hard to reverse once connections are exposed." Once private relationships are mapped and made visible, the damage to brand trust is permanent, even if the feature is disabled. The cost of failure exceeds the potential upside.
4.  **Alternative:** A cheaper, safer path exists: opt-in invitations or a standalone social product that users choose to enter. These preserve the inbox's sanctity while still testing the social hypothesis. The proposed method does not beat "do nothing" or "opt-in" because it risks the core business for uncertain gains.

**Blockers:**
*   **Trust Violation:** The stated fact that connections will be exposed by default contradicts the implicit contract of webmail privacy.
*   **Irreversibility:** The admission that the action is hard to reverse creates a non-acceptable downside risk for a speculative feature.
*   **Regulatory Exposure:** Automatic enrollment of existing users for a new data-sharing purpose is a known trigger for regulatory action.

**Risks:** Low adoption, brand dilution, user churn.

**Kill Criteria:** If user complaints regarding privacy exceed 0.1% of the base within 48 hours, or if any regulatory body issues a formal inquiry, the feature must be disabled and data reverted immediately. However, given the irreversible nature of the exposure, prevention is the only viable control.

DECISION: NO-GO
