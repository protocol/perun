First call: NO-GO. The proposal demands committing the entire company to a single, unproven product line while lacking the capital for essential factory and battery production, creating an immediate existential liquidity crisis.

Steelman: Betting the company on a single high-end EV sedan in a dedicated large factory could secure first-mover advantage and brand purity if capital markets remain open and production ramps smoothly.

However, the attack on this claim reveals fatal flaws based strictly on the provided facts:
1.  **Capital Mismatch:** The proposal explicitly admits the factory and battery packs require "far more capital than the company has." Committing to this path without a verified funding source for the gap is not a strategy; it is insolvency.
2.  **Infrastructure Dependency:** With charging stations described as "scarce," the product's utility is severely limited, directly suppressing the demand needed to justify the massive factory investment.
3.  **Execution Void:** The company has "little experience with mass production." Buying a large factory does not transfer operational knowledge; it amplifies the cost of errors. A new entrant attempting complex mass production without experience, while under-capitalized, faces near-certain failure.
4.  **Competitive Exposure:** Established makers can respond with superior resources. If they delay or undercut, this company has no secondary revenue stream (due to the "only battery-electric" constraint) to survive the war of attrition.

The "do nothing" or "start smaller" alternatives are not merely cheaper; they are the only paths that avoid immediate bankruptcy. A cheaper, staged alternative (e.g., contract manufacturing, limited run) reaches the same upside of market entry without the catastrophic downside of total capital exhaustion before the first unit sells. The stated facts show the target users face infrastructure barriers, and the company lacks the means to overcome production hurdles.

Blockers:
- The plan requires capital explicitly stated as unavailable ("far more capital than the company has").
- The strategy eliminates all fallback revenue streams ("producing only") while facing known infrastructure deficits.

Risks:
- Production delays due to inexperience.
- Competitive retaliation.
- Slow consumer adoption due to charging scarcity.

The absence of a funding plan for the known capital gap is a factual blocker, not a risk. The proposal fails as proposed because it commits to costs it cannot cover.

DECISION: NO-GO
