First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to launch and widely promote a product in 1988 based on test markets that have not yet confirmed user acceptance, while facing explicit risks of taste rejection, usability friction, and regulatory hostility toward health claims.

Steelman: This heated device offers a genuinely cleaner smoking experience with less smoke, potentially retaining smokers who want nicotine without the social stigma of heavy smoke, justifying the massive sunk cost if adoption sticks.

However, the attack on this claim reveals fatal blockers under the stated facts. First, the "cleaner" promotion triggers the stated regulatory risk: in 1988, promoting a tobacco product with implied health benefits ("cleaner") invites immediate regulatory action and legal challenges regarding unsubstantiated health claims. Second, the product fails the user fit test: smokers dislike the taste and find the lighting mechanism complicated compared to ordinary cigarettes. A product that is harder to use and tastes worse than the incumbent cannot survive a wide launch, regardless of development spend. Sunk costs are not a reason to launch a flawed product; they are a reason to cut losses before spending more on a failed promotion.

The proposal conflates "test markets" with "successful validation." The risks raised are not hypothetical; they are stated as active objections (dislike, complicated, regulators object). Launching widely before resolving these specific failures guarantees a public relations disaster and regulatory crackdown. The "do nothing" alternative (holding the launch) preserves capital and avoids the reputational damage of a withdrawn product. A cheaper, safer alternative is to halt the wide promotion, fix the taste and usability issues, and resolve the regulatory stance on claims before any further spend.

Blockers:
1. Promotion as "cleaner" directly triggers stated regulatory objections to health claims in 1988.
2. Stated user rejection (taste, complexity) ensures market failure against ordinary cigarettes.
3. Sunk costs do not validate a go-forward decision; they increase the cost of failure.

Risks:
1. Brand damage from a high-profile failure.
2. Wasted marketing spend on a product users reject.

DECISION: NO-GO
