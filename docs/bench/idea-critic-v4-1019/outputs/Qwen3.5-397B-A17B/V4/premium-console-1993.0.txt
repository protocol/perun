First call: NO-GO. The proposed $700 price point is prohibitive against established competitors selling at a loss, and the licensing model creates a fatal margin conflict for hardware partners.

**Steelman:** Licensing shifts capital risk to partners, allowing rapid market saturation if the high price signals superior value to early adopters.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $700, the console costs nearly double the prevailing market leaders. Without an overwhelming library of exclusive "killer apps" at launch, early adopters will not pay a 100% premium for unproven graphics.
2.  **Channel Conflict:** Established competitors subsidize hardware to maximize software attach rates. Licensees in this proposal *must* profit on hardware sales. This forces a retail price that includes both the licensee's margin and the start-up's royalty, making it mathematically impossible to compete with subsidized units.
3.  **Incentive Misalignment:** If the console fails to sell due to price, licensees lose money on tooling and inventory. Unlike an integrated manufacturer who can absorb hardware losses to gain software market share, third-party licensees have no downstream software revenue to offset hardware failures. They will abandon the platform at the first sign of weakness.
4.  **Market Timing:** Entering a crowded market with a premium-priced, non-subsidized device requires a monopoly on content. The proposal offers only "high-end graphics," which is insufficient to disrupt entrenched ecosystems.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Viability:** The requirement for licensee hardware profit + start-up royalty guarantees a retail price too high to achieve the volume necessary for a standard.
*   **Competitive Disadvantage:** Inability to match the loss-leader pricing strategy of incumbents.

**Risks:**
*   Lack of exclusive launch titles.
*   Fragmentation among multiple hardware manufacturers.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   If no major publisher commits to exclusives before tooling begins, stop.
*   If projected BOM (Bill of Materials) + Royalty + Licensee Margin exceeds $500, abort.

The model assumes partners will accept lower margins for a speculative future, which contradicts standard electronics manufacturing behavior in competitive markets.

DECISION: NO-GO
