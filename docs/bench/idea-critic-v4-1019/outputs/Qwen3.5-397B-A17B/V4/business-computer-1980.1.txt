First call: NO-GO. Phasing out the only successful product before the successor proves reliable and adopted is a fatal strategic error.

**Steelman:** A business-ready successor with lowercase and 80-column display is essential to capture the enterprise market, but it must coexist with the current machine until maturity.

**Attack & Options:**
The proposal fails on **fit** and **risk management**. While the feature gap (lowercase, 80-column) is real, the plan to "phase out the existing machine" immediately upon launch contradicts the stated risks of unproven reliability and potential user resistance.
1.  **Do Nothing:** Lose the business market; retain current hobbyist base.
2.  **Proposed Plan:** High risk of revenue collapse if the new hardware fails or software makers lag. Alienates the existing base.
3.  **Better Way:** Launch the new machine as a premium tier alongside the existing one. Let the market decide the transition pace. Only phase out the old model once the new one proves reliable and software compatibility is established.

**Pre-mortem:** The new machine ships with bugs (unproven reliability). Business users reject it. Meanwhile, the old machine is discontinued, angering loyal users and cutting off cash flow. The company runs out of money before a fix is ready.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal admits hardware reliability is "unproven" and the schedule is "tight." These are not mere risks; they are **blockers** to the specific action of *phasing out* the current product. You cannot retire a proven revenue generator based on an unproven, complex replacement arriving on a tight schedule. The math of cash flow does not work if the new unit fails.

**Decision:**
The feature set is correct, but the transition strategy is suicidal. The plan must be revised to run both products in parallel.
