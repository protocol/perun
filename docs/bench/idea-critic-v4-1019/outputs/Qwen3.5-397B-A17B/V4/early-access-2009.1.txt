First call: NO-GO. Releasing an unfinished, unsupported game to a paying public without marketing or a support plan invites immediate reputation damage and refund churn that a solo developer cannot survive.

**Steelman:** Early revenue funds development while real user data dictates the highest-value features, validating demand before full completion.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed (Paid Alpha):** High cash flow potential, but extreme risk of negative reviews killing long-term sales due to rough state and lack of support.
2.  **Free Technical Demo:** Distribute a limited, stable build for free to gather feedback without reputational risk; monetize later upon feature completeness.
3.  **Do Nothing (Finish First):** Delay revenue but ensure a polished launch that maximizes initial sales velocity and review scores.

**Fit:** Assuming the goal is sustainable indie development, preserving studio reputation is critical. A "rough state" launch contradicts the need for positive word-of-mouth in a budget-less environment.

**Pre-mortem:** The game launches; players encounter bugs and lack of features. Unable to get support, they flood forums with negative reviews. The "unfinished" tag sticks permanently, preventing future full-price sales. The solo dev burns out trying to answer hundreds of support tickets instead of coding.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the game is "unfinished," the dev is "solo," and there is "no marketing budget." These facts confirm the inability to manage the influx of paying customers or mitigate negative sentiment.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Support Capacity:** A solo developer cannot support a "large audience" of paying customers expecting service, leading to inevitable backlash.
2.  **Value Proposition:** Selling an explicitly "unfinished" product with known risks (rough state) creates a high probability of refund requests and negative reviews that algorithmically suppress future visibility.
3.  **Market Reality:** Without a marketing budget, the only growth engine is organic word-of-mouth, which this strategy actively poisons.

**Risks:** Running out of funds before completion; feature creep based on vocal minority feedback.

**Kill Criteria:** If a free demo cannot attract 1,000 active testers within 30 days, paid launch is unjustified.

**Decision:** The risks of reputational destruction and support overload outweigh the benefit of early cash flow given the stated constraints.

DECISION: NO-GO
