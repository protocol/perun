First call: GO. The company already possesses the core competency in silicon design, proven at scale in phones and tablets. The primary risk (software compatibility) is a known engineering constraint, not a fundamental blocker, given the industry's history of architecture transitions.

**Steelman:** Leveraging existing high-volume silicon IP to unify the product ecosystem, reducing long-term BOM costs, and differentiating performance/power metrics against competitors reliant on generic supplier roadmaps.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue paying supplier premiums, accepting feature ceilings, and maintaining fragmented architecture.
2.  **Hybrid:** Split lines (high-end custom, low-end supplier). This dilutes the ecosystem benefit and doubles validation overhead.
3.  **Acquire:** Buy a chip designer. Slower and more expensive than utilizing internal talent already aligned with company culture.

**Fit:** Aligns with the strategic north star of vertical integration and end-to-end user experience control.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the legacy software emulation layer performs too poorly, causing pro-user churn during the transition year. This is a two-way door in the long run (can revert to suppliers), but the short-term brand damage is the cost of delay.

**Evidence:** The proposal states the company *already* designs successful processors for other form factors. This proves the design capability exists. The "unproven" nature against suppliers is a temporary state resolved by benchmarking, not a permanent deficit.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (software compatibility, engineering cost, supplier relationship) are execution challenges inherent to any platform shift, not facts that prove the plan fails as proposed. The cost of engineering time is an investment, not a contradiction. The end of a supplier relationship is the intended outcome, not an accidental side effect.

**Risks:** Transition friction, initial performance gaps in niche professional apps, supply chain ramp-up delays. These become kill criteria: if initial benchmark scores fall below 80% of the incumbent within 12 months, or if developer adoption lags by >20% YoY, the rollout pauses.

**Decision:** The strategic upside of controlling the silicon roadmap outweighs the transitional friction. The company has the requisite skills.

DECISION: GO
