First call: GO. The company already possesses the core competency (chip design) and a proven architecture in its mobile line, suggesting high potential for margin improvement and product differentiation, provided the software transition is managed.

**Steelman:** Leveraging existing mobile silicon expertise to unify the hardware stack across all devices, enabling superior performance-per-watt, tighter hardware-software integration, and long-term supply chain control, while mitigating transition risks through a phased software compatibility layer.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The primary blocker risk is software incompatibility. However, this is an execution challenge, not a fundamental flaw. The "unproven" nature of the chips is mitigated by their proven success in phones; the architecture is validated, only the thermal envelope and instruction set scaling differ. The cost of engineering time is an investment in asset creation, not sunk cost. Ending the supplier relationship is a strategic feature, not a bug, removing dependency on a competitor's roadmap.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Continue paying supplier premiums, accepting feature ceilings imposed by third-party roadmaps, and maintaining fragmented architecture.
2.  **Hybrid Approach:** Co-develop with the supplier. This retains dependency and dilutes the unique value proposition.
3.  **The Proposal:** High upfront effort, but captures full value chain control and differentiation.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the company underestimates the developer ecosystem friction, launching before key professional applications are native. The "confusion" risk is real but temporary; marketing must frame this as a performance leap, not a compatibility break.

**Fit:** Aligns with a strategy of vertical integration and premium differentiation.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (software, cost, confusion) are manageable execution hurdles inherent to any major platform shift, not facts proving the plan fails as proposed. The company has the requisite design capability.

**Risks:** Developer adoption speed, thermal throttling in laptop chassis, short-term sales dip during transition. These become kill criteria: if native app adoption lags by >20% at 12 months, or if thermal limits prevent sustained performance gains, the strategy must pivot.

**Decision:** The strategic upside of controlling the core silicon outweighs the transient transition costs. The company has the unique asset (mobile chip design) to make this viable where others would fail.

DECISION: GO
