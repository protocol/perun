First call: GO. The new plant creates a fixed cost imperative; filling it with a product that matches the facility's scale is the primary operational necessity, outweighing the uncertainty of dealer costs or competitor pricing.

Steelman: Launching an 1100cc compact family car is the only viable path to utilize the new plant's capacity, leveraging a dedicated network to control brand positioning despite high upfront costs and established low-price rivals.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** High capex for dealers, but secures volume for the plant and defines a specific market segment.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Plant remains underutilized; fixed costs bleed cash immediately; no market presence.
3.  **Larger/Existing Car:** Mismatches the new plant's likely tooling for compacts; enters a saturated segment with different competitors.
The proposal beats "do nothing" because idle capacity is an immediate financial loss, whereas dealer costs are a recoverable investment if volume is achieved.

**Fit:** Assumes the strategy is industrial survival through volume production. The 1100cc engine targets the emerging family demographic, aligning with post-war mobility trends.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the dedicated network fails to attract buyers who prefer established used cars or cheaper rivals, leaving the plant full of unsold inventory. This is a two-way door regarding marketing tactics, but a one-way door on plant construction; delay costs are high as the asset depreciates daily without output.

**Evidence:** Based on stated facts: new plant exists (sunk cost), rivals exist (competition), and buyer preference is split (risk). No external data contradicts the need to fill the plant.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (costly network, rival prices, buyer preference) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The alternative of not running the plant is financially worse given the sunk cost of construction.

**Risks:** Dealer network ROI may lag; price wars with rivals could compress margins; consumer shift to larger vehicles. These become kill criteria: if plant utilization drops below 80% within 18 months or dealer acquisition cost exceeds 20% of vehicle price, pivot distribution strategy.

**Decision:** The imperative to amortize the new plant overrides the execution risks. The plan must proceed to generate cash flow, with strict monitoring on dealer uptake.

DECISION: GO
