First call: NO-GO. A $1,000 device with an unclear value proposition and limited content library cannot survive against cheaper, evolving alternatives in the 1991 market.

**Steelman:** This player creates a new "interactive living room" category by converging audio, photos, and games, justifying its premium price through unique multimedia capabilities unavailable on consoles or PCs.

**Attack & Options:**
The claim fails on **fit** and **economics**.
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $1,000, the unit costs 4-5x a standard game console. Without a "killer app" library (admitted as small), the hardware is a solution searching for a problem.
2.  **Substitution:** Personal computers are rapidly gaining multimedia abilities. A PC offers productivity *plus* emerging media features, making a single-function $1,000 box obsolete before launch.
3.  **Consumer Confusion:** Positioned between a console and a PC, the device lacks a clear identity. Marketing a "multimedia disc" format to a mass market unfamiliar with the term invites adoption failure.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume launch failed in 1993. Cause: Consumers bought cheaper consoles for games and waited for PC prices to drop for multimedia. The "interactive disc" format became a niche curiosity, leaving the manufacturer with unsold inventory and no software ecosystem.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Mismatch:** The $1,000 price point exceeds the perceived value of a "living room accessory" without established software demand.
*   **Strategic Ambiguity:** The proposal admits buyers "may not understand what the product is for." Launching a high-capital hardware product with undefined user utility is a fatal flaw.
*   **Competitive Obsolescence:** PCs are closing the capability gap; the window for a dedicated, expensive hybrid device is closing.

**Risks:** Small title library, format confusion, rapid PC commoditization. These are not merely risks; the admitted confusion and high cost relative to substitutes constitute immediate failure conditions.

**Conclusion:** The proposal relies on creating a market that does not yet exist while ignoring immediate substitutes that are becoming cheaper and more versatile. The price creates a barrier that the small content library cannot overcome.

DECISION: NO-GO
