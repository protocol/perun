First call: GO. The shift in buyer demographics toward American-brand customers seeking smaller sedans indicates a market gap that current European offerings do not fill. Leveraging an existing mass-market platform reduces development time and cost, allowing rapid entry into this emerging segment.

Steelman: Launching a compact luxury model on a shared platform enables the brand to capture value-conscious luxury buyers who prioritize size and brand cachet over exclusive engineering, while maintaining margin through premium trim and features.

Options:
1. **Do nothing**: Risk losing the growing segment of American-brand upgraders to competitors who may fill the gap first.
2. **Develop a new compact platform**: High cost, long lead time, and uncertain demand make this risky and slow.
3. **Shared platform with premium branding (proposed)**: Balances speed, cost, and brand positioning; fastest route to test the market.

Fit: Aligns with the strategic need to expand the customer base without diluting the core brand's prestige, assuming the new model is clearly differentiated in marketing and sales channels.

Pre-mortem: If buyers perceive the car as a rebadged mass-market vehicle, sales will falter, and brand equity could suffer. However, this is a two-way door; the model can be discontinued or repositioned if the market rejects it. The cost of delay is losing first-mover advantage in the compact luxury segment.

Evidence: The proposal cites a observable shift in buyer origin (American-brand customers) and preference (smaller sedan). No internal data contradicts this trend.

Blockers: NONE. The risks raised (platform transparency, price justification, salesperson behavior, brand dilution) are valid concerns but not factual blockers. They are execution risks manageable through marketing, training, and clear brand segmentation.

Risks: Brand perception dilution, channel confusion, price sensitivity. These become kill criteria: if brand sentiment scores drop or sales mix cannibalizes core models beyond 10%, the program halts.

Kill Criteria: Monitor brand sentiment indices and sales cannibalization rates quarterly. If premium pricing fails to sustain margins or brand perception declines measurably within 18 months, discontinue the model. Leading metric: Sales volume from non-traditional (American-brand) buyers vs. core European buyers.

DECISION: GO
