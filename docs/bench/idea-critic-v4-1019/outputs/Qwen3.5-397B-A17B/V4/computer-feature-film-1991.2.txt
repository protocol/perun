First call: GO. The offer solves the company's two hardest problems—capital for unproven tech and access to distribution—while the creative risk is mitigated by the team's specific expertise in short-form CG.

**Steelman:** Partnering with a major studio provides the necessary budget and distribution network to pioneer the first all-CG feature, leveraging the company's unique animation talent to tell a contained story (toys) that manages technical complexity while establishing a new market category.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Remain a commercial/short shop; likely lose key talent to competitors and miss the window to define the CG feature genre.
2.  **Self-fund/Independent:** Impossible given the high cost of rendering and lack of distribution channels; high probability of bankruptcy before completion.
3.  **Wait for tech to improve:** Cedes first-mover advantage to rivals; rendering speeds will always improve, but the opportunity to be "the first" is unique to this moment.

**Fit:** Aligns with the vision of transitioning from short-form experiments to narrative storytelling. The "toy" concept is a strategic constraint that fits current technical limits (limited environments, manageable character count).

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the production timeline balloons due to rendering bottlenecks, causing the studio to pull funding, or if the story fails to resonate because the novelty of CG wears off before the emotional arc lands. This is a one-way door; once resources are committed, returning to the prior state is impossible. However, the cost of delay is losing the lead animator and the strategic partnership permanently.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (inexperience with features, slow rendering, partner leverage) are inherent to pioneering new technology, not factual contradictions. The partner's strong hand is the price of entry for distribution and capital.

**Risks:** Technical bottlenecks delaying release; creative control dilution; financial overrun if rendering costs exceed estimates. These are managed by the "toy" scope constraint and the partnership itself.

**Decision:** The proposal represents a classic "barbell" strategy: capped downside (failure means returning to shorts, but with enhanced reputation) and uncapped upside (defining a new industry standard). The alternative is stagnation.

DECISION: GO
