First call: NO-GO. The proposal risks the studio's entire solvency on an unproven format against explicit advice from industry insiders and family, with no evidence that audiences will accept feature-length animation.

**Steelman:** If feature-length animation captures the same per-minute engagement as shorts, the studio could dominate a new market category and multiply profits beyond the cumulative value of short films.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue steady, thin profits; zero risk of bankruptcy.
2.  **Pilot:** Produce a medium-length special (3x cost) to test audience endurance before committing to a full feature.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Bet the company on a single 10x-cost production immediately.

**Fit:** Assumes the vision is "market domination via innovation," but contradicts the current stage of "steady but thin profits." A company with thin margins cannot absorb a 10x cost overrun or a total box-office failure.

**Pre-mortem:** The film releases to confused audiences accustomed to short formats; the budget overruns by 50% due to unforeseen production complexity; the studio exhausts its cash reserves from shorts funding the deficit and declares bankruptcy before the next revenue cycle. This is a one-way door; failure destroys the entity.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits risks are "raised" by insiders and family, yet offers no counter-evidence (e.g., survey data, ticket pre-sales) to refute them. The financial math relies entirely on the assumption that "longer equals more profit," which is unproven.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Capital Insufficiency:** Funding a 10x budget from "steady but thin" earnings implies extreme fragility; any delay or cost overrun triggers insolvency.
2.  **Unvalidated Demand:** No evidence exists that audiences will sit through a feature; proceeding without a smaller-scale test violates basic product validation.
3.  **Expert Dissent:** Unified advice from family and industry insiders to stop constitutes a strong negative signal that the plan ignores.

**Risks:** Total loss of studio; reputation damage; inability to return to short production if funds are depleted.

**Kill Criteria:** If a medium-length test fails to retain 80% of the audience, abort. If production costs exceed 12x the short budget, stop.

The absence of a pilot phase and the reliance on fragile cash flow to fund a 10x leap makes this reckless. The plan must be revised to include a lower-cost proof of concept.

DECISION: NO-GO
