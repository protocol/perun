First call: GO. The offer solves the company's critical constraints: capital for expensive rendering and distribution access it lacks. The "toy's point of view" narrows the scope to a manageable, character-driven story suitable for a debut feature.

Steelman: Partnering leverages the studio's distribution and funding to overcome the prohibitive costs and inexperience of producing the first all-CG feature, while the simple premise mitigates creative risk.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Remain a short-film house; likely lose the lead animator to the suitor eventually; no path to features.
2. Self-fund: Impossible given rendering costs and lack of infrastructure; high probability of bankruptcy before completion.
3. Wait for tech to improve: Cedes first-mover advantage; competitor (the suitor) will likely proceed with or without this team.

Fit: Aligns with the vision of pioneering feature-length CG animation. The partnership is the only viable vehicle to achieve this north star.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the studio interferes creatively, the render farm cannot scale, or the story fails to resonate beyond a novelty. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental blockers. The "toy" concept is a two-way door; if it fails, the company still gains invaluable pipeline experience. Delay costs the entire strategic window.

Evidence: The suitor's active attempt to hire the lead animator proves market demand for this specific talent. The offer itself validates the feasibility of the partnership model.

Blockers: NONE. The lack of prior feature experience is a risk, not a blocker, as the partner provides the necessary publishing infrastructure. The technical hurdles are surmountable with the proposed funding.

Risks: Creative control dilution; technical bottlenecks; market rejection of CG format. These are addressed by the partnership structure and the focused scope of the story.

Kill criteria: If the studio demands creative changes that dilute the core "toy" perspective within the first six months, or if render times exceed budget projections by >50% after the first act, renegotiate or halt. Leading metric: Completion of the first 10 minutes of animation within budget and schedule by Q4 1992.

DECISION: GO
