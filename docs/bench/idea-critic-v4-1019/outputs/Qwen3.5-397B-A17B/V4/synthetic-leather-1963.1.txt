First call: NO-GO. The proposal asserts a premium launch despite higher costs, unproven comfort, and manufacturer resistance, creating a negative unit economics scenario before scale.

**Steelman:** The synthetic offers superior durability that justifies a price premium, overcoming comfort and adoption hurdles through aggressive marketing.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** The material costs *more* than leather. Shoe manufacturers operate on thin margins and will not absorb higher input costs for an unproven material without guaranteed consumer demand. Marketing spend cannot bridge a structural cost disadvantage if the end product is inferior (comfort) or unwanted (fashion risk).
2.  **Adoption Friction:** Manufacturers are unwilling to retool factories. Without a compelling cost saving or massive consumer pull, they have zero incentive to switch. The proposal asks them to take all the risk (retooling, higher cost) for a material that might be less comfortable.
3.  **Market Fit:** Fashion is volatile. Launching a "premium" product with known comfort deficits invites immediate rejection by style leaders. If wearers find it uncomfortable, repeat purchases vanish, destroying the brand before durability benefits matter.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A better path is a niche pilot (e.g., work boots where durability trumps comfort) to prove value without heavy marketing spend or factory retooling demands.

**Blockers:**
*   **Negative Unit Economics:** Higher cost than the incumbent (leather) combined with lower perceived value (comfort risk) makes the core value proposition unsellable to manufacturers.
*   **Channel Resistance:** Explicitly stated manufacturer unwillingness to change factories is a hard gate; marketing budget does not solve physical retooling inertia.

**Risks:** Fashion trends shifting away; consumer rejection due to comfort. These are secondary to the primary economic blocker.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot cannot secure manufacturer participation without subsidizing retooling, or if blind wear-tests show significant comfort degradation vs. leather, the project terminates.

**Decision:** The plan fails on basic economics and channel incentives. It requires manufacturers to pay more for a potentially worse product.

DECISION: NO-GO
