First call: NO-GO. The core claim—that luxury buyers will accept a mass-market platform with added trim at a premium price—contradicts the fundamental driver of luxury purchasing: exclusivity and perceived engineering superiority.

**Steelman:** A compact entry point captures American buyers seeking status without full-size dimensions, leveraging existing manufacturing to maximize margin while expanding the brand's footprint.

**Attack:**
1.  **Platform Contamination:** Luxury value is psychological. If buyers perceive the car as a "dressed-up" mass-market vehicle (a high risk given the shared platform), the premium price becomes indefensible. The proposal admits buyers "may see through" this; in luxury, suspicion is fatal.
2.  **Channel Friction:** Salespeople refusing to use the main brand name indicates internal cultural rejection. If the sales force distances the product from the flagship to protect the brand's image, the new model becomes an orphan, failing to lift the parent brand while diluting its scarcity.
3.  **Cannibalization vs. Expansion:** Without a distinct architectural identity, this car risks cannibalizing entry-level sales of larger models without attracting true new buyers who prioritize badge prestige over size.
4.  **Prestige Dilution:** The risk that the main brand's prestige may suffer is not merely a "risk"; it is a likely outcome of associating the badge with a mass-market underpinning. Once prestige erodes, it is nearly impossible to restore.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Maintain high margins and brand equity; wait for a dedicated small-car platform.
*   **Sub-Brand:** Launch the compact under a completely new name (no shared badge) to test the water without risking the core asset.
*   **Acquisition:** Buy an existing small premium player rather than diluting the core identity.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-launch, the car is discounted heavily to move inventory, salespeople hide it in the back lot, and core customers defect to competitors who maintained platform exclusivity. The brand is now viewed as "accessible" rather than "aspirational."

**Blockers:** The proposal relies on a contradiction: selling a mass-market foundation at a luxury premium while expecting the market to ignore the foundation. The admitted risk of prestige damage is a strategic blocker when the mitigation is undefined.

**Risks:** Sales channel rejection, price justification failure, brand dilution.

**Kill Criteria:** If initial buyer surveys indicate >20% awareness of the mass-market donor platform, or if transaction prices drop >15% below MSRP within six months, terminate the program immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
