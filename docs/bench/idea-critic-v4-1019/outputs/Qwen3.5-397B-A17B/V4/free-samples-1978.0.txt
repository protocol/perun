First call: NO-GO. The initial four-city test demonstrated little demand, and the proposal offers no evidence that a name change or free distribution to a single demographic (office workers) addresses the root cause of that failure.

**Steelman:** Renaming and targeted sampling will uncover latent demand among office workers that the initial broad test missed, validating a national rollout.

**Attack:**
1.  **Contradictory Evidence:** The primary fact is "disappointing sales" and "little demand." A name change is a superficial fix for a likely functional or value-proposition mismatch. If the product lacks utility, a new label and free units will not create retention.
2.  **Sampling Bias:** Limiting free samples to "offices of one mid-sized city" creates a high risk of false positives. Office workers receiving free items may express intent to buy out of politeness or because the item is free, not because they value it at a market price. This does not predict national paid adoption.
3.  **Financial Exposure:** The proposal admits a national rollout requires a "large marketing budget." Committing this capital based on a potentially flawed, single-city sentiment survey violates basic risk management. The cost of free samples plus the potential wasted rollout budget exceeds the cost of a more rigorous, low-cost validation.
4.  **Better Way:** Before renaming or sampling, the company must identify *why* the first test failed. Was it price, placement, or lack of need? A cheaper alternative is to conduct qualitative interviews with non-buyers from the first test or run a small, paid A/B test on the value proposition without a full rebrand.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Initial test showed little demand.
*   **Fact:** Proposal relies on "would buy" sentiment from free recipients, which is an unreliable proxy for actual sales.
*   **Missing:** No mechanism to verify if the "need" exists independent of the product being free.

**Risks:** High probability of wasting marketing budget on a product with no market fit; false confidence from biased sampling.

**Conclusion:** The plan attempts to solve a demand problem with marketing tactics (renaming, sampling) rather than validating product-market fit. The reliance on stated intent from free users is a critical flaw that could lead to a costly failed national launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
