First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades a manufacturer's core competency (building reliable hardware) for a speculative royalty stream in a saturated market, relying entirely on a third party's marketing effort while ceding brand control.

**Steelman:** Licensing allows rapid market entry with zero capital expenditure, leveraging a toy company's distribution to reach consumers the computer maker cannot, creating a low-risk foothold in the living room.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Misalignment:** A toy licensee prioritizes short-term holiday sales volume over long-term platform ecosystem health. They lack the incentive to sustain the "internet console" vision if initial hardware margins are thin.
2.  **Brand Dilution:** In 1995, "internet" and "gaming" are nascent. If the low-cost device fails or feels cheap, it directly tarnishes the licensor's reputation for quality computing, contradicting the goal of building a consumer brand.
3.  **Market Reality:** Established console makers dominate with proprietary ecosystems. A licensed, low-cost clone without exclusive software or strong marketing backing (which the licensor explicitly abdicated) will likely be ignored.
4.  **Economic Fragility:** Small royalties per unit require massive volume to matter. Without control over marketing, the licensor cannot guarantee this volume. The "do nothing" option preserves brand equity; this plan risks it for negligible upside.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, sells moderately during the holidays, then vanishes. The toy company moves to the next trend. The computer maker is left with a brand associated with a forgotten, cheap gadget, having earned insufficient royalties to justify the reputational damage.

**Blockers:**
*   **Strategic Contradiction:** The proposal seeks to build a consumer brand but removes the primary lever for doing so (marketing control).
*   **Economic Failure:** The revenue model (small royalty) cannot sustain the platform against dominant incumbents without the licensor's direct investment in marketing, which is explicitly excluded.

**Risks:** Licensee under-investment, brand confusion, failure to compete with established consoles.

**Kill Criteria:** If the licensee cannot commit to a minimum marketing spend equal to 20% of projected hardware revenue, or if exclusivity on key software titles is not guaranteed, the deal destroys more value than it creates.

DECISION: NO-GO
