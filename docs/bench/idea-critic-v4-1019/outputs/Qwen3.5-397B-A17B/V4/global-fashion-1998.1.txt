First call: NO-GO. The plan attempts to solve two unproven variables (online fashion demand and cross-border logistics) simultaneously across multiple continents, multiplying execution risk beyond the capacity of typical venture funding.

Steelman: A massive, simultaneous global launch creates an insurmountable moat against competitors by capturing the entire young demographic before anyone else can react.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Wait for market maturity; low risk, zero upside.
2. Single-market pilot: Launch in one country (e.g., UK or Germany); proves logistics and demand with capped capital; reversible.
3. Proposed plan: Multi-country launch; highest cost, highest risk, lowest reversibility.

Fit: The vision of being the "largest retailer" requires scale, but the strategy confuses speed of expansion with speed of learning. Expanding before validating the core unit economics in one region violates basic operational hygiene.

Pre-mortem: The company runs out of cash 18 months in. Logistics networks in three countries fail to meet delivery promises simultaneously, destroying brand reputation. Marketing spend is diluted across regions, failing to achieve critical mass anywhere. Venture capitalists refuse follow-on funding due to missed milestones and burning cash without a proven model.

Door: One-way. Once capital is deployed into offices and inventory across multiple continents, retreat is catastrophic and likely leads to total insolvency.

Evidence: The proposal admits online fashion buying is "unproven" and venture money "may run out." Launching in multiple unproven markets with finite capital contradicts the stated constraint. There is no evidence that managing complex international logistics is easier than domestic logistics; historically, it is exponentially harder.

Blockers:
1. Capital mismatch: Finite venture funding cannot sustain simultaneous multi-country losses while waiting for unproven demand to materialize.
2. Unproven core mechanism: The fundamental premise (online fashion sales) is unvalidated; scaling an unproven model multiplies failure probability, not success.

Risks: Logistics collapse, brand dilution, premature cash exhaustion.

Kill criteria: If a single-country pilot does not achieve positive unit economics within 12 months, stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
