First call: GO. The proposal targets a distinct, price-sensitive segment with a simpler, cheaper solution, despite valid execution risks.

**Steelman:** A low-cost, randomized robot vacuum can succeed by prioritizing affordability and simplicity over perfect coverage, capturing customers excluded by expensive mapping rivals.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** High volume, low margin, immediate market entry. Risk: Brand trust, coverage gaps. Reversibility: High (inventory liquidation).
2.  **Do Nothing:** Zero risk, zero revenue. Competitors dominate the high-end; the mass market remains unserved.
3.  **High-End Mapping:** Direct competition with established players. Risk: Extremely high R&D cost, slow time-to-market. Reversibility: Low (sunk costs).
The proposed plan beats "do nothing" by creating a new category and is safer than building a mapping rival without prior experience.

**Fit:** Assumes the goal is rapid market penetration and volume over premium branding. This aligns with a "disruptive innovation" strategy, targeting non-consumers.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if random cleaning is perceived as "broken" rather than "good enough," or if lack of consumer channels prevents distribution. It is a two-way door; failure allows a pivot or exit with limited sunk cost compared to mapping tech. Cost of delay: Competitors might release a budget model first.

**Evidence:** Based on stated facts: two floor-care companies rejected it (validating they don't see it fitting their *existing* high-end models, not necessarily market demand), and the price differential is significant. No data contradicts the existence of a low-end market.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of consumer experience is a risk, not a factual blocker preventing launch. The rejection by two partners is not a market veto; it suggests a direct-to-consumer or different channel strategy is required.

**Risks:** Brand trust, coverage perception, distribution execution. These are mitigated by the low price point (lowering the trust barrier) and the ability to iterate quickly.

**Kill Criteria:** Stop if sales < 1,000 units in the first quarter or return rate > 15%. Leading metric: Units sold per week. Review date: 90 days post-launch.

DECISION: GO
