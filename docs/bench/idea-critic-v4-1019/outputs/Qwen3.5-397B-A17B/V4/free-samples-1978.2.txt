First call: NO-GO. The initial four-city test demonstrated weak market demand, and the proposed remedy (renaming) addresses perception rather than the fundamental lack of utility or need identified in that failure.

Steelman: Renaming and targeted sampling could uncover a specific use case (office organization) missed by general retail, validating demand before a costly national rollout.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** Rename and sample offices. Cost: Moderate. Risk: High (ignores prior demand signal). Reversibility: High.
2. **Do Nothing:** Cut losses or maintain status quo. Cost: Zero. Risk: Missed niche opportunity. Reversibility: N/A.
3. **Pivot Usage:** Market strictly as a bookmark or file tab without renaming. Cost: Low. Risk: Limited market size. Reversibility: High.

Fit: The strategy assumes the product failed due to branding, not function. However, "disappointing sales" in four cities suggests a functional mismatch. A name change does not create a need where none exists.

Pre-mortem: The rollout occurs; offices accept free samples out of politeness but never reorder. The company burns cash on a national campaign based on false positive feedback from free goods. The "need" remains unproven.

Evidence: The only hard data is the failed four-city launch. The proposal relies on the assumption that "saying they would buy" equals actual purchasing, a known cognitive bias in market research. There is no evidence that a name change alters the physical utility of the adhesive.

Blockers:
- **False Metric:** Relying on verbal intent ("say they would buy") after receiving free goods is a fatal flaw. It guarantees positive feedback regardless of actual demand.
- **Contradiction:** The plan ignores the primary evidence (low sales) in favor of an unproven hypothesis (branding failure).

Risks: Sunk cost fallacy driving further investment; confusion between sampling uptake and retail conversion.

Kill Criteria: If less than 15% of sample recipients reorder within 60 days without further prompting, the project stops immediately.

The proposal fails because it substitutes a cheap, unreliable metric (verbal intent on free goods) for actual market validation. It attempts to solve a demand problem with a branding solution.

DECISION: NO-GO
