First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades a dominant brand's equity for negligible royalties in a market where execution risk lies entirely with an unmotivated third party.

**Steelman:** Licensing converts fixed R&D costs into variable revenue, allowing rapid market entry without capital expenditure or operational distraction.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Misalignment:** The licensor earns "small royalties," while the licensee bears all marketing costs. In a market dominated by established players, aggressive marketing is the *only* path to share. The licensee, facing high upfront costs and low margins per unit, will likely under-invest in promotion, ensuring product failure.
2.  **Brand Dilution:** Consumers may not connect the product to the licensor. If the low-cost console fails or offers a poor experience (likely given the "toy company" partner), the computer maker's premium brand suffers reputational damage without any control over quality or messaging.
3.  **Opportunity Cost:** "Do nothing" preserves brand equity. A "better way" would be a controlled, limited rollout or a partnership where the computer maker retains marketing control to ensure brand alignment, even at higher cost.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sells poorly due to lack of marketing visibility, and is discontinued. The computer maker gains minimal revenue but is now associated with a failed "toy," confusing its core business identity.

**Blockers:**
*   **Incentive Structure:** The financial model (small royalty vs. full marketing cost) guarantees the licensee cannot or will not fight established incumbents. The plan fails as proposed because the partner's economic incentive to win is near zero.
*   **Strategic Fit:** Entering a consumer market via a passive license contradicts the need for strong brand positioning against dominant rivals.

**Risks:** Market dominance by others, execution failure, brand confusion. These are severe, but the *blocker* is the structural flaw in the deal terms themselves.

**Kill Criteria:** If the licensee cannot commit to a minimum marketing spend equal to 20% of projected revenue within 30 days, the deal must be scrapped.

DECISION: NO-GO
