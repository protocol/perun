First call: GO. The company already possesses the core competency (chip design) and a proven architecture in its mobile line; extending this to laptops creates a unified ecosystem, improves margins, and differentiates the product, provided software compatibility is solved.

**Steelman:** Leveraging existing silicon expertise to replace commodity laptop processors with custom designs will yield superior performance-per-watt and tighter hardware-software integration, justifying the transition costs and supply chain disruption.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" maintains status quo but cedes long-term differentiation to competitors who integrate vertically. Sticking with the supplier is safer short-term but limits innovation ceiling. The proposed path is the only one offering unique value.
2.  **Fit:** This aligns with a strategy of total ecosystem control. The "unproven" nature of the chips is a risk, not a blocker, mitigated by the proven mobile architecture.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if legacy software breaks or performance lags the supplier's high-end offerings. However, the proposal explicitly flags software continuity as a risk, implying awareness. The cost of delay is losing the architectural advantage window.
4.  **Evidence:** The premise rests on the fact that the company *already* designs successful phone/tablet chips. This is not an assumption; it is the company's current reality. The "confusion" risk is transient marketing noise, outweighed by the long-term brand strength of unified silicon.
5.  **Blockers vs. Risks:**
    *   *Blockers:* NONE. No fact presented proves the plan fails as proposed. The supplier relationship ending is a feature, not a bug, of insourcing.
    *   *Risks:* Software compatibility, initial performance gaps, engineering load. These are execution challenges to be managed via kill criteria (e.g., benchmark parity by date X), not reasons to stop.

The risks are standard for vertical integration and are outweighed by the strategic necessity of owning the core compute layer. The "unproven" argument ignores the proven mobile baseline. The transition cost is an investment, not a loss.

DECISION: GO
