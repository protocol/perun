First call: NO-GO. Doubling production while sales lag by 60% during a recession with rising interest rates ignores basic cash flow constraints.

**Steelman:** The claim assumes that flooding the market now secures future market share or forces dealer acceptance before competitors react, betting that demand will rebound sharply enough to clear the massive inventory before cash runs out.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cash Burn:** Producing 7,500 more units (doubling output implies a massive ramp) requires immediate capital for parts and labor. With only 3,000 sold, revenue is insufficient to fund operations. High interest rates make bridging this gap prohibitively expensive or impossible given "limited cash."
2.  **Inventory Glut:** Adding to an already growing stock of unsold cars increases storage costs and depreciation. Dealers explicitly cannot finance more stock; forcing production creates a bottleneck where finished goods sit idle, tying up working capital with zero return.
3.  **Demand Mismatch:** A recession reduces consumer purchasing power. High interest rates further suppress auto loans. Increasing supply when demand is contracting guarantees a deeper crash.
4.  **Blocker:** The proposal states the company has "limited cash" and dealers "may not be able to finance more stock." Doubling production directly contradicts these verified constraints, ensuring insolvency before any hypothetical demand recovery.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing/Reduce:* Align production with sales to preserve cash.
*   *Pivot:* Focus on clearing existing inventory via incentives rather than building new units.

**Pre-mortem:** The company runs out of cash in six months, unable to pay suppliers or service debt, leading to bankruptcy while sitting on thousands of unsold, depreciating vehicles.

**Conclusion:** This plan accelerates failure. It ignores the binding constraint (cash) and exacerbates the primary risk (unsold inventory). There is no scenario where doubling output in a recession with limited liquidity succeeds without external infinite funding, which is not proposed.

Blockers: Limited cash cannot fund doubled production; dealers cannot finance inventory; demand is falling.
Risks: Total insolvency, asset devaluation, supplier default.

DECISION: NO-GO
