First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to launch a capital-intensive hardware company with no manufacturing track record, no distribution, and a price premium for an unproven consumer benefit against entrenched incumbents who can easily replicate the mechanical concept.

Steelman: A superior, bag-free technology justifies a premium price and can disrupt the market if brought to scale immediately by the inventor.

Attack: The "better way" analysis fails. The cheapest alternative is licensing the patent to an established manufacturer. This captures value without the massive downside risk of building factories, supply chains, and retail relationships from scratch. The proposal ignores Chesterton's fence: incumbents have bags for reasons of cost and familiarity; overcoming this requires education capital the new entity lacks.

Pre-mortem: The company burns cash setting up production, fails to secure shelf space against competitor slotting fees, and goes bankrupt before reaching unit economics that justify the price. The technology is then bought by a competitor for pennies.

Evidence: The facts state "no manufacturing record" and "cost noticeably more." In hardware, margins are tight; a higher price point with higher costs and no brand equity is a fatal unit economic mismatch. The risk of incumbents copying is high, but they can only copy if the inventor survives long enough to prove the market.

Blockers:
1. No path to profitability: Higher price + higher cost (implied by new manufacturing) + no volume = negative margin trajectory.
2. Superior alternative exists: Licensing transfers execution risk to parties with existing distribution and manufacturing, securing immediate royalties without capital expenditure.

Risks: Consumer rejection of bagless tech; retail exclusion; rapid imitation. These are severe, but the lack of a viable business model compared to licensing is the blocker. The proposal to "found a company" is the wrong vehicle for this asset at this stage. The inventor should monetize the IP, not attempt to become an operator in a field where they have zero comparative advantage.

DECISION: NO-GO
