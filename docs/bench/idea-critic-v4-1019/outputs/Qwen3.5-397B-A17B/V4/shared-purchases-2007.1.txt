First call: NO-GO. The core mechanism trades user trust for ad data by exposing sensitive off-platform behavior to a user's social graph, a failure mode the proposed controls cannot contain.

**Steelman:** Sharing purchase data with explicit notification and opt-out respects user agency while unlocking viral ad value.

**Attack:** The "notification + opt-out" model is fundamentally broken for privacy-sensitive data. It shifts the burden to the user to protect themselves after the fact, creating a "privacy trap" where embarrassment occurs before the opt-out can be exercised. Once a sensitive purchase (e.g., medical, financial, adult) is broadcast to friends, the reputational damage to the user and the partner is immediate and irreversible; an apology or retraction does not undo the social exposure. The risk of partners being embarrassed by public reactions is not a side effect but a certainty that will cause high-value advertisers to flee. Furthermore, the proposal admits the feature is "hard to withdraw," meaning a single misstep could trigger a mass exodus of users who feel betrayed, destroying the platform's core asset: trust. The "sensitive" nature of purchases means the average user cannot reliably predict what they might want to hide, making informed opt-out impossible.

**Alternatives:** The "do nothing" approach preserves trust. A "aggregated, anonymized insights" model for advertisers achieves the revenue goal without exposing individual user behavior to their peers.

**Pre-mortem:** The feature launches, a high-profile user's sensitive purchase is broadcast, causing a media firestorm. Users mass-opt-out, partners demand immediate removal, and the platform is forced to kill the feature, having permanently damaged its brand as a safe space.

**Blockers:** The proposal relies on a reactive privacy model (opt-out) for inherently sensitive, irreversible social exposures. The stated risk that the feature is "hard to withdraw" confirms that the cost of failure is catastrophic and unmanageable. The mechanism itself guarantees the negative outcome it seeks to mitigate.

**Risks:** User backlash, partner churn, regulatory scrutiny, irreversible brand damage.

DECISION: NO-GO
