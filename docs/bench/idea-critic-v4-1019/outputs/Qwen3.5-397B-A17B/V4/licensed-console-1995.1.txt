First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades a dominant brand's equity for negligible royalties in a market where execution risk sits entirely with an unmotivated licensee.

**Steelman:** Licensing allows rapid, capital-light entry into the consumer console market by leveraging a toy partner's distribution, generating incremental royalty revenue without distracting the core engineering team from high-margin computer sales.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Misalignment:** A "small royalty" gives the toy licensee little margin to fight established console giants. If marketing is weak (a stated risk), sales fail. If sales fail, royalties are zero. The computer maker bears the brand damage of a failed, cheap product while earning nothing.
2.  **Brand Dilution:** Entering the low-cost toy sector via a third party disconnects the product from the licensor's premium brand identity. Consumers will not associate the "toy console" with the "serious computer," cannibalizing neither competitors nor building future loyalty.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" option preserves brand integrity. A superior alternative is a branded, higher-margin peripheral for existing computers or a joint venture where the computer maker retains marketing control to ensure brand alignment. Licensing away control in a hit-driven entertainment market is surrendering the only lever that ensures success.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sells poorly due to lack of marketing push, and is perceived as a cheap knockoff. The computer maker's brand is tarnished as "toy-like," hurting core business sales. The royalty income is insufficient to cover the reputational cost.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fatal Incentive Flaw:** The financial structure (small royalty) + operational structure (licensee controls marketing) guarantees the licensee will not invest enough to overcome market dominance by rivals. The plan fails on its own stated terms.

**Risks:** Brand erosion, zero revenue, market confusion. These are certainties under the current structure, not mere possibilities.

The proposal asks to monetize a brand by handing it to a partner with no skin in the game beyond a small fee, in a market requiring massive marketing spend to displace incumbents. This is value destruction disguised as expansion.

DECISION: NO-GO
