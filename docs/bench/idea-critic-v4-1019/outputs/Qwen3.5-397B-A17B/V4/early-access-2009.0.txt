First call: NO-GO. Releasing an unfinished, unsupported game to a broad public without a safety net invites reputational damage and support collapse that a solo developer cannot survive.

**Steelman:** Early revenue funds development, and real user data prevents building unwanted features, outweighing the risk of negative reviews if the core loop is fun.

**Attack & Options:**
The proposal ignores the "support cliff." A solo dev selling an alpha faces immediate, unmanageable ticket volume from a general audience, halting all coding. "Do nothing" preserves reputation but starves the project. A better alternative is a **closed, free technical beta** for a limited group (e.g., 500 users) under NDA or clear "bug-hunter" expectations. This validates the engine and gathers feedback without the financial contract that triggers entitlement and mass refunds.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
In 2009, "Early Access" as a validated model did not exist; selling broken games was typically seen as scams or failures. Assuming modern tolerance is a fatal error. If released now, the pre-mortem shows: Day 1 brings 500 support emails about crashes; the dev spends two weeks answering emails instead of fixing bugs; Steam/forum reviews tank due to missing features; sales stop; the project dies with a "abandoned" tag.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal admits the game is "unfinished" and the dev "cannot support a large audience." These are not risks; they are **blockers**. Selling a product implies a warranty of functionality and support. The math fails: Revenue from early adopters < Cost of reputation destruction + Time lost to support. The lack of marketing budget means relying on organic word-of-mouth, which will be negative if the first impression is a broken, unsupported product.

**Decision:**
Blockers:
1. Explicit admission of inability to support the audience generated by a sale.
2. Historical context (2009) where selling unfinished goods lacked the "Early Access" social contract, guaranteeing negative reception.

Risks:
1. Running out of money before feature completion.
2. Building features users don't want.

Kill Criteria (if pivoted to Closed Beta): Stop if >10% of testers report game-breaking bugs within 48 hours. Leading Metric: Retention rate of beta testers, not revenue.

DECISION: NO-GO
