First call: GO. The proposal targets a massive, underserved market with a radically simpler and cheaper solution, accepting a known trade-off (random vs. mapped) to achieve adoption.

Steelman: A low-cost, randomized robot provides "good enough" cleaning for daily maintenance, unlocking a new consumer segment priced out of complex mapping rivals, while mitigating execution risk through simplicity.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Cede the low-end market to future competitors; high opportunity cost.
2. Build a mapper: Prohibitive cost and time; likely failure given lack of experience.
3. Launch randomized (Proposed): Immediate entry, lower price point, simpler mechanics. It beats "do nothing" by creating a new category.

Fit: Aligns with a "democratize robotics" vision. Randomization is a feature for cost reduction, not a bug, assuming the goal is frequency of clean rather than perfection.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if random motion gets stuck constantly or misses entire rooms, leading to returns. However, simple bump-and-turn logic is robust. This is a two-way door; a failed launch provides data for a pivot or shutdown with limited sunk cost compared to a mapper. Cost of delay allows competitors to define the standard.

Evidence: The lack of interest from floor-care companies validates the direct-to-consumer approach; incumbents are blinded by high-margin commercial models. The "no consumer experience" risk is mitigated by the product's mechanical simplicity.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (trust, coverage) are inherent to the category, not specific flaws in this approach. The lack of guaranteed spot-cleaning is an acceptable trade-off for the price difference.

Risks: Brand perception as "dumb," higher support calls for stuck units. These are managed via kill criteria.

Kill Criteria: Stop if return rate exceeds 15% due to navigation failures within 6 months. Leading metric: Daily active usage (proving users accept random cleaning).

The proposal correctly identifies that a cheaper, simpler solution is better than no solution or an unbuildable perfect one. The skepticism regarding "randomness" misses the value proposition of affordability and autonomy.

DECISION: GO
