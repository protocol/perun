First call: NO-GO. The initial four-city test demonstrated weak demand; repeating the experiment with a different name and a free sample distribution does not address the core lack of product-market fit or the fundamental need for the item.

Steelman: Renaming and sampling might uncover a specific use case among office workers that general consumers missed, validating a niche before a costly national rollout.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** Rename and sample. Cost: Moderate (samples + rebranding). Risk: High (ignores prior failure signal). Reversibility: Low (brand confusion).
2. **Do Nothing:** Halt expansion. Cost: Zero. Risk: Lost potential upside. Reversibility: High.
3. **Pivot/Research:** Conduct qualitative interviews with the original test failures to understand *why* it failed before spending on samples. Cost: Low. Risk: Time delay. Reversibility: High.

Fit: The proposal assumes the failure was due to awareness or naming, not utility. Without evidence that "office workers" differ fundamentally from the initial test demographic regarding the *need* for re-positionable notes, this is an assumption, not a strategy.

Pre-mortem: The pilot fails because recipients view the notes as unnecessary clutter despite being free. The company wastes budget on a rename and samples, delaying the inevitable realization that the product solves no urgent problem, or that the price point is wrong. The "most recipients say they would buy" metric is flawed; stated intent in a free scenario rarely converts to paid sales.

Evidence: The only hard data is the "disappointing sales" in four cities. The proposal offers no corrected figures or new data suggesting the office segment is different. The reliance on stated intent ("say they would buy") rather than actual purchase behavior in the pilot is a critical flaw.

Blockers:
1. **Metric Validity:** The success criterion ("say they would buy") is a vanity metric that does not prove willingness to pay, contradicting the goal of a profitable national rollout.
2. **Root Cause Ignored:** The plan treats a potential utility/pricing failure as a marketing/naming failure without evidence.

Risks: Wasted sample costs; false positive from free users; brand dilution from renaming.

The proposal relies on a weak validation metric and ignores the primary signal of market rejection. It must be revised to test actual purchase intent or identify the specific utility gap before proceeding.

DECISION: NO-GO
