First call: NO-GO. The core mechanism—broadcasting sensitive user transactions to their social circle—fundamentally conflicts with the stated risk of privacy invasion, creating an immediate trust deficit that notifications cannot fix.

Steelman: A transparent, opt-out system leveraging social proof to drive ad revenue while giving users a brief window to suppress specific activities.

Attack: The proposal assumes a "notification step" and "option to decline" mitigate privacy concerns. This is false. The default state is exposure; the burden is on the user to constantly monitor and suppress sensitive data (medical purchases, gifts, controversial items). This creates high anxiety and inevitable errors. Once a purchase is broadcast, the "hard to withdraw" risk means embarrassment is permanent even if deleted later. Partners face immediate reputational damage from public commentary on their products before they can react.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserves trust, zero revenue lift.
2. Aggregated, anonymized insights for friends ("3 friends bought this"): Retains social proof without exposing individual transactions.
3. Explicit opt-in sharing per transaction: Higher friction, but aligns incentives and eliminates the "invasion" narrative.

Fit: Moving a north star of "connection" by turning private acts into public performance risks severing the connection entirely.

Pre-mortem: Launch occurs. Users immediately discover sensitive purchases (e.g., pregnancy tests, addiction treatments) appearing in friends' feeds. Media outrage ensues. Partners demand exit. The feature is killed, but brand trust is permanently fractured, causing user churn that outweighs ad revenue.

Evidence: The proposal admits purchases "can be sensitive" and the feature is "hard to withdraw." These are not hypothetical risks; they are inherent properties of the design. The "opt-out" model fails when the cost of failure (public embarrassment) is high.

Blockers:
1. The default behavior exposes sensitive data before user intervention, directly contradicting the need to prevent privacy invasion.
2. The irreversibility of social broadcasting makes the "opt-out" safety net insufficient for high-stakes transactions.

Risks: Partner backlash, regulatory scrutiny, mass user opt-out of the platform entirely.

Kill criteria: If >0.1% of shared transactions result in user-reported embarrassment or support tickets within week one, terminate immediately. Leading metric: User trust score (surveyed) rather than just ad revenue.

DECISION: NO-GO
