First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to launch and promote widely based on test markets that have not yet been described as successful, while admitting core user experience flaws (taste, lighting complexity) and regulatory barriers to the primary marketing angle ("cleaner").

Steelman: A heated tobacco product offers a genuine reduction in smoke and could satisfy health-conscious smokers if the taste and ignition mechanism are indistinguishable from or superior to conventional cigarettes, and if regulators permit specific harm-reduction claims.

Attack:
1.  **User Experience Blocker:** The proposal explicitly states smokers "may dislike the taste" and the lighting mechanism is "complicated." In a mass-market consumer good, especially one reliant on habit, friction in ignition and inferior taste are fatal adoption barriers. You cannot "promote widely" a product users find difficult to light and unpleasant to taste.
2.  **Regulatory Blocker:** The marketing strategy relies on promoting it as a "cleaner way to smoke." The proposal admits regulators "may object to health claims." Without the ability to make this specific claim, the value proposition collapses for the target demographic, yet the cost structure remains high due to complexity.
3.  **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** The argument leans on "development has already cost a very large sum." This is irrelevant to the go/no-go decision; spending more on a launch to recoup past spend is irrational if the unit economics and adoption drivers are broken.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids reputational damage from a failed launch and further regulatory scrutiny.
*   **Revise & Re-test:** Halt the "wide promotion" plan. Fix the ignition mechanism and taste profile first. Conduct rigorous blind taste tests against ordinary cigarettes. Secure regulatory pre-approval on specific wording before committing to a launch campaign.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, smokers try it once, struggle to light it, dislike the taste, and return to ordinary cigarettes. Regulators issue cease-and-desist orders on "cleaner" advertising. The brand suffers a high-visibility failure, and the massive development spend is totally written off.

Blockers:
1.  Unresolved negative user experience (taste/ignition) cited in the proposal itself.
2.  Unverified regulatory pathway for the core marketing claim.

Risks: High manufacturing complexity, consumer inertia.

The plan fails because it proposes a full launch despite admitting the product may be inferior in taste and usability and lacks confirmed regulatory clearance for its main selling point.

DECISION: NO-GO
