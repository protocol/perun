First call: NO-GO. The proposed $700 price point is prohibitively high compared to existing consoles, and the licensing model prevents the subsidization required to compete.

Steelman: Licensing spreads manufacturing risk and generates immediate royalty revenue without capital expenditure, leveraging partners' scale to penetrate the market with superior graphics.

However, the attack on this claim reveals fatal flaws based on the provided facts. The console market operates on a razor-and-blades model where incumbents sell hardware at a loss to secure lucrative game software sales. By forcing licensees to sell hardware at a profit to cover their costs and margins, the final consumer price hits $700. This is far above competing products. Early adopters may pay a premium, but not double or triple the market rate solely for graphics when cheaper alternatives exist.

Furthermore, the licensing model creates a misalignment of incentives. Incumbents control the ecosystem tightly; here, multiple manufacturers might compete on price or quality inconsistently, diluting the brand. More critically, if the hardware is too expensive due to the required profit margin for licensees, unit sales will stagnate. Low unit sales mean fewer game sales, which undermines the royalty stream on software—the actual long-term value driver. The proposal fails to address how to break the incumbent cycle of subsidized hardware.

Alternatives like manufacturing internally to subsidize costs or launching at a lower price point with less margin were not considered but are necessary to compete. The current plan guarantees a non-competitive price.

Blockers: The $700 price point (derived from the requirement that licensees make a profit) is factually stated as "far above existing consoles," ensuring market rejection against competitors selling at a loss.
Risks: Fragmented quality control among licensees; insufficient install base to attract game developers.

The plan cannot succeed as proposed because the economic model forces a price the market will not accept against subsidized competitors.

DECISION: NO-GO
