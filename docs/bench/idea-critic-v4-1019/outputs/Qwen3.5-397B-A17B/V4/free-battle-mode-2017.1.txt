First call: NO-GO. The plan ignores the studio's lack of free-to-play (F2P) operational experience while entering a winner-take-all market against an entrenched rival.

Steelman: Leverage existing assets to rapidly capture F2P market share with minimal capex, monetizing via cosmetics to create a new revenue stream without abandoning the core paid product.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High risk of failure due to operational inexperience and cannibalization; low probability of displacing the genre leader.
2. **Do Nothing:** Protects current paid revenue; misses F2P trend but avoids brand damage from a failed live-service attempt.
3. **Pilot/Consult:** Partner with an F2P publisher or run a small-scale beta to validate monetization before full build. This costs time but de-risks the business model.

Fit: Assumes "growth" is the north star, but ignores the "sustainability" constraint implied by the studio's paid-only history. A sound idea must move the north star without breaking the business model; this plan threatens the latter.

Pre-mortem: The mode launches, player count spikes then crashes due to lack of live-ops content cadence. Cosmetic sales are negligible because the studio cannot balance pricing or rarity for a non-paying audience. The free mode dilutes the brand, causing paid sales to drop without replacement revenue. Two months is insufficient to build the backend analytics and anti-cheat required for a competitive shooter.

Evidence: The proposal admits "the studio has not run a free-to-play business before." This is a factual blocker, not a risk. F2P success relies on live operations, data analytics, and community management—capabilities the team explicitly lacks. The "two months" timeline contradicts the complexity of launching a competitive multiplayer title against a market leader.

Blockers:
1. **Capability Gap:** The team lacks F2P operational experience (stated fact), making the business model execution impossible as proposed.
2. **Timeline Fallacy:** Two months is insufficient to build a competitive, stable multiplayer environment and the necessary live-ops infrastructure.

Risks: Cannibalization of paid sales; inability to compete with the rival's network effect.

The lack of required operational expertise is a fatal flaw that cannot be solved by reusing assets. The plan must be revised to include capability acquisition or a partnership before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
