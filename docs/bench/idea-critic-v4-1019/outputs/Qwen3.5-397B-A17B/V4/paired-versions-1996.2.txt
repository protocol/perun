First call: GO. The mechanic directly solves the core engagement problem (completion) by manufacturing social dependency, turning a single-player limitation into a network effect.

**Steelman:** Releasing two distinct versions forces interpersonal trade to achieve 100% completion, creating a viral loop that compensates for declining hardware sales through mandatory peer-to-peer interaction.

**Attack & Options:**
The "price grab" risk is real but mitigated if the creature divergence is significant enough to make solo play impossible. The alternative ("do nothing" or single version) guarantees lower engagement and zero organic word-of-mouth via trading. A "digital patch" alternative is impossible given the 1996 cartridge constraints. The "declining popularity" argument actually supports this: only a radical social hook can reverse the trend. Standard releases die quietly; this forces visibility.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
Fits the north star of "maximizing player interaction." If it fails, it will be because the creature differences are perceived as trivial or the link cable adoption is too low. However, waiting for hardware resurgence is a losing strategy; the software must drive the hardware utility now.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
No external data exists, but the logic holds: scarcity drives demand. The constraint (link cable) is a feature, not a bug, as it filters for dedicated users who will recruit others.
*Blockers:* NONE. The proposal addresses the publisher's sales fear by creating a unique value proposition unavailable to competitors.
*Risks:* Consumer backlash on price (mitigated by perceived value of collection); logistical complexity (double SKUs). These are execution risks, not fundamental flaws.

**Decision:**
The plan converts a weakening platform into a social necessity. The "two version" strategy is the only path to viral growth in this constrained environment. Proceed immediately with distinct creature sets to ensure trading is mandatory, not optional.

DECISION: GO
