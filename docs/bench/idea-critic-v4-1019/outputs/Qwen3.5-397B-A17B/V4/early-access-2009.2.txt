First call: NO-GO. Releasing an unfinished, unsupported product to a broad public without marketing or a support plan invites immediate reputational damage that a solo developer cannot recover from.

**Steelman:** Early revenue and user feedback will fund and guide development faster than working in isolation, validating demand before full completion.

**Attack:**
1.  **Support Collapse:** A "large audience" (implied by public sale) creates a support ticket volume a solo dev cannot handle. Unanswered bugs lead to negative reviews, killing sales instantly. The proposal admits no support capacity exists.
2.  **Value Mismatch:** Selling an "unfinished" game sets an expectation of a product, not a donation. If the rough state outweighs the fun, refunds and bad word-of-mouth will outpace feature updates.
3.  **Discovery Failure:** With zero marketing budget, how does the audience find the game? Relying on "build it and they will come" in 2009 is a fatal assumption for a rough title.
4.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" alternative (finish privately) preserves reputation. A superior alternative is a **free** technical demo to a niche community to gather feedback without the transactional expectation of a finished product, or a capped "founder's circle" pre-order with explicit, legally binding disclaimers on missing features and support limits.

**Pre-mortem:** The game launches, players encounter game-breaking bugs, the developer is overwhelmed by emails, Steam/portal reviews tank at 20% positive, and the project is abandoned due to toxicity and burnout.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capacity Mismatch:** The proposal explicitly states the dev "cannot support a large audience" yet proposes a public sale that risks attracting one. This is a self-contradictory operational plan.
*   **Reputation Risk:** Negative early access reviews are permanent and disproportionately damaging to solo devs; the plan lacks a mitigation strategy for this specific high-probability outcome.

**Risks:** Market indifference, feature creep based on noisy feedback, cash flow insufficiency.

**Kill Criteria:** If a free demo fails to retain 20% of players for 30 minutes, do not sell. If support tickets exceed 10/day, pause sales immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
