First call: NO-GO. The core mechanism trades user trust for ad data by exposing sensitive off-platform behavior to a user's social graph, a failure mode that notifications and opt-outs cannot mitigate.

**Steelman:** Users will tolerate purchase sharing if given notice and a decline option, generating incremental ad revenue without triggering mass churn.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Mismatch:** An "option to decline" defaults to sharing until acted upon. Most users ignore notifications or misunderstand settings. Sensitive purchases (medical, financial, personal) will leak immediately upon launch before users can react.
2.  **Partner Risk:** Partners fear public embarrassment from negative friend reactions to their products appearing in feeds. They will likely block the integration or demand legal indemnities, killing the supply of trackable data.
3.  **Irreversibility:** Once private behavior is socialized, trust is broken permanently. Unlike a UI bug, you cannot "patch" the feeling of betrayal. The "hard to withdraw" risk is actually a certainty; the feature creates a permanent liability.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves current trust levels. A cheaper alternative is aggregating anonymized purchase data for ad targeting *without* broadcasting specific transactions to friends. This captures revenue upside without the nuclear risk of social exposure.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal admits purchases can be sensitive and the feature is hard to withdraw.
*   **Contradiction:** You cannot launch a system designed to expose sensitive data by default and claim an "opt-out" protects users. The damage occurs before the opt-out is exercised.
*   **Evidence:** The stated risk of user backlash is not a possibility but a near-certainty given the sensitivity of financial/health data. No metric or pilot can undo the PR catastrophe of the first leaked sensitive purchase.

**Risks:** (None need listing as blockers already decide the call).

The proposal relies on users actively protecting their own privacy against a default designed to invade it. This fails the basic test of product safety. The existence of a "decline" button does not neutralize the harm of default public exposure for sensitive activities.

DECISION: NO-GO
