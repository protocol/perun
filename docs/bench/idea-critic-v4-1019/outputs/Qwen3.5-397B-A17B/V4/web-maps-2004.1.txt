First call: GO. The shift from desktop to web, combined with satellite imagery and routing, creates a fundamentally superior user experience that outweighs the stated risks for a search company with distribution leverage.

**Steelman:** Leveraging the acquirer's search dominance to distribute a free, browser-based mapping tool with unique satellite data will rapidly acquire users, creating a defensive moat and long-term strategic value despite high initial licensing costs and technical browser limitations.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Retain the desktop niche. Result: Slow obsolescence as the web becomes the primary computing platform.
*   **Paid/Licensed Model:** Generates immediate revenue but caps adoption, failing to leverage the acquirer's core asset: traffic.
*   **Technical/Browser Limits:** In 2004, browsers are indeed slow for graphics. However, this is a transient engineering hurdle, not a permanent blocker. Specialized rendering techniques (pre-fetching, tiled images) can mitigate this.
*   **Cost/Revenue:** Licensing is expensive, and direct revenue is unclear. However, for a search company, maps are not a profit center but a query enhancer. The "revenue" is increased search stickiness, data collection, and ad inventory expansion. The cost is justified as customer acquisition expense.
*   **Competition:** Incumbents exist, but none offer the specific combination of *free*, *web-native*, and *satellite* views at search-engine scale.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if the engineering team underestimates the browser rendering challenge, resulting in a sluggish product that users reject. It also fails if licensing negotiations stall, leaving the service empty.

**Blockers:** None. The risks are executional (engineering difficulty, cost management) rather than fundamental flaws in the premise. The lack of a direct revenue model is not a blocker for a search company whose business model is advertising; the map serves as a high-value engagement layer.

**Risks:** High burn rate on data licensing; potential performance issues on legacy browsers; difficulty monetizing directly.

**Kill Criteria:** If active user growth does not exceed 10% month-over-month within six months of launch, or if licensing costs exceed 20% of the projected incremental ad revenue value, pivot to a limited API model or cease expansion.

**Decision:** The strategic fit for a search giant is perfect. The technical and financial hurdles are significant but surmountable and secondary to the opportunity to own the geospatial layer of the web.

DECISION: GO
