First call: GO. The growth of small-computer makers signals a market shift that the current integrated model cannot capture quickly enough; a separate team with off-the-shelf parts is the only viable path to speed.

Steelman: A dedicated, isolated team can bypass internal inertia to deliver a competitive personal computer within one year by leveraging existing ecosystem components and retail channels, sacrificing margin for market entry speed.

Options analysis confirms this approach beats "do nothing," which cedes the emerging segment to rivals. Building custom parts or using internal sales channels would delay entry beyond the critical window, failing to address the observed growth trend. While margins are lower and copycats are a risk, the primary value is establishing a foothold in a new category, not maximizing per-unit profit immediately.

The proposal fits the strategic necessity of addressing the "small-computer" threat. Waiting for perfect conditions or higher margins is a one-way door to irrelevance in this specific segment. The cost of delay—losing the entire personal computer market—is far greater than the risk of lower margins or imitation.

Evidence is the observed fast growth of competitors. The risks raised (copying, supplier dependence, lower margins, no retail experience) are valid but manageable. Copying is inherent to open designs but speed to market creates the brand defense. Supplier dependence is the trade-off for speed. Lower margins are accepted for volume and footprint. Lack of retail experience is mitigated by partnering with independent stores already in place.

Blockers: NONE. The facts presented show a clear market opportunity that the current operating model cannot seize. The proposed structure (separate team, off-the-shelf parts) directly addresses the speed constraint.
Risks: Imitation and channel conflict are real but secondary to the existential risk of missing the market shift.

Kill criteria: If the team cannot prototype with off-the-shelf parts within 6 months, or if independent retailers refuse to carry the unit due to design flaws, pivot or stop. Leading metric: Units sold through independent retailers in the first quarter post-launch.

DECISION: GO
