First call: GO. The $35 price point creates a new category of ownership for students, distinct from shared family tablets, directly serving the charity's mission despite execution risks.

**Steelman:** A ultra-low-cost, bare-bones computer removes the financial barrier to entry, forcing engagement with hardware and code in a way pre-assembled consumer devices do not.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Students continue using locked-down or shared consumer devices; programming interest remains stagnant.
2.  **Subsidize laptops:** Cost per unit is 5-10x higher ($200+), drastically reducing reach for a fixed budget.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** High risk of supply chain failure and lack of support, but offers the only path to ubiquitous, individual ownership at a toy-like price.

**Fit:** Aligns perfectly with the "rekindle interest" north star by making the computer a project itself, not just an appliance.

**Pre-mortem:** The venture fails if the charity cannot manage manufacturing quality or if the "bring your own peripherals" friction proves too high for schools. However, these are execution hurdles, not fundamental flaws. The lack of experience is mitigated by licensing distributors to handle logistics.

**Evidence:** The core claim rests on the $35 Bill of Materials being achievable in 2012. Assuming this figure is accurate based on component commoditization, the economic argument holds. The risk of selling out is a sign of demand, not a reason to stop; it dictates a capped initial run.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented proves the plan fails as proposed. The lack of experience is a risk, not a blocker.
**Risks:** Supply chain bottlenecks, insufficient peripheral availability in schools, potential quality control issues. These become kill criteria: if the first batch cannot be fulfilled within 6 months or defect rates exceed 5%, the project pauses.

The alternative (doing nothing or buying expensive laptops) guarantees failure to reach the scale required. The proposed path is the only one with a non-zero probability of massive success.

DECISION: GO
