First call: GO. The core mechanic transforms a single-player limitation into a viral social engine, directly addressing the publisher's sales uncertainty by manufacturing demand through scarcity and social obligation.

**Steelman:** Releasing two distinct versions forces interdependence, making the link cable not an accessory but a requirement for 100% completion, thereby driving unit sales beyond the declining install base via peer-to-peer recruitment.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Release one complete version. Risk: Low engagement, no viral loop, sales capped by existing fanbase.
*   *DLC/Expansion:* Technically impossible on 1996 cartridge hardware without complex peripherals; high cost, low friction.
*   *Price Grab Perception:* Valid risk. Mitigation: Market as "different experiences" rather than "cut content." The value prop is the social event, not just the data.
*   *Declining Hardware:* The software must justify the hardware. This mechanic creates a "must-have" exclusive that revitalizes the platform's utility, turning the aging console into a social token.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the creature pools are too imbalanced (one version objectively superior) or if the trading process is too brittle. If players cannot easily find partners, the mechanic becomes a frustration point rather than a feature.

**Evidence & Fit:** The proposal relies on behavioral psychology (completionism + social pressure) rather than technical novelty. No external data exists yet; the "evidence" is the logical inevitability of the mechanic forcing interaction.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (popularity, price perception, hardware requirements) are market conditions, not factual contradictions of the plan's internal logic. The plan specifically leverages the hardware constraint (link cable) as a feature.

**Risks:** Consumer backlash if perceived as greedy; failure to launch if critical mass isn't reached in local pockets. These are managed by marketing narrative and regional rollout, not by changing the core product architecture.

**Decision:** The mechanism aligns perfectly with the goal of maximizing sales despite a shrinking platform. It turns a weakness (aging hardware, isolated play) into the primary strength.

DECISION: GO
