First call: NO-GO. Automatic enrollment with default public visibility for a social layer inside a private communication tool violates the core expectation of inbox privacy and invites immediate regulatory and reputational backlash.

**Steelman:** Leveraging the existing user base via opt-out enrollment is the only way to achieve critical mass quickly enough to compete with dedicated social networks, treating email contacts as a pre-validated social graph.

**Attack:**
1.  **Context Mismatch:** Email is a private, task-oriented channel. Social networking is public and leisure-oriented. Forcing social behavior into a private utility creates cognitive dissonance and distrust. Users do not want their professional or personal contacts automatically notified of their activity.
2.  **Regulatory & Trust Blocker:** In 2010, privacy regulations (and public sentiment) are increasingly sensitive to data usage. Changing privacy defaults retrospectively on existing users without explicit consent is a definitive trust violation. This is not a risk; it is a guaranteed catalyst for churn and regulatory scrutiny.
3.  **Reversibility:** The proposal admits the launch is "hard to reverse once connections are exposed." This is a one-way door with high downside. Once private contact maps are made visible, the damage to user trust is permanent, even if the feature is disabled.
4.  **Alternatives:** An opt-in model respects user agency. While growth may be slower, it ensures that early adopters actually want the feature, providing valid signal rather than inflated, resentful metrics. "Do nothing" preserves the core value proposition of the webmail service: reliable, private communication.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Automatic enrollment changes privacy settings for existing users without consent.
*   **Consequence:** Immediate violation of user trust and high probability of regulatory intervention.
*   **Contradiction:** The proposal acknowledges the difficulty of reversal but proceeds with a mechanism that makes reversal insufficient to repair trust.

**Risks:** Low adoption due to context mismatch; brand damage associating the search/mail brand with privacy violations.

**Decision Logic:** The plan fails the fundamental test of fitting the product's primary value (privacy/utility). The "cheaper, safer alternative" (opt-in) exists and achieves the same upside (social graph creation) without the existential risk. The stated facts confirm a negative outcome (hard to reverse, privacy disregard) that removes the route to success.

DECISION: NO-GO
