First call: NO-GO. The proposal risks the studio's entire solvency on an unproven format with a budget ten times the norm, against explicit advice from industry insiders and the founder's family, while relying on thin profits from shorts to fund it.

**Steelman:** A feature-length film could unlock a new revenue tier and establish market dominance if audiences accept the format.
**Attack:** The core assumption—that audiences will sit through a feature—is untested. If they do not, the studio faces catastrophic loss, as the budget consumes years of short-film profits. The "thin" existing margins offer no buffer for the predicted overruns.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue steady, thin profits; zero risk of insolvency.
2.  **Staged pilot:** Produce a medium-length special (3x cost) to test audience endurance before committing to a full feature.
3.  **Proposed plan:** All-in on a 10x budget feature immediately.
Option 2 offers a path to validation without immediate existential risk. Option 3 fails to beat "do nothing" on safety and lacks evidence of demand.

**Fit:** Assumes the goal is aggressive growth over stability. However, risking the entire enterprise contradicts the duty to preserve the ongoing business.

**Pre-mortem:** The film releases, audiences laugh for 20 minutes then leave, the studio cannot fund the next short, and bankruptcy follows within a year. This is a one-way door; once the cash is spent, it cannot be recovered. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of failure.

**Evidence:** No data exists on feature-length cartoon viability. The "evidence" at hand is negative: insiders and family advise against it. The financial math shows a single failure wipes out the funding source (shorts).

**Blockers:**
1.  **Unproven Demand:** No evidence audiences will watch a feature; high probability of total rejection.
2.  **Existential Financial Risk:** Budget (10x) exceeds the capacity of "thin" profits to absorb failure; one miss kills the studio.
3.  **Expert Dissent:** Unified advice from insiders and family indicates hidden structural flaws.

**Risks:** Budget overrun, production collapse, brand damage.

The plan lacks a pilot, a cap, or any proof of concept. It bets the company on a hypothesis contradicted by current experts.

DECISION: NO-GO
