First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to enter a saturated market with an unproven business model while threatening existing revenue, all under an unrealistic timeline.

**Steelman:** Reusing assets allows a rapid, low-cost entry into the popular last-player-standing genre to capture users via a free-to-play model, diversifying revenue beyond the initial paid sale.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Fit:** A rival already dominates the genre. Entering late with a clone offers no unique value proposition to displace the incumbent.
2.  **Cannibalization:** Making a core mode free directly devalues the paid cooperative game. Existing customers may demand refunds or feel cheated, while potential buyers will wait for the free version.
3.  **Operational Reality:** The studio lacks free-to-play experience (live ops, anti-cheat, monetization balancing). Building this infrastructure in two months is impossible; rushing it guarantees a broken economy or security failures.
4.  **Timeline:** Two months is insufficient to design, test, and balance a competitive multiplayer mode, even with asset reuse.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Protects the paid game's value and avoids operational overreach.
*   *DLC Expansion:* Add cooperative content to the paid game. This leverages existing strengths and monetizes the current user base without competing with the rival's genre.
*   *Pivot:* Delay launch to build a proper live-ops team, though this increases cost and risk.

**Pre-mortem:** The mode launches in two months with unbalanced weapons and no anti-cheat. The paid game's sales collapse due to perceived devaluation. The studio cannot sustain the server costs or content cycle required for free-to-play, shutting down the mode within six months after brand damage.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Incentives:** The plan explicitly risks cannibalizing the only proven revenue stream (paid game) with an unproven one.
*   **Capability Gap:** The team has zero experience in the required business model (free-to-play), making success highly improbable.
*   **Impossible Timeline:** Two months is factually inadequate for a stable competitive multiplayer launch.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, community backlash, wasted development resources.

**Conclusion:** The proposal fails on strategy, capability, and timeline. It attacks the studio's current strength to chase a rival's strength without a differentiator.

DECISION: NO-GO
