First call: NO-GO. The $1,000 price point creates an immediate mass-market barrier that the stated value proposition cannot clear.

**Steelman:** A dedicated $1,000 appliance bridges the gap between passive TV and complex PCs by offering superior interactive media performance today.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Substitutes:** At $1,000, this device costs significantly more than game consoles and competes directly with full personal computers, which are rapidly acquiring the exact multimedia capabilities proposed. The "middle" position is a value trap; consumers will pay less for a console or more for a versatile PC.
2.  **Content Chicken-and-Egg:** The proposal admits the library of interactive titles will be small. High hardware costs suppress install base, which discourages software development, further reducing hardware appeal. This feedback loop is fatal at launch.
3.  **Market Confusion:** If buyers do not understand the product's purpose, a $1,000 price tag ensures they will not experiment to find out. High friction kills adoption for undefined categories.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for PC multimedia standards to mature and prices to drop. Cost: $0. Risk: Missing first-mover branding. Reversibility: N/A.
*   **PC Partnership:** License the technology to PC manufacturers instead of building a standalone $1,000 box. Cost: Lower CapEx. Risk: Brand dilution. Reversibility: High.
*   **Lower-Cost Entry:** Launch a stripped-down player under $300 to build install base first. Cost: Lower margin. Risk: Perceived as a toy. Reversibility: Medium.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to poor sales due to price shock. Developers ignore the tiny user base. The unit is discounted heavily within 18 months, destroying brand equity, and the project is cancelled as PCs absorb the market.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price Mismatch:** The $1,000 cost exceeds the perceived value of a single-function media player when a multi-function PC is nearby in price.
*   **Content Void:** The admitted lack of software titles removes the primary justification for the high hardware cost.

**Risks:** Market confusion, rapid PC commoditization of features.

**Conclusion:** The proposal fails the basic unit economics of consumer electronics in 1991. It asks users to pay a premium for a device that does less than the alternative (PC) and costs far more than the entertainment alternative (console).

DECISION: NO-GO
