First call: NO-GO. The plan attempts to solve three hard problems (cross-border logistics, unproven consumer behavior, and capital efficiency) simultaneously with maximum surface area, violating basic operational constraints for a 1998 startup.

Steelman: A massive, simultaneous transatlantic launch creates an unassailable brand moat before incumbents can react, leveraging first-mover advantage in a nascent market.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital, but cede the emerging online channel to future competitors.
2. Single-market pilot (e.g., UK or Germany only): Validates logistics and demand with 10% of the cost; reversible if failed.
3. Niche vertical focus (sports only): Reduces inventory complexity while testing the fulfillment model.

Fit: While the vision of a global retailer is clear, the execution strategy ignores the "north star" of survival. In 1998, internet penetration and trust in online payments are low; assuming immediate global scale is a fantasy, not a strategy.

Pre-mortem: The company runs out of cash within 18 months. Marketing spend is diluted across too many regions to gain traction anywhere. Logistics networks fracture under cross-border complexity, leading to delayed shipments and brand damage. Venture funding dries up due to missed milestones, forcing a fire sale.

Door/Delay: This is a one-way door; once capital is burned on failed international offices, it cannot be recovered. The cost of delay is low; being six months later with a proven single-market model is vastly superior to being early and bankrupt.

Evidence: The proposal admits online fashion buying is "unproven" and venture money "may run out." These are not risks to be managed; they are facts that contradict the core plan. No evidence suggests 1998 infrastructure supports simultaneous multi-country fulfillment.

Blockers:
1. Capital mismatch: The burn rate of multi-country offices exceeds likely venture runway given unproven unit economics.
2. Operational impossibility: No existing logistics partner in 1998 supports seamless pan-European/American fashion fulfillment for a startup.
3. Unproven demand: Launching everywhere assumes universal product-market fit without a single data point.

Risks: Brand dilution, inventory obsolescence, regulatory hurdles in multiple jurisdictions.

Kill criteria: None possible as the plan fails at step one.

DECISION: NO-GO
