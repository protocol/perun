First call: NO-GO. Doubling production while sales lag by 60% during a recession with rising interest rates destroys cash without a verified buyer.

Steelman: The claim is that flooding the market now forces dealer uptake or achieves scale efficiencies that outweigh the immediate cash burn, betting on a rapid economic turnaround.

Options:
1. **Double Production:** Maximizes cash burn; risks insolvency if demand does not instantly rebound. High irreversibility (cash spent).
2. **Do Nothing/Reduce:** Preserves cash, aligns output with demand. Low risk, but may miss a sudden recovery.
3. **Incentivize Sales:** Use limited cash for customer financing subsidies rather than building unsold inventory. Targets the root cause (demand/cost of money).

Fit: A survival-stage company's north star is liquidity. This proposal contradicts the verified state of recession and high interest rates which suppress demand.

Pre-mortem: The company doubles output, inventory sits unsold because dealers cannot finance it due to high rates, cash runs out in months, and the firm enters bankruptcy before the economy recovers. This is a one-way door; spent cash cannot be recovered.

Evidence: The proposal admits 4,500 unsold units (7,500 produced - 3,000 sold). Interest rates are rising, directly increasing financing costs for both the manufacturer and dealers. No evidence suggests demand will spike immediately to absorb 2x output.

Blockers:
1. **Cash Flow Mismatch:** Producing more cars requires immediate cash outflow for parts and labor, while revenue is delayed by slow sales. With limited cash and rising borrowing costs, this path leads directly to insolvency.
2. **Dealer Capacity:** Dealers cannot finance more stock due to high interest rates; pushing more inventory onto them creates friction without moving units to end users.

Risks: Economic recession deepening; inability to secure bridge financing; inventory depreciation.

The plan fails the basic test of liquidity preservation during a credit crunch. Increasing supply when demand is constrained by macroeconomic factors and financing costs is a fatal strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
