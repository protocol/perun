First call: NO-GO. The core claim—that luxury buyers will accept a mass-market platform with added trim at a premium price—contradicts the fundamental driver of luxury sales: exclusivity and distinct engineering.

Steelman: A compact entry point captures American brand switchers who want luxury status but smaller dimensions, leveraging shared costs to maximize margin.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the 1981 context. Luxury buyers in this era purchase status and engineering superiority, not just "trim." If the platform is visibly shared with mass-market siblings (as history shows with badge-engineering failures), the "premium price" becomes indefensible. The risk that "buyers may see through" is not a risk; it is a near-certainty given the scrutiny of this demographic. Furthermore, if salespeople refuse to endorse the car, the channel collapses. The brand's prestige is the primary asset; diluting it with a "fake" luxury product threatens the entire portfolio's pricing power.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve brand equity; miss the segment.
2. Develop a unique small platform: High cost, but protects brand integrity.
3. Acquire an existing small luxury competitor: Faster, cleaner brand separation.

The proposal fails the "fit" test. A luxury brand cannot sustain a premium price on a mass-market foundation without deceiving the customer, which destroys long-term trust. The "better way" is to either build a truly distinct small car or wait until the brand can justify the segment without cannibalizing its core value proposition. The stated risks (platform transparency, salesforce resistance, prestige erosion) are not hypothetical; they are structural blockers that the proposal admits but does not solve.

Blockers:
1. The value proposition relies on customers *not* noticing the mass-market underpinnings, which is unsustainable for a knowledgeable luxury buyer.
2. Internal channel resistance (salespeople) ensures poor execution regardless of product quality.

Risks: Brand dilution, inventory pile-up due to unjustified pricing.

Kill criteria: If initial focus groups identify the mass-market donor platform within 30 seconds, cancel immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
