First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on unverified assumptions about developer adoption and hardware execution without a mechanism to secure either before the crowdfunding commitment.

**Steelman:** A crowdfunded, open-software console can succeed by targeting a niche underserved by closed ecosystems, leveraging community-driven game development to bypass traditional publisher gatekeepers, provided hardware risks are mitigated by using off-the-shelf components.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** High risk. Requires simultaneous success in hardware manufacturing, developer recruitment, and marketing with zero budget. Failure in any pillar kills the product.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, zero risk. Misses the potential niche opportunity but preserves capital.
3.  **Software-First Alternative:** Build the open software platform for existing hardware (PC/Raspberry Pi) first. Validates developer interest and creates a library before committing to custom silicon. Lower cost, reversible, proves the core value proposition.

**Fit:** Assuming the goal is to establish a new gaming platform, jumping straight to custom hardware contradicts the constraint of "no marketing budget" and "no experience." Successful platforms solve the chicken-and-egg problem (users vs. developers) before scaling hardware.

**Pre-mortem:** The campaign succeeds, raising funds, but the team encounters a 6-month supply chain delay (common in hardware). Backers demand refunds; the team burns cash on incomplete units. Without a shipped game library, early adopters return the device. The project collapses due to cash flow insolvency before public release.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "hardware delays are common" and "team has no marketing budget" yet proceeds with a hardware-first strategy. These are stated facts that directly contradict the feasibility of the timeline and distribution model. There is no evidence of existing developer commitment or a pre-order list to validate demand.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The team has no marketing budget and no existing audience.
*   **Contradiction:** Crowdfunding success for hardware historically requires significant pre-launch marketing momentum to reach funding goals.
*   **Fact:** Developers will not build for a platform with no users.
*   **Contradiction:** The plan offers no incentive or existing user base to attract developers prior to launch.

**Risks:** Supply chain failure, insufficient game library, inability to compete with established consoles.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It requires solving the hardest problems (hardware execution, developer adoption) with the fewest resources (no budget, no track record) before validating demand. The "Software-First" alternative reaches the same upside (validating the platform) with significantly lower risk and cost.

DECISION: NO-GO
