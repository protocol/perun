First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to spend heavily on marketing a material that costs more than leather, offers lower comfort, and faces resistance from manufacturers, with no stated upside to justify these deficits.

Steelman: The synthetic offers superior durability and a consistent "leather-like" appearance that could appeal to specific high-wear or uniform markets if the comfort and cost gaps are closed.

Attack:
1. **Economics:** The material costs *more* than leather. Shoe manufacturers operate on thin margins; they will not adopt a pricier input that yields a less comfortable product without a massive consumer pull.
2. **Consumer Value:** "Less comfortable" is a fatal flaw for footwear. Durability does not compensate for pain. Fashion volatility makes a heavy marketing spend on a potentially unloved material reckless.
3. **Adoption Friction:** Manufacturers are unwilling to retool. Without a clear cost saving or performance leap, they have zero incentive to switch.
4. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A better path is to target non-footwear applications (luggage, upholstery) where comfort is secondary and durability is primary, or to R&D until cost/comfort parity is reached.

Blockers:
- **Negative Unit Economics:** Higher cost + lower performance = no market fit.
- **Channel Resistance:** Manufacturers explicitly unwilling to change factories.
- **End-User Rejection:** Inferior comfort guarantees low repeat purchase regardless of marketing.

Risks:
- Fashion trends shifting away from the aesthetic.
- Sunk cost in marketing before product-market fit is proven.

The proposal fails the basic test of offering a better or cheaper alternative to the status quo. It attempts to solve adoption friction with marketing spend rather than product merit. Launching now guarantees financial loss.

DECISION: NO-GO
