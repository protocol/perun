First call: GO. The new plant creates a fixed cost imperative; filling it with a product matching the facility's design (compact, 1100 cc) is the only way to avoid immediate financial bleed, outweighing the risk of dealer setup costs.

**Steelman:** Launching a dedicated 1100 cc compact via a new dealer network is the minimal viable path to utilize the new plant's capacity, securing unit economics that existing rivals cannot match on this specific chassis.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** High upfront dealer cost, but secures plant utilization and brand segmentation.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Plant sits underutilized; fixed costs drain capital without revenue offset.
3.  **Larger/Cheaper Car:** Mismatches the new plant's tooled capacity or cannibalizes existing low-margin lines.
The proposal beats "do nothing" because idle capacity is a guaranteed loss, whereas dealer costs are an investment with a clear path to payback via volume.

**Fit:** Assumes the strategic north star is maximizing the return on the newly built asset. The 1100 cc segment fits the "compact family" vision implied by the plant's construction.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the dealer network takes too long to recruit, leaving cars built but unsold, or if price wars force margins below the break-even point needed to service the plant debt. This is a two-way door; if sales lag, the dealer network can be consolidated or the car sold through existing channels later, though at a brand cost. The cost of delay is the daily burn rate of the idle plant.

**Evidence:** Based on the stated fact that the plant exists and must run near capacity. No external data contradicts the 1100 cc suitability.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (dealer cost, rivalry, buyer preference) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The plant's existence makes inaction more dangerous than action.

**Risks:** Dealer network ramp-up speed; price sensitivity in a crowded small-car market. These become kill criteria: if dealer sign-ups fall 20% short of target by Q2, or if average transaction price drops below cost-plus-10%, pivot to shared distribution.

**Decision:** The necessity of filling the new plant overrides the caution regarding dealer costs.

DECISION: GO
