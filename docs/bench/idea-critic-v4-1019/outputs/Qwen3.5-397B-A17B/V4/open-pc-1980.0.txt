First call: GO. The market shift toward small computers is an existential threat to a mainframe-centric model; failing to enter this segment risks obsolescence regardless of margin compression.

Steelman: A dedicated, isolated team can bypass corporate inertia to rapidly deploy a commodity-based PC via retail channels, capturing market share before the window closes.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain high margins but cede the emerging personal computing market to agile rivals, likely leading to long-term decline.
2. Internal development: Using standard processes and proprietary parts would miss the critical one-year window, rendering the product irrelevant upon launch.
3. Acquisition: Likely too slow and culturally difficult to integrate for the required speed.

Fit: This diverges from current vertical integration but aligns with the survival imperative of addressing the "small-computer" growth vector.

Pre-mortem: The project fails if the mainframe culture suffocates the skunkworks team, if supply chain dependencies cause delays, or if the open design allows clones to undercut price before scale is achieved. However, the cost of delay (missing the 1980-1981 explosion) outweighs these execution risks. The door is two-way; if the retail experiment fails, the company can retreat with limited brand damage compared to missing the trend entirely.

Evidence: The premise relies on the observed rapid growth of small-computer makers versus the stagnation of the mainframe niche. The proposal's admission of lower margins and copy risks is honest; these are features of the commodity market, not bugs in the plan.

Blockers: NONE. The stated risks (copying, supplier dependence, lower margins, no retail experience) are inherent to the target market, not flaws in the proposed approach. They are mitigated by speed and the separation of the team.

Risks: Execution failure due to cultural clash; slower-than-expected ramp in retail channels. These become kill criteria: if the team cannot ship within 12 months or secure key retail partners within 6, the project must pivot or stop.

The proposal correctly identifies that doing things "contrary to usual practice" is the only way to succeed in this new domain.

DECISION: GO
