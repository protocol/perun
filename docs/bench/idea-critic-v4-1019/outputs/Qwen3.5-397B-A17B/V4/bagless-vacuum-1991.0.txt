First call: GO. The core claim—that a superior, bagless technology can command a premium despite higher costs and incumbent competition—is defensible if the performance gap is undeniable.

**Steelman:** A cyclone-based vacuum offers sustained suction and eliminates recurring bag costs, creating a distinct value proposition that justifies a higher upfront price for frustrated consumers, bypassing traditional retail via direct channels if necessary.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Cost/Price:** Higher unit cost is a blocker only if buyers refuse the premium. If the "bagless" benefit is tangible (no loss of suction), the market exists. Do nothing yields zero return; licensing to incumbents risks shelving the tech (a known historical tactic).
2.  **Incumbents:** Large rivals copying the idea validates the market but takes time. First-mover advantage in branding ("the bagless one") creates a moat before clones arrive.
3.  **Manufacturing:** Lack of record is a risk, not a blocker. Contract manufacturing or phased rollout mitigates this.
4.  **Adoption:** Buyers won't pay extra for "features," but they will pay to solve a pain point (suction loss). The pitch must focus on performance, not novelty.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the founder runs out of cash trying to build a factory before proving demand, or if the price premium exceeds the perceived value of "no bags." The door is two-way; if sales stall, the IP can still be sold. Delay costs first-mover status and allows incumbents to saturate the narrative.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal admits risks but offers no internal contradiction. The higher price is a feature of the premium positioning, not a flaw. The lack of manufacturing history is a standard startup hurdle, not a fatal defect.

**Risks:** Cash flow exhaustion before scale; incumbents launching a cheaper copycat quickly; consumer inertia regarding bag purchases.

**Kill Criteria:** Halt if pre-orders or initial batch sales do not cover variable costs within 18 months, or if a major incumbent releases a functionally identical unit at 20% lower price within 12 months.

**Decision:** The plan addresses the primary value driver (performance) and accepts the necessary trade-offs (price, distribution). The risks are executable, not structural.

DECISION: GO
