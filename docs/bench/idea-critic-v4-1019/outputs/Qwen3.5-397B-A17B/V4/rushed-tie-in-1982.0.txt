First call: NO-GO. Compressing a complex software project to five weeks while committing to massive hardware inventory ignores basic development variance and demand uncertainty.

**Steelman:** A single elite developer can distill the film's core appeal into a minimal viable product within five weeks, and the brand's strength guarantees sufficient sales to clear a large inventory run before the holiday deadline.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capacity vs. Scope:** Five weeks allows only for a trivial prototype, not a polished commercial product. Any unforeseen technical hurdle (likely in a new environment) consumes the entire buffer, guaranteeing a missed deadline or a broken product.
2.  **Inventory Risk:** Ordering a "very large" run before validating quality or market reception creates a binary outcome: total sell-out or catastrophic loss. The high license fee is a sunk cost; compounding it with high variable costs (cartridges) amplifies downside exposure without verifying the asset's value.
3.  **Single Point of Failure:** Relying on one programmer means illness, burnout, or a single architectural misstep kills the entire project. There is no redundancy.
4.  **Alternatives:** A smaller initial batch reduces inventory risk. A slightly delayed release into Q1 avoids the Christmas crunch while allowing time for quality assurance. Both alternatives preserve capital better than the proposed "all-in" strategy.

**Pre-mortem:** The game ships late or buggy. Retailers return unsold units. The company is left with warehouses of expensive plastic and silicon, having paid a premium for rights to a product that damaged the brand rather than extending it. The "Christmas deadline" forces a choice between shipping nothing or shipping garbage.

**Blockers:**
*   **Time:** Five weeks is objectively insufficient for full-cycle development, testing, and manufacturing lead times for a complex title.
*   **Inventory Commitment:** Locking in large-scale production before verifying the product's quality or market fit violates basic risk management.

**Risks:** Brand damage, total capital loss on inventory, developer burnout.

**Conclusion:** The plan conflates hope with strategy. It bets the entire investment on a perfect execution window that does not exist in software development. The inventory commitment is unjustifiable without a proven product.

DECISION: NO-GO
