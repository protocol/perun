First call: NO-GO. The proposal asserts a premium launch despite higher costs, unproven comfort, and significant manufacturer friction without evidence of consumer demand or a comfort advantage.

Steelman: If the synthetic offers unique durability or consistency that justifies a price premium, and if marketing can overcome manufacturer inertia, the product could carve a niche. However, the current facts suggest it is more expensive and potentially inferior in comfort.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High spend, premium positioning. Cost: High. Risk: High (rejection on price/comfort). Reversibility: Low (brand damage).
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoid sunk costs. Cost: Zero. Risk: Missing a future trend. Reversibility: N/A.
3. **Industrial/B2B First:** Target non-apparel uses (e.g., upholstery, automotive) where comfort is less critical and durability is paramount. Cost: Moderate. Risk: Lower market size initially. Reversibility: High.

Fit: The proposal assumes fashion will embrace a costlier, less comfortable alternative without a stated "North Star" benefit like animal welfare or extreme weather resistance. It conflicts with basic economic incentives for both manufacturers (cost) and consumers (comfort).

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because shoe manufacturers refuse to retool for a material that increases their unit cost and generates consumer complaints about stiffness. Marketing spend burns cash without moving volume. This is a one-way door for capital; once spent, it cannot be recovered.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the synthetic costs more than leather and may be less comfortable. There is no data showing wearers prefer it or will pay extra.

Blockers:
1. **Negative Unit Economics:** The material costs more than the incumbent (leather) while offering perceived lower utility (comfort).
2. **Adoption Friction:** Manufacturers have no incentive to change factories for a more expensive, riskier input.

Risks: Fashion volatility, consumer rejection.

The plan fails the basic test of offering a superior or cheaper alternative to the incumbent. It asks the market to accept worse performance at a higher price based solely on marketing.

DECISION: NO-GO
