First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a capital-intensive strategy without a verified path to fund the factory or battery supply, creating an immediate solvency risk.

**Steelman:** Becoming a pure-play EV manufacturer in 2008 by acquiring an existing large factory allows the company to bypass greenfield construction delays and leverage underutilized industrial assets to accelerate time-to-market for a premium sedan.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capital Mismatch:** The proposal explicitly admits the factory and batteries require "far more capital than the company has." Committing to *only* EVs and a *large* factory creates a binary failure mode: if funding stalls, the company owns an expensive, idle asset with no alternative revenue stream (e.g., ICE vehicles) to sustain operations.
2.  **Market Infrastructure:** In 2008, charging infrastructure is scarce. A full-size sedan targets a broader market than early adopters; without reliable charging, the product utility is severely compromised, limiting sales volume needed to justify the large factory.
3.  **Execution Risk:** Mass production experience is low. Scaling a complex EV powertrain in a newly acquired, large facility multiplies operational complexity. Established makers can respond with superior supply chains and distribution.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "better way" exists: contract manufacturing (avoiding factory CAPEX) or a phased approach (smaller pilot facility) to prove demand before committing to "only" EVs and a "large" plant. The proposal rejects these by design.

**Blockers:**
*   **Funding Gap:** The stated lack of capital versus the high cost of the factory/batteries is a factual blocker. The plan fails as proposed because the necessary resources are acknowledged as missing.
*   **Strategic Rigidity:** Committing to "only" EVs removes flexibility to pivot if the market or infrastructure lags, increasing the probability of total failure given the 2008 context.

**Risks:** Competitor response, production delays, low adoption due to charging scarcity. These are severe but secondary to the immediate funding blocker.

**Conclusion:** The proposal demands a massive, irreversible financial commitment despite admitting the funds do not exist and the market infrastructure is immature. It fails the basic test of feasibility.

DECISION: NO-GO
