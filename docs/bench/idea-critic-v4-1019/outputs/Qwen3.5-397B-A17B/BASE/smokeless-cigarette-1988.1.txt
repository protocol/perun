The proposal presents a heated tobacco product developed over several years at significant cost, aiming to launch in 1988 following test markets with a marketing campaign centered on it being a "cleaner" way to smoke. While the investment is sunk, proceeding as proposed carries immediate and substantial risks that cannot be ignored.

First, the core consumer value proposition is fragile. The proposal explicitly notes that smokers may dislike both the taste and the lighting mechanism. Furthermore, the product is described as complicated to use compared to ordinary cigarettes. In a market driven by habit and convenience, introducing friction and potential dissatisfaction among the existing user base threatens commercial viability regardless of the development spend.

Second, and more critically, the regulatory landscape poses an existential threat to the proposed strategy. The plan relies on promoting the product as a "cleaner" way to smoke. The proposal itself flags that regulators may object to such health claims. In 1988, making implied or explicit health comparisons without rigorous, pre-approved scientific consensus and regulatory clearance invites severe legal repercussions, fines, and forced product withdrawal. Launching a campaign based on a claim that regulators are likely to reject is strategically unsound.

The suggestion to "launch after test markets" is insufficient mitigation if the fundamental marketing angle is legally vulnerable and the product experience is potentially inferior. The large sum already spent creates a sunk cost fallacy; throwing good money after bad by launching a flawed product with a problematic marketing message will only increase losses. The risks regarding consumer acceptance and regulatory compliance are too high to approve the current plan. The strategy requires fundamental revision regarding claims and product usability before any market introduction can be considered.

DECISION: NO-GO
