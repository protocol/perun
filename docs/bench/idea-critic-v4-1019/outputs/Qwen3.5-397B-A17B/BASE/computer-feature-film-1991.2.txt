The proposal represents a pivotal moment for the company, transitioning from shorts to a feature-length production. While the risks are substantial, they are not insurmountable given the specific circumstances. The primary concern is the lack of precedent for an all-computer-animated feature. However, innovation inherently requires being first; waiting for others to validate the medium cedes the competitive advantage to rivals. The company's existing portfolio of shorts proves technical competency, even if scaling to feature length introduces new logistical challenges regarding rendering time and cost. These are operational hurdles, not fundamental flaws in the concept.

The narrative approach, telling the story from a small toy's point of view, is strategically sound. It leverages the unique visual capabilities of computer animation to create perspectives impossible in traditional media, turning a technical limitation into a storytelling strength. The partnership with a large studio, while creating a power imbalance regarding distribution and control, provides the essential capital and market access required for such an ambitious project. Without this partner, the financial burden of rendering and production would likely be prohibitive for a small entity. The risk of the larger partner holding the "stronger hand" is a standard trade-off for securing the resources necessary to attempt the impossible.

Rejecting this offer because the path is uncharted ignores the company's core identity as a pioneer in computer graphics. The alternative—remaining a producer of shorts while competitors potentially seize the feature market—is a greater long-term risk. The lead animator being targeted by the studio confirms the high value of the team's talent; accepting the deal secures their future within the company rather than losing them to a competitor. The potential reward of defining a new industry standard outweighs the operational growing pains. The market is ready for a fresh visual experience, and the team possesses the unique skills to deliver it.

DECISION: GO
