The proposal to shift laptop production to in-house designed processors leverages existing success in mobile devices, yet critical gaps prevent immediate approval. The primary obstacle is software compatibility; existing applications must function seamlessly on the new architecture, but the proposal offers no verified strategy to ensure this continuity. Without a proven migration path, the risk of rendering current software unusable is high, which would directly alienate the existing customer base.

Furthermore, the company's laptop-specific chip designs remain unproven against established supplier performance metrics. While the design approach mirrors that of phones, laptops face distinct thermal and power demands that mobile chips do not typically encounter. Assuming parity without empirical data introduces significant product failure risk. The transition also demands substantial engineering resources, diverting focus from other innovations during a period of market uncertainty.

Buyer confusion presents another tangible threat. Changing the core architecture could disrupt purchasing decisions if the value proposition is not clearly communicated and demonstrated. Finally, terminating the supplier relationship eliminates a safety net. If the in-house chips underperform or face production delays, the company lacks an immediate alternative, potentially halting laptop sales entirely.

The facts presented highlight severe operational and market risks without offering mitigation plans for software compatibility, performance validation, or supply chain continuity. Proceeding now would gamble the entire laptop division on unverified assumptions. The lack of a phased testing plan or a fallback strategy makes the current proposal too dangerous to execute. A comprehensive feasibility study addressing software emulation, performance benchmarking, and a gradual rollout strategy is required before any commitment can be made. The current information is insufficient to justify the massive investment and potential market fallout.

DECISION: NO-GO
