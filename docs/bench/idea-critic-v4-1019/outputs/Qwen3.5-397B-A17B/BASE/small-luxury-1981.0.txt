The proposal targets a shifting market where American-brand buyers seek smaller luxury sedans, a segment currently underserved by European competitors. Leveraging an existing mass-market platform from the parent company offers immediate cost efficiencies and faster time-to-market compared to developing a unique chassis. However, the identified risks are severe and threaten the core asset: the brand's prestige.

Luxury purchasing is driven largely by perception and exclusivity. If buyers perceive the vehicle as merely a trimmed-up mass-market car, the premium pricing strategy becomes unsustainable. The risk that salespeople will refuse to use the main brand name indicates an internal lack of confidence, which will inevitably bleed into customer interactions and erode trust. Furthermore, diluting the brand identity by associating it too closely with non-luxury platforms can cause long-term damage that outweighs short-term sales gains from this specific segment.

The current plan relies on the assumption that added trim and features are sufficient to mask the shared architecture and justify the price point. History suggests that discerning luxury consumers, even those transitioning from American brands, value distinct engineering and heritage over superficial upgrades. Without a clear strategy to physically or perceptually distance this compact model from its mass-market roots, the likelihood of brand erosion is high. The concerns regarding salesforce adoption and price justification are not minor hurdles but fundamental flaws in the value proposition.

Approving this now invites a scenario where the company incurs the costs of development and marketing only to face rejection from both the target demographic, who see through the badge engineering, and the traditional base, who feel the brand has been cheapened. A "revise first" approach is insufficient because the core concept relies on a shared platform that inherently conflicts with the luxury positioning required to command a premium. The risks to the brand equity are too significant to proceed without a fundamentally different architectural or branding strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
