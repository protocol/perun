The proposal seeks to increase advertising revenue by broadcasting user purchasing behavior and activities from partner websites directly to friends' news feeds. While the plan includes a notification step and an opt-out mechanism, the fundamental risks outweigh the potential financial gains.

The core issue is the treatment of sensitive personal data. Purchasing habits often reveal private details about health, finances, relationships, or personal struggles. Even with an option to decline, the default setting of sharing this information presumes consent rather than earning it. This approach is likely to be perceived as a severe invasion of privacy, triggering immediate and intense user backlash. Trust, once broken in this manner, is exceptionally difficult to rebuild.

Furthermore, the risk extends beyond the users to the partner websites. If users react negatively to having their purchases publicized, the resulting controversy could embarrass these partners, damaging their brands and potentially leading them to sever ties with the social network. This would undermine the very ecosystem required for the advertising model to function.

The proposal itself acknowledges that the feature will be hard to withdraw once launched. This creates a dangerous lock-in effect where a misstep becomes permanent, compounding reputational damage over time. Relying on an opt-out model for such sensitive data is insufficient in an environment where users are increasingly vigilant about their digital footprint. The potential for a catastrophic loss of user trust and partner confidence presents an existential threat that far exceeds the projected increase in advertising revenue. Approving this now invites a crisis that the organization may not survive.

DECISION: NO-GO
