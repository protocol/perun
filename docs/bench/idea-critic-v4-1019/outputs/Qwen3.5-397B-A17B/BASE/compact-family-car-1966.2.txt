The proposal faces critical structural weaknesses that threaten immediate viability. Establishing a dedicated dealer network introduces prohibitive upfront costs without guaranteeing market penetration, especially when rivals already dominate the small car segment with low-price offerings. This dual pressure of high fixed costs for distribution and aggressive competitive pricing creates a dangerous margin squeeze before sales even begin.

Furthermore, the financial model relies entirely on the new plant operating near full capacity to amortize its construction costs. This assumption is fragile given the identified consumer uncertainty: buyers may reject the 1100 cc compact in favor of either cheaper alternatives or larger vehicles. If market demand shifts toward these extremes, production volumes will fall short, leaving the company with underutilized assets and crippling debt service obligations. The risk of building a costly, exclusive sales channel for a product that might not resonate with the mass market is too severe to ignore.

Launching now locks the company into a rigid, high-cost structure in an environment defined by price sensitivity and uncertain preference. The combination of an unproven product fit, an expensive go-to-market strategy, and the absolute necessity of high volume creates a perfect storm for financial failure. Proceeding without first validating demand or exploring integration with existing distribution channels exposes the firm to unacceptable losses. The current plan does not adequately mitigate the threat of competitor pricing or the capital intensity of the new infrastructure. Approval in this state would be fiscally irresponsible.

DECISION: NO-GO
