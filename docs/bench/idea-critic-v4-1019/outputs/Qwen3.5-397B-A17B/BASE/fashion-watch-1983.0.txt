The proposal to launch an inexpensive plastic watch collection as fashion items in 1983 carries significant strategic risks that currently outweigh the potential rewards. The primary concern is brand erosion; introducing a low-cost plastic product directly threatens the company's established reputation for quality. Once perceived as a manufacturer of cheap goods, reclaiming a premium market position is notoriously difficult and costly.

Furthermore, the sales target of one million units in the first year appears excessively aggressive given the identified vulnerabilities. The fashion sector is inherently volatile, with tastes shifting rapidly, making a large initial inventory of twelve distinct models a substantial liability if trends turn. Additionally, the threat of established brands engaging in price wars could squeeze margins or block shelf space entirely, rendering the volume targets unachievable.

The proposal lacks critical mitigation strategies for these specific threats. There is no outlined plan to protect the core brand identity, such as creating a distinct sub-brand or separate distribution channel. Nor is there evidence of market testing to validate the demand for twelve models simultaneously or contingency plans for inventory management should fashion trends shift unexpectedly. Proceeding immediately without addressing these fundamental gaps exposes the organization to unnecessary financial loss and long-term reputational damage. The aggressive nature of the plan combined with the absence of defensive measures suggests a high probability of failure under current conditions. Approval requires a revised strategy that explicitly addresses brand protection, validates sales assumptions through pilot data, and includes flexible production capabilities to adapt to changing fashion dynamics. Until these elements are incorporated, moving forward is imprudent.

DECISION: NO-GO
