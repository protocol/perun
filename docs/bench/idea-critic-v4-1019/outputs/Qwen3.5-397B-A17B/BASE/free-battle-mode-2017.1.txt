The proposal carries excessive risk relative to the potential reward given the current constraints. Entering a market where a rival already dominates the last-player-standing genre is dangerous, especially when that rival has established network effects and player loyalty that cannot be overcome simply by releasing a free alternative. The likelihood of displacing the incumbent or capturing significant market share within two months is negligible.

Furthermore, the threat of cannibalization is substantial. Converting a successful paid cooperative title into a free-to-play competitor, even as a separate download, risks devaluing the original product. Players may delay purchasing the paid game in anticipation of the free mode or migrate entirely, eroding the studio's existing revenue stream without a guaranteed replacement.

The timeline of two months is critically insufficient for developing a stable, competitive multiplayer experience, particularly one requiring robust anti-cheat measures, server infrastructure, and balanced gameplay mechanics. Rushing this development will almost certainly result in a buggy launch that damages the studio's reputation rather than enhancing it.

Most critically, the studio lacks any prior experience operating a free-to-play business model. Success in this sector relies on sophisticated live operations, data analytics, and monetization strategies centered on cosmetic sales. Without this institutional knowledge, the team is ill-equipped to design compelling purchasable items or manage the economy required to sustain the game. Attempting to learn these complex systems while simultaneously fighting an established market leader with an under-resourced, rushed product creates a high probability of total failure.

The combination of a dominant competitor, internal inexperience, an unrealistic schedule, and the direct threat to existing revenue makes this initiative untenable in its current form. The potential losses outweigh the speculative gains.

DECISION: NO-GO
