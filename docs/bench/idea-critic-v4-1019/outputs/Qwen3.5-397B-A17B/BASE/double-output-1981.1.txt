The proposal to double monthly production while holding 4,500 unsold units during a recession is fundamentally flawed. The company has already produced 7,500 vehicles but sold only 3,000, leaving a massive inventory overhang. With the economy sliding into recession and interest rates rising, consumer demand for big-ticket items like cars will inevitably contract further. Higher rates also increase financing costs for both the manufacturer and potential buyers, creating a double squeeze on liquidity.

The company explicitly has limited cash. Doubling production requires significant upfront capital for materials, labor, and overhead without any guarantee of immediate revenue. This strategy would rapidly deplete remaining cash reserves, likely leading to insolvency before the additional units could be sold. Furthermore, dealers are already struggling to finance existing stock; forcing more inventory onto them risks damaging critical distribution relationships and triggering defaults.

Increasing supply when demand is collapsing violates basic economic principles. The priority must be preserving cash and reducing inventory, not expanding it. Continuing to build cars that cannot be sold converts liquid assets into stagnant metal, compounding storage costs and depreciation. The risks identified in the proposal—growing unsold stock, hurting demand, rising financing costs, and dealer inability to finance—are not merely concerns; they are certainties under current conditions. Proceeding as proposed ignores these realities and threatens the immediate survival of the enterprise. The logical path is to halt or drastically reduce production to align with actual sales velocity and conserve liquidity until market conditions improve.

DECISION: NO-GO
