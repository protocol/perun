The proposal presents a high-risk strategy for a young carmaker in 2008. The plan requires committing exclusively to battery-electric vehicles while acquiring a large factory from established competitors. However, the facts indicate a severe mismatch between ambition and capability. The company lacks the necessary capital to fund both the factory acquisition and the expensive battery packs required for production. Furthermore, the external infrastructure is insufficient, as charging stations are currently scarce, which will likely limit consumer adoption.

Competitive threats are significant, with established makers poised to respond aggressively to any market entry. Perhaps most critically, the company possesses little experience with mass production, yet the proposal involves scaling immediately in a large facility. Attempting to manage complex manufacturing operations without prior expertise, while simultaneously facing capital shortages and an underdeveloped charging network, creates a high probability of failure. The combination of financial insolvency risks, infrastructural barriers, and operational inexperience makes the current proposal untenable. Proceeding now would likely exhaust resources before a single vehicle reaches the market successfully. The risks overwhelmingly outweigh the potential benefits at this specific time and under these specific constraints.

DECISION: NO-GO
