The proposal suggests releasing an unfinished sandbox game directly to the public for sale in 2009, relying on buyer feedback to guide future development. This approach carries significant dangers given the current constraints. The product is explicitly described as rough and incomplete. In the gaming market, first impressions are critical; releasing a visibly unfinished product risks permanent reputation damage. If early buyers feel the price does not match the value, negative word-of-mouth will spread rapidly, especially without a marketing budget to control the narrative or highlight future potential.

Furthermore, the developer is a single individual. Supporting a large audience of paying customers requires robust infrastructure for updates, bug fixes, and community management. A solo developer cannot realistically scale support to meet the demands of a public release while simultaneously coding new features. This bottleneck will likely lead to long delays in addressing critical issues, causing player frustration and refund requests. The risk that players will stop buying once they realize the extent of the unfinished state is high, potentially killing revenue streams before the game ever reaches a viable state.

Without a publisher to share financial risk or a marketing budget to manage public perception, the project is exposed to immediate market forces it cannot withstand. The strategy of "build it and they will come" rarely works for rough prototypes, particularly when money changes hands upfront. The combination of a solo team, no safety net, and a potentially hostile reception to an incomplete product makes this specific plan too dangerous to execute as written. The risks of brand destruction and financial loss outweigh the potential benefits of early revenue. The proposal must be rejected in its current form to prevent likely failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
