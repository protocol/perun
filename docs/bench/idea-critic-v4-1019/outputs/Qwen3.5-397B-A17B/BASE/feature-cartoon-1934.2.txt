The proposal to produce a full-length animated feature represents a monumental shift from the studio's current operational model. While the existing short films generate steady, albeit thin, profits, the suggested budget is approximately ten times that of an average short. This massive capital requirement means the studio's entire earnings stream from shorts must be diverted to fund this single venture, leaving no financial buffer for ongoing operations or unexpected setbacks.

The risks identified are severe and multifaceted. First, there is no market precedent for audiences sustaining interest in a feature-length cartoon; consumer behavior suggests they may reject the format entirely. Second, the potential for budget overruns is high given the uncharted technical and logistical territory. Third, significant opposition exists from both the founder's family and seasoned film-industry insiders, indicating a lack of confidence from those with the most stake and experience.

Proceeding immediately would jeopardize the company's solvency. If the film fails to attract an audience or exceeds its budget, the studio faces catastrophic loss, as all current revenue is being wagered on this single outcome. The thin profit margins from shorts provide no safety net. Without external financing, a proven track record for features, or mitigation strategies for the identified risks, the venture is too speculative to approve in its current form. The prudent course is to halt this specific execution plan until the financial exposure is reduced or external validation is secured.

DECISION: NO-GO
