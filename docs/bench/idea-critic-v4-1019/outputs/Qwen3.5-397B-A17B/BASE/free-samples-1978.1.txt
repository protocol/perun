The initial test launch across four cities yielded disappointing sales, indicating a fundamental lack of market demand or product-market fit. Proceeding immediately to a national rollout based on a single, unproven hypothesis in one mid-sized city ignores these critical early warning signs. The proposal relies entirely on renaming the product and distributing free samples to office workers, assuming this will generate sufficient intent to purchase. However, the identified risks are substantial: office workers may still perceive no need for the item regardless of its name, and the cost of producing and distributing free samples represents a sunk cost with no guaranteed return on investment.

Furthermore, the condition for a national rollout depends on recipients stating they "would buy" the product. Stated intent in a survey or feedback form often diverges significantly from actual purchasing behavior, especially when the initial free trial did not convert to sales in the first test. A national rollout requires a large marketing budget, which would be jeopardized if the underlying demand remains unproven. The failure of the first test suggests the value proposition is unclear or unnecessary to the target audience. Simply changing the name does not address the core issue of utility or desire.

Allocating resources for a second test in a new city without first analyzing why the first four cities failed is strategically unsound. The company risks compounding losses by investing in sampling costs and potential national marketing spend before validating that the product solves a real problem. The disconnect between the poor initial performance and the optimism of the proposed pivot is too wide to bridge without further data analysis or product iteration. Approval at this stage exposes the organization to unnecessary financial liability with a low probability of success. The prudent path requires halting the current plan to investigate the root causes of the initial failure before committing to further expenditure.

DECISION: NO-GO
