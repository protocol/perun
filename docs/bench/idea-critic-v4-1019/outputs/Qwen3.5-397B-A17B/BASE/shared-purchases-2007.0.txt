The proposal seeks to increase advertising revenue by broadcasting user purchases and activities from partner sites to friends via the news feed. While the plan includes a notification step and an opt-out mechanism, the identified risks are severe and potentially existential. Users are likely to perceive this as a significant invasion of privacy, especially given that purchase data can be highly sensitive. Unlike benign social interactions, financial transactions and specific consumption habits often carry personal implications users wish to keep private.

Furthermore, the potential for embarrassment extends beyond the individual user to the partner websites. Public reactions to shared purchasing data could damage partner reputations, threatening the very alliances required for this revenue stream. Perhaps most critical is the assessment that this feature will be difficult to withdraw once launched. In the social media landscape, trust is the primary currency. Eroding user trust by normalizing the exposure of private commercial behavior creates a precedent that is nearly impossible to reverse without causing mass user attrition.

The proposed mitigations—a notification and an option to decline—are reactive rather than proactive. They place the burden of privacy protection on the user after the system has already been designed to exploit their data for public display. Given the high probability of user backlash, the risk to partner relationships, and the irreversible nature of the reputational damage, proceeding with this strategy in its current form is inadvisable. The potential short-term advertising gains do not justify the long-term threat to the platform's viability and user base. The feature fundamentally misaligns with user expectations of privacy regarding financial and personal consumption data.

DECISION: NO-GO
