The proposal represents a radical departure from the company's established vertical integration model, introducing significant exposure to external dependencies and channel unfamiliarity. However, the market signal is clear: small-computer makers are growing rapidly, indicating a shift in customer preference that the current mainframe-centric approach cannot address. Waiting to develop proprietary components or build an internal retail network would take far longer than the proposed one-year timeline, likely causing the company to miss the window of opportunity entirely.

The risks identified are real but manageable within the context of this specific strategic pivot. While rivals can copy a design based on standard parts, speed to market and brand reputation can serve as the primary defenses. The reliance on outside software is not a weakness but a necessity for creating a vibrant ecosystem quickly; attempting to build everything internally would delay launch and limit application availability. Lower margins are an acceptable trade-off for entering a high-volume market segment that currently generates zero revenue for the firm. Finally, the lack of retail experience is precisely why the proposal suggests using independent stores; this leverages existing infrastructure rather than requiring costly and time-consuming internal development.

Rejecting this plan due to deviation from tradition ignores the existential threat of being left behind as the industry evolves. The "small separate team" structure is crucial, as it allows this new venture to operate with the agility required for the personal computer market, free from the bureaucratic processes suited for mainframes. The use of off-the-shelf parts and open design accelerates development and encourages third-party innovation, which is vital for platform success. The potential reward of capturing a share of this exploding market far outweighs the discomfort of operating outside historical norms. Immediate approval is required to capitalize on the current momentum before competitors solidify their dominance.
