The proposal to launch twelve plastic watch models as fashion items in 1983 carries significant strategic weight. The core tension lies between leveraging a new, inexpensive manufacturing capability and protecting an established reputation for quality. While the risk of brand dilution is real, positioning these units strictly as "fashion items" rather than "timekeepers" creates a necessary psychological separation. This allows the company to compete in a volume-driven segment without directly undermining its premium heritage, provided the marketing clearly distinguishes the product lines.

The sales target of one million units in the first year is undeniably aggressive. However, the low cost of plastic production likely offers the margin flexibility required to support the high-volume distribution needed to hit such numbers. If the unit economics hold, the financial upside of capturing a mass-market share outweighs the operational strain of scaling quickly.

Regarding external threats, the volatility of fashion tastes and potential price cuts by established brands are valid concerns. Yet, waiting for perfect conditions often results in missed windows of opportunity. The plastic watch market in this era represents a shifting paradigm where durability and low cost are becoming as valuable as traditional craftsmanship. By entering now with a diverse collection of twelve models, the company hedges against specific style failures; if one design misses the mark, others may succeed. Retreating due to fear of reputation damage or competition cedes this emerging territory to rivals who may not hesitate to define the category first.

The risks identified are manageable through careful brand architecture and agile inventory control, whereas the cost of inaction is the permanent loss of a massive customer demographic. The potential to democratize access to the brand while generating substantial cash flow justifies the immediate execution of this plan. The strategic pivot to fashion accessories transforms a potential liability into a growth engine.
