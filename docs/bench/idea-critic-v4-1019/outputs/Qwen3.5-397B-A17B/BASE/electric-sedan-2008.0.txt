The proposal presents a high-risk strategy for a young carmaker in 2008. The plan requires an immediate, total commitment to battery-electric vehicles while attempting to acquire a large factory from established competitors. However, the facts indicate the company lacks the necessary capital to fund both the facility purchase and the expensive battery packs required for production. Furthermore, the market infrastructure is insufficient, with scarce charging stations likely to deter consumer adoption.

The competitive landscape poses another severe threat. Established makers possess significant resources and could easily respond to this new entrant, potentially crushing it before it gains traction. Additionally, the company admits to having little experience with mass production, a critical deficiency when attempting to operate a large-scale factory. Combining severe undercapitalization, inadequate market infrastructure, strong potential competitive retaliation, and operational inexperience creates a scenario where failure is highly probable.

Proceeding as proposed would likely exhaust the company's limited resources immediately without achieving sustainable production or sales. The gap between current assets and required investment is too wide to bridge without revision or additional funding sources not currently available. The risks outweigh any potential first-mover advantage given the specific constraints of the 2008 market environment. Therefore, the proposal cannot be approved in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
