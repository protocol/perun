The proposal to simultaneously launch across Europe and America in 1998 presents an unacceptable level of risk given the constraints and market conditions described. The core objective of becoming the world's largest retailer is ambitious, but the execution strategy ignores critical operational realities. Launching in multiple countries at once inherently multiplies logistics and marketing costs, creating a massive burn rate before any revenue is proven.

The fact that online fashion buying remains unproven in this era significantly heightens the danger. Without historical data or a tested model, scaling immediately to two continents removes the ability to learn from initial mistakes in a controlled environment. If the concept fails in one region, the failure is compounded globally, exhausting resources instantly.

Furthermore, the reliance on venture capital introduces a strict time limit. The raised concern that funds may deplete before sales catch up is valid and likely understated. Simultaneous expansion delays launch timelines due to coordination complexity, extending the period where the company burns cash without income. If the launch slips even slightly, or if customer acquisition costs prove higher than anticipated, the company faces insolvency before achieving traction.

A prudent approach would require a phased rollout, starting in a single market to validate the business model, optimize logistics, and prove unit economics before expanding. The current plan lacks these safety valves. It bets the entire venture on an unproven hypothesis while maximizing fixed costs and operational complexity. The combination of unproven demand, high multi-regional overhead, and finite funding creates a scenario where the probability of total failure outweighs the potential for rapid success. The risks identified in the proposal itself are sufficient to reject the plan in its current form. Proceeding now would be financially reckless.

DECISION: NO-GO
