The proposal to double monthly production while holding 4,500 unsold units during a recession is financially catastrophic. With only 3,000 cars sold against 7,500 produced, inventory turnover is critically low. Doubling output will exacerbate this glut, tying up scarce cash in static assets rather than generating liquidity.

High interest rates directly increase the cost of carrying this excess inventory and raise financing costs for both the manufacturer and its dealers. Since dealers are already struggling to finance current stock, forcing more units onto their lots risks collapsing the distribution network. If dealers cannot borrow to hold inventory, they cannot sell cars, creating an immediate bottleneck that halts revenue entirely.

The company has limited cash reserves. Manufacturing requires significant upfront capital for materials and labor. Diverting these finite funds to build cars that cannot be sold depletes the runway needed for survival. In a sliding economy, demand is contracting, not expanding. Producing more into a shrinking market ignores basic supply and demand dynamics, guaranteeing deeper losses per unit produced due to fixed cost absorption failures and potential write-downs.

This strategy assumes that availability drives sales, but the constraint is clearly affordability and credit access, not supply. The risks identified—growing unsold stock, recessionary demand, high financing costs, and dealer insolvency—are not merely hurdles; they are terminal threats under this plan. Executing this proposal would likely accelerate bankruptcy by burning cash at maximum velocity while revenue stagnates. The logical path is to align production with actual sales velocity to preserve capital, not to blindly expand output.

DECISION: NO-GO
