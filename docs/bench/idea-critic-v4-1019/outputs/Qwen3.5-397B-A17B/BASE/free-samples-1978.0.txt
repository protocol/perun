The initial test launch across four cities yielded disappointing sales, indicating a fundamental lack of market demand or product-market fit. Proceeding immediately to a national rollout based on a single, unproven hypothesis in one mid-sized city is financially imprudent. The proposal relies on renaming the product and distributing free samples to office workers, assuming this will generate sufficient intent to purchase. However, the identified risks are substantial: the original data shows little demand, free samples incur costs without guaranteed conversion, and the target demographic may not perceive a need for the item.

Furthermore, a national rollout requires a large marketing budget. Committing these significant resources before validating that a simple name change and sample distribution can actually reverse the negative trend from the first test is reckless. The plan assumes that "most recipients" will say they would buy the product after receiving a free sample, but stated intent often diverges sharply from actual purchasing behavior, especially when the initial market signal was weak. The failure in four diverse cities suggests the issue may be deeper than branding or awareness; it could be a lack of utility.

Without evidence that the repositioning addresses the core reason for the initial failure, expanding the scope introduces unnecessary financial exposure. The company should first analyze why the initial launch failed rather than guessing that a new name will solve the problem. A more measured approach would involve a controlled study to verify if the rename genuinely shifts perception and leads to actual sales, not just verbal interest, before considering any national expansion. Given the high costs associated with a national launch and the clear warning signs from the initial data, approving this proposal as currently structured poses an unacceptable risk to capital. The gaps in validation regarding actual purchase behavior versus stated intent are too wide to ignore at this stage.

DECISION: NO-GO
