The proposal presents an existential gamble for a young carmaker in 2008. The strategy demands an immediate, total commitment to battery-electric vehicles while acquiring a large factory from established competitors. However, the facts provided highlight four critical, unresolved barriers that make immediate approval impossible.

First, the capital requirement is explicitly stated as far exceeding the company's current resources. Committing to a project without the funds to execute it invites immediate insolvency. Second, the infrastructure necessary for the product's viability—charging stations—is described as scarce, severely limiting market adoption potential at this specific moment in time. Third, the competitive landscape poses a direct threat; established makers possess the resources to respond aggressively, potentially crushing a new entrant before it gains traction. Finally, the company lacks experience with mass production, a complex operational hurdle that cannot be bypassed simply by purchasing a factory.

Attempting to build a full-size sedan under these conditions ignores the fundamental constraints of liquidity, infrastructure, competition, and operational capability. The risks are not merely challenges to be managed; they are structural blockers that threaten the entire enterprise. Proceeding now, as proposed, would likely result in rapid financial collapse before a single vehicle reaches a customer. The gap between the ambitious vision and the harsh reality of 2008 is too wide to bridge without significant revision, additional funding, or a phased approach. Therefore, the proposal cannot be approved in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
