The proposal suggests releasing an unfinished sandbox game directly to the public for sale in 2009, relying on buyer feedback to guide future development. While this approach minimizes upfront costs and leverages community input, the identified risks are severe and currently unmitigated. Selling a rough, incomplete product without a publisher or marketing budget exposes the project to immediate reputational damage. In the 2009 market, consumers generally expected polished retail experiences; an unfinished alpha sold at full or near-full price would likely generate negative reviews that could permanently tarnish the brand before it matures.

Furthermore, a solo developer lacks the infrastructure to support a large, paying audience. Technical support, bug fixing, and community management require significant time and resources that a single individual cannot sustain while simultaneously developing new features. If players encounter critical issues they cannot resolve, dissatisfaction will spread rapidly, halting sales momentum. The fear that players will stop buying once they perceive the game as too unfinished is well-founded; without a clear roadmap or demonstrated progress, trust will erode quickly.

Proceeding immediately as proposed ignores these structural weaknesses. There is no plan for managing customer expectations, no strategy for handling support loads, and no buffer for the potential backlash of selling an incomplete product. The absence of a marketing budget means the game relies entirely on word-of-mouth, which is dangerous if the initial reception is negative due to the game's rough state. To ensure long-term viability, the strategy must be revised to include a free demo, a clearer development roadmap, or a delayed launch until critical stability issues are resolved. Approving this plan in its current form invites failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
