The proposal to simultaneously launch a global online sports and fashion retailer across Europe and America in 1998 presents an unacceptable level of risk given the constraints identified. The strategy relies on executing complex logistics and marketing campaigns in multiple countries at once, which inherently multiplies costs and operational friction. In an environment where online fashion purchasing remains unproven, this approach eliminates the opportunity to validate the business model in a single market before scaling.

The financial structure depends entirely on venture capital, creating a fragile timeline where funding may deplete before sales volume can stabilize operations. Launching in several jurisdictions simultaneously increases the likelihood of significant delays, further burning through limited capital without generating revenue. The combination of unverified consumer behavior, high fixed costs from multiple international offices, and the pressure of a finite funding runway creates a scenario where failure is highly probable.

A more prudent path would involve selecting a single, controlled market to test demand, refine logistics, and prove unit economics before expanding geographically. The current plan ignores the critical need for iterative learning and capital preservation. Without evidence that online fashion can sustain the projected growth rates, committing to a broad, simultaneous international rollout is reckless. The risks of delayed launch and cash exhaustion outweigh the potential first-mover advantages in untested territories. Approval cannot be granted for a strategy that jeopardizes the entire venture by overextending resources before product-market fit is established.

DECISION: NO-GO
