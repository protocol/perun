The proposal to double monthly production while holding 4,500 unsold units during a recession is fundamentally flawed. With only 3,000 cars sold against 7,500 produced, inventory turnover is critically low. Increasing output now exacerbates an already dangerous surplus.

The economic environment directly contradicts this strategy. A sliding recession reduces consumer purchasing power, while rising interest rates increase financing costs for both the manufacturer and potential buyers. Dealers, facing their own credit constraints due to high rates, are unlikely to accept additional stock they cannot finance or sell.

The company's limited cash position makes this approach catastrophic. Doubling production requires significant upfront capital for materials, labor, and overhead without any guarantee of immediate revenue. This will accelerate cash burn, likely leading to insolvency before market conditions improve. Holding excess inventory incurs storage costs and ties up working capital that is desperately needed for survival.

Success in this sector requires aligning supply with actual demand, not speculative hopes. The current gap between production and sales indicates a severe overcapacity issue. The logical response to falling demand and tightening credit is to reduce costs, slow production to match sales velocity, and preserve cash reserves. Expanding output when the market is contracting ignores basic economic principles and threatens the firm's existence.

The risks identified in the proposal—growing unsold stock, hurting demand, rising financing costs, and dealer inability to finance—are not merely concerns; they are definitive barriers that make the plan unviable. Proceeding would ignore the reality of the balance sheet and the macroeconomic headwinds. Approval would authorize a path to rapid financial collapse.

DECISION: NO-GO
