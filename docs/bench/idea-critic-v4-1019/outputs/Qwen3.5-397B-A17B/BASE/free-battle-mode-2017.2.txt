The proposal presents a high-risk strategic pivot with critical gaps in execution capability. The studio lacks any prior experience operating a free-to-play business model, which relies on complex live-service economics, retention loops, and cosmetic monetization strategies distinct from single-sale transactions. Attempting to enter a market where a rival already dominates the genre creates an immediate disadvantage; displacing an established leader requires significant differentiation or marketing spend, neither of which is outlined.

The timeline of two months is dangerously optimistic for developing a stable, balanced last-player-standing mode, even with asset reuse. This genre demands rigorous server infrastructure, anti-cheat measures, and extensive playtesting to ensure fairness and engagement, tasks that typically exceed such a brief window. Rushing this launch risks releasing a broken or unpolished product that could damage the studio's reputation rather than enhance it.

Furthermore, the risk of cannibalization is substantial. Offering a free version of the core experience may erode sales of the existing paid cooperative game without guaranteeing sufficient conversion to cosmetic purchases to offset the loss. Without a proven track record in live operations or a clear plan to mitigate these specific threats, proceeding immediately invites financial loss and brand dilution. The combination of an unproven business model, an overcrowded market, an unrealistic schedule, and internal inexperience makes the current proposal untenable.

DECISION: NO-GO
