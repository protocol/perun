The proposal seeks to pivot from a stable, low-margin short-film model to a high-stakes, full-length feature with a budget ten times the current average. While the ambition is notable, the factual basis for approval is critically weak. The studio currently relies on steady but thin profits from shorts, meaning there is no financial cushion to absorb failure. Funding this massive undertaking entirely from existing short-film earnings places the entire company's liquidity at immediate risk.

Significant external warnings have been raised by both the founder's family and industry insiders, suggesting a consensus among experienced observers that the market is not ready for feature-length animation. The specific risk that audiences may refuse to sit through a long cartoon represents a fundamental uncertainty regarding product-market fit. Unlike a minor expansion, this project requires a complete restructuring of production capacity and financial exposure without any pilot data or guaranteed distribution beyond current channels.

Proceeding now would gamble the studio's entire operational future on an unproven format during a period of financial fragility. The potential for budget overruns, combined with the lack of diversified revenue streams to support such a deficit, creates an unacceptable probability of insolvency. Prudent management dictates that a venture of this magnitude requires more than internal optimism; it demands validated audience demand and a safer financial structure before committing resources that currently sustain the business. Without these safeguards, the proposal threatens the survival of the studio itself.

DECISION: NO-GO
