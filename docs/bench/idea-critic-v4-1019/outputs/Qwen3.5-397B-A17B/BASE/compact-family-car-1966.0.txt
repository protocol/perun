The proposal faces immediate structural contradictions. Building a dedicated dealer network introduces high fixed costs precisely when rivals are competing on low prices. This dual pressure squeezes margins from both sides: revenue is capped by competitor pricing, while overheads surge due to the new network construction.

The requirement for the new plant to run near capacity to pay off creates a dangerous volume dependency. Success demands high sales velocity immediately upon launch. However, the market risks suggest buyers may reject this specific offering in favor of either cheaper existing models or larger vehicles. If the target demographic prefers lower prices, the added cost of the dedicated network makes profitability impossible without raising prices, which further reduces competitiveness. If they prefer larger cars, the 1100 cc engine size becomes a liability regardless of distribution method.

Launching with an unproven product in a crowded segment, while simultaneously burdening the operation with the capital expenditure of a new sales infrastructure, violates basic financial prudence. The plant's need for high utilization cannot be guaranteed when the value proposition is unclear and the cost base is artificially inflated by the dedicated network strategy. The risks of underutilization and price wars are too severe to proceed without modifying the approach. Specifically, leveraging existing distribution channels rather than building a new network would reduce the break-even point and mitigate the risk of failing to fill plant capacity. As currently structured, the plan exposes the company to unsustainable losses if initial sales targets are missed, which is a distinct possibility given the stated buyer preferences.

DECISION: NO-GO
