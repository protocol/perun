The proposal suggests selling an unfinished sandbox game directly to the public in 2009, relying on buyer feedback to guide development. While this approach leverages community involvement, the identified risks are severe and currently unmitigated. Selling a rough product immediately exposes the brand to negative first impressions that could permanently damage reputation. In the gaming market, early reviews often dictate long-term viability; a flawed launch may deter future customers regardless of subsequent improvements.

Furthermore, a solo developer lacks the capacity to support a large, paying audience. Technical issues, bug reports, and feature requests will overwhelm a single individual, leading to delayed updates and frustrated users who have already paid. This disconnect between expectation and delivery frequently results in refund demands and public backlash. The absence of a marketing budget exacerbates these issues, as there is no controlled narrative to manage player expectations or highlight the roadmap. Without a publisher to share the load or provide financial cushioning, the developer faces total exposure to market volatility.

The assumption that players will continue buying an unfinished product despite its rough state is optimistic and historically risky without a proven track record or a strong existing community. Releasing now invites scrutiny that the current team structure cannot handle. The potential for immediate revenue does not outweigh the high probability of project failure due to reputational damage and operational collapse. A more prudent path involves completing a stable minimum viable product, securing a small closed test group for feedback, and establishing a support framework before any commercial transaction occurs. Proceeding as proposed ignores the critical infrastructure needed to sustain a live service model.

DECISION: NO-GO
