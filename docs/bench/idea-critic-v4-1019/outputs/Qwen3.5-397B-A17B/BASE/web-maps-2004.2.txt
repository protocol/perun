The proposal suggests transforming a desktop mapping tool into a free, browser-based web application with satellite imagery and route planning. While the vision aligns with making information universally accessible, the specific constraints of 2004 present insurmountable barriers to immediate approval.

The primary obstacle is the technological limitation of contemporary browsers. In 2004, standard web browsers lack the native capability to render complex, interactive graphics smoothly. Attempting to deliver a rich mapping experience with satellite imagery and dynamic routing without a dedicated download will likely result in a sluggish, unresponsive user interface that fails to meet user expectations. The risk of launching a product that feels broken or unusable is extremely high.

Furthermore, the financial model is fundamentally flawed. The proposal explicitly acknowledges high licensing costs for map data and satellite imagery while admitting there is no direct revenue model. Acquiring a search company implies a need for strategic asset growth, but burning capital on expensive data licenses without a clear path to monetization or cost recovery is fiscally irresponsible. Competitors already possess established user bases, meaning this new entrant must offer a superior experience to gain traction. Given the browser limitations, the experience will be inferior, not superior.

Without a plan to address the browser performance gap, a strategy to monetize the high operational costs, or a method to differentiate from entrenched rivals, proceeding now guarantees resource depletion with little chance of success. The risks outweigh the potential benefits in the current environment. The concept may have merit in a future technological landscape, but the execution plan for 2004 is not viable.

DECISION: NO-GO
