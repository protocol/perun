The proposal to simultaneously launch a global online sports and fashion retailer across Europe and America in 1998 presents an unacceptable level of risk given the constraints and market conditions described. The core ambition to become the world's largest retailer immediately ignores the foundational challenges of establishing logistics, brand trust, and operational stability in a single market, let alone multiple continents at once.

The identified risks are not merely potential hurdles; they are likely fatal flaws for a startup environment. Running operations in several countries simultaneously will indeed multiply logistics and marketing costs, stretching the venture capital funding thin before any revenue stream is proven. In an era where online fashion buying remains unproven, customer acquisition costs will be high, and conversion rates uncertain. If the launch is delayed due to the sheer complexity of coordinating international offices and supply chains, the remaining capital may be insufficient to sustain the company until sales mature.

Venture funding is finite. Burning cash on a broad, untested geographic expansion without validating the business model in a controlled environment is a strategic error. The probability of running out of money before achieving profitability is extremely high under this plan. A successful entry into the e-commerce space requires iterative learning, localized adaptation, and proof of unit economics before scaling. This proposal skips the validation phase entirely in favor of immediate, massive scale.

The combination of unproven consumer behavior, high operational complexity, and limited financial runway creates a scenario where failure is the most probable outcome. Approving this plan as proposed would jeopardize the entire venture. The strategy must be fundamentally rethought to focus on a single market or region to prove the concept before attempting global domination.

DECISION: NO-GO
