**Steelman:** Simultaneous US/EU launch captures maximum market share before competitors emerge, leveraging VC capital to build an unassailable brand presence in fashion-forward demographics globally.

**Attack:** The proposal assumes logistics, marketing, and consumer behavior are uniform enough to scale instantly across continents without a proven unit economy. In 1998, cross-border e-commerce infrastructure (payments, trust, delivery) was fragmented. Launching everywhere means fixing broken supply chains in multiple jurisdictions simultaneously while burning cash on unproven customer acquisition. If online fashion buying is "unproven" (as stated), multiplying the experiment by ten countries multiplies the failure modes without increasing the likelihood of product-market fit. Venture capital running out is not a risk; under this plan, it is a certainty because the burn rate exceeds any plausible early revenue from an unverified model.

**Better Way:** Launch in a single, dense market (e.g., UK or Germany) to prove the logistics model and unit economics. Once the "online fashion" hypothesis is validated with real sales data, expand to the US using the refined playbook. This reduces initial capital requirements and allows for iterative learning.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed if the company cannot survive 18 months with zero revenue in *one* country, let alone ten. The lack of a pilot market makes this a gamble on execution rather than a test of viability.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, cash is depleted, warehouses in three countries are underutilized due to low traffic, and local marketing campaigns failed to resonate culturally. The team is stretched too thin to fix operational bottlenecks in any single region, leading to poor customer experience and brand damage globally.

**Verdict:** The proposal conflates ambition with strategy. Without a proven core model, simultaneous global expansion guarantees resource dilution and premature insolvency. The risks identified in the proposal itself (logistics complexity, unproven market, capital runway) are fatal under the current scope.

DECISION: NO-GO
