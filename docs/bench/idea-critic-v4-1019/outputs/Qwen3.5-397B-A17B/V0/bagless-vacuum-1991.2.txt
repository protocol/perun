The claim is that a founder-led company can successfully launch a premium, bagless vacuum cleaner in 1991 despite higher costs, zero manufacturing history, and aggressive incumbent competition. The steelman: a superior, bag-free mechanism creates enough perceived value to overcome price sensitivity and distribution barriers, allowing a new entrant to carve out a profitable niche before competitors copy the design.

However, critical failures loom. First, **distribution blockade**: incumbents control retail shelves. Without a manufacturing track record, retailers may refuse to stock an unproven, higher-priced unit, starving the venture of volume. Second, **copycat speed**: if the idea succeeds, large rivals with existing supply chains can replicate the cyclone technology and undercut prices immediately, eroding the first-mover advantage. Third, **consumer skepticism**: buyers accustomed to low upfront costs for bagged units may reject the premium price without tangible proof of long-term savings or performance, which is hard to demonstrate in a static retail environment.

The "do nothing" option (licensing the tech) avoids capital risk but sacrifices control and potential upside. Building a company requires massive upfront capital with high reversibility costs (failed inventory, brand damage). The cost of delay is high if patents are weak or expiring, but the cost of premature scaling is bankruptcy.

Crucially, the proposal lacks a **kill criterion**. There is no defined metric for early traction (e.g., "secure 3 major retail partners within 6 months") or a pivot point if direct sales fail. Without a verified path to shelf space or a direct-to-consumer channel (which was nascent in 1991), the assumption that consumers will seek out this specific machine is unsupported. The risk of incumbents copying the feature while leveraging their distribution dominance is a concrete failure scenario not addressed by the current plan. The proposal assumes product superiority equals market success, ignoring the distribution moat of established players.

DECISION: NO-GO
