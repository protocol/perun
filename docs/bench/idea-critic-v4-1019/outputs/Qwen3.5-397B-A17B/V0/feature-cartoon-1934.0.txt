The claim is that the studio should risk its solvency on a single, unproven format ten times costlier than its breadwinners. The steelman: a hit feature creates an insurmountable moat and transforms the business from a job-shop into an empire; staying small guarantees eventual irrelevance as shorts become commodities.

However, the failure modes are catastrophic. First, the "audience endurance" assumption is untested; if viewers reject the medium at this length, the studio loses its entire war chest with no fallback. Second, the funding model cannibalizes the very shorts generating the cash flow; a delay or overrun starves the ongoing operation. Third, the unified opposition from insiders and family suggests a blind spot in the founder's optimism, not just conservatism.

Alternatives exist: produce a medium-length special to test endurance, or secure external financing to isolate the risk rather than betting the farm. The proposal lacks a "stop-loss" mechanism; it is an all-in gamble without a defined pivot point if early production signals trouble. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of total failure; waiting for a proven technology or co-financing partner reduces ruin risk without forfeiting the opportunity.

The strongest attack is the concentration of risk: one failure destroys the entity. This survives only if the potential reward is truly infinite and the founder accepts personal ruin. Without external validation or a phased approach, the variance is too high for a prudent executive team. The proposal demands a binary outcome (glory or bankruptcy) rather than a managed bet.

Verdict: The plan ignores the survival constraint. It requires revision to include phased funding, external risk-sharing, or a shorter proof-of-concept before committing the full treasury. As proposed, it is reckless.

DECISION: NO-GO
