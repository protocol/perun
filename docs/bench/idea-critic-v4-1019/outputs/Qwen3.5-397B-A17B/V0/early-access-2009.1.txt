**Steelman:** Releasing an unfinished alpha immediately generates essential cash flow and validates demand through real user behavior, bypassing the need for a marketing budget or publisher.

**Attack:** The proposal assumes players in 2009 will tolerate a "rough state" in exchange for early access. However, the "Early Access" model was not yet a recognized or trusted convention; without that framework, an unfinished product sold at full price is likely perceived as broken or scam-like, triggering refunds and reputation damage. Furthermore, a solo developer cannot technically support a "large audience" of paying customers expecting immediate fixes; the influx of feedback from a paid cohort often overwhelms a single creator, halting feature development entirely. The risk of players stopping purchases upon seeing the unfinished state is high because there is no established community trust to sustain them through the rough patches.

**Better Way:** Release a free, limited demo to gather feedback and build a mailing list first. This validates interest without the reputational risk of selling a broken product. Alternatively, seek a small publisher advance to fund the completion of a "vertical slice" before any sale.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** The strategy ignores the market context of 2009, where "buying unfinished" was not a standard consumer expectation. If launched as proposed, the likely outcome is a wave of negative reviews labeling the game "incomplete," killing future sales before the game is actually good. The cost of delay (waiting to finish more) is lower than the cost of a failed launch.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed if there is no clear communication plan setting expectations for "buggy software." There is no metric defined here to stop the bleed if refunds exceed 20%.

**Verdict:** The proposal fails to account for the specific market conditions of 2009 regarding unfinished goods and the operational capacity of a solo dev against paid expectations. The risk of permanent reputation damage outweighs the potential early revenue.

DECISION: NO-GO
