**Steelman:** A $35 single-board computer removes the primary cost barrier for schools, creating a dedicated, hackable platform that cheap tablets cannot replicate, despite requiring users to source peripherals.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The "low cost" claim is fragile. A $35 board requires a display, keyboard, mouse, power supply, and SD card. In 2012, these peripherals often exceed $50 combined, negating the price advantage over used laptops or bulk tablets which are ready-to-use. The charity lacks manufacturing, supply chain, and support experience; a "sell-out" first batch indicates poor capacity planning, not success, risking long lead-times that frustrate schools.

Alternatives considered:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Schools continue using existing cheap laptops/tablets. Risk: Stagnant engagement, but zero execution risk.
2.  **Bulk Used Laptops:** Higher upfront unit cost but includes screen, keyboard, and trackpad. Lower total cost of ownership (TCO) for immediate deployment.
3.  **Tablet Grants:** Leverage existing cheap tablet market. Easier support, but less "programmable" openness.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
The proposal aims to rekindle programming interest. However, if the total system cost exceeds $80, schools will reject it for complete laptops. Failure mode: The charity becomes a hardware distributor, drowning in logistics and support tickets for broken SD cards and power issues, diverting funds from educational content. The "sell out" risk suggests under-capitalization; if demand spikes, they cannot scale, losing momentum.

**Evidence & Verification:**
No current data on peripheral costs or manufacturing capacity is provided. The assumption that $35 is "low cost" ignores the hidden ecosystem costs. The assumption that a charity can manage licensed distributors and hardware support without experience is unverified and high-risk.

**Decision:**
The proposal fails the TCC (Total Cost of Ownership) test against alternatives. The hidden costs of peripherals likely push the solution above the price of functional used laptops. The operational risk for a non-expert charity is extreme. The "sell-out" scenario is a capacity failure, not a validation.

**Kill Criteria:**
If the total system cost (board + essential peripherals) exceeds $75, the value proposition collapses. Without a verified manufacturing partner and support plan, the project will stall.

DECISION: NO-GO
