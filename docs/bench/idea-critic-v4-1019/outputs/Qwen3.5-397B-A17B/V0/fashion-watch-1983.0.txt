**Steelman:** Leveraging low-cost manufacturing to enter the high-volume fashion segment creates a new revenue stream, diversifying beyond traditional luxury while targeting a younger demographic with disposable income for trends rather than heirlooms.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is brand cannibalization and reputation erosion. If the "inexpensive plastic" perception bleeds into the core brand, long-term equity is destroyed for short-term volume. The 1M unit target assumes immediate market acceptance without proof of concept, ignoring the risk of unsold inventory if fashion tastes shift before production scales. Established competitors cutting prices could squeeze margins to zero.

Alternatives include:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve brand purity but miss the quartz revolution's volume opportunity.
2.  **Sub-brand Launch:** Introduce these models under a completely distinct name to insulate the parent company's reputation.
3.  **Pilot Program:** Release only two models in a limited region to validate the 1M projection before full commitment.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
The proposal treats a functional component (movement) as a fashion accessory, which is a valid strategic pivot. However, assuming 1M sales in year one is a classic overconfidence bias. A pre-mortem reveals that if the watches are perceived as "cheap" rather than "stylish," the company faces a warehouse of worthless plastic and a tarnished image. The "fashion" angle increases volatility; unlike timekeeping accuracy, style has no objective metric, making the sales target highly fragile.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:**
No data is provided on consumer willingness to buy plastic from a movement maker, nor on the marketing budget required to shift perception. The proposal lacks a clear exit strategy if the fashion bet fails. Without a sub-brand firewall or a phased rollout, the risk to the core business is disproportionate to the potential gain. The aggressive target ignores the lag time in retail distribution.

**Verdict:**
The plan relies on unverified assumptions about brand elasticity and market size. Launching twelve models simultaneously with a million-unit target is reckless without a pilot or brand insulation. The risk of permanent reputational damage outweighs the potential volume gain in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
