The claim is that a founder-led company can successfully launch a premium, bagless vacuum cleaner in 1991 despite higher costs, zero manufacturing history, and aggressive incumbent competition. The steelman posits that the cyclone mechanism offers a unique, patentable performance advantage strong enough to override price sensitivity and distribution barriers, creating a new luxury segment where functionality trumps brand loyalty.

However, the "better-way" analysis reveals critical flaws. The proposal ignores the "do nothing" or licensing alternative. Given the lack of manufacturing record and capital, building a factory and supply chain from scratch is the highest-risk path. A superior alternative would be licensing the technology to an established manufacturer (e.g., Hoover, Electrolux) for royalties, eliminating execution risk while monetizing the invention immediately. The proposal fails to explain why capturing 100% of a potentially zero market is better than 5% of a guaranteed one.

The pre-mortem indicates a high probability of failure due to cash flow starvation before retail scale is achieved. Incumbents can legally design around patents or engage in a price war that a startup cannot survive. The "bagless" benefit is unproven to consumers who may view the higher upfront cost as unjustified without extensive, expensive education campaigns. The proposal lacks a kill criterion for customer acquisition cost or a specific pilot channel to validate willingness to pay before full-scale manufacturing commitment.

While the invention is technically sound, the business strategy of vertical integration without prior industry foothold is reckless. The risks of copying, distribution blockades, and consumer rejection are not mitigated by the plan; they are merely acknowledged. The failure to consider licensing—a lower-risk, capital-efficient route to market—renders the current "found a company" approach suboptimal. The evidence suggests the inventor should monetize the IP through partnership rather than attempting to become a manufacturer overnight. Without a validated path to overcome the distribution moat or proof of premium price elasticity, proceeding as proposed invites catastrophic capital loss.

DECISION: NO-GO
