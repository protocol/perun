Steelman: Launching a heated tobacco product now captures first-mover advantage in a "cleaner" smoking niche, leveraging sunk R&D costs before competitors react.

However, the proposal fails the evidence and risk tests required for a GO decision. First, the core value proposition is unverified. The proposal admits smokers may dislike the taste and the lighting mechanism. In a consumer goods market where habit and sensory experience drive retention, a product that feels "complicated" and tastes different than the standard is likely to suffer high churn, regardless of marketing spend. Promoting it widely as "cleaner" without explicit regulatory approval for that specific health claim invites immediate legal action and reputational damage. The risk of regulators objecting is not theoretical; it is a near-certainty given the era's increasing scrutiny of tobacco health assertions.

Second, the argument to launch based on "very large" sunk development costs is a logical fallacy. Past spending cannot justify future losses if the product fails in the market. Throwing good money after bad by funding a wide promotion for a product with known usability and taste defects compounds the financial error.

Third, the proposal lacks a kill criterion or a phased approach. It jumps straight to "wide promotion" after test markets, ignoring the possibility that test markets might confirm the taste and usability concerns. A "do nothing" or "limited regional pilot" alternative is superior. These options allow the company to gather real-world data on acceptance and regulatory response without exposing the entire brand to a backlash over unproven health claims.

The strongest attack is the regulatory and consumer acceptance gap. If regulators ban the "cleaner" messaging, the product loses its primary differentiator. If smokers reject the taste, the distribution channel collapses. The proposal offers no mitigation for these existential threats, relying instead on the hope that marketing can overcome fundamental product flaws and legal barriers.

Without verified data showing consumer preference over traditional cigarettes and explicit regulatory clearance for health claims, proceeding to a wide launch is reckless. The prudent path is to halt the wide launch plan, resolve the taste/usability issues, and secure regulatory guidance before committing further capital.

DECISION: NO-GO
