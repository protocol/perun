The claim is that releasing two versioned variants of a creature-collecting game will drive sales via mandatory social trading, despite hardware decline and publisher skepticism.

Steelman: The mechanic transforms a single-player limitation (incomplete collection) into a social necessity, creating viral demand that overrides hardware aging and perceived greed.

However, critical failure scenarios exist. First, if the player base is too small due to the console's decline, the "trading" mechanic becomes a frustration rather than a feature, leading to negative word-of-mouth. Second, without a guaranteed critical mass at launch, the perception of a "price grab" will dominate, causing a boycott rather than adoption. Third, the dependency on a physical link cable and a second human creates a high friction barrier; if either is missing, the core value proposition collapses immediately.

Better ways include releasing a single complete edition with optional online features (if infrastructure existed) or focusing on a robust single-player campaign to build a base before attempting social mechanics. The "do nothing" option avoids the reputational risk of alienating a shrinking install base with a perceived cash grab.

The pre-mortem reveals that if the initial sales velocity does not hit a specific threshold within the first two weeks to ensure enough local players for trading, the ecosystem fails. The cost of delay is low; the cost of a failed dual-launch is high, potentially killing the IP before it starts.

Evidence is entirely assumed regarding the size of the active install base and the density of potential players in social proximity. No data supports the assumption that enough users have both the cable and a friend ready to play simultaneously. The publisher's uncertainty is a leading indicator of market fragility.

This proposal bets the entire project on a network effect that cannot be jump-started without existing scale. Approving this now ignores the fundamental chicken-and-egg problem of social mechanics on a dying platform. The risk of being perceived as greedy without delivering the requisite social utility is too high to proceed without a verified strategy to guarantee initial player density.

DECISION: NO-GO
