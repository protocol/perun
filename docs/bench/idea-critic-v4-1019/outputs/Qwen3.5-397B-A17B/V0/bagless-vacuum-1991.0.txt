The claim is that a founder-led company can successfully manufacture and sell a premium, bagless vacuum cleaner in 1991 despite higher costs, no manufacturing history, and aggressive incumbent competition.

The steelman: A superior, bag-free technology creates a distinct premium category where performance justifies a higher price point, allowing a new entrant to bypass traditional distribution via direct marketing or specialized retail before incumbents can effectively clone the mechanism.

However, critical failures loom. First, the "no manufacturing record" risk is fatal for hardware; prototypes do not equal scalable, reliable mass production. Without a proven supply chain, defect rates could destroy the brand before launch. Second, pricing above established brands requires convincing buyers to pay a premium for an unproven mechanism, ignoring the "good enough" nature of existing bagged vacuums. Third, incumbents copying the idea is not just a risk but a near-certainty; without strong patents (unverified here), they can leverage existing distribution to crush a niche player once the market is validated.

Better ways exist: licensing the technology to an established manufacturer immediately captures value without capital expenditure or operational risk. Alternatively, seeking a joint venture provides the missing manufacturing credibility while sharing downside risk. Doing nothing preserves capital until patents are secured and a manufacturing partner is lined up.

The pre-mortem reveals the company runs out of cash within 18 months due to production delays and low initial volume, forcing a fire sale of the IP to the very incumbents feared. This is a one-way door with high irreversible cost. The cost of delay is low; the technology is not perishable, and the market will not vanish in a year.

Evidence is entirely anecdotal (thousands of prototypes) and lacks unit economics, patent status, or committed retail partners. The proposal assumes buyers value "bagless" enough to override brand loyalty and price sensitivity without data.

DECISION: NO-GO
