**Steelman:** Leverage existing assets to rapidly enter the trending last-player-standing market with a low-risk, free-to-play title, creating a new revenue stream via cosmetics without abandoning the core paid product.

**Attack:** The proposal fails on three verified constraints. First, **market timing**: entering a genre where a rival already dominates popularity in 2017 suggests a "winner-take-most" dynamic; being late with a clone rarely succeeds without a unique hook, which is not defined. Second, **cannibalization risk** is treated as hypothetical, yet releasing a free version of a paid cooperative game directly devalues the original product, likely collapsing paid sales before cosmetic revenue can compensate. Third, **operational mismatch**: the studio lacks free-to-play infrastructure (live ops, anti-cheat, economy balancing). Building this in "two months" while simultaneously developing content is operationally impossible; the timeline ignores the complexity of sustaining a live service versus shipping a static product.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" option preserves the paid game's integrity. A superior alternative is a limited-time free event within the existing paid ecosystem to test retention without fragmenting the user base or brand.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in six months. Cause: The player count never reaches the critical mass required for matchmaking, servers sit empty, and the brand is diluted by a perceived "cash grab" that failed to compete with the market leader. The two-month deadline forces a buggy launch, accelerating exit.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a one-way door for brand reputation and a high-cost pivot for a studio with no F2P experience. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed launch killing the studio's reputation. The proposal relies on unproven assumptions about asset reusability and market appetite for a second-mover clone.

**Verdict:** The plan ignores the dominance of the incumbent, underestimates the operational lift of F2P, and risks destroying the existing paid revenue model without a validated differentiator.

DECISION: NO-GO
