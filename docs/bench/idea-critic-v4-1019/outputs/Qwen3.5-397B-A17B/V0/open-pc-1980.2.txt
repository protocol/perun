The proposal correctly identifies an existential threat: small-computer makers are growing fast while the mainframe maker remains insular. The steelman is that speed and market access outweigh the loss of vertical integration margins. However, the plan relies entirely on capabilities the company explicitly lacks: retail experience, outside software management, and open-design speed.

The "better way" analysis fails to address the core competency gap. Building a separate team helps, but "independent retail stores" require a distribution infrastructure that takes years to build, not months. Relying on outside suppliers for software creates a dependency where the company controls neither the product quality nor the release schedule, directly contradicting its "builds everything itself" DNA. The risk of rivals copying standard parts is real, but the greater risk is internal paralysis: the mainframe culture will likely strangle the open, fast-moving PC team or fail to support the low-margin retail channel effectively.

The pre-mortem reveals a likely failure mode: the company launches a year late because internal procurement processes stall the "off-the-shelf" strategy, or the sales force refuses to sell low-margin units through unfamiliar channels. The proposal assumes the separate team can operate truly independently, but history shows corporate antibodies often reject such transplants. Without a guaranteed, immediate channel to market (which building from scratch does not provide) and a concrete plan to manage external software dependencies without internal bottlenecks, the "one year" timeline is illusory.

The strongest attack survived is the speed argument; however, the execution plan does not actually guarantee speed because it ignores the friction of entering a completely new business model (retail) and supply chain (outsourced software) simultaneously. The company is betting on doing two things it has never done, in a market moving faster than its internal clock. The risks of channel failure and cultural rejection are not mitigated, only noted.

DECISION: NO-GO
