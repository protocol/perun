The claim is that a luxury brand can successfully sell a rebadged mass-market compact car to American buyers without eroding brand prestige. The steelman: American buyers in 1981 prioritize size and fuel efficiency over European heritage, creating a gap for a "accessible entry point" that leverages existing manufacturing to lower costs while maintaining a premium margin through trim differentiation.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in this specific 1981 context. First, the "shared platform" risk is not merely perceptual but structural; luxury buyers of this era purchase status and exclusivity. If the mechanical underpinnings are visibly identical to a mass-market parent vehicle, the premium price becomes indefensible, turning the product into an overpriced economy car rather than an entry-level luxury good. Second, the sales channel friction is critical: if salespeople refuse to embrace the model or fail to distinguish it from the mass-market counterpart, the brand narrative collapses at the point of sale. Third, brand dilution is a one-way door; once the luxury marque is associated with "cheap" underpinnings, recovering the halo effect for flagship models is historically near-impossible.

The proposal fails the "better way" test because it attempts to stretch the existing brand downward rather than creating a distinct sub-brand or acquiring a different badge entirely (a strategy later proven necessary in this exact historical scenario). The pre-mortem shows the likely outcome: cannibalization of the brand's core value proposition with low volume, as the target demographic rejects the "fake luxury" signal. The evidence suggests that in 1981, the psychological distance between "mass-market compact" and "luxury" was too wide to bridge with trim alone. The risk of permanent prestige damage outweighs the potential gain of capturing a new segment with a compromised product. The kill criteria—maintaining brand equity and justifying premium pricing—are unlikely to be met given the transparent platform sharing.

DECISION: NO-GO
