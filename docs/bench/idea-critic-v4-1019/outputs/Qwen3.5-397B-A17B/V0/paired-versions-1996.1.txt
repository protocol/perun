The claim is that releasing two versioned variants of a creature-collecting game will drive sales via mandatory social trading, despite hardware decline and publisher skepticism.

Steelman: The mechanic transforms a single-player limitation (incomplete collection) into a social necessity, creating organic marketing and doubling initial unit sales per social pair, outweighing the risk of perceived greed.

However, critical assumptions fail verification against the provided facts. First, the "aging handheld" with "declining popularity" suggests a shrinking install base. If the active user density is too low, the friction of finding a trading partner with the *opposite* version and a link cable becomes insurmountable, rendering the core loop broken. Second, the publisher's uncertainty indicates a lack of market confidence; forcing a double-purchase model on a skeptical market for a dying platform risks brand damage and total inventory failure. Third, the "price grab" perception is a valid threat; without a proven fanbase willing to overlook monetization tactics, this strategy could trigger a backlash rather than viral adoption.

The proposal lacks a "do nothing" or "single version with DLC" comparison. A single robust version lowers the barrier to entry, potentially maximizing the shrinking audience's participation. The cost of delay or revision to test demand (e.g., a single release) is lower than the risk of flooding a declining market with confused, incompatible SKUs.

The strongest attack is the dependency on high user density for trading to function. On a dying console, the probability of a user finding a trading partner with the specific cable and opposite cartridge is low. If trading fails, the game's primary value proposition collapses, leaving players with an incomplete product they paid twice to access. This leads to negative word-of-mouth, accelerating the platform's death and ensuring the game fails to meet even the publisher's low confidence thresholds.

There is no evidence provided that the specific demographic for this aging console values collection completion enough to overcome the hardware friction and perceived greed. The kill criterion—successful trades leading to completion—cannot be met if the ecosystem is too sparse.
