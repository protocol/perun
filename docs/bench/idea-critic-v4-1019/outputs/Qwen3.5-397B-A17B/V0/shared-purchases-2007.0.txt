The claim is that sharing user purchase data with friends via news feeds, even with an opt-out, will increase ad revenue without causing unacceptable backlash. The steelman: transparency and user control (notification + decline) mitigate privacy concerns enough to unlock valuable social proof for advertisers.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws. First, the "notification step" creates a negative user experience where users feel surveilled rather than empowered; being told your private purchase is about to be broadcast invites anxiety, not trust. Second, "sensitive purchases" (medical, gifts, adult content) cannot be reliably filtered by partner sites in 2007, leading to inevitable public embarrassments that damage the platform's reputation irreparably. Third, the "option to decline" places the burden of privacy on the user, shifting the default from private to public, which violates the implicit social contract of the time. Once launched, the "hard to withdraw" risk means a single viral scandal regarding a sensitive purchase could force a reactive shutdown, appearing as an admission of guilt rather than a strategic pivot.

The better way is to aggregate data for advertisers without exposing individual transactions to friends, or to allow users to voluntarily share specific purchases (opt-in) rather than forcing an opt-out model. The cost of delay is low compared to the catastrophic risk of a privacy scandal that could trigger regulatory scrutiny or mass user exodus. The pre-mortem shows a scenario where a high-profile user's sensitive purchase is leaked, causing media outrage and partner withdrawal; the proposed kill criteria (user decline rate) only measure reaction after the damage is done, not prevention.

The strongest attack survived is the revenue potential, but it fails because the mechanism relies on a level of user comfort with data sharing that does not exist in this context. The assumption that a simple notification suffices to neutralize privacy invasion is unsupported by the sensitivity of the data involved.

DECISION: NO-GO
