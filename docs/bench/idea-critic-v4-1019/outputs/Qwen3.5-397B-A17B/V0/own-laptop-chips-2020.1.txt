**Steelman:** Transitioning laptops to in-house silicon leverages existing mobile IP to unify architecture, reduce long-term BOM costs, and differentiate performance, provided software compatibility is maintained.

**Skeptic:** The proposal assumes mobile design approaches scale to laptop thermal/power envelopes without performance regression. It treats "existing software must keep running" as a solvable constraint rather than a potential showstopper if binary translation incurs unacceptable overhead. The cost of engineering time is understated; porting an entire OS ecosystem and developer tools often takes years, not quarters.

**Better-Way:** The "do nothing" option retains immediate compatibility and zero transition risk. A hybrid approach (select models only) reduces blast radius. Sticking with the supplier allows focusing engineering on system integration rather than foundational silicon validation.

**Kill-Criteria & Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: The transition launches 18 months late with 20% performance loss in pro workflows, causing enterprise churn. The one-way door is signing off on the tape-out before validating real-world laptop workloads. Cost of delay: Losing one holiday season to perfect the chip may be cheaper than launching a broken product line.

**Evidence Gap:** The proposal offers no data on the performance-per-watt gap between current mobile designs and laptop requirements. It lacks a specific migration path for legacy software that doesn't rely on unproven emulation. There is no cap on the engineering budget or a defined "stop" date if benchmarks miss targets.

**Verdict:** The proposal is a strategic aspiration, not an executable plan. It lacks a concrete technical bridge for software compatibility, a validated thermal/power model for laptop workloads, and specific kill criteria for the transition phase. Approving this now commits capital to a high-risk pivot without proof that the mobile design approach can satisfy laptop demands. The risks of ecosystem fragmentation and performance failure outweigh the theoretical benefits until a prototype validates the core assumption.

DECISION: NO-GO
