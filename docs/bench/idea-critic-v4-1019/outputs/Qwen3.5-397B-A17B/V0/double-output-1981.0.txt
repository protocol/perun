Steelman: Doubling production aims to achieve economies of scale, lower unit costs, and signal market confidence to secure further financing during a recession.

However, this claim collapses under immediate scrutiny. The company has already produced 2.5 times more cars than it has sold (7,500 vs. 3,000), creating a massive inventory overhang. With the economy sliding into recession and interest rates rising, demand is contracting, not expanding. High interest rates specifically cripple both consumer auto loans and dealer floor-plan financing, meaning dealers cannot absorb more stock even if they wanted to.

The proposal ignores the primary constraint: cash flow. Producing more cars requires significant upfront capital for parts and labor. With limited cash and 4,500 unsold units tying up existing capital, doubling output accelerates the burn rate without a corresponding revenue mechanism. This strategy bets on a demand surge that macroeconomic indicators explicitly contradict.

Alternatives considered:
1. **Do Nothing/Reduce Production:** Aligns output with the 3,000-unit sales run rate to preserve cash.
2. **Incentivize Sales:** Use limited cash for consumer rebates or dealer financing support to clear existing inventory.
3. **The Proposal:** Doubles the mismatch between supply and demand.

Pre-mortem: If executed, the company exhausts its cash reserves within months attempting to build cars it cannot sell. Dealers refuse deliveries due to financing costs. The increased inventory becomes a liability rather than an asset, forcing a fire sale or bankruptcy before the recession ends. The "confidence signal" is interpreted by lenders as financial recklessness, cutting off credit lines entirely.

There is no evidence that unit cost savings from doubling production would outweigh the carrying costs of 4,500+ additional unsold vehicles in a high-interest environment. The risk of total insolvency is immediate and catastrophic. The only logical path is to stop the bleeding, reduce production to match sales, and clear existing stock. Doubling production in this specific context is not a strategy; it is a suicide pact.

DECISION: NO-GO
