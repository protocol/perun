The proposal attempts to solve a capacity problem by creating a demand problem. The core failure mode is channel conflict and brand dilution. Introducing a dedicated dealer network for a single compact model while presumably selling other vehicles through existing channels creates immediate internal competition. The cost to build this network is sunk before a single unit is sold, directly threatening the profitability required to justify the new plant's operation.

Rivals already dominate the low-price small car segment. Entering this space with a new, unproven brand identity requires massive marketing spend to dislodge established competitors, yet the proposal offers no unique value proposition other than "newness." The risk that buyers prefer either cheaper existing options or larger, more versatile cars is not mitigated; it is ignored. An 1100cc engine in 1966 sits in an awkward middle ground: too large to be the budget entry-level choice, too small to be the primary family hauler if larger, affordable options exist.

The assumption that a dedicated network will drive volume is unverified. History suggests that fragmenting sales channels increases overhead without guaranteeing market share. The plant needs volume, but this strategy restricts volume to a single, narrow channel rather than leveraging existing infrastructure to reach the broadest audience. The "dedicated" approach isolates the product, making it easier for competitors to target and easier for consumers to ignore.

Furthermore, the financial model relies on near-capacity utilization. If the dedicated network fails to move units quickly—which is likely given the setup time and consumer inertia—the plant becomes a liability rather than an asset. The proposal lacks a fallback plan to sell through existing channels if the dedicated network underperforms. It bets the company's new capital expenditure on a distribution gamble rather than product superiority or cost advantage.

The strongest attack is that the dedicated network is a solution in search of a problem, adding cost without solving the core issue of competitive differentiation. Without a clear reason why this specific car needs its own stores, the risk of financial bleed is too high.

DECISION: NO-GO
