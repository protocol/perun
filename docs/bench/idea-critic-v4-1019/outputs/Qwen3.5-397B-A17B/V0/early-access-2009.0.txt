The claim is that selling an unfinished 2009 sandbox alpha immediately will succeed via community feedback. The steelman: early revenue funds development while loyal users shape the product, bypassing traditional publishing gatekeepers.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in this specific context. First, the "feedback loop" assumes a tolerant audience that did not exist in 2009. The "early access" model was not a recognized or accepted commercial contract until nearly five years later; releasing an unfinished product then is not "alpha testing," it is selling broken goods, inviting review bombing and reputation destruction. Second, the proposal ignores the support bottleneck. A solo developer cannot manage a paying customer base expecting functionality; the resulting churn from angry buyers will outpace feature development. Third, without marketing, visibility relies on word-of-mouth, which spreads negativity faster than enthusiasm for a rough product. The risk of "players stopping buying" is not a possibility; it is the near-certain outcome when the value proposition is "pay to debug."

The "do nothing" alternative (finish a vertical slice first) or seeking a publisher carries lower reputational risk. The "cheaper" path of self-publishing now is actually more expensive due to the high probability of total project abandonment after a failed launch. The pre-mortem shows the game dies within three months as the developer burns out handling refunds and hostility instead of coding. There is no mechanism to reverse the reputational damage once the "rough state" becomes the permanent public record. The kill criterion is clear: if the market does not accept unfinished goods (which it didn't in 2009), the venture fails immediately upon launch.

The strongest attack survived is the financial necessity of the solo dev, but selling a defective product in an unreceptive era is not a solution; it is a guarantee of failure. The evidence of market norms in 2009 contradicts the core assumption that players will pay for unfinished work.

DECISION: NO-GO
