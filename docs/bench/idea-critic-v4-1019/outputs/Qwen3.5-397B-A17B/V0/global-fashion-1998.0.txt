**Steelman:** Simultaneous global launch secures first-mover dominance in online fashion before competitors establish footholds, leveraging venture capital to scale rapidly across Europe and America.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the "unproven" nature of the core behavior (online fashion buying) while multiplying execution complexity. Launching in multiple countries simultaneously creates a "logistics and marketing cost" multiplier that directly accelerates the risk of running out of venture money. If online fashion adoption is slower than anticipated (a stated risk), the burn rate from maintaining offices in several countries will deplete funds before sales can catch up. There is no "two-way door" here; over-extending capital in an unproven market leaves no runway to pivot or retreat.

**Better Way:** A single-market pilot (e.g., one country) validates the "online fashion buying" hypothesis with controlled burn. Success there funds the expansion. Doing nothing waits for clearer market signals, but given the founder's ambition, a focused launch is superior to a fragmented one.

**Fit:** The goal is to be the "largest," but speed without survival yields zero market share. The north star requires longevity, which this cash-intensive approach jeopardizes.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, the company faces insolvency. Cause: Logistics failures in three countries simultaneously drained the war chest while customer acquisition costs remained high due to unproven demand. The brand is associated with delayed shipments, not fashion leadership.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:** The proposal admits online fashion is "unproven" and venture money "may run out." These are not mitigated; they are amplified by the multi-country scope. A sound kill criterion would be: "If customer acquisition cost exceeds X in the first market, halt expansion." This proposal lacks any such guardrail, committing all resources upfront.

**Decision:** The risk of total capital exhaustion in an unproven market, driven by unnecessary geographic complexity, outweighs the theoretical benefit of speed. The proposal fails to account for the "unproven" variable in its resourcing model.

DECISION: NO-GO
