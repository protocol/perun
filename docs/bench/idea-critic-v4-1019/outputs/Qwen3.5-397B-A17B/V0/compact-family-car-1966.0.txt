The claim is that a dedicated 1100 cc compact with a new dealer network will succeed despite high fixed costs and entrenched low-price rivals. The steelman: this specific configuration captures an underserved "family" segment willing to pay slightly more than bare-bones rivals, utilizing the new plant's capacity where smaller models cannot.

However, the proposal fails the "better-way" and "fit" tests critically. First, building a *dedicated* dealer network for a single small car model is economically indefensible in 1966. Small cars rely on volume and low overhead; duplicating real estate, staff, and inventory costs for one model destroys the margin needed to compete with rivals selling through established, multi-brand networks. The risk of the network remaining underutilized is extreme.

Second, the plant capacity requirement creates a dangerous rigidity. If the 1100 cc size misses the mark (too small for families, too expensive for budget buyers), the entire factory becomes a stranded asset. There is no flexibility to shift lines to larger or smaller engines if demand signals change. The proposal ignores the "do nothing" alternative of utilizing existing networks to test the engine size before committing to massive infrastructure spend.

The pre-mortem is clear: the car sells moderately, but the cost of the standalone dealer network prevents profitability, while the rigid plant volume targets force price cuts that erode capital. The "cheaper path" is to sell through existing channels or share a network. The "larger car" risk is unmitigated; if families prefer more space, this dedicated system collapses entirely.

The strongest attack survived is none; the economic model of a single-model dedicated network for a low-margin segment is fundamentally flawed compared to leveraging existing distribution. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of building a ghost network. Without a shared distribution strategy or a broader product mix to justify the dealers, this is a capital destruction event waiting to happen.

DECISION: NO-GO
