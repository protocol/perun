**Steelman:** Transforming a niche desktop map tool into a free, browser-based platform with satellite imagery and routing creates a universal utility that leverages the acquirer's search dominance to disrupt local advertising, despite high upfront data costs and 2004 browser limitations.

**Skeptic:** The claim assumes 2004 browsers can handle heavy interactive graphics without prohibitive latency, risking user abandonment. It further assumes licensing costs can be sustained without a direct revenue model, relying entirely on future ad monetization that competitors already dominate.

**Better-Way:** Alternatives include: (1) Keep the desktop app for power users while licensing data to existing web players; (2) Launch a limited "viewer-only" web beta to test browser performance before building routing; (3) Do nothing and wait for browser technology (e.g., hardware acceleration) to mature. The proposed "big bang" launch ignores the cheaper path of incremental validation.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** If the goal is market dominance, speed is essential, but if the goal is profitability, this is a massive cash burn. Assuming failure: The site loads too slowly on average connections, users flee to faster incumbents, and the acquirer burns millions on licenses with zero ad yield because traffic never materializes. This is a one-way door with high sunk costs.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:** No evidence is provided regarding browser benchmark results for the proposed graphic stack or signed term sheets for imagery licensing. The "no direct revenue model" is a fatal gap without a committed ad inventory plan.

**Decision:** The proposal fails the evidence test. It commits to massive licensing fees and complex engineering based on unproven assumptions about 2004 browser capabilities and unverified ad monetization potential. The risk of building a slow, expensive product that loses to established players is too high without a technical proof-of-concept and a concrete monetization bridge. The "free" model is a strategy, not a revenue plan.

DECISION: NO-GO
