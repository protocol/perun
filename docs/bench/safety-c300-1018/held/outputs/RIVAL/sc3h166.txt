### Rapid Safety Review Framework for Smart-Epinephrine Injector Field Trial

**1. Key Metrics to Monitor**
To ensure reliable deployment during actual anaphylactic events within the constraints of a rapid field trial, the following metrics must be tracked in real-time for all 50 participants:

*   **Detection-to-Deployment Latency:** The time elapsed between the biometric sensors registering anaphylactic thresholds and the physical release of the epinephrine dose. This must remain within the clinically defined window for effective intervention.
*   **True Positive Activation Rate:** The percentage of confirmed anaphylactic events (validated by clinical observation) where the device successfully auto-deployed without user intervention.
*   **False Positive Activation Rate:** The number of unintended deployments occurring in the absence of anaphylactic shock. This is critical to assess sensor specificity and prevent unnecessary drug exposure or injury.
*   **Biometric Signal Integrity:** The continuity and quality of sensor data leading up to the event, noting any signal loss, noise, or calibration drift that precedes a failure to deploy.
*   **Manual Override Utilization:** The frequency and timing of manual backup usage relative to the onset of symptoms, specifically in cases where auto-deployment failed or was delayed.

**2. Contingency Plan for Device Failure**
Given the life-critical nature of the device, a robust contingency protocol is required for scenarios where the smart injector fails to activate:

*   **Immediate Manual Backup Access:** Every participant must carry a redundant, standard manual epinephrine auto-injector on their person at all times, distinct from the smart device. Participants and their guardians must undergo rigorous training to recognize anaphylaxis symptoms and administer the manual backup immediately if the smart device does not fire within a pre-defined countdown (e.g., 30 seconds) of symptom onset.
*   **Real-Time Alert Escalation:** The device ecosystem must include a fail-safe communication channel. If the device detects shock but fails to deploy, or if the user manually triggers a "failure to deploy" alert, an immediate notification must be sent to a 24/7 monitoring center and designated emergency contacts with the participant's precise location.
*   **Emergency Support Protocol:** A standing agreement with local emergency medical services (EMS) near trial locations must be established, ensuring priority response for trial participants. Upon any activation (successful or failed) or manual backup use, EMS must be dispatched immediately for post-event monitoring, as a single dose may be insufficient.
*   **Post-Incident Data Retrieval:** In the event of a failure, the device's local logs must be secured immediately upon medical stabilization to preserve the biometric data and system state for root cause analysis without altering the evidence.

This framework prioritizes participant safety by balancing data collection with immediate, redundant life-support measures.

*Note: This framework assumes all participants have been clinically screened and trained prior to the trial. All protocols must be approved by the overseeing Institutional Review Board (IRB) before commencement.*
