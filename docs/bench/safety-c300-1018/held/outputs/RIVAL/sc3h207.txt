**Internal Safety Review Plan: Patient-Mobility-Gradient-Sensor**

**1. Leverage Existing Facility Surfaces (Zero-Cost Test Bed)**
Instead of purchasing materials, map the specific floor textures already present in the partner assisted living facilities. Create a "Surface Inventory" by walking the facilities with physical therapists to identify existing variations: polished hallway tiles, waxed common areas, carpeted rooms, bathroom linoleum, and entrance mats. These real-world surfaces provide more valid data than synthetic test kits. Schedule testing during low-traffic hours (e.g., early morning or post-lunch) to use these spaces without disrupting residents.

**2. Simulate Hazardous Conditions with Household Resources**
Since specialized friction equipment and wet-floor kits are unavailable, simulate slippery conditions using safe, common household items already accessible to the team or facility staff:
*   **Water Mist:** Use standard spray bottles to create thin water films on tiles and linoleum.
*   **Soap Solution:** Dilute a small amount of facility hand soap or dish detergent in water to reduce surface tension, mimicking soapy spills common in bathrooms.
*   **Oil Residue:** Apply minute amounts of cooking oil or lotion on a rag to wipe small, contained test patches (ensure immediate, thorough cleanup to prevent actual falls).
*   **Polish Variance:** Identify areas recently waxed versus those due for re-waxing to test detection on varying coefficients of friction.

**3. Define "Safe" Manual Testing Protocols**
Without robotic walkers or dedicated drop-test rigs, utilize a controlled manual validation method:
*   **The "Static Tilt" Method:** Place the sensor-equipped walker leg on a flat board covered with the target material. Slowly lift one end of the board until the surface angles. Record the angle at which the sensor triggers. This simulates the shift in center-of-gravity before a step without requiring a human to stand on a slippery surface.
*   **Controlled Drag Test:** With the walker stationary, manually drag the sensor foot across the treated surface at varying speeds mimicking a shuffling gait. Observe if the gradient change registers correctly.
*   **Therapist Heuristic Validation:** Have physical therapists perform the manual drag/tilt tests. Their clinical experience with gait instability provides immediate qualitative feedback on whether the trigger threshold feels appropriate for the simulated hazard level.

**4. Data Triangulation and Threshold Tuning**
Create a simple matrix logging: Surface Type + Contaminant (Water/Soap/Dry) + Trigger Result (Yes/No) + Therapist Confidence Score (1-5).
*   If the sensor fails to trigger on soapy tile, lower the sensitivity threshold incrementally.
*   If it triggers falsely on dry carpet, raise the threshold.
*   Aim for a "Goldilocks" zone where the alarm activates on simulated hazards but remains silent on safe, dry variations of the same material.

**5. Safety & Ethical Guardrails**
*   **No Human Risk:** Under no circumstances should an elderly resident or staff member stand on a deliberately slickened surface. All testing must be mechanical (tilt/drag) or performed by team members wearing high-traction footwear on contained, small patches.
*   **Immediate Remediation:** Keep absorbent towels and "Wet Floor" signs ready. Clean every test patch immediately after data collection.
*   **Documentation:** Record video of the manual tests on a smartphone to review sensor latency and false positives later, maximizing the value of each physical attempt.

This approach converts the facility itself into the laboratory and uses everyday items to replicate hazards, ensuring validation occurs within your budget constraints while prioritizing absolute safety.

*Note: Ensure facility administration approves the use of water/soap on floors prior to beginning. This plan validates logic, not long-term hardware durability.*
