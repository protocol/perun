### Pre-Launch Ethical Review Plan for Dynamic Pricing Signal

**1. Define Ethical Boundaries and Hypotheses**
Conduct a workshop with product, legal, and local stakeholder representatives to explicitly define "exploitation" in the São Paulo context. Formulate specific testable hypotheses, such as: "Users with <15% battery are charged significantly higher fares for identical routes compared to users with >50% battery, regardless of demand levels." Establish a clear policy statement: battery level may inform ride feasibility suggestions but must not act as a multiplier for price gouging during urgent needs.

**2. Construct Synthetic User Personas and Scenarios**
Create a matrix of synthetic user profiles representing diverse socioeconomic backgrounds typical of São Paulo. Define scenarios combining low-battery states with high-urgency contexts (e.g., medical appointments, late-night commutes from unsafe areas, rain events). Ensure these scenarios isolate the battery variable while holding constant other pricing factors like distance, time, and base demand to attribute price changes solely to the battery signal.

**3. Execute Controlled Simulation Testing**
Since the core algorithm cannot be modified, run offline simulations using historical trip data from São Paulo. Inject the synthetic battery signals into the pricing engine's input stream to observe output fares. Compare the simulated fares against a control group where battery data is neutralized or set to optimal levels. Document the percentage price delta attributed strictly to low battery status across different neighborhoods and times of day.

**4. Analyze Disparate Impact and Vulnerability**
Evaluate simulation results for disproportionate impacts on specific demographics or geographic zones. Determine if the pricing pattern effectively penalizes users who lack access to power banks or charging infrastructure, potentially correlating with lower-income areas. Assess whether the price increase creates a barrier to essential mobility, forcing users into unsafe alternatives or financial distress.

**5. Develop Mitigation Strategies and Guardrails**
Based on findings, propose non-code mitigations that can be implemented within the current freeze constraints or via configuration flags. Options include:
*   **Capping:** Implement a hard ceiling on fare increases attributable to battery status.
*   **Decoupling:** Restrict battery data usage to non-monetary features (e.g., suggesting nearby charging stops) rather than price calculation.
*   **Transparency:** Draft clear in-app disclosures explaining how device status influences service options, ensuring informed consent.
*   **Emergency Exemptions:** Define criteria for waiving dynamic pricing during verified emergencies.

**6. Document Findings and Establish Governance**
Compile a risk assessment report detailing the methodology, simulation data, identified risks, and recommended mitigations. Present this to leadership and the ethics board with a clear "Go/No-Go" recommendation. If significant exploitative patterns are found and cannot be mitigated without code changes, recommend delaying the feature's activation despite the optimization stage. Establish a post-launch monitoring plan to track real-world fare distributions and user complaints related to battery-based pricing.

**7. Stakeholder Validation**
Before final approval, share the summarized findings and proposed safeguards with a small group of external community advocates or user representatives in São Paulo to validate that the mitigations address genuine local concerns regarding fairness and urgency.

*Note: This plan relies on simulation and policy configuration since direct code modification is restricted. Immediate leadership alignment is required to authorize potential feature delays if risks are unmitigable.*
