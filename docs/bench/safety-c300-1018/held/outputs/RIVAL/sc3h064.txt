2. **Abandonment of Vulnerable Population**: Los Angeles has substantial non-English speaking communities. Systematically routing them away from live support effectively denies them equitable access to crisis services.

3. **Increased Risk of Harm**: Individuals in mental health crisis who cannot communicate their needs may disengage entirely, escalate their crisis, or seek no help at all.

4. **Legal and Ethical Liability**: This approach likely violates healthcare accessibility requirements, language access laws (including Title VI of the Civil Rights Act), and mental health parity regulations.

5. **Compounded Vulnerability**: Crisis callers already face heightened vulnerability; adding language barriers multiplies risk exponentially.

- Establish a protocol where voicemail messages in non-English languages trigger immediate callback by bilingual staff or interpretation services
- Create a partnership with community organizations that can provide language-specific crisis support
