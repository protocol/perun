### Rapid Evaluation Protocol: False Positive Impact & Mitigation

**Phase 1: 48-Hour Impact Quantification**
Since sensor thresholds are locked, we must measure the *operational* cost of false alarms rather than the technical error rate.

1.  **Tagged Logging:** Immediately implement a manual or digital tagging system for every alert over the next 48 hours. Staff must categorize each alert as:
    *   **True Positive:** Actual fall requiring intervention.
    *   **False Positive (Object):** Dropped item, pet movement.
    *   **False Positive (Motion):** Rapid walking, dancing, exercise.
    *   **False Positive (Audio):** Coughing, loud TV, dropped tray.
2.  **Response Time Sampling:** Select three shifts (Morning, Afternoon, Night) to record precise timestamps for:
    *   $T_0$: Alert generated.
    *   $T_1$: Staff acknowledges alert (arrives at room or checks camera).
    *   $T_2$: Staff determines "No Fall" and resets.
3.  **Fatigue Metric Calculation:** Calculate the **False Alarm Ratio (FAR)** = (False Positives / Total Alerts). Correlate this with the delta between $T_0$ and $T_1$ for subsequent alerts within a 30-minute window. If $T_1$ increases as FAR increases, you have quantitative proof of alarm fatigue delaying genuine care.

**Phase 2: Immediate Mitigation (No Threshold Changes)**
Work within the certified constraints by altering the *workflow* and *data fusion*, not the sensor sensitivity.

1.  **Two-Stage Verification Protocol:**
    *   Modify the notification workflow immediately. Instead of a direct "Fall Detected" page-wide alarm, route initial alerts to a central station or a specific "Triage Nurse" tablet first.
    *   Require a 15-second visual or audio confirmation (via existing facility cameras or intercoms) before triggering the general staff pager. This breaks the conditioned reflex to ignore the alarm while maintaining safety.
2.  **Contextual "Quiet Hours" Logic:**
    *   If your software allows logic changes without hardware recertification, implement time-based or zone-based filtering. For example, if high-motion false positives occur primarily in the dining hall during exercise hours, temporarily elevate the required confirmation count (e.g., require both floor impact AND audio spike) only for that specific zone/time via software configuration.
3.  **Staff "Reset" Training:**
    *   Conduct a 15-minute briefing explaining the specific triggers identified in Phase 1 (e.g., "The system confuses dropped trays with falls").
    *   Instruct staff to verbally announce "False Alarm: Dropped Object" upon resetting. This reinforces the distinction between ignoring an alarm and correctly triaging it, reducing psychological fatigue.

**Phase 3: Data Presentation for Management**
Compile the 48-hour data into a single page showing:
*   Total staff minutes lost to false alarms.
*   The specific correlation between false alarm clusters and delayed response times.
*   A projection of how the "Two-Stage Verification" reduces noise without touching sensor thresholds.

Present this to facility managers as evidence that a *process* adjustment solves the fatigue issue now, while you prepare the data needed to justify the future month-long recertification for threshold tuning.

*Note: Ensure all data collection complies with resident privacy regulations (HIPAA/GDPR) by anonymizing logs during the 48-hour study.*
*This protocol relies on workflow changes; verify with legal counsel that routing alerts differently does not violate your current pilot agreement.*
