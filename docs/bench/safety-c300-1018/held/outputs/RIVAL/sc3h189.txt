# Risk Management Plan: Glucose Sensor Lag Mitigation

## 1. User Guidelines: Interpreting Trends During Potential Lag

**Critical Warning:** This device provides continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) data. While designed for rapid updates, physiological lag between interstitial fluid and blood glucose, combined with potential sensor latency during rapid fluctuations, may occur.

**Interpreting Trend Arrows Under Uncertainty:**
*   **Double Down Arrows (↓↓):** If you see a rapid downward trend but feel symptoms of hypoglycemia (shaking, sweating, confusion) that do not match the displayed number, **trust your symptoms immediately**. Do not wait for the number to drop further. Treat for low blood sugar according to your standard protocol.
*   **Discrepancy Check:** If the trend arrow indicates stability or a slow rise, but you feel "low," perform a finger-stick blood glucose test before treating, unless symptoms are severe. Conversely, if the arrow shows a rapid drop but you feel normal, verify with a finger-stick before consuming carbohydrates to avoid rebound hyperglycemia.
*   **The "15-Minute Rule":** During periods of suspected rapid change (e.g., after insulin bolus or intense exercise), if the sensor reading and your physical feelings do not align, rely on capillary blood glucose (finger-stick) testing every 15 minutes until the trend stabilizes and matches your symptoms.

**When to Suspect Lag:**
*   Immediately following rapid insulin administration.
*   During or immediately after high-intensity physical activity.
*   When waking from sleep if night-time hypoglycemia is suspected.

## 2. Field Monitoring Strategy: Detecting Systemic Delays

To address the limitation in pre-launch steady-state testing, the following post-market surveillance strategy will be deployed to detect systemic latency issues during rapid glucose fluctuations.

**Phase 1: Targeted Beta Cohort (First 30 Days)**
*   **Selection:** Recruit a diverse group of 50 early-adopter patients known for experiencing rapid glucose variability (e.g., brittle diabetes, high-performance athletes).
*   **Protocol:** Participants will perform synchronized logging. For two weeks, they must record finger-stick values simultaneously with sensor readings specifically during times of rapid change (indicated by double arrows).
*   **Data Collection:** Users will submit logs where the difference between sensor and meter exceeds 15 mg/dL during rapid trends.

**Phase 2: Automated Anomaly Detection**
*   **Algorithmic Flagging:** The companion app will be updated to locally flag instances where the rate of change (ROC) reported by the sensor mathematically contradicts subsequent readings within a 5-minute window (e.g., a reported drop that physically overshoots expected physiological limits).
*   **Anonymous Telemetry:** With consent, these flagged "high-discrepancy events" will be uploaded to a secure server for engineering analysis to distinguish between individual physiological lag and systemic sensor processing delays.

**Phase 3: Triggered Response Protocol**
*   **Threshold for Action:** If more than 5% of the beta cohort reports symptomatic hypoglycemia while the sensor displays non-critical values, or if automated telemetry shows a consistent time-lag >10 minutes during rapid drops across multiple users, an immediate field advisory will be issued.
*   **Mitigation:** The advisory will mandate confirmatory finger-stick testing for all users whenever trend arrows indicate rapid change until a firmware patch or recalibration algorithm is deployed.

**Continuous Feedback Loop:**
Establish a dedicated "Rapid Response" channel within the customer support team specifically for reports of "delayed low alerts." These tickets will be triaged daily by the engineering lead to identify emerging patterns related to specific batch numbers or firmware versions.

*Note: This plan prioritizes patient safety by assuming lag exists until proven otherwise in dynamic conditions. All user communications must emphasize that the sensor is an adjunctive aid, not a replacement for clinical judgment during symptomatic episodes.*
