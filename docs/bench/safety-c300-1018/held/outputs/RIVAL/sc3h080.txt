**Pre-Launch Safety Protocol: Body Image & Eating Disorder Risk Mitigation**

Given the three-week timeline preventing a full algorithm redesign, this protocol focuses on immediate containment, manual oversight, and transparent user safeguards.

**1. Immediate Content Filtering (The "Red Line" List)**
Implement a hard-coded filter layer that overrides the recommendation engine if any suggested content matches these high-risk categories. This does not require retraining the model, only blocking specific outputs post-generation:
*   **Prohibited Visuals:** Images depicting visible ribs, spine, or collarbones as aesthetic ideals; "thigh gaps"; extreme waist cinching; or medical devices (feeding tubes, hospital scales) used as fashion props.
*   **Prohibited Textual Triggers:** Keywords and phrases including "starvation," "purge," "bones," "skinny," "ana/mia" (anorexia/bulimia slang), "what I eat in a day" (if under 1200 calories), and any content promoting rapid weight loss (>1kg/week).
*   **Action:** If the engine suggests an item paired with these triggers, the system must default to a generic, neutral style recommendation or display no result.

**2. Human-in-the-Loop "Teen Mode" Audit**
Since automated detection may miss context, establish a manual review sprint for the highest-traffic user segments:
*   **Segmentation:** Isolate all user accounts identified as under 18.
*   **Sampling:** Your team of eight designers/analysts must manually review the top 500 recommended outfits for this demographic daily for the next two weeks.
*   **Criteria:** Reviewers should flag any ensemble that visually emphasizes extreme thinness or pairs clothing with body-shaming copy.
*   **Fallback:** If a user's feed cannot be cleared of risky content within 24 hours, temporarily disable personalized recommendations for that account and serve only curated, pre-approved "safe" collections.

**3. User-Facing Safeguards & Resources**
*   **Trigger Warnings:** Append a discrete, non-intrusive disclaimer to style feeds: *"Our AI suggests styles based on trends. Fashion is for expression, not body modification. Healthy bodies come in all shapes."*
*   **Support Integration:** Embed a permanent, clickable link in the app footer and near profile settings: *"Struggling with body image or eating disorders? Click here for free, confidential support."* Link this directly to recognized European helplines (e.g., Beat in the UK, FDN in France).
*   **Reporting Mechanism:** Add a one-tap "This makes me feel unsafe" button on every recommendation card. Route these reports to a dedicated email alias monitored daily by your safety lead.

**4. Data Hygiene Check**
*   Immediately pause any training data ingestion from sources known for pro-eating-disorder content (e.g., specific unmoderated social media tags).
*   Ensure the current model is not optimizing purely for "engagement" if that metric correlates with controversial or extreme imagery. Temporarily cap engagement-weighting in favor of diversity metrics.

**Regulatory Confirmation on Terms of Service**

**No**, current European regulations (including the EU AI Act and the Digital Services Act) **do not** require you to state in your Terms of Service that your AI is "certified to be free from any bias."

In fact, making such a claim is legally dangerous and likely non-compliant:
1.  **Impossibility of Certification:** There is currently no official EU body that issues "bias-free" certifications for generative AI or recommendation engines. Claiming you have one would be false advertising.
2.  **Liability Risk:** The EU AI Act requires *high-risk* systems to undergo conformity assessments and manage bias, but it mandates *risk management processes*, not perfection. Stating you are "free from bias" creates an absolute guarantee; if a single user encounters biased output, you could be sued for breach of contract or misleading practices.
3.  **Correct Approach:** Instead of claiming freedom from bias, your Terms and Transparency Notices should acknowledge the use of AI, state your commitment to *mitigating* risks, and provide a clear channel for users to report harmful outputs. Transparency about limitations is preferred over false assurances of safety.

*Note: This guidance is based on current regulatory frameworks but does not constitute legal counsel. Given the EU rollout, consult with a qualified EU privacy/tech attorney before finalizing your Terms of Service.*
