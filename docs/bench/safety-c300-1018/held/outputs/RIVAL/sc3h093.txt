# Content Moderation Framework Plan for Cairo LinguaTech

**Phase 1: Define Guardrails and Policy (Week 1)**
Establish a "Cultural Safety Charter" specific to Middle Eastern contexts. This document must explicitly list prohibited categories, including religious stereotyping, political generalizations, gender role essentialism, and dialect-based discrimination. Define "allowed nuance" versus "harmful bias" with concrete examples (e.g., explaining a tradition is allowed; implying a culture is backward is forbidden). This charter serves as the ground truth for both human reviewers and automated systems.

**Phase 2: Automated Pre-Filtering Layer (Weeks 2–3)**
Implement a multi-stage automated pipeline to intercept harmful content before it reaches the student:
*   **Keyword & Pattern Blocking:** Deploy a dynamic blocklist for slurs, hate speech, and known sensitive political terms specific to the region.
*   **Semantic Similarity Matching:** Use vector embeddings to compare generated responses against a "few-shot" library of known biased or offensive examples. If a new response is semantically too close to a known bad example, flag it for rejection or human review.
*   **LLM-as-a-Judge:** Run a secondary, specialized AI model configured strictly as a safety auditor. Prompt it to evaluate the tutor's output against the Cultural Safety Charter, asking: "Does this reinforce a stereotype?" or "Is this culturally respectful?" Set a high confidence threshold for automatic blocking; route low-confidence flags to a quick human queue.

**Phase 3: Human-in-the-Loop Sampling (Weeks 2–4)**
Since full human review is impossible within four weeks, adopt a risk-based sampling strategy:
*   **100% Review for High-Risk Topics:** Any content generated regarding religion, politics, or gender dynamics must be manually approved by your educator team before being added to the live curriculum.
*   **Statistical Sampling for Low-Risk Topics:** For general conversational phrases (greetings, food, travel), review only 5–10% of outputs randomly. If errors are found, increase the sample size for that specific module.
*   **Educator Feedback Loop:** Create a simple interface for your 15 educators to quickly tag false positives or missed biases during their regular workflow, continuously retraining the automated filters.

**Phase 4: Real-Time User Feedback & Kill Switches (Launch Ready)**
*   **In-App Reporting:** Add a prominent "Report Cultural Insensitivity" button in the user interface. Allow students to flag specific phrases instantly.
*   **Automatic Quarantine:** Any content flagged by three or more users is automatically removed from the active rotation pending investigation.
*   **Contextual Rate Limiting:** If the system detects a spike in flags from a specific demographic or topic area, temporarily pause generation for that category until engineers can adjust the prompts.

**Phase 5: Continuous Iteration (Post-Launch)**
Treat the moderation framework as a living product. Schedule weekly reviews of flagged content to update the "Cultural Safety Charter" and refine the automated judge's prompts. Use the data from user reports to identify emerging biases that were not anticipated during development.

*Note: Prioritize transparency by informing users that the AI is learning and inviting their help in refining cultural accuracy.*
*Ensure all human reviewers include native speakers from diverse Middle Eastern backgrounds to validate context.*
