### Strategy to Evaluate Disparate Impact and Chilling Effects

**1. Proxy-Based Disparate Impact Analysis**
Since direct socioeconomic indicators are unavailable, construct proxy variables from existing legacy data to approximate protected attributes. Analyze correlations between available fields (e.g., high school type, district codes, extracurricular availability, or language patterns in essays) and known demographic distributions in Seoul. Run the predictor on historical data stratified by these proxies to calculate the "adverse impact ratio." If the selection rate for any proxy group is less than 80% of the rate for the highest-scoring group, a prima facie case of disparate impact exists. Document which specific input features drive score reductions for these groups.

**2. Counterfactual "Chilling Effect" Simulation**
Simulate applicant behavior by generating synthetic profiles that represent qualified candidates from underrepresented backgrounds. Systematically vary only the proxy-identified sensitive features while holding academic merit constant. Record the predicted success scores. If the tool assigns significantly lower probabilities to these counterfactual applicants despite identical academic qualifications, it creates a high risk of discouraging applications. Map these score drops against typical applicant decision thresholds; if scores fall below the psychological threshold where students feel "welcome" to apply, the tool is actively reinforcing inequality.

**3. Uncertainty Quantification and Calibration Checks**
Legacy data often lacks representation of minority groups, leading to high variance and unreliable predictions for those populations. Calculate confidence intervals for every prediction. If the model exhibits wide error bars or low calibration for applicants matching underrepresented proxies, flag these outputs as "low confidence." A deterministic score presented without uncertainty metrics misleads users. The review must verify if the model is extrapolating beyond its training distribution for these groups, which invalidates the predictive claim for those specific applicants.

**4. Communication Protocol for Users**
Develop a mandatory disclosure framework to accompany every predicted score. Instead of displaying a single number, present a range (e.g., "65%–85% probability") alongside a plain-language explanation of data limitations. Explicitly state: "This prediction is based on historical patterns that may not reflect your unique potential or recent changes in educational access." Include a prominent disclaimer that the score is an advisory metric, not a definitive judgment of capability, and encourage application regardless of the output if the student meets basic eligibility criteria.

**5. Human-in-the-Loop Governance**
Establish a review board comprising the five admissions officers and an external equity auditor. Mandate that any applicant flagged by the system as "low probability" but who possesses strong qualitative indicators (e.g., compelling personal statements or upward grade trends) undergoes manual review before any communication is sent. The tool should function as a decision-support aid for identifying resources, not as a gatekeeper. Freeze any automated messaging that discourages application until the disparate impact analysis is complete and mitigations are deployed.

*Note: This plan relies on statistical inference via proxies due to data restrictions; results should be treated as indicative rather than definitive proof of bias.*
*Immediate execution requires isolating the legacy dataset to a secure, offline environment to maintain privacy compliance during testing.*
