To evaluate and adjust your carbon-footprint tracker's messaging before launch, implement the following low-compute evaluation plan:

**1. Define "Essential Travel" Heuristics (No Retraining Required)**
Instead of retraining models, overlay a rule-based filter on your existing classification output. Create a whitelist of destination categories and keywords that automatically flag travel as "essential," bypassing high-emission shaming logic.
*   **Destination Mapping:** Map coordinates to known medical facilities (hospitals, clinics, dialysis centers, pharmacies). If the destination matches, tag as essential.
*   **Keyword Triggers:** Scan user-entered trip purposes for terms like "doctor," "therapy," "medical," "hospital," "caregiver," or "prescription."
*   **Frequency Patterns:** Identify recurring short trips to the same location (often indicative of treatment routines) versus one-off leisure travel.

**2. Messaging A/B Testing Framework**
Create two distinct messaging tiers to test user stress levels and behavioral response without altering the core emission calculation engine.
*   **Tier A (Current):** High-emission alerts with negative framing (e.g., "This trip heavily impacts your footprint").
*   **Tier B (Adjusted):** Neutral data presentation for flagged essential trips (e.g., "This trip generated X kg CO2. We understand this travel is necessary for your health.").
*   **Evaluation Metric:** Deploy a simple in-app survey after trip logging asking, "How did this feedback make you feel?" (Scale: Stressed/Neutral/Motivated). Compare scores between Tier A and Tier B for medical-tagged trips.

**3. Synthetic Scenario Walkthroughs**
Since you cannot afford extensive live user testing yet, conduct internal "paper prototype" sessions with your five-person team acting as diverse user personas.
*   **Scenario Creation:** Draft 10 specific user journeys involving essential high-emission travel (e.g., "Emergency room visit," "Weekly chemotherapy," "Rural specialist appointment").
*   **Manual Audit:** Run these scenarios through your current app logic. Document every instance where the app produces shaming language.
*   **Rule Validation:** Apply your new heuristics manually to these scenarios. Verify if the "essential" flag triggers correctly and if the alternative messaging appears.

**4. Resource-Efficient Implementation Strategy**
*   **Client-Side Logic:** Implement the "essential" flagging logic on the client side (mobile app) as a simple lookup table or regex check. This requires zero server-side model retraining and minimal compute.
*   **Configuration File:** Store the list of medical keywords and facility types in a remote configuration file. This allows you to update the "essential" definitions instantly without releasing a new app version if you miss categories during beta.

**5. Success Criteria for Beta**
Define clear pass/fail metrics for the public beta:
*   **False Positive Rate:** Less than 5% of essential medical trips should receive shaming messages.
*   **User Sentiment:** 80% of users flagged as "essential travelers" should report neutral or positive sentiment regarding the app's feedback.
*   **Retention:** No drop-off in app usage among users who log frequent medical travel compared to the general user base.

By focusing on rule-based overlays and targeted messaging adjustments rather than model retraining, you can effectively decouple emission tracking from behavioral shaming for essential services within your resource constraints.

*Note: Ensure your medical facility database is updated regularly via open-source geospatial datasets to maintain accuracy.*
*Prioritize user privacy by performing keyword matching locally on the device whenever possible.*
