### Multilingual Suicide Risk Detection Review Strategy

**Phase 1: Resource Assembly (Days 1–3)**
Leverage open, community-maintained datasets to bypass budget constraints.
*   **Lexicons:** Integrate the NRC Emotion Lexicon (available in multiple languages) and the LIWC dictionary to establish baseline keyword triggers for despair, hopelessness, and self-harm in your supported languages.
*   **Crowdsourced Corpora:** Utilize the "Self-harm and Suicide" datasets available on Hugging Face, filtering for non-English entries (e.g., Spanish, French, Mandarin). Supplement these with translated samples from the CDC's suicide prevention communication guidelines, using high-quality machine translation (e.g., DeepL or NLLB) followed by a rapid peer-review cycle among your four team members to catch obvious cultural mismatches.
*   **Cultural Context Guides:** Download WHO's "Preventing Suicide: A Global Imperative" and specific country reports for your target demographics to identify culture-specific idioms of distress that direct translation misses.

**Phase 2: Scenario Simulation & Adversarial Testing (Days 4–10)**
Construct a "Red Team" matrix of 50–75 synthetic conversation scripts per language. Do not rely solely on direct translations of English scenarios.
*   **Direct Expression:** Explicit statements of intent ("I want to end it all").
*   **Idiomatic/Cultural Expression:** Phrases that imply suicide culturally but not literally in English (e.g., specific metaphors for "disappearing" or "sleeping forever" unique to the target culture).
*   **Ambiguous Distress:** High-severity depression markers that should trigger a human handoff even without explicit suicide mentions.
*   **Code-Switching:** Simulate users mixing languages within a single sentence, a common behavior in multilingual regions like Montreal, to test if the detector fails when context switches mid-stream.

**Phase 3: The "Four-Eyes" Validation Loop (Days 11–17)**
With no budget for external consultants, institutionalize a rigorous internal review protocol:
1.  **Blind Run:** Run all synthetic scripts through the bot. Log every instance where the bot fails to escalate or provides a generic, non-empathetic response.
2.  **Native Speaker Swap:** Assign each team member a language they are *least* proficient in (but can access via translation tools) to review the failure logs. This forces a fresh perspective on whether the bot's response feels safe or dismissive.
3.  **False Positive Check:** Ensure the bot isn't flagging benign cultural expressions as crises, which causes alert fatigue.
4.  **Threshold Tuning:** Adjust confidence thresholds for risk detection based on these findings. If a language consistently under-performs, lower the threshold for human escalation for that specific language until more data is gathered.

**Phase 4: Go/No-Go Decision Matrix (Days 18–21)**
Define strict launch criteria. If any supported language has a false-negative rate (missed suicide risk) above 0% in your synthetic testing, that language module must be disabled or forced into "high-alert mode" (escalating all ambiguous queries to humans) before the campaign launch. Document all known gaps in a public-facing transparency note if full coverage isn't achieved, prioritizing safety over feature completeness.

**Note:** Prioritize disabling features over risking missed detections; safety is the only acceptable metric for launch.
**Note:** Continue collecting real-world anonymized failure data post-launch to refine models iteratively.
