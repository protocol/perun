# Pre-Launch Safety Plan: Mumbai MarketWatch Stock-Alert Bot

**Objective:** Prevent false crash warnings that could trigger panic selling among retirees by implementing immediate, low-tooling safeguards within your three-week timeline.

### 1. Identify Critical Failure Modes
Based on your description of "normal volatility triggering false crash warnings," the primary risks are:
*   **Threshold Rigidity:** Static percentage-drop thresholds (e.g., "alert if down 5%") failing to account for normal intraday swings or sector-specific volatility.
*   **Context Blindness:** The algorithm detecting a price drop without verifying if it is isolated to a single stock or a broad market event.
*   **Noise Amplification:** Temporary data glitches or low-liquidity spikes being interpreted as a crash signal.
*   **Alert Storms:** Rapid, repeated notifications for the same event causing disproportionate user anxiety.

### 2. Immediate Mitigation Steps (No Advanced Simulation Required)

**A. Implement "Cool-Down" and Confirmation Logic**
*   **Time-Weighted Verification:** Do not alert on a single data point. Require the threshold breach to persist for a set duration (e.g., 5–15 minutes) before triggering. This filters out flash crashes and data spikes.
*   **Volume Confirmation:** Cross-reference price drops with trading volume. A significant drop on low volume is often a false signal; require volume to exceed a moving average before issuing a "crash" alert.

**B. Dynamic Thresholding via Simple Heuristics**
*   **Sector Relative Check:** Before alerting, compare the stock's drop against its sector index (e.g., Nifty 50 or specific sectoral indices). If the whole sector is down, it may be a real trend; if only one stock is down while the sector is flat, suppress the "market crash" language and label it as "individual stock volatility."
*   **Historical Baseline (Manual):** Since you lack simulation tools, manually review the last 6 months of data for your top 20 tracked stocks. Identify the maximum normal intraday drop for each and set your alert threshold 20% higher than that historical maximum.

**C. Message Framing and Calibration**
*   **De-escalate Language:** Replace alarming terms like "CRASH DETECTED" or "SELL NOW" with neutral, factual language: "Significant Volatility Detected" or "Price Drop Alert."
*   **Add Contextual Disclaimers:** Every alert must include a standard footer: *"This is an automated observation of price movement, not financial advice. Market fluctuations are normal. Please consult a financial advisor before making decisions."*
*   **Cap Frequency:** Hard-code a limit of one alert per stock per hour to prevent panic-inducing notification storms.

### 3. Low-Tech Stress Testing Protocol
With four developers and three weeks, execute a "Manual Tabletop" simulation:
*   **Day 1-2:** Gather 10 historical instances of high volatility that were *not* crashes (false positives) and 5 real crash events (true positives).
*   **Day 3-4:** Run your current logic against these 15 scenarios manually (input the data points and trace the code flow). Record which false positives trigger alerts.
*   **Day 5:** Adjust thresholds and logic based on findings. Repeat until all 10 false positives are suppressed while retaining the 5 true positives.

### 4. Launch Governance
*   **Staged Rollout:** Do not launch to all users immediately. Release to an internal "beta group" (friends/family of the team) for the first 48 hours of live market trading.
*   **Kill Switch:** Implement an immediate global toggle to disable all alerts instantly if a systemic error is detected post-launch. Assign one developer to monitor this dashboard during market hours.

By focusing on time-weighted confirmation, volume checks, and careful message framing, you can significantly reduce false positives without needing complex simulation infrastructure.

*Note: Ensure your legal team reviews the new disclaimer language before launch. Prioritize the "Kill Switch" implementation as your highest coding priority.*
