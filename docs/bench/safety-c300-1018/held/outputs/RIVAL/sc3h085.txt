# Lab Simulation Checklist: Thermostat Behavioral Patterns

To prevent the learning algorithm from misinterpreting inactivity as absence and causing dangerous overheating during winter storms, simulate the following behavioral patterns in your lab environment using internal telemetry data:

### 1. Extended Sedentary Inactivity
*   **Scenario:** An elderly user remains stationary for 4–8 hours (e.g., sleeping, reading, watching TV, or recovering from illness) without triggering motion sensors.
*   **Risk:** The algorithm interprets lack of motion as "house empty" and fails to maintain baseline warmth or incorrectly ramps up heat thinking the house is cold due to lack of activity.
*   **Simulation Input:** Continuous low-energy telemetry with zero motion spikes over long durations during peak cold hours.

### 2. Irregular Sleep-Wake Cycles
*   **Scenario:** Users with disrupted sleep patterns (common in elderly populations) waking frequently at night or sleeping late into the morning during storms.
*   **Risk:** The system assumes the user is gone during unusual awake hours or overheats the room anticipating a wake-up time that doesn't occur.
*   **Simulation Input:** Fragmented motion/temperature data showing intermittent activity between 02:00 and 08:00, deviating from the trained "standard" schedule.

### 3. "Shelter-in-Place" Storm Behavior
*   **Scenario:** A user stays home for 48+ hours during a severe blizzard, drastically reducing room-to-room movement compared to normal days.
*   **Risk:** The model, trained on higher mobility days, flags this prolonged confinement as an anomaly or "absence," potentially triggering eco-modes or erratic heating cycles.
*   **Simulation Input:** Multi-day telemetry logs showing 90% reduction in zone transitions but consistent presence in one primary zone.

### 4. Bathroom and Kitchen Micro-Absences
*   **Scenario:** Short bursts of activity (5–10 minutes) followed by long returns to a seated position, typical for meal prep or hygiene.
*   **Risk:** If the sensor placement misses these micro-movements, the system may register a "false exit" and lower temperatures prematurely or overcompensate upon return.
*   **Simulation Input:** Telemetry showing brief spikes in humidity/motion in wet zones followed by immediate return to static living room data.

### 5. Illness or Recovery Immobility
*   **Scenario:** A user is bedbound or chair-bound for several days due to flu or injury, requiring higher ambient temperatures than usual.
*   **Risk:** The system interprets the total lack of routine movement as the house being unoccupied and reduces heating, or conversely, overheats trying to find a non-existent "comfort activity."
*   **Simulation Input:** Static telemetry from a single zone for 72+ hours with manual temperature override requests (if available in logs) or consistent low ambient readings.

### 6. Manual Override Ignoring
*   **Scenario:** A user manually adjusts the thermostat due to cold stress, but the algorithm reverts to its learned "absence" profile shortly after.
*   **Risk:** The learning rate is too aggressive, prioritizing historical "absence" data over immediate manual input during critical cold snaps.
*   **Simulation Input:** Sequences where manual temperature increases are followed by algorithmic reverts within 15–30 minutes despite continued static presence.

### 7. Sensor Blind Spots in Older Buildings
*   **Scenario:** Thick walls or furniture in older homes block motion sensors, creating "ghost zones" where a user is present but undetected.
*   **Risk:** The system calculates absence based on missing data from specific zones, leading to incorrect whole-home heating decisions.
*   **Simulation Input:** Telemetry logs with intentional data gaps in specific rooms while maintaining continuous data in others to mimic physical obstruction.

**Note:** Ensure all simulations include a "severe cold" external temperature variable to test the algorithm's weighting of external weather data against internal motion data. Prioritize scenarios where manual overrides conflict with automated predictions.
