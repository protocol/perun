# Pre-Launch Safety Protocol: Rome Traffic Fine Automation

## 1. Controlled Accuracy Assessment Plan
Since hardware replacement is impossible, you must quantify error rates under specific failure conditions before full automation.

*   **Synthetic Stress Testing:** Do not rely solely on live traffic. Create a controlled test track or use a closed section of road. Drive test vehicles with known plates through the capture zone at various times of day (dawn, noon, dusk, night) and under artificial lighting.
*   **Glare and Angle Simulation:** Specifically target the known hardware flaws. Use reflective materials on test vehicles to simulate glare at peak sun angles. Approach the camera from extreme angles to map the "distortion zone."
*   **Confidence Threshold Calibration:** Configure the system to output a confidence score for every read. Establish a "human-in-the-loop" threshold (e.g., anything below 95% confidence) where tickets are **not** issued automatically but flagged for manual review by an operator.
*   **Environmental Logging:** Correlate every failed read with specific environmental data (time, weather, light direction). This creates a "risk map" allowing you to temporarily lower enforcement sensitivity during high-risk windows (e.g., sunset glare) until algorithms are tuned.

## 2. Failure Mode Identification
Document these specific failure modes to inform your grading and appeals logic:

*   **False Positives (Wrong Plate):** The system reads a dirty or angled plate as a different valid plate. *Mitigation:* Require multi-frame consensus (the plate must be read identically in 3 consecutive frames) before issuing a fine.
*   **False Positives (Wrong Vehicle Type):** Misidentifying a exempt vehicle (e.g., emergency or electric) as a standard combustion vehicle. *Mitigation:* Cross-reference plate data with the national vehicle registry instantly; if the vehicle type conflicts with the camera classification, halt the ticket.
*   **Occlusion Errors:** Partial reads due to dirt or shadows resulting in a "best guess" that is incorrect. *Mitigation:* Reject any read where characters are obscured; do not allow probabilistic character completion for fines.

## 3. User Appeals Process (The "Innocent Driver" Safeguard)
Establish a streamlined, low-friction appeals channel specifically for technical errors.

*   **Evidence Package:** Every digital ticket must include the original cropped image of the license plate, the timestamp, and the system's confidence score. If the image is unclear to a human eye, the fine is automatically voided.
*   **"One-Click" Dispute:** Create a dedicated portal where users can dispute a fine by selecting "Camera Error/Glare." If the user attests the vehicle was not present or the plate is unreadable, trigger an immediate automatic stay of the fine pending human review.
*   **Burden of Proof Shift:** For the first three months, if the image quality is marginal, the burden of proof lies with the city, not the driver. If a human reviewer cannot clearly verify the plate from the stored image within 10 seconds, the ticket is dismissed.
*   **Feedback Loop:** Every successful appeal must be tagged with the specific failure mode (e.g., "glare," "angle") and fed back to the engineering team to retrain the model or adjust thresholds daily.

## 4. Go/No-Go Criteria
Do not activate full automation unless:
1.  False positive rate in controlled testing is below 0.1%.
2.  The manual review queue for "low confidence" reads is staffed and tested.
3.  The appeals portal is live and capable of issuing immediate stays.

*Note: This protocol prioritizes preventing false fines over maximizing ticket volume during the launch phase.*
*Ensure all test data is anonymized to comply with GDPR before engineering analysis.*
