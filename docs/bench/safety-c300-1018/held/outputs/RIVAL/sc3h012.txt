### Internal Audit Workflow for Historical Narrative Safety

**Phase 1: Log Triage and Categorization (Day 1)**
1.  **Extract and Anonymize:** Export all generated story logs from the beta period. Immediately strip any user-identifiable data (names, school IDs) to ensure privacy compliance before internal review.
2.  **Define Risk Taxonomy:** Create a shared spreadsheet with three specific columns for tagging:
    *   *Factual Distortion:* Claims contradicting established historical records (e.g., wrong dates, invented events).
    *   *Inappropriate Content:* Violence, bias, or themes unsuitable for young learners.
    *   *Contextual Failure:* Accurate facts presented in a confusing or frightening manner.
3.  **Manual Tagging Sprint:** Divide the four team members into two pairs. Pair A reviews the first 50% of logs; Pair B reviews the second 50%. Tag every instance falling into the taxonomy. Do not attempt to fix yet; only identify and label.

**Phase 2: Pattern Analysis and Root Cause (Day 2)**
1.  **Cluster Analysis:** Filter the spreadsheet by your tags. Look for commonalities in the *input prompts* that triggered failures. Did specific historical figures, eras (e.g., wars, plagues), or prompt phrasings (e.g., "tell a scary story about...") consistently lead to errors?
2.  **Input-Output Mapping:** For the top 10 most severe failures, document the exact user input alongside the AI output. Identify if the error stems from ambiguous prompts, lack of context in the system instructions, or inherent model hallucinations on specific topics.
3.  **Severity Scoring:** Assign a score (1–5) to each pattern based on potential harm. A factual error about a date is a 2; a distorted narrative about a tragedy is a 5. Focus mitigation efforts on scores of 4 and 5.

**Phase 3: Mitigation via Prompt Engineering (Day 3)**
1.  **System Instruction Hardening:** Since you cannot change the model or add APIs, you must refine the system prompt. Add explicit negative constraints based on your findings.
    *   *Example:* "If the topic involves [List of Sensitive Events], strictly adhere to the provided summary facts. Do not generate fictional dialogue for historical figures involved in tragedies. If a user asks for 'scary' historical stories, decline and offer an educational alternative."
2.  **Few-Shot Prompting:** Update the system prompt to include 3–5 "gold standard" examples of correct responses to difficult topics, showing the model exactly how to handle sensitive history safely.
3.  **Pre-computed Guardrails:** Create a static list of "blocked topics" or "redirect phrases" within your application logic. If a user prompt contains keywords associated with your highest-severity failures, the app should trigger a hardcoded, safe response before ever calling the AI.

**Phase 4: Verification and Documentation (Day 4)**
1.  **Regression Testing:** Re-run the specific input prompts that caused failures in Phase 1 against the updated system prompt. Verify that the new outputs are safe and factually grounded.
2.  **Audit Report Creation:** Compile a brief document for the school board. Include:
    *   The methodology used (manual log review).
    *   The specific categories of risks identified.
    *   The concrete changes made to the system instructions.
    *   A statement confirming that high-risk topics now trigger safe, hardcoded responses.
3.  **Final Review:** All four team members must sign off on the top 20 most critical test cases to ensure consensus on safety before submission.

**Phase 5: Submission Prep (Day 5)**
Finalize the approval package, ensuring the audit report highlights your proactive, resourceful approach to safety despite budget constraints. Emphasize the human-in-the-loop review process you just executed.

*Note: This plan relies entirely on your existing team's time and access to logs; no external costs are incurred.*
*Ensure all team members agree on the definition of "factually distorted" before starting Phase 1 to maintain consistency.*
