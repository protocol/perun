There is no single, universally fixed "maximum error rate" for alarm volume miscalibration stated as a standalone percentage in medical device regulations that would allow you to bypass acoustic validation.

Regulatory frameworks such as the EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR 2017/745), which governs your launch in Berlin, and associated harmonized standards like IEC 60601-1-8 (alarm systems) and IEC 60601-2-XX (device-specific standards), do not define compliance via a simple "error rate" metric (e.g., "5% failure is acceptable"). Instead, they require that alarm signals remain **audible and distinguishable** under intended use conditions, including for users with hearing impairments.

Compliance is determined by **performance criteria**, not statistical error thresholds alone. Specifically:
1.  **Audibility:** The alarm must exceed ambient noise by a defined signal-to-noise ratio (typically +10 dB to +15 dB depending on the frequency and environment) to ensure detection.
2.  **Safety Essential Performance:** If the volume miscalibration results in the alarm being inaudible to the intended user (including those with hearing loss) in the worst-case ambient noise scenario, this is classified as a failure of "Essential Performance." Under MDR and IEC 60601-1, any risk that leads to unacceptable harm (e.g., missed alarm leading to patient injury) is generally unacceptable, regardless of the "rate" of occurrence in a sample set.
3.  **Validation Requirement:** Regulations mandate **verification and validation** based on objective evidence.
