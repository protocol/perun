### Pre-Launch Contract Validation Workflow

**Phase 1: Risk-Based Sampling Strategy**
Do not rely on random sampling. Construct a targeted dataset of 50–100 "complex service description" inputs specifically designed to trigger the known liability limit errors.
*   **Input Construction:** Manually draft inputs that combine multiple service tiers, ambiguous deliverables, and high-value transaction terms. These should mimic the edge cases where static analysis failed.
*   **Stratification:** Group these inputs by industry sector (e.g., IT services, consulting, retail) and contract value, as liability limits often vary by jurisdiction and sector.
*   **Execution:** Run these specific inputs through the document generator to produce a dedicated "Risk Batch" of draft contracts.

**Phase 2: Expert-Led Semantic Validation**
Since automated tools cannot catch semantic legal errors, human expertise is the only valid control.
*   **Reviewer Assignment:** Assign the 11 developers to pair with external legal consultants or senior internal counsel. Developers understand the input logic; lawyers understand the regulatory output.
*   **Clause Isolation:** Instruct reviewers to ignore formatting and standard clauses. Focus exclusively on the "Limitation of Liability," "Indemnification," and "Damages" sections.
*   **Regulatory Matrix:** Create a simple checklist based on current UK regulations (e.g., Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977, Consumer Rights Act 2015). The checklist should ask binary questions: "Is the liability cap clearly stated?", "Does it exclude death/personal injury (which is illegal)?", "Is the language unambiguous?"
*   **Blind Review:** Where possible, have a second legal expert review the flagged documents without knowing the specific input that generated them to avoid bias.

**Phase 3: Triage and Remediation**
*   **Categorization:** Tag every error found as either "Critical" (illegal clause, incorrect liability cap), "Major" (ambiguous wording that could lead to litigation), or "Minor" (stylistic issues).
*   **Root Cause Mapping:** For every Critical/Major error, trace it back to the specific input pattern. Document the exact phrase in the user input that caused the generator to fail. This creates a "Do Not Use" list for the demo if a code fix is impossible in two weeks.
*   **Hardening:** If a code fix cannot be deployed before the launch, implement a temporary "human-in-the-loop" guardrail. Any generated contract containing keywords from the "Root Cause" list must be flagged for mandatory manual review before download.

**Phase 4: Demo Safety Protocol**
*   **Curated Scenarios:** For the launch event, pre-generate and legally validate a specific set of 10–15 demo scenarios. Hard-code these into the demo environment if possible, or strictly restrict the live demo to these known-safe inputs.
*   **Disclaimer:** Ensure the demo interface clearly displays a watermark or notice stating "Draft for Review Only – Not Legally Binding" to mitigate risk during the live presentation.
*   **Rollback Plan:** Prepare a static PDF brochure of correct contracts as a fallback. If the live generator shows signs of instability or produces an error during the demo, immediately switch to presenting the pre-validated static examples.

**Phase 5: Sign-off**
*   Obtain written sign-off from the lead legal counsel confirming that the "Risk Batch" has been cleared or that the mitigation strategies (manual review gates) are active and staffed. Do not launch without this explicit authorization.

*Note: This plan prioritizes human legal review over automated testing, as semantic legal errors are outside the scope of static analysis.*
*Ensure all external consultants sign NDAs before accessing the proprietary generator logic.*
