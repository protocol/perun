**Pre-Launch Bias Evaluation Plan: Accent & Dialect Stress Test**

Given the ten-day constraint and inability to retrain, the objective is to quantify bias severity to inform risk mitigation (e.g., human-in-the-loop overrides) rather than model correction. Execute the following four-step protocol immediately using your internal database.

**1. Stratified Sample Selection (Days 1–2)**
Do not test random samples. Construct a "Challenge Set" of 60–100 interviews from your existing database, strictly balanced across these variables:
*   **Target Groups:** Select candidates with distinct non-standard accents or dialects relevant to your client base (e.g., North African French, Sub-Saharan African accents, Caribbean French, strong regional meridional accents).
*   **Control Groups:** Select candidates with standard "Parisian" French accents.
*   **Outcome Matching:** Crucially, pair these groups by *known qualification status*. For every accented candidate in the set, include a standard-accent candidate with similar experience, education, and role seniority. If historical hiring data exists, ensure equal representation of "hired/successful" vs. "rejected" outcomes within each accent group to isolate the variable of speech pattern from actual competence.

**2. Blind Scoring Execution (Days 3–4)**
Run the Challenge Set through the video analyzer in a isolated batch process.
*   **Blinding:** Ensure the evaluation team sees only the model's output scores and confidence intervals, not the candidate identity or accent label.
*   **Data Capture:** Record the raw "job fit" score, sub-scores (e.g., clarity, confidence, articulation), and the binary recommendation (hire/no-hire) for every sample.
*   **Metadata Tagging:** Internally tag each result with the pre-determined accent category and the ground-truth qualification status established in Step 1.

**3. Disparity Analysis (Days 5–6)**
Calculate the "Accent Penalty Delta" using these three specific metrics:
*   **Mean Score Deviation:** Compare the average job fit score of the Target Groups against the Control Groups. A statistically significant drop (>5%) indicates systemic bias.
*   **False Negative Rate (FNR) Disparity:** This is the critical risk metric. Calculate the percentage of *qualified* candidates (based on your ground truth) who were incorrectly rejected by the model for each accent group. If the FNR for non-standard accents exceeds the FNR for standard accents by more than 10%, the risk of reputational damage is high.
*   **Confidence Calibration:** Check if the model expresses lower confidence scores for non-standard accents even when the final score is high. Low confidence on accented speech suggests the model is uncertain, flagging these cases for human review.

**4. Mitigation & Go/No-Go Decision (Days 7–8)**
Based on the Delta:
*   **Green Light:** If disparity is <5%, proceed with pilot but monitor live data daily.
*   **Yellow Light (Recommended):** If disparity is 5–15%, do not disable the tool, but implement a "Human Review Gate." Any candidate with a score below the threshold *or* flagged with a non-standard accent profile must be reviewed by a human before rejection. This stops unfair automated rejections while keeping the tool active for scoring.
*   **Red Light:** If disparity is >15% or FNR is double that of the control group, pause the automated rejection feature entirely for the pilot. Use the tool only as a "second opinion" for human recruiters, not as a filter.

**Reporting (Days 9–10)**
Prepare a brief for stakeholders showing the False Negative Rate by accent. Frame the narrative around "risk containment": you are not claiming the model is perfect, but that you have installed a safety net to prevent discriminatory outcomes during the pilot.

*Note: Ensure all internal data used is anonymized before processing to maintain candidate privacy compliance.*
*This plan detects bias; it does not fix the underlying model weights.*
