### Clinical Manual Verification Guidelines for Inconclusive Drug Interaction Alerts

**1. Immediate Triage and Hold Protocol**
When the automated system returns an "inconclusive" result due to data gaps (particularly for rare medications), the prescription must be automatically placed in a "Pending Clinical Review" state. No recommendation containing an inconclusive flag may be transmitted to the patient or the prescribing physician until a human clinician completes the verification steps below.

**2. Multi-Source Data Reconciliation**
Since real-time databases are limited, the reviewing clinician must manually cross-reference the flagged medication against at least two static, high-authority sources:
*   The latest printed or PDF-based compendium of drug interactions (e.g., standard pharmacopeia updates available offline).
*   The specific patient's full medication history retrieved directly from the primary pharmacy database, ignoring cached summaries.
*   If the medication is rare, consult the manufacturer's official prescribing information sheet directly rather than relying on aggregated third-party data.

**3. Patient-Specific Risk Stratification**
Evaluate the potential interaction in the specific context of the elderly patient's physiology:
*   **Renal and Hepatic Function:** Check recent lab results. An interaction that is theoretically minor may be critical for a patient with reduced clearance.
*   **Therapeutic Duplication:** Verify if the "rare" medication is chemically similar to any current prescriptions, even if the names differ.
*   **Symptom Overlap:** Determine if side effects of the new drug mimic existing conditions, which could mask an adverse event.

**4. The "Two-Pharmacist" Rule for High-Risk Cases**
If the manual review cannot definitively rule out a severe interaction (e.g., risk of arrhythmia, respiratory depression, or severe bleeding) due to lack of data:
*   A second senior clinical pharmacist must independently review the case.
*   Both clinicians must sign off on the decision to proceed, modify, or reject the prescription within the system logs.
*   If consensus cannot be reached, the prescription is rejected by default, and the prescribing physician is notified to select an alternative therapy with a known safety profile.

**5. Documentation and Feedback Loop**
Every manual verification must be logged with:
*   The specific data sources consulted.
*   The rationale for overriding or upholding the inconclusive alert.
*   The identity of the reviewing clinicians.
*   These logs will be reviewed weekly by the safety lead to identify patterns in data gaps, prioritizing which rare medications need urgent integration into the automated database before the next grant milestone.

**6. Patient Monitoring Plan**
If a prescription is approved despite an inconclusive automated check:
*   The patient must be enrolled in an enhanced monitoring protocol (e.g., a follow-up call within 48 hours).
*   Clear written instructions must be provided to the patient and caregiver regarding specific warning signs related to the potential interaction.

**Note:** These guidelines prioritize patient safety over deployment speed; any ambiguity must result in a default "do not dispense" status. Ensure all thirteen team members are trained on this workflow before the pilot launch.
