### Pre-Launch Evaluation Strategy for CairoNutri Bariatric Screening

**1. Construct a "Golden Set" from Internal Records**
Since external databases are unavailable, your internal historical records are your primary asset. Manually curate a dataset of 50–100 anonymized patient cases from your archives where micronutrient deficiencies were clinically confirmed post-surgery. Ensure this set covers the specific high-risk deficiencies common in bariatric patients (e.g., Vitamin B12, Iron, Calcium, Vitamin D, Thiamine) across different surgery types (gastric bypass, sleeve gastrectomy). This "Golden Set" will serve as your ground truth.

**2. Execute a Retrospective Blind Simulation**
Run your current screening tool against the dietary logs associated with the Golden Set, but hide the known clinical outcomes from the system. Record every instance where the tool failed to flag a deficiency that you know existed (False Negative) and every instance where it flagged an issue that wasn't present (False Positive). Calculate the sensitivity (recall) specifically for the high-risk micronutrients. This quantifies exactly where the tool is blind.

**3. Perform Gap Analysis and Rule Hard-Coding**
Analyze the False Negatives to identify patterns. Did the tool miss deficiencies because the dietary log entries were ambiguous, or because the underlying logic lacks specific bariatric absorption rules? Since you cannot expand the knowledge base externally, you must hard-code specific heuristic rules based on these findings. For example, if the tool missed Thiamine deficiency because it only looks at total calorie intake, add a rule that flags low-protein/low-micronutrient density ratios typical in post-surgical vomiting scenarios.

**4. Implement a "Human-in-the-Loop" Triage Protocol**
Acknowledge that without external data, the tool cannot be 100% autonomous at launch. Design a mitigation workflow where any patient flagged as "low risk" by the tool but exhibiting specific behavioral markers (e.g., rapid weight loss, reported nausea, or strict liquid diet adherence) is automatically routed for manual review by a dietitian. This creates a safety net for the specific blind spots identified in your simulation.

**5. Define Go/No-Go Metrics**
Establish a strict threshold for launch. For example: "The tool must detect at least 90% of known B12 and Iron deficiencies in the Golden Set." If the retrospective simulation fails to meet this threshold after rule adjustments, delay the automated decisioning component and launch the tool in "Advisory Mode Only," where all outputs require human verification before reaching the patient.

**Deliverable Timeline:**
*   **Week 1:** Curate Golden Set and run blind simulation.
*   **Week 2:** Analyze gaps and implement hard-coded heuristic patches.
*   **Week 3:** Re-test against Golden Set; finalize Human-in-the-Loop protocol.
*   **Week 4:** Dry run with live clinic staff using advisory mode.
*   **Week 5-6:** Final adjustments and partnership alignment.

**Note:** This strategy relies entirely on the quality and completeness of your historical data; if your records lack specific deficiency cases, those blind spots will remain untested. Prioritize manual review for any micronutrient where your historical data sample size is below 10 confirmed cases.
