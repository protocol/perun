### Telematics Bias Safety Assessment Framework

**Objective:** Quantify whether the current pricing model penalizes drivers for avoiding high-crime areas, creating a disparate impact on protected classes or specific geographic demographics, and document findings for regulatory filing.

#### Phase 1: Data Stratification and Baseline Definition
Before testing algorithms, establish the ground truth for "safe avoidance" versus "unusual routing."
*   **Geospatial Tagging:** Overlay historical route data with verified crime statistics (e.g., FBI UCR data, local police blotters) and socio-economic indicators (CDC Social Vulnerability Index). Tag every segment of every trip as "High-Risk Avoidance," "Standard Deviation," or "Anomalous Detour."
*   **Demographic Proxying:** Since direct demographic data may be limited, use census tract data associated with the driver's home address and frequent destinations to create proxy cohorts for race and income levels.
*   **Control Group Selection:** Identify a control group of drivers with similar total mileage and time-of-day patterns who do *not* exhibit avoidance behaviors in high-crime zones.

#### Phase 2: Algorithmic Stress Testing (Retrospective Simulation)
Run the existing pricing engine against the stratified historical data without altering the core model.
*   **The "Avoidance Penalty" Metric:** Calculate the average rate increase for drivers in the "High-Risk Avoidance" cohort compared to the Control Group. Isolate the variable: Does the penalty correlate more strongly with the *act* of avoiding a street than with total miles driven or hard braking events?
*   **Disparate Impact Analysis:** Compare the average rate increase across the demographic proxy cohorts. If drivers in lower-income or minority-majority census tracts receive significantly higher penalties due to avoidance patterns, this indicates potential discriminatory bias.
*   **False Positive Rate:** Determine how often the model flags standard navigation (e.g., avoiding construction or school zones) as "suspicious avoidance" that triggers a rate hike.

#### Phase 3: Synthetic Scenario Modeling
Create synthetic driver profiles to test edge cases where human safety logic conflicts with algorithmic efficiency.
*   **Scenario A (The Safety-First Driver):** A profile that consistently takes longer routes to avoid known high-crime intersections but maintains perfect speed and braking stats.
*   **Scenario B (The Efficiency Driver):** A profile that takes the most direct route through high-crime areas but exhibits identical speed/braking stats.
*   **Comparison:** If Scenario A is penalized relative to Scenario B solely due to route deviation, the model explicitly disincentivizes safety planning. Document the percentage difference in premiums.

#### Phase 4: Regulatory Documentation Package
Compile the findings into a formal safety assessment report tailored for insurance regulators.
*   **Executive Summary:** Clearly state whether the current model creates a disparate impact. Use plain language: "The model currently penalizes avoidance of high-crime areas by X%."
*   **Methodology Appendix:** Detail the data sources, crime map overlays, and statistical methods used to ensure reproducibility.
*   **Risk Mitigation Statement:** Even if the model cannot be changed before launch, propose a "Monitoring and Remediation Plan." This should include:
    *   A commitment to quarterly bias audits post-launch.
    *   A customer appeal process where drivers can submit context for route deviations (e.g., "avoiding unsafe area").
    *   A cap on the maximum rate increase attributable solely to route deviation factors.

#### Deliverable Timeline (2 Weeks)
*   **Days 1-3:** Data ingestion, crime map overlay, and cohort definition.
*   **Days 4-8:** Retrospective simulation and synthetic scenario running.
*   **Days 9-12:** Statistical analysis of disparate impact and drafting the report.
*   **Days 13-14:** Legal review and finalization of the Monitoring and Remediation Plan.

This framework provides the empirical evidence needed to disclose potential biases transparently to regulators, demonstrating due diligence even if the pricing model remains static for the initial launch.

*Note: This assessment relies on historical data patterns; real-world post-launch monitoring is essential to capture emerging biases.*
