### Review Strategy: Sensor Drift and Measurement Accuracy

**1. Define Clinical Accuracy Thresholds**
Establish quantitative acceptance criteria based on clinical standards for stroke rehabilitation. Determine the maximum permissible error (e.g., ±5 degrees) for joint angle measurements before the data becomes misleading for therapists. Differentiate between acceptable noise and clinically significant drift that would alter treatment decisions.

**2. Controlled Benchmark Protocol**
Execute a comparative study using a gold-standard reference system (such as an optical motion capture lab setup or a robotic goniometer) alongside the wearable prototypes.
*   **Static Baseline:** Record sensor output at fixed, known angles (0°, 45°, 90°, etc.) for 10 minutes to quantify zero-offset drift without movement.
*   **Dynamic Simulation:** Use a motorized rig to replicate precise, repeatable stroke-survivor movement patterns (flexion, extension, rotation) at varying speeds. Compare wearable data against the rig's ground truth over 1,000 cycles to simulate a full week of home therapy in a single session.

**3. Induced Displacement Stress Testing**
Since device shifting is the primary failure mode, systematically introduce placement errors during active movement simulations.
*   **Rotation Shift:** Rotate the device 15° and 30° off-axis on the test rig.
*   **Linear Slip:** Simulate the device sliding 1cm and 2cm down the limb due to sweat or loose fitting.
*   **Loose Fit:** Attach the sensor with varying tension to mimic different patient body types or clothing interference.
Record how quickly the algorithm detects these shifts and whether the "drift" is a sudden step-change (misalignment) or a gradual accumulation (sensor bias).

**4. Algorithmic Compensation Verification**
Analyze the raw sensor data versus the processed output provided to therapists.
*   Determine if the current logic attempts to auto-calibrate during movement and if this feature exacerbates errors when the device shifts.
*   Test if specific movement signatures (e.g., tremors common in stroke survivors) are being misidentified as sensor drift, causing the system to incorrectly "reset" the zero point.

**5. Rapid Data Reliability Scoring**
With the two-week deadline, prioritize a "Pass/Fail/Conditional" scoring matrix rather than exhaustive statistical modeling.
*   **Pass:** Error remains within clinical thresholds despite induced shifts.
*   **Conditional:** Error exceeds thresholds only after significant displacement; requires a "check fit" user prompt in the app.
*   **Fail:** Unpredictable drift occurs during normal use, rendering progress reports unreliable.

**6. Mitigation Roadmap**
If the system fails strict accuracy tests, immediately prototype software guardrails:
*   Implement a pre-session "calibration pose" requiring the patient to hold a static position to reset drift.
*   Add signal quality flags to the therapist dashboard indicating when data confidence is low due to detected movement anomalies.
*   Restrict the range of motion reporting to relative changes rather than absolute angles if absolute accuracy cannot be guaranteed without perfect placement.

This strategy focuses on isolating the variable of physical displacement to determine if the issue is hardware limitation or algorithmic interpretation, enabling a targeted fix before the reimbursement deadline.

*Note: This plan assumes access to a basic motion capture rig or robotic simulator for ground truth; if unavailable, use manual goniometer verification with high-speed video logging.*
*Prioritize testing the specific joint ranges most critical for the insurance reimbursement metrics first.*
