# Pre-Launch Safety Review Plan: Patient-Rehab-Endurance-Test Module

**Objective:** Validate that fatigue detection terminates tests only when physiological risk exists, preventing premature cessation that hinders recovery, within a four-week timeline using existing staff.

### Phase 1: Retrospective Data Simulation (Week 1)
*Action:* Use existing historical session logs from your database. Do not recruit new patients yet.
*Method:*
1.  **Replay Analysis:** Run the current algorithm against 50–100 completed historical sessions where patients finished safely without adverse events.
2.  **False Positive Identification:** Flag any session where the algorithm *would have* terminated the test earlier than the actual human-supervised stop time.
3.  **Clinical Review:** Your five team members (including any with clinical backgrounds) review these "false positive" cases. Determine if the early stop was actually a safety catch the human missed, or an overly sensitive error.
*Deliverable:* A list of specific movement patterns or vital sign thresholds triggering premature stops.

### Phase 2: Threshold Calibration & "Safe Failure" Definition (Week 2)
*Action:* Adjust algorithm sensitivity based on Phase 1 findings.
*Criteria for Adjustment:*
*   **Specificity Over Sensitivity:** Shift the threshold to reduce false positives, provided no true safety risks are ignored.
*   **The "Two-Strike" Rule:** Require two consecutive data points (e.g., 5 seconds apart) to exceed fatigue thresholds before termination, filtering out sensor noise or momentary spasms common in stroke recovery.
*   **Contextual Weighting:** If the system uses multiple inputs (heart rate, movement smoothness, self-report), weight subjective patient feedback lower than objective biometrics during the initial calibration to prevent patients from "pushing through" dangerous levels unknowingly.
*Deliverable:* A calibrated configuration file and a defined "Safe Failure" matrix documenting exactly which parameter combinations justify immediate termination.

### Phase 3: Internal "Wizard-of-Oz" Validation (Week 3)
*Action:* Simulate live usage without patient risk.
*Method:*
1.  **Team-as-Patient:** Team members perform the exercises while wearing the sensors. One member acts as the "safety officer" ready to physically intervene if the algorithm fails to stop a genuinely unsafe simulation (e.g., deliberate erratic movement).
2.  **Edge Case Injection:** Deliberately introduce noise or simulate fatigue patterns identified in Phase 1 to ensure the adjusted algorithm behaves predictably.
3.  **Insurance Readiness:** Document every test case, the algorithm's decision, and the safety officer's verdict. This log serves as evidence for the national health insurance review.
*Deliverable:* A validation report showing 100% concordance between the adjusted algorithm and the safety officer on critical stop decisions.

### Phase 4: Controlled Pilot & Final Documentation (Week 4)
*Action:* Limited deployment with strict guardrails.
*Method:*
1.  **Select 3–5 Low-Risk Patients:** Choose patients with high cognitive awareness and low fall risk for a 3-day trial.
2.  **Remote Monitoring:** Require a family member to be present, with your team monitoring data streams in real-time during these specific sessions.
3.  **Final Policy Lock:** Based on pilot data, finalize the termination criteria.
*Deliverable:* Final Safety Review Document for insurance submission, including the calibration logic, false-positive reduction metrics, and pilot success rates.

### Criteria for Adjusting Fatigue Detection Sensitivity
To balance safety and efficacy, adjust sensitivity only when:
1.  **False Positive Rate > 5%:** If more than 1 in 20 safe historical sessions are flagged for early termination, reduce sensitivity.
2.  **Recovery Plateau Evidence:** If data shows patients consistently stop due to algorithm flags before reaching target repetition counts *and* no adverse vitals were recorded, the threshold is too conservative.
3.  **Sensor Noise Correlation:** If terminations correlate with known sensor artifacts (e.g., specific rapid movements that aren't fatigue), implement signal filtering rather than changing the fatigue threshold itself.

**Note:** Do not alter core safety thresholds without a documented "rollback" plan if the pilot shows increased physiological stress markers. Ensure all simulated data used in Phase 1 is anonymized to comply with privacy regulations before the insurance review.
