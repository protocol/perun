### Structured Safety Review Process for Patient-Physio-Assistant

Given the constraints of a tight budget and no additional medical consultants, your team of four must adopt a rigorous, internal "defense-in-depth" review strategy. This process prioritizes conservative safety margins over aggressive progression, ensuring that no recommendation can aggravate a recent surgical site.

**Phase 1: The "Red-Flag" Rule Engine (Logic Hardening)**
Before reviewing specific exercises, hard-code absolute contraindications into your logic layer. Create a mandatory pre-check algorithm that blocks *any* exercise recommendation if specific high-risk criteria are met, regardless of the user's reported pain level.
*   **Time-Based Lockouts:** Define strict "no-go" windows post-surgery (e.g., Days 0–14: No flexion/extension; Days 15–30: No loaded movement). If the surgery date falls within these windows, the system defaults to a static "Rest & Breath" protocol only.
*   **Pain Threshold Triggers:** If a user reports pain above a conservative threshold (e.g., >4/10) or describes "sharp/shooting" pain, the system must immediately halt progression and recommend ceasing activity, rather than suggesting a modified exercise.
*   **Surgical Specificity:** Map specific surgery types (e.g., Fusion vs. Discectomy) to forbidden movement vectors. A fusion patient must never receive rotational torque recommendations in the early phase.

**Phase 2: The "Four-Eyes" Content Audit Matrix**
Divide your content library into a grid for manual review. Do not review exercises in isolation; review them by patient state.
*   **Role Assignment:**
    *   **Member A (The Skeptic):** Attempts to "break" the logic by inputting worst-case scenarios (e.g., high pain, day 3 post-op) to see if dangerous exercises are suggested.
    *   **Member B (The Clinician Proxy):** Reviews the exercise descriptions against standard physiotherapy textbooks or open-access clinical guidelines (e.g., NASS, AAOS) available online. Focus strictly on biomechanical safety.
    *   **Member C (The Logic Verifier):** Checks that the "Red-Flag" rules from Phase 1 correctly intercept the dangerous scenarios identified by Member A.
    *   **Member D (The Final Gatekeeper):** Performs a blind run-through of the app as a naive user, ensuring no path exists to a risky recommendation without triggering a safety warning.
*   **The "Conservative Default" Policy:** If any team member flags an exercise as "potentially risky" for a specific subgroup and you cannot verify its safety with 100% certainty using open literature, **remove it** from the library for the initial release. It is safer to under-prescribe than to over-prescribe.

**Phase 3: The "Safe-Start" Launch Protocol**
Limit the scope of your initial release to minimize risk exposure.
*   **Restricted Library:** Launch with only the bottom 20% of exercise intensity (isometric holds, diaphragmatic breathing, neutral spine walking). Exclude all dynamic loading or range-of-motion challenges for the first 30 days.
*   **Mandatory Disclaimer & Feedback Loop:** Require users to acknowledge that the app does not replace professional medical advice. Implement a prominent "Report Pain Increase" button that immediately locks the user out of further recommendations and advises them to contact their surgeon.
*   **Data Monitoring:** Dedicate one team member to monitor user feedback daily. Any report of increased pain following a specific recommendation triggers an immediate suspension of that specific exercise module pending re-evaluation.

By strictly enforcing time-based lockouts, adopting a "remove if unsure" policy, and launching with a hyper-conservative subset of exercises, you can create a safe environment for your patients without needing external consultants.

*Note: This process relies on strict logical constraints and conservative defaults to mitigate risk in the absence of clinical oversight.*
*Ensure all team members agree that "safety" in this context means "absence of harm" rather than "optimal rehabilitation speed."*
