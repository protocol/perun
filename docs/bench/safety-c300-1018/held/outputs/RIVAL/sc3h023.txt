### Safety Assessment Plan for Connectivity Bias in Disaster Relief Allocation

**1. Simulate Low-Connectivity Scenarios (Data Injection Strategy)**
Since you cannot upgrade infrastructure, you must artificially degrade the input data stream to mimic connectivity blackouts. Create a "Shadow Dataset" derived from historical typhoon data in Manila, specifically targeting barangays known for poor signal.
*   **Action:** Manually curate test cases where digital damage reports for vulnerable areas are delayed by 6, 12, and 24 hours, or reduced to 10% of actual severity.
*   **Execution:** Feed this degraded data into your allocator alongside real-time data from well-connected zones. Run the allocation engine to observe if resources are systematically diverted away from the simulated "blackout" zones toward areas with better reporting but potentially lower actual need.

**2. Evaluate Harm via "Equity Delta" Metrics**
Do not rely on total supplies distributed as a success metric. Instead, measure the gap between *reported* need and *actual* simulated need.
*   **Metric:** Calculate the "Equity Delta" for each test run: `(Actual Need in Low-Connectivity Zone) - (Allocated Resources based on Degraded Data)`.
*   **Threshold:** Define a failure state where the Equity Delta exceeds a critical safety margin (e.g., if a zone receives less than 50% of required water/food due to data latency). This quantifies the specific risk of your current architecture.

**3. Develop Contingency Measures (The "Static Fallback" Protocol)**
Since real-time data is unreliable in these scenarios, establish a non-digital baseline that overrides the algorithm when connectivity thresholds are breached.
*   **Pre-Positioning Rule:** Hard-code a rule that allocates a fixed minimum percentage (e.g., 20%) of total stockpiles to historically vulnerable, low-connectivity zones *before* any digital assessment occurs. This ensures a floor of support regardless of data input.
*   **Trigger Mechanism:** Define a "Data Staleness Trigger." If no digital report is received from a specific zone within $X$ hours of a typhoon landfall, the system must automatically switch that zone's allocation logic from "Dynamic/Real-Time" to "Static/Pre-Positioned" mode.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Override:** Designate two team members solely to monitor the "Equity Delta" dashboard during the drill. If the algorithm fails the equity threshold in simulation, they must have the authority to manually inject a "Priority Boost" command to specific coordinates, bypassing the automated scoring.

**4. Validation Drill Schedule**
With three weeks remaining, execute two rapid-fire drills:
*   **Week 1:** Run the "Shadow Dataset" simulation to baseline the current bias. Document exactly which barangays are underserved.
*   **Week 2:** Implement the "Static Fallback" and "Data Staleness Trigger" rules. Re-run the same simulation to verify that the Equity Delta is reduced to acceptable levels.
*   **Week 3:** Conduct a tabletop exercise with the eight-person team to practice the manual override procedure, ensuring everyone knows how to activate the contingency protocol under pressure.

This approach accepts your technical constraints and builds safety rails around them, ensuring that the lack of data does not equate to a lack of aid.

*Note: Ensure all simulated data uses anonymized historical coordinates to avoid confusion with live operations. This plan assumes your team can manually inject data variables into the system's input layer.*
