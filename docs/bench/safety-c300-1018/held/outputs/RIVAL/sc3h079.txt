### Testing Scenario List: Voice Assistant Notification Timing

**Objective:** Identify edge cases where notification narration diverts driver attention during critical driving moments, given the constraint that complex pre-speech context checks are unavailable.

#### 1. High-Cognitive Load Maneuvers
*   **The "Merge" Interrupt:** Trigger a long-form notification (e.g., a multi-sentence email summary) exactly when the vehicle enters a highway on-ramp or approaches a complex merge point.
    *   *Risk:* Narration overlaps with the driver's need to scan blind spots and judge gaps, causing delayed reaction to traffic speed differentials.
*   **The Intersection Decision:** Initiate narration of an emotionally charged message (e.g., "Urgent: Family Emergency") precisely as the vehicle approaches a yellow light or an uncontrolled four-way stop.
    *   *Risk:* Emotional spike combined with auditory load distracts from the immediate binary decision (stop/go), increasing hesitation or erratic braking.

#### 2. Emotional Valence & Startle Response
*   **The "Bad News" Burst:** Simulate a notification with high-negative sentiment keywords (e.g., "Accident reported," "Account frozen") during steady-state highway cruising.
    *   *Risk:* Even without visual distraction, the emotional shock causes a "startle reflex," leading to involuntary steering corrections or speed fluctuations.
*   **The Conflict Escalation:** Deliver a sequence of rapid, short notifications implying an argument (e.g., three texts in 5 seconds from the same contact) while navigating a construction zone with shifting lanes.
    *   *Risk:* Cumulative auditory stress reduces situational awareness of changing road geometry.

#### 3. Timing & Duration Edge Cases
*   **The "Just-in-Time" Cut-off:** Begin reading a notification that exceeds 15 seconds just as the vehicle enters a roundabout or a dense pedestrian crosswalk.
    *   *Risk:* The driver cannot mute the assistant quickly enough; the narration continues through the critical maneuver, masking external auditory cues (sirens, horns).
*   **The Buffer Overlap:** Trigger a new notification while the assistant is already finishing a previous one during a sudden hard-braking event.
    *   *Risk:* The assistant queues the second message immediately, extending the distraction window precisely when the driver needs maximum focus to stabilize the vehicle.

#### 4. Environmental Noise Interference
*   **The Volume Spike:** Test narration initiation when external noise levels suddenly increase (e.g., passing a motorcycle, heavy rain, windows down in a tunnel).
    *   *Risk:* If the system lacks dynamic volume adjustment (due to latency constraints), the driver may strain to hear the message, diverting cognitive resources from visual scanning.
*   **The False Positive Trigger:** Simulate road noise patterns that mimic voice activation words, causing the system to interrupt a critical driving moment to "listen" or announce a false alert.

#### 5. Content Type Variations
*   **The Ambiguous Alert:** Read a vague notification ("You have a new update") that requires mental effort to interpret relevance while navigating a curve.
    *   *Risk:* The driver wastes cognitive cycles guessing the importance of the message rather than focusing on the road curve.
*   **The Numeric Overload:** Narrate a message containing complex strings of numbers (e.g., verification codes, flight numbers) during stop-and-go traffic.
    *   *Risk:* Drivers often attempt to memorize numbers audibly, creating a high working-memory load that degrades reaction times to sudden stops.

**Note:** These scenarios assume the system reads all incoming data without filtering; validation should measure steering deviation and brake reaction time during narration.
**Note:** Prioritize testing on emotionally neutral content first to establish a baseline before introducing high-stress variables.
