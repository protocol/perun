### Mitigation Plan and Communication Strategy

**1. Immediate Mitigation: The "Known Gap" Protocol**
Since code changes are impossible before launch, we must treat the missing sodium data from medications and water as a known system limitation rather than a feature bug.
*   **Interface Warning:** Implement a persistent, non-dismissible banner on the dashboard and logging screens: *"Important: This tracker estimates sodium from food logs only. It does not include sodium from medications or drinking water. Please consult your physician for a complete daily total."*
*   **Input Field Addition:** If the UI allows, add a manual "Other Sources" text field where users can note medication names, even if the system cannot calculate the milligrams. This keeps the user mindful of the gap without requiring backend logic changes.
*   **Alert Calibration:** Adjust the existing alert thresholds to trigger at 80% of the recommended limit based on food logs alone. This creates a safety buffer to account for the untracked external sources.

**2. Communication Strategy**
*   **Internal Alignment:** Brief all customer support staff immediately. Provide them with a script acknowledging the limitation and directing users to consult their care providers for medication-specific sodium content.
*   **User Transparency:** Include a dedicated section in the app's "About" or "Help" menu titled "How We Calculate Sodium," explicitly listing excluded sources. Do not hide this information in the Terms of Service; it must be visible during onboarding.
*   **Medical Partner Notification:** Proactively inform partnering clinics and physicians about this limitation so they can advise their patients to manually cross-reference their prescriptions with the app's data.

**3. Content Team Review Protocol: High-Risk Medication Identification**
To identify which medications pose the highest risk for your user warnings, the content team should execute the following rapid review protocol:

*   **Step A: Source Aggregation (Day 1)**
    Compile a list of the top 50 most prescribed hypertension and comorbidity medications in Egypt (e.g., diuretics, effervescent tablets, antacids). Focus on local formulary data from Nile Health's existing patient records.
*   **Step B: Sodium Flagging (Day 2)**
    Cross-reference this list against pharmaceutical databases (e.g., FDA labels, Egyptian Drug Authority) specifically looking for "sodium" in inactive ingredients or chemical composition (e.g., sodium bicarbonate, sodium phosphate). Flag any medication containing >20mg of sodium per dose as "High Risk."
*   **Step C: Warning Drafting (Day 3)**
    For each "High Risk" item, draft a specific static warning message.
    *   *Example:* "Warning: Effervescent pain relievers often contain high sodium. If you take these, your total intake may exceed safe limits even if your food log looks low."
*   **Step D: Validation (Day 4)**
    Have a senior pharmacist or medical director review the flagged list and warning copy for accuracy. Publish this list as a downloadable PDF or a static "Medication Safety" page within the app.

**4. Suggested User Warnings for Immediate Implementation**
Insert these copy blocks into the app's existing notification or tooltip system:
*   *On Logging Screen:* "Did you take medication today? Some prescriptions contain hidden sodium not tracked here."
*   *On Alert Trigger:* "You are nearing your food-based sodium limit. Remember to account for your medications and water intake separately."
*   *On Profile Setup:* "Please tell your doctor you are using this app, as it does not track non-food sodium sources."

This approach manages liability through transparency, empowers users with knowledge, and leverages your medical team's expertise without delaying the launch.

*Note: Ensure legal counsel reviews all disclaimer text prior to release. Schedule a patch update for post-launch to integrate medication data as soon as feasible.*
