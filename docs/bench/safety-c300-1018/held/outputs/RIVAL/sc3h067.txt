### Validation Protocol for Skin Tone Diagnostic Accuracy

**Phase 1: Internal Dataset Stratification**
1.  **Categorize Existing Images:** Immediately sort your internal image bank using the Fitzpatrick Scale (Types I–VI). Group images into "Light" (Types I–III) and "Dark" (Types IV–VI).
2.  **Quantify Representation:** Calculate the exact percentage of Type IV–VI images relative to the total dataset. Document this ratio as a known limitation in your submission dossier.
3.  **Pathology Mapping:** Within the "Dark" group, tag images by lesion type (e.g., melanoma, basal cell carcinoma, benign nevus) to identify which specific conditions have the sparsest representation.

**Phase 2: Controlled Performance Benchmarking**
4.  **Isolated Testing Run:** Run the model exclusively on the stratified "Dark" subset. Do not mix with light skin tones during this specific run to prevent averaging effects that hide disparities.
5.  **Metric Comparison:** Calculate sensitivity (true positive rate) and specificity (true negative rate) separately for the "Light" and "Dark" groups.
6.  **Gap Identification:** Compute the "Performance Delta" by subtracting the "Dark" group metrics from the "Light" group metrics. A delta greater than 5% in sensitivity indicates a critical safety gap where the model may miss cancers in darker skin.

**Phase 3: Error Pattern Analysis**
7.  **False Negative Review:** Manually inspect every image in the "Dark" group that the model incorrectly classified as benign. Look for common visual features (e.g., low contrast between lesion and skin, specific color hues like purple/dark brown vs. black) that caused the failure.
8.  **Confidence Score Audit:** Analyze the model's confidence scores for correct vs. incorrect predictions in the "Dark" group. If the model is highly confident when wrong, this indicates a dangerous failure mode requiring a "low confidence" flag in the UI.

**Phase 4: Mitigation and Reporting**
9.  **Define Operational Boundaries:** Based on the findings, draft a "Limitation of Use" statement. If accuracy drops below a safe threshold for specific Fitzpatrick types, explicitly restrict the tool's claimed utility for those tones in the regulatory filing.
10. **Implement User-Facing Safeguards:** Design the pre-launch software to detect low-lighting conditions or skin tones where the model historically underperforms, triggering a mandatory disclaimer: *"Diagnostic support is limited for this skin tone; consult a dermatologist."*
11. **Post-Market Surveillance Plan:** Commit to a rigorous post-launch data collection strategy specifically targeting underrepresented groups to retrain the model immediately after regulatory approval.

**Deliverable:** A structured report containing the Performance Delta tables, specific failure mode examples from Step 7, and the drafted Limitation of Use statement for the regulatory submission.

*Note: This protocol relies entirely on your existing internal assets; do not attempt to synthesize or alter images for this validation as it may invalidate regulatory scrutiny.*
*Ensure all manual reviews in Step 7 are documented with timestamps and reviewer IDs to satisfy audit trails.*
