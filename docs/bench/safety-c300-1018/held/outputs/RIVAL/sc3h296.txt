# Pre-Launch Safety Review Plan: Defibrillator Charge-Time Optimizer

## 1. Objective
To identify root causes of prolonged charge times in low-battery states and establish an immediate operational contingency protocol to ensure patient safety during the initial five-week deployment window prior to the software patch.

## 2. Scenario Identification & Stress Testing (Days 1–7)
Since live extension is impossible, we will utilize historical field data and simulated battery degradation profiles to replicate failure modes.

*   **Low-Battery Edge Case Simulation:** Construct test matrices combining battery health levels (10%, 15%, 20%) with extreme ambient temperatures (0°C and 35°C) to isolate thermal throttling interactions with the optimizer algorithm.
*   **Sequence Interruption Analysis:** Simulate rapid power-cycle events and partial-charge interruptions to determine if the optimizer fails to reset its prediction model, causing it to "hunt" for an optimal sequence that does not exist in the current power state.
*   **Sensor Noise Injection:** Introduce signal noise into voltage and current sensors during the prediction phase to verify if the optimizer misinterprets transient drops as a requirement for a slower, safer charge curve.
*   **Worst-Case Latency Mapping:** Define the maximum observed delay ($T_{max}$) under these specific failure conditions to set the upper bound for the contingency protocol.

## 3. Contingency Protocol: "Safe-Fallback Mode" (Effective Launch Day)
Until the patch is deployed, emergency response units must operate under a modified Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) triggered by specific device indicators.

*   **Trigger Condition:** If the device display indicates a charge time exceeding 12 seconds (or the calculated $T_{max}$ + 2s buffer) while the battery indicator shows <20%, the operator must immediately engage Safe-Fallback Mode.
*   **Operator Action:**
    1.  **Abort Optimization:** Manually override the smart optimizer by pressing and holding the "Direct Charge" hard-key (bypassing the predictive algorithm).
    2.  **Power Source Swap:** If the delay persists >5 seconds after override, immediately swap to the secondary hot-swap battery pack, which bypasses the low-voltage optimization logic entirely.
    3.  **CPR Continuity:** During any charge delay, team members must maintain high-quality CPR without interruption; the team lead must verbally announce "Charging Delay – Continue Compressions" to prevent premature pauses.
*   **Device Configuration:** A temporary configuration flag will be pushed via MDM (Mobile Device Management) to all units to disable the "Predictive Sequence" feature when battery voltage is below the critical threshold, forcing a linear charge curve that is slower but deterministic.

## 4. Monitoring & Feedback Loop
*   **Telemetry Tagging:** All devices will tag events where charge time >10s as "Critical Review Required." These logs must be uploaded within 24 hours of the shift.
*   **Daily Triage:** A dedicated two-person engineering team will review these tags daily to confirm if the contingency protocol was effective and to detect any new failure patterns.
*   **Patch Verification:** The software patch targeting the low-battery logic gate will be validated against the specific failure scenarios identified in Section 2 before the five-week deadline expires.

## 5. Communication Directive
Issue an immediate "Field Notice" to all London emergency response units detailing the visual indicators of the low-battery delay and the mandatory steps for the Safe-Fallback Mode. Training videos demonstrating the manual override must be distributed to all shift supervisors within 48 hours.

***

*Note: This plan assumes the existence of a manual override mechanism and hot-swap capability; if hardware limitations prevent this, the protocol must shift to strict battery replacement mandates before shift start.*
*All testing described relies on simulation and historical data analysis as no live network or extended physical testing phase is available.*
