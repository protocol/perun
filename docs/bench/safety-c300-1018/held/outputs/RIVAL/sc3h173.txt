### Communication Strategy for Patients

The goal is to maintain trust without inducing "alert fatigue," where patients ignore all warnings.

**1. Reframe the Language:**
Avoid definitive terms like "Dangerous Interaction" for alerts derived from older data tiers. Instead, use probabilistic and advisory language:
*   *Current:* "Do not take Drug A with Drug B."
*   *Proposed:* "Potential Interaction Notice: Older data suggests a possible interaction between Drug A and Drug B. While recent updates may have clarified this, please consult your pharmacist before proceeding."

**2. The "Pharmacist-in-the-Loop" Badge:**
Visually distinguish alerts that require manual verification. Add a clear icon or label stating: *"Requires Pharmacist Verification – Data Under Review."* This shifts the burden of decision to the professional rather than the patient, ensuring the patient does not self-dismiss the warning but understands it is being actively checked.

**3. Educational Footer:**
Include a brief, non-alarming note on the printed or digital slip: *"Our system prioritizes safety by flagging potential risks. Some alerts are being updated with the latest local data. Your pharmacist will confirm if this applies to you."*
