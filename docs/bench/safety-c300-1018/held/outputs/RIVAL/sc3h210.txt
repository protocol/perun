### Safety Review Framework for Extrapolating Fall Risks

**1. Pharmacological Bridging Protocol**
Establish a "Mechanism-of-Action (MoA) Mapping" layer to bridge the two-year data gap. Instead of treating drugs as isolated entities, cluster both historical and new sedating therapies by their primary receptor affinity (e.g., H1-histamine, GABA-A, mu-opioid) and pharmacokinetic profiles (half-life, peak plasma time).
*   **Action:** Assign a "Sedation Equivalency Score" to new drugs based on their MoA similarity to historical drugs with known fall outcomes.
*   **Safety Margin:** Apply a conservative inflation factor (e.g., +15% risk probability) to any new drug lacking a direct historical analogue to account for unknown formulation variables, ensuring risks are never underestimated.

**2. Synthetic Cohort Generation**
Since live data is prohibited, generate synthetic patient profiles that reflect current demographic shifts (e.g., increased average age or comorbidity loads) rather than relying solely on the static two-year-old distribution.
*   **Action:** Use the historical de-identified records as a baseline distribution, then perturb variables such as "number of concurrent medications" and "renal function metrics" to match current hospital admission trends reported in public health literature.
*   **Validation:** Run the assessor against these synthetic "high-complexity" profiles to verify that the model triggers early warnings under heavier polypharmacy loads than seen in the historical dataset.

**3. Formulation Sensitivity Analysis**
Explicitly model the uncertainty introduced by changes in medication formulations (e.g., extended-release vs. immediate-release versions introduced in the last 24 months).
*   **Action:** Create "Stress Test" scenarios where the input parameters for drug concentration curves are widened to simulate worst-case absorption rates associated with new formulations.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the model's predicted fall probability fluctuates wildly under these widened parameters, flag the specific drug class for mandatory human review rather than automated clearance.

**4. The "Conservative Bias" Grader**
Implement a post-processing logic gate that prioritizes sensitivity over specificity for any prediction involving a new drug or a significant time-gap variable.
*   **Action:** Hard-code a rule where any predicted fall risk between 10–20% for a new sedating therapy is automatically escalated to the "High Risk" category for protocol initiation.
*   **Documentation:** Log every instance where this override occurs to build a prospective audit trail for when live validation eventually becomes permissible.

**5. Review Board Simulation**
Conduct a "Pre-Mortem" exercise with the five-person team.
*   **Action:** Assume a fall incident has occurred six months post-deployment involving a new drug. Work backward to identify which assumption in the extrapolation framework (MoA mapping, synthetic demographics, or formulation variance) failed.
*   **Output:** A signed "Residual Risk Statement" detailing exactly which populations or drug classes remain outside the confidence interval of the historical data, to be included in the user manual for hospital staff.

***

*Note: This framework relies entirely on theoretical mapping and synthetic stress-testing; it does not replace clinical validation.*
