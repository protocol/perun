# Risk Assessment: Lighting-Dependent Failure Modes in Autonomous Foundation Inspection

## 1. Identified Failure Modes
Based on the constraint of midday-only testing and the reliance on computer vision (CV), the following failure modes are projected for low-light or variable lighting conditions:

*   **False Negative Detection (Critical):** The CV model fails to identify hairline or structural cracks due to insufficient contrast or shadow obscuration in evening/low-light conditions. This poses a direct safety liability where damaged foundations are certified as safe.
*   **False Positive Detection (Operational):** Artificial lighting (flashlights, streetlamps) or uneven shadows create high-contrast artifacts that the model misinterprets as cracks. This leads to unnecessary follow-up inspections, eroding customer trust and increasing operational costs.
*   **Navigation and Depth Perception Errors:** Autonomous drones often rely on visual odometry or stereo vision for stabilization and proximity detection. Low light may degrade depth estimation, causing collision risks with foundation walls or unstable hovering near critical inspection zones.
*   **Sensor Saturation/Noise:** In mixed lighting (e.g., a dark foundation corner illuminated by a bright drone LED), sensor dynamic range limitations may cause blooming or noise, rendering the captured imagery unusable for analysis.

## 2. Strategy for Diverse Environmental Data Gathering
To mitigate these risks before commercial authorization, the following data acquisition strategy is proposed to supplement the existing midday dataset:

### Phase A: Controlled Simulation (Immediate)
*   **Action:** Utilize the existing limited test houses during evening hours using portable, calibrated lighting rigs to simulate various low-light scenarios (dusk, deep shadow, artificial-only).
*   **Goal:** Generate an initial "low-light" dataset without requiring new property access. This allows for immediate model stress-testing against known ground truth (the cracks identified during midday).

### Phase B: Strategic Partnerships (Short-term)
*   **Action:** Partner with 2–3 local home inspection firms or real estate staging companies. Offer free automated preliminary scans in exchange for access to properties during varied times of day, including evenings and overcast conditions.
*   **Goal:** Acquire real-world data from diverse architectural styles and lighting environments without the cost of leasing new test sites.

### Phase C: Synthetic Data Augmentation (Parallel)
*   **Action:** Apply digital image processing to the existing midday dataset to simulate low-light conditions, adding noise, reducing exposure, and casting synthetic shadows.
*   **Goal:** Expand the training set to teach the model robustness against lighting variations while physical data collection is underway. *Note: This must be validated against real low-light data from Phase A/B to ensure realism.*

### Phase D: Sensor Fusion Validation
*   **Action:** If hardware permits, integrate or test auxiliary sensors (e.g., LiDAR or thermal imaging) that are less dependent on visible light.
*   **Goal:** Determine if a multi-modal approach can verify structural integrity when visual confidence scores drop below a defined threshold.

## 3. Recommendation for Commercial Authorization
Do not authorize full commercial use for evening or low-light operations until the False Negative rate in these conditions matches the midday baseline. Implement a "geofenced" or "time-restricted" deployment policy initially, limiting autonomous operations to daylight hours with specific luminance thresholds until the diverse data strategy validates broader operational envelopes.

*Prioritize safety-critical false negatives over operational false positives in your evaluation metrics.*
*Ensure all new data collection includes precise metadata regarding light intensity (lux) and angle.*
