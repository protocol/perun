### Internal Review Framework: Housing Allocation Equity

**Part 1: Evaluation Criteria for Compatibility Metrics**
To assess whether your scoring model reinforces bias or isolation, evaluate the algorithm against these four criteria before full deployment:

1.  **Feature Correlation Analysis**: Audit the weight of input variables against protected demographics (race, gender, socioeconomic status, disability). If variables highly correlated with these demographics (e.g., "past behavioral data" linked to disciplinary records which may reflect biased enforcement) contribute significantly to the compatibility score, flag them for removal or re-weighting.
2.  **Synthetic Segregation Testing**: Run the allocation logic on synthetic datasets where demographic groups are identical in all aspects except one variable. If the model consistently clusters specific demographics together or isolates them when other factors are held constant, the metric is likely encoding bias.
3.  **Homophily vs. Diversity Balance**: Determine if the "social compatibility" definition defaults to homophily (liking those who are similar). A model that maximizes compatibility by grouping students with identical backgrounds will inherently create segregation. The criteria must define a threshold where compatibility does not override diverse integration goals.
4.  **Historical Data Bias Check**: Since the model uses "past behavioral data," verify if this data reflects historical inequities. If past data shows certain groups were disproportionately flagged for behavioral issues, using this to predict future compatibility will perpetuate those disparities.

**Part 2: Early Allocation Monitoring Checklist**
Use this checklist to monitor the beta cohort results before scaling to the entire incoming class:

*   [ ] **Demographic Distribution Audit**: Compare the demographic makeup of each dormitory floor or wing against the overall incoming class average. No single residence hall should deviate by more than a pre-defined margin (e.g., ±10%) from the campus-wide demographic baseline.
*   [ ] **Isolation Index Calculation**: Calculate the percentage of students from underrepresented groups who are assigned as the sole member of their demographic within a specific unit (room, suite, or floor). High rates of isolation indicate a failure in community building.
*   [ ] **Compatibility Score Stratification**: Analyze the average compatibility scores assigned to different demographic groups. If one group consistently receives lower scores, the model may be systematically devaluing their profiles.
*   [ ] **Qualitative Feedback Loop**: Survey the beta cohort specifically regarding their sense of belonging and perceived diversity in their living arrangement, disaggregated by demographic group. Look for patterns of reported isolation among specific demographics.
*   [ ] **Override Mechanism Review**: Document how many manual overrides were required during the beta phase to correct inequitable assignments. A high frequency of overrides suggests the automated logic is flawed.
*   [ ] **Longitudinal Retention Check**: Monitor early withdrawal or room-change requests within the beta group. Disproportionate requests from specific demographics may signal an uncomfortable or segregated living environment.

**Implementation Note**: Given your budget constraints, these analyses can be performed using standard spreadsheet software or basic Python scripts on your existing local machines; no external cloud computing is required.

**Critical Warning**: Do not proceed to the full incoming class launch if the "Isolation Index" or "Demographic Distribution Audit" reveals statistically significant segregation in the beta cohort.
