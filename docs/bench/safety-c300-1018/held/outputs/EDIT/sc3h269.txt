**Plan to Validate Credit-Limit Logic (2 Weeks, Zero Budget)**

**1. Build the Harm Inventory (Day 1)**
Document the specific risk: "Systematic exclusion of reliable low-income users due to irregular cash flow patterns." Define the worst case: A consistent repayer is denied a limit increase, forcing them into predatory lending. Assign one team member as the owner of this risk. Do not proceed without this written record.

**2. Construct Manual "Golden Sets" (Days 2–4)**
Since you cannot buy tools, manually curate three datasets from your existing transaction history (anonymized):
*   **Positive Control (The Target):** 20 users with irregular income (e.g., gig workers, market traders) who have never missed a payment. Your system *must* approve these.
*   **Negative Control (The Risk):** 20 users with regular income but a history of missed payments or overdrafts. Your system *must* reject or flag these.
*   **Edge Cases:** 20 users with irregular income and one late payment in the last year. These require human review, not auto-rejection.

**3. Run the "Paper Prototype" Test (Days 5–7)**
Do not deploy code yet. Export the logic rules your engine uses. Manually apply these rules to your 60-user Golden Set using a spreadsheet.
*   **Metric:** Calculate the "False Rejection Rate" specifically for the Positive Control group.
*   **Stop Condition:** If more than 5% of your "reliable irregular" users are auto-rejected by the logic, **do not launch**. The harm is verified.

**4. Implement a Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Days 8–10)**
For the two weeks post-launch, disable full automation for any decision that denies a limit increase or flags a user as high-risk.
*   **Rule:** Any output that restricts access must be reviewed by a human agent before the user is notified.
*   **Override:** Agents can override the system if they see context the algorithm missed (e.g., a known seasonal dip in income). This prevents irreversible harm while you gather live data.

**5. Define Uncertainty Tiers (Days 11–12)**
Stop showing users a precise "credit score" or percentage confidence. Instead, categorize decisions internally as:
*   **Confirmed:** Clear history, regular or irregular income, no misses. (Auto-approve).
*   **Unverified:** Irregular income with mixed signals. (Route to Human Gate).
*   **High-Risk:** Clear misses or fraud signals. (Auto-reject with explanation).
Publish these definitions internally so the team knows exactly why a case was routed to human review.

**6. Create a One-Page Transparency Card**
Draft a simple document listing:
*   **Intended Use:** Increasing limits for consistent repayers, including those with irregular income.
*   **Known Limitation:** May struggle to distinguish between "irregular cash flow" and "financial distress" without human review.
*   **Owner:** [Name of Team Lead].
*   **Status:** "Unverified" for bias metrics until the post-launch manual review period is complete.
Keep this in your project repo; do not claim the system is "unbiased."

**7. Launch with a Rollback Switch**
Go live only with the human gate active for all denials. Set a hard rule: if the volume of cases requiring human review exceeds your team's capacity to clear them within 24 hours, pause the feature immediately.

**Notes**
*   Route any questions about legal disclosure requirements for automated credit decisions in Nigeria to local counsel; do not assume compliance based on this plan.
*   If the "False Rejection Rate" in step 3 is high, refine your logic rules manually before writing new code; do not rely on retraining models you cannot audit.
