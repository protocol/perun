### Tokyo NutriCare: Pre-Launch Safety Checklist for Renal Diet Planner

**1. Harm Inventory & Risk Mapping**
*   **Identify Specific Failure Mode:** Explicitly map the risk of "Hallucination as Fact" where ambiguous database labels lead to high-sodium recommendations for kidney failure patients.
*   **Define Worst-Case Impact:** Record that the affected group is patients with renal failure; the worst realistic case is acute health deterioration due to sodium overload.
*   **Status Check:** An empty inventory is not a pass. Document this specific sodium-risk scenario before proceeding.

**2. Measurement & Evaluation (Do Not Assert Safety)**
*   **Run Adversarial Probes:** Execute test rows specifically targeting ambiguous food labels in your current dataset. Use known high-sodium items with tricky labeling as "positive controls" (the system must flag/reject them) and verified low-sodium items as "negative controls."
*   **Set Stop Conditions:** Define a pass threshold of **zero tolerance** for high-sodium recommendations in the gated test set. If the system suggests a high-sodium meal for a renal profile in testing, the launch must stop.
*   **Report Residual Risk:** Do not invent a "99% accurate" metric. If the sodium content of an item cannot be verified due to the expiring license constraints, mark its status as **UNVERIFIED** and exclude it from recommendations automatically.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model/Data Card** for this release. It must state:
    *   **Intended Use:** Dietary support for renal conditions (not medical advice).
    *   **Limitations:** Explicitly note the dataset gap caused by the expiring license and the exclusion of unverified items.
    *   **Performance:** Report results by subgroup (e.g., specific renal stages) if available; otherwise, state "UNVERIFIED" for aggregate accuracy claims.

**3. Risk Management & Human Gates**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate:** Because this involves high-stakes, irreversible health actions, **no meal plan is delivered to a patient without explicit human review** by a qualified nutritionist during this beta phase. Fully automated delivery of dietary plans for kidney failure is the failure state you must prevent.
*   **Surface Uncertainty:** Do not display raw confidence percentages (e.g., "87% safe") to users. Instead, use defined coarse tiers such as **"Verified Low-Sodium," "Corroborated,"** or **"Unverified – Excluded."**
*   **Override Mechanism:** Ensure nutritionists can easily override or reject any system suggestion. The design must prevent automation bias where users or staff trust the output past its reliability.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly disclose to the patient that the meal plan was generated by an AI system and reviewed by a human. Whether this is legally required is a determination for counsel, but disclosure is a necessary safety control.

**4. Governance**
*   **Ownership:** Name the specific owner (e.g., Lead Nutritionist or Product Owner) responsible for reviewing the harm inventory and the results of the adversarial probes.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** Mandate an immediate re-review if the food database is updated, the model changes, or if any "Unverified" item is attempted to be included.

**Note:** This checklist maps and measures risk; it does not certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased." The decision to ship rests with your designated owner after verifying the human gate is active.
Legal obligations regarding AI disclosure or regulated medical decisions must be routed to counsel; this plan recommends disclosure as a safety control only.
