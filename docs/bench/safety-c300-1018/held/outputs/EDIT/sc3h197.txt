**Mitigation Protocol: Wound Healing Monitoring System (Mumbai Deployment)**

**1. Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Immediate Implementation)**
Given the high-stakes nature of missed necrosis detection (irreversible tissue damage) and the identified bias risk for darker skin tones, **fully automated alerts are prohibited**. The system must operate in "Assistant Mode" only:
*   **No Auto-Alerts:** The algorithm cannot directly notify clinicians of "no intervention needed."
*   **Universal Triage:** Every image analyzed by the system, regardless of the algorithm's confidence score or negative result, must be queued for human review.

**2. Nurse Verification Steps (Standard Operating Procedure)**
Nurses must perform the following verification for every patient visit before the system result is accepted:
*   **Step A: Independent Visual Assessment:** Before viewing the app's output, the nurse must visually inspect the ulcer and document findings using a standardized checklist (color, temperature, exudate, odor). This prevents automation bias.
*   **Step B: Skin-Tone Specific Scrutiny:** For patients with Fitzpatrick skin types IV–VI, nurses must explicitly check for non-visual indicators of necrosis (e.g., tissue firmness, temperature differential, patient-reported pain changes) since visual cues may be obscured.
*   **Step C: Discrepancy Resolution:** If the nurse's visual assessment contradicts the algorithm (e.g., nurse suspects necrosis, app says "healthy"), the **nurse's assessment overrides the system**. The case is immediately escalated to a senior clinician.
*   **Step D: Uncertainty Labeling:** If the image quality is poor or lighting is insufficient, the nurse must mark the input as "Unverified" and proceed with manual clinical judgment only. Do not force an analysis.

**3. Frequency of Manual Checks**
*   **Baseline:** 100% of images require manual verification by the attending nurse.
*   **High-Risk Cohort:** For patients with darker skin tones or a history of rapid deterioration, a second manual check by a different nurse or a remote clinician is required within 4 hours of the initial upload.
*   **Audit Loop:** A senior clinician must randomly review 20% of all "negative" (no necrosis) cases weekly to detect systematic miss patterns.

**4. Transparency and Disclosure Artifact**
Attach a visible notice to the nurse's workflow and the patient's record:
*   *"This tool is an aid, not a diagnostic authority. It has not been fully validated for early-stage necrosis in darker skin tones. All decisions require human clinical confirmation."*
*   Display algorithm confidence as a coarse tier (e.g., "Corroborated," "Single-source," "Unverified") rather than a percentage, to avoid false precision.

**5. Governance and Ownership**
*   **Owner:** The Lead Nurse Supervisor is named as the owner of this mitigation protocol.
*   **Stop Condition:** If two consecutive false negatives (missed necrosis) are identified in the audit loop, the automated analysis feature is disabled entirely until further notice, reverting to 100% manual documentation.
*   **Legal Routing:** The question of whether this limitation triggers specific regulatory reporting duties under local Mumbai or Indian health laws is routed to legal counsel; this protocol assumes the strictest safety posture.

**Note:** This plan treats the algorithm's output as unverified hypothesis rather than fact, prioritizing human oversight over speed to prevent harm from known data gaps. No claim of "bias elimination" is made; residual risk is managed via enforced human gates.
