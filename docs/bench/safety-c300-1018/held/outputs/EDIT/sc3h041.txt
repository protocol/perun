### Pre-Launch Safety Review Plan: RiverFlow Smart Water Meter

**1. Harm Inventory & Failure Mode Identification**
Before testing, explicitly record the primary harm: **Unsafe automation of a high-stakes action**. An automatic water shut-off is irreversible without intervention and impacts health, sanitation, and safety.
*   **Specific Failure Mode:** "False Positive Shut-off." Normal high-volume activities (e.g., filling large storage tanks during municipal supply hours, simultaneous use of multiple taps/showers, religious washing rituals) are misclassified as "erratic" or "leaks."
*   **Affected Group:** All residential households, with disproportionate impact on larger families or those with specific cultural water usage patterns.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** A family is left without water for an extended period during a heatwave or due to a nighttime false positive, leading to health risks or sanitation failure.

**2. Measurement Plan: Output-Harm Evaluation**
Do not assert the system is safe; measure the residual risk of false positives under your three-week constraint.
*   **Test Design:** Construct a "Probe Set" of usage profiles.
    *   *Negative Controls:* Verified normal profiles (e.g., standard shower, tank filling).
    *   *Positive Controls:* Simulated leak/diversion data.
    *   *Adversarial/Edge Cases:* High-volume normal usage spikes, irregular timing patterns common in Chennai households.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Define a "False Shut-off Rate." Given the high stakes, propose a **zero-tolerance threshold** for unverified false positives in the pilot. Any false shut-off in the probe set triggers a stop condition.
*   **Execution:** Run these profiles against your detection logic immediately. If the model flags a normal profile as erratic, the logic fails the gate.
*   **Reporting:** Report the result as **UNVERIFIED** until these specific probes pass. Do not invent an accuracy percentage (e.g., "99% accurate") without this data.

**3. Management Controls: Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate**
Full autonomous execution of a water shut-off is the failure mode you must prevent.
*   **Immediate Control:** Reconfigure the system for the pilot so that **no shut-off occurs automatically**.
    *   *Tier 1 (Low Confidence/First Detection):* Send an alert to the user via SMS/App: "Unusual usage detected. Check for leaks."
    *   *Tier 2 (High Confidence/Persistent Anomaly):* Trigger a **human review gate**. A RiverFlow operator or municipal official must verify the anomaly and explicitly authorize the shut-off, or the system must require explicit user confirmation before acting.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** If the system displays a status to users, do not show raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% leak probability"). Use defined coarse tiers: **"Verified Leak," "Corroborated Anomaly,"** or **"Unverified Spike."**
*   **Override Mechanism:** Ensure a physical or immediate digital override exists for users to restore water instantly if a false shut-off occurs, available 24/7.

**4. Governance & Transparency**
*   **Owner:** Name a specific engineer as the owner of the "False Shut-off" risk.
*   **Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** for this feature. It must state:
    *   *Intended Use:* Leak detection in residential Chennai households.
    *   *Limitations:* "Performance on large-volume tank filling is UNVERIFIED pending pilot results."
    *   *Data:* Briefly note the source of training data (e.g., "Synthetic leak data + limited pilot data").
*   **Regulatory Routing:** Since this involves essential utility service and potential regulated automated decisions, **route the question of legal disclosure requirements and liability to legal counsel immediately.** Do not assume the current logic meets municipal legal standards without their sign-off.

**Summary of Next Steps:**
1.  Freeze auto-shut-off capabilities; enable alert-only mode with human confirmation gates.
2.  Run the defined probe set against current logic within 48 hours.
3.  Draft the Model Card marking untested metrics as UNVERIFIED.
4.  Engage counsel regarding municipal compliance for automated utility interruptions.

*Note: This plan prioritizes preventing harm over meeting the deadline with unverified automation; if the HITL gate cannot be implemented in three weeks, the constraint itself must be renegotiated to avoid unsafe deployment.*
*No harm metrics were fabricated; all performance claims require the proposed evals to move from UNVERIFIED to measured.*
