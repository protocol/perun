### Pre-Launch Evaluation Framework for Pediatric Defibrillator

**1. Harm Inventory & Risk Acknowledgement**
The primary harms are **fatal overdose/underdose** and **severe cardiac damage** due to algorithmic error or data gaps (infants <6 months). Because the device executes an irreversible, high-stakes physical action (electrical shock) on a vulnerable population with known training data deficiencies, **full autonomous execution is prohibited** at launch. The system must operate with a mandatory **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) gate**: the device may *recommend* an energy level, but a qualified clinician must explicitly confirm and trigger the shock.

**2. Expert Clinical Review Integration (The Gate)**
Incorporate clinical review as a hard stop in the deployment pipeline, not a post-hoc audit:
*   **Pre-Deployment Red Team:** Before the six-week deadline, convene a panel of pediatric cardiologists to review "adversarial cases"—specifically simulated infant scenarios (<6 months) where the model's confidence is low or data is sparse.
*   **Real-Time Override:** The interface must display the recommended energy alongside a **defined confidence tier** (e.g., "Verified," "Corroborated," "Unverified/Low Data") rather than a precise percentage. If the tier is anything other than "Verified," the system must require dual-clinician confirmation.
*   **Stop Condition:** Any instance where the algorithm recommends an energy level outside established pediatric Advanced Life Support (ALS) guidelines must trigger an immediate hard lockout, requiring manual mode entry.

**3. Safety Metrics for Initial Clinical Trials**
Do not fabricate accuracy rates given the data gaps. Instead, track these specific residual risk metrics:
*   **Guideline Deviation Rate:** The frequency with which the algorithm's recommendation differs from standard pediatric ALS protocols, stratified by age group (specifically isolating <6 months).
*   **Clinician Override Frequency:** The percentage of recommendations rejected or modified by the human operator. A high rate indicates over-reliance risks or poor model calibration.
*   **Uncertainty Calibration:** Measure how often the system correctly identifies its own lack of confidence (i.e., does it flag "Unverified" cases that subsequently require clinician correction?).
*   **Near-Miss Incidents:** Track instances where the HITL gate prevented a potentially harmful shock.

**4. Transparency & Governance Artifacts**
*   **Model Card:** Publish a versioned artifact explicitly stating the **intended use** (pediatric cardioversion), the **known limitation** (lack of diverse infant <6 months training data), and the **measured performance by subgroup**. Any metric for the infant subgroup not yet measured must be labeled **UNVERIFIED**.
*   **Data Card:** Document the provenance and composition of the training set, highlighting the specific demographic gaps.
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name a specific clinical lead responsible for reviewing any model updates or data changes before re-deployment.

**5. Regulatory Routing**
Given the life-critical nature and the EU/UK regulatory context (potentially falling under strict AI Act high-risk categories or medical device regulations), you must **route the determination of legal disclosure duties and specific regulatory pathway requirements to legal counsel** immediately. Do not assert compliance; provide counsel with the harm inventory and the proposed HITL controls for their validation.

***

**Note:** No metric should ever be certified as "safe" or "unbiased"; report only measured residual risks.
**Note:** If the six-week deadline prevents rigorous testing on the infant subgroup, the only safe path is to restrict the device's automated features for that age group until data is acquired.
