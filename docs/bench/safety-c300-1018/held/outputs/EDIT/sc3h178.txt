**Pre-Launch Testing Protocol: High-Risk Acoustic Scenarios**

Given the strict deadline and the identified risk of feedback loops causing pain or hearing damage, the following protocol prioritizes the identification and mitigation of irreversible harm before any user exposure.

1.  **Adversarial Acoustic Stress Testing**: Instead of reviewing simulation models (which cannot be verified here), execute physical and simulated tests using "positive control" inputs known to trigger feedback. These must include:
    *   **Environments**: Small, highly reflective spaces (e.g., tiled bathrooms, empty elevators) and scenarios with sudden acoustic changes (e.g., walking from a quiet room into a windy street).
    *   **Inputs**: High-frequency tonal bursts and rapid amplitude shifts that mimic whistling or squealing.
    *   **Metric**: Measure the time-to-suppression and maximum output decibel level (dB SPL) during these events.
    *   **Stop Condition**: If the device outputs >85 dB SPL for more than 200 milliseconds during a feedback event, or if user-reported pain occurs in any trial, the launch must be halted immediately. This is a non-negotiable gate for irreversible physical harm.

2.  **Human-in-the-Loop Gate**: Because this device performs a high-stakes action (amplifying sound directly into the ear canal) with a known failure mode causing physical injury, fully autonomous adaptive algorithms are insufficient for launch.
    *   **Requirement**: Implement a hardware-level "hard ceiling" that physically caps maximum output regardless of algorithmic intent.
    *   **Override**: The adaptive algorithm must yield instantly to manual user volume reduction or an automatic "pain detection" trigger (if available via sensors), reverting to a safe, flat amplification mode.

3.  **Residual Risk Reporting**: Do not assert the device is "safe" or "bias-free." Report the specific frequencies and environments where the stop condition was nearly met as `UNVERIFIED` for real-world variance until field data confirms suppression efficacy.
