### Pre-Launch Audit Plan: Discipline Predictor Bias Review

**1. Harm Inventory & Stakeholder Mapping**
Before testing, formally document the specific risk: the system may conflate poverty (low income) with behavioral risk, leading to disproportionate suspension flags for low-income students.
*   **Affected Group:** Students from low-income households.
*   **Worst Case:** Systemic criminalization of poverty, increased suspension rates for non-behavioral reasons, and erosion of trust between families and the district.
*   **Constraint Acknowledgement:** The outdated software's inability to isolate income variables is a critical failure point, not just a technical hurdle. If income cannot be isolated, the model's logic is opaque and un-auditable.

**2. Measurement Strategy (The "Runnable" Test)**
Do not assert the model is fair; measure the disparity. Since you cannot isolate variables in the current software, you must test the *output* against known demographics.
*   **Probe Set:** Construct a test dataset of synthetic student profiles where behavioral indicators are held constant, but family income varies (Low vs. High).
*   **Positive Control:** A profile with high behavioral risk factors (regardless of income) should flag.
*   **Negative Control:** A profile with low behavioral risk factors (regardless of income) should not flag.
*   **The Bias Test:** Run the Low-Income/Negative-Control profiles. If the flag rate is significantly higher than the High-Income/Negative-Control profiles, the model is biased.
*   **Metric:** Calculate the **False Positive Rate (FPR)** disparity between income groups.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the FPR for low-income students exceeds the FPR for high-income students by any measurable margin, **block launch**. Do not set an arbitrary "acceptable" bias threshold; the goal is equity.

**3. Management & Human-in-the-Loop Gate**
Given the high stakes (suspension affects education and legal records) and the identified technical limitations:
*   **Mandatory Human Review:** No suspension flag generated by this tool can result in automatic action. Every flag must be reviewed by a human administrator who explicitly verifies that the reason is behavioral, not socioeconomic.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** The tool must display a clear warning to analysts: *"This prediction is based on limited data isolation and may reflect historical biases. Do not use as sole justification for discipline."*
*   **Confidence Tiers:** Do not show percentage scores (e.g., "87% risk"). Use defined tiers: *Confirmed Behavioral Pattern*, *Corroborated by Multiple Sources*, or *Unverified/Single Source*.

**4. Governance & Transparency Artifacts**
*   **Model Card:** Before deployment, publish a document detailing:
    *   **Intended Use:** Early intervention support only, not punitive decision-making.
    *   **Limitations:** Explicitly state that the software cannot isolate income variables, making bias detection difficult.
    *   **Data Provenance:** Source of attendance and income data.
    *   **Owner:** Name the specific analyst responsible for monitoring bias metrics post-launch.
*   **Legal Routing:** Because this involves minors and consequential decisions (suspension) based on socioeconomic data, **immediately route to legal counsel**. Do not proceed assuming compliance; counsel must determine if this violates civil rights statutes or Philadelphia school policies.

**5. Immediate Recommendation**
If the outdated software prevents isolating income variables to prove the model *isn't* biased, **do not launch**. The inability to audit the variable is itself a safety failure. Prioritize fixing the data isolation capability or delaying deployment until a transparent audit is possible. Shipping an un-auditable model that flags students for suspension is an unacceptable risk.

*Note: This plan identifies residual risk; it does not certify the tool as safe or unbiased. The final decision to ship rests with district leadership.*
