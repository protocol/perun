### Pre-Launch Equity Safety Plan: London Traffic Light Optimizer

**1. Harm Inventory & Worst-Case Definition**
*   **Identified Harm:** Systemic bias causing disproportionately long red-light wait times for pedestrians in lower-income districts due to simulation gaps.
*   **Affected Group:** Residents and pedestrians in poorer districts (specifically those with unmodeled behaviors).
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** The optimizer systematically prioritizes vehicle flow in wealthy areas, trapping pedestrians in lower-income zones at crossings for unsafe durations, leading to increased accident risk or effective segregation of movement.

**2. Measurement Strategy (Working Within Simulation Limits)**
Since the simulation cannot fully model pedestrian behavior in target districts, we must treat the simulation output for these areas as **UNVERIFIED** regarding pedestrian impact. We will not assert safety based on incomplete data.

*   **Proxy Metric Construction:** Create a "Pedestrian Stress Proxy" within the simulation. Even if specific behaviors are missing, the model likely tracks signal phase durations.
    *   *Test Row 1 (Bias Probe):* Run simulations comparing average maximum continuous red-light duration for pedestrian crossings in lower-income zones vs. city average.
    *   *Positive Control:* Inject a known bad scenario where the optimizer ignores a specific zone entirely; verify the proxy metric spikes.
    *   *Negative Control:* Run a static-timing baseline; ensure the proxy metric remains stable.
*   **Threshold & Stop Condition:**
    *   *Metric:* Ratio of (Max Pedestrian Red Time in Target District) / (City Average Max Pedestrian Red Time).
    *   *Threshold:* Owner-set limit (e.g., ratio > 1.2 triggers review).
    *   *Stop Condition:* If the ratio exceeds the threshold in any simulation run, the automated deployment to those specific intersections is blocked.
*   **Addressing the Data Gap:** Explicitly label the simulation results for these districts as "Partial – Behavior Unmodeled." Do not fabricate a safety score. The plan must include a **manual field audit** post-deployment for the first 48 hours in these specific districts, as simulation cannot clear this risk.

**3. Risk Management & Human Gate**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Because the action (changing signal timing) affects public safety and the data is incomplete, full autonomous optimization is prohibited for the target districts.
    *   *Control:* Any signal timing change generated by the optimizer for a lower-income district must be queued for **human review and explicit confirmation** by a traffic engineer before execution.
    *   *Fallback:* If the human reviewer cannot validate the change due to the simulation gap, the system must revert to the previous static timing (safe default).
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Internal dashboards must display confidence as a **defined coarse tier** (e.g., "Verified" for modeled areas, "Unverified – Manual Review Required" for lower-income districts) rather than a precise percentage. Do not display raw model confidence scores to operators.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a preliminary **Model Card** documenting:
    *   *Intended Use:* Traffic flow optimization.
    *   *Limitations:* Simulation does not model pedestrian behavior in specific socioeconomic zones; equity metrics for these zones are UNVERIFIED by simulation.
    *   *Owner:* Name the specific team lead responsible for the manual gate.

**4. Governance**
*   Assign a single owner responsible for reviewing the "Unverified" logs daily during the first month.
*   Schedule a re-evaluation immediately if the simulation software is updated to include better pedestrian models or if real-world incident reports arise.

**Note:** This plan treats the simulation gap as a critical risk, not a minor limitation. The human gate on affected intersections is mandatory until real-world data validates the model.
Legal counsel must be consulted to determine if this automated decision-making triggers specific regulatory disclosures under UK or EU AI governance regimes.
