**BLUF:** Do not deploy this feature as an automated suggestion engine within three weeks without a hard human-in-the-loop gate and verified edge-case performance; the current timeline precludes the clinical validation required to prove the protocol does not harm patients via over- or under-ventilation.

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory & Gate (Do This First)**
Your "risk assessment" must explicitly name the specific harms before writing another line of code:
*   **Unsafe Automation of High-Stakes Actions:** Suggesting weaning in an ICU is a high-stakes, potentially irreversible action. If the system fails to suggest weaning (keeping a patient ventilated unnecessarily), it increases infection risk and ICU stay. If it suggests weaning too early (false positive), it risks respiratory failure and death.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The model might interpret noisy vitals during a transient fluctuation as stable readiness, presenting a "wean now" suggestion with unwarranted confidence.
*   **Over-reliance:** Clinicians may trust the automated suggestion over their own judgment if the interface does not clearly display uncertainty or conflicting data.

**Action:** Implement a mandatory **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) gate**. The system must *never* execute a weaning protocol change or present a suggestion as a definitive command. It must frame outputs as "Potential Candidate for Weaning – Requires Clinician Verification" with a one-click override to dismiss. Full autonomous execution is a failure condition.

**2. Measurement Plan (Runnable Tests, Not Assertions)**
You cannot claim the system is "safe" or "accurate" without running specific probes. Since you cannot run extended clinical trials, you must simulate the edge cases you identified:
*   **Probe Set:** Construct a test suite using historical ICU data specifically targeting "rapid fluctuation" edge cases (e.g., sudden desaturation events, artifact noise from patient movement, sepsis onset).
*   **Controls:** Include known "bad positives" (patients who appeared stable but crashed shortly after) and "good negatives" (patients correctly kept on support).
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Define a stop condition. For example: "If the system suggests weaning for any patient in the 'bad positive' control set, the feature fails launch." Do not invent a "99% accuracy" number; if you haven't tested against a specific subgroup, mark that metric as **UNVERIFIED**.
*   **Injection/Leakage Check:** Ensure the model isn't making decisions based on spurious correlations (e.g., time of day, specific nurse shifts) rather than physiology.

**3. Manage Residual Risk & Transparency**
*   **Surface Uncertainty:** Do not show a raw probability (e.g., "87% ready"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers: **"Confirmed Stability"** (multiple corroborating vitals over time), **"Corroborated"** (single strong signal), or **"Unverified/Fluctuating"** (noisy data). Publish the definition of these tiers to your clinical partners.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a **Model Card** immediately. It must state:
    *   **Intended Use:** Decision support only, not autonomous control.
    *   **Limitations:** Explicitly list the edge cases where performance is **UNVERIFIED** due to the lack of extended trials.
    *   **Owner:** Name the specific clinician and developer responsible for reviewing failure modes.
    *   **Data Provenance:** Source of the training data and known gaps.
