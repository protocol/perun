### Rapid Safety Review Framework: Smart-Epinephrine Injector Field Trial

**1. Critical Metrics to Monitor**
Given the high-stakes nature of automatic drug deployment, you must track these specific output behaviors during the trial. Do not aggregate these into a single "safety score"; report them distinctly.

*   **False Negative Rate (Missed Detection):** The frequency where anaphylactic shock occurs but the device fails to deploy.
    *   *Target:* Zero tolerance. Any instance is a critical failure requiring immediate trial pause.
    *   *Measurement:* Compare device logs against clinical confirmation of shock events by medical staff.
*   **False Positive Rate (Unnecessary Deployment):** The frequency where the device deploys without a confirmed anaphylactic event.
    *   *Risk:* Unnecessary epinephrine exposure causes cardiac stress; repeated errors erode user trust.
    *   *Measurement:* Ratio of automatic deployments to medically verified shock events.
*   **Latency to Deployment:** Time elapsed between biometric threshold breach and needle deployment.
    *   *Target:* Must meet clinical efficacy windows (define specific milliseconds based on medical guidance).
*   **Override Success Rate:** Frequency with which a user successfully manually stops a deployment after the alert triggers but before injection.
    *   *Context:* Tests the "human-in-the-loop" window if your design includes a brief delay.

**2. Contingency Plan for Activation Failure**
Since the device automates a life-saving action, the failure mode (non-deployment) requires a rigid, pre-defined response protocol.

*   **Immediate Manual Backup Protocol:**
    *   **Requirement:** Every participant must carry a standard, non-connected epinephrine auto-injector on a different part of the body at all times during the trial.
    *   **Trigger:** If biometric symptoms are present and the smart device has not fired within [X] seconds (defined by your clinical lead), the participant or bystander must immediately use the manual backup.
    *   **Training:** All 50 participants and their designated guardians must demonstrate successful manual injection technique before receiving the smart device.
*   **Emergency Support Link:**
    *   **Active Monitoring:** If the device connects to a network, a failure to deploy upon detecting shock must trigger an immediate high-priority alert to a 24/7 monitoring center capable of dispatching EMS.
    *   **Offline Protocol:** If offline, the trial design must ensure a guardian is within [X] meters of the participant at all times, equipped with a satellite phone or direct emergency line.
*   **Post-Incident Review:**
    *   Any failure to activate triggers an automatic suspension of that specific device unit for forensic analysis before any further use.

**3. Governance and Disclosure**
*   **Human Gate:** While the device is automated, the *decision to enroll* a participant requires a physician's sign-off confirming they understand the experimental nature and the backup protocol.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a one-page "Device Limitations Card" for each participant. It must explicitly state: "This device is experimental. It may fail to detect shock. Always carry your standard manual injector." Do not claim the device is "safe" or "bias-free"; state its intended use and known limitations clearly.
*   **Legal Routing:** Because this involves automated medical decisions and potential harm, you must route the specific disclosure language and liability waivers to legal counsel immediately. Do not draft final legal disclaimers internally.

**Note:** This framework treats the device's automated decision as a high-stakes action requiring rigorous measurement of failure modes rather than a certification of safety. The "Zero Tolerance" metric for missed detections is a proposed stop-condition, not a guarantee of performance.
