# Risk Assessment: Lighting-Dependent Failure Modes & Data Strategy

## 1. Harm Inventory: Lighting-Related Failure Modes
Before pilot deployment, the following specific harms must be recorded regarding low-light operations:

*   **False Negatives (Missed Damage):** In low-light or shadowed conditions, the computer vision model fails to detect significant structural cracks.
    *   *Affected:* Homebuyers, sellers, and inspectors relying on the report.
    *   *Worst Case:* A buyer purchases a home with critical foundation flaws undetected by the drone, leading to catastrophic structural failure or massive financial loss post-sale.
*   **Over-Reliance / Automation Bias:** The autonomous nature of the drone and the "clean" report generated in low-light conditions may lead inspectors to trust the output despite known environmental limitations.
    *   *Affected:* Inspection professionals.
    *   *Worst Case:* Inspectors skip manual verification in dim areas (basements, crawl spaces) because the drone reported "no issues," creating a false sense of security.
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** Authorizing the drone to finalize a "Pass/Fail" structural assessment without human review in unverified lighting conditions.
    *   *Affected:* End-users and the company (liability).
    *   *Worst Case:* Irreversible financial transactions (home sales) occur based on an unverified automated decision.

## 2. Measurement Plan: Addressing the Data Gap
Current testing is insufficient because it lacks negative controls for low-light scenarios. The following evaluation steps are required before commercial authorization:

*   **Probe Set Construction:**
    *   *Positive Controls:* Introduce known artificial cracks in test environments under varied lighting (dusk, heavy shadow, artificial light only).
    *   *Negative Controls:* Intact foundations under the same low-light conditions to measure false positive rates.
    *   *Segmentation:* Tests must be sliced by light intensity (lux levels) and shadow contrast, not just "day vs. night."
*   **Metric & Threshold:**
    *   *Metric:* Recall rate (ability to find actual cracks) specifically within the "low-light" segment.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If recall drops below a defined safety threshold (e.g., missing >1% of critical cracks) in low-light segments, the autonomous "final assessment" feature must be disabled for those conditions.
*   **Residual Risk Status:** Until evening/low-light data is gathered and tested, the safety performance for these conditions is **UNVERIFIED**. No accuracy percentage can be claimed for low-light operations.

## 3. Risk Management Strategy
Given the fixed timeline and limited test houses, the following controls must be implemented immediately:

*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** The system must **not** autonomously issue a final "Structurally Sound" certification if operating in low-light conditions. Instead, the output must flag: *"Lighting conditions insufficient for automated verification; manual human inspection required for this zone."* This prevents irreversible decisions based on unverified data.
*   **Defined Confidence Tiers:** Do not display raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% sure") to users. Use coarse, defined tiers:
    *   *Verified:* Good lighting, high model confidence.
    *   *Unverified:* Low lighting or out-of-distribution environment (triggers human gate).
*   **Data Gathering Strategy for Pilot:**
    *   Partner with local inspection firms to collect anonymized image data during evening inspections in exchange for discounted pilot access.
    *   Utilize controlled lighting rigs in available test houses to simulate dusk/darkness rather than waiting for natural evening windows.
    *   Implement an "opt-in" data collection mode for early users to capture diverse environmental data, explicitly disclosed in the user agreement.

## 4. Governance
*   **Owner:** One engineer must be named as the owner of the "Lighting Failure Mode" risk.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** The risk assessment must be updated before any model update or expansion into new geographic regions with different lighting profiles.

**Note:** This assessment identifies risks and controls; it does not certify the system as safe. The decision to ship with the human-in-the-loop gate rests with product leadership. Legal counsel should review disclosure requirements for automated structural assessments.
