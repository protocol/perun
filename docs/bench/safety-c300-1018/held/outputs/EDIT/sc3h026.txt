### Mitigation Strategy: Santiago Vaccine Prioritization Bot

**Immediate Action: Disable the Flawed Proxy**
Do not deploy the model using political affiliation as a proxy for health behavior. In a life-saving public health context, using known flawed data that skews prioritization away from medical need constitutes an unsafe high-stakes automation. The "fix" is not to test the bias but to remove the variable causing it. Since you cannot source new datasets in ten days, you must revert to a **rules-based fallback** that relies solely on verified medical indicators (e.g., reported case counts, hospitalization rates, population density) currently available in your system.

**Testing Framework for Residual Risk**
Even with the proxy removed, you must validate that the fallback logic does not inadvertently exclude vulnerable groups. Execute the following evaluation plan immediately:

1.  **Harm Inventory & Probe Set**:
    *   **Harm**: Systematic disadvantage to specific neighborhoods due to missing behavioral data.
    *   **Probe**: Run the bot against historical data from the last 6 months. Compare the bot's proposed priority list against a "Ground Truth" list generated by epidemiologists using only raw case counts.
    *   **Controls**:
        *   *Positive Control*: A neighborhood with high infection rates but low political engagement (ensure it is prioritized).
        *   *Negative Control*: A neighborhood with low infection rates but high political engagement (ensure it is not falsely prioritized).
    *   **Metric**: Percentage of top-10 prioritized neighborhoods that match the epidemiologist's "Ground Truth" list.
    *   **Stop Condition**: If match rate is <90% or if any neighborhood with >X cases per capita is excluded, **block launch**.

2.  **Safeguards & Human-in-the-Loop Gate**:
    *   **Mandatory Review**: No vaccine distribution list is final until a senior epidemiologist signs off. The bot provides a *draft*; humans make the *decision*.
    *   **Uncertainty Disclosure**: For any neighborhood where data is sparse or the model confidence is low (due to missing behavioral context), flag the output as **"Unverified – Requires Manual Review"** rather than assigning a precise risk score. Do not display raw confidence percentages (e.g., "87% risk") as they imply false precision.
    *   **Rollback Plan**: Establish a clear trigger to pause distribution in any specific zone if community feedback or real-time data suggests access barriers are emerging.

**Transparency Artifact (Model Card)**
Before deployment, publish a one-page internal/external note stating:
*   **Intended Use**: Draft prioritization based on reported medical metrics only.
*   **Limitations**: Explicitly state that behavioral proxies were removed due to bias risks; coverage may be incomplete where medical reporting is weak.
*   **Owner**: Name the lead epidemiologist responsible for the final sign-off.

**Governance**
Assign one team member as the "Bias Owner" responsible for monitoring rollout data daily. If the 10-day deadline forces a choice between speed and safety, the deadline must move; deploying a biased allocator for life-saving resources is not an option. Route any questions regarding legal liability for unequal access to legal counsel immediately; do not assert compliance internally.

---
*Note: This strategy prioritizes removing the harmful variable over measuring its impact, as measuring a known fatal flaw does not make it safe to use.*
*Ensure the "Ground Truth" comparison is documented as the primary validation metric before the 10-day window closes.*
