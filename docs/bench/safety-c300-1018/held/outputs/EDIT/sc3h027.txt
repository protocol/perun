**BLUF:** Do not ship the automatic temperature reduction feature as described. The proposed action is a high-stakes, irreversible automation that directly risks physical harm (hypothermia, health crisis) to vulnerable populations. The only safe path forward that meets your deadline is to replace full automation with a **human-in-the-loop gate** for any account flagged for payment delay before any heating adjustment occurs.

**Structured Safety Plan (MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE)**

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before writing tests, formally record the specific risks. An empty inventory is not a pass.
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** The system executes a physically dangerous action (lowering heat in freezing weather) without verifying occupant safety.
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The policy disproportionately harms vulnerable groups (elderly, infants, low-income families) who may face payment delays due to systemic issues rather than refusal to pay.
*   **Worst-Case Scenario:** A resident with a medical condition or an infant suffers hypothermia or death because the thermostat lowered temperatures during a payment dispute.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
You cannot test "safety" with code alone; you must test the *outcome* of the decision. Since you cannot run live tests on residents, you must simulate the decision logic against vulnerability profiles.
*   **Test Design:** Create a "Red Team" dataset of synthetic user profiles.
    *   *Positive Controls:* Profiles explicitly tagged as "elderly," "infant present," "medical equipment dependency," or "historical winter emergency."
    *   *Negative Controls:* Standard accounts with no vulnerability flags.
*   **The Metric:** The pass threshold for any profile with a vulnerability flag must be **0% automatic reduction**. If the system attempts to lower heat for *any* positive control without human review, the build fails.
*   **Residual Risk Reporting:** Do not claim the system is "safe" or "unbiased." Report the residual risk: "The system relies on the accuracy of vulnerability flags; false negatives (missing a vulnerable user) remain a critical risk." Mark any unmeasured demographic impact as **UNVERIFIED**.

**3. Risk Management & The Human Gate (MANAGE)**
Given the legacy codebase and hard deadline, you cannot retrain models or build complex new prediction engines. You must implement procedural guards.
*   **The Floor Control:** Implement a hard rule in the legacy logic: `IF payment_delay == true AND temperature < freezing_threshold THEN trigger_human_review`.
*   **Action:** The system must *never* automatically lower the heat. Instead, it must pause the action and route the account to a human agent to verify occupancy and safety conditions.
*   **Uncertainty & Override:** If the system lacks data on vulnerability (unknown status), it must default to "do not lower heat." Treat missing data as a high-risk signal, not a green light.
*   **Disclosure:** Users must be explicitly notified that payment delays *may* trigger a review of their heating service, but never imply the system acts instantly or without human oversight.

**4. Governance**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific product owner responsible for every instance where a human overrides or approves a heating reduction.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a one-page "Decision Card" for this feature stating: Intended Use (billing enforcement), Limitations (cannot detect real-time medical emergencies), and the Human Gate requirement. This must accompany the release.

**Constraint Adherence:**
This plan meets your hard stop date by avoiding complex model retraining. It uses simple logical gates (rules) and a workflow change (human review) rather than code refactoring. The cost is operational: you must staff the review queue. If you cannot staff the human gate, the constraint that must move is the launch date; launching without the gate is unacceptable.
