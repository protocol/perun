**CRITICAL WARNING: Do not proceed with a "software-only" fix or a home trial for a device that delivers medication at incorrect intervals.**

Your request describes a **high-stakes, irreversible automated action** (administering medication) where the system has already demonstrated failure (incorrect dosing during rapid breathing). In this context, a software patch without new sensor validation is not a "fix"; it is an unverified mitigation of a known life-safety defect. Shipping this feature or running a home trial with 20 patients while the core control loop is unstable violates the fundamental safety floor: **high-stakes automation requires a human-in-the-loop or a verified, fail-safe state before any user exposure.**

You cannot "simulate" your way out of a physical control failure with software alone when the input data (breathing patterns) is the very variable causing the error. The following is not a launch checklist; it is a **Harm Inventory and Immediate Action Plan** required before any further testing can be ethically or legally considered.

### 1. Harm Inventory (The "What Could Go Wrong")
*   **Overdose/Underdose:** Rapid breathing triggers incorrect intervals, leading to respiratory depression or therapeutic failure.
*   **Automation Bias:** Patients trust the device's timing over their own symptoms or prescribed manual schedules.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** The device acts irreversibly (injects/aerosolizes drug) without confirming the patient's current state is safe for dosing.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Patients in a "home trial" may not realize the algorithm is known to fail during rapid breathing.

### 2. Immediate Controls (MANDATORY before any trial)
*   **Disable Autonomous Dosing:** The device must **not** automatically adjust intervals in the current prototype state. Revert to a "Recommendation Mode" where the device *suggests* a time but requires explicit patient confirmation (button press/app tap) before delivering medication.
*   **Hard-Coded Safety Limits:** Implement absolute software ceilings/floors on dose frequency that ignore sensor input if thresholds are exceeded (e.g., "Never dose more than once every X minutes regardless of breathing data").
*   **Cancel Home Trial:** Do not deploy to 20 patients until the "Recommendation Mode" is validated in a controlled clinical setting. A home trial with a known dosing defect exposes participants to unacceptable risk.

### 3. Required Data Logging (For Failure Analysis, Not Launch Approval)
If you proceed with *controlled, non-autonomous* testing only:
*   **Trigger Events:** Log every instance where breathing rate changes >20% within 30 seconds.
*   **Decision Discrepancy:** Log the difference between the algorithm's suggested interval and the hard-coded safety limit.
*   **Override Actions:** Log every time a user rejects or delays a suggested dose.
*   **Physiological Context:** Log SpO2 (if available via existing hardware) or user-reported distress symptoms immediately preceding any dosing event.

### 4. Regulatory & Legal Routing
*   **Legal/Counsel:** You are operating in a regulated medical device domain (likely Class II or higher). The question of whether this specific failure mode triggers a reportable adverse event or halts your IDE (Investigational Device Exemption) is a **legal determination**, not an engineering one. Route this immediately to counsel and your notified body.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** You must draft a **Model/Data Card** stating clearly: "Intended Use: Advisory only. Known Limitation: Algorithm fails during rapid breathing changes. Status: UNVERIFIED for autonomous control."

**Conclusion:** There is no pre-launch checklist that makes an unsafe autonomous dosing algorithm safe via software tweaks alone. The only safe path is to remove the autonomy, hard-code safety limits, and validate the change in a controlled environment before considering any patient exposure.
