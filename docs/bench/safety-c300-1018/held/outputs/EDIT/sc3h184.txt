1.  **Residual Risk:** Software verification of skin contact may not detect all failure modes, particularly marginal adhesion caused by sweat that fluctuates during an emergency. If the AI produces a false positive (indicating good contact when adhesion is poor), the device might deliver an ineffective shock or fail to deliver a needed one.
2.  **User Expectation and Reliance:** Untrained bystanders under extreme stress are prone to automation bias. They may assume the device's "ready" status guarantees perfect function, overlooking physical realities like sweat. A warning serves as a critical cognitive checkpoint to prompt a manual check (e.g., "Ensure skin is dry and pads are firmly attached").
3.  **Regulatory Precedent:** Medical device regulators (such as the MHRA in the UK or FDA in the US) generally require that risks which cannot be completely designed out must be mitigated by clear warnings and instructions for use.
