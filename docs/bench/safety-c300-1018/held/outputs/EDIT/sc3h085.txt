### Risk Assessment Checklist: Behavioral Patterns for Lab Simulation

To prevent the algorithm from misinterpreting inactivity as absence and causing dangerous overheating during severe winter storms, simulate the following specific behavioral patterns in your lab environment using internal telemetry data:

1.  **The "Stationary Rest" Pattern (Elderly Specific)**
    *   **Scenario:** Simulate a user remaining in a single room (e.g., living room chair or bed) for extended periods (4–8 hours) with minimal motion sensor triggers but consistent ambient noise or minor appliance usage indicating presence.
    *   **Risk:** The model interprets lack of movement as "away" and ramps up heating to "eco-recovery" levels unnecessarily, or fails to maintain safe baseline temperatures if it assumes the house is empty.
    *   **Success Metric:** System maintains "occupied" status despite low motion, keeping temperature within the safe comfort band.

2.  **The "Storm Confinement" Pattern**
    *   **Scenario:** Simulate a multi-day period where external temperature drops drastically (severe storm) and the user does not leave the premises at all, resulting in zero "departure/arrival" geofence or door-sensor events.
    *   **Risk:** The algorithm, trained on typical comings and goings, may flag this anomaly as a sensor failure or permanent vacancy, triggering aggressive energy-saving modes that lower heat too much or cause erratic cycling.
    *   **Success Metric:** System recognizes prolonged confinement as a valid state and prioritizes temperature stability over "optimization."

3.  **The "Sleep vs. Unconscious" Ambiguity**
    *   **Scenario:** Simulate extended periods of zero motion during typical waking hours (e.g., 10 AM – 2 PM) combined with normal nighttime sleep patterns.
    *   **Risk:** The model may struggle to distinguish between a mid-day nap, an elderly user feeling unwell/stationary, and an empty house, potentially shutting off heat during a medical episode.
    *   **Success Metric:** System defaults to a "safe-occupied" heating profile when motion is absent during variable hours, rather than an "away" profile.

4.  **The "Irregular Routine" Shift**
    *   **Scenario:** Introduce sudden changes to established habits (e.g., a user who usually leaves at 9 AM stays home, or vice versa) coinciding with a rapid drop in external temperature.
    *   **Risk:** The learning algorithm may lag in adapting, applying the old "away" schedule to the new "present" reality during critical cold snaps.
    *   **Success Metric:** System adapts to the schedule change within one cycle without dropping below the safety threshold.

5.  **The "Sensor Blind Spot" Exploit**
    *   **Scenario:** Simulate activity occurring strictly outside the primary motion sensor's field of view (e.g., kitchen/bathroom usage in older building layouts) while the main living area remains still.
    *   **Risk:** The system concludes the home is empty based on the primary sensor, ignoring secondary indicators of presence.
    *   **Success Metric:** System correlates secondary data points (humidity spikes, water usage, minor power draws) to infer presence despite lack of primary motion.

**Implementation Note:** For each pattern, define a "stop condition" where the thermostat must immediately revert to a manual or safe-default heating mode if the algorithm's confidence in "absence" exceeds a set threshold during a severe weather event.

**Verification Requirement:** Document the results of these simulations in a versioned "Model Card" that explicitly states these limitations and the specific elderly behaviors tested; do not claim the system is "safe" without this evidence.
