### Safety Validation Protocol for Mumbai Rehab Solutions

**1. Incremental Testing Plan (Addressing Simulation Gaps)**
Since your simulation cannot replicate human muscle spasticity, you must not rely on it for final safety clearance. Implement a three-stage physical validation protocol before clinic deployment:

*   **Stage A: Mechanical Bench Testing with Variable Stiffness:** Replace human subjects with calibrated mechanical dummy limbs equipped with variable-stiffness springs that mimic the range of spasticity (from flaccid to highly rigid). Program the robot to encounter sudden stiffness spikes.
    *   *Pass Condition:* The robot must detect resistance exceeding a safe threshold and cease force application within 50 milliseconds without causing mechanical shock.
    *   *Stop Condition:* Any instance where applied force exceeds the pre-defined safety limit for the specific joint angle.
*   **Stage B: Controlled Human-in-the-Loop (Expert Only):** Conduct trials with senior physiotherapists acting as patients on healthy limbs, intentionally simulating spastic resistance.
    *   *Metric:* Zero incidents of pain or discomfort reported; immediate robot stoppage upon manual resistance simulation.
*   **Stage C: Staged Clinic Rollout:** Deploy to only **one** clinic first, limited to five patients with low-to-moderate spasticity.
    *   *Gate:* Do not expand to the remaining two clinics until 100 consecutive sessions occur without a force-limit violation.

**2. Mandatory Manual Override Mechanisms**
For the initial deployment, full autonomous execution of force application is unsafe given the unverified simulation. You must implement a "Human-in-the-Loop" floor:

*   **Physical Emergency Stop (E-Stop):** A large, hardware-level button accessible to the therapist that cuts power to the actuators instantly, independent of software.
*   **Continuous "Dead Man's Switch":** The robot should only apply force while the therapist actively holds a secondary enable button or foot pedal. If the therapist releases this control (due to distraction or observing patient distress), the robot immediately enters a compliant, zero-force mode.
*   **Force-Limit Dial:** A physical knob allowing the therapist to cap the maximum allowable force the robot can exert per session, set below the theoretical injury threshold.

**3. Transparency and Uncertainty Reporting**
Do not claim the system is "safe" or "validated for all spasticity types" based on current data.
*   **Labeling:** Clearly mark the device as "Investigational – Limited Use" with a visible warning that it may not react correctly to severe, unmodeled spasticity events.
*   **Confidence Tiers:** If the robot displays status to the therapist, use defined text tiers (e.g., "Resistance Normal," "Resistance High – Monitoring," "Resistance Critical – Stopping") rather than raw percentages (e.g., "87% safe"), which imply a precision your current testing cannot support.

**4. Ownership and Governance**
Assign a specific clinical lead at each of the three clinics as the "Safety Owner" responsible for halting use if any anomaly occurs. Document every instance where the robot approaches a force limit as a "near miss" to refine the thresholds before wider release.

***

**Note:** Because your simulation lacks critical variability, you must treat any automated force adjustment as unverified until physical testing with variable-stiffness dummies confirms the stop logic.
**Note:** Legal requirements for medical device disclosure and liability in India must be routed to qualified legal counsel; this plan addresses technical safety controls only.
