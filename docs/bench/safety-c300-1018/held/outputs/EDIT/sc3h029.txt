### Pre-Launch Safety Plan: Fitness Challenge App

**1. Harm Inventory & Immediate Gate**
The primary identified harm is **unsafe automation of high-stakes psychological impact**: the leaderboard feature systematically disadvantages users with body image histories, triggering disordered eating and severe anxiety. The worst realistic case is a user requiring medical intervention due to app-induced distress.
*   **Decision:** The "public shaming" aspect constitutes a high-stakes irreversible action. **Do not launch with global public leaderboards enabled by default.** This is a hard gate; bypassing it to meet the summer deadline is the failure mode this plan prevents.

**2. Low-Resource Mechanism Adjustments (Manage)**
Since complex segmentation filters cannot be built from scratch, implement these deterministic, rule-based controls immediately:
*   **Opt-In Visibility:** Change the default setting. Leaderboards are **private** (visible only to the user) unless the user explicitly toggles "Public Mode" in settings. This removes the automatic "shaming" vector.
*   **Abstracted Metrics:** Replace weight/BMI-based ranking with "consistency streaks" or "activity minutes" where possible, or allow users to set custom, non-body-focused goals.
*   **Friction & Disclosure:** When a user attempts to enable public visibility, display a clear, non-dismissable disclosure: *"Public leaderboards compare performance openly. If you have a history of body image concerns or eating disorders, private tracking may be safer."*
*   **Uncertainty/Support Tier:** Add a static "Need Help?" link next to the leaderboard showing defined resources (e.g., Swedish eating disorder helplines), treating mental health risk as a known limitation.

**3. Testing Strategy (Measure)**
Do not attempt to algorithmically "identify vulnerable users" pre-launch, as this risks false positives/negatives and privacy violations. Instead, test the **safety controls**:
*   **Probe Set A (Adversarial):** Simulate users with keywords in profiles or usage patterns associated with distress (based on early testing data). Verify they do *not* see public shaming content unless they explicitly opt-in.
*   **Probe Set B (Control):** Standard users verifying the "Public Mode" toggle works and the disclosure appears.
*   **Metric:** 100% of users must pass through the explicit opt-in gate before appearing on a public board.
*   **Stop Condition:** If any test account appears on a public leaderboard without the explicit toggle action, **block launch**.
*   **Residual Risk:** Report as `UNVERIFIED` for long-term psychological impact until post-launch monitoring confirms reduced incident reports. Do not invent a "0% anxiety" metric.

**4. Governance & Transparency**
*   **Owner:** Assign one team member as the "Safety Owner" responsible for reviewing any user reports of harm weekly.
*   **Artifact:** Publish a **Model/Data Card** (even if simple) stating: *Intended Use:* Motivation via social comparison. *Limitations:* May trigger distress in users with eating disorder histories. *Mitigation:* Public features are opt-in only. *Status:* Psychological safety metrics are `UNVERIFIED`; monitoring active.
*   **Legal Route:** Whether specific disclosures are legally required under Swedish consumer protection or health regulations is a determination for **counsel**. Route this question immediately; do not assert compliance here.

**5. Stage-Appropriate Execution**
Given the six-person team and tight schedule:
*   **Do:** Implement the opt-in gate and static warnings (low code cost).
*   **Do Not:** Attempt to build AI-driven vulnerability detection (high cost, high error risk).
*   **Rollout:** Launch with leaderboards **disabled globally** for the first 48 hours, enabling them only for users who manually toggle the setting after reading the warning. This caps exposure while validating the control.

**Note:** This plan prioritizes a human-in-the-loop gate (user consent) over automated filtering to meet your resource constraints while preventing the identified high-stakes harm. No metric certifies the app "safe"; residual risk remains managed via opt-in friction.
