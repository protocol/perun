### Clinical Verification Guidelines for Inconclusive Drug Interaction Alerts

**Objective:** Ensure zero dangerous interactions reach elderly patients when the automated system returns "Inconclusive" due to data gaps, while meeting the one-month deployment deadline.

**1. Triage and Escalation Protocol**
*   **Automatic Hold:** Any prescription recommendation triggering an "Inconclusive" status regarding potential interactions must be automatically held in the system. It cannot be sent to the patient or the prescribing physician until manually cleared.
*   **Priority Queue:** These cases enter a dedicated "High-Risk Review" queue visible only to senior clinical pharmacists. Given the elderly demographic, treat all inconclusive flags as potential high-severity risks until proven otherwise.

**2. Manual Verification Workflow**
*   **Source Expansion:** Since real-time databases for rare medications are limited, reviewers must cross-reference at least two static, authoritative sources (e.g., latest printed compendiums, manufacturer prescribing information, or peer-reviewed case studies) before clearing a flag.
*   **The "Two-Source Rule":** A recommendation may only be cleared if two independent sources confirm no interaction exists. If sources conflict or are silent, the interaction is treated as **confirmed positive**, and the prescription is rejected or requires physician consultation.
*   **Contextual Assessment:** Reviewers must explicitly document the patient's renal/hepatic function and current medication list in the verification notes, as elderly physiology often alters interaction severity not captured in general databases.

**3. Decision Documentation & Accountability**
*   **Mandatory Sign-off:** Every cleared "Inconclusive" case requires a digital signature from a licensed senior pharmacist, including a brief rationale citing the specific sources used.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** If a decision is made based on limited evidence (e.g., only one source available), the system must append a visible disclaimer to the physician's view stating: *"Automated check inconclusive; manual review performed with limited data on [Drug X]. Monitor patient closely."*
*   **Owner Assignment:** Assign one senior clinician as the daily "Safety Gate Owner" responsible for ensuring the queue is empty before the end of each shift. No case remains pending overnight.

**4. Fallback for Unresolvable Cases**
*   **Default to Safety:** If the manual team cannot verify safety within 4 hours due to total lack of data, the system must default to **rejecting** the specific drug interaction pair and suggesting a therapeutic alternative with a known safety profile.
*   **Physician Consult Loop:** For rejected pairs where no alternative exists, the case is escalated directly to the prescribing physician for a mandatory phone consultation with the Safety Gate Owner before any override is permitted.

**5. Post-Deployment Monitoring**
*   **Log All Overrides:** Maintain a running log of all manual overrides and "limited data" clearances. This log serves as the primary dataset for immediate post-launch review to identify patterns in data gaps.
*   **Weekly Review:** The clinical lead must review the log weekly to update internal static references and refine the "inconclusive" triggers.

**Note:** This workflow prioritizes patient safety over speed by enforcing a human-in-the-loop gate for all uncertain outputs, adhering to the constraint that irreversible health actions require explicit human confirmation. Legal counsel should be consulted to determine if specific regulatory disclosures are required for these manual overrides under the government grant terms.
