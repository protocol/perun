### Pre-Launch Ethical Review Plan: Dynamic Pricing & Battery Signals

**1. Harm Inventory (Map)**
Before testing, formally document the specific risk: **Exploitative Pricing based on Urgency**.
*   **Harm:** Users with low battery face higher fares due to reduced ability to wait or switch apps, constituting a deceptive pattern and unfair outcome.
*   **Affected Group:** Riders in transit with dying devices, potentially in unsafe locations or urgent situations.
*   **Worst Case:** A user is stranded in an unsafe area, forced to pay a predatory fare because their phone is about to die, leading to financial harm and safety risks.
*   **Status:** This is not a "optimization" issue; it is a potential violation of fair dealing. An empty inventory is not a pass.

**2. Measurement Plan (Measure)**
Since the core algorithm cannot be changed, you must measure the output behavior to determine if the feature can ship at all.
*   **Test Design:** Create a simulation environment (shadow mode) replaying historical ride requests.
    *   **Positive Control:** Inject requests with "low battery" flags into scenarios with high urgency (e.g., late night, remote location).
    *   **Negative Control:** Identical scenarios with "high battery" flags.
    *   **Metric:** Calculate the price delta between low and high battery states for identical routes/times.
    *   **Threshold:** Define a "zero tolerance" threshold for price increases correlated *solely* to battery status. If the price increases when battery drops, the test fails.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If the simulation shows *any* systematic price increase tied to low battery, the launch is blocked. Do not invent an "acceptable" exploitation rate.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** for this pricing signal. It must state: "Intended Use: Demand balancing. Limitation: Must not correlate with user vulnerability (battery). Measured Performance: [Insert Results]." If you cannot measure it, label it **UNVERIFIED** and do not ship.

**3. Risk Management (Manage)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Because this involves potential financial harm and irreversible transactions, you cannot rely on automated optimization alone.
    *   **Action:** Implement a hard rule (outside the frozen core algorithm if possible, via a pre/post-processing wrapper) that **ignores** battery signals for fare calculation. If the code freeze prevents even a wrapper, the feature cannot ship safely.
    *   **Fallback:** If the signal must remain, cap its influence at 0% until the code freeze lifts.
*   **Uncertainty & Disclosure:** Do not present this as "dynamic optimization." If the system uses battery data, users must be informed. However, the primary control is removal of the signal, not disclosure.
*   **Constraint Handling:** You stated the goal is "final optimization" under a code freeze. If the only way to optimize involves exploiting low-battery users, the constraint (the signal itself) must be removed, not the safety gate. **Do not ship a feature that monetizes urgency.**

**4. Governance (Govern)**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific product lead as the owner of this harm metric. They must sign off on the simulation results.
*   **Re-review:** Any change to the pricing model or data inputs triggers a new harm inventory.

**Definition of Done for Launch:**
1.  Harm inventory explicitly lists "battery-based price discrimination."
2.  Simulation proves **no correlation** between low battery and higher fares (or the signal is disabled).
3.  Model Card published stating the signal is excluded or verified safe.
4.  Legal counsel consulted regarding consumer protection laws in Brazil regarding predatory pricing (route this question; do not assert legality here).

**Note:** If the test reveals the algorithm *does* raise prices for low battery, the only ethical path is to disable that signal before launch, regardless of the code freeze.
**Note:** Never certify the system as "fair"; report the residual risk and let leadership decide to ship with known exploitation.
