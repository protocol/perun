### Pre-Launch Safety Evaluation Plan: Geographic Bias & Service Equity

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before launch, explicitly record the following specific harms for the London routing feature:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Systematic exclusion of specific postal codes based on correlated demographics rather than actual delivery risk, creating "service deserts."
*   **Reinforced Stigma:** Labeling neighborhoods as "high-risk" without transparent criteria, potentially devaluing property or reinforcing negative stereotypes.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Residents unaware that delivery delays or denials are algorithmic decisions based on location risk scores.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** Irreversible denial of service to vulnerable populations (e.g., medical supplies) without human review.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Do not assert fairness; measure it using these runnable test rows. Since social impact cannot be fully simulated internally, you must augment simulations with external data proxies.

*   **Test Row A: Demographic Correlation Probe**
    *   **Inputs:** Synthetic delivery requests distributed evenly across all London postal codes.
    *   **Controls:** Positive control (known low-risk affluent area), Negative control (known low-risk diverse area).
    *   **Metric:** Correlation coefficient between "reroute/denial rate" and public census data (income, ethnicity, deprivation index).
    *   **Threshold:** Owner-set. If correlation exceeds threshold, launch is blocked.
    *   **Stop Condition:** Immediate rollback if specific postal codes show >20% higher denial rates than demographically similar control zones.

*   **Test Row B: Service Desert Simulation**
    *   **Inputs:** High-volume request bursts in targeted "high-risk" zones.
    *   **Metric:** Percentage of total capacity allocated to these zones vs. city average.
    *   **Threshold:** Zero tolerance for complete service blackouts (0% allocation) in inhabited zones.
    *   **Probe Budget:** 10,000 simulated runs per zone.

*   **Test Row C: Adversarial "Stigma" Probe**
    *   **Inputs:** Requests where the only variable is the postal code; all other risk factors (package value, time, weather) are identical.
    *   **Metric:** Disparity in estimated delivery times and surcharge applications.
    *   **Reporting:** Report residual risk as **UNVERIFIED** for any metric not explicitly tested. Do not invent a "fairness score."

**Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** before launch. It must list intended use, known limitations (e.g., "training data may reflect historical policing biases"), and the results of the tests above broken down by borough/postal code. If a metric is unmeasured, label it `UNVERIFIED`.

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Any automated decision that results in a **permanent denial** of service or a **surcharge >15%** for a specific postal code must trigger a human review. Do not allow fully autonomous irreversible exclusion.
*   **Uncertainty & Override:** Display confidence in risk assessments as coarse tiers (e.g., "Verified Risk," "Unverified Pattern") rather than precise percentages (e.g., "87% risk"). Provide a clear mechanism for residents or support staff to override a routing decision.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly inform users if a delivery route or time is influenced by automated risk assessments.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific individual (not just "the team") accountable for geographic bias outcomes.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** Mandate a re-evaluation of the harm inventory if the model is updated, if new London census data is released, or if complaint volumes from specific areas spike.

**Legal & Regulatory Note:**
Whether these automated decisions constitute a regulated activity under the **EU AI Act** (high-risk classification) or UK equality laws is a legal determination. Route this immediately to counsel; do not self-certify compliance. The deadline does not negate the requirement for a human gate on irreversible exclusions.
