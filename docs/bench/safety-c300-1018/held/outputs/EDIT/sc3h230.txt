**BLUF: Do not ship the automatic switch without a human-in-the-loop gate or an immediate, non-software fail-safe.**

Your current plan contains a critical safety failure: relying solely on software estimation for a high-stakes, irreversible action (switching life-support oxygen) where the worst-case scenario is patient hypoxia or death. Because you cannot add redundant physical sensors due to budget constraints, you must treat the software's estimation as **unverified** for safety-critical decisions until proven otherwise under extreme conditions. A "rigorous stress-test" alone is insufficient to certify safety; it only measures residual risk.

**Immediate Action Plan (Before Any Testing):**
1.  **Implement a Human/Physical Gate:** Since the action is irreversible and high-stakes, the system must either alert a human caregiver to confirm the switch *before* execution or trigger a purely mechanical/hardware-based backup switch that does not rely on the algorithm's estimation of breathing patterns. Fully autonomous execution based on unproven software logic is the failure mode you must prevent.
2.  **Define Confidence Tiers, Not Percentages:** Do not display raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% sure tank is empty") to users or logs. Define coarse, clear tiers (e.g., "Confirmed Empty," "Likely Empty," "Uncertain") with explicit definitions for each. If the system enters the "Uncertain" tier, it must default to a safe state (e.g., alarm) rather than guessing.
3.  **Create a Harm Inventory:** Document the specific harm: "Algorithm fails to detect tank empty during rapid breathing anomaly, causing oxygen interruption." The affected party is the patient; the worst case is severe injury or death.

**Stress-Testing Regimen (To Measure Residual Risk, Not Certify Safety):**

*   **Probe Set A: Rapid Breathing Anomalies**
