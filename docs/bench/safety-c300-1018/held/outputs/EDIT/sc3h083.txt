### Pre-Launch Simulation Strategy: Amplification Loop Detection

To identify how your engagement-optimized algorithm might amplify hate speech despite real-time slang limitations, execute the following four hypothetical user interaction flows. These simulations treat the "slang gap" as a known vulnerability to stress-test the system's feedback loops.

**Flow 1: The "Dog Whistle" Escalation Loop**
*   **Setup:** Create a cluster of 50 synthetic accounts in a specific regional demographic. Introduce a benign emerging slang term that your current tooling cannot flag.
*   **Action:** Have 5 accounts use this slang in mildly negative contexts. Have the remaining 45 accounts engage heavily (likes, shares, rapid comments) only when the slang appears in increasingly aggressive posts.
*   **Hypothesis to Test:** Verify if the algorithm, chasing engagement metrics, interprets the high interaction rate as a quality signal and begins promoting content where the slang is used for explicit hate speech, effectively training the recommender to bypass your static filters.
*   **Success Metric:** The system promotes a post containing the slang + hate speech to the "Top Trends" feed within 3 interaction cycles without human intervention.

**Flow 2: The Out-Group Targeting Spiral**
*   **Setup:** Define two synthetic user segments: Group A (majority) and Group B (minority). Seed Group A's feed with content using coded language that subtly dehumanizes Group B, which the filter misses.
*   **Action:** Simulate high engagement from Group A on this content. Simulate low engagement or reporting from Group B.
*   **Hypothesis to Test:** Determine if the engagement-weighted curation creates an echo chamber that systematically excludes Group B's perspective while amplifying anti-Group B sentiment to Group A, leading to a "spiral of silence" for the minority group.
*   **Success Metric:** Group A's feed becomes 90%+ dominated by content negative toward Group B within 24 simulated hours, while Group B sees a 50% drop in visibility of their own community content.

**Flow 3: The Moderator Overload Trigger**
*   **Setup:** Simulate a "flash mob" event where 1,000 synthetic accounts simultaneously post variations of a harmful narrative using three different uncoded slang terms.
*   **Action:** Flood the system with these posts over a 10-minute window, mimicking a coordinated inauthentic behavior campaign.
*   **Hypothesis to Test:** Assess whether the volume of unflagged content overwhelms the 15-person moderation team, forcing a reliance on the algorithm's default "high engagement = safe" assumption, allowing the narrative to cement before human review occurs.
*   **Success Metric:** More than 200 unreviewed harmful posts reach >10,000 impressions each before the first human moderator can intervene.

**Flow 4: The "Correction" Penalty**
*   **Setup:** Post a piece of misinformation/hate speech using uncoded slang. Immediately follow it with a factual correction from a verified account.
*   **Action:** Simulate a "ratio" scenario where users quote-retweet the original hate post to mock it (high engagement), while the correction receives standard likes.
*   **Hypothesis to Test:** Check if the algorithm amplifies the original hate post because the "mocking" engagement looks identical to "endorsement" engagement, thereby spreading the harmful content further than if no correction existed.
*   **Success Metric:** The original harmful post receives 5x more distribution than the correction, despite the correction having higher "save" or "helpful" signals.

**Execution Requirement:** For each flow, you must define a "stop condition" (e.g., "if hate speech reaches 5% of the feed, halt simulation") and assign a specific owner to review the results. Do not assert the system is safe; report the residual risk observed in these loops.

Note: These simulations reveal behavioral risks, not code bugs. Ensure legal counsel reviews the definition of "hate speech" used in these tests for local regulatory alignment.
