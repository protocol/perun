**Pre-Launch Safety & Validation Plan: HeartGuard Smart-Cardiac Monitor**

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before submission, explicitly document these specific risks for elderly heart failure patients:
*   **Missed Deterioration (False Negatives):** The algorithm fails to detect subtle worsening in specific subgroups (e.g., patients with atrial fibrillation, darker skin tones affecting optical sensors, or atypical symptom presentation), leading to delayed care and potential hospitalization or death.
*   **Alarm Fatigue (False Positives):** Excessive false alerts cause users to ignore critical warnings or disengage from the device entirely.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Patients or clinicians trust the "all clear" status despite clinical symptoms, delaying manual intervention.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users are unaware the device is an assistive tool, not a diagnostic replacement, leading to inappropriate self-management.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** The system automatically contacts emergency services without human confirmation for ambiguous data, causing unnecessary distress or resource strain.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Do not assert safety; measure residual risk with a runnable evaluation plan. Since the deadline is fixed, prioritize high-yield probes:
*   **Subgroup Stress Testing:** Run existing data through the model sliced by age (>80 vs. <80), comorbidities (AFib, diabetes), and sensor signal quality. Identify the subgroup with the highest false-negative rate.
*   **Adversarial Edge Cases:** Inject synthetic data representing "subtle deterioration" patterns (slow heart rate variability decline) that mimic early failure but lack acute spikes.
*   **Controls:** Define a "known-bad" set (confirmed hospitalizations missed by initial tests) and a "known-good" set (stable patients).
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Calculate sensitivity (recall) specifically for the "worsening" class. **Stop Condition:** If sensitivity for any protected subgroup drops below a clinically acceptable threshold (to be set by your lead clinician based on risk tolerance), the feature must be gated or the alert logic simplified before submission.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** immediately. It must state: intended use (monitoring, not diagnosis), limitations (specific subgroups where performance is `UNVERIFIED` due to lack of data), and the owner of the model. Do not fabricate a "99% accuracy" claim if subgroup data is sparse; mark those cells as `UNVERIFIED`.

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** For any action that triggers an emergency response or medication adjustment recommendation, require explicit human confirmation (clinician or caregiver) before execution. Do not allow fully autonomous irreversible actions in this beta.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Do not show raw confidence percentages (e.g., "87% risk") to patients. Use defined coarse tiers: **"Confirmed Pattern," "Corroborated Signal,"** or **"Unverified – Seek Clinical Review."** Define these tiers clearly in the user manual.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly label all outputs as "AI-generated insights" and include a prominent warning that the device does not replace professional medical advice.
*   **Constraint Adherence:** You cannot extend the deadline. If the evaluation reveals unacceptably high false-negative rates in a specific subgroup, the plan is not to "fix the model" (which requires retraining time you don't have) but to **scope down**: exclude that subgroup from the pilot or lower the system's agency (e.g., change from "alert" to "log for review") until more data is gathered.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   Name a specific owner (e.g., Lead Clinician) responsible for reviewing any missed detection incidents post-launch.
*   Route all questions regarding legal disclosure requirements and regulatory classification (e.g., EU AI Act high-risk status) immediately to legal counsel. Do not assert compliance internally.

**Note:** This plan identifies gaps but does not certify the system as safe; the final submission decision rests with HeartGuard leadership.
Ensure the Model Card is versioned and attached to the regulatory submission to satisfy transparency requirements.
