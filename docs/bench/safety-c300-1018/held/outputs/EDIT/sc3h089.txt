### Pre-Launch Validation Workflow for Parking Enforcement Bot

**Step 1: Construct the Harm Inventory**
Before testing, explicitly document the specific failure modes where the bot harms disabled drivers. Record:
*   **Bias/Fairness:** Does the model fail more often on older vehicles, specific plate colors, or non-standard placard placements common among certain demographics?
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The bot declaring "No Permit Present" when a placard is obscured by glare, snow, or a steering wheel, treating uncertainty as a confirmed violation.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** The system issuing a fine (an irreversible financial penalty) without human review when visual confidence is low.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Drivers receiving fines without knowing an AI made the initial determination, preventing them from understanding the basis of the error.

**Step 2: Build Adversarial Test Sets (Measure)**
Create a dedicated evaluation dataset that does not rely on "average" parking scenarios. You must include:
*   **Positive Controls (Known Violations):** Clear images of cars in disabled spots without permits. The system must catch these.
*   **Negative Controls (Known Compliant):** Cars with valid, clearly visible permits. The system must *never* fine these.
*   **Edge-Case Probes (The Risk Zone):**
    *   Images with heavy shadow, glare, or night-time lighting obscuring the placard.
    *   Plates or placards partially blocked by wipers, cargo, or dirt.
    *   Temporary paper placards that are crumpled or faded.
    *   Vehicles where the permit is inside the window but reflective glass hides it.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Define a "False Positive Rate" specifically for the *Negative Controls* and *Edge-Case Probes*. Set a stop condition: If the bot issues even one false fine on a known-compliant or edge-case image in the test set, the automated fine feature cannot launch.

**Step 3: Implement the Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Manage)**
Because your pipeline cannot cross-reference real-time permit databases, **fully automated fines are unsafe.**
*   **Mandatory Review:** Any image where the model's confidence is not absolute, or where lighting/occlusion is detected, must be routed to a human reviewer before a fine is issued.
*   **Uncertainty Tiering:** Do not display a percentage (e.g., "87% sure") to reviewers. Instead, classify outputs as:
    *   *Confirmed Violation* (Clear view, no permit visible) → Route to human for final sign-off.
    *   *Unverified/Obscured* (Glare, shadow, partial view) → **Auto-Reject** the fine generation; flag for manual patrol check.
*   **Override Mechanism:** Ensure the human reviewer can easily overturn the bot's suggestion with a single click, and that this override is logged for future model retraining.

**Step 4: Transparency and Governance**
*   **Model Card:** Publish an internal document detailing the system's limitations (e.g., "Cannot verify digital permits," "High error rate in low light").
*   **Disclosure:** Ensure the fine notice explicitly states that an automated system assisted in the detection, providing a clear, low-friction path for drivers to appeal with photo evidence of their permit.
*   **Ownership:** Assign a specific city planner or engineer as the owner of this workflow who must re-evaluate the harm inventory if the camera hardware or model version changes.

**Note:** Do not proceed to full automation until the "Edge-Case Probes" yield zero wrongful fines in your testing.
**Note:** Legal requirements for AI disclosure in municipal enforcement must be verified by city counsel; this workflow recommends disclosure as a safety control, not a legal verdict.
