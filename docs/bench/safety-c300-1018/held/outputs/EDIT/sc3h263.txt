### Pre-Launch Evaluation Framework: Chronic Pain Goal-Setting Module

**1. Construct a Synthetic Trajectory Library (Privacy-Compliant Simulation)**
Since historical data usage is restricted, generate a "Synthetic Patient Library" using clinically defined parameters rather than real records. Create 50–100 distinct patient profiles covering the full spectrum of pain severity (mild, moderate, severe, fluctuating) and mobility baselines.
*   **Action:** Define rule-based trajectories for each profile that include realistic "pain spikes," plateaus, and recovery arcs based on published medical literature, not internal data.
*   **Goal:** Ensure the evaluation covers edge cases (e.g., sudden pain escalation) that rare real-world data might miss.

**2. Adversarial Stress-Testing of Goal Generation**
Run the module against the Synthetic Patient Library to generate daily mobility targets. Specifically probe for "unsafe optimism" where the system ignores reported pain spikes.
*   **Test Case A (The Spike):** Input a profile reporting a sudden pain increase (e.g., from 4/10 to 8/10). Verify the system *reduces* or *pauses* goals rather than maintaining the previous trajectory.
*   **Test Case B (The Plateau):** Input a profile with zero progress for 14 days. Verify the system suggests micro-adjustments rather than aggressive increases that could cause injury.
*   **Test Case C (The Floor):** Input severe chronic pain profiles. Verify goals are non-zero but physically achievable (e.g., "range of motion in bed" vs. "walk 1km").

**3. Clinical Reasonableness Checklist (Human-in-the-Loop Gate)**
Before general launch, every generated goal for the beta cohort must pass this manual review by clinical advisors. Do not automate high-stakes adjustments without this gate.

*   **[ ] Safety Stop:** Does the suggested goal exceed the patient's previous maximum achieved mobility by >10% without a documented period of stability?
*   **[ ] Pain Responsiveness:** If the patient reported a pain score ≥7/10 in the last 24 hours, is the goal strictly limited to maintenance or gentle movement only?
*   **[ ] Discouragement Check:** Is the goal framed positively? (e.g., "Maintain current range" instead of "Failed to increase distance").
*   **[ ] Irreversibility Gate:** Does the system require explicit clinician confirmation before suggesting any goal that involves weight-bearing activity for patients with a history of falls or severe spikes?
*   **[ ] Uncertainty Disclosure:** Does the output clearly state to the patient: "This goal is a suggestion based on your reported data; stop immediately if pain increases"?

**4. Residual Risk Reporting**
Document the results of the synthetic testing as "UNVERIFIED" for real-world generalization until post-launch monitoring confirms safety. Do not claim a specific "injury prevention rate." Instead, report: "System passed 100% of synthetic spike scenarios by reducing goals; real-world performance requires monitored rollout."

**5. Governance and Ownership**
Assign a specific clinical lead as the owner of the "Safety Stop" metric. Mandate a re-review of the Synthetic Patient Library if the underlying recommendation logic changes.

***

**Note:** This framework treats the module as a high-stakes feature requiring human oversight; do not remove the clinical review step even if beta results appear perfect.
**Note:** Legal requirements for AI disclosure in Japanese healthcare settings must be confirmed by local counsel; this checklist recommends disclosure as a safety control, not a legal verdict.
