**Immediate Action Plan: Equitable Enforcement Checklist**

**1. Activate a Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Do Not Auto-Issue)**
Immediately disable fully automated fine issuance. Given the disproportionate impact on specific neighborhoods and the high-stakes nature of financial penalties, the system must transition to a "recommendation-only" mode. Every generated fine requires explicit human review and confirmation by a municipal officer before being sent to a resident. Full automation of irreversible financial actions is the primary failure point here; a human gate is the non-negotiable floor for safety, regardless of contract timelines.

**2. Construct a Rapid Harm Inventory**
Document the specific risk: "Bias/Unfair Outcomes" where the output systematically disadvantages residents in densely populated immigrant enclaves. Record the affected group (residents of specific zip codes/demographics) and the worst-case realistic scenario (systematic financial harassment of a protected class, legal liability, and loss of public trust). An empty inventory is not a pass; write this down now.

**3. Deploy Targeted Adversarial Testing (Measure, Don't Assert)**
Since you cannot retrain the model immediately, you must measure the current failure rate. Create a test set using "positive controls" (known noise events in affluent areas) and "negative controls" (ambient city noise in the flagged immigrant enclaves). Run these against your current threshold settings.
*   **Metric:** Disparity ratio of false positives between the two groups.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the false positive rate in the flagged enclaves exceeds the rate in control areas by any margin, the system fails the launch gate. Do not invent a "safe" threshold; if the disparity exists, the automation must remain off.

**4. Surface Uncertainty and Override Mechanisms**
Do not present the decibel reading as an absolute fact. The interface for the human reviewer must display confidence as a defined coarse tier (e.g., "Corroborated" vs. "Single-source/Unverified") rather than a precise percentage, which implies uncalibrated accuracy. Ensure the human reviewer has a one-click "Reject/Override" button that logs the reason for rejection. This data is critical for future auditing.

**5. Draft a Transparency Artifact (Model Card)**
Prepare a basic model card for internal and municipal stakeholder review. It must state:
*   **Intended Use:** Noise monitoring for fine recommendation (not final issuance).
*   **Known Limitations:** Explicitly state that current testing indicates potential bias against high-density immigrant neighborhoods and that demographic data tools are currently lacking.
*   **Status:** Mark the bias metric as **UNVERIFIED** until the adversarial testing above is complete. Never fabricate a "0% bias" claim.

**6. Route Legal and Regulatory Duties**
The question of whether this automated decision-making violates local anti-discrimination laws or the EU AI Act (given the high-risk classification of law enforcement automation) is a legal determination. Immediately route this finding to legal counsel and your business operations lead. Do not assert compliance yourself; name the regime (EU AI Act) and let counsel determine the binding obligations and disclosure duties.

**Note:** Contract deadlines do not override the requirement for a human gate on high-stakes financial penalties; if the contract forbids human review, the constraint itself must be renegotiated.
**Note:** This plan identifies residual risk; it does not certify the system as safe or unbiased.
