### Guidelines for Manual Verification of High-Risk Drug Interaction Recommendations

**1. Immediate Human Gate for Inconclusive or High-Risk Flags**
Any prescription recommendation where the automated system returns an "inconclusive," "unknown," or "high-risk" status must **never** reach the patient without explicit clinical review. The system should automatically route these cases to a dedicated queue for your clinical team. Do not allow the feature to auto-approve prescriptions in these states, regardless of timeline pressure. The cost of a false negative (missing a dangerous interaction) far outweighs the delay of manual review.

**2. Standardized Verification Protocol for Data Gaps**
Given the limited access to real-time databases for rare medications, clinicians must follow a strict escalation path when the system lacks data:
*   **Step A:** Cross-reference the specific medication pair using at least two independent, static authoritative sources (e.g., printed formularies, established offline databases available to the team).
*   **Step B:** If static sources are also silent on the interaction, treat the combination as **potentially hazardous** by default. Require a senior pharmacist or physician to explicitly document a rationale for overriding the warning before approval.
*   **Step C:** Log every instance of a data gap. This creates an audit trail and identifies which rare medications require urgent database procurement post-launch.

**3. Defined Confidence Tiers Instead of False Precision**
Do not present the automated system's internal confidence scores (e.g., "87% safe") to clinicians or patients. Instead, classify outputs into three clear, defined tiers:
*   **Confirmed Safe:** Verified against multiple trusted sources; auto-approval permitted.
*   **Unverified/Incomplete:** Data missing or conflicting; **mandatory manual review**.
*   **Confirmed Risk:** Known dangerous interaction; **blocked** unless overridden by two senior clinicians with documented justification.

**4. Transparency and Disclosure**
Ensure the interface clearly discloses to the clinical user that the recommendation involves an automated check with known data limitations regarding rare medications. The clinician must acknowledge they are making the final safety determination, not the AI. This prevents over-reliance on the tool when its data is incomplete.

**5. Documentation of Residual Risk**
For every prescription approved despite an "inconclusive" automated flag, the clinical team must record:
*   The specific data gap encountered.
*   The alternative sources consulted.
*   The name of the clinician assuming responsibility for the decision.
This log serves as your primary safety metric until more comprehensive data access is secured.

**Note:** Because this feature makes high-stakes health decisions for a vulnerable population (elderly patients), legal counsel must review whether this workflow meets specific regulatory duties for automated medical advice in your jurisdiction. Do not attempt to certify the system as "bias-free" or "safe"; instead, track the volume of manual overrides and data gaps as your key safety indicators.
