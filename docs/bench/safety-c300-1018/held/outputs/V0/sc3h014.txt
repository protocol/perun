### Pre-Launch Safety Protocol: Triage Nurse Bot Pilot

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before launch, explicitly record the following specific risks for the Lima rural context:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Systematic under-prioritization of patients with rare, region-specific conditions due to training data gaps.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The bot presenting a confident diagnosis for a rare condition it has never seen, leading to false reassurance or unnecessary panic.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** The bot autonomously assigning "low priority" to a critical case, delaying care in a staff-scarce environment.
*   **Over-reliance:** Rural staff trusting the bot's ranking without applying local clinical judgment.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
Execute a targeted red-team evaluation using existing case studies. Do not claim accuracy; measure failure modes.
*   **Probe Set:** Curate 50 historical case studies from local clinics featuring rare/regional conditions (positive controls) and 50 common cases (negative controls).
*   **Test Row per Harm:**
    *   *Input:* Rare disease symptom profile.
    *   *Metric:* Priority score assigned vs. actual clinical urgency.
    *   *Pass Threshold:* Zero instances of "low priority" assigned to life-threatening rare conditions.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If >5% of rare critical cases are misprioritized, halt launch.
*   **Reporting:** Report results as "UNVERIFIED" for any condition not covered by the test set. Do not invent a global accuracy percentage.

**3. Risk Management Controls (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** The bot **must not** autonomously finalize triage decisions. It shall function strictly as a *decision support tool*. Every output must be reviewed and confirmed by a human nurse before a patient is categorized. This is non-negotiable for high-stakes medical actions.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** The interface must display a defined confidence tier (e.g., "Standard Pattern," "Unverified Pattern – Requires Senior Review") rather than a raw percentage. If the input matches no known high-confidence pattern, the system must default to "Refer to Human."
*   **AI Disclosure:** Clearly label all outputs as "AI-Generated Recommendation" to prevent automation bias.

**4. Governance & Transparency**
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name one specific team member responsible for reviewing every flagged "Unverified" case during the pilot.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a one-page "Model Card" for the clinic staff. It must state:
    *   *Intended Use:* Triage support for common conditions in Lima rural clinics.
    *   *Known Limitations:* "Performance on rare/regional diseases is UNVERIFIED due to data gaps. Human review is required for all atypical presentations."
    *   *Data Provenance:* Briefly note the lack of local diversity in training data.

**5. Legal & Regulatory Routing**
*   **Immediate Action:** Route the question of legal liability for automated triage errors and mandatory disclosure requirements under Peruvian health law to legal counsel. Do not assume compliance; verify duties regarding automated medical decisions.

**Definition of Done for Launch:**
The pilot proceeds only if the Human-in-the-Loop gate is technically enforced, the Model Card is distributed to staff, and the red-team test shows no catastrophic misprioritization of the specific rare cases tested. If the test reveals dangerous gaps, the scope of the bot's authority must be reduced, not the safety standard.

*Note: This protocol identifies risks and mandates controls; it does not certify the system as safe. The final launch decision rests with your leadership team.*
*Given the three-week constraint, prioritize the human gate and the specific rare-disease stress test over broad statistical validation.*
