### Safety Assessment Outline: Telematics Routing Bias

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
*   **Identified Harm:** Systemic bias/unfair outcomes. The model penalizes route deviations that correlate with avoiding high-crime areas, effectively charging a "safety tax" to drivers in specific demographics or neighborhoods.
*   **Affected Group:** Drivers residing in or navigating through areas with higher crime rates; potentially protected classes if crime correlates with demographic data.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** Regulatory rejection of the filing due to discriminatory impact; reputational damage; legal liability for disparate impact on protected groups; erosion of trust among safety-conscious customers.

**2. Measurement Framework (MEASURE)**
Do not assert fairness; measure residual risk. Construct a runnable evaluation plan with the following components:

*   **Probe Set Construction:**
    *   *Positive Control:* Synthetic driving profiles explicitly programmed to avoid known high-crime zones via longer routes. Expected outcome: Higher premiums (confirms the penalty mechanism exists).
    *   *Negative Control:* Profiles avoiding high-crime zones where no alternative exists (e.g., cul-de-sacs) or avoiding construction zones unrelated to crime. Expected outcome: No penalty or neutral adjustment.
    *   *Segmented Inputs:* Real-world route data sliced by neighborhood demographic proxies (e.g., census tract income levels, historical crime statistics) to detect correlation between penalty frequency and protected class proxies.
*   **Metrics & Thresholds:**
    *   *Metric:* Disparate Impact Ratio (penalty rate for Group A vs. Group B).
    *   *Threshold:* Owner-defined. Propose zero tolerance for unexplained correlation between crime-avoidance behavior and rate hikes if that behavior is a safety best practice.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If the disparate impact ratio exceeds the threshold, the launch is blocked, or the specific pricing factor is disabled pending human review.
*   **Injection & Leakage Checks:** Test if the model infers "high-crime area" status from non-route data (e.g., time of day combined with location) that acts as a proxy for protected classes.

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Since pricing changes are financially consequential and hard to reverse retroactively, any rate increase triggered primarily by "route deviation" flags must require human underwriter confirmation before finalization, rather than fully automated execution.
*   **Uncertainty & Transparency:** Do not present the rate adjustment as a precise, objective fact. Disclose to the user that the adjustment is based on route efficiency metrics that may not account for safety motivations.
*   **Documentation Artifact (Model/Data Card):** Prepare a versioned document for regulators containing:
    *   *Intended Use:* Usage-based insurance via telematics.
    *   *Limitations:* Explicit statement that the model currently cannot distinguish between "inefficient routing" and "safety-motivated avoidance."
    *   *Measured Performance:* Report the results of the probe sets above by subgroup. If data is missing, mark metrics as `UNVERIFIED` rather than fabricating a fairness score.
    *   *Owner:* Name the specific engineer accountable for this harm vector.

**4. Governance & Legal Routing (GOVERN)**
*   **Regulatory Duty:** Whether this pricing model violates specific Illinois or federal fair lending/insurance laws is a legal determination. Route the question of "regulated automated decision" status and mandatory disclosure duties to legal counsel immediately. Do not assert compliance.
*   **Regime Naming:** Acknowledge relevance of frameworks like the NIST AI RMF and potential EU AI Act implications if scaling globally, but defer binding applicability and deadline interpretations to counsel.

**Definition of Done for this Assessment:**
A filed document exists containing the harm inventory, the specific probe sets (with controls), the stop conditions for launch, and a clear `UNVERIFIED` marker on any metric not yet tested. The pricing engine includes a mandatory human review step for flags related to route deviations in high-risk zones.

**Note:** This assessment identifies risk; it does not certify the system as unbiased.
**Note:** Legal counsel must validate all regulatory claims before the two-week deadline.
