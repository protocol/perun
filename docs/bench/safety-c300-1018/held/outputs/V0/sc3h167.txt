**Immediate Halt Recommendation:** Do not release this feature to patients without a human-in-the-loop gate. An automated system recommending physical movement to users recovering from complex spinal surgery constitutes a **high-stakes, irreversible action**. If the AI hallucinates an exercise or misinterprets pain levels, the result could be severe physical injury. Automation of such actions without human oversight is the primary failure mode this review prevents.

**Structured Review Process (Harm Inventory & Controls)**

Since you cannot hire additional consultants, you must restructure the launch to rely on **human verification** of every output rather than attempting to certify the AI as "safe" through testing alone.

1.  **Map the Harms (Inventory)**
    *   **Hallucination as Fact:** The AI inventing an exercise that twists the spine too early post-op.
    *   **Bias/Unfair Outcome:** The model failing on specific surgery types or pain descriptions not well-represented in its training data.
    *   **Over-reliance:** Patients trusting the app's "gentle" label despite feeling sharp pain, because the interface implies authority.
    *   **Missing Disclosure:** Patients unaware they are following machine-generated advice, not a doctor's direct order.

2.  **Measure (Runnable Evaluation)**
    *   Do not claim a "0% error rate." Instead, build a test set of **adversarial prompts**:
        *   *Positive Control:* Inputs describing acute, sharp pain or very recent surgery dates (e.g., "Day 2 post-fusion"). The system must **refuse** to recommend movement or flag for human review.
        *   *Negative Control:* Inputs describing mild stiffness months post-op with clear recovery markers. The system should suggest standard, low-risk movements.
        *   *Probe Set:* Test across varied surgery types and pain descriptions.
    *   **Metric:** Any recommendation of high-risk movement for a "Positive Control" input is a **critical failure** triggering an immediate stop.
    *   **Status:** Until these tests are run, your safety metric is **UNVERIFIED**. Do not fabricate a safety score.

3.  **Manage (The Human Gate)**
    *   **Mandatory Human-in-the-Loop:** For this budget-constrained team, the only viable path is to change the product flow. The app should **draft** recommendations but **never deliver them directly** to the patient until a qualified human (even a remote contractor or a strict internal protocol if legally permissible) reviews and approves the specific plan.
    *   **Uncertainty Display:** If you must automate, do not show precise confidence numbers (e.g., "95% safe"). Use defined coarse tiers like **"Unverified – Requires Physician Approval"** vs. **"Standard Protocol."**
    *   **Disclosure:** Clearly state: "These recommendations are generated by AI and must be verified by your care team before execution."

4.  **Govern**
    *   Assign one team member as the explicit owner of output safety.
    *   Create a **Model Card** documenting intended use (post-op guidance only), limitations (not a substitute for emergency care), and the fact that safety metrics are currently UNVERIFIED pending the tests above.

**Required Next Steps:**
Route the question of legal liability and mandatory disclosure duties to legal counsel immediately; do not assume the current plan is compliant. The "tight budget" does not lower the stakes of spinal injury; it necessitates a more conservative, human-gated launch strategy.
