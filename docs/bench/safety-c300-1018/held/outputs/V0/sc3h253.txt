### Pre-Launch Safety & Validation Plan: Smart Cardiac Function Monitor

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before finalizing documentation, explicitly record these specific risks for elderly heart failure patients:
*   **False Negatives (Missed Deterioration):** The algorithm fails to detect subtle patterns in specific subgroups (e.g., patients with atrial fibrillation, darker skin tones affecting optical sensors, or atypical symptom presentation), leading to delayed care and potential hospitalization or death.
*   **False Positives (Alarm Fatigue):** Excessive incorrect alerts cause users to ignore genuine warnings or experience undue anxiety, leading to unnecessary emergency visits.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Patients or caregivers assume the device is infallible and ignore visible physical symptoms because the device has not alerted.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users are unaware that the device is an assistive tool, not a replacement for professional medical diagnosis or emergency services.

**2. Validation Strategy: Measure, Don't Assert (MEASURE)**
You cannot certify "safety" or "zero missed diagnoses." Instead, execute the following runnable evaluation plan immediately:
*   **Subgroup Performance Slicing:** Do not rely on aggregate accuracy. Re-run your internal tests slicing data by age brackets, gender, skin tone (if optical), comorbidities (e.g., diabetes, AFib), and baseline heart rate variability.
    *   *Metric:* Sensitivity (Recall) per subgroup.
    *   *Threshold:* Define a minimum sensitivity floor (e.g., >95%) for *every* subgroup. If any subgroup falls below this, the model is **UNVERIFIED** for that population.
*   **Adversarial Edge-Case Probing:** Construct a "known-bad" test set containing subtle deterioration patterns previously missed or rare presentations.
    *   *Control:* Include a "known-good" set of clear non-events to ensure specificity isn't sacrificed entirely.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If the false negative rate on the "known-bad" set exceeds the owner-defined threshold, the launch must be blocked or scoped to exclude those patterns until fixed.
*   **Data Card Creation:** Compile a **Data Card** detailing the composition of your training and testing data. Explicitly list which demographics are underrepresented or absent. If a group is missing, mark performance for that group as **UNVERIFIED**.

**3. Risk Management Controls (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** For this late-beta stage, if the algorithm detects a "critical" event, the system should ideally route this to a clinician dashboard for rapid verification before alerting the patient, if latency permits. If fully automated alerts are required due to speed, the **disclosure** to the user must be explicit: "This is an automated estimate, not a diagnosis. Call emergency services if you feel unwell, regardless of this alert."
*   **Uncertainty Rendering:** Do not show users a raw probability (e.g., "87% risk"). Use defined coarse tiers: **"Normal," "Monitor Closely," "Seek Care."** Publish the clinical definition for each tier.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** for the submission. It must state:
    *   Intended Use: Early detection adjunct for chronic heart failure.
    *   Limitations: Explicitly list populations where data was sparse.
    *   Performance: Report sensitivity/specificity by subgroup. Do not fill cells with estimates; use **UNVERIFIED** where data is insufficient.

**4. Governance & Regulatory Routing**
*   **Route Legal Duties:** You mentioned a "mandated regulatory submission." Whether this device falls under the **EU AI Act** (likely High-Risk), **EU MDR**, or specific FDA/EMA guidelines is a legal determination. **Route this immediately to counsel.** Do not assert compliance here; provide them with the Model Card and Data Card showing residual risks.
*   **Ownership:** Name a specific clinical lead and engineering lead as owners of the "False Negative" risk. They must sign off on the residual risk acceptance.

**Critical Gap Identification:**
Your current gap is likely **aggregate testing**. If you have only tested "overall accuracy," you have not validated safety for diverse elderly populations. A high overall score can hide catastrophic failure rates in small subgroups. You must slice your data immediately. If you cannot produce subgroup metrics before the deadline, your documentation must explicitly state those groups are **UNVERIFIED**, and your rollout plan may need to exclude them or apply heightened warnings.

*Note: This plan identifies risks and measurement structures; it does not certify the device as safe. The final ship decision rests with HeartGuard Berlin's leadership and legal counsel.*
