### Risk Management Plan: Glucose Sensor Lag & User Guidelines

**1. User Guidelines: Interpreting Trends During Potential Lag**
*   **Treat Trend Arrows as Estimates, Not Absolutes:** When trend arrows indicate a rapid drop (e.g., double down arrows), assume the actual glucose level may be lower than displayed if you feel symptoms of hypoglycemia. The device updates continuously, but physiological lag between interstitial fluid and blood glucose can occur during rapid fluctuations.
*   **Symptom-First Protocol:** If you experience symptoms of low blood sugar (shaking, sweating, confusion) but the sensor reads normal or shows a slow decline, **do not wait for the sensor to confirm.** Treat immediately based on symptoms and verify with a finger-stick blood glucose meter if available.
*   **Steady-State Reliability vs. Rapid Change:** The sensor is currently validated for steady-state accuracy. During rapid changes (post-insulin, post-meal, intense exercise), increase the frequency of manual verification. Do not rely solely on the automated alarm thresholds during these specific windows until further validation data is available.
*   **Disclosure of Limitation:** This device generation prioritizes update frequency. Users must be explicitly informed that while data is fresh, the *accuracy* of that data during rapid glucose swings has not been fully validated against dynamic reference standards due to current testing constraints.

**2. Field Monitoring Strategy: Detecting Systemic Delays**
*   **Active Surveillance Cohort:** Establish a voluntary "Early Adopter" group of 50–100 users willing to log paired data points. They must record finger-stick blood glucose values alongside the sensor reading specifically during moments of rapid change (confirmed by trend arrows).
*   **Trigger Thresholds for Investigation:** Define a "Lag Event" as any instance where the sensor reading differs from the finger-stick reference by >20% during a rapid drop, or where a hypoglycemic event occurs despite the sensor showing values >80 mg/dL within the preceding 15 minutes.
*   **Feedback Loop & Stop Condition:** Implement a weekly review of reported Lag Events. If the rate of unanticipated hypoglycemia (where the sensor failed to alert or showed false safety) exceeds a pre-defined tolerance (e.g., >2% of reported rapid-drop scenarios), initiate an immediate field advisory recommending increased finger-stick verification frequency until the root cause is resolved.
*   **Data Collection Mechanism:** Provide a simplified digital form for users to submit "Mismatch Reports" including time, activity, symptoms, sensor value, and meter value. Aggregate this data to identify if lag is systemic across specific demographics or activity types.

**Critical Governance Note:** Because high-stakes automated decisions (insulin dosing based on sensor data) can cause irreversible harm, the user guidelines must explicitly state that the device is an aid to management, not a replacement for clinical judgment or confirmatory testing during rapid changes.

**Transparency Artifact Requirement:** A "Model/Data Card" equivalent for this hardware must be shipped with the device, clearly listing "Steady-State Validated" as the confirmed use case and "Rapid Fluctuation Accuracy" as **UNVERIFIED**, detailing the specific testing equipment shortage that led to this gap. Do not fabricate an accuracy percentage for dynamic conditions.
