### Rapid Assessment and Mitigation Plan for Age-Based Bias

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory & Scope**
Define the specific harm: "Systematic exclusion of workers over age X from high-value or sufficient shifts despite availability and acceptable performance." The affected group is older drivers; the worst-case scenario is regulatory action, reputational damage, and livelihood loss for a protected demographic.

**2. Rapid Measurement Plan (Run on Simulation Data)**
Execute these three specific analyses on your recent simulation logs immediately. Do not rely on aggregate averages.

*   **Segmented Allocation Rates:** Calculate the percentage of requested shifts successfully assigned, broken down by age brackets (e.g., <30, 30–45, 45–60, 60+). Compare the allocation rate of the oldest bracket against the youngest. A disparity greater than 5% without a performance-based justification is a critical flag.
*   **Performance-Controlled Comparison:** Filter your data to include *only* workers with "High" or "Medium" performance scores. Within this filtered set, re-calculate the shift allocation rates by age. If older high-performers receive fewer shifts than younger high-performers, the bias is likely embedded in the model's weighting of age-correlated features rather than actual performance.
*   **Shift Quality Distribution:** Analyze not just *if* a shift was assigned, but *which* shifts. Compare the average earnings potential, distance, or time-of-day desirability of shifts assigned to older vs. younger workers. Systematic assignment of less desirable shifts to older workers constitutes a harmful outcome even if total shift counts appear equal.

**3. Immediate Mitigation Controls (Pre-Launch)**
Since the core algorithm cannot be rebuilt, apply these external controls before going live:

*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (High-Stakes Filter):** Implement a rule that flags any worker over the identified risk age who receives zero shifts or significantly below-average shift quality in a scheduling cycle. These cases must trigger a manual review by a human operator before the schedule is finalized and published. This prevents irreversible harm (loss of income) while the model is monitored.
*   **Post-Hoc Rebalancing Script:** Write a deterministic script that runs *after* the AI generates the schedule but *before* it is shown to users. If the analysis shows older workers are under-assigned, this script should manually override the allocation to inject shifts for these workers from the unassigned pool or by swapping with voluntary opt-ins, ensuring a minimum floor of opportunity.
*   **Uncertainty & Disclosure:** Do not present the schedule as "optimized" or "final" without qualification. Internally, tag these allocations as "Automated Draft – Subject to Review." If possible, provide a mechanism for drivers to appeal a lack of shifts, ensuring a human reviews appeals from the affected age group with priority.

**4. Governance & Next Steps**
Assign one owner to monitor the daily allocation metrics by age bracket post-launch. If the disparity persists despite the rebalancing script, you must be prepared to disable the automated allocator for the affected demographic and revert to manual assignment for that segment. Do not certify the system as "unbiased"; instead, report the residual risk and the effectiveness of the human gate.

**Note:** This plan identifies risk and applies a safety layer; it does not prove the absence of bias.
