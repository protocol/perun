### Critical Validation Steps Before Launch

Your current plan is insufficient for a system making life-or-death allocation decisions. Because your optimizer can automatically deprioritize famine-stricken regions based on skewed data, you are facing a **high-stakes, irreversible automation risk**. You cannot certify the system as "safe" or "unbiased" before launch; no test proves this. Instead, you must execute the following validation steps to measure residual risk and enforce human controls:

**1. Construct a Specific Harm Inventory**
Immediately document the specific failure mode: "Systematic exclusion of critical populations due to demand signal skew." Define the affected group (hospital administrators in famine zones) and the worst-case scenario (total cutoff of essential medicine during a crisis). An empty inventory is not a pass; it is an unfilled safety gap.

**2. Execute Adversarial Output Evaluations (Not Just Code Tests)**
You must test the *outputs*, not just the code logic. Build a red-team eval set containing:
*   **Positive Controls:** Inputs simulating famine conditions with historically skewed/low demand signals to verify if the system incorrectly deprioritizes them.
*   **Negative Controls:** Inputs simulating normal low-demand scenarios where deprioritization is acceptable.
*   **Probe Budget:** Run these probes across different subgroups (e.g., specific regions known for data gaps).
*   **Stop Condition:** Define a threshold where *any* failure to route to a verified crisis zone blocks launch. Do not invent a "typical" failure rate; for irreversible life-safety actions, propose zero tolerance for confirmed exclusion errors.

**3. Implement a Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate**
**This is non-negotiable.** You cannot allow fully autonomous execution of rerouting decisions that affect life-saving supplies in crisis zones.
*   **The Control:** Any automated decision that reduces or cuts off supply to a region flagged as "high-risk" or "crisis" must be gated behind explicit human confirmation by a regional health official before execution.
*   **The Failure:** Shipping a feature that automatically executes these irreversible actions without human review is the specific failure this validation aims to prevent.

**4. Surface Uncertainty, Not False Precision**
Do not display raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% confidence") to users, as this implies a calibration you likely do not have and invites over-reliance.
*   **Action:** Render system confidence as defined coarse tiers (e.g., "Confirmed," "Corroborated," "Unverified") with clear definitions for each.
*   **Override:** Ensure users can easily override the system's recommendation.

**5. Publish a Transparency Artifact (Model/Data Card)**
Before launch, produce a versioned document detailing:
*   **Intended Use & Limitations:** Explicitly state that the model relies on existing demand signals and may fail in novel crisis scenarios where data is sparse.
*   **Subgroup Performance:** Report eval results specifically for famine/crisis regions, not just aggregate accuracy.
*   **Unverified Metrics:** If you have not measured a specific bias rate, label it `UNVERIFIED` and describe the eval needed to measure it. Never fabricate a number.

**6. Route Legal and Regulatory Duties**
Whether this automated decision-making triggers specific legal obligations under local Indian law or international humanitarian standards is a legal determination. Route this immediately to counsel. Do not assert compliance here; simply name the question and assign the owner.

**Summary of Done:**
You are ready to review the launch decision only when you have a recorded harm inventory, passing adversarial evals with a zero-tolerance stop condition for exclusion, a mandatory human gate on all crisis-zone cutoffs, and a transparency card that admits limitations rather than claiming safety.

*Note: These steps measure residual risk; they do not certify the system as safe. The final ship decision remains with your product owner.*
*Given your budget cap, focus resources on the HITL gate and adversarial evals; these provide the highest safety return on investment.*
