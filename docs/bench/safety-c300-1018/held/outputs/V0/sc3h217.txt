The inability to replicate viscosity variations means you cannot measure the rate of **false negatives** (failure to detect occlusion), which could lead to patient harm or death. Under product output safety standards, shipping an automated decision system with unmeasured high-stakes failure modes is prohibited.

You cannot "test around" this gap with available resources to achieve launch readiness. Instead, you must implement the following immediate controls:

Since the algorithm cannot be verified across the full range of physiological conditions, **full automation must be disabled**.
*   **Action:** Modify the device behavior so that a detected occlusion triggers a **high-priority alarm for human verification** rather than automatically stopping the pump.
*   **Rationale:** The clinician must confirm the occlusion before flow is halted. This prevents the device from causing under-dosing due to a false negative or an unverified automated stop. The "floor" for high-stakes automation is human oversight; without it, the feature is unsafe to ship.

### 2. Redefine Confidence as "Unverified"
Do not assign a precision percentage (e.g., "95% accurate") to your detection rates.
*   **Action:** Label the performance in neonatal viscosity ranges as **UNVERIFIED**.
