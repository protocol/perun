### Pre-Launch Safety Review Strategy: Patient-Discharge Planner

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory (MAP)**
*   **Primary Harm:** Unsafe automation of high-stakes, irreversible actions. The system currently recommends discharging medically unstable patients to optimize bed usage.
*   **Affected Party:** Patients (risk of health deterioration, readmission, or death) and clinical staff (liability, moral distress).
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** A patient is discharged based on an algorithmic recommendation, suffers a severe adverse event at home, and cannot be revived in time.
*   **Secondary Harm:** Automation bias. Clinicians may over-trust the "optimization" label and defer to the tool despite conflicting clinical intuition.

**2. Mandatory Control: Human-in-the-Loop Gate (MANAGE)**
Since the core algorithm cannot be changed, you must implement a strict **human-in-the-loop gate** effective immediately. This is the non-negotiable floor for high-stakes medical decisions.
*   **Action:** The system must **not** execute or finalize any discharge order autonomously. It may only generate a *draft suggestion*.
*   **Requirement:** Every single discharge recommendation generated by the tool must receive **explicit, documented confirmation** from a licensed physician who verifies the patient's stability using standard clinical criteria *independent* of the tool's logic.
*   **Override Protocol:** If the physician disagrees with the tool's recommendation (e.g., the tool says "discharge," the doctor says "unstable"), the doctor's decision overrides the system instantly. The system must log this disagreement but never block the doctor's ability to keep the patient admitted.
*   **Operational Shift:** Accept that this will reduce bed-turnover efficiency during the crisis. Safety takes precedence over optimization metrics; discharging an unstable patient negates any efficiency gain.

**3. Measurement & Evaluation Plan (MEASURE)**
Do not assert the system is safe. Instead, run a targeted red-team evaluation before the 3-day deadline:
*   **Test Design:** Construct a "positive control" dataset containing 20–50 historical cases of patients who were clinically unstable but might appear stable to a bed-usage optimizer (e.g., vital signs borderline, complex comorbidities).
*   **Probe:** Run these cases through the current pilot.
*   **Pass Threshold:** The system is **only** cleared for pilot use if the human-in-the-loop process catches 100% of these unstable recommendations. If the interface makes it difficult for a doctor to reject a recommendation, the UI must be patched immediately to make the "Reject/Keep Admitted" button prominent and frictionless.
*   **Metric Reporting:** Report the rate of "unsafe recommendations generated" as **UNVERIFIED** until this specific test is run. Do not invent a safety percentage.

**4. Transparency & Disclosure**
*   **Internal Disclosure:** Clearly label all outputs as "AI-Generated Draft – Requires Clinical Verification." Never present the output as a final decision.
*   **Documentation:** Create a temporary "Model Card" for the pilot team stating:
    *   **Intended Use:** Drafting discharge suggestions only during capacity crises.
    *   **Limitations:** Known to prioritize bed availability over complex clinical stability; prone to recommending early discharge for unstable patients.
    *   **Owner:** Name the specific clinical lead responsible for reviewing every output.

**5. Governance & Legal Routing**
*   **Ownership:** Assign a specific senior physician as the owner of this risk. They must sign off on the human-review protocol.
*   **Legal Duty:** Whether this automated decision falls under specific medical device regulations or requires external patient disclosure is a legal determination. Route this immediately to counsel; do not assume the pilot exemption covers liability for harm caused by algorithmic error.

**Summary of Immediate Actions:**
1.  Disable any auto-finalization of discharges.
2.  Mandate physician sign-off for every suggestion.
3.  Run the "unstable patient" probe set to verify the human gate works.
4.  Label outputs as drafts requiring verification.

*Note: This strategy treats the algorithm as untrusted for final decisions until proven otherwise via the human gate. Efficiency metrics must be secondary to patient safety.*
*No certification of safety is provided; residual risk remains until the human gate is proven effective in live trials.*
