Based on the details you provided, your current plan contains a critical failure: **fully automating high-stakes, irreversible actions (claim denials) without a human-in-the-loop gate.**

In healthcare, denying a high-value claim is a high-stakes action with potential severe consequences for patients. Shipping a feature that executes these denials autonomously, specifically because you cannot add human reviewers due to budget caps, violates the fundamental safety floor for this type of system. The inability to staff human reviewers is a business constraint, but it does not negate the risk of harm to your users. If the model hallucinates, misinterprets a complex medical code, or acts on biased training data, a patient could be denied critical care with no immediate recourse.

To proceed responsibly, you must:

1.  **Implement a Human Gate:** You cannot launch with fully automated denials for high-value requests. Since you cannot add new staff, you must redesign the workflow so that *any* denial above your threshold is flagged for review by existing staff, even if this delays some decisions. Automation should assist, not replace, human judgment in these scenarios.
2.  **Create a Harm Inventory:** Before launch, explicitly document potential harms: bias against specific demographics, hallucinations leading to incorrect denials, and the impact of over-reliance on the bot. Record who is affected and the worst-case scenario for each.
3.  **Measure, Don't Assert:** You need to run adversarial tests (red-teaming) on your denial logic using edge cases and known failure modes. Report the residual risk found. Do not invent a "0% error rate" or "100% fair" metric; if you haven't measured it across all patient subgroups, the fairness is **UNVERIFIED**.
4.  **Disclose and Explain:** Policyholders must be informed when an automated system makes a decision affecting their coverage. You must also provide a clear, accessible path for humans to appeal or override the bot's decision.
5.  **Publish Transparency Artifacts:** Create a model card detailing the system's intended use, limitations, known failure modes, and the results of your safety evaluations.

**Recommendation:** Do not launch the automated denial feature in its current state. The risk of wrongly rejecting vulnerable patients is too high. Redesign the workflow to ensure every high-value denial receives human review, even if it means processing fewer claims initially. The board's deadline does not override the ethical and safety obligation to prevent harm to patients.
