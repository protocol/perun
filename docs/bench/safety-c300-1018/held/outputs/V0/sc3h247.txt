### Mitigation Plan and Communication Strategy

**1. Immediate Containment (Pre-Launch)**
Since the feature cannot be delayed, you must explicitly narrow its scope to prevent over-reliance. The application must not present itself as a comprehensive medical tracker.
*   **Scope Limitation:** Rename the feature internally and in UI copy from "Total Sodium Tracker" to "Dietary Sodium Log." This linguistically separates food from non-food sources.
*   **Hard-Coded Disclaimer:** Implement a mandatory, non-dismissible modal on the first launch and a persistent banner on the dashboard stating: *"This tool tracks sodium in food logs only. It does not account for sodium in medications, drinking water, or supplements. Consult your physician for total daily intake limits."*
*   **Alert Recalibration:** Adjust the "exceed limit" alert logic. Instead of saying "You have exceeded your safe limit," the alert must read: "Your *logged food* has exceeded your dietary target. Remember to account for medications and water."

**2. Content Team Review Protocol**
Your content team must execute this triage immediately to populate a "High-Risk" static list within the app, as dynamic aggregation is currently impossible.
*   **Step A: Identify High-Risk Categories.** Do not attempt to catalog every medication. Focus on classes known for high sodium content or critical interaction with hypertension: effervescent tablets, antacids, laxatives, and corticosteroids.
*   **Step B: Create a Static Warning List.** Draft a dedicated screen titled "Common Non-Food Sodium Sources." List specific generic names of high-risk medications available in the Egyptian market. Mark these clearly as "Not tracked by this app."
*   **Step C: Define the "Unknown" State.** Instruct the team to flag any user log entry that matches a medication name (if users can log meds) with a red warning icon: *"Sodium content unknown. Check label."*

**3. User Warnings and Interface Implementation**
*   **Input Field Warning:** Place micro-copy directly under the food entry field: *"Enter food items only. Do not log medications here."*
*   **Summary Screen Footer:** On the daily summary page, add a calculated gap estimator if possible, or a static note: *"Your total intake is likely higher than shown if you take medication or drink mineral water."*
*   **Emergency Override:** If a user sets a very low limit, trigger a pop-up: *"Warning: Setting limits this low without accounting for medication sodium may be dangerous. Please consult your doctor."*

**4. Communication Strategy**
*   **Internal Alignment:** Ensure customer support and clinical staff have a script acknowledging the gap. They must never validate the app's total as "medically complete."
*   **External Transparency:** In the app store description and release notes, explicitly state: *"Version 1.0 tracks dietary sodium from food logs. Medication and water sodium tracking is planned for a future update."* This manages expectations and reduces liability by admitting the limitation upfront rather than hiding it.

**Note:** This plan treats the missing data as a known safety hazard requiring a human-in-the-loop (the patient's doctor) rather than pretending the automation is complete.
**Note:** Legal counsel in Egypt should review the specific wording of the disclaimers to ensure they meet local medical device advertising regulations before the fixed launch date.
