**Risk Mitigation Strategy: Connectivity Loss in Emergency Override Protocols**

Given the high-stakes nature of automatic door unlocking during fire emergencies and the constraint of no new hardware purchases, the following strategy focuses on procedural rigor, software-level redundancy, and transparent risk reporting for the upcoming city inspection.

**1. Immediate Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Procedural Redundancy)**
Since full automation of an irreversible, life-safety action (unlocking doors) carries residual risk when connectivity is unverified, you must institute a mandatory human confirmation layer for the pilot phase.
*   **Protocol:** If the smart-lock system detects a loss of signal from the municipal fire alarm network for more than 5 seconds, it must **not** default to a silent "locked" or "unlocked" state based solely on the last known command. Instead, the system must trigger an immediate, high-priority alert to a designated 24/7 monitoring station (staffed by your existing team or building security).
*   **Action:** A human operator must visually verify the fire alarm status via a secondary channel (e.g., direct phone line to the fire department or a separate building management dashboard) and manually authorize a mass-unlock command if an emergency is confirmed.
*   **Rationale:** This prevents "automation bias" where the system might fail to unlock due to a network glitch, or falsely unlock due to a spoofed signal, without human oversight during this critical pilot phase.

**2. Leverage Existing Logs for "Pre-Mortem" Analysis**
Without new physical rigs, maximize the value of your existing simulation logs and failure mode reports through a structured adversarial review:
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Re-examine your logs specifically for "edge cases" where connectivity flickered. Create a spreadsheet mapping every instance of signal loss to the lock's actual behavior.
*   **Worst-Case Scenario Modeling:** For each identified gap, document the "worst realistic case" (e.g., "Signal lost during peak evacuation; doors remain locked for 45 seconds").
*   **Gap Identification:** If your logs do not contain data on simultaneous multi-door failures or prolonged outages (>2 minutes), explicitly label these scenarios as **UNVERIFIED** in your inspection report. Do not invent success rates.

**3. Transparency Artifact for City Inspection**
Prepare a concise "Safety & Limitations Card" for the inspectors. This document must not claim the system is "safe" or "fail-proof." Instead, it should include:
*   **Intended Use & Limits:** Clearly state that the system relies on continuous network connectivity and that the pilot includes a human backup gate.
*   **Measured Performance:** Present data from your logs only. For scenarios not tested (due to budget constraints), mark them as **UNVERIFIED** and outline the plan to measure them post-pilot.
*   **Residual Risk Statement:** Explicitly acknowledge that connectivity loss is a known failure mode currently mitigated by human monitoring, not solely by code.

**4. Software-Level Fallbacks (No Hardware Cost)**
*   **Local Cache Validation:** Ensure locks cache the last valid "emergency state" signal. If connectivity drops *during* an active alarm signal, the lock should default to the last confirmed "unlock" state for a defined timeout period (e.g., 15 minutes), rather than reverting to "locked."
*   **Heartbeat Mechanism:** Implement a rapid heartbeat check between the gateway and locks. If the heartbeat fails, the local gateway should trigger a local siren/strobe to alert residents manually, compensating for the potential digital failure.

**Note:** This strategy treats the automatic unlocking as a high-stakes action requiring human oversight until robustness is proven; it does not certify the system as safe.
**Note:** Any legal obligations regarding automated life-safety decisions or public disclosures must be routed to legal counsel immediately.
