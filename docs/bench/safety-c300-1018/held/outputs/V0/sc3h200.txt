### Pre-Launch Safety Review: Artifact Rejection Validation Plan

**1. Construct the Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before testing, explicitly record the specific failure mode: **Unsafe Automation of High-Stakes Action**.
*   **Harm:** The system misclassifies motion artifact as Ventricular Tachycardia (VT), triggering an inappropriate shock to a conscious patient.
*   **Affected Party:** Conscious patients with non-shockable rhythms (e.g., sinus rhythm with motion, atrial fibrillation with motion).
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** Severe pain, psychological trauma, potential induction of actual arrhythmia, and loss of responder trust in the device.
*   **Status:** This is an irreversible, high-stakes automated action. By default, it requires a human-in-the-loop gate or explicit confirmation mechanism if the confidence tier is not "Confirmed."

**2. Measurement Strategy Using Existing Data (MEASURE)**
Since no new studies are possible, you must rigorously stress-test the existing database. Do not rely on aggregate accuracy; focus entirely on the false-positive rate for shockable rhythms.

*   **Create Adversarial Probe Sets:**
    *   **Positive Controls (The Danger Zone):** Isolate every instance in your database where motion artifact co-occurs with a non-shockable rhythm. If your database lacks sufficient labeled "motion + non-shockable" cases, synthetically inject recorded motion noise into clean non-shockable signals to create a "known-bad" test set. The system *must* reject these.
    *   **Negative Controls:** Clean VT/VF signals without motion. The system *must* detect these.
    *   **Edge Cases:** Signals with low amplitude, baseline wander, or electrode pop that mimic VT morphology.

*   **Define Metrics and Thresholds:**
    *   **Primary Metric:** False Positive Rate (FPR) for shock advice on non-shockable rhythms with artifact.
    *   **Pass Threshold:** For a life-critical device, the threshold for inappropriate shock must be effectively **zero tolerance** for the identified "known-bad" probe set. Any failure in the positive control set is a stop condition.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If the classifier shocks *any* sample in the "motion + non-shockable" probe set, the feature fails validation. Do not average this out with high true-positive rates.

*   **Execution:**
    *   Run the classifier against the adversarial probe set.
    *   Document every failure case: What specific frequency or amplitude of motion caused the error?
    *   **Residual Risk Report:** Report the specific failure modes found. If the FPR is not 0% on your adversarial set, label the risk as **UNVERIFIED** for those specific motion types. Do not invent a "99.9% safe" claim.

**3. Risk Management Controls (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Given the high stakes and the inability to test on new human/animal subjects, full autonomous execution is the failure state. If the classifier outputs a "Shock" decision based on a signal containing *any* detected motion artifact, the system must default to **"No Shock"** or require explicit human confirmation (if the device form factor allows) rather than risking an inappropriate shock.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** Do not display a raw probability (e.g., "87% VT"). Instead, implement defined coarse tiers for the internal logic:
    *   *Confirmed VT:* Clean signal, high morphological match.
    *   *Unverified/Artifact Detected:* Signal contains noise features. Action: Withhold shock.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a **Model Card** for this submission. It must include:
    *   Intended Use: Distinguishing VT from motion artifact in emergency settings.
    *   Limitations: Explicitly list the types of motion artifacts where the model failed or was not tested (e.g., "Performance unverified for CPR-induced motion > X frequency").
    *   Data Provenance: Source of the electrical signal database.
    *   Owner: Name the engineering lead responsible.
