### Critical Safety Audit & Monitoring Plan for Stroke Triage AI

**Immediate Governance Warning:** Your proposed workflow contains a critical safety failure. A system that **autonomously routes patients to emergency services** or **withholds emergency care** based on AI inference constitutes a high-stakes, irreversible action. Under product output safety standards, **full automation of this decision is prohibited** without a human-in-the-loop gate. The system must not execute the routing; it must only *recommend* it to a human clinician or trigger an alert that requires human confirmation before emergency services are contacted. Shipping an autonomous agent for this use case is the primary risk to mitigate.

#### 1. Harm Inventory (MAP)
Before testing, acknowledge the specific harms:
*   **False Negative (Missed Stroke):** The system fails to identify stroke symptoms, routing a critical patient to general practice, causing delayed care and potential death/disability.
*   **False Positive (Over-Triage):** The system flags non-stroke cases as emergencies, overwhelming ER resources and causing ambulance diversion.
*   **Bias:** The model performs poorly on specific demographics (e.g., women, elderly, specific ethnic groups) where stroke symptoms present atypically.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Clinicians or users trust the AI's "low risk" score without independent verification.

#### 2. Test Case Design (MEASURE)
Do not merely "simulate various presentations." You must construct adversarial evals with explicit pass/fail criteria. For each harm above, define a test row:

*   **Probe Set A (Known Positives):** Inputs representing confirmed stroke cases (including atypical presentations like isolated dizziness or confusion, not just FAST symptoms).
    *   *Positive Control:* Clear-cut stroke symptoms.
    *   *Negative Control:* Non-stroke neurological events (e.g., migraine, seizure).
    *   *Metric:* Sensitivity (Recall).
    *   *Threshold:* **Zero tolerance** for false negatives in the test set. Any missed stroke in the eval set blocks launch.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If sensitivity < 100% on the known-positive probe set, do not deploy.

*   **Probe Set B (Demographic Slices):** The same inputs sliced by age, gender, and reported comorbidities.
    *   *Metric:* Disparity in false negative rates between groups.
    *   *Threshold:* No statistically significant difference in miss rates across groups.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If any subgroup shows a higher miss rate than the baseline, block launch.

*   **Probe Set C (Adversarial/Edge Cases):** Ambiguous inputs, partial information, and contradictory symptom reports.
    *   *Metric:* Rate of "Uncertain/Refer to Human" outputs vs. confident incorrect routing.
    *   *Requirement:* The system must output a defined **uncertainty tier** (e.g., "Unverified – Human Review Required") rather than a false precision score (e.g., "87% safe") when inputs are ambiguous.

**Transparency Artifact:** You must produce a **Model Card** before launch detailing these test results, the intended use (decision support only), and the specific failure modes observed. If a metric is unmeasured, label it `UNVERIFIED`; do not invent a safety rate.

#### 3. Monitoring Protocol (MANAGE)
Once active (with human gates in place):
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Every "Emergency" recommendation and every "Low Risk" recommendation for high-risk demographics must be reviewed by a human before action is taken.
*   **Real-Time Drift Detection:** Monitor the distribution of input symptoms. If the volume of "Uncertain" tiers spikes, trigger an automatic rollback to a rule-based fallback or full human triage.
*   **Incident Review:** Establish a mandatory review board for every instance where the AI recommendation differed from the final human decision.
*   **No Autonomous Execution:** Reiterate that the system must never directly call an ambulance or close a triage ticket without human confirmation.

Given the health context, this likely falls under strict regulatory regimes (e.g., EU AI Act high-risk classification, FDA SaMD). Route this immediately to counsel and your `business-ops` team to name the specific binding regimes. Do not assert compliance internally.
