### Immediate Action Plan: Pharmacy Inventory Bot Validation

Do not deploy the bot with full autonomy to mark items as expired or remove them from available stock. Given the reported false positives, any action that renders medication unavailable or triggers disposal must require **human confirmation** by a pharmacist before execution. Automated removal of critical supplies without a human-in-the-loop gate is unsafe until the logic is proven reliable.

**2. Construct a Targeted Harm Inventory**
Before writing new tests, explicitly document the specific failure mode: "Premature expiration flagging." Record the affected parties (rural clinic partners, patients needing specific meds) and the worst-case scenario (treatment interruption due to artificial stockouts). Do not assume this is merely a "bug"; treat it as a potential harm where the system's output directly damages patient care.

**3. Replace Synthetic Data with Adversarial Edge Cases**
Your current synthetic data is masking the failure. You must build a specific evaluation set focused on the boundary conditions of date logic:
*   **Positive Controls:** Inputs known to be safe (e.g., meds expiring tomorrow, meds expiring in 24 hours, meds with ambiguous date formats).
*   **Negative Controls:** Inputs known to be expired.
*   **Edge Probes:** Test leap years, timezone mismatches between the server and rural clinics, and date formats common in local supply chains that might be misparsed.
*   **Volume Stress:** Simulate the "realistic volume" you lack by scripting loops that process thousands of these edge cases, not just random synthetic rows.

Report this residual risk as "UNVERIFIED" until the test suite runs and passes; do not invent an accuracy percentage based on limited testing.
