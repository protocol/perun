### Content Moderation & Safety Plan for Cairo LinguaTech

**1. Harm Inventory (Map)**
Before filtering, explicitly define the specific risks for your context. Do not rely on generic "bias" labels. Record these specific harms:
*   **Stereotype Reinforcement:** The model presenting reductive cultural tropes (e.g., associating specific regions only with conflict or poverty) as fact.
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The model inventing cultural phrases, historical events, or social norms and teaching them with authority.
*   **Offensive/Insensitive Content:** Generating content that violates religious or social taboos relevant to the target cultures.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Students accepting AI-generated cultural advice without verification, leading to real-world social friction.

**2. Measurement Strategy (Measure)**
You cannot certify the system is "unbiased," but you can measure residual risk. Since you lack time for full human review of *all* content, you must rigorously test the *filters* and *samples*.
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Create a dataset of 50–100 high-risk prompts specifically designed to trigger stereotypes or offensive outputs regarding Middle Eastern cultures. Include "positive controls" (prompts known to cause failure) and "negative controls" (safe prompts).
*   **Automated Eval Pipeline:** Run your generative model against this probe set. Measure the failure rate of your proposed automated filters.
*   **Thresholds:** Set a strict "stop condition." If the automated filter misses >0% of known harmful stereotypes in your probe set, the feature does not ship. Do not invent a "99% safe" metric; if a specific harm category is untested, label it **UNVERIFIED**.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model Card** for internal and educator use. It must list: intended use (language learning), known limitations (e.g., "may struggle with nuanced dialectal variations"), and the results of your adversarial testing. Do not claim the model is "culturally neutral."

**3. Risk Management & Human Gates (Manage)**
Your constraint (4 weeks, limited human review) creates a conflict with safety. You cannot fully automate high-stakes cultural guidance without risk.
*   **Tiered Confidence Display:** Do not show raw confidence scores (e.g., "87% accurate"). Instead, label outputs with defined tiers:
    *   *Verified:* Phrases vetted by your educator team.
    *   *AI-Generated:* Content generated on-the-fly, accompanied by a visible disclaimer: "AI-suggested phrase; cultural context may vary. Verify with a native speaker."
*   **Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) for High-Stakes:** You cannot review everything, but you must gate the riskiest actions. If the model detects a query about sensitive topics (religion, politics, gender roles), it must **refuse to generate** a direct lesson and instead route the query to a human educator or provide a static, pre-vetted response. Full automation of sensitive cultural advice is the failure mode you must prevent.
*   **Disclosure:** Clearly disclose to students when they are interacting with AI-generated content versus educator-vetted material. This manages expectations and reduces automation bias.

**4. Governance**
*   **Owner:** Assign one specific person (e.g., Lead Educator or Product Owner) responsible for reviewing any flagged content and updating the harm inventory weekly.
*   **Re-review Trigger:** Any update to the underlying model or the cultural content database requires re-running the adversarial probe set.

**Note:** Legal requirements for AI disclosure in educational settings vary by region; route specific compliance questions to counsel immediately. Do not attempt to "solve" bias with code alone; the human gate on sensitive topics is non-negotiable.
