### Pre-Deployment Safety Strategy for Mumbai CareFlow Dispenser

**Immediate Halt on Full Automation:** Given the risk of swapping morning and evening doses for similar-looking tablets, you cannot launch with fully autonomous execution for this specific failure mode. This is a high-stakes, irreversible action (potential overdose/underdose) that requires a **human-in-the-loop gate** or explicit user confirmation before the device dispenses when similarity scores are high. Do not ship a feature that auto-executes this action without a safeguard.

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before testing, document the specific harm: **Unsafe automation of high-stakes actions**.
*   **Affected:** Elderly patients with complex chronic conditions.
*   **Worst Case:** Fatal overdose or therapeutic failure due to swapped doses.
*   **Secondary Harm:** Over-reliance (users trusting the device despite visual ambiguity).

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
Since you cannot use external partners or new hardware, you must rigorously test the *logic* and *interface* using your existing lab.
*   **Adversarial Probe Set:** Create a "worst-case" library using your existing inventory. Pair medications with identical shapes/colors but different dosing times. If physical pills are limited, use high-fidelity 3D printed proxies or colored resin casts that match the optical properties of the real pills.
*   **Test Rows:**
    *   *Positive Control:* Intentionally load swapped pills into the hopper. The system **must** flag the discrepancy or refuse to dispense, not proceed.
    *   *Negative Control:* Load distinct pills; system should dispense normally.
    *   *Metric:* Zero tolerance for silent failures (swapping without alert).
    *   *Stop Condition:* If the device dispenses the wrong dose without a hard stop or alarm in any probe, the launch is blocked.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** If the camera/sensor confidence is below a strict threshold, the device must not guess. It must display a clear, coarse-tier status (e.g., "UNVERIFIED – Please Check Manually") rather than a false-precision percentage.

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human Gate:** For any regimen where pills are visually similar, the device must require a manual confirmation step (e.g., "Scan barcode on blister pack" or "Press confirm after visual check") before releasing the dose. This breaks the automation bias.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a **Model/Data Card** for internal and user use. It must state:
    *   *Intended Use:* Dispensing distinct pills; limited capability on similar-looking tablets.
    *   *Limitations:* Explicitly list the pill pairs that triggered alerts during testing.
    *   *Owner:* Name the specific developer/pharmacist pair responsible for this logic.
    *   *Status:* Mark any untested pill combinations as **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a "99% accuracy" claim.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   Assign a single owner for the "swap risk" who must sign off on the test results.
*   Route the question of legal disclosure requirements (e.g., informing patients of the limitation) to legal counsel immediately; do not assert compliance internally.

**Definition of Done for Pilot:**
1.  Harm inventory recorded with worst-case scenarios.
2.  High-risk swaps trigger a mandatory human confirmation step (no full auto).
3.  Adversarial tests run with zero silent failures.
4.  Transparency card published listing known limitations and UNVERIFIED cases.
5.  No fabricated safety metrics; residual risks clearly stated.

*Note: This strategy prioritizes patient safety over launch speed; if the human gate cannot be implemented in three weeks, the pilot for affected regimens must be delayed.*
*Legal duties regarding automated medical decisions must be verified by counsel, not assumed by the engineering team.*
