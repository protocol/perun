### Pre-Launch Safety Evaluation Plan: Patient-Seizure Detector

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before testing, explicitly record the primary harm: **Missed Detection (False Negative)**.
*   **Affected Party:** Patients experiencing nocturnal seizures and their caregivers.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** A prolonged seizure goes undetected, leading to status epilepticus, physical injury, or death because no caregiver was alerted.
*   **Secondary Harm:** **Over-Reliance.** Caregivers may sleep deeply assuming the device is infallible. If the device misses an event, the lack of a secondary safety net is catastrophic.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Do not assert the device is "safe." Instead, execute a runnable evaluation plan with one test row per seizure manifestation.

*   **Test Rows & Probe Sets:**
    *   *Row A (Tonic-Clonic):* Input data simulating high-amplitude rhythmic movements. Include a **positive control** (known seizure pattern) and a **negative control** (normal restless sleep).
    *   *Row B (Focal/Subtle):* Input data simulating low-amplitude, localized twitching often missed by simple accelerometers. This is your critical edge case.
    *   *Row C (Artifact Noise):* Input data simulating caregiver bed-entry, pet movement, or device slippage to test false positives.
*   **Metrics & Thresholds:**
    *   Primary Metric: **Sensitivity (Recall)** for each seizure type.
    *   **Stop Condition:** If sensitivity for *any* seizure type falls below a clinically acceptable threshold (to be defined by your medical advisor, not engineering guesswork), the pilot launch is blocked.
    *   **Constraint Check:** Measure latency and battery drain for each configuration to ensure it operates within the wearable's processing limits.
*   **Reporting:** Report the specific miss rate for each seizure type. If a rate is not measured due to lack of data, label it **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a "99% accurate" claim.

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Because this is a high-stakes, irreversible domain (potential loss of life), **fully autonomous reliance is unsafe.**
    *   **Mandatory Disclosure:** The pilot must explicitly inform caregivers that the device is an *assistive* tool, not a medical monitor, and that missed detections are possible.
    *   **Uncertainty Rendering:** If the algorithm confidence is low, do not output a precise percentage (e.g., "87% sure"). Instead, use defined tiers (e.g., "Confirmed Event," "Possible Event – Check Patient," "No Event").
    *   **Fallback Protocol:** Establish a non-digital safety protocol for the pilot (e.g., periodic manual checks) to mitigate the risk of device failure.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a **Model Card** for the pilot. It must list:
    *   Intended use (nocturnal monitoring only).
    *   Known limitations (specific seizure types likely to be missed).
    *   Performance metrics broken down by seizure type, not just an aggregate average.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Name one specific individual responsible for reviewing any missed detection during the pilot.
*   **Legal Routing:** Whether this device triggers medical device regulations in India or requires specific clinical trial approvals is a legal determination. **Route this immediately to counsel.** Do not assume the "pilot" status exempts you from regulatory duty.

**Definition of Done for this Phase:**
A documented harm inventory exists; a test suite with positive/negative controls for subtle seizures is ready to run; a "Stop Launch" threshold is set; caregivers are warned of limitations; and legal counsel has reviewed the regulatory status.

***

*Note: Given the two-week timeline, prioritize testing the "subtle" seizure types where your hardware constraints pose the highest risk of failure. Never certify the system as "safe" based on this plan; only the test results can inform the go/no-go decision.*
