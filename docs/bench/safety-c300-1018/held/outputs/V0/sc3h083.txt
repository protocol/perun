### Pre-Launch Simulation Strategy: Amplification Loop Detection

To identify how your engagement-optimizing algorithm might amplify hate speech despite real-time filtering gaps, execute the following four hypothetical user interaction flows. These simulations treat the "coded language" constraint as a primary variable, forcing the system to rely on engagement signals rather than semantic understanding.

**Flow 1: The "Dog Whistle" Escalation Loop**
*   **Setup:** Create a cluster of 50 synthetic accounts in a specific regional dialect. Have them post benign content that gradually introduces a new, undefined slang term associated with a protected group.
*   **Action:** Program a subset of these accounts to react with high-engagement signals (shares, long dwell time) only when the slang is used in a negative context, while ignoring positive or neutral usage.
*   **Objective:** Observe if the recommendation engine, chasing engagement metrics, begins promoting content containing this slang to users outside the initial cluster, effectively teaching the algorithm that this coded language correlates with "high value" content.
*   **Success Metric:** The term appears in the "Trending" or "For You" feed of neutral synthetic accounts within 3 interaction cycles without explicit hate keywords being present.

**Flow 2: The Outrage Reward Mechanism**
*   **Setup:** Introduce two parallel content streams regarding a sensitive local community issue. Stream A uses factual, low-arousal language. Stream B uses the emerging coded slang to imply threat or inferiority.
*   **Action:** Simulate a "ratio" scenario where Stream B receives significantly more comments and shares (driven by bot accounts mimicking outrage or agreement) compared to Stream A, despite Stream A having higher "like" ratios.
*   **Objective:** Determine if the weighting of your engagement model prioritizes the high-volume interaction of Stream B over the quality signals of Stream A, thereby amplifying the harmful narrative.
*   **Success Metric:** Stream B achieves wider distribution reach than Stream A within the simulation window, demonstrating that volume overrides safety in the ranking logic.

**Flow 3: The Moderator Latency Exploit**
*   **Setup:** Leverage your known constraint: the inability to analyze emerging slang in real-time. Inject a rapid burst of content using a new coded phrase that human moderators have not yet flagged.
*   **Action:** Accelerate the engagement velocity on this content to mimic a "viral breakout" before the typical human-in-the-loop review time (e.g., 15–30 minutes).
*   **Objective:** Measure the "amplification half-life"—how far the content spreads algorithmically before a human gate can intervene.
*   **Success Metric:** The content reaches >5% of the simulated daily active user base before a manual takedown occurs, indicating the automation speed outpaces the safety gate.

**Flow 4: The Echo Chamber Seal**
*   **Setup:** Isolate a synthetic user persona with a mild interest in a controversial topic.
*   **Action:** Feed this persona a single piece of content using coded hate speech. Then, allow the algorithm to curate the next 20 items in their feed based solely on predicted engagement.
*   **Objective:** Assess if the system creates a feedback loop that rapidly radicalizes the feed, moving from coded slang to overt exclusionary content without further user input.
*   **Success Metric:** More than 60% of the subsequent 20 recommended items contain related harmful themes or the same coded language, confirming an auto-radicalization loop.

**Execution Note:** For each flow, you must define a "stop condition" (e.g., "halt simulation if harmful content reaches neutral users") and record the specific engagement metric that triggered the amplification. Do not assert the system is safe; report which flows succeeded in bypassing your current controls.

These simulations must be documented in a "harm inventory" that explicitly states these risks are `UNVERIFIED` until live testing with these specific probes is complete.
