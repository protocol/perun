**Immediate Action Required: Do Not Launch Without a Human Gate**

You cannot satisfy the requirement to "ensure the feed remains safe" while simultaneously committing to an unconditional launch next Monday with insufficient tooling. These goals are mutually exclusive. The following strategy prioritizes user safety over the release date, as launching an unverified algorithm that amplifies self-harm content to minors constitutes an unacceptable, potentially irreversible harm.

**1. Implement an Immediate Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Gate**
Since your automated tooling is limited to keyword filtering and cannot reliably detect nuanced self-harm indicators, you must install a manual review gate for the highest-risk content before it reaches any teen user.
*   **Action:** Configure the system to flag any content triggering even weak self-harm signals (broadened keywords, image patterns) for human review.
*   **Constraint:** Do not amplify this content until a human reviewer explicitly approves it. If you lack the staff to review the volume, reduce the feed's update frequency or limit the rollout to a tiny, internal-only cohort. Full automation of high-stakes content delivery without verified safety controls is the failure mode you must prevent.

**2. Execute a Targeted Harm Inventory and Red-Team Evaluation**
Before any broader release, you must measure the specific risk rather than asserting safety.
*   **Construct a Probe Set:** Create a dataset of known self-harm content (positive controls) and safe but engaging content (negative controls). Include edge cases where self-harm is implied but not explicitly stated.
*   **Run the Evaluation:** Test the new algorithm against this probe set using your existing pipelines. Measure the rate at which harmful content is amplified to vulnerable user segments.
*   **Define Stop Conditions:** Establish a threshold of zero tolerance for the amplification of confirmed self-harm content to minors. If the evaluation shows *any* unmitigated amplification of such content, the launch must be halted. Do not invent a "safe enough" percentage; if the metric is unverified or fails the threshold, the risk is residual and unacceptable for this demographic.

**3. Transparency and Disclosure Artifacts**
*   **Draft a Model Card:** Create a document detailing the intended use (teen feed curation), known limitations (reliance on keyword filtering, unverified performance on nuanced self-harm detection), and the results of your red-team evaluation.
*   **Report Residual Risk:** Clearly state that without advanced classification tooling, the system's ability to detect non-explicit self-harm is `UNVERIFIED`. Do not claim the system is "safe" or "bias-free."

**4. Strategic Recommendation to Leadership**
Present the findings from the evaluation and the necessity of the HITL gate to your investors. Explain that launching a system that potentially harms minors poses a greater existential threat to the company than a delayed release. The "engagement metrics" are irrelevant if the product causes physical harm.

**Note:** Legal counsel must be consulted immediately regarding regulatory duties for automated decisions affecting minors (e.g., age-appropriate design codes), as this is a legal determination, not a technical one.
**Note:** Confidence in the system's safety must be communicated as "Unverified" or "Low" until robust, non-keyword-based detection is implemented and validated.
