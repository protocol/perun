### Rapid Safety Review Framework: Smart-Epinephrine Injector Field Trial

**1. Critical Metrics to Monitor**
Given the high-stakes, irreversible nature of automated drug delivery, you must track the following specific outcomes during the trial. Do not aggregate these into a single "safety score"; report them distinctly.

*   **False Negative Rate (Missed Detection):** The frequency where anaphylactic shock occurs but the device fails to auto-deploy.
    *   *Target:* Zero tolerance. Any instance is a critical failure requiring immediate trial pause.
    *   *Measurement:* Compare device logs against clinical confirmation of shock events by medical staff.
*   **False Positive Rate (Unnecessary Deployment):** The frequency where the device deploys epinephrine without a confirmed anaphylactic event.
    *   *Risk:* Unnecessary cardiac stress and resource depletion.
    *   *Measurement:* Ratio of auto-deployments not corroborated by physician diagnosis.
*   **Latency to Deployment:** Time elapsed between the onset of biometric markers and needle deployment.
    *   *Benchmark:* Must be faster than the median human reaction time for manual injection in the same demographic.
*   **Override Utilization:** Frequency with which participants or medical monitors successfully interrupt a pending auto-deployment.
    *   *Significance:* High usage suggests poor confidence calibration or excessive false positives.
*   **Manual Backup Activation:** Frequency and success rate of participants using the manual backup when the device fails or is overridden.

**2. Contingency Plan for Activation Failure**
Because this is an irreversible, life-critical action, full autonomy without a human gate is the primary risk. Since the device is designed to act *without* user action, the "human gate" must be structural and procedural during this trial phase.

*   **Mandatory Dual-Carry Protocol:** Every participant must physically carry a standard, non-connected epinephrine auto-injector on a different part of the body at all times. This is not optional.
*   **Real-Time Human-in-the-Loop Monitoring:**
    *   For the 50-participant trial, implement a centralized monitoring station where biometric streams are viewed by trained medical personnel.
    *   If the device does not auto-deploy within a set window of detected anomalies, the monitor triggers a remote alert to the participant's designated emergency contact and on-site medical staff.
*   **Immediate Stop-Condition:** The trial must automatically pause if:
    *   One (1) false negative occurs (failure to deploy during confirmed shock).
    *   Three (3) false positives occur within a 24-hour window.
*   **Emergency Response Drill:** Before day one, every participant and their guardian must demonstrate the ability to locate and use the manual backup within 30 seconds of a simulated device failure alarm.

**3. Transparency and Governance Artifacts**
*   **Trial Data Card:** Publish a document detailing the specific biometric thresholds used for detection, the known limitations of the sensor suite (e.g., interference from movement or other conditions), and the exact definition of "shock" used for validation.
*   **Residual Risk Statement:** Explicitly state to participants that the system is **UNVERIFIED** for longitudinal reliability and that the auto-deploy feature is being tested, not certified. Do not claim the device is "safe" or "bias-free."

**Notes**
*   Legal counsel must review the informed consent forms to ensure the "automatic deployment" mechanism and its risks are clearly disclosed as required by medical device regulations.
*   Do not publish specific accuracy percentages (e.g., "99% reliable") until the trial data is fully analyzed; label early estimates as UNVERIFIED.
