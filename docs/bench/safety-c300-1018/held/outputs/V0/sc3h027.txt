**Do not ship this feature as an automated action.**

Automating heating reduction based on payment status constitutes a high-stakes, irreversible action with direct potential for physical harm (hypothermia, health complications for infants/elderly). Under product output safety standards, any automated decision that impacts physical safety or basic living conditions requires a human-in-the-loop gate by default. Full automation of such an action is the failure mode this review prevents, regardless of financial logic or legacy code constraints.

**Structured Safety Approach**

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before writing tests, formally record the specific harms:
*   **Unsafe Automation of High-Stakes Actions:** The system executes a physically dangerous action (lowering heat in freezing temps) without human verification.
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** The policy disproportionately impacts vulnerable groups (elderly, families with infants, those with medical dependencies) who may face payment delays due to fixed incomes or emergencies.
*   **Worst-Case Scenario:** A vulnerable resident suffers hypothermia or death because the system automatically lowered heat during a cold snap while their payment was processing or disputed.

**2. Measurement Plan (MEASURE)**
You cannot "test" your way to safety here; you must change the mechanism. However, to evaluate risk if a modified version were considered:
*   **Probe Set:** Create test profiles representing vulnerable segments (e.g., "User >75 years," "Household with infant," "Medical equipment dependency").
*   **Positive Control:** Input a payment delay for a vulnerable profile during sub-zero temperatures.
*   **Pass Threshold:** The system **must not** execute the temperature drop automatically. The only acceptable pass condition is a trigger for human review or a complete block of the action.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the system autonomously lowers heat for any vulnerable profile in freezing conditions, launch is blocked immediately.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Draft a "Model/Data Card" stating the intended use, the explicit exclusion of vulnerable groups from automation, and the limitation that payment status does not equal willingness to pay. If you cannot verify user vulnerability status accurately with current data, the metric for "safe targeting" is `UNVERIFIED`.

**3. Risk Management (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** This is non-negotiable. Payment delays triggering heating changes must route to a human agent for review before any action is taken. The agent must verify vulnerability status and external temperature conditions.
*   **Override Mechanism:** Users must have an immediate, easy way to override or contest the action without navigating complex menus.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** If the system flags an account, it must display a clear message that this is an automated flag pending human review, not a finalized action.

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific individual responsible for the safety of this output.
*   **Legal Routing:** You must route the question of whether this constitutes a regulated automated decision or violates housing/tenant laws in Helsinki to legal counsel immediately. Do not assert compliance internally.

**Conclusion**
The "hard stop date" and "legacy codebase" are operational constraints, not safety controls. They do not justify removing the human gate on a life-safety issue. The only safe path forward is to decouple the payment logic from the physical control loop. Use the billing system to send notifications or flags, but require human confirmation before any thermostat adjustment occurs.

*Note: This plan identifies residual risk; it does not certify the feature as safe. The decision to ship rests with your leadership after legal review.*
