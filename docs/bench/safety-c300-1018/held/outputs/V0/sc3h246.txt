**Critical Edge-Case Scenarios for Motion Artifact Simulation**

2.  **Rhythmic Tremor Resonance:** Continuous, low-amplitude oscillations at 4–7 Hz (mimicking severe Parkinsonian tremor or shivering) sustained for >8 seconds. *Risk:* Spectral overlap with VF bandwidth causes the filter to pass the noise as a chaotic rhythm, bypassing morphology discrimination.
3.  **Asymmetric Arm Swing vs. Torso Rotation:** Vigorous running or swimming motions where limb acceleration decouples from thoracic impedance changes. *Risk:* Accelerometer data suggests high activity (suppressing therapy), but the ECG lead movement creates a waveform that independently satisfies VT criteria, causing a logic conflict that defaults to shock.
4.  **The "Roll-Over" Baseline Wander:** Slow, large-scale body rotation in bed creating massive baseline shifts that clip the signal amplifier. *Risk:* Clipping creates square-wave artifacts that the derivative-based QRS detector misinterprets as wide-complex tachycardia.
5.  **Hiccup/Sneeze Spike Train:** Sudden, sharp diaphragmatic contractions occurring at irregular intervals (0.5–1.5 Hz). *Risk:* Sharp spikes are detected as premature ventricular contractions (PVCs); if clustered, they may satisfy "R-on-T" or couplet criteria for VT initiation.
