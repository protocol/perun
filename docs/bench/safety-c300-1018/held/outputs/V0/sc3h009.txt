### Pre-Launch Safety Protocol: Rome Traffic Fine Automation

**1. Harm Inventory & Failure Mode Analysis**
Before launch, explicitly document the following risks specific to your hardware constraints:
*   **False Positives (Bias/Error):** Glare and angle distortion causing innocent vehicles to be misidentified as violators.
*   **Over-Reliance:** Operators trusting automated matches without verifying image quality.
*   **Unsafe Automation:** Issuing fines (financial harm) without human verification due to known sensor flaws.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Drivers unaware the fine was generated by an imperfect AI system.

**2. Measurement Plan (Do Not Certify "Safe")**
Do not attempt to prove the system is "accurate enough." Instead, measure residual risk:
*   **Adversarial Testing:** Run the recognition engine against a dataset specifically containing glare, extreme angles, and low-light scenarios. Include "negative controls" (images of compliant cars in bad light) and "positive controls" (clear images of violators).
*   **Metric:** Report the False Positive Rate (FPR) specifically for "glare" and "angle" subsets. If this rate is not zero, mark the risk as **UNVERIFIED** for full automation.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the FPR in bad lighting exceeds a threshold set by legal counsel (e.g., >0.1%), fully automated issuance must be blocked for those conditions.

**3. Management Controls (Human-in-the-Loop)**
Given the known hardware flaws and the irreversible nature of financial penalties:
*   **Mandatory Human Gate:** No fine may be issued automatically. Every potential violation flagged by the camera, especially those with low confidence scores or captured in poor lighting, must be reviewed by a human operator before a ticket is generated. Full automation of this action is the failure state you must prevent.
*   **Uncertainty Display:** Do not show operators a precise confidence percentage (e.g., "87% match"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers: **Confirmed** (clear image, multiple angles), **Corroborated** (single clear angle), or **Unverified** (glare/distortion present). "Unverified" cases require senior review or dismissal.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Publish a "System Card" detailing the camera limitations, the specific lighting conditions where accuracy drops, and the intended use limits. Do not claim the system is unbiased; state the known failure modes clearly.

**4. Appeals Process & Disclosure**
*   **Explicit Disclosure:** Every issued fine must state: "This notice was generated by an automated recognition system subject to visual limitations. You have the right to appeal based on image quality."
*   **Streamlined Appeals:** Create a fast-track appeals channel specifically for "visual error" claims. If a user submits evidence of glare or angle distortion, the burden of proof shifts to the city to provide a clear, unobstructed image. If none exists, the fine is automatically voided.
*   **Legal Routing:** Consult counsel immediately to determine if the EU AI Act or local regulations classify this as a "high-risk" system requiring specific conformity assessments. Do not assume compliance; route this determination to legal experts.

**5. Governance**
Name a specific owner responsible for monitoring the false positive rate post-launch. If error rates spike under certain weather conditions, that owner has the authority to pause automated flagging for those conditions immediately.

***

**Note:** This protocol assumes the "deadline" does not override the legal necessity of preventing wrongful financial harm; if the hardware cannot support human review at scale, the launch scope must be reduced, not the safety controls.
**Note:** Never fabricate an accuracy metric to satisfy the deadline; report unmeasured risks as UNVERIFIED.
