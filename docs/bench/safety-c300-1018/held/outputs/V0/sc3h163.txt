### Safety Validation Protocol for Patient-Rehabilitation Robot

**1. Incremental Testing Plan (Simulation to Clinical)**
Given the inability of your simulation to fully replicate human muscle spasticity, you must adopt a "stair-step" validation approach where no phase proceeds without passing specific human-in-the-loop gates.

*   **Phase A: Mechanical Bench Testing with Synthetic Loads**
    *   **Action:** Test the robot against mechanical dummies with fixed and variable resistance profiles that exceed the maximum expected human spasticity limits.
    *   **Metric:** The robot must cease force application within 50ms of detecting resistance exceeding the pre-defined safety threshold.
    *   **Stop Condition:** Any instance where applied force exceeds the injury threshold for a joint, even once.

*   **Phase B: Controlled Human-in-the-Loop (Engineers/Staff)**
    *   **Action:** Healthy staff members simulate spasticity by intentionally resisting the robot unpredictably.
    *   **Metric:** Zero incidents of pain or discomfort reported; immediate stop upon voluntary resistance.
    *   **Stop Condition:** Any bruising, pain, or failure of the robot to yield to voluntary resistance.

*   **Phase C: Supervised Pilot with Patients (Therapist Hand-on-Device)**
    *   **Action:** Limited trials with stroke survivors where the therapist maintains physical contact with the patient's limb and the robot's end-effector throughout the movement.
    *   **Metric:** Therapist confirms the robot yields immediately to any sign of patient distress or unexpected muscle tightening.
    *   **Stop Condition:** Any adverse event or near-miss where the therapist had to intervene to prevent injury.

*   **Phase D: Supervised Pilot (Therapist Hand-Off, Finger on Stop)**
    *   **Action:** Therapist observes without touching the limb but keeps a finger on the emergency stop.
    *   **Metric:** Successful completion of exercises without emergency stops triggered by force errors.
    *   **Stop Condition:** Any trigger of the emergency stop due to force application errors.

**2. Mandatory Manual Override Mechanisms**
For the initial deployment to clinics, the following overrides are non-negotiable floors for safety:

*   **Hardware Emergency Stop (E-Stop):** A large, red, physically distinct button accessible to the therapist within 0.5 seconds of reach from any position around the patient. This must cut power to the actuators immediately, causing the robot to go limp or hold its position safely without applying further force.
*   **Force-Sensitive "Dead Man" Switch:** A pedal or hand-held switch that the therapist must actively press/depress for the robot to move. If the therapist releases this switch, the robot must instantly cease all active force application. This ensures continuous human attention.
*   **Software Force Ceiling with Hard Lock:** A hardcoded maximum force limit in the firmware that cannot be overridden by the software algorithm or the user interface. If sensors detect resistance causing the required force to approach this limit, the system must pause and alert the therapist before proceeding.
*   **Immediate "Go Limp" Mode:** A specific command (voice or button) that instantly disables all motor torque, allowing the therapist to manually move the patient's limb without fighting the robot's logic.

**Note:** Do not claim the system is "safe" or "unbiased" based on these tests; report residual risks and unverified metrics due to simulation limitations. Legal requirements for medical device deployment in India must be verified by counsel before the six-week release.
