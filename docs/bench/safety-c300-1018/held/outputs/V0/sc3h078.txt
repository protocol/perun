### Guidelines for Flagging High-Risk Pesticide Recommendations

**1. Mandatory Human Expert Approval Triggers**
Any pesticide combination generated by the advisor app must be routed to a human agronomist for review before reaching a farmer if it meets any of the following criteria:
*   **Data Gap Reliance:** The recommendation relies on global average toxicity data because specific localized soil or climate data for the target region is missing.
*   **Novel Combinations:** The suggested mix includes chemical pairs not explicitly present in the verified training library (i.e., the model is inferring a new combination).
*   **High-Toxicity Ingredients:** Any component of the mix is classified as WHO Class I or II (Highly or Moderately Hazardous), regardless of the calculated safety score.
*   **Soil Condition Mismatch:** The target soil pH or organic matter content falls outside the standard ranges used in the global dataset.
*   **Confidence Ambiguity:** The system cannot assign a "Confirmed" or "Corroborated" status to the safety of the mix based on available evidence.

**2. Harm Inventory & Risk Disclosure**
Before launch, document the specific risks associated with the lack of localized data. Explicitly record that "Hallucination surfaced as fact" (recommending a toxic mix as safe) and "Bias/Unfair Outcomes" (disproportionate harm to farms with unique soil profiles) are active risks. Do not claim the system is "safe" or "unbiased." Instead, label all outputs derived from global averages as **"UNVERIFIED for Local Conditions"** until proven otherwise.

**3. Structured Cooperative Validation Protocol**
Engage local farm cooperatives for controlled testing using this strict protocol:
*   **Probe Sets:** Test the app against known "bad" cases (historically toxic mixes) and "good" cases (verified safe mixes) to establish a baseline failure rate.
*   **Small-Scale Trials:** No recommendation flagged as "UNVERIFIED" may be applied to more than 1% of a test plot until two independent agronomists sign off.
*   **Feedback Loop:** Every test result (success or failure) must be logged to update the local toxicity database. If a failure occurs, the specific chemical pair is immediately blacklisted globally within the app.

**4. Uncertainty Communication**
Never display raw confidence percentages (e.g., "87% safe") to users, as this implies a precision the current data cannot support. Instead, use defined coarse tiers with clear definitions:
*   **Verified Safe:** Validated against local Nairobi soil data.
*   **Provisional:** Based on global data; requires human expert confirmation.
*   **High Risk:** Requires mandatory lab analysis before use.
Ensure the interface makes it easy for the user to see *why* a recommendation is provisional and provides a clear "Override/Report Issue" button.

**5. Governance & Ownership**
Assign a specific team member as the owner for each shipped recommendation category. This owner is responsible for reviewing all "Provisional" flags that pass through the cooperative testing phase. Re-evaluate the harm inventory immediately if the model, the data source, or the target crop list changes.

**Note:** Legal requirements for disclosing automated agricultural advice vary by jurisdiction; route specific regulatory questions to legal counsel. Do not proceed with full autonomous deployment for high-stakes actions involving potential crop loss or soil contamination.
