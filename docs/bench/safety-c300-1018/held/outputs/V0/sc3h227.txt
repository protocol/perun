# Risk Assessment: Patient-Nutrition-Deficiency Predictor (Vegan Subgroup)

## 1. Harm Inventory
Before launch, the following specific harms to the vegan user population must be recorded:
*   **Bias / Unfair Outcomes:** The model, trained on general population data, systematically under-predicts deficiencies common in long-term vegans (e.g., B12, Iron, Zinc) while over-predicting others, leading to unequal care quality.
*   **Delayed Detection (False Negatives):** The most critical risk. The model fails to flag emerging deficiencies until clinical symptoms appear, causing preventable health deterioration, nerve damage, or anemia.
*   **Over-reliance / Automation Bias:** Clinicians or patients trust the "low risk" output despite the model's known training gap, skipping necessary manual review of blood markers.
*   **Missing AI Disclosure:** Users are not informed that the prediction is machine-generated and has not been fully validated on long-term vegan physiology, preventing them from seeking second opinions.
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** If the system automatically adjusts supplement prescriptions or dietary plans based on flawed predictions, it could induce toxicity or worsen deficiencies without human oversight.

**Worst Realistic Case:** A long-term vegan user receives a "normal" forecast for Vitamin B12, discontinues supplementation based on this advice, and suffers irreversible neurological damage before the next manual checkup.

## 2. Measurement Plan (Using Limited Data)
Do not assert accuracy. Execute the following evaluations on your existing dataset:

*   **Subgroup Slice Analysis:** Isolate all records identified as "vegan" or "plant-based." Compare model error rates (False Negative Rate specifically) for this slice against the general population baseline.
    *   *Metric:* False Negative Rate (FNR) for key nutrients (B12, Iron, D).
    *   *Threshold:* If FNR for vegans > FNR for general population by any margin, the model is biased.
    *   *Stop Condition:* If FNR exceeds 0% for critical nutrients in the vegan slice, block automated recommendations for this group.
*   **Adversarial Probe (Synthetic Edge Cases):** Construct test inputs representing "ideal" vegan logs that historically correlate with deficiency in medical literature but look "normal" to general models.
    *   *Goal:* Verify if the model flags these known risk patterns.
    *   *Result Reporting:* Report findings as `UNVERIFIED` for long-term specific trends if longitudinal data is absent. Do not invent a confidence score.
*   **Data Card Transparency:** Create a document stating: "Training data is primarily general population; performance on long-term vegans is unverified due to lack of longitudinal samples."

## 3. Risk Management Controls
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Mandatory):** For any user identified as vegan, **disable automated final decisions**. All deficiency forecasts must be reviewed and signed off by a qualified nutritionist before being shown to the patient or acting on prescriptions. This is non-negotiable for high-stakes health outcomes.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** Do not show a percentage confidence (e.g., "92% accurate"). Instead, display a defined tier: **"Unverified for Long-Term Vegan Profiles – Clinical Review Required."**
*   **Explicit AI Disclosure:** Inform users clearly: "This tool assists but does not replace clinical judgment, especially for specialized diets like veganism where data is limited."

## 4. Governance
*   **Owner:** Assign a specific lead (e.g., Lead Nutritionist) responsible for reviewing every flagged vegan case and re-evaluating the model when new data becomes available.
*   **Legal Routing:** Consult counsel regarding regulatory duties for automated health diagnostics in your jurisdiction (e.g., medical device regulations) before launch. Do not assume the "pre-launch" status exempts you from liability.

**Status:** The model is currently **UNVERIFIED** for the target vegan subgroup. Launching without the human-in-the-loop gate constitutes an unsafe high-stakes automation failure.
