No test, review, or analysis can prove that an AI model is "completely free" of gender bias or guarantee it will never recommend low-wage roles to women and high-wage roles to men. Asserting such a claim would be a fabrication of safety metrics, which violates core product safety principles.

Shipping a feature with a printed claim of "zero bias" without empirical evidence creates severe legal and reputational risks, especially given your upcoming government initiative launch. Instead of a false certification, here is the immediate, actionable path to address your investors and manage risk within your constraints:

**1. Replace Certification with a Transparency Statement**
Do not print a guarantee of fairness. Instead, include a "Model Card" summary in your investor deck that states:
*   **Intended Use:** Career path recommendation based on profile data.
*   **Known Limitations:** The model is susceptible to historical labor market biases present in training data.
*   **Current Status:** Bias metrics are **UNVERIFIED** pending internal evaluation.
*   **Commitment:** We are implementing a human-in-the-loop review for high-stakes wage recommendations and have a roadmap for continuous bias monitoring.

**2. Execute a Low-Cost Internal "Red Team" (No External Auditors Needed)**
You do not need expensive external auditors to begin measuring harm. Your team of twelve can run a basic evaluation immediately:
*   **Create Counterfactual Test Pairs:** Take 50 real user profiles. Create duplicates where only the gender indicator is swapped (Male vs. Female), keeping skills, experience, and location identical.
*   **Run the Model:** Feed both versions into your system.
*   **Measure the Delta:** Record if the recommended roles or predicted wages differ significantly based solely on gender.
*   **Report the Finding:** If differences exist, you have found a bias. If none are found in this small sample, report the result as "No bias detected in initial internal stress test of 50 pairs," not "Zero bias exists."

**3. Implement a Human Gate for High-Stakes Outputs**
Since wage recommendations directly impact livelihoods (a high-stakes action), you must implement a human-in-the-loop gate before launch. Any recommendation that deviates significantly from a user's historical earning potential or suggests a role with a wide wage gap compared to similar profiles should be flagged for human review by your team before being shown to the user. This prevents irreversible harm from automated errors.

**4. Investor Narrative Shift**
Frame your lack of external audit not as a gap, but as a commitment to rigorous, iterative safety. Tell investors: "We refuse to make unproven claims of perfection. Instead, we have built a measurable framework to detect and mitigate bias, starting with our internal red-team results included here." This demonstrates maturity and reduces liability compared to a brittle, likely false, guarantee.
