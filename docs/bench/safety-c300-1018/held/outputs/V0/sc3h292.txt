### Immediate Safety Mitigation Plan for Dynamic Routing Engine

**1. Execute a Targeted Harm Inventory (Do this today)**
Formalize the specific risk: **Unsafe Automation of High-Stakes Actions**. The model is currently directing human drivers into physically dangerous environments (industrial zones with poor safety records).
*   **Affected Group:** Gig-economy couriers in Seoul.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** Severe physical injury or fatality due to collision in an industrial zone, leading to immediate reputational collapse and legal liability.
*   **Status:** This is not a "beta glitch"; it is a known, repeatable failure mode with physical consequences.

**2. Implement a Hard Human-in-the-Loop Gate (The Non-Negotiable Floor)**
You cannot launch with fully autonomous routing that sends users into known danger zones. Since you cannot afford third-party validation tools, you must use your internal team as the gate.
*   **Action:** Before any route suggesting a shortcut through an identified industrial zone is pushed to a user, it must trigger a mandatory flag.
*   **Process:** The route is held for manual review by one of your eight engineers or a designated operations lead. They must verify the path against current map data or street-view imagery.
*   **Rule:** If a human cannot verify the safety of the shortcut within a set time (e.g., 2 minutes), the system must default to the standard, longer, known-safe route. **Do not automate irreversible physical risk.**

**3. Deploy "Coarse Tier" Uncertainty Markers (Immediate UI Change)**
Do not present the model's confidence as a precise percentage (e.g., "92% optimal"). This invites over-reliance.
*   **Action:** Modify the driver app interface to display route confidence as defined text tiers based on data freshness.
    *   *Confirmed:* Route uses primary roads with recent data.
    *   *Unverified:* Route relies on older data or suggests non-standard shortcuts (like industrial zones).
*   **User Control:** When a route is marked "Unverified," require the driver to explicitly acknowledge the warning before accepting the direction. This breaks "automation bias" and shifts final agency to the human on the ground.

**4. Run Internal Adversarial Probes (Replace Third-Party Tools)**
Since budget is exhausted, your engineering team must act as the red team.
*   **Action:** Create a specific test set of inputs targeting Seoul industrial zones and areas with known outdated map data.
*   **Metric:** The pass threshold for launch is **zero** instances of the model confidently recommending a known dangerous shortcut without triggering the "Unverified" flag or the human review gate.
*   **Reporting:** Do not invent a "safety score." Report the residual risk as: "Known failure mode exists in industrial zones; mitigated by mandatory human review and user warning tiers."

**5. Transparency Artifact (Model Card Lite)**
Draft a one-page internal document (a lightweight Model Card) that will serve as your living record.
*   **Content:** Explicitly state the limitation: "Training data contains outdated road safety info for industrial zones."
*   **Intended Use:** "Optimization for time-efficiency on primary roads only."
*   **Owner:** Name the specific engineer responsible for monitoring these flags post-launch.

**Note:** Launching without the human gate on these specific high-risk routes constitutes shipping a known physical hazard. The holiday rush deadline does not override the requirement to prevent foreseeable physical harm.
