### Rapid Evaluation Protocol & Mitigation Plan for Fall-Alert System

**1. Immediate Mitigation Steps (Within Certification Constraints)**
Since sensor sensitivity thresholds cannot be altered without re-certification, you must introduce a **human-in-the-loop gate** and **output transparency** layers at the software or workflow level:

*   **Implement a "Corroborated vs. Unverified" Alert Tier:** Do not send all detections as high-priority pages. Classify alerts based on signal convergence:
    *   **Confirmed (High Priority):** Audio impact + sustained floor pressure + lack of subsequent movement. Notify immediately via loud alarm.
    *   **Unverified (Low Priority):** Audio impact only OR brief pressure spike. Send a silent notification to a central dashboard or a secondary device, requiring a staff member to visually verify via camera (if available) or a quick physical check before triggering a full response team. This reduces "alarm fatigue" by filtering low-confidence events out of the primary interrupt channel.
*   **Mandate Explicit Acknowledgment:** Require staff to actively dismiss or categorize every alert (e.g., "True Fall," "Dropped Object," "False Alarm") within the app. This creates an immediate feedback loop for data collection without changing sensor firmware.
*   **Contextual Disclosure:** Clearly label lower-tier alerts on the staff interface as "AI-Assisted Detection – Requires Verification" to prevent over-reliance on automated decisions.

**2. Rapid Evaluation Protocol (48-Hour Sprint)**
To quantify the impact of false positives on staff response times before launch, execute the following focused measurement plan:

*   **Define the Metric:** Measure **Time-to-Verification** (time from alert to staff acknowledgment) and **Time-to-Resolution** (time from alert to confirmed status), segmented by alert tier (Confirmed vs. Unverified).
*   **Probe Set (Adversarial Simulation):**
    *   *Positive Controls:* Simulate 5 known falls (using mannequins) during different shifts.
    *   *Negative Controls:* Simulate 20 known non-fall events (dropping keys, running, sitting down heavily) that previously triggered false alarms.
    *   *Subgroup Slicing:* Run these probes during peak activity (morning rush) vs. quiet hours to test staff load impact.
*   **Execution:** Run these 25 simulated events over two shifts. Record exactly how long staff take to respond to the "Unverified" silent alerts versus the "Confirmed" loud alarms.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the average response time to *Confirmed* falls increases by more than 30 seconds compared to baseline (due to distraction from Unverified alerts), the current tiering logic is unsafe. Do not launch until the filtering logic is adjusted to reduce noise further.
*   **Residual Risk Reporting:** Report the false positive rate observed during this sprint as **UNVERIFIED** for the general population until long-term data is collected. Do not claim a specific accuracy percentage (e.g., "99% accurate") based on this small sample.

**3. Governance & Transparency Artifact**
Before pilot launch, draft a one-page **Model Card** for the facility managers containing:
*   **Intended Use:** Fall detection in assisted living; **Out-of-Scope:** Medical diagnosis or unsupervised autonomous emergency dispatch.
*   **Known Limitations:** High false-positive rate for dropped objects; reliance on staff verification for non-convergent signals.
*   **Measured Performance:** Insert the results from the 48-hour sprint (e.g., "In testing, Unverified alerts reduced nuisance pages by X%, but added Y seconds to verification time").
*   **Owner:** Name the specific engineer or care specialist responsible for reviewing alert logs weekly.

**Note:** Because this system involves high-stakes safety outcomes, fully autonomous notification without the proposed verification tier constitutes an unsafe automation risk. Legal requirements for automated decision disclosures in healthcare settings must be routed to counsel immediately.
