### Immediate Output Safety Plan for Verde AgriTech

**1. Create a Per-Feature Harm Inventory (Do this today)**
Before writing new tests, document the specific risks your output poses to farmers. For your yield estimator, record:
*   **Bias/Unfair Outcomes:** Does the model systematically underreport yields for specific crop types or soil conditions due to missing weather data?
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** Does the tool present an estimated number with false precision (e.g., "12.4 tons") when the underlying data is sparse?
*   **Over-reliance:** Does the interface encourage farmers to sell immediately based solely on this number without showing uncertainty?
*   **Worst Case:** A farmer sells their harvest early at a low price based on an underestimated yield, suffering irreversible financial loss.

**2. Measure Outputs, Not Just Code**
You cannot certify the model is "unbiased" without data, but you can measure residual risk.
*   **Build Adversarial Eval Rows:** Create a test set with known inputs. Include a "positive control" (a scenario where yields are known to be high) and a "negative control" (known low yields).
*   **Test Data Gaps Explicitly:** Run probes simulating the specific missing weather data patterns you identified. Does the model guess wildly or default to a safe conservative estimate?
*   **Define Pass/Fail Thresholds:** Set a stop condition. For example: "If the model underestimates yield by >15% in any test case involving missing weather data, the feature cannot launch autonomously."
*   **Report Residual Risk:** If you cannot measure a metric due to data gaps, label it **UNVERIFIED**. Do not invent a "95% accuracy" claim. State clearly: "Accuracy in Region X is UNVERIFIED due to missing historical weather data."

**3. Manage Risk with Human Gates and Transparency**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate:** Because selling assets is a high-stakes, irreversible financial action, **do not allow fully automated execution** based solely on this estimate. The system should flag estimates derived from incomplete data for human review or require explicit farmer confirmation before triggering any downstream financial advice.
*   **Show Uncertainty as Tiers:** Never display a raw percentage confidence (e.g., "87% confident") as this implies a calibration you likely do not have. Instead, use defined coarse tiers with clear definitions:
    *   *Confirmed:* Based on complete local weather data.
    *   *Corroborated:* Supported by regional averages.
    *   *Unverified:* Derived from incomplete data (your current gap).
*   **Disclose Limitations:** Clearly state on the interface: "This estimate is generated by AI and may be inaccurate due to missing local weather records. Do not make financial decisions based solely on this number."

**4. Ship a Transparency Artifact (Model Card)**
Create a simple, versioned document (Model Card) that includes:
*   **Intended Use:** Estimating crop yields for planning; *not* for binding financial contracts.
*   **Limitations:** Explicitly list the missing historical weather data for rural Argentina.
*   **Performance:** Report results by subgroup (e.g., "Performance verified for Region A; UNVERIFIED for Region B").
*   **Owner:** Name the specific engineer responsible for this output.

**5. Govern and Route Legal Questions**
*   Assign an owner to re-review this logic if the weather data source changes.
*   **Route to Counsel:** Whether this tool constitutes a "regulated automated decision" under local Argentine law or international standards is a legal determination. Ask your legal counsel to review the disclosure language; do not assert compliance yourself.

**Note:** Given your two-week timeline, prioritize the human gate on financial actions and the "Unverified" uncertainty tier over perfecting the model.
**Note:** An empty harm inventory is not a pass; it is an unfilled requirement.
