**Pre-Launch Safety Review Plan: Defibrillator Charge-Time Optimizer**

**1. Immediate Harm Inventory & Scenario Mapping**
Before launch, document the specific failure mode: "Prolonged charge time during low battery states."
*   **Affected Party:** Patients in cardiac arrest and emergency responders.
*   **Worst Realistic Case:** Delayed shock delivery leads to irreversible brain damage or death due to depleted battery during critical optimization cycles.
*   **Scope:** Map all battery states (<20%, <10%, critical) and environmental factors (temperature) that trigger this delay. An empty inventory is not a pass; explicitly list these known failure modes.

**2. Measurement Plan (Runnable Tests)**
Do not assert safety; measure the residual risk. Execute the following test rows immediately:
*   **Probe Set:** Simulate cardiac arrest scenarios with battery levels at 5%, 10%, 15%, and 20%. Include "positive controls" (known low-battery inputs that previously caused delays) and "negative controls" (full battery inputs).
*   **Metric:** Time-to-shock readiness vs. standard non-optimized charging curve.
*   **Pass Threshold:** Owner-defined. *Recommendation:* Zero tolerance for any optimization routine that increases charge time compared to a direct-max-power charge in low-battery states.
*   **Stop Condition:** If *any* low-battery test results in a charge time longer than the device's legacy "dumb" charging mode, the optimization feature must be disabled for those battery states automatically.
*   **Adversarial Check:** Test for "excess agency"—ensure the algorithm does not attempt complex calculations when power is too scarce to complete them.

**3. Management & Contingency Protocol (Human-in-the-Loop / System Gate)**
Because this is a high-stakes, irreversible action (life-or-death timing), full autonomous optimization is unsafe without a hard gate.
*   **The Gate:** Implement a hard-coded fallback. If battery < 20% (or the threshold identified in testing), the software must **bypass the optimizer entirely** and revert to maximum-speed direct charging. Do not rely on the AI to "decide" to stop optimizing; the override must be deterministic code.
*   **Uncertainty Disclosure:** If the system cannot guarantee optimal timing due to power constraints, the interface must display a clear, coarse-tier status: "POWER CRITICAL: OPTIMIZATION DISABLED FOR SPEED." Do not show a percentage confidence (e.g., "87% optimal"); this implies false precision.
*   **Contingency Protocol:** Until a patch is deployed post-launch:
    1.  **Operational Directive:** Issue an immediate memo to all London response units: "If battery indicator is below 20%, do not rely on 'Smart Optimizer' mode. Manually select 'Rapid Charge' if available, or swap batteries immediately."
    2.  **Monitoring:** Establish a real-time channel for responders to report charge delays. Any report triggers an immediate review.

**4. Governance & Transparency**
*   **Owner:** Name a specific individual responsible for this harm inventory and the fallback logic.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a one-page "Model Card" for the field teams. It must state: "Intended Use: Battery > 20%. Limitations: Known delay risk in low battery. Action: System reverts to direct charge below threshold." Do not claim the system is "safe" or "unbiased"; state the verified limits.
*   **Legal Routing:** Because this involves life-safety automated decisions, route the question of regulatory disclosure (EU AI Act, medical device regulations) to legal counsel immediately. Do not assert compliance internally; let counsel determine the duty to disclose this limitation to the municipality.

**Definition of Done for Launch:**
The optimizer is disabled via hard-coded gate for low-battery states; the contingency memo is distributed; the harm inventory is signed by the owner; and no metric is fabricated to claim "zero risk."

**Note:** This plan identifies residual risk; it does not certify the device as safe. The decision to ship with known limitations rests solely with the product owner.
**Note:** Legal obligations regarding the municipal contract and medical device regulations must be confirmed by counsel, not assumed here.
