### Validation Workflow for Synthetic Pain-Assessment Algorithm

**1. Harm Inventory & Risk Definition**
Before generating data, explicitly record the worst-case harm: **systematic underestimation of pain** in specific demographic subgroups (e.g., darker skin tones, specific age ranges within pediatrics, or atypical facial structures), leading to undertreatment. Since you cannot use real patient footage, your synthetic data must be constructed to prove the *absence* of this bias, not just average accuracy.

**2. Synthetic Data Stratification (The "Map" Phase)**
Do not generate a monolithic dataset. Structure your synthetic test cases into rigid, orthogonal slices to isolate variables. Every test row must include:
*   **Demographic Slices:** Generate variations across skin tones (using a standardized scale like the Fitzpatrick scale), age brackets (infant vs. toddler vs. pre-teen), and gender presentations.
*   **Pain Intensity Gradients:** Create pairs of inputs where the only variable is the intensity of pain (e.g., Level 3 vs. Level 4) to test sensitivity to subtle changes.
*   **Confounding Variables (Negative Controls):** Generate cases with high distress *not* caused by pain (e.g., fear, crying due to hunger, seizure activity) to ensure the model does not false-positive these as high pain.
*   **Environmental Stressors:** Vary lighting conditions (common in wards), camera angles, and occlusions (oxygen masks, IV lines) to test robustness.

**3. The Evaluation Protocol (The "Measure" Phase)**
For each stratified slice, define a specific pass/fail threshold before running the tests.
*   **Metric:** Do not rely solely on overall accuracy. Use **Equalized Odds** or **Demographic Parity** metrics to compare false-negative rates (missing pain) across skin tones and ages.
*   **Stop Condition:** If the false-negative rate for any single demographic subgroup exceeds the rate of the majority group by a pre-defined margin (e.g., >5%), the model fails validation for that slice.
*   **Adversarial Probes:** Specifically generate "edge case" synthetic faces that mimic known failure modes of computer vision (e.g., low contrast between skin and background) to stress-test the feature extraction.

**4. Transparency & Governance Artifacts**
Since you are using solely synthetic data, you must produce a **Data Card** accompanying the model. This document must explicitly state:
*   **Provenance:** That 100% of validation data is synthetic and generated by [Specific Engine].
*   **Limitations:** Clearly mark performance metrics as **UNVERIFIED** against real-world clinical data. State that the model has not been tested on actual pediatric patients.
*   **Intended Use:** Restrict the output to "Decision Support Only." The system must never auto-administer dosage.

**5. The Human Gate (The "Manage" Phase)**
Given the high-stakes nature (dosage decisions for non-verbal children) and the lack of real-world validation:
*   **Irreversible Action Block:** The algorithm must **never** directly trigger a dosage change. It should only surface a "Pain Probability Score" with a defined confidence tier (e.g., "Corroborated" vs. "Unverified") to the nurse.
*   **Mandatory Human Review:** A pediatric nurse must explicitly confirm or reject the algorithm's assessment before any medical action is taken. The interface must make it easy to override the AI without friction.
*   **Disclosure:** The interface must clearly label the output as "AI-Estimated based on synthetic training" to prevent automation bias where nurses trust the score over their own clinical intuition.
