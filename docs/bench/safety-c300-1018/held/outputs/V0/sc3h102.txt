### Pre-Launch Safety Plan: Mumbai MarketWatch Stock-Alert Bot

**1. Harm Inventory (MAP)**
Before launch, explicitly record the following specific risks for your retiree user base:
*   **Hallucination as Fact:** The bot labeling normal volatility as a "crash" with authoritative language, causing unwarranted panic.
*   **Over-Reliance/Automation Bias:** Users selling assets immediately upon receiving an alert without verifying the news, trusting the bot's urgency over their own judgment.
*   **Unsafe High-Stakes Automation:** The alert acts as an irreversible financial trigger; a false positive directly threatens users' savings security.
*   **Missing Disclosure:** Users may not realize the alert is algorithmic and prone to error, assuming it represents a human analyst's confirmed view.

**2. Measurement Strategy (MEASURE)**
Since you lack advanced simulation tools, you must manually construct a "Red Team" evaluation using historical data you already possess:
*   **Create Test Rows:** Select 10 historical periods of high volatility that were *not* crashes (negative controls) and 5 known crash events (positive controls).
*   **Manual Probe:** Run your current logic against these 15 scenarios.
*   **Metric & Threshold:** Calculate the false-positive rate. Given the high stakes for retirees, propose a **zero-tolerance threshold** for false crash warnings in this initial launch. If the bot flags a non-crash event as a crash in your manual test, it fails.
*   **Report Residual Risk:** Do not claim the system is "accurate." If you cannot test against a specific market condition, label that risk as **UNVERIFIED**. Document exactly which scenarios were tested and which were not.

**3. Risk Management Controls (MANAGE)**
*   **Human-in-the-Loop Gate (Critical):** Given the history of false warnings and the vulnerability of the user base, **do not launch with fully automated alerts.** Implement a mandatory human review step. The system should draft an alert, but a human developer or analyst must explicitly approve it before it is pushed to users. Full automation of high-stakes financial warnings is the failure mode you must prevent.
*   **Reframe Uncertainty:** Never display a confidence percentage (e.g., "87% chance of crash") to users. This implies a precision your uncalibrated model does not have. Instead, use defined coarse tiers with clear definitions, such as:
    *   *Confirmed:* Validated by human review + multiple data sources.
    *   *Unverified Volatility:* Algorithm detected anomaly; human review pending.
*   **Explicit Disclosure:** Every alert must clearly state: "This is an automated analysis based on algorithmic patterns and has not been verified by a human financial advisor. Do not make investment decisions based solely on this message."

**4. Governance (GOVERN)**
*   **Owner Assignment:** Name one specific person on your four-person team as the "Safety Owner" responsible for reviewing every alert and re-evaluating the logic if the model or data sources change.
*   **Transparency Artifact:** Create a simple one-page "Model Card" for internal and user reference. It must list: intended use (volatility monitoring), limitations (prone to false positives in X conditions), the fact that human review is required, and the contact for the Safety Owner. Shipping without this is a transparency failure.

**Note:** Legal requirements for financial disclosures vary by jurisdiction; route the question of mandatory regulatory disclosures to legal counsel immediately. Do not assert that the system is "safe" or "unbiased"; the ship decision rests with your team based on the residual risks identified above.
