### Critical Safety Assessment: Do Not Ship as Proposed

**Immediate Halt Required:** You cannot proceed with the beta release of a patient-medication-alert system that relies on outdated data without a human-in-the-loop gate. In a high-stakes domain like healthcare, automating warnings based on unverified or obsolete data constitutes an unsafe automated action. The risk of "false positives" (unnecessary alarm) is secondary to the catastrophic risk of "false negatives" (missing a real interaction due to data gaps) or users learning to ignore all warnings due to noise.

**1. Evaluation of Incorrect Warning Rate**
I cannot estimate the rate of incorrect warnings. Any specific percentage provided would be a fabricated metric because:
*   I have no access to your specific logic, the "older dataset," or the "known common medication pairs" you mentioned.
*   You explicitly stated you lack access to the latest local database updates required for verification.
*   **Action:** You must treat the current error rate as **UNVERIFIED**. To measure it, you must run a red-team evaluation using a probe set of known-positive interactions (confirmed dangerous pairs) and known-negative controls (safe pairs) against your current system. Until this test is run and reviewed by a clinical expert, no claim about accuracy can be made.

**2. Communication Strategy**
Do not attempt to "soften" the message to prevent alarm fatigue; this creates a deceptive pattern where users cannot distinguish between a system glitch and a life-saving warning.
*   **Mandatory Disclosure:** Every output must carry a clear, prominent disclosure: *"This alert is generated by an automated system using incomplete data. It has not been verified by a pharmacist."*
*   **Uncertainty Tiering:** Do not show confidence scores (e.g., "87% likely"). Instead, use defined coarse tiers. Given your data constraints, all alerts should be tiered as **"Unverified – Requires Human Review."**
*   **The Human Gate:** The system must **not** deliver the final warning directly to the patient. The workflow must be: System Flag → Pharmacist Review → Pharmacist delivers verbal/written warning to patient. The pharmacist is the required human-in-the-loop for this irreversible/high-stakes action.

**3. Prioritization Plan for Manual Verification**
Since you cannot verify everything, prioritize based on severity and prevalence, not convenience:
*   **Tier 1 (Critical/High-Prevalence):** Focus immediately on drug pairs common in Cairo that result in death or permanent injury (e.g., anticoagulants, insulin, opioids). These require 100% manual verification against whatever primary sources (textbooks, senior pharmacists) are available before any software use.
*   **Tier 2 (Moderate/High-Prevalence):** Common drugs causing hospitalization.
*   **Tier 3 (Low Severity):** Side effects that are uncomfortable but not dangerous.
*   **Stop Condition:** If you cannot manually verify Tier 1 pairs before the two-week deadline, the beta launch must be cancelled. Launching with unverified critical interaction logic is negligent.
