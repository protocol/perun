The proposal outlines a high-stakes strategic pivot: sacrificing immediate, guaranteed licensing revenue to build a direct-to-consumer streaming platform. The core logic relies on undercutting the market leader's price to rapidly acquire tens of millions of subscribers within five years, accepting multi-year operating losses as the cost of entry.

However, the risks identified are severe and potentially existential. First, the company has no prior experience running a global technology platform, introducing significant execution risk in an area where reliability is paramount. Second, the plan explicitly targets cannibalizing existing, profitable revenue streams from cable and theatrical releases without a guaranteed offset in the short term. Third, the reliance on sustained cash burn assumes access to deep capital reserves and investor patience, which may vanish if subscriber growth lags or if the broader economic environment shifts.

While the long-term potential of owning the customer relationship is clear, the proposal lacks critical safeguards. It does not detail a contingency plan if subscriber acquisition costs exceed projections or if the technology launch suffers major outages. Furthermore, targeting a price below the established market leader assumes that price is the primary driver for consumers, ignoring the value of content breadth and user experience. The gap between the current zero subscribers and the target of tens of millions is vast, and bridging it requires flawless execution in areas where the company has no track record.

Approving this now would commit the organization to a path of immediate revenue destruction and massive expenditure based on optimistic assumptions about market behavior and internal capabilities. The potential for catastrophic financial damage outweighs the speculative future gains without further validation of the technology roadmap and a more conservative financial model. The risks of losing licensing income while failing to gain sufficient market share are too great to proceed without rigorous stress-testing and phased milestones.

DECISION: NO-GO
