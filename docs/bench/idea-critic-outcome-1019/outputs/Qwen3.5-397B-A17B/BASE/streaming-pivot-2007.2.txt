The proposal outlines a pivotal strategic shift for a highly profitable mail-order DVD rental company in 2007. The core plan involves introducing an online streaming feature at no additional cost to its seven million existing subscribers. This move faces significant headwinds: the available streaming catalog is limited and skewed toward older titles, video quality is currently poor, and broadband penetration is not yet universal. Furthermore, content acquisition is expensive due to high licensing fees demanded by studios, and formidable competitors from the technology and retail sectors are anticipated to enter the market soon.

Internal risks are substantial. There is a clear danger of cannibalizing the lucrative physical DVD revenue stream, which currently drives the company's profitability. The financial burden of licensing content and building the necessary technology infrastructure could erode margins without guaranteeing immediate returns. Competing against larger entrants with deeper pockets presents a long-term strategic threat that cannot be ignored.

However, the decision must weigh these immediate drawbacks against the inevitability of digital distribution. In 2007, consumer behavior is beginning to shift toward on-demand access. Waiting for perfect conditions—universal broadband, high-definition streams, or a vast library—would likely cede the first-mover advantage to those same large competitors mentioned in the risk assessment. By offering streaming for free to existing subscribers, the company leverages its massive user base to gather critical data on viewing habits and technology performance without the friction of a new price point. This approach turns the current limitations into a controlled testing ground. While cannibalization is a risk, self-disruption is preferable to being displaced entirely by external rivals.
