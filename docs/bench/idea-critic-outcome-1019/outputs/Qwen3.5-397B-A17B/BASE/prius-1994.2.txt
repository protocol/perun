The proposal presents a high-stakes gamble with insufficient foundational support. Committing to mass production by 1997 requires a timeline that appears unrealistic given the novelty of the power-split transmission and the unproven nature of nickel-metal-hydride batteries in volume applications. While being first to market offers a potential strategic advantage, the current environment lacks the necessary drivers to justify such immense risk.

Fuel prices in the primary export market remain low, removing the immediate economic incentive for consumers to adopt a potentially more expensive hybrid vehicle. Furthermore, without regulatory mandates forcing efficiency improvements, there is no external pressure compelling buyers to choose this technology over conventional internal combustion engines. The absence of a near-term demand signal suggests that the market is not ready to absorb the likely premium pricing required to recoup development and manufacturing costs.

The technical risks are substantial. Battery durability has not been validated, creating a significant exposure to warranty claims and reputation damage if failures occur post-launch. Reliability issues with a novel transmission system could lead to costly recalls. With no competitors currently pursuing production hybrids, the industry hesitation likely reflects shared concerns about feasibility and profitability that this proposal fails to adequately address.

Proceeding now with a bold public schedule locks the corporation into a rigid path before critical technical and market uncertainties are resolved. The combination of unproven technology, lack of consumer demand, low fuel costs, and high financial exposure creates a scenario where the probability of failure outweighs the potential first-mover benefits. Resources should not be committed to full-scale production under these conditions. A pause is required to validate battery life, reduce transmission costs, and wait for clearer market signals or regulatory changes before advancing.

DECISION: NO-GO
