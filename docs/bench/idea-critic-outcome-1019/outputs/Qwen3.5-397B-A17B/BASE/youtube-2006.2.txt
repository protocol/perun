The proposal involves acquiring the dominant online video platform for $1.65 billion in stock despite the target having zero meaningful revenue, a tiny staff, and massive operational costs. The timing is October 2006, meaning the target is less than two years old. The primary asset is market leadership in user-generated content, but this comes with severe liabilities: soaring bandwidth bills and imminent copyright lawsuits from major media companies regarding unauthorized TV and film clips.

The acquiring entity already operates a smaller, internal video service, suggesting redundancy unless the target's scale is deemed insurmountable to replicate organically. However, the financial mechanics are deeply flawed based on the provided facts. Paying a premium valuation for an entity with no proven advertising model and negative cash flow creates immediate dilution for shareholders without guaranteed returns. The "unproven ad model" represents a speculative bet that may never materialize, while the bandwidth costs will escalate directly with user growth, worsening the burn rate.

Furthermore, the legal exposure is critical. With media companies already threatening litigation over widespread copyright infringement, the acquisition transfers massive legal liability to the buyer. Resolving these issues could require costly licensing deals or fundamental changes to the platform's functionality, potentially destroying the very user engagement that justifies the purchase price. The combination of an unverified revenue stream, exponential infrastructure costs, and existential legal threats makes the $1.65 billion price tag indefensible based strictly on current facts. The risks overwhelmingly outweigh the potential benefit of market share, especially when the buyer already possesses a functioning, albeit smaller, alternative. Proceeding now exposes the company to catastrophic financial and legal jeopardy without a clear path to profitability.

DECISION: NO-GO
