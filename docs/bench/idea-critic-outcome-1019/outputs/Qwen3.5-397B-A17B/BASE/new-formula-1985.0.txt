The proposal relies heavily on quantitative data from blind taste tests, surveys, and focus groups, all indicating a consumer preference for a sweeter formula over both the existing product and the main rival. While the data suggests the new formula wins on pure taste, the strategy contains a critical blind spot: the testing methodology failed to inform participants that the original formula would be permanently withdrawn. This omission is fatal because it ignores the profound emotional attachment consumers have to the century-old brand and its heritage.

Taste is only one component of brand loyalty; the other is identity and tradition. By removing the original entirely without a fallback option, the company risks alienating its most loyal customer base and provoking a backlash from bottlers who rely on the established product's stability. The risks identified—emotional attachment, brand heritage, lack of a fallback, and potential reaction from stakeholders—are not merely theoretical concerns but immediate threats to market position.

Proceeding with a total replacement based solely on blind taste metrics disregards the psychological contract between the brand and its consumers. The absence of the original formula in the future marketplace changes the context of the choice entirely, rendering the current test results incomplete. A strategy that eliminates the legacy product without a phased approach or a parallel offering exposes the company to unnecessary volatility. The potential damage to brand equity and the relationship with the distribution network outweighs the projected gains from a sweeter taste profile. Approval cannot be granted for a plan that intentionally withholds material information from testers and ignores the non-rational drivers of consumer behavior. The proposal must be rejected in its current form to prevent a catastrophic loss of trust and market share.

DECISION: NO-GO
