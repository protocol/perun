The proposal to split the combined service into two distinct brands with separate billing, logins, and queues introduces significant friction for the customer base. This move follows immediately after a 60 percent price increase for the combined plan, creating a compounding negative experience. The rationale cites the declining nature of the DVD business and the need for strategic independence; however, the execution plan ignores the severe risks explicitly identified: customer confusion, the inconvenience of managing two accounts, and the loss of valuable cross-service data sharing.

Separating the brands eliminates the synergy that likely justified the original combined model. By siloing ratings and recommendations, the company reduces the value proposition for subscribers who utilize both formats. The timing is particularly dangerous. Implementing a complex structural separation and forcing customers to navigate new websites and billing systems right after a massive price hike signals a disregard for user experience. This approach is highly likely to trigger a backlash, accelerating churn rather than stabilizing the declining DVD segment or boosting streaming adoption.

The risks of confusion and dissatisfaction outweigh the theoretical management benefits of separation at this specific moment. The market has not had time to absorb the recent price adjustment. Introducing further complexity now appears tone-deaf to customer needs and prioritizes internal operational desires over external stability. Without a phased approach or a clear mitigation strategy for the identified risks, proceeding immediately threatens the brand's reputation and subscriber retention. The proposal fails to address how the separation improves the immediate customer reality, focusing instead on internal segmentation during a period of high price sensitivity. Approval in the current form would likely result in avoidable revenue loss and reputational damage.

DECISION: NO-GO
