The proposal seeks to structurally separate a combined DVD and streaming service following a recent 60 percent price increase for the bundled plan. The rationale cites the declining nature of the DVD business and the need for distinct management strategies. However, the execution plan introduces severe friction points that directly compound existing customer dissatisfaction.

Splitting the services into separate brands with unique accounts, queues, and billing systems forces customers to manage two distinct relationships with the company. This creates immediate operational confusion and frustration, particularly for a user base already reeling from a massive price hike. The loss of shared ratings and recommendations dismantles the integrated value proposition that likely justified the previous premium pricing, leaving customers with a fragmented experience that feels like a reduction in service quality rather than an optimization.

The risks identified in the proposal itself are critical and currently unmitigated. Customer confusion is guaranteed when forcing a migration to new websites and login credentials. The removal of cross-service data intelligence weakens the product's core utility. Most dangerously, implementing this complex separation immediately after a 60 percent price increase signals to the market that the company is prioritizing internal structural convenience over customer retention. This timing suggests a disconnect from consumer sentiment, likely accelerating churn rather than stabilizing the declining DVD segment or boosting streaming adoption.

Approving this now would validate a strategy that alienates the remaining loyal customer base during a period of extreme price sensitivity. The proposal lacks any phase-in period, communication strategy, or incentive structure to soften the blow of this dual shock (price hike followed by service fragmentation). Without addressing the compounded negative reaction to both the cost and the complexity, proceeding is reckless. The plan must be halted to redesign a approach that does not treat existing subscribers as an afterthought.

DECISION: NO-GO
