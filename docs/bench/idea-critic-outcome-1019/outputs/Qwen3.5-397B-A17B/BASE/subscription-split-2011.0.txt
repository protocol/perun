The proposal seeks to separate a combined DVD and streaming service into two distinct brands with separate billing, logins, and recommendation engines. This follows a recent 60 percent price increase for the combined plan. The stated rationale is that the declining DVD business requires different management than the growing streaming sector. However, the risks identified are severe and immediate. Splitting the services creates significant friction for customers who must now manage two accounts, two passwords, and two bills. This directly compounds the negative sentiment likely generated by the recent steep price hike.

Furthermore, severing the link between the two services eliminates cross-platform data sharing. Customers lose the benefit of unified ratings and recommendations, degrading the user experience that likely drove initial adoption. The proposal offers no mitigation strategy for the anticipated customer confusion or the potential backlash from appearing to force a breakup after a price increase. Executing this split immediately, without a transitional period or a clear communication plan to address the dual-billing burden, invites high churn rates. The operational complexity of launching a new brand, website, and billing system while managing public relations fallout suggests the organization is not ready to proceed as currently outlined. The potential loss of customer trust outweighs the strategic benefit of separate management structures at this specific moment. The risks of alienating the existing subscriber base are too high to approve the plan in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
