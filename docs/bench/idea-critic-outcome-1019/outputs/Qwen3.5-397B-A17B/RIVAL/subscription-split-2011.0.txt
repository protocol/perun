This proposal attempts to solve a pricing backlash and strategic misalignment by executing a corporate divorce mid-crisis. The logic contains a fatal flaw: it treats a symptom (brand confusion regarding price hikes) while ignoring the disease (a 60% price increase that alienated the core user base).

Splitting the services into separate brands, accounts, and billing systems creates immediate, tangible friction for the customer. Users currently enjoying a unified experience will suddenly face two logins, two queues, and two credit card charges. In the context of a recent massive price hike, this feels less like a strategic pivot and more like a punitive unbundling designed to obscure costs. The loss of shared ratings and recommendations degrades the product value for both sides; the DVD library informs streaming tastes and vice versa. Severing this data link makes both services dumber.

The rationale that the DVD business is "declining" is valid, but the proposed solution accelerates that decline into a death spiral. By walling off the DVD service under a new, unknown brand, you signal to those loyal customers that they are unwanted baggage. They will not migrate to a new site with a new password; they will cancel. Meanwhile, the streaming service loses the goodwill and content depth associated with the legacy brand during its most vulnerable growth phase.

The risks identified in the proposal—customer confusion, friction, and backlash—are not merely potential side effects; they are the guaranteed primary outcomes. This move doubles down on the arrogance of the price hike by adding operational complexity and user hostility. It solves a management convenience problem (wanting to run the units separately) by creating a customer experience nightmare.

A better path exists: keep the brand unified, absorb the pricing mistake, and manage the transition internally without forcing the customer to manage two separate relationships. Do not fracture the user experience to satisfy internal organizational charts. The cost of re-acquiring lost DVD customers and rebuilding trust for streaming will far exceed the management benefits of a split brand.

DECISION: NO-GO
