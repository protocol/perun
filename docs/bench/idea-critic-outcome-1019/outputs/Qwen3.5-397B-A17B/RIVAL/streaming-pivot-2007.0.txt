The proposal to launch streaming in 2007, despite limited content, poor quality, and high licensing costs, is the correct strategic move. The alternative—protecting the profitable DVD monopoly—is a path to obsolescence.

The core argument for approval rests on three pillars:

First, **bandwidth is inevitable**. In 2007, broadband adoption is accelerating. Waiting for "perfect" conditions cedes the customer relationship to future entrants. By launching now, even with a small catalog, the company captures critical data on user viewing habits, buffering tolerance, and interface preferences. This data becomes an unassailable moat that late entrants cannot buy.

Second, **cannibalization is self-defense**. If the company does not eat its own DVD business, a competitor will. A streaming offering at no extra charge locks subscribers into the ecosystem, increasing churn resistance. It transforms the value proposition from "waiting for mail" to "instant access," raising the switching cost even if the instant library is currently thin.

Third, **licensing leverage requires presence**. Studios are charging high prices because they view streaming as experimental. They will not grant favorable terms to a company without a live platform. Launching now creates the negotiation table necessary to secure better rights later. The "small and old" catalog is a necessary starting point to prove the model to rights holders.

The risks of technology cost and competition are real, but the risk of irrelevance is fatal. Big tech entrants lack the existing seven-million-subscriber distribution channel. This company owns the billing relationship and the brand trust. Using that existing base to bootstrap a flawed but functional streaming service is the only way to transition the business model before the DVD window closes.

Delaying for a "better" product or cheaper licenses allows competitors to define the standard. The proposal accepts short-term margin pressure and operational friction to secure long-term survival. The flaws cited (quality, catalog size) are temporary; the strategic position lost by waiting is permanent.

DECISION: GO
