This proposal asks a graphics-chip maker to bet its entire future on a market that does not yet exist. The company currently derives almost all revenue from gaming and workstation graphics, a sector engaged in a fierce, speed-critical arms race with a primary rival. The plan requires adding silicon area, building a complex compiler toolchain, and funding academic programs, thereby raising the unit cost of every consumer chip sold.

The fundamental flaw is economic and strategic timing. In 2004, the addressable market for general-purpose GPU computing is effectively zero. Academic experimentation with hacked APIs proves technical feasibility but demonstrates no commercial willingness to pay. By embedding these features into every mass-market chip, the company imposes a "tax" on its core gaming customers to subsidize a speculative future venture.

In a hyper-competitive duopoly, raising costs without immediate reciprocal value is suicide. If the rival continues to optimize purely for gaming performance and price, they will undercut this proposal's architect on the only metric that currently matters to their shared customer base: frames per second per dollar. The engineering distraction is equally dangerous; diverting top talent to build a compiler stack while the core graphics race accelerates risks losing the cash cow that funds the speculation in the first place.

While the vision of programmable parallel computing is technically profound, the execution strategy is inverted. One does not build a universal supercomputer on every consumer device before securing anchor tenants. The risk profile is asymmetric: failure means bankruptcy via loss of core market share; success means a distant, uncertain payoff.

A viable path would require a phased approach: prove the software stack on high-margin professional cards first, secure committed enterprise pilots, and only then trickle the technology down to mass-market silicon once the economics are proven. As proposed, this is an unjustifiable gamble with the company's survival.

DECISION: NO-GO
