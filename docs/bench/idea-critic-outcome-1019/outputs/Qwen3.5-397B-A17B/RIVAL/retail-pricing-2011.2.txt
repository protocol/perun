This proposal represents a catastrophic strategic mismatch. The incoming CEO is attempting to transplant a playbook from high-velocity, brand-loyal electronics retail into a low-margin, promotion-dependent department store environment. The fundamental error lies in ignoring the behavioral economics of the existing customer base. These shoppers are not buying "value" in the abstract; they are hunting for the psychological win of a discount. Removing coupons does not elevate the brand; it removes the primary utility the customer derives from visiting.

The decision to bypass a pilot program is reckless. Retail transformations of this magnitude require iterative validation. A "big bang" rollout across the entire chain eliminates the ability to course-correct based on real-world data. If the hypothesis fails—and the risk suggests it likely will—the company faces immediate, unrecoverable revenue collapse before any corrective measure can be implemented. The electronics model relies on product novelty and rapid turnover; department stores rely on habitual traffic driven by perceived savings. Confusing these models ignores the core revenue engine.

Furthermore, rebuilding stores into branded shops requires massive capital expenditure (CapEx) at the exact moment sales are already falling. This burns cash reserves needed for operational stability while simultaneously alienating the current revenue base. The plan assumes the customer base will evolve to match the new strategy, rather than the strategy evolving to retain the customer base. In a turning business, the first rule is to stabilize the core before attempting a reinvention. This proposal does the opposite: it destabilizes the core to fund a speculative reinvention without proof of concept.

The lack of a test phase is the deciding factor. Even a brilliant strategy requires validation when the stakes involve the entire chain's survival. Executing this untested, high-cost, high-friction transformation immediately invites insolvency. The risks of customer churn, cash burn, and operational disruption are unmitigated.

DECISION: NO-GO
