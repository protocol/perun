This proposal represents a catastrophic strategic misalignment between the new CEO's past success and the current reality of the department store chain.

The core failure is the assumption that what worked for branded electronics will translate to a mid-market department store with an older, coupon-driven demographic. Electronics are often high-consideration, brand-loyal purchases where value is perceived through quality and innovation. Department store goods for this demographic are habitual, price-sensitive purchases where value is explicitly defined by the discount mechanism itself.

The proposal ignores three fatal facts:
1. **Customer Psychology:** The existing base is "trained to wait for coupons." Removing the coupon structure does not elevate their perception of value; it simply removes the trigger for purchase. They will not interpret "everyday low price" as a benefit; they will interpret it as the absence of a deal.
2. **Revenue Composition:** Since most sales historically come from discounted items, the revenue model is structurally dependent on the promotion cycle. Eliminating this without a transitional bridge guarantees an immediate, precipitous drop in transaction volume that everyday pricing cannot offset in the short term.
3. **Execution Risk:** Launching a chain-wide transformation without a pilot is negligent. The pace of changing every store simultaneously removes the ability to detect failure early, adjust pricing elasticity, or refine the "branded shop" concept. It bets the entire company on a single unproven hypothesis.

The "branded in-store shops" concept may have merit as a long-term differentiation strategy, but coupling it with an immediate elimination of the pricing model that funds the business is suicidal. The new CEO is solving for their own comfort and past glory rather than the customer's actual behavior.

A viable strategy would require a phased approach: pilot the new format in 5-10 locations, maintain a hybrid pricing model during the transition to retrain customer expectations, and validate that the "everyday pricing" actually sustains margin before stripping the promotional engine entirely.

As proposed, this plan ignores the fundamental economic drivers of the existing business and alienates the core customer base overnight. The lack of a pilot demonstrates a dangerous overconfidence that history suggests leads to insolvency in retail turnarounds.

DECISION: NO-GO
