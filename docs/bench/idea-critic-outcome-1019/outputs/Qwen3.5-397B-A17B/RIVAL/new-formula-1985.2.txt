This proposal commits a fundamental strategic error: it conflates sip-level preference with brand-level value. The blind taste tests successfully prove that consumers prefer a sweeter liquid in isolation, but they fail catastrophically to measure the value of the brand itself. By withholding the critical fact that the original formula would be removed entirely, the testing protocol generated false positives. It measured taste, not ownership.

The cola market is not a commodity market where the best-tasting product automatically wins. It is a heritage market driven by nostalgia, identity, and emotional ritual. The "risks raised" are not minor side effects; they are the core product. The emotional attachment and brand heritage *are* the reason people buy this cola over the rival, even if the rival tastes slightly better in a single sip. Removing the original formula severs the emotional contract with the most loyal customer base—the very people who defend the brand against competitors.

Furthermore, withdrawing the original entirely eliminates any fallback position. If the market reacts negatively, there is no immediate path to restoration without admitting failure publicly. This creates a binary outcome: total success or total disaster. Given the identified risks of bottler rebellion and consumer backlash, the probability of a severe negative reaction is high. The data provided is insufficient to support a decision of this magnitude because it explicitly excluded the variable (removal of the original) that will drive the actual market response.

The proposal assumes the product is just liquid in a can. It is not. It is a cultural icon. Replacing the icon with a "better" tasting liquid ignores the intangible equity that commands the price premium. The testing methodology was flawed by omission, rendering the results misleading for this specific strategic move. To proceed based on this data is to gamble the company's core asset on a technicality of taste while ignoring the psychology of purchase.

The correct path is to launch the sweeter formula as a *new* product line alongside the original, allowing the market to choose without forcing a breakup. Replacing the flagship entirely based on blinded sip tests is reckless.

DECISION: NO-GO
