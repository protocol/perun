The proposal to split the DVD and streaming services into separate brands, websites, accounts, and billing systems is fundamentally flawed in its current form. While the strategic intent to isolate a declining legacy business from a high-growth digital future is sound, the execution plan ignores critical customer realities and compound risks.

First, the timing is catastrophic. Implementing a complex structural split immediately after a 60% price hike compounds customer anger. Users perceive this not as strategic clarity, but as a "double penalty": paying significantly more for a service that is now being fractured into two inconvenient, disjointed products. This dramatically increases churn risk for both units.

Second, the operational friction described—separate logins, separate queues, separate billing, and no shared data—degrades the user experience. In an era where convenience drives retention, forcing customers to manage two distinct relationships for what was previously a unified value proposition is a competitive disadvantage. The loss of cross-service recommendations destroys data synergy, making both services less valuable to the user.

Third, the branding strategy creates unnecessary confusion. Launching a new brand for the DVD service while the company is under scrutiny for price hikes dilutes marketing focus and signals a lack of confidence in the core brand's ability to manage a transition.

The premise that the DVD business needs "different management" does not require a customer-facing split. Operational separation can occur backend without punishing the user with fragmented accounts and lost features.

This proposal prioritizes internal accounting convenience over customer cohesion during a period of extreme price sensitivity. It solves an internal management problem by creating a massive external user experience problem. The risks of mass defection and brand erosion outweigh the benefits of administrative separation at this specific moment.

The plan must be revised to maintain a unified customer account and billing interface, even if backend operations diverge. A single login and shared recommendation engine are table stakes for retention. Do not proceed with the customer-facing split as described.

DECISION: NO-GO
