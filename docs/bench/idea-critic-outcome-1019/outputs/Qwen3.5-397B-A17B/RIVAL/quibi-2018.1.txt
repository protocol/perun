This proposal exhibits critical structural flaws that warrant immediate rejection in its current form.

**Market Misalignment:** The core premise contradicts established consumer behavior. In 2018, short-form video consumption is dominated by free, ad-supported, user-generated content (YouTube, emerging TikTok). Users do not associate "short-form" with "premium paid content." Attempting to monetize a format consumers expect to be free, while simultaneously fighting "subscription fatigue," creates an unsolvable value proposition gap.

**Capital Inefficiency:** Raising over $1 billion to produce high-budget, A-list talent for episodes under 10 minutes is economically indefensible. Premium production costs rely on long-form engagement to amortize expenses and drive retention. Short episodes increase churn risk while maximizing cost-per-minute. Competitors can replicate content strategies faster and cheaper without the burden of massive fixed costs.

**Technological Moat Illusion:** The proposed differentiator—video orientation adaptation—is a basic player feature, not a defensible moat. Existing platforms already handle vertical/horizontal switching seamlessly. Building a business case on a commodity UI function invites rapid commoditization.

**Competitive Landscape:** The proposal ignores the imminent dominance of algorithmic, free short-form feeds. Competing for "phone attention" against free, infinite-scroll entertainment with a paid, limited library is a losing battle. The presence of major studio investors suggests groupthink rather than strategic advantage; studios historically struggle with mobile-native distribution models.

**Financial Reality:** The path to profitability is obscured. With high churn inherent to short-form consumption and massive upfront content spend, the unit economics likely collapse before reaching scale. The $1 billion raise sets an impossible bar for ROI given the unproven demand for *paid* short-form.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed. The intersection of "premium," "short-form," and "subscription" is a graveyard of value. If the team insists on this space, they must pivot to an ad-supported model, drastically reduce content budgets to test demand iteratively, or focus on long-form where subscription models have proven viability. As proposed, this is a capital destruction event waiting to happen.

DECISION: NO-GO
