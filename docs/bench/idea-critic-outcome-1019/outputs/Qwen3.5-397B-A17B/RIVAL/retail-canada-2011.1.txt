This proposal represents a classic "boil the ocean" expansion strategy that ignores the fundamental risks of international retail entry. The plan attempts to solve three distinct, high-complexity challenges simultaneously: market entry, supply chain creation, and technology deployment.

**Critical Flaws:**

1.  **Unproven Infrastructure:** Launching a new inventory-management system and supply chain concurrently with opening 130+ stores is operationally reckless. Retail margins rely on inventory precision; a new, untested system in a cross-border environment invites stockouts or overstock situations that will destroy unit economics before the brand gains traction.

2.  **Pace vs. Learning:** Opening nearly all locations within two years eliminates the opportunity for iterative learning. The retailer explicitly needs to learn cross-border logistics, regulation, and bilingual labeling. Doing this at scale rather than in a controlled pilot guarantees that early mistakes will be replicated across the entire footprint, compounding losses.

3.  **Competitive Vulnerability:** Entering a market dominated by an entrenched competitor with higher prices suggests a fragile value proposition. If the new supply chain stutters, the retailer cannot compete on price or availability, leaving them exposed to immediate counter-moves by the incumbent.

4.  **Resource Dilution:** Managing 150 lease negotiations, cross-border regulatory compliance, and a greenfield technology stack simultaneously will stretch leadership attention too thin to detect early warning signs of failure.

**Required Revision:**
A viable strategy would decouple these risks. First, establish a functional cross-border supply chain and validate the inventory system with a limited distribution channel or a small pilot cluster (5–10 stores). Prove the unit economics and operational workflow in the new regulatory environment before committing to 130+ leases. The current proposal bets the entire international thesis on unproven assumptions.

The risk of total market failure due to operational collapse outweighs the potential speed-to-market benefit. The plan must be revised to prioritize operational validation over rapid footprint expansion.

DECISION: NO-GO
