This proposal represents a high-stakes inflection point. The target is the clear market leader in a nascent, explosive category (online video), yet it lacks a defensible business model, faces existential legal threats, and burns cash on infrastructure. The buyer, a search giant, has a failing internal competitor and the capital to absorb the shock.

**The Bull Case:**
The "price" is irrelevant if the category becomes the future of television. $1.65 billion is a premium for certainty of dominance. Acquiring the network effect (users + creators) is impossible to replicate organically at this speed. The legal risks are real but manageable for a company with deep pockets and existing media relationships; lawsuits are a cost of doing business, not necessarily a death sentence. The bandwidth cost is a scaling problem that the buyer's engineering scale can optimize better than a startup.

**The Bear Case:**
The valuation is astronomical for a company with near-zero revenue and unproven monetization. The copyright litigation could result in massive statutory damages or an injunction that shuts the service down entirely. The ad model for long-form video is untested; short clips may not sustain the CPMs required to cover hosting costs. Integrating the cultures and technologies could fail, leaving the buyer with a bloated liability.

**Strategic Verdict:**
The core question is whether online video is a feature or a platform. If it is a platform (the likely truth), missing it is fatal to a search company's long-term relevance. The internal alternative is already losing; building a third option is too slow. The legal and monetization risks are severe, but they are "solvable" problems for this specific buyer, whereas "being irrelevant" is not. The cost of being wrong ($1.65B write-down) is high, but the cost of missing the shift to video dominance is existential. The acquisition buys the category king, the talent, and the time needed to solve the monetization puzzle before competitors do.

The risks are priced in; the opportunity is uncapped. Proceed, but immediately mobilize legal and engineering task forces to address copyright and infrastructure efficiency upon close.

DECISION: GO
