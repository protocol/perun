This proposal represents a classic "innovator's dilemma" pivot, sacrificing guaranteed near-term licensing revenue for long-term direct-to-consumer ownership. The strategic logic is sound: relying on rivals to distribute content cedes control of customer relationships, data, and pricing power to competitors who may eventually exclude the studio entirely. Owning the pipe is superior to merely supplying the water.

However, the execution risks are extreme. The company admits it has never operated a technology platform at this scale; building a stable, global streaming infrastructure is a distinct business from film production, with high fixed costs and complex engineering demands. History shows that technical failures at launch can permanently damage brand reputation.

The financial model relies heavily on a "blitzscaling" approach: funding multi-year operating losses to buy market share via low pricing. This assumes capital markets will remain forgiving indefinitely and that subscriber acquisition costs will drop sufficiently as scale increases. The target of tens of millions of subscribers is ambitious but plausible given the depth of the library, yet cannibalization of existing high-margin cable and licensing deals will create an immediate, visible revenue hole. The net revenue impact in years 1–3 will likely be negative, testing shareholder patience.

Crucially, the proposal lacks a defined "kill switch" or specific milestones for pausing the burn if subscriber growth lags. Without strict guardrails, the multi-year loss could extend indefinitely without achieving profitability. The low-price strategy also risks devaluing the premium nature of the IP, making future price hikes difficult.

While the direction is correct for the industry's future, the proposal as stated is too vague on operational readiness and financial discipline. It treats technology execution as a commodity rather than a core competency gap. Approving this without a proven technical leadership team in place and specific interim profitability targets is reckless. The company is betting the entire enterprise on a single unproven vertical integration.

DECISION: NO-GO
