This proposal represents a classic "platform bet": sacrificing short-term margin and focus to create a new computational paradigm. The core tension is between defending the lucrative gaming/workstation stronghold against a rival versus pioneering a nascent market with no immediate revenue.

**Strategic Merit:**
The proposal correctly identifies that general-purpose programmability is the future of high-performance computing. By embedding these features in mass-market chips, the company creates a massive installed base, lowering the barrier for adoption. Funding university teaching is a brilliant long-term play; it trains the next generation of engineers on this specific architecture, creating lock-in before the market even matures. If successful, this transforms the company from a component supplier into a foundational platform provider, potentially unlocking markets (AI, scientific simulation, data analytics) far larger than gaming.

**Critical Risks:**
The risks are existential. Adding chip area increases unit costs and power consumption, potentially making their gaming cards less competitive on price/performance against a rival focused solely on graphics. Engineering distraction could cause them to lose the core gaming race, starving the company of the cash flow needed to fund this moonshot. With no committed customers, this is entirely speculative. If the software toolchain is difficult to use or the performance gains for scientific computing are marginal, the investment yields zero return while ceding the core market.

**Verdict:**
While the vision is transformative, the timing and execution strategy are fatally flawed for a public company in a hyper-competitive duopoly. Betting the core business on a non-existent market while actively losing ground to a rival is reckless. The "mass-market" approach dilutes focus; this capability should first be proven in high-margin professional segments where customers pay a premium for compute features, not forced onto every consumer chip. The engineering team must win the graphics race first to secure the funding runway for future diversions.

The proposal asks to prioritize a phantom opportunity over a tangible threat. It requires a level of capital allocation and risk tolerance that jeopardizes the company's immediate survival. The strategy puts the cart (ecosystem) before the horse (market demand and core dominance).

DECISION: NO-GO
