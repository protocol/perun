**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant code host secures the world's largest developer network, preventing a competitor from owning it and enabling deep integration of enterprise tools, provided the community's trust is preserved through operational independence.

**Attack:** The primary failure mode is not regulatory blockage or price, but immediate community defection. The target's users are vocal, distrustful, and already migrating to free alternatives due to the buyer's historical hostility. If the acquisition triggers a mass exodus, the $7.5 billion premium pays for a hollowed-out asset with no network effect. The absence of a permanent CEO at the target indicates internal instability, making the integration of culture and retention strategies highly fragile.

**Better Ways:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid the massive integration risk and capital outlay; let the target remain independent or seek a less controversial partner.
2.  **Minority Stake:** Invest significantly without taking control, gaining insight without triggering a trust crisis.
3.  **Build/Buy Smaller:** Acquire a smaller, more compatible platform and grow it, avoiding the "hostile takeover" narrative entirely.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Assuming the goal is developer mindshare, buying a hostile environment contradicts the north star if the community leaves. In a pre-mortem where this fails: the buyer imposes subtle integration taxes or policy changes within 12 months; the community perceives this as the "embrace, extend, extinguish" strategy returning; top projects migrate to the free competitor en masse; revenue collapses while costs remain fixed.

**Evidence & Constraints:** The proposal admits the target is loss-making and the community is distrustful. There is no evidence provided that the buyer has a proven mechanism to retain open-source contributors who fundamentally oppose their historical stance. The "run independently" promise is often broken post-acquisition.

**Kill Criteria:** The deal lacks a concrete, binding mechanism to guarantee long-term independence that the community would believe. Without a verifiable trust anchor beyond a verbal promise, the risk of asset evaporation is too high. The price assumes the network remains intact; the evidence suggests it is volatile.

**Verdict:** The risk of paying a premium for a community that immediately abandons the platform upon acquisition is unmitigated. The cultural clash is a known, active variable, not a hypothetical.

DECISION: NO-GO
