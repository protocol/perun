The claim assumes a market exists for high-cost, short-form premium video on mobile despite abundant free alternatives and rising subscription fatigue. The steelman is that a vertical/horizontal tech feature and A-list talent can overcome fatigue by offering a uniquely convenient, premium experience unavailable elsewhere.

However, three fatal flaws emerge. First, the "technology feature" (orientation flexibility) is a hygiene factor, not a differentiator; major platforms already handle both orientations seamlessly. Paying a premium for this is irrational. Second, the economics are inverted: high production budgets and A-list talent require long-form engagement to amortize costs, yet the format is under 10 minutes, limiting ad inventory and perceived value per dollar. Third, "subscription fatigue" is explicitly cited as a risk, yet the proposal ignores the dominant consumer shift toward ad-supported free content (AVOD) which directly competes with the value proposition.

Alternatives were not sufficiently weighed. The "do nothing" option avoids burning $1 billion in a contracting SVOD market. A "better way" would be an ad-supported model leveraging existing studio libraries without the burden of new high-cost productions, aligning with current consumption trends. The proposal fails to prove why users would add another bill when free, high-quality short content is ubiquitous.

The pre-mortem is clear: the service launches, users try it for the novelty of A-list names, but churn immediately due to the high price point relative to content volume and the availability of free substitutes. The $1 billion raise becomes a sunk cost with no path to profitability. The kill criterion—proven demand for *paid* short-form—is missing; no pilot data or pre-sales justify the scale. Investing over $1 billion based on the hope that talent alone drives subscription behavior in a saturated market is speculative gambling, not strategy. The orientation feature does not solve the fundamental unit economics or market fit problem.

DECISION: NO-GO
