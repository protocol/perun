The claim is that opening high-cost proprietary retail stores will reverse losses for a niche PC maker in a recessionary, low-margin industry. The steelman: only direct control over the customer experience can demonstrate the integrated hardware-software music strategy sufficiently to drive premium sales, and third-party retailers are actively hostile to this value proposition.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current economic and operational context. First, the "fixed lease cost" risk during a recession is existential for a company already running losses; retail real estate commitments are irreversible without severe penalties, creating a cash-burn trap if the "digital-music strategy" fails to gain immediate traction. Second, the proposal ignores the "channel conflict" lethality: alienating the existing 95-97% of sales volume (third-party retailers) to chase a hypothetical direct channel could collapse total revenue before the new stores mature. Third, the industry's "poor retail track record" suggests a structural mismatch between PC buying habits and mall-based showrooms, not merely a presentation problem.

The "do nothing" alternative—improving demos within existing partner stores or launching a lower-risk online direct model—avoids the massive capital expenditure and lease liability. The "cheaper way" is to subsidize dedicated demo zones in key partner locations rather than signing full leases. The pre-mortem shows failure occurring when the music strategy stalls, leaving the company saddled with empty malls and no wholesale partners.

While the strategic logic of controlling the narrative is sound, the timing (recession), the financial state (losses), and the channel dynamics (retailer hostility) make this specific execution method a high-probability path to insolvency. The kill criterion is clear: if the company cannot survive a 12-month period of zero contribution margin from these stores while absorbing lease costs, the venture fails. Given the current losses, that buffer likely does not exist. The risk outweighs the potential reward in this specific configuration.

DECISION: NO-GO
