The claim is that rapid, large-scale international expansion via lease acquisition and new systems will succeed despite operational inexperience and entrenched competition.

The steelman: Leveraging a loyal domestic base to quickly capture a neighboring market with higher prices by scaling rapidly before competitors react, using acquired leases to bypass construction delays.

However, critical failures loom. First, the "new supply chain and inventory-management system" is unproven; launching 130–150 stores simultaneously creates a single point of failure where any system glitch halts revenue across the entire international division. Second, the pace (nearly all stores in two years) eliminates the ability to learn from early mistakes before committing full capital. Third, "higher prices on some goods" does not guarantee a winning value perception against a dominant, entrenched local competitor who understands local nuances better. The retailer lacks verified knowledge of cross-border logistics, regulation, and bilingual labeling, increasing the risk of costly compliance errors or stock mismatches.

Better ways exist: A phased rollout (e.g., 10–20 stores) would allow the team to validate the new supply chain and adapt to local regulations before scaling. Partnering with an existing local distributor could mitigate logistics risks rather than building a new system from scratch. Acquiring fewer sites initially reduces lease liability if the concept fails.

The pre-mortem reveals that within 18 months, the unproven inventory system causes widespread stockouts or overstocking, while regulatory fines on labeling erode margins. The dominant competitor undercuts prices on key basket items, neutralizing the retailer's value proposition. The cost of delay (waiting to perfect the system or test the market) is far lower than the cost of a catastrophic, simultaneous failure of 150 stores.

There is no evidence that the new systems work at scale or that the price advantage is sufficient to displace the incumbent. The proposal lacks a kill criterion or a pilot phase to de-risk the massive operational leap.

DECISION: NO-GO
