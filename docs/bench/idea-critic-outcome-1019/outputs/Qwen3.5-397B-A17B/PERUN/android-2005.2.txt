The claim assumes that acquiring a six-person team building an open-source mobile OS in July 2005 will successfully expand mobile web usage and search ad revenue for a search-advertising company. The steelman is that owning the platform layer prevents exclusion from the mobile web, turning a potential distribution bottleneck into a growth engine for core ads.

However, the failure hypothesis is severe: handset makers and carriers, who currently control distribution and have entrenched relationships with existing OS vendors, have zero incentive to adopt a new, unproven platform from a search company. Without a hardware business or carrier partnerships, the OS risks becoming a ghost town—downloaded by hobbyists but pre-installed on no significant devices. If the OS fails to gain traction, the $50 million acquisition yields no platform leverage, while the multi-year development burn distracts from the core search business. The "open source" angle does not solve the distribution problem; it merely gives away the IP without guaranteeing adoption.

The "do nothing" alternative preserves capital and focuses on optimizing search for existing mobile browsers (WAP, early smartphones) rather than betting on creating a new infrastructure. A cheaper alternative would be licensing or partnering with existing OS vendors to ensure search default status, avoiding the massive operational overhead of building and maintaining an OS.

The cost of delay is real if mobile shifts faster than expected, but the risk of building the wrong thing (an OS nobody installs) is higher. The proposal lacks a concrete mechanism to force carrier or handset adoption beyond "free software," which historically has not been sufficient in the locked-down mobile market of 2005. The kill criterion is missing: there is no defined metric for adoption within a specific timeframe to justify continued investment. Without a clear path to pre-installation or a committed hardware partner, the probability of total failure outweighs the potential upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
