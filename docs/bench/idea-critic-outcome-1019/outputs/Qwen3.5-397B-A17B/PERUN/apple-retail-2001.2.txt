**Steelman:** Direct retail is the only viable channel to demonstrate the integrated hardware-software music strategy, as third-party staff actively undermine the premium value proposition.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The proposal faces catastrophic fixed costs (mall leases) during a recession for a company already posting losses. Industry history suggests high failure rates for manufacturer-owned retail. However, the "do nothing" alternative guarantees continued margin erosion and brand dilution by resellers pushing cheaper competitors. Cheaper alternatives like "store-within-a-store" concepts fail to solve the staff incentive problem.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This aligns with the "digital-music strategy" north star; without controlling the demo environment, the strategy cannot launch. If this fails, it will be due to rigid lease terms preventing rapid contraction, not the concept itself. This is a one-way door for capital but a two-way door for strategy (leases can eventually be exited, though painfully).

**Evidence & Verification:**
No external data is available to verify foot traffic or specific lease rates. We must assume the "3-5% share" provides enough volume to fill stores if conversion rates are high. The critical unverified assumption is that the margin lift from direct sales exceeds the operational cost of running stores within 18 months.

**Kill Criteria & Decision:**
The risk of inaction (brand irrelevance in the music shift) outweighs the financial risk of leases, provided the rollout is capped. The proposal lacks a specific cap on initial locations or a clear "stop-loss" metric.

**Recommendation:**
Approve the strategic pivot but mandate a capped pilot (e.g., max 5 stores) with a strict kill criterion: if any pilot store does not achieve positive unit economics within 12 months, the expansion halts immediately. The "channel conflict" is a feature, not a bug; it forces resellers to focus on volume while the brand owns the margin and experience.

DECISION: GO
