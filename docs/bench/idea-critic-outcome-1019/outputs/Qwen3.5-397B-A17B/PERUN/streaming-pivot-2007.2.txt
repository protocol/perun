The claim is that adding a low-quality, small-catalog streaming tier in 2007 protects a dominant DVD rental business against future tech giants. The steelman: this is a low-cost defensive experiment to retain subscribers and gather data before the market shifts, leveraging existing cash flow to fund a transition that otherwise leaves the company vulnerable to obsolescence.

However, the skepticism is severe. In 2007, broadband penetration is insufficient for mass adoption, and the catalog is too weak to drive value. Licensing costs are high while revenue potential is low. The greatest risk is cannibalization: offering a superior (despite quality) convenience model for free may accelerate the decline of the high-margin DVD segment before the streaming unit can sustain itself. Competing against well-capitalized tech entrants with a "mostly older titles" library is a losing battle on content alone.

Is there a better way? Doing nothing preserves current profits but guarantees eventual disruption. Charging extra for streaming tests willingness to pay but slows adoption. The proposed "free add-on" maximizes user uptake for data gathering but burns cash on licensing without immediate ROI.

The pre-mortem reveals failure: the company burns through DVD profits subsidizing expensive licenses for a service few can use well, accelerating subscriber churn to DVD-only competitors or new entrants with better content deals, leaving the firm with neither a profitable legacy business nor a viable future one. The door is two-way only if the pilot is strictly capped; otherwise, it becomes a capital sink.

Kill criteria are missing from the proposal. Without a strict cap on licensing spend, a defined timeline to pivot or kill based on adoption rates versus DVD cancellation rates, and a clear metric for "strategic learning" versus "financial bleed," this is a leap of faith. The proposal lacks evidence that the small catalog justifies the high fixed costs or that the timing aligns with infrastructure readiness. The risk of destroying the golden goose to chase a phantom market is too high without tighter constraints.

DECISION: NO-GO
