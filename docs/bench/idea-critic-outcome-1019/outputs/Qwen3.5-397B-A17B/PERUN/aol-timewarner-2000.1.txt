**Steelman:** Combining dominant content with the fastest-growing distribution channel creates an unbeatable ecosystem, locking in subscribers and monetizing media across new digital pipes before competitors react.

**Attack:** The proposal relies on three fragile premises. First, it values the internet provider at a peak bubble multiple based on a dial-up model that broadband will rapidly obsolete; if the tech sector corrects, the "majority" shareholders hold worthless paper, diluting the media asset without adding real value. Second, "cross-promotion" assumes cultural synergy where conflict is more likely: agile, stock-option-driven tech teams clash with slow, rights-managed media bureaucracies, stalling integration. Third, the broadband delivery promise depends on cable partners who may view the merged entity as a competitor, risking access denial or costly renegotiation.

**Better Way:** Instead of a full merger risking cultural implosion and overpayment, the media conglomerate should pursue targeted licensing deals and minority stakes. This captures digital upside without inheriting the dial-up baggage or inflating the balance sheet with volatile stock.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume it is 2002. The tech stock has crashed 80%, wiping out the supposed premium. The combined company is paralyzed by internal fighting over content rights vs. free distribution. Regulators force divestitures. The debt load (if assumed to service the deal) becomes unsustainable.

**Kill Criteria:** If the internet provider's subscriber count drops by more than 10% in any quarter post-announcement, or if regulatory approval takes longer than 12 months, the deal thesis collapses.

**Verdict:** The valuation mismatch is too severe. Trading stable, cash-generating media assets for overvalued, declining dial-up access is capital destruction disguised as innovation. The cultural friction alone threatens to freeze the very cross-promotion strategies cited as the primary benefit. The risk of the tech bubble bursting before synergies materialize is near certain.

DECISION: NO-GO
