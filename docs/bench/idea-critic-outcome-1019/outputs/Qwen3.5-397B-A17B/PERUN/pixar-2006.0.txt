**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio secures the industry's most valuable creative pipeline, prevents a rival from gaining it, and revitalizes the buyer's struggling hand-drawn unit by installing proven leadership.

**Attack:** The $7.4 billion valuation relies entirely on the continued dominance of a small creative team producing only one film annually. This creates extreme concentration risk: if the key leaders depart or the next film underperforms, the acquisition destroys massive shareholder value. Furthermore, an all-stock deal makes the conglomerate's largest existing shareholders subordinate to the studio's founder, potentially shifting corporate control and strategic focus away from the conglomerate's broader portfolio. History shows that "culture clashes" in creative acquisitions often lead to the exact talent exodus this deal aims to prevent, leaving the buyer with an overpriced empty shell.

**Better Way:** Instead of a risky, high-premium buyout, the conglomerate could offer a lucrative, long-term distribution guarantee with profit participation tied to performance milestones. This secures the content pipeline without assuming the full downside of production failures or diluting current equity control. Alternatively, a minority stake with a right of first refusal on future partnerships could block rivals at a fraction of the cost.

**Kill Criteria:** The deal should be rejected if the studio's creative leaders refuse binding multi-year employment contracts or if independent valuation places the per-film expected return below the conglomerate's cost of capital. The "one film a year" cadence offers no diversification to justify the premium.

**Verdict:** The proposal fails the risk/reward test. The price is predicated on perpetual success in a hit-driven business, the structure dilutes existing control, and the operational dependence on a few individuals is unmitigated. The "better way" of a structured partnership achieves the strategic goal (securing content) without the existential financial risk of an outright purchase at this valuation.

DECISION: NO-GO
