**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant video platform eliminates a future competitor, secures the market's primary traffic source, and converts a legal threat into a negotiated licensing framework, betting that user growth outweighs immediate losses and litigation costs.

**Skeptic & Better-Way Analysis:**
The price ($1.65B) is astronomical for a company with near-zero revenue and massive operating losses. The "unproven ad model" risk is critical; if monetization fails to cover bandwidth, the asset becomes a cash incinerator. Copyright litigation could result in statutory damages exceeding the purchase price or an injunction shutting down the core service. A "better way" exists: build a licensed, compliant video service internally or partner with media owners, avoiding the toxic liability pile entirely. The buyer's existing smaller service suggests a "build" strategy was already underway; this acquisition abandons that for a high-risk "buy" play.

**Pre-Mortem & Kill Criteria:**
Assume failure: Two years post-acquisition, media lawsuits succeed, courts mandate expensive filtering technology that breaks user experience, and ad rates remain too low to cover hosting costs. The integration distracts engineering from core search profitability. The "door" is likely one-way; integrating a massive, legally precarious culture and codebase is irreversible without total divestiture.

**Fit & Evidence:**
The proposal lacks evidence that copyright holders will license content rather than litigate to destruction. It assumes ad models for long-form video will mirror search text ads, which is unproven. The "north star" of organizing information is threatened by hosting unorganized, illegal content.

**Decision:**
The risks are existential and unquantified. The valuation relies on speculative future monetization while ignoring immediate, catastrophic legal exposure. The "do nothing" or "build internally" options carry significantly lower downside. Without a guaranteed settlement from media companies *before* closing, this is a gamble with shareholder capital on an unproven legal theory. The kill criterion (solvable licensing) is not met; the threat is active and unresolved.

DECISION: NO-GO
