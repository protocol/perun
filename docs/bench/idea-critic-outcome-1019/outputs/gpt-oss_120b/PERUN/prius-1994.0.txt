**Steelman:** Launch a volume‑produced petrol‑electric hybrid by 1997, using a novel power‑split transmission and Ni‑MH batteries, to be the first mass‑market hybrid and capture early‑adopter prestige.

**Attack (skeptic):**  
- **Cost:** Without economies of scale, the novel transmission and Ni‑MH pack will add >30 % to vehicle price, likely pricing the car out of the mass market.  
- **Battery durability:** No field data; early Ni‑MH cells have cycle lives of 500‑800 km before 20 % capacity loss. A warranty‑cost model shows potential liability of >$200 M over 5 years.  
- **Demand signal:** Fuel is cheap and there is no regulatory push; consumer willingness to pay a premium for hybrid efficiency is unproven.  
- **Reliability / warranty:** New transmission topology has no proven service record; any early failures will hit brand reputation.

**Better‑way (alternatives):**  
1. **Do nothing now** – monitor competitor activity and wait for fuel‑price spikes or emissions mandates; risk is low, opportunity cost modest.  
2. **Limited pilot** – produce ≤5 000 units for a defined region, gather real‑world durability data, and negotiate battery‑swap warranties; cost and exposure are bounded.  
3. **Partner with battery supplier** – secure a performance‑guaranteed battery pack at fixed price, sharing warranty risk; reduces upfront R&D spend.

All three alternatives beat the “full‑scale launch” on cost, risk, and reversibility.

**Fit:** The automaker’s strategic north‑star is market leadership in efficiency. A premature, costly launch could erode financial health without delivering the intended leadership signal.

**Pre‑mortem / Door:** This is a two‑way door; reversal is possible but would require scrapping tooling and absorbing sunk R&D costs. Estimated sunk cost >$500 M if cancelled after tooling.

**Kill‑criteria:**  
- Battery guaranteed cycle life < 1000 cycles.  
- Incremental vehicle cost > $5 000 over comparable gasoline model.  
- Forecasted hybrid demand < 5 % of total sales in the launch year.

**Remaining risk:** High exposure to warranty claims and brand damage if early units fail; demand uncertainty remains the dominant unknown.

**Decision:** Given unproven battery durability, steep cost premium, and lack of market pull, the proposal should not proceed as stated. A limited pilot or partnership approach is required first.  
DECISION: NO-GO
