**Steelman** – Buying the agency and its ad‑serving platform gives instant ownership of display‑ad technology, a ready client base, and a foothold to challenge the search leader’s recent similar purchase. The cash deal avoids dilution and signals seriousness.

**Attack** –  
*Price*: $6 bn at a large premium likely exceeds the platform’s intrinsic value, especially given market volatility in 2007.  
*Conflict*: Existing agency customers may view the buyer as a competitor, risking loss of revenue and relationships.  
*Technology risk*: Display‑ad standards and formats are evolving rapidly; a $6 bn platform could become obsolete before ROI is achieved.  
*Cash drain*: Such a massive outlay reduces financial flexibility for organic growth or other strategic moves.

**Alternatives** –  
1. **Do nothing** – Continue building an in‑house platform; lower risk, preserves cash, but slower market entry.  
2. **Acquire a smaller, niche ad‑serving firm** – Reduces premium, eases integration, still gains technology.  
3. **Strategic partnership or joint venture** – Shares risk, tests market fit before full commitment.

**Fit with strategy** – The acquisition aligns with the goal of entering display advertising, but the cost and conflict risk clash with the company’s financial health and existing agency relationships.

**Pre‑mortem** – Integration delays could push the platform past its useful life; agency churn could cut revenue >10 %; the $6 bn cash outflow could force debt increases, limiting future investments.

**Kill criteria** – Abort if (a) integration cost exceeds 15 % of the purchase price within 12 months, (b) agency‑client revenue drops >10 % post‑close, or (c) the platform fails to support emerging ad standards within 18 months.

**Recommendation** – The strategic intent is sound, but the price, conflict risk, and technology volatility make the proposed deal too hazardous in its current form. Pursue a smaller acquisition or partnership first to validate fit and preserve cash.

DECISION: NO-GO
