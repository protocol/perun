**Steelman:** The merger creates a vertically integrated powerhouse: the ISP’s shareholders gain control, the media conglomerate receives a premium, and the combined firm can deliver high‑speed broadband via cable while cross‑selling content, unlocking new revenue streams.

**Attack & Better Ways:**  
1. **Business‑model mismatch:** The ISP’s core 20 M dial‑up base is rapidly eroding as broadband spreads. Valuation is tied to a declining asset, making the stock price volatile and inflating the equity cost to the media partner.  
2. **Network risk:** Access to the cable network is not guaranteed; the conglomerate’s cable arm is a separate regulated entity with existing carriage agreements that could be renegotiated or blocked.  
3. **Cultural clash:** A tech‑focused, fast‑moving ISP versus a legacy media house will likely suffer integration friction, slowing execution.  
4. **Regulatory hurdles:** A combined entity controlling both content and broadband distribution raises antitrust red flags, especially given the market‑share shift toward broadband.

**Alternative Paths:**  
- **Strategic partnership** rather than full merger, allowing joint broadband rollout while preserving separate governance.  
- **Acquisition of the ISP’s broadband assets only** (if any), leaving dial‑up and valuation issues behind.  
- **Media conglomerate invests in a newer broadband‑focused ISP** with proven growth, avoiding legacy dial‑up exposure.

**Fit & Premortem:** The proposal does not align with the north‑star of sustainable growth; it bets on a declining dial‑up business to fund a high‑cost integration that could be blocked by regulators. A failed merger would waste shareholder value and distract both firms from core strategies.

**Kill‑criteria (proposed):** If broadband adoption exceeds 30 % of the ISP’s subscriber base within 12 months, or regulators issue a “no‑cause” antitrust refusal, the deal must be aborted.

**Remaining risk:** Valuation volatility, regulatory approval, network access certainty, cultural integration.

Given the structural mis‑alignment and high‑risk profile, the proposal should not be approved as presented.

DECISION: NO-GO
