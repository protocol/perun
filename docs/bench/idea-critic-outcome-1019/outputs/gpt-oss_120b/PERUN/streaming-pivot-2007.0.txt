**Steelman** – The proposal argues that adding a free streaming tier for existing DVD‑rental members will deepen engagement, future‑proof the brand, and lock in customers before competitors arrive.

**Skeptic attack**  
- **Licensing cost vs. revenue** – Studios charge premium fees for streaming rights; with a small, mostly older catalog the incremental value per subscriber is low, likely insufficient to cover the per‑title licensing fees.  
- **Cannibalisation** – Even a modest shift from DVD to streaming reduces high‑margin DVD shipments. At seven million subscribers, a 5 % migration would cut DVD revenue by tens of millions while streaming adds little because of poor quality and limited titles.  
- **Technology & broadband** – In 2007 only ~30 % of U.S. households have broadband capable of reliable streaming. The remaining base would experience buffering or be forced to stay with DVDs, eroding the promised “no‑extra‑charge” benefit.  
- **Competitive landscape** – Large tech and retail firms (e.g., Amazon, Apple) will soon enter with deep pockets and superior infrastructure. Entering now commits capital that will be quickly out‑matched.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
1. **Paid streaming tier** – Charge a modest fee to offset licensing and infrastructure costs, testing demand without eroding DVD margins.  
2. **Partnership model** – License streaming rights to an existing platform (e.g., Netflix) and share revenue, avoiding heavy tech investment.  
3. **Delay rollout** – Wait until broadband penetration exceeds 60 % and streaming catalogs expand, preserving capital for a stronger launch.

**Kill‑criteria**  
- If projected licensing spend exceeds 10 % of current DVD profit margin, abort.  
- If projected subscriber churn from cannibalisation > 3 % in the first year, abort.  
- If technology rollout cost exceeds $150 M without a clear breakeven within 24 months, abort.

**Conclusion** – The proposal’s upside is outweighed by high licensing fees, clear cannibalisation risk, insufficient broadband coverage, and imminent competition. A safer path is a paid, limited‑scope streaming pilot or partnership, preserving DVD profitability while gathering market data.

DECISION: NO-GO
