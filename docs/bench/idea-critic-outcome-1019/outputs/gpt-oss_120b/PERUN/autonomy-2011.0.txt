**Steelman claim**  
Acquiring the UK enterprise‑search and analytics firm for $11.7 bn will give the hardware company a foothold in software and enterprise data analytics, accelerating its strategic shift toward higher‑margin, recurring‑revenue businesses.

**Attack**  
1. **Price** – The offer is 64 % above the target’s recent share price and 11 × its annual revenue, far exceeding typical software‑industry multiples.  
2. **Accounting quality** – The target’s financial statements have been questioned in the press since 2009, raising the possibility of restatements or hidden liabilities.  
3. **Integration** – The buyer has little software experience; cultural, technical, and go‑to‑market integration are high‑risk and could erode value.  
4. **CFO reservation** – The finance chief’s expressed doubts signal internal disagreement on valuation and risk.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Do nothing** – Continue the hardware focus while scouting smaller, cheaper software targets.  
- **Organic development** – Build internal analytics capabilities, leveraging existing engineering talent at lower cost and with full control.  
- **Strategic partnership** – Partner with an established analytics vendor to test market fit before a full acquisition.

**Fit with strategy**  
The move aligns with the new CEO’s software‑centric vision, but the scale and price of this deal may outpace the company’s current software expertise, jeopardizing the intended transformation.

**Pre‑mortem**  
If post‑close accounting adjustments cut earnings by >10 %, the purchase price effectively rises further, destroying shareholder value. Integration delays beyond 12 months could force write‑downs. A cultural clash could lead to talent exodus, eroding the very analytics capability sought.

**Kill criteria**  
- Any material accounting discrepancy discovered in due‑diligence that would adjust earnings >5 % of the purchase price.  
- Integration plan lacking clear milestones and accountable owners within 90 days.  
- CFO’s final recommendation remains “no” after the diligence window.

Given the inflated price, unresolved accounting concerns, and lack of software integration experience, the proposal should not be approved now.

DECISION: NO-GO
