**Steelman claim** – Buying the handset division for €5.4 bn will give the software firm full control of the reference device, lock the OEM into its OS, and stop a migration to the rival platform, thereby preserving and eventually growing its smartphone market share.

**Attack** – The strongest objection is that hardware control does not address the root cause of the low‑share problem: the app ecosystem. Even with a captive device, developers will continue to favor the platform with the largest user base and richest app catalogue. History shows that forced hardware‑software integration rarely reverses a network‑effect deficit (e.g., Nokia‑Microsoft, BlackBerry 10).  

**Better‑ways** –  
1. **Strategic partnership** – co‑fund OS improvements and subsidise OEMs to keep the platform alive at a fraction of the acquisition cost.  
2. **App‑ecosystem investment** – acquire a major app store or launch a developer‑incentive fund to grow the app catalogue, directly tackling the app‑availability gap.  
3. **Do nothing** – focus on differentiating services (e.g., security, enterprise features) that can attract niche users without the massive integration risk.

All three alternatives cost ≤ €2 bn and preserve cash for R&D.

**Fit with strategy** – The company’s north‑star is to become a platform‑agnostic services leader, not a hardware manufacturer. Acquiring a declining handset business misaligns resources and distracts from core competencies.

**Premortem** – If the acquisition proceeds, likely failure modes within 12 months:  
- Integration overruns push total cost > €7 bn.  
- OEMs and rival manufacturers retaliate by favouring the rival platform, accelerating the target’s sales decline.  
- App‑developer migration continues, leaving the OS with < 30 % of the app count of the rival, making the hardware advantage moot.

**Kill‑criteria** – Abort if (a) integration costs exceed €2 bn beyond the purchase price, (b) market‑share gain < 3 percentage points after 12 months, or (c) the app‑store index remains > 40 % behind the rival after 6 months.

**Remaining risk** – High financial exposure with low probability of ecosystem turnaround; the primary value driver (apps) is untouched.

Given the disproportionate cost, integration risk, and existence of cheaper, higher‑impact alternatives, the proposal should not be approved as presented.  

DECISION: NO-GO
