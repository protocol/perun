**Steelman** – The automaker will create a distinct mid‑priced brand, complete with its own dealer network and a full model line, to capture growth in the middle segment, differentiate from its low‑priced and premium marques, and challenge the market leader’s similar offerings. Extensive research and a 1957 launch campaign are expected to drive strong consumer uptake and profitability.

**Attack** –  
1. **Cannibalisation** – Introducing a new brand risks eating sales from the existing low‑priced and premium lines, eroding overall margins.  
2. **Capital intensity** – Building a dealer network and brand identity demands large upfront spend; without proven demand the break‑even horizon may extend beyond realistic cash‑flow windows.  
3. **Economic exposure** – The mid‑priced segment is sensitive to a recession; a downturn in 1957 could leave a costly, under‑utilised network.  
4. **Brand confusion** – Consumers may struggle to differentiate three marques from the same parent, weakening loyalty across the portfolio.

**Alternatives** –  
- **A. Extend current mid‑priced models under an existing marque** – leverages existing dealers, cuts infrastructure cost, and limits brand dilution.  
- **B. Partner with independent dealers to carry a “sub‑brand”** – reduces capital outlay while testing market response.  
- **C. Postpone launch until macro‑economic indicators improve** – preserves capital and allows refinement of positioning.

**Fit** – The proposal aligns with a growth‑centric strategy but conflicts with the principle of protecting existing brand equity and maintaining financial discipline during uncertain macro conditions.

**Pre‑mortem / Door** – If a recession hits, the new dealer network becomes a sunk cost with low utilization—a two‑way door that can be partially unwound (dealer contracts) but leaves irreversible marketing spend. A limited pilot could convert this to a one‑way door.

**Kill‑criteria (proposed)** – Abort if:  
- Mid‑priced segment growth < 2 % YoY by Q4 1956, **or**  
- Forecast recession probability > 30 % for 1957, **or**  
- Projected dealer‑network cost > 10 % of expected first‑three‑year revenue.

**Evidence Gap** – The proposal provides no quantitative market size, cost estimates, or recession probability, making risk assessment speculative.

**Verdict** – Without concrete financial modelling, clear kill thresholds, and a lower‑cost test path, the plan is premature.

DECISION: NO-GO
