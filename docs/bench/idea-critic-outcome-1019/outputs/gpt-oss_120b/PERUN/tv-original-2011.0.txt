**Steelman claim** – Launching a 2‑season, 26‑episode prestige drama (no pilot) will offset rising licensing costs, attract new subscribers, and generate enough binge‑viewing revenue to justify the large share of the content budget.

**Skeptic attack** –  
1. **Assumption A:** Viewing data alone proves audience appetite for a full‑season, high‑budget series. No data is provided on binge‑completion rates for comparable long‑form content on this platform.  
2. **Assumption B:** The cost share is sustainable. Without a budget cap (% of total spend) we cannot know if the series will crowd out other content that already drives engagement.  
3. **Assumption C:** A well‑known director/star guarantees success. Star power does not always translate to subscriber conversion for an online‑only service; no benchmark is cited.

**Better‑way alternatives** –  
- Commission a shorter, lower‑budget pilot (6–8 episodes) to test audience reaction before committing to two full seasons.  
- Co‑produce with another studio to split risk and cost.  
- Allocate a modest “originals” budget to multiple niche series, diversifying risk rather than banking on a single prestige title.

**Kill‑criteria** –  
- Abort if production cost exceeds 30 % of the total 2011 content budget.  
- Abort if first‑season viewership (unique viewers) falls below 10 % of the active subscriber base within 30 days of release.  
- Abort if delivery slips beyond Q4 2011, delaying revenue impact.

**Concrete next step** – Run a market test: produce an 8‑episode pilot (or limited series) with the same director/star, set a firm cost ceiling, and measure binge‑completion and subscriber lift before scaling.

**Dissent ledger** – The proposal rests on unverified data, lacks a cost‑cap, and skips the industry‑standard pilot de‑risking step. The risk of over‑investing in a single untested property is high.

**Remaining risk** – Audience acceptance and ROI remain uncertain without pilot data; cost overruns could jeopardize the broader content slate.

DECISION: NO-GO
