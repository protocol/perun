The plan assumes that eliminating coupons and moving to everyday low prices will instantly re‑engage customers and lift margins. However, the chain’s historical sales mix shows that a majority of revenue comes from heavily discounted items; no data is provided on price elasticity for the core customer segment. Without a pilot, the organization cannot measure the impact on foot traffic, basket size, or brand perception before committing the entire footprint to a costly remodel.

**Key risks**
1. **Customer behavior lock‑in** – Decades of coupon culture create strong price‑sensitivity; abrupt removal will likely drive shoppers to competitors that still offer promotions.
2. **Revenue shock** – Discount‑driven sales currently subsidise margins; a rapid shift could cause a steep short‑term sales dip, jeopardising cash flow.
3. **Operational disruption** – Re‑branding each store simultaneously taxes supply‑chain, construction, and training resources, leaving little margin for error.
4. **Competitive response** – Rivals can exploit the transition by amplifying their own promotional offers.

**Better‑way alternatives**
- Run a controlled pilot in 5–10 representative locations, measuring sales lift, customer satisfaction, and operational costs before scaling.
- Introduce a hybrid pricing model: everyday low price on a core assortment while retaining limited, targeted promotions on high‑margin items.
- Phase store remodels over 12–18 months to spread capital outlay and allow learning.

**Kill‑criteria**
- If pilot stores show >5 % drop in comparable sales week‑over‑week after two months, abort full rollout.
- If cash‑flow forecasts indicate a >10 % shortfall in operating cash during the transition period, pause the program.
- If Net Promoter Score falls more than 10 points in pilot locations, stop the pricing change.

Given the absence of any test, the magnitude of the operational shift, and the high dependency on coupons, the proposal carries unacceptable risk in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
