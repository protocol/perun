**Steelman** – The product is a premium countertop juicer ($699) that uses proprietary, pre‑packed fruit/vegetable bags sold by subscription. It connects to Wi‑Fi, runs a custom app that verifies bag freshness, and promises consistent, mess‑free juice with minimal user effort.

**Attack & Risks**  
1. **Price barrier** – $699 far exceeds typical home‑juicer prices (≈$100‑$250). Early adopters may balk unless clear health/value ROI is demonstrated.  
2. **Subscription economics** – Even at $3‑$5 per bag, monthly cost quickly surpasses buying fresh produce and juicing at home, especially for price‑sensitive consumers.  
3. **Internet dependency** – Requiring Wi‑Fi for freshness checks creates a single point of failure; outages or privacy concerns could deter buyers.  
4. **Market size** – The niche of consumers willing to pay premium hardware *and* a recurring bag fee is unproven; comparable subscription food‑prep services have struggled to achieve mass adoption.

**Better‑Way Alternatives**  
- Offer a standard, high‑quality juicer at ≤$250 and sell bags separately, removing the hardware premium.  
- Provide a “bag‑only” subscription for users with existing juicers, testing demand without the capital‑intensive hardware rollout.  

**Fit with Strategy** – The proposal diverges from a lean, scalable consumer‑hardware model; it locks the company into high‑cost manufacturing and inventory of proprietary bags, increasing capital risk.

**Premortem** – If sales fall below 5 % of projected volume, the company faces sunk hardware costs, unsold inventory, and a negative brand perception for “overpriced” gadgets.

**Kill‑Criteria** – Abort if pre‑order conversion <10 % of target or if average customer acquisition cost exceeds $150.

Given the steep price, unvalidated subscription demand, and reliance on constant connectivity, the proposal does not meet a viable risk/reward threshold.

**Decision**: NO‑GO
