**Steelman:** The new CEO will replace the coupon‑driven, discount‑heavy model with everyday low pricing and reorganize each location into branded specialty shops, rolling the change out chain‑wide without a pilot.

**Skeptic checks**
- **Customer price‑sensitivity:** 70 %+ of historic sales come from discounted items; abandoning coupons will likely alienate the core base.
- **Operational shock:** Simultaneous store redesign and pricing overhaul stresses supply‑chain, merchandising, and staff training.
- **Revenue risk:** No evidence that everyday pricing can sustain current volume; comparable retailers saw 5‑15 % sales dips when cutting promotions abruptly.

**Better‑way alternatives**
1. **Limited pilot (3–5 stores)** for 6 months, measuring sales, basket size, and NPS before full rollout.
2. **Hybrid pricing:** Keep coupons on high‑margin categories while introducing everyday pricing on staples, easing the transition.
3. **Gradual rebranding:** Convert one department per store per quarter, limiting capital outlay and allowing staff to adapt.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**
- If pilot sales fall >10 % vs. baseline, abort chain‑wide rollout.
- If Net Promoter Score drops >5 points, revert to mixed pricing.
- If gross margin contracts >2 % after 3 months, pause rebranding.

**Evidence gap:** No data from a test market, no financial model projecting margin impact, and no customer‑behavior research to validate the shift.

**Risk rating:** High – irreversible brand change with no safety net.

**Recommendation:** Proceed only after a controlled pilot and clear stop‑loss metrics.

DECISION: NO-GO
