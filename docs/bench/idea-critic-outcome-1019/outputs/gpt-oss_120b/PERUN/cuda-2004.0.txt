**Steelman claim** – The chipmaker will embed general‑purpose compute units in every consumer graphics chip, deliver a full compiler and toolchain, and fund university teaching so that a future scientific‑computing market will emerge from today’s academic experiments, justifying the added silicon and higher unit cost.

**Key assumptions**  
1. A sizable scientific‑computing market will materialise soon enough to recoup the extra silicon cost.  
2. Academic users will become the primary early adopters and will drive commercial demand.  
3. The cost increase per chip can be absorbed without eroding the company’s price‑performance lead in gaming.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Do nothing** until a clear paying market appears; continue to dominate gaming GPUs.  
- **Target professional/enterprise GPUs only**, adding compute features where higher margins can offset cost.  
- **Partner with HPC vendors** to co‑develop a specialized accelerator, sharing R&D risk.

**Fit with strategy** – The firm’s north‑star is to win the high‑performance gaming GPU race against its main rival. Diverting silicon and engineering effort to a speculative scientific market mis‑aligns with that core mission.

**Pre‑mortem** – If the added area raises the bill‑of‑materials by >5 % and no pre‑orders exist, the product will be priced out of the gaming segment, causing market‑share loss to the rival. Engineering resources diverted >15 % could delay the next gaming architecture, further weakening the competitive position. Without committed customers, the R&D spend would never be amortised, leading to a multi‑year financial drag.

**Kill criteria** – Abort if (a) projected unit‑cost rise >5 % with zero pre‑commitments, (b) engineering headcount for the feature exceeds 15 % of the gaming‑GPU team, or (c) a competitor releases a faster gaming GPU while the proposed chip is still in development.

Given the absence of a paying market, the clear cost penalty, and the strategic distraction from the core gaming battle, the proposal does not satisfy the company’s primary objectives and fails the defined kill criteria.

**Decision:** the programme should not be approved in its current form.  

DECISION: NO-GO
