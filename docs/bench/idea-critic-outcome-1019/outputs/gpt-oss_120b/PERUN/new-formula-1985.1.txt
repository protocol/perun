**Steelman claim** – The company will cease production of its century‑old flagship cola and replace it worldwide with a newly tested sweeter formula that outperformed both the original and the main rival in blind taste tests, surveys and focus groups.

**Skeptic attack** – The proposal assumes taste‑test preference will translate directly into market share, ignoring the strong emotional attachment many consumers have to the legacy brand. Historical cases (e.g., “New Coke”) show that even superior taste can trigger a backlash that outweighs short‑term gains. The plan also assumes bottlers will accept an abrupt product swap without renegotiating contracts or retooling lines, a risk not quantified.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
1. **Dual‑product launch** – Introduce the sweeter formula as a new line (e.g., “Cola‑Plus”) while keeping the original on shelves for a transition period. This provides a safety net and lets the market self‑select.  
2. **Gradual rollout** – Pilot the new formula in limited regions, monitor real‑world sales and sentiment, then decide on a full switch. This reduces exposure to a single‑point failure.  
Both alternatives preserve brand heritage, give bottlers time to adapt, and create a fallback if the new taste does not sustain demand.

**Kill‑criteria** – Abort the full replacement if any of the following occurs within the first six months:  
- A ≥5 % drop in overall cola‑category sales attributable to consumer backlash (measured by SKU‑level volume).  
- Formal objections from ≥30 % of key bottler partners, triggering contract renegotiation.  
- Negative net‑promoter score (NPS) for the brand falling below the pre‑launch baseline by more than 10 points.

**Conclusion** – The risks of brand erosion, bottler resistance, and lack of a fallback product are material and not mitigated by the taste‑test data alone. The proposal should be revised to incorporate a phased, dual‑product strategy and explicit kill‑criteria before proceeding.

DECISION: NO-GO
