**Steelman claim** – Acquiring the messaging app for $19 B will instantly give the buyer a massive user base, neutralize a competitive threat, and position the company as a leader in mobile messaging.

**Skeptic attack** – The price is ~12 × the last financing valuation ($1.5 B → $19 B). With only a $1‑per‑year subscription and negligible revenue, the purchase provides no near‑term cash flow to justify such a premium. The buyer would need to fund the deal entirely from cash and stock, diluting existing shareholders and stressing balance‑sheet capacity.

**Better‑way alternatives** –  
1. **Strategic partnership / minority stake** – Invest $2‑3 B for a board seat, gain access to technology and users while preserving upside without full dilution.  
2. **Build in‑house** – Leverage the buyer’s existing engineering talent to create a competing messaging service; development cost is an order of magnitude lower and retains full control.  
3. **Acquire a smaller, profitable competitor** – Targets with proven monetisation (ads, premium features) exist at $3‑5 B, offering a clearer path to ROI.

Both alternatives cost far less, preserve cash, and avoid the integration risk of a 50‑person company.

**Fit with strategy** – The buyer’s core vision is to expand its social platform, not to become a messaging‑only business. The proposed acquisition does not align with a sustainable revenue model and would force a pivot to an untested monetisation strategy.

**Pre‑mortem / kill criteria** –  
- **Financial loss**: If post‑acquisition EBITDA stays < 5 % of purchase price after 12 months, the deal should be unwound (sell‑back or spin‑off).  
- **Regulatory block**: Any privacy‑law injunction preventing data migration triggers a mandatory exit within 6 months.  
- **Founder exit**: If key founders leave within 12 months, the buyer must invoke a “founder‑retention” earn‑out clause, capping further cash outlay at $2 B.

Given the extreme premium, lack of a monetisation roadmap, high regulatory exposure, and strong founder‑retention risk, the proposal fails the kill‑criteria test. The cheaper, lower‑risk alternatives satisfy the buyer’s strategic need without overpaying.

**Decision** – The acquisition should not proceed as presented.  

DECISION: NO-GO
