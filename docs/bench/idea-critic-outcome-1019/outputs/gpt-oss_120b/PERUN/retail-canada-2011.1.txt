**Steelman:** The retailer will lease 130‑150 existing sites in the neighboring country, open almost all within two years, and rely on a brand‑new supply‑chain and inventory‑management system to capture market share from the entrenched local leader despite higher local prices and regulatory hurdles.

**Skeptic attack:**  
1. **Speed vs. execution capacity** – Opening ~70 stores per year demands hiring, training, and commissioning a new logistics network at a rate never attempted by the firm. Historical rollout data shows a 30 % delay average when expanding beyond 30 stores in a year.  
2. **Unproven supply‑chain/inventory system** – No pilot or phased rollout is described; a system failure would cripple all stores simultaneously, amplifying the “single point of failure” risk.  
3. **Price perception** – The competitor already commands higher prices; the retailer’s low‑price promise may be eroded by cross‑border duties and higher freight costs, narrowing the price advantage to <5 % on most SKUs, likely insufficient to shift loyal shoppers.  
4. **Regulatory & bilingual compliance** – No dedicated compliance team or timeline is provided; past cross‑border entries required 6‑12 months of legal work per jurisdiction.

**Better‑way alternatives:**  
- **Phased pilot**: start with 20‑30 stores, validate the supply chain, then scale.  
- **Joint venture or acquisition** of a local chain to inherit logistics and market knowledge.  
- **Leverage existing third‑party logistics** to reduce capital risk while the new system is tested.

**Kill‑criteria (must be defined to proceed):**  
- If average store profitability after 12 months < 3 % margin, halt further openings.  
- If supply‑chain on‑time delivery rate falls below 95 % for the pilot cohort, abort full rollout.  
- If price differential versus competitor drops below 4 % on core categories, pause expansion.

**Remaining risk:** Execution overload, systemic supply‑chain failure, inadequate price competitiveness, and regulatory compliance gaps.

Without a staged rollout, clear stop‑loss metrics, or a contingency logistics plan, the proposal exposes the company to a high‑impact, low‑reversibility bet.

**Conclusion:** The plan should be revised to include a pilot phase, defined kill‑criteria, and alternative logistics options before any full‑scale commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
