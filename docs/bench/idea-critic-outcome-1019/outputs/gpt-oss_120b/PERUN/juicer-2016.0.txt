**Steelman claim:** Launching a $699 Wi‑Fi‑connected countertop juicer with proprietary, subscription‑based pre‑packed fruit/veg bags and a freshness‑checking app will capture a profitable niche of health‑conscious consumers willing to pay premium for convenience and data‑driven nutrition.

**Attack points**

1. **Price barrier** – $699 is far above typical home juicers (≈$150‑$300). Early adopters may balk unless clear value‑add justifies >2× cost.  
2. **Subscription economics** – Assuming $8‑$12 per bag, a family of four needs ~2 bags/week → $800‑$1 200/yr, comparable to buying fresh produce outright; price advantage is unclear.  
3. **Internet dependency** – Requiring Wi‑Fi for core functionality risks abandonment in households with spotty connectivity or privacy concerns.  
4. **Market size** – The intersection of premium appliance buyers, subscription‑ready shoppers, and tech‑savvy health fans is likely a single‑digit percentage of the overall kitchen appliance market (≈$10 B US).  

**Alternatives**

- **Do nothing** – Allocate capital to lower‑cost, non‑connected juicer with optional subscription add‑on later.  
- **Partner with existing juicer brand** – Leverage established distribution and brand trust, reducing go‑to‑market risk.  
- **Offer a “lite” model** – Reduce hardware price to ≈$299, sell bags separately; test demand before full IoT integration.

**Fit with strategy** – The startup’s focus on high‑margin hardware aligns, but the heavy reliance on recurring revenue and connectivity deviates from pure hardware play and adds software maintenance overhead.

**Pre‑mortem scenario** – Low adoption leads to <5 k units sold in year 1, high churn (≥40 % after 3 months) on bag subscriptions, generating negative cash flow and inventory of unsold bags.

**Kill criteria** – If projected unit sales <10 k in 12 months *or* subscription churn >30 % after 6 months, abort.

**Conclusion** – Given the steep price, unclear subscription economics, connectivity risk, and limited addressable market, the proposal lacks a defensible path to profitability without further validation.

DECISION: NO‑GO
