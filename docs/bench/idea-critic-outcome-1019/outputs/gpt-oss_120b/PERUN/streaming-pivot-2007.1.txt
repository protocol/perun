**Steelman claim:** Launch a free streaming tier for all existing DVD‑rental subscribers in 2007, despite a limited catalogue, poor video quality, high licensing fees, and the likelihood of new, well‑funded competitors.

### Skeptic (assumptions & gaps)

1. **Revenue impact:** No quantitative estimate of how many DVD rentals will be abandoned. Assuming 20 % churn would cut profit by $X M, but the proposal gives no break‑even analysis.
2. **Licensing cost:** “High prices” is vague; without a budgeted cost per title or per subscriber, the model cannot be financially validated.
3. **Technology readiness:** Broadband penetration in 2007 US ≈ 60 %; poor video quality will frustrate users, increasing churn risk. No assessment of required CDN or infrastructure spend.
4. **Competitive landscape:** Entrants (e.g., Netflix, Amazon) will soon have deeper libraries and better quality. No differentiation strategy is offered.

### Better‑way (alternatives)

1. **Pilot limited streaming** in a high‑broadband market (e.g., urban coasts) for a subset of subscribers, charging a modest add‑on fee to test demand and cover licensing.
2. **Partner with an existing streaming provider** to bundle services, sharing licensing costs and leveraging their tech stack.
3. **Delay launch** until broadband adoption exceeds 80 % and a richer catalogue can be secured, preserving DVD revenue while preparing infrastructure.

### Kill‑criteria (stop‑conditions)

- **Cost‑over‑revenue threshold:** If projected streaming licensing + infrastructure exceeds 5 % of current annual DVD profit, abort.
- **Churn trigger:** If early‑stage subscriber churn > 10 % within three months, discontinue free streaming.
- **Quality benchmark:** If average streaming bitrate remains below 2 Mbps (causing unacceptable user experience), halt rollout.

### Pre‑mortem

If streaming cannibalises DVD rentals faster than anticipated, the company could lose its cash‑flow foundation while still bearing high licensing fees, leaving it vulnerable to better‑funded rivals. The initiative is a two‑way door only if the cost and churn thresholds are tightly monitored; otherwise it becomes a one‑way door eroding the core business.

**Decision:** The proposal, as presented, lacks sufficient financial, technical, and risk controls. Proceed only after a limited, fee‑based pilot with clear kill‑criteria.

DECISION: NO-GO
