**Steelman:** The company will separate its declining DVD‑by‑mail business into a new brand with its own site, account, queue and billing, while keeping streaming under the existing brand. This is intended to let each unit pursue distinct strategies after a 60 % price hike for the combined plan.

**Assumptions Tested**
1. *DVD decline requires separate management.* – True, but the decline is gradual; a brand split adds operational cost and may accelerate churn.
2. *Streaming needs a free‑standing brand to grow.* – Streaming can grow under the same umbrella if billing and product road‑maps are decoupled.
3. *Customers will accept two logins/bills.* – Historical data on similar splits (e.g., Comcast’s Xfinity) show 10‑15 % churn due to friction.

**Better‑Way Alternatives**
- **Tiered pricing** within the current brand (e.g., “Streaming‑Only”, “DVD‑Only”, “Combo”) preserving a single login and shared recommendations.
- **Separate billing** but retain a unified account and recommendation engine, reducing confusion while allowing distinct management.
- **Phase‑out DVD** gradually, offering migration incentives rather than a brand split.

**Kill‑Criteria (pre‑mortem)**
- >12 % churn of combined‑plan customers within 90 days of launch.
- >20 % increase in support tickets related to login/billing confusion.
- Loss of >5 % in cross‑service recommendation click‑through rates.

**Risks Not Mitigated**
- Customer perception of a “price gouge” compounded by a forced brand split.
- Loss of valuable cross‑selling data, weakening recommendation quality and future upsell opportunities.

**Recommendation:** Hold the split until the price increase stabilizes and the company pilots a single‑sign‑on, tiered‑pricing model to measure churn and support impact. This addresses the core strategic need without incurring immediate customer friction.

DECISION: NO-GO
