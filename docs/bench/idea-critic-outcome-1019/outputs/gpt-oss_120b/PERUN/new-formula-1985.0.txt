**Steelman claim:** A sweeter formula, proven in blind taste tests, surveys and focus groups to beat both the historic flagship and the rival, will be launched in 1985 as the sole cola offering, withdrawing the original to recapture lost market share.

**Assumptions challenged**  
1. Test preference will directly convert to sales.  
2. Removing the original will not trigger strong emotional backlash.  
3. Bottlers will accept an abrupt product switch without friction.  
4. Brand heritage is expendable for short‑term taste advantage.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Dual‑product launch:** Keep the classic as a heritage line (“Cola Classic”) while introducing the sweeter “Cola Plus” as a second SKU.  
- **Gradual rollout:** Pilot the new formula in limited regions, monitor real‑world purchase data, then decide on full replacement.  
- **Re‑brand the original:** Position it for nostalgic or premium segments, preserving brand equity.

**Fit with strategy** – The move aligns with the goal of regaining share but clashes with the long‑term brand‑heritage pillar that differentiates the company from generic competitors.

**Pre‑mortem & kill criteria** – Failure modes include (a) >10 % sales drop for the new formula within six months, (b) >5 % bottler‑supply disruption, or (c) >15 % dip in brand‑sentiment scores. Any of these would trigger an immediate re‑introduction of the classic formula.

**Decision:** The risks of a single‑product switch outweigh the unproven translation of taste‑test wins into sustained market performance. A phased, dual‑product strategy provides a safety net and preserves heritage value.

DECISION: NO‑GO
