**Claim:** Approve a $1 billion spend on large automated warehouses, a fleet of trucks, and rapid multi‑city expansion before the startup has validated unit economics in its first market.

**Steel‑man:** The company could lock in market leadership, achieve economies of scale, and meet customer expectations for 30‑minute delivery, thereby creating a defensible logistics network that competitors cannot quickly replicate.

**Attack (skeptic):**  
- **Capital intensity vs. thin margins:** Groceries operate on ~2‑4 % net margins; a $1 B outlay will require massive, sustained volume to break even.  
- **Customer‑density requirement:** High‑density neighborhoods are needed to justify large warehouses; without proven demand patterns, the risk of under‑utilised space is high.  
- **No operating track record:** The firm has only one city’s data; scaling assumptions are untested, making cash‑burn forecasts unreliable.  
- **Liquidity risk:** Raising and deploying such capital before breakeven could exhaust runway, forcing a premature fundraising round or shutdown.

**Better‑way (alternatives):**  
1. **Phase‑in rollout:** Build a modest pilot warehouse in the current city, iterate on layout and technology, then expand based on proven unit economics.  
2. **Leverage third‑party logistics:** Partner with existing distributors to test 30‑minute delivery without upfront CAPEX.  
3. **Modular micro‑fulfilment centers:** Smaller, lower‑cost nodes that can be added incrementally.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem):**  
- If after six months the average order‑per‑square‑foot in the pilot city is < 30, abort further warehouse construction.  
- If monthly cash burn exceeds 15 % of total runway without approaching breakeven, halt expansion.  
- If net loss per order remains > $2 after three quarters, cease capital‑intensive rollout.

**Decision:** The proposal’s risk profile outweighs its potential upside without a validated economic foundation. The recommended path is a staged, data‑driven expansion.

DECISION: NO-GO
