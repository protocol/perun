**Steelman claim** – The automaker will launch a volume‑produced petrol‑electric hybrid by 1997, using a novel power‑split transmission and NiMH batteries, despite cheap fuel, no regulatory pressure, no competitors, and unproven battery durability.

**Attack & better ways**  
1. **Delay & pilot** – Postpone full‑scale production to 1999 and run a limited‑run pilot (e.g., fleet customers) to validate battery life and transmission reliability.  
2. **Risk‑share partnership** – Co‑develop the battery with a specialist supplier, sharing warranty exposure.  
3. **Incremental rollout** – Introduce the hybrid as an option on an existing model rather than a brand‑new platform, reducing tooling cost.

**Fit** – The proposal aligns with a “technology‑leader” vision but conflicts with the current cost‑focus and lack of near‑term demand signal.

**Pre‑mortem** – Likely failure points: rapid battery capacity loss → warranty spikes; complex transmission → production cost overruns; low consumer interest → excess inventory. These are two‑way doors; reversal would require costly re‑tooling.

**Kill criteria** – Halt the program if:  
- Battery capacity drops >20 % after 2 years, **or**  
- Production cost exceeds conventional model cost by >30 %, **or**  
- Warranty claims exceed 5 % of units in the first year.

**Decision** – Given the high uncertainty in durability, cost, and demand, the safest path is to postpone mass production and validate the technology first.

DECISION: NO-GO
