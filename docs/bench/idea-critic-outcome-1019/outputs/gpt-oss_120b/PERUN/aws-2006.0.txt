**Steelman:** Launch a dedicated cloud‑compute and storage service, charging developers and enterprises per‑use, to diversify revenue beyond retail and monetize existing infrastructure.

**Skeptic attack:**  
1. **Market viability:** In 2006 the “pay‑per‑use” server market was nascent; no comparable public demand data exist. Early adopters were limited to large enterprises, not the mass developer segment the proposal targets.  
2. **Capability gap:** The retailer has never operated a developer platform at scale. Building multi‑tenant isolation, billing, SLA monitoring, and API ecosystems requires expertise and years of engineering that cannot be shortcut.  
3. **Margin pressure:** Retail operates on thin margins; cloud services demand high upfront hardware, networking, and support costs. Without proven scale, per‑unit economics are likely negative, further eroding investor confidence already strained by heavy cap‑ex.  
4. **Competitive landscape:** Established vendors (e.g., Amazon, IBM, HP) already possess global data‑center footprints and brand trust. Entering now forces a price war or heavy discounting, accelerating margin dilution.

**Better‑way alternatives:**  
- **Do nothing** and focus on core retail efficiency improvements, which directly protect margins.  
- **Partner** with an existing cloud provider to offer “retail‑branded” resale of capacity, gaining exposure without capital risk.  
- **Pilot** a limited internal‑use private cloud for the retailer’s own services, proving technology and cost models before any external launch.

**Kill‑criteria / pre‑mortem:**  
- If the pilot’s net‑margin after 12 months is < 5 % of revenue, abort the external service.  
- If capital spend on the new data‑center exceeds 10 % of the annual retail cap‑ex budget, stop development.  
- If projected churn exceeds 30 % within six months of launch, discontinue the line.

**Concrete failure scenario:** Launch the service, spend $150 M on new hardware, achieve only 2 % utilization, incur $30 M monthly operating loss, and force a 3 % reduction in retail profit margins, triggering investor backlash and share‑price decline.

Given the unproven market, lack of expertise, severe margin risk, and strong competitive forces, the proposal should not be approved in its current form. A staged partnership or internal pilot is required first.

DECISION: NO-GO
