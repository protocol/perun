**Steelman claim** – Acquiring the digital advertising agency and its ad‑serving platform for $6 B will instantly give the company the technology and inventory needed to compete with the search‑engine leader, whose own acquisition signals that display‑ad capability is a strategic imperative.

**Skeptic attacks**

1. **Price premium** – $6 B in cash for a platform that is still maturing likely exceeds a fair market multiple (typical ad‑tech deals in 2007 ranged 1–1.5 × EBITDA). Paying a large premium erodes the balance sheet and leaves little runway for integration.

2. **Customer conflict** – The target’s agency business serves many of the buyer’s current advertisers. Post‑acquisition, agency clients may fear preferential treatment of the buyer’s own inventory and could defect, jeopardizing a significant revenue stream.

3. **Technology volatility** – Display‑ad serving is being reshaped by emerging standards (Rich Media, early programmatic, mobile). An all‑in cash purchase locks the buyer into a platform that may become obsolete within 2‑3 years, while a lighter partnership could preserve flexibility.

**Better‑way alternatives**

- **Strategic partnership or joint venture** with the target to share technology without full cash outlay, reducing risk and preserving capital.
- **Incremental acquisition** of a smaller, more focused ad‑serving firm (e.g., a niche SSP) at a fraction of the cost, then integrate it with existing agency assets.
- **In‑house development** leveraging the company’s engineering talent, funded by a modest R&D budget, to build a modular ad‑serving stack aligned with emerging standards.

**Kill‑criteria (must be met to proceed)**

- Acquisition price ≤ 2 × target’s EBITDA (verified from audited financials).  
- Post‑deal churn risk of existing agency customers ≤ 15 % (based on customer‑satisfaction surveys).  
- Road‑map shows ≥ 80 % of the platform’s core functions will remain relevant after 24 months (technology‑trend analysis).

**Remaining risk** – Even if price and churn limits are satisfied, the fast‑changing display market could outpace integration, making the large cash outlay a strategic liability.

**Conclusion** – Given the high premium, integration conflict, and technology uncertainty, the proposal does not meet the essential kill‑criteria and superior, lower‑risk alternatives exist.

DECISION: NO-GO
