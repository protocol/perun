**Steelman claim** – Opening a chain of company‑run retail stores will let the PC maker showcase its hardware and new digital‑music platform, improve margins by cutting reseller cuts, and strengthen the brand in prime mall locations.

**Skeptic checks**  
1. **Market share & financial health** – 3‑5 % share and recent losses mean limited cash to absorb high fixed lease costs.  
2. **Margin structure** – PC retail historically runs < 5 % gross margin; adding expensive mall leases erodes profitability further.  
3. **Recession risk** – Fixed leases during an economic downturn lock in costs while consumer PC demand falls, quickly turning stores unprofitable.  
4. **Inventory risk** – Rapid product cycles and uncertain demand create high carrying costs; unsold stock would tie up cash.  
5. **Channel conflict** – Existing third‑party retailers will likely reduce orders or demand higher discounts, harming an already fragile reseller relationship.  
6. **Industry track record** – Few PC OEMs have succeeded with owned retail; past attempts (e.g., Dell’s short‑lived stores) ended in closures.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Flagship experience stores** in a few high‑traffic cities rather than a full chain, limiting lease exposure.  
- **Pop‑up or “experience” kiosks** in malls to demonstrate the music platform without long‑term commitments.  
- **Strengthen e‑commerce and direct‑to‑consumer channels**, leveraging lower overhead and data‑driven inventory.  
- **Co‑marketing with premium retailers** to secure dedicated demo space and better margins without cannibalising existing partners.

**Kill‑criteria (pre‑mortem)**  
- Projected breakeven > 12 months after opening.  
- Lease cost > 15 % of projected store revenue in the first year.  
- Inventory turnover < 3 months for each SKU.  
- GDP contraction ≥ 5 % before store rollout.

**Conclusion** – Given the firm’s thin margins, recent losses, high fixed‑cost exposure, and the likelihood of channel conflict, the proposed full‑scale retail rollout fails the kill‑criteria and is outperformed by lower‑cost alternatives that still achieve brand exposure.

DECISION: NO-GO
