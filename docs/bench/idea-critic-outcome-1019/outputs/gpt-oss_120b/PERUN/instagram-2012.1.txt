**Steelman**: Acquire the photo‑sharing app for $1 B in cash and stock to eliminate the most serious mobile threat to the buyer’s photo product, instantly gain a tens‑of‑millions‑user base, and accelerate the buyer’s mobile strategy by folding the app’s technology and talent into its own platform.

**Attack & Key Flaws**  
1. **Valuation vs. Revenue** – Paying $1 B for a company with zero revenue implies a >$100 M per‑user price (assuming 10 M active users). Comparable deals for zero‑revenue mobile apps have historically ranged far lower; the premium is unsupported by any cash‑flow justification.  
2. **Business‑Model Uncertainty** – No proven monetisation means the buyer inherits a speculative model. Even aggressive ad‑based or subscription roll‑outs would require years to approach breakeven, exposing the buyer to a long‑term drag on earnings.  
3. **Integration Risk** – A twelve‑person team cannot sustain a $1 B operation. Scaling, product road‑mapping, and cultural alignment with a much larger organisation are historically failure points; the risk of “culture kill” is high and can erode the very user growth that made the target attractive.  
4. **Regulatory Hurdles** – A $1 B acquisition of a leading competitor will attract antitrust scrutiny, especially given the buyer’s existing photo product. A blocked deal would waste advisory fees and damage market perception.  
5. **Buyer’s Weak Mobile Product** – The buyer has not demonstrated the capability to ship a competitive mobile experience; merely adding a user base does not solve product‑quality deficits.

**Better‑Way Alternatives**  
- **Do Nothing** – Continue to improve the buyer’s own product; the target will likely be acquired by a more capable competitor, preserving the buyer’s market share.  
- **Strategic Partnership / Minority Stake** – Secure access to technology and users for a fraction of the price, limiting exposure while testing integration.  
- **Targeted Talent Acquisition** – Hire key engineers and designers without buying the whole company, preserving culture and reducing cost.

**Fit with Strategy** – The proposal aligns with a “mobile‑first” vision but conflicts with fiscal prudence and risk tolerance.

**Pre‑mortem** – If integration stalls, the buyer writes down >$800 M, faces a prolonged antitrust battle, and the app’s user base migrates elsewhere, resulting in a net loss of market share and brand credibility.

**Kill‑Criteria** – Abort if (a) regulatory approval is not secured within 6 months, (b) projected 2‑year revenue < $50 M, or (c) integration cost exceeds $200 M.

**Decision** – Given the disproportionate valuation, lack of a proven business model, high integration and regulatory risk, and viable lower‑cost alternatives, the proposal should not be approved as presented.  

DECISION: NO-GO
