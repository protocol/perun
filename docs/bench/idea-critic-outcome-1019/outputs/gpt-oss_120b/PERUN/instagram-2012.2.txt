**Steelman of the proposal**  
Acquire the mobile photo‑sharing app for $1 billion to eliminate the most significant competitive threat, instantly add tens of millions of active users, and embed its technology into the buyer’s own photo product, thereby securing long‑term dominance in mobile photo sharing.

**Critical attack**  
1. **Valuation vs. revenue** – Paying $1 B for a pre‑revenue company implies a price‑to‑sales multiple that cannot be justified without a proven business model.  
2. **Business‑model uncertainty** – No existing monetisation means the buyer must invent one, a process that historically fails for “acqui‑hired” apps.  
3. **Buyer’s weak mobile product** – Integrating a sophisticated app into a weak platform raises the risk of a dysfunctional user experience and brand dilution.  
4. **Regulatory scrutiny** – A $1 B cash‑stock deal by a dominant social network will attract antitrust review, potentially delaying or blocking the transaction.  
5. **Cultural disruption** – Absorbing a small, fast‑moving team into a large corporate structure often destroys the very innovation that made the target valuable.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Do‑nothing** – Preserve cash, monitor the app’s growth, and let market forces determine a more realistic valuation.  
- **Strategic partnership** – License technology or co‑develop features, gaining access without ownership risk.  
- **Internal build** – Accelerate development of the buyer’s mobile photo product, leveraging existing resources at a fraction of the cost.

**Fit with strategic goals** – While the acquisition aligns with the goal of market dominance, the cost‑risk profile is mis‑aligned with sustainable growth objectives.

**Pre‑mortem** – If the deal closes, likely outcomes include a massive write‑down, user churn >30 % due to integration missteps, and a prolonged regulatory battle that stalls product rollout.

**Kill‑criteria** – Abort if post‑acquisition revenue remains < $50 M after 24 months, user churn exceeds 30 %, or antitrust authorities issue a definitive block.

Given the severe valuation mismatch, unproven monetisation, integration and regulatory risks, the prudent course is to reject the acquisition as proposed.

**DECISION: NO-GO**
