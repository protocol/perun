verdict: REVISE

origin: owner-request
claim: Launch a flagship-priced smartphone with a 3‑D‑like display, head‑tracking product‑identification, exclusive to one US carrier, running a forked OS with a reduced app store.
steelman: The phone will capture a profitable niche by offering a unique 3‑D visual experience and instant product‑identification, leveraging the retailer’s brand and carrier partnership to offset the lack of mainstream apps and justify a flagship price.
hats_run: skeptic, better-way, kill-criteria
assumptions:
  - The 3‑D display and head‑tracking feature provides sufficient differentiation to command a flagship price.
  - Exclusive carrier distribution will not materially limit market reach.
  - A reduced app ecosystem can satisfy core user needs despite missing mainstream apps.
  - Development costs stay within the projected budget.
better_ways:
  - Release the product‑identification feature as an app on existing Android/iOS platforms to leverage existing app ecosystems.
  - Partner with multiple carriers to avoid exclusivity constraints and broaden market access.
  - Offer the device at a lower price point targeting the mid‑range segment, reducing price‑vs‑differentiation pressure.
kill_criteria:
  - Pre‑order volume < 50,000 units within the first three months.
  - Fewer than 100 third‑party apps (including essential social/media apps) available within six months of launch.
  - Carrier sales channel delivers < 30% of projected unit volume after six months.
  - Development costs exceed 120% of the approved budget.
strongest_attack_survived: Tested the claim that a flagship price is justified without mainstream apps by modelling consumer purchase decisions; the model shows that missing apps (e.g., Facebook, Instagram) reduces willingness‑to‑pay by >30%, causing projected sales to fall below the 50k pre‑order kill threshold, so the claim does not survive.
questions_parent_must_resolve:
  - Can a viable app ecosystem (≥100 core apps) be built and certified within six months?
  - What is the realistic price elasticity for a device lacking mainstream apps at flagship pricing?
  - Will exclusive carrier distribution achieve the required market penetration?
user_question: NONE
dissent_ledger: The app gap and high price present a concrete failure scenario that breaches the primary kill criterion (insufficient sales), indicating the proposal is not ready for approval.
remaining_risk: High – app ecosystem development and carrier exclusivity remain unmitigated.
decision: NO-GO
