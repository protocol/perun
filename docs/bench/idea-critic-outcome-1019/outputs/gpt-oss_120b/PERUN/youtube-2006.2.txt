**Steelman:** The acquisition would instantly give the buyer the market‑leading video platform, a massive user base and a brand that could be leveraged to launch a profitable ad‑supported video business, while eliminating a competitor.

**Attack & Alternatives:**  
- **Price:** $1.65 bn for a company with no meaningful revenue is extreme; the buyer would dilute its stock far beyond the value of any near‑term cash flow.  
- **Litigation exposure:** The site hosts large volumes of copyrighted TV/film clips; media‑company lawsuits could easily exceed hundreds of millions, threatening the buyer’s balance sheet.  
- **Bandwidth cost:** Already high at current scale; scaling to the buyer’s larger audience will multiply costs, eroding any ad revenue.  
- **Unproven ad model:** No track record of monetising the traffic; building a proven ad platform internally would be cheaper and lower risk.  

**Better‑way options:** (1) Continue developing the buyer’s own smaller service and invest in content‑ID tools; (2) Form a revenue‑share partnership with the target rather than an outright purchase; (3) Wait for the market to price‑compress the target.

**Fit with strategy:** The buyer’s stated goals emphasize sustainable growth and protecting shareholder value. This deal adds massive legal and operational risk with no clear path to profitability, mis‑aligning with those goals.

**Pre‑mortem:** Immediate stock dilution, costly lawsuits, bandwidth overruns, and inability to monetize within two years would force a write‑down or divestiture.

**Kill criteria:** If projected litigation exceeds $200 M or post‑acquisition cash‑flow remains negative after 24 months, the deal should be aborted.

Given the overwhelming risks and lack of revenue, the proposal should not be approved as presented.  

DECISION: NO-GO
