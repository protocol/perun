**Steelman claim** – The company will withdraw its library from rival platforms, launch a low‑price subscription streaming service funded by multi‑year operating losses, and aim for “tens of millions” of subscribers within five years, accepting short‑term loss of licensing revenue and possible cannibalisation of cable and theatrical earnings.

**Attack**  
1. **Revenue loss** – Licensing currently yields billions annually; abandoning it could cut cash flow by >$1 B / yr.  
2. **Cash‑burn** – Funding a loss‑making service for several years demands capital far beyond typical “growth‑stage” burn rates; without a clear break‑even horizon the balance sheet may be jeopardised.  
3. **Technology risk** – The firm has never built a streaming platform; comparable roll‑outs have suffered cost overruns of 30‑50 % and launch delays of 12‑18 months, eroding subscriber confidence.  
4. **Market head‑room** – The incumbent already commands >100 M subs. Capturing even 20 % (20 M) at a price below the leader would compress margins and likely trigger a price war, further hurting profitability.

**Better‑way alternatives**  
- **Hybrid licensing**: retain high‑value licensing while testing a premium, ad‑supported tier on an existing platform.  
- **White‑label partnership**: co‑launch with a proven streaming tech provider to avoid platform risk.  
- **Acquisition**: buy a smaller streaming service with subscriber base and tech stack, reducing time‑to‑market.  
- **Gradual rollout**: start with a niche catalogue or regional launch before full‑scale withdrawal.

**Fit** – The move aligns with a “direct‑to‑consumer” ambition but conflicts with the core cash‑generating licensing model and with existing cable/theatrical strategies.

**Pre‑mortem & kill criteria**  
- If cumulative cash burn exceeds 30 % of annual cash flow by Year 2 → terminate.  
- If subscriber count <5 M after 24 months → shut down.  
- If platform launch slips >12 months → abort.

**Conclusion** – The proposal’s upside is outweighed by severe revenue, cash‑flow, and execution risks, and viable lower‑risk pathways exist.  

DECISION: NO-GO
