The deal pairs a large carrier with a smaller, niche‑market operator whose push‑to‑talk (PTT) service is valuable but built on legacy, data‑poor technology. Merging two incompatible networks forces the combined firm to either (a) keep the legacy system running indefinitely, incurring ongoing maintenance costs and limiting data‑service growth, or (b) invest heavily in a wholesale network replacement, a capital outlay that could exceed the $36 bn purchase price.

Key risks:

1. **Technology incompatibility** – the buyer’s modern broadband‑oriented architecture cannot seamlessly carry PTT traffic, risking service degradation for the high‑value business segment that the target provides.
2. **Integration cost** – industry precedent shows network‑integration projects often run 30‑50 % over budget; with two dissimilar platforms, the cost could dwarf the deal value, eroding any anticipated synergies.
3. **Goodwill exposure** – the buyer would assume substantial goodwill tied to the target’s brand and PTT service. If the legacy network must be retired or if data services falter, that goodwill is likely to be impaired, creating a large, non‑cash charge.

A more prudent alternative would be a strategic partnership or a selective asset purchase of the PTT business, preserving the buyer’s network integrity while still accessing the target’s customer base.

Given the high integration risk, uncertain data‑service future, and potential goodwill impairment, the proposal is not financially justified in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
