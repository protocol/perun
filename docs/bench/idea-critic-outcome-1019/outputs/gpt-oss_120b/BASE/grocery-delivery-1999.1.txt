The venture is at a pre‑revenue, learning stage. Its first city has only just begun taking orders, yet it proposes a $1 billion capital outlay to build large, highly automated regional warehouses in more than a dozen markets and to acquire a full delivery fleet before any unit‑economics have been proven.

Key concerns:

1. **Capital intensity vs. cash burn** – A $1 bn spend will dramatically increase burn rate, forcing the company to raise additional equity or debt while still lacking proven cash‑flow generation. In a low‑margin sector, any mis‑forecast quickly erodes runway.

2. **Thin margins** – Grocery retail operates on single‑digit gross margins. Adding high‑fixed‑cost infrastructure (automation, real‑estate, trucks) magnifies the break‑even volume needed. Without clear contribution margins, the model is financially fragile.

3. **Customer density requirement** – The 30‑minute delivery promise only works when a sufficient number of orders can be packed into each slot. Early‑stage demand is unknown; spreading resources across many metros dilutes the density needed to achieve cost efficiencies.

4. **No operating track record** – The team has no historical data on order frequency, pick‑rate, delivery loss, or churn. Scaling before validating these metrics removes the feedback loop essential for optimizing labor, inventory, and routing costs.

5. **Technology risk** – Highly automated warehouses are complex, expensive, and take time to commission. Any delay or integration issue will push the start‑up date further out, compounding cash‑flow pressure.

A more prudent path would be:

* Continue operating in the initial market, iterating on pricing, slot utilization, and fulfillment processes.
* Build a modest, flexible warehouse (or partner with existing facilities) to test automation ROI.
* Expand to a second city only after achieving a clear, repeatable unit‑economics target (e.g., contribution margin ≥ 10 % per order).
* Use the capital raised to fund incremental growth, preserving runway and allowing data‑driven decisions.

Given the magnitude of the proposed spend relative to the company’s nascent operational evidence, the risk profile is unacceptably high for immediate approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
