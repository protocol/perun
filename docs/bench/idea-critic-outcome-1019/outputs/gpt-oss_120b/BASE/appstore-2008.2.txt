The smartphone market is moving from web‑only experiences to native‑app driven ecosystems. Competitors that already host open app stores are gaining a decisive advantage in user engagement, developer loyalty, and revenue streams. By offering a curated, single‑store model the company can capture the upside of an app ecosystem while retaining control over quality, security, and carrier relationships.

**Security & stability** – A mandatory review process, sandboxed execution, and a clear set of API restrictions can contain the risk of malicious or unstable code. The 30 % revenue share aligns the company’s incentives with developers, encouraging them to produce polished, well‑maintained products.

**Carrier concerns** – Because the store is company‑run, the firm can enforce policies that prevent apps from bypassing carrier‑provided services (e.g., billing, network‑specific features). This mitigates carrier push‑back and preserves existing partnership revenue.

**Operational workload** – Initial review capacity will be a bottleneck, but the load scales with the number of submissions. Investing in an automated pre‑screening pipeline and a modest, specialized review team will keep turnaround times acceptable while protecting brand reputation.

**Product polish** – A curated store allows the firm to set strict UI/UX guidelines, ensuring that third‑party apps complement rather than dilute the device’s overall experience.

**Strategic timing** – Waiting for a rival to dominate the native‑app space would likely erode market share. Launching a controlled store now positions the company to attract early adopters and developers, establishing a foothold before the market saturates.

Overall, the benefits of entering a native‑app ecosystem under a tightly managed store outweigh the identified risks, which can be mitigated through clear policies, robust review processes, and incremental scaling of resources.
