**Executive Summary**  
The conglomerate faces an imminent loss of its most productive animation partner. Its internal hand‑drawn unit has under‑delivered, and the rival studio could capture the coveted computer‑animation talent. The proposal is to acquire the partner for $7.4 bn in an all‑stock transaction, installing its creative leadership over the conglomerate’s animation operations.

**Key Advantages**  

1. **Strategic Continuity** – Secures the proven pipeline of box‑office and critically acclaimed computer‑animated titles, preventing a competitor from gaining the asset.  
2. **Creative Upgrade** – Replaces an underperforming hand‑drawn division with a proven, market‑leading creative team, potentially raising overall franchise value.  
3. **Long‑Term Synergy** – Shared ownership aligns incentives; the studio’s leaders will drive the conglomerate’s broader animation strategy.

**Principal Risks**  

1. **Valuation Gap** – At roughly $7.4 bn, the price equates to multiple‑times earnings for a studio that releases only one film annually. The premium may be difficult to recoup unless the studio expands its slate or monetizes ancillary assets.  
2. **Cultural Integration** – The studio’s independent, creator‑centric culture may clash with the conglomerate’s corporate processes, risking talent attrition.  
3. **Leadership Concentration** – Dependence on a small cadre of creative heads creates a “key‑person” risk; their departure could erode the anticipated upside.  
4. **Shareholder Dilution & Governance** – An all‑stock deal makes the studio’s founder a major shareholder in the conglomerate, potentially shifting voting power and influencing future strategic direction.

**Mitigating Actions (if approved)**  

- Negotiate earn‑out provisions tied to future film performance to bridge the valuation gap.  
- Establish a joint integration task force with clear cultural‑preservation mandates.  
- Implement retention packages and succession planning for the creative leadership team.  
- Draft shareholder agreements that limit the founder’s veto rights while preserving board independence.

**Recommendation**  
Given the high acquisition price relative to current output, the concentration of risk in a few individuals, and the potential for cultural friction, the proposal should not be approved in its current form. The board should seek to renegotiate terms that address valuation, integration, and governance concerns, or explore alternative partnership structures that preserve the strategic relationship without full ownership.

DECISION: NO-GO
