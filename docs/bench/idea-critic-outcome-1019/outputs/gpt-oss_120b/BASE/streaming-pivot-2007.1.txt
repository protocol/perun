The company’s DVD rental model is profitable and well‑established, with a seven‑million‑subscriber base that generates reliable cash flow. Introducing a streaming service at no extra charge would add costs without an immediate revenue offset:

* **Content licensing** – Studios already charge premium fees for streaming rights. Adding a catalog, even a small one, will increase operating expenses substantially.
* **Technology investment** – Building a streaming platform, handling bandwidth, and supporting poor‑quality video on a limited broadband market will require significant up‑front spend and ongoing maintenance.
* **Cannibalisation risk** – Existing subscribers can replace DVD shipments with the free streaming option, eroding the high‑margin DVD business that currently funds the company’s profitability.
* **Competitive landscape** – Large technology and retail firms are expected to enter the streaming market with deeper catalogs, superior video quality, and broader broadband penetration. Entering now with a weak offering could damage brand perception and lock the company into a costly, low‑value position.

Potential upside—enhanced customer loyalty and early‑stage data on streaming demand—does not outweigh the immediate financial and strategic drawbacks. A prudent approach would be to pilot a paid, limited‑access streaming tier or negotiate more favorable licensing terms before a full‑scale launch. As presented, the proposal adds cost, threatens the core business, and offers little competitive advantage.

**Conclusion:** Approve the concept only after redesigning the model to mitigate licensing and cannibalisation risks; the current plan should not be executed.

DECISION: NO-GO
