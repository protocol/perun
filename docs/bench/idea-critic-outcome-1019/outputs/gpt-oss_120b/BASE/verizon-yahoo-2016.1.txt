The carrier’s recent acquisition of a legacy media‑advertising firm gives it a foothold in content, but it still lacks deep consumer‑media expertise and a proven track record of scaling ad‑tech platforms. The proposed $4.8 bn purchase adds a declining portal whose traffic and revenue have been sliding for years, and whose leadership turnover signals operational instability.

Strategic upside rests on creating a mobile‑first ad network large enough to challenge Google and Facebook. However, the target’s audience is eroding, reducing the immediate inventory needed to reach scale. Even if the carrier can inject mobile distribution and data‑driven targeting, rebuilding a viable publisher base will require substantial investment and time—resources that will be stretched by integration costs and cultural mis‑alignment.

Financially, the price appears high relative to the target’s declining cash‑flow trajectory. Without a clear path to reverse the revenue slide, the acquisition could dilute the carrier’s balance sheet and strain earnings. The carrier’s core business is telecommunications; diverting capital to a non‑core, high‑risk media play could distract from its primary growth drivers.

Given the magnitude of the integration challenge, the lack of proven media competence, and the target’s downward trend, proceeding now carries considerable downside risk. A more prudent approach would be to either negotiate a lower valuation, seek alternative assets with stronger growth profiles, or develop the mobile ad capability organically before committing to a large, legacy acquisition.
