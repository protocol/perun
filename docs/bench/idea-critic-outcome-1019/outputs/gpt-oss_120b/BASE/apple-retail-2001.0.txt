The company’s market position—only 3‑5 % share—offers little brand pull to drive traffic to a costly, company‑run retail network. Recent losses indicate insufficient cash flow to absorb the high fixed lease expenses that mall locations demand, especially in a downturn where consumer discretionary spending on PCs contracts sharply.

Computer‑retail margins are already thin; adding inventory risk (capital tied up in stock that may become obsolete quickly) exacerbates the financial strain. The proposed stores would compete directly with the very third‑party electronics retailers that currently carry the brand, creating channel conflict that could alienate those partners and erode existing sales channels just when the firm needs every outlet.

Historical industry experience shows that PC manufacturers rarely succeed with proprietary retail chains. The most successful models (e.g., Apple) rely on strong brand equity, premium pricing, and a differentiated ecosystem—attributes this firm does not yet possess. Without a compelling value proposition that can justify higher foot‑traffic costs, the new stores are unlikely to achieve the volume needed to break even.

The stated strategic rationale—demonstrating the product and the new digital‑music offering—could be achieved more cost‑effectively through pop‑up experiences, partnerships with existing retailers, or dedicated showroom spaces within high‑traffic venues, preserving flexibility and limiting fixed overhead. These alternatives would also maintain good relations with current resellers while still showcasing the digital‑music strategy.

Given the combination of limited market share, recent financial weakness, high fixed costs, inventory exposure, recessionary risk, and the likelihood of damaging existing reseller relationships, proceeding with a full‑scale mall‑store rollout is financially imprudent at this time.

**Recommendation:** Defer the retail‑store plan. Explore lower‑cost, partnership‑based avenues for product demonstration and digital‑music promotion before committing to permanent, high‑lease locations.

DECISION: NO-GO
