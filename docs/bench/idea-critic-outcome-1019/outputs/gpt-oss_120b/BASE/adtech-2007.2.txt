The target acquisition would give the company an in‑house ad‑serving platform and a digital agency that can feed that technology with creative inventory. Owning the stack is attractive because the search leader is simultaneously expanding its display capabilities, and the market is moving toward integrated search‑plus‑display buying. However, three concrete risk factors outweigh the strategic upside at the proposed terms.

**1. Price premium** – A $6 billion cash outlay is the company’s largest deal ever and is being paid at a “large premium.” Paying above market valuation erodes the intrinsic value of the purchase and puts immediate pressure on cash reserves and leverage ratios. With the firm already trailing the leader, there is limited margin for error; an over‑pay could impair earnings for years.

**2. Customer conflict** – The agency’s existing roster includes advertisers that also buy media from the company’s current search platform. Integrating the agency could force a renegotiation of those contracts or lead to loss of agency business, directly undermining the revenue base that would otherwise fund the acquisition.

**3. Technology volatility** – Display‑ad serving is undergoing rapid innovation (real‑time bidding, programmatic buying, AI‑driven targeting). Acquiring a platform that may become obsolete in 2–3 years creates a technology‑obsolescence risk that is hard to mitigate through a single, large purchase.

Given these points, the proposal should be paused for a deeper valuation analysis, exploration of alternative structures (e.g., minority stake, joint venture, phased integration), and a clear plan to neutralize agency‑customer conflicts. Approving the deal as presented would expose the company to financial strain and strategic mis‑alignment.

DECISION: NO-GO
