The target is a 20‑month‑old video platform that commands the largest share of online video traffic, yet it operates with a skeletal staff, carries massive bandwidth expenses, and generates virtually no revenue. Its content library is dominated by user‑uploaded TV and film excerpts that infringe copyright, prompting active threats of litigation from media owners. The buyer’s existing video service is considerably smaller and already faces the same cost and legal dynamics without the benefit of scale.

**Price:** $1.65 billion in stock translates to a valuation far above any earnings multiple, because the target has no meaningful cash flow. Even a pure‑play acquisition of a high‑growth, revenue‑generating business would be hard to justify at this multiple. The stock‑based price also dilutes existing shareholders without a clear path to return.

**Legal exposure:** Ongoing and future copyright lawsuits could easily exceed the purchase price, especially given the volume of infringing clips. Defending or settling such actions would impose substantial legal fees and potential damages, eroding any upside from the user base.

**Business model risk:** The ad‑supported video model has not yet proven sustainable at scale. Monetization strategies remain experimental, and the buyer would need to invest heavily in sales, technology, and content licensing to convert traffic into revenue.

**Operational cost:** Bandwidth at the current scale is already a major expense. Scaling the buyer’s own service using the acquired infrastructure would amplify this cost, requiring significant capital expenditure to maintain performance and compliance.

**Strategic fit:** While the acquisition would instantly grant market leadership, the buyer already possesses a video platform that can be grown organically. Building a compliant, ad‑supported service within the existing framework avoids the massive legal and financial headwinds associated with the target.

Given the inflated price, high litigation risk, unproven monetization, and substantial ongoing costs, proceeding with the deal as presented would likely destroy shareholder value. The executive team should reject the current terms and explore alternative growth paths.

DECISION: NO-GO
