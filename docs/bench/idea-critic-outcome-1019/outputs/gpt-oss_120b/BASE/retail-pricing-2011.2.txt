The new CEO’s plan seeks to replace a deep‑discount, coupon‑driven model with everyday low pricing and a “shop‑within‑a‑shop” format. While the vision aligns with modern retail trends, three critical gaps make an immediate chain‑wide rollout too risky:

1. **Customer‑behaviour lock‑in** – The core shopper base has been conditioned for years to hunt coupons and wait for sales. Abruptly removing those levers can cause a sharp drop in footfall and basket size before the new value proposition is understood.

2. **Lack of empirical validation** – No pilot stores have been tested. Without real‑world data on how the new pricing, merchandising and store layout affect conversion, average transaction value, and repeat visits, the company cannot quantify the upside versus the potential revenue shock.

3. **Speed of execution** – Re‑branding an entire mid‑market chain in a single wave removes any ability to iterate. Operational disruptions (re‑signage, staff retraining, supply‑chain adjustments) are amplified when applied to all locations simultaneously.

A prudent approach would be to select a representative cluster of stores (varying in geography, size and sales mix) and run a controlled experiment for 3–6 months. Metrics to track include:

* Net sales and same‑store growth versus baseline  
* Coupon redemption rates and customer sentiment (NPS)  
* Labor and inventory costs under the new layout  
* Attrition of existing loyalty members  

Results would inform whether everyday pricing can sustain the current volume, where promotional “anchor” events remain necessary, and how the branded shop concept should be scaled. The CEO’s track record in electronics retail suggests strong execution capability, but the department‑store context differs markedly in product mix and shopper expectations.

Given the high probability of a short‑term sales dip and the absence of a test phase, the proposal should not be approved in its current all‑or‑nothing form.

DECISION: NO-GO
