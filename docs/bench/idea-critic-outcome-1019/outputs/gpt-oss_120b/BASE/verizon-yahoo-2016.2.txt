**Executive Summary**

The carrier’s ambition to create a mobile‑first media platform that can rival Google and Facebook is strategically sound, given the long‑term shift of ad spend to mobile. Owning a legacy media property gives a foothold in content, data, and ad‑tech, while the target adds a recognizable portal brand and an existing (though shrinking) audience.

**Key Considerations**

1. **Financial Valuation** – A $4.8 bn price for a business whose traffic and revenue have been in steady decline raises immediate red‑flag concerns. Without clear evidence that the target can be turned around or that the price reflects a substantial discount to intrinsic value, the deal risks overpaying for a loss‑making asset.

2. **Revenue Trend** – The target’s ad revenue has been falling for years. The carrier would inherit that downward trajectory and must invest heavily to reverse it. No indication is provided that the decline is cyclical or that new mobile formats will instantly stabilize earnings.

3. **Management Instability** – Repeated leadership changes suggest cultural and operational dysfunction. Integrating such a team into a carrier that has never run consumer media adds complexity and heightens the likelihood of execution delays.

4. **Integration Risk** – The carrier’s core competence is network operations, not content creation, audience development, or programmatic sales. Bridging the gap between telecom infrastructure and media‑ad technology will require new talent, processes, and possibly a cultural overhaul—areas where past experience is limited.

5. **Strategic Fit** – The acquisition could deliver valuable data assets and a platform to experiment with mobile ad formats, but only if the carrier can quickly build media expertise, retain key talent, and halt the target’s revenue erosion. The proposal does not outline a concrete integration roadmap, milestone‑based synergies, or contingency plans for continued ad‑revenue decline.

**Recommendation**

Given the high acquisition price, the target’s deteriorating performance, and the carrier’s lack of proven media execution capability, the proposal carries a material risk of value destruction. A prudent course is to pause, conduct deeper due‑diligence on financials, synergies, and integration plans, and explore alternative, lower‑cost pathways (e.g., strategic partnerships, selective asset purchases, or organic development).

**Decision:** DECISION: NO-GO
