The proposal mirrors the emerging model that is already proving successful for a direct competitor: a centrally‑run marketplace that lets third‑party developers sell native applications while the platform retains a 30 % commission and retains full control over what reaches customers.  

**Strategic advantage** – A curated store gives the phone a compelling, extensible value proposition that rivals already provide. Without an app ecosystem the device will be perceived as a closed, “toy‑only” platform, limiting user acquisition and developer interest.  

**Security & stability** – By sandboxing each app and requiring a pre‑release review, the company can enforce code signing, permission limits and quality standards. This approach is far safer than an open‑source style distribution where any binary can be installed without oversight. The review process can start with a modest team focused on high‑impact categories (payment, connectivity, carrier‑related functions) and scale as volume grows.  

**Carrier concerns** – Carriers can be accommodated through policy rules that forbid apps that directly compete with their services (e.g., VoIP, messaging) or that bypass billing. Those rules can be codified in the store’s terms and enforced during review, preserving carrier relationships while still allowing a broad set of innovative apps.  

**Workload management** – The 30 % revenue share creates a direct financial incentive to expand the store quickly; the resulting volume will fund additional reviewers. A phased rollout—initially accepting only vetted partners and a limited number of independent developers—will keep the workload manageable while demonstrating market demand.  

**Product polish** – The store can require adherence to UI guidelines, performance benchmarks and crash‑rate limits. Apps that fail to meet these standards are rejected, protecting the brand’s reputation. Over time a rating and feedback system will surface the best experiences, further enhancing perceived polish.  

**Competitive pressure** – Competing platforms already host thriving app markets, many of which are open and less curated. By offering a curated store, the company differentiates itself on quality and security while still delivering the breadth of functionality that users now expect.  

Given the strategic necessity of an app ecosystem, the mitigations available for the identified risks, and the precedent set by a leading competitor, the proposal should be approved as presented.

DECISION: GO
