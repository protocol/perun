The proposal seeks to add a low‑quality, small‑catalog streaming service to a highly profitable, seven‑million‑subscriber DVD rental business at no extra charge. While the idea anticipates future consumer shifts toward digital delivery, the facts supplied indicate several decisive drawbacks.

1. **Cost versus Value** – Streaming licences are described as “high‑price” and the catalogue consists mainly of older titles. Paying premium fees for content that delivers poor video quality and limited appeal will erode margins without delivering commensurate subscriber value.

2. **Cannibalisation of Core Business** – Existing subscribers would receive the streaming option for free, creating a direct incentive to replace DVD rentals with a sub‑par alternative. This threatens the cash‑generating DVD segment that currently sustains the company.

3. **Technology Constraints** – Broadband penetration in 2007 is not universal, and the proposed service would be hampered by poor streaming quality. The required technology investment adds further expense while the market may be unable to use the service effectively.

4. **Competitive Landscape** – Large technology and retail firms are expected to enter the streaming space. Competing against entities with far greater capital, content libraries, and distribution networks would place the company at a strategic disadvantage.

Given the high licensing outlay, the risk of eroding a proven revenue stream, the limited consumer readiness, and the looming threat from much larger entrants, the prudent course is to postpone or redesign the initiative rather than launch it as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
