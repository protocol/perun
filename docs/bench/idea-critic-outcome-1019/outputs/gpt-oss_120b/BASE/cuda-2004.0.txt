The proposal seeks to transform every consumer graphics chip into a general‑purpose accelerator by adding silicon, a full compiler and tools, and university outreach. The company’s current revenue stream is almost entirely from gaming and workstation graphics, a market where price‑performance and rapid feature cycles dominate. Introducing extra silicon will raise the bill‑of‑materials on every unit, directly impacting the price‑sensitivity of the core gaming segment and potentially eroding market share against the main rival.

There is presently no sizable paying market for GPU‑based scientific computing. Academic users are only experimenting with unofficial, “hacked” APIs, indicating that demand is still exploratory and not yet commercial. Without committed customers, the incremental cost cannot be offset by volume sales, and the program would rely on speculative future revenue.

Engineering effort would be split between maintaining the competitive edge in the primary graphics race and building a new, unproven software stack. Diverting resources risks falling behind the rival’s performance roadmap, a critical factor in the company’s dominant revenue source.

On the upside, early ecosystem seeding (compiler, tools, university programs) could create a future pipeline of developers and users, positioning the firm as a de‑facto standard‑setter when the scientific‑computing market matures. However, this long‑term strategic benefit is uncertain and would materialize only after several product cycles, while the short‑term cost and distraction are immediate.

Given the current absence of a paying market, the guaranteed cost increase on every chip, and the high likelihood of diverting focus from the core competitive battle, the prudent course is to defer the full‑scale program. A more measured approach—such as a limited pilot on a non‑core product line or a partnership with a research institution—would allow validation of demand before committing to a multi‑year, company‑wide effort.

**Recommendation:** Do not approve the proposal as written; postpone until market traction is demonstrated or a scaled‑down pilot is approved.
