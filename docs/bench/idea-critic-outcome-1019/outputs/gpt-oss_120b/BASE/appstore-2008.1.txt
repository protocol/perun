The proposal would give the phone a native‑app capability that rivals already offer, potentially expanding the device’s appeal, creating a new revenue stream (30 % share), and encouraging a developer community that can differentiate the product beyond the limited web‑app experience the CEO has championed.

However, the plan leaves three critical risk areas unaddressed:

1. **Security & stability** – Allowing untrusted native code on a platform that has not yet built a hardened sandbox or automated testing pipeline creates a high probability of crashes, data leaks, or malware. The requirement that “the company reviews and approves every app” does not guarantee that the underlying OS can safely execute that code without a dedicated security architecture.

2. **Carrier relationships** – Carriers have historically resisted any functionality that bypasses their services (e.g., billing, content). Introducing third‑party apps that could route calls, messaging, or data outside carrier‑controlled channels will likely trigger push‑back, jeopardizing distribution agreements and possibly leading to blocked devices in key markets.

3. **Operational workload** – A manual review process for every submission will demand a sizeable, continuously staffed team. Without clear staffing, tooling, or SLA commitments, the bottleneck could delay launches, frustrate developers, and erode the ecosystem’s momentum—especially when rival stores already provide rapid, automated onboarding.

The proposal’s strengths (new revenue, market relevance) are outweighed by the absence of concrete mitigation strategies for these high‑impact concerns. Before moving forward, the team should define:

* A sandbox or permission model to isolate native code.
* A carrier‑engagement plan that outlines acceptable app categories and revenue‑sharing arrangements.
* A scalable review workflow, including automation, staffing estimates, and turnaround‑time targets.

Given the current gaps, proceeding as written would expose the product to security incidents, carrier disputes, and operational strain.

**Recommendation:** Hold the proposal for revision to address security architecture, carrier policy, and review capacity before approval.
