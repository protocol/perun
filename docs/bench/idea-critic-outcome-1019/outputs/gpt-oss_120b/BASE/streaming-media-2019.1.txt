The conglomerate’s core cash‑flow now comes from licensing its library to rivals and from theatrical/cable distribution, generating billions annually. Removing that stream eliminates a proven, low‑risk income source and replaces it with a speculative subscriber base that must be built from scratch.

**Revenue trade‑off** – Licensing revenue is recurring and largely insulated from the volatility of consumer subscriptions. Even a modest 10 % decline in licensing would erase a substantial portion of the projected profit margin. The plan assumes the new service will capture “tens of millions” of subscribers within five years, but the market leader already commands >100 million. Achieving, for example, 30 million at a price point below the leader still yields far less total revenue than the current licensing stream, especially when accounting for churn and promotional discounts.

**Cash‑burn and financing** – Funding a multi‑year operating loss presupposes deep, inexpensive capital. The company’s balance sheet is not described as cash‑rich; sustained losses will erode liquidity, increase debt, and potentially trigger covenant breaches. Without a clear path to profitability, the risk of a financial downgrade is high.

**Execution risk** – The firm has never run a consumer‑facing tech platform. Building a scalable, secure streaming infrastructure entails substantial upfront investment in engineering, content delivery networks, and ongoing maintenance. Competitors have already sunk billions into these capabilities; starting from zero raises the probability of service outages, poor user experience, and brand damage.

**Cannibalisation** – Pulling content from cable and theatrical partners undermines existing distribution agreements and may provoke retaliatory pricing or loss of future partnership opportunities. The anticipated subscriber base may be offset by reduced ticket sales and cable affiliate fees, further compressing margins.

**Strategic fit** – While owning a direct‑to‑consumer channel aligns with industry trends, the timing and financial exposure appear mis‑aligned with the company’s current strengths. A more prudent approach would be to test a niche over‑the‑top offering, retain licensing revenue, and gradually build tech competency before a full‑scale launch.

Given the magnitude of lost licensing cash, the uncertain ability to fund prolonged losses, and the high execution risk, the proposal is not ready for approval in its present form.

DECISION: NO-GO
