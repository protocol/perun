The merger promises a compelling strategic narrative—pairing a massive, albeit volatile, internet user base with a premier content and cable‑distribution platform. The upside hinges on three assumptions: (1) the internet firm can transition its 20 million dial‑up users to high‑speed broadband, (2) the cable network will be made available for that broadband rollout, and (3) cross‑selling of magazines, movies and music will generate incremental revenue that offsets integration costs.

**Key risks**

1. **Business‑model mismatch** – The internet company’s core revenue is tied to a dial‑up model that is already eroding as broadband spreads. Converting a large subscriber base to a new delivery platform will require massive capital investment and a clear migration path, neither of which is guaranteed.

2. **Valuation volatility** – The all‑stock structure gives the internet shareholders majority control, but their market price is highly speculative. A modest correction would instantly dilute the conglomerate’s equity stake and could trigger shareholder dissent.

3. **Network access uncertainty** – The cable assets are owned by third‑party partners who may resist wholesale use for broadband, especially if they see competition for their own broadband offerings. No contractual guarantee is presented.

4. **Cultural integration** – The fast‑moving, tech‑centric culture of the internet firm clashes with the legacy, content‑driven mindset of the media conglomerate. Past mergers of similar scale have shown that cultural friction can stall product launches and erode talent.

5. **Regulatory scrutiny** – Combining a dominant internet platform with a major content and cable distributor raises antitrust concerns, particularly around vertical integration and potential exclusion of rivals.

**Recommendation**

Given the immediate exposure to a declining dial‑up base, the lack of secured broadband infrastructure, and the heightened regulatory and cultural integration risks, proceeding on the current terms is premature. The board should seek either a revised structure that ties the exchange ratio to post‑merger performance milestones, or a phased partnership that validates broadband rollout before full equity consolidation.

DECISION: NO-GO
