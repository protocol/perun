**Assessment**

The chain’s current revenue model is heavily dependent on deep discounting and coupon promotions, a habit ingrained in its core, older customer base. Switching abruptly to everyday low pricing (EDLP) and a “shop‑within‑a‑shop” format represents a radical shift in both pricing psychology and store layout.

**Key Risks**

1. **Customer Expectation Shock** – Long‑standing shoppers are conditioned to wait for coupons; removing that stimulus can cause immediate sales drop and brand alienation.  
2. **Revenue Impact** – A large share of sales originates from discounted items; eliminating those discounts without a proven alternative pricing strategy threatens top‑line performance.  
3. **Operational Overhaul** – Re‑branding every location simultaneously strains supply‑chain, merchandising, and staff training resources, increasing the probability of execution errors.  
4. **No Pilot Data** – Implementing chain‑wide changes without a controlled test prevents measurement of real‑world customer response, price elasticity, and operational feasibility.

**Potential Upsides**

- EDLP can simplify pricing, reduce promotional costs, and improve margin stability if customers accept the new value proposition.  
- Branded in‑store shops could attract higher‑margin, specialty traffic and modernize the shopping experience.

**Mitigation Path**

A phased rollout—starting with a small, demographically representative cluster of stores—allows the company to capture data on price elasticity, shopper sentiment, and operational challenges. Results can inform a calibrated expansion, adjusting promotion depth, signage, and staff training to preserve loyalty while transitioning pricing philosophy.

**Conclusion**

Given the magnitude of the shift, the entrenched coupon culture, and the absence of pilot evidence, proceeding with a full‑scale launch is premature. Approve a limited‑scope pilot to validate assumptions before committing the entire network.

**Decision:**  
DECISION: NO-GO
