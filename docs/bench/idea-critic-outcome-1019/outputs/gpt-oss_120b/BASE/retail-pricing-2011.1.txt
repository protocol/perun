The proposal targets two fundamental levers of the chain’s business model: pricing philosophy and store format. Shifting from a heavy‑coupon, high‑discount approach to everyday low pricing (EDLP) is a radical change for a customer base that has been conditioned for years to “wait for the coupon.” The existing clientele—primarily older shoppers accustomed to hunting for deals—are likely to react negatively to the removal of frequent promotions, potentially accelerating the current sales decline rather than reversing it.

The plan also calls for a wholesale redesign of the physical footprint into branded “in‑store shops.” This entails significant capital outlay (store remodels, new signage, merchandising systems) and operational disruption. Without a phased rollout, the organization risks overwhelming its supply chain, workforce, and marketing functions at a time when cash flow is already under pressure.

Key risk mitigators are absent:

1. **Pilot testing** – No controlled experiment in a representative subset of stores exists to gauge customer response to EDLP and the new shop concept. A pilot would provide data on sales lift/erosion, foot‑traffic changes, and operational challenges before committing the entire network.

2. **Customer communication** – The plan lacks a detailed transition strategy to educate and retain coupon‑savvy shoppers (e.g., phased coupon phase‑out, loyalty incentives, clear value messaging).

3. **Financial modeling** – There is no evidence that the projected uplift from higher margin EDLP offsets the loss of volume from discount‑driven shoppers and the cost of store renovations.

Given the magnitude of change, the absence of empirical validation, and the high likelihood of alienating the core customer segment, proceeding with a full‑scale rollout is premature. The executive team should first launch a limited‑scale pilot, refine the pricing communication plan, and validate the new store format’s impact on sales and profitability.

**Recommendation:** Hold the current proposal, require a pilot and supporting transition plan before full implementation.

DECISION: NO-GO
