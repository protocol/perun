The acquisition offers a clear strategic shortcut into the open‑source ecosystem, granting the buyer immediate control of the market‑leading code‑hosting platform and a ready channel for influencing developer workflows. However, the proposal is fraught with material risks that outweigh the potential upside under the current terms.

**Financial exposure** – The target is loss‑making and its valuation of $7.5 bn in stock is not justified by existing revenue streams. Paying a premium for a non‑profitable business amplifies the buyer’s balance‑sheet risk, especially given the buyer’s need to fund the deal with equity that will dilute existing shareholders.

**Leadership vacuum** – The absence of a permanent chief executive for months signals governance instability. Without a clear operational leader, post‑close integration and the ability to steer community relations will be hampered.

**Community backlash** – The developer community, the platform’s core asset, is openly distrustful of a historically anti‑open‑source acquirer. Past experience shows that community sentiment directly drives usage; a wave of defections to free alternatives could rapidly erode the platform’s user base, revenue, and strategic value.

**Regulatory and antitrust risk** – The target is the dominant code‑hosting service. Combining it with a major software vendor will draw intense scrutiny from competition authorities in multiple jurisdictions. Even with an “independent” operating model, the perception of market concentration is likely to trigger investigations, delay closing, or impose costly remedies.

**Mitigation gaps** – The proposal does not include concrete steps to address the leadership gap, to secure community trust (e.g., governance guarantees, open‑source stewardship commitments), or to structure the deal to reduce antitrust exposure (e.g., divestitures, licensing safeguards).

Given these unresolved concerns, proceeding as currently outlined would expose the buyer to significant financial loss, regulatory hurdles, and damage to its brand among developers. The prudent course is to pause, renegotiate terms, secure a stable leadership team, and develop a robust community‑engagement plan before any transaction proceeds.

DECISION: NO-GO
