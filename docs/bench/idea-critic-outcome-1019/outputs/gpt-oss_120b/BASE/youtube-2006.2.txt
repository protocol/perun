**Strategic Fit**  
- The target is already the market‑leader in online video, giving the buyer instant scale and a massive user base.  
- The buyer’s own video service is smaller; acquisition could provide a platform to leap‑frog competitors.

**Financial Assessment**  
- Purchase price: $1.65 billion in stock for a company with essentially zero revenue.  
- Valuation is therefore driven solely by user metrics and future growth assumptions, which are highly speculative.  
- The buyer will dilute its own shareholders and must finance the transaction with equity, increasing the cost of capital on an unproven asset.

**Operational Risks**  
- Bandwidth costs at the present scale are already “high”; at full integration they will rise dramatically, potentially eroding any future profit margin.  
- The target operates with a “very small staff,” implying limited internal expertise to manage the massive infrastructure required for sustained growth.

**Legal Exposure**  
- A large share of uploaded content is copyrighted TV and film clips.  
- Media companies have already threatened litigation; past cases in this space have resulted in costly settlements and forced compliance overhauls.  
- The buyer would inherit this exposure, adding an unpredictable liability that could far exceed the purchase price.

**Revenue Model Uncertainty**  
- The ad‑based video model has not yet been proven at scale; the target has “no meaningful revenue.”  
- Monetization would require building an ad sales organization, negotiating rates, and proving inventory quality—none of which are currently in place.

**Alternatives & Mitigation**  
- The buyer could continue to grow its own service organically, preserving cash and avoiding legal baggage.  
- Negotiating a lower price or a contingent‑payment structure tied to revenue milestones would reduce risk.  
- Securing licensing agreements with key media owners before acquisition would mitigate litigation risk, but this would add time and cost.

**Conclusion**  
The combination of an inflated, revenue‑free valuation, substantial ongoing bandwidth expense, and a high probability of costly copyright litigation makes the proposal financially and legally untenable in its current form. The buyer would be better served by either renegotiating terms or pursuing a lower‑risk growth path.

DECISION: NO-GO
