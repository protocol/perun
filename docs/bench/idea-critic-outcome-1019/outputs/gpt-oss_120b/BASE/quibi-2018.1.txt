The plan targets a niche that has never been proven at scale: premium, short‑form, subscription‑based video on mobile devices. While the concept of “vertical‑friendly” playback is technically interesting, it does not solve the core market problem—getting users to pay for content that is already abundant for free. Existing platforms (TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts) deliver user‑generated and lightly produced short videos at no cost, and they dominate mobile attention. Subscription fatigue is real; many consumers already juggle multiple SVOD services for long‑form content and are reluctant to add another fee for even shorter episodes.

The financial ask—well over $1 billion—implies very high production budgets and aggressive marketing spend. Even with A‑list talent, the cost per minute of content will dwarf that of the free alternatives, putting pressure on price points. To achieve a sustainable subscriber base, the service would need to charge a premium that many mobile‑first users are unwilling to pay, especially when free options meet their entertainment needs.

Investor backing from major studios and brands adds credibility but does not mitigate the fundamental demand uncertainty. The competitive landscape for mobile attention is crowded, and the proposed service would need to win both content and distribution battles without a clear path to scale quickly enough to justify the capital outlay.

Given the untested demand, high cost structure, and strong competition from free short‑form platforms, the proposal carries a high risk of poor ROI. A more prudent approach would be to pilot the concept with a limited budget, test pricing and content appetite, and only scale after clear market validation.

DECISION: NO-GO
