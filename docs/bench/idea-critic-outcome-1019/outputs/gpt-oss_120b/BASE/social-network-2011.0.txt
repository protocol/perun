The proposal seeks to create a full‑scale social network that would be woven into the company’s existing suite of search, mail, video and photo services. The plan leans on two modest differentiators—group‑based “circles” and real‑time video hangouts—delivered first through an invite‑only beta and backed by executive bonuses tied to user adoption.

**Strengths**  
* Leveraging existing products could lower the cost of acquiring users compared with building a stand‑alone platform.  
* An invite‑only rollout can generate scarcity and early‑adopter buzz, helping to seed the network before a public launch.  

**Weaknesses**  
* The market leader already enjoys massive network effects; users typically stay where their friends already are. The proposed differentiators are incremental rather than disruptive.  
* The company’s track record includes three prior social‑network attempts that failed to gain traction, indicating a possible mismatch between internal capabilities and market expectations.  
* Tying executive compensation to adoption creates pressure to inflate short‑term metrics, risking premature scaling or feature bloat.  
* Forced integration of a social layer into core services may irritate existing users who do not want a social experience embedded in search or mail, potentially reducing overall product satisfaction.

**Risks**  
1. **User migration:** Without a compelling, unique value proposition, users are unlikely to move from the incumbent.  
2. **Brand dilution:** A clumsy social overlay could tarnish the reputation of the company’s core services.  
3. **Resource diversion:** Development, marketing and support effort for a new network may detract from the company’s primary strengths in search and email.

**Recommendation**  
Given the formidable network effects of the incumbent, the modest nature of the proposed differentiators, and the company’s history of unsuccessful social launches, the initiative carries a high probability of low adoption and negative side‑effects on existing products. A more prudent approach would be to either pilot a lightweight, optional social feature set without executive bonus pressure, or to focus on partnerships rather than a full‑scale launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
