**Executive Summary**

The proposal targets a largely untapped “casual‑player” segment by offering a low‑cost console that emphasizes motion‑based interaction rather than raw graphics. This approach could differentiate the company from the two dominant high‑definition platforms, generate volume sales, and create a new ecosystem that leverages the company’s existing brand equity in family entertainment.

**Key Strengths**

1. **Market Gap** – In 2005‑06 the console market is polarized between expensive HD systems and limited handhelds. A budget, motion‑centric device can capture price‑sensitive families, seniors, and children who are excluded from the high‑end market.
2. **Cost Advantage** – By stripping HD hardware, component costs fall dramatically, allowing a retail price well below rivals. Thin margins can be offset by higher unit volumes and lower R&D spend.
3. **Brand Leverage** – The firm already owns family‑friendly IPs and a reputation for approachable entertainment; these can be ported or adapted as launch titles, providing an immediate content pipeline.
4. **First‑Mover Potential** – No competitor is focusing on motion control as the core input method; this could establish a de‑facto standard for casual gaming and create a barrier to entry for later imitators.

**Principal Risks & Mitigations**

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|--------|------------|
| Limited third‑party support | Few exclusive titles, weak library | Offer revenue‑share incentives, provide a simple SDK, and secure exclusive titles from internal studios to demonstrate viability. |
| Perception as a “toy” | Diminished credibility, lower willingness of adults to adopt | Position the device as a “family entertainment hub” with educational and fitness apps; partner with well‑known lifestyle brands for co‑marketing. |
| No HD output | Potentially obsolete as HD adoption grows | Emphasize that the console’s purpose is social, kinetic play—not cinematic fidelity. Plan a future “HD upgrade” path if market conditions shift. |
| Untested casual audience | Uncertain demand, possible low conversion | Conduct focused market trials in key demographics (e.g., suburban families) before full launch; use the data to refine pricing and game mix. |
| Thin hardware margin | Vulnerable to cost overruns | Lock in component prices via early bulk contracts; maintain a strict BOM cap; rely on economies of scale to improve margin as volume rises. |

**Financial Outlook (high‑level)**  

- **Unit Cost**: Approx. $90 (components, assembly, licensing).  
- **Retail Price**: $149 – $169, creating a $60‑$80 gross per unit before marketing and distribution.  
- **Break‑Even Volume**: ~1.5 M units, achievable given the size of the casual market (estimated >10 M households in target regions).  
- **Revenue Upside**: Additional income from downloadable content, peripheral accessories, and licensing of motion‑control tech to other manufacturers.

**Strategic Recommendation**

Proceed with the low‑cost, motion‑controller console, but attach a concise mitigation plan: secure at least three exclusive launch titles from internal studios, lock component pricing, and launch a limited‑region pilot to validate consumer response. The upside of establishing a new market niche outweighs the manageable risks.

DECISION: GO
