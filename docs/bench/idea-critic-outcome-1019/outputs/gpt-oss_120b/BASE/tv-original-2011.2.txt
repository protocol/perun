The service’s core strength—data‑driven insight into subscriber preferences—offers a solid, though not infallible, foundation for an original‑content gamble. Its licensed library is becoming cost‑prohibitive, so diversifying into owned IP is strategically necessary; without it, the business model will erode as margins shrink.

**Strategic fit**  
- Owning high‑profile IP creates a barrier to competitor entry and can be leveraged across future distribution channels (e.g., licensing, merchandising).  
- A prestige drama aligns with the brand’s desire to shift perception from a “catalog” to a “destination” for exclusive storytelling, a move that competitors are beginning to emulate.

**Financial exposure**  
- The two‑season, 26‑episode order will consume a sizable fraction of the current content budget. If the series underperforms, the opportunity cost is the loss of additional licensed titles that still attract viewers at lower expense.  
- However, the all‑at‑once release model can amplify binge‑viewing metrics, driving higher engagement and potentially reducing churn—a key driver of long‑term revenue.

**Risk mitigation via data**  
- The service’s granular viewing analytics can identify the exact demographic segments most likely to consume a high‑budget drama (e.g., users who binge similar genres, those who watch the director’s past work, or fans of the attached star). Targeted marketing can concentrate spend on these high‑propensity users, improving acquisition efficiency.  
- Existing data also shows a willingness among top‑tier users to pay for premium experiences, suggesting a willingness to stay for original prestige content.

**Pilot omission**  
- Bypassing a pilot removes a low‑cost validation step. Yet the attached talent (renowned director and film star) reduces creative risk, and the straight‑to‑series commitment locks in talent at a predictable rate, avoiding later price escalations.  
- The service can still conduct internal “soft pilots” by releasing a limited teaser or behind‑the‑scenes footage to gauge reaction before the full launch, preserving some early feedback without formal pilot costs.

**Competitive landscape**  
- No online‑only service has yet produced a prestige scripted series; being first confers a market‑differentiation advantage. Early entry can capture audience mindshare before legacy broadcasters or emerging rivals replicate the model.

**Conclusion**  
The proposal balances strategic necessity with manageable risk, leveraging data to target likely viewers and capitalizing on unique talent attachments. While the budget impact is significant, the potential for subscriber growth, reduced churn, and long‑term IP ownership outweighs the downside, provided the marketing plan is tightly aligned with the identified audience segments.

DECISION: GO
