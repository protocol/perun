**Proposal Summary**  
The company plans to replace its century‑old flagship cola with a newly formulated, sweeter version that outperformed both the current product and the main rival in blind taste tests, surveys, and focus groups. The original formula would be withdrawn entirely, and participants in the testing were not told about this discontinuation.

**Key Risks**  
1. **Brand Heritage & Emotional Attachment** – The flagship has been on the market for over 100 years; many consumers associate it with tradition and identity. Removing it could trigger strong negative sentiment, especially among long‑time loyalists.  
2. **No Fallback Product** – If the new formula fails to sustain market traction after launch, there is no existing alternative to revert to, leaving the brand exposed.  
3. **Bottler & Distribution Reaction** – Bottlers have invested in the current product line and may resist a sudden, complete switch, potentially disrupting supply chains and causing friction with key partners.  
4. **Market Positioning** – While taste tests favor sweetness, the broader market trend shows growth in diet and non‑cola segments. Doubling down on sweetness may not address the underlying shift away from traditional colas.

**Recommendation**  
Proceed with a **phased rollout** that retains the original formula as a limited‑edition or regional offering, providing a safety net and allowing bottlers to adjust. Conduct further longitudinal studies on brand perception and supply‑chain impact before a full discontinuation.

**Conclusion**  
The current all‑or‑nothing approach carries significant strategic and operational risks that outweigh the short‑term taste‑test advantage.

DECISION: NO-GO
