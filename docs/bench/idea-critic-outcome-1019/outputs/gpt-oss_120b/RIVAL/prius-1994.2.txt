The proposal targets an aggressive timeline—mass‑production of a petrol‑electric hybrid by 1997—using a novel power‑split transmission and nickel‑metal‑hydride (Ni‑MH) batteries that have not yet proven durability in volume use.  

**Strategic context**  
* Fuel is cheap in the primary export market, reducing immediate operating‑cost incentives for customers.  
* Regulators have not mandated hybrid technology, so there is no external pressure creating demand.  
* No competitor currently offers a production hybrid, giving a first‑mover claim but also removing any market validation.

**Key risks**  
1. **Cost** – The new transmission and Ni‑MH pack will add material and engineering expense. Without a clear premium price that customers are willing to pay (fuel cheap, no mandate), the unit cost may exceed market tolerance.  
2. **Reliability & warranty exposure** – Battery life and performance are untested at scale. Early‑generation hybrids historically suffered premature degradation, leading to costly warranty claims and brand damage.  
3. **Demand uncertainty** – No near‑term consumer signal exists; buyers have little incentive to pay extra for a technology that offers modest fuel‑economy gains when gasoline is inexpensive.  
4. **Programmatic pressure** – A bold public schedule creates internal pressure that can force premature launch, increasing the likelihood of the above issues materializing.

**Mitigation pathways** (not part of the current proposal) would include: phased rollout with a limited pilot fleet, securing a supply‑chain guarantee for battery durability, and aligning the launch with emerging regulatory trends or fuel‑price shifts.

Given the high technical uncertainty, cost exposure, and lack of market pull, proceeding as outlined would likely generate significant financial and reputational risk before the program can prove its value.

**Conclusion** – The plan should be paused for further validation, demand assessment, and risk mitigation before committing to the 1997 mass‑production schedule.

DECISION: NO-GO
