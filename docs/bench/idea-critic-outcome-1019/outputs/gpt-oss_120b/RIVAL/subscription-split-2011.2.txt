**Executive Summary**  
The proposal to spin off the DVD‑by‑mail operation into a distinct brand while leaving streaming under the current name aims to give each line its own strategic focus. The DVD side is indeed in long‑term decline, and a dedicated management team could tailor pricing, inventory, and logistics without being constrained by streaming’s growth agenda. Streaming, meanwhile, would avoid being tied to a legacy‑heavy product and could pursue aggressive content acquisition and pricing.

**Key Benefits**  
1. **Focused Management** – Separate P&L and product roadmaps allow optimized cost structures for the DVD business (e.g., lower fulfillment spend) and unencumbered investment in streaming features.  
2. **Brand Clarity** – A pure‑streaming brand can market itself as a modern, on‑demand service without the “old‑media” baggage that the DVD name conveys.  

**Primary Risks**  
1. **Customer Confusion** – Existing subscribers who bought the combined plan will now face two accounts, two login portals, and two monthly bills. This friction can increase churn, especially after a 60 % price hike.  
2. **Loss of Cross‑Service Recommendations** – The current recommendation engine leverages viewing and rental data to surface relevant titles across formats. Decoupling the data silos will degrade discovery and reduce perceived value.  
3. **Price‑Increase Backlash** – The 60 % uplift already strains price‑sensitive users; adding administrative overhead may amplify negative sentiment and generate public relations issues.  

**Mitigation Recommendations**  
- Implement a **single‑sign‑on (SSO)** bridge that lets customers access both sites with one credential while preserving separate billing.  
- Preserve a **shared recommendation service** that aggregates data across the two brands, even if the UI remains distinct.  
- Offer **grandfathered pricing** or a transition discount for current combined‑plan members to soften the price shock.  

**Conclusion**  
The strategic rationale is sound, but the execution plan omits critical friction‑reduction measures. Approving the split without addressing login, billing, and recommendation continuity would likely damage subscriber satisfaction and churn rates.

DECISION: NO-GO
