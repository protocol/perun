**Executive Summary**  
The conglomerate would acquire a proven computer‑animation studio for $7.4 B in an all‑stock transaction, placing its creative leadership over the conglomerate’s animation division. The deal would instantly secure a top‑tier content pipeline but carries significant financial, cultural, and governance risks.

**Key Benefits**  
1. **Content Strength** – The target consistently delivers box‑office hits and critical acclaim, providing a reliable annual blockbuster that the conglomerate’s hand‑drawn unit has failed to match.  
2. **Strategic Control** – Installing the studio’s creative heads at the helm could revitalize the conglomerate’s animation portfolio and align creative vision across both units.  
3. **Shareholder Upside** – An all‑stock deal preserves cash, and the founder‑shareholder becomes a major equity holder, aligning his incentives with long‑term success.

**Major Risks**  
1. **Price Premium** – At $7.4 B the acquisition values the studio at roughly 10× its annual cash flow (≈1 film/yr). Comparable deals in the sector command lower multiples, raising the likelihood of an earnings shortfall.  
2. **Cultural Integration** – The studio’s technology‑driven, computer‑animation culture differs sharply from the conglomerate’s traditional hand‑drawn processes. Past mergers of creative studios have shown that misaligned workflows can erode productivity and morale.  
3. **Leadership Concentration** – Relying on a small group of creative leaders creates a “key‑person” risk; their departure would jeopardize the expected performance uplift.  
4. **Governance Complexity** – The founder becoming a major shareholder introduces a powerful external voice that could clash with existing board dynamics, potentially hindering decisive action.  
5. **Alternative Partnerships** – The studio could walk to a rival, meaning the conglomerate would lose the partnership without gaining any asset, leaving it with a costly, possibly under‑performing integration.

**Financial Outlook**  
Assuming the studio’s average annual EBITDA is roughly $600 M, the implied purchase multiple is ~12.3×. To justify this, the combined entity must achieve at least a 30 % uplift in net earnings within 3 years—a target that historical post‑acquisition performance in the industry rarely meets.

**Recommendation**  
Given the steep valuation, integration uncertainty, and concentration of leadership risk, the prudent course is to **pause** and seek either a lower‑priced structure (e.g., partial stake or earn‑out) or negotiate stronger governance protections. A full acquisition at the current terms presents an outsized downside relative to the incremental upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
