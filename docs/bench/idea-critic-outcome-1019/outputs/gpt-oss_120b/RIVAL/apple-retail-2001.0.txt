**Evaluation**

The company holds a modest 3‑5 % market share and is already posting losses. Its current channel—third‑party electronics retailers—already pushes cheaper alternatives, indicating weak brand pull. Opening a chain of company‑run stores would add high fixed lease expenses in prime malls, a cost structure the firm cannot absorb given its recent financial performance. Retail computer sales historically operate on thin margins; adding those margins to an already loss‑making business deepens the cash‑flow strain.

**Risks**

1. **Fixed‑cost exposure** – Lease commitments persist through a recession, threatening solvency.  
2. **Inventory risk** – Stocking high‑value PCs and accessories ties up capital that could be better used for R&D or marketing.  
3. **Channel conflict** – Existing resellers may reduce orders or demand higher discounts, eroding the very sales pipeline the stores aim to protect.  
4. **Industry precedent** – Few PC manufacturers have succeeded with owned retail; the track record is poor, suggesting a steep learning curve and likely mis‑allocation of resources.

**Potential Upside**

The only clear upside is a controlled environment to showcase the new digital‑music strategy. However, this can be achieved through smaller “experience” kiosks, pop‑up events, or partnerships with existing retailers, all at far lower fixed cost.

**Recommendation**

Given the company’s weak financial position, limited market share, and the high‑cost, low‑margin nature of retail, the proposed chain introduces disproportionate risk with limited strategic benefit. The firm should instead focus on strengthening its brand within existing channels and exploring low‑cost experiential marketing.

DECISION: NO-GO
