**Executive Summary**  
The chain’s historic revenue model relies heavily on deep discounts and coupon‑driven traffic. Switching wholesale to everyday low pricing (EDLP) and a full‑store redesign eliminates the primary value proposition for its core, price‑sensitive customers. The proposal lacks a controlled pilot, ignores the behavioral lock‑in of the existing shopper, and attempts a wholesale rollout across a mid‑market retailer in a single, rapid wave.

**Risks**  
1. **Customer Habit Entrenchment** – Decades of coupon culture mean shoppers will likely defer purchases awaiting future deals, causing an immediate sales dip.  
2. **Revenue Shock** – With “most sales historically coming from discounted items,” a sudden price‑level shift could erase margin buffers before the new pricing stabilizes.  
3. **Brand‑Fit Mismatch** – The chief executive’s success in a branded‑electronics niche does not guarantee transferability to a diversified department‑store assortment where price elasticity varies by category.  
4. **Operational Overload** – Simultaneous store redesign, inventory repricing, and staff retraining across the entire chain risks supply‑chain disruption, staffing errors, and inconsistent customer experience.  
5. **No Pilot Data** – Without test stores, there is no empirical evidence to calibrate price points, promotional cadence, or shopper response, making forecasting highly speculative.

**Mitigation Recommendations**  
- **Pilot Program** – Deploy the EDLP model in 3–5 geographically diverse stores for 6‑12 months. Capture sales lift, basket size, and foot‑traffic metrics.  
- **Hybrid Pricing** – Introduce a “baseline low price + limited promotional events” hybrid to ease customers into the new regime while preserving some coupon‑driven traffic.  
- **Customer Communication** – Run a targeted education campaign explaining the benefits of everyday pricing (e.g., price transparency, simplified shopping).  
- **Phased Store Redesign** – Stagger physical remodels to align with inventory cycles and staffing availability.

**Conclusion**  
Given the magnitude of change, the absence of pilot data, and the high dependency on discount‑driven sales, approving the full‑scale rollout now is premature. A controlled pilot and hybrid pricing approach are essential to de‑risk the transition and validate assumptions.

**Decision**  
Proceed with a pilot and phased implementation before committing the entire chain to the proposed model.

DECISION: NO-GO
