The company’s 3‑5 % market share places it far from a dominant position, and recent losses already indicate a fragile financial base. Opening a chain of company‑run stores would add substantial fixed costs—prime‑mall leases, staffing, inventory, and store‑level overhead—at a time when computer‑retail margins are notoriously thin. Those costs would have to be covered before any incremental sales materialize, putting further pressure on cash flow.

The stated strategic benefit—showcasing the product and a new digital‑music offering—can be achieved through lower‑cost channels: dedicated brand‑experience kiosks inside existing retailers, pop‑up showcases at events, or enhanced online‑to‑physical experiences (e.g., virtual demos, augmented‑reality tools). Leveraging third‑party electronics retailers already provides a distribution network; adding a parallel owned channel creates direct channel conflict, risking reduced support from those partners who already favor cheaper competing machines.

External risk factors are severe. A recession would depress discretionary spending on personal computers, making the high‑lease commitments a liability. Inventory risk is amplified by the need to stock a broad product line in each store, potentially leading to excess or obsolete stock. The industry’s historical poor performance with proprietary retail chains suggests a low probability of achieving the required sales lift to justify the investment.

Given the company’s current loss streak, modest market share, and the high fixed‑cost, high‑risk nature of the proposal, the prudent course is to defer the retail‑store plan. Instead, focus on strengthening margins, improving the digital‑music ecosystem, and exploring cost‑effective brand‑experience initiatives within existing retail partnerships.

DECISION: NO-GO
