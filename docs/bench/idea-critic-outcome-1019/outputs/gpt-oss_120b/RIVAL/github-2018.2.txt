The acquisition price of $7.5 billion in stock vastly exceeds the target’s current revenue, which is negative and has no clear path to profitability. Paying a premium for a loss‑making company without a permanent chief executive adds execution risk: leadership vacuum will delay strategic decisions and make integration harder.

The developer community’s mistrust of the buyer is a concrete threat. Since the service’s value derives from its active user base, even modest defection could reduce the platform’s network effects, lower future revenue, and damage the buyer’s brand. Competing free alternatives already enjoy vocal support, increasing the likelihood of churn.

Regulatory and antitrust scrutiny are significant. Acquiring the dominant code‑hosting service consolidates market power in a sector that governments are increasingly monitoring for anti‑competitive behavior. The transaction could trigger lengthy investigations, possible remedies, or even a forced divestiture, all of which would erode shareholder value.

Given the combination of an inflated price, leadership instability, community backlash, viable free competitors, and strong regulatory risk, the proposal does not meet a prudent risk‑adjusted return threshold. The board should either renegotiate terms, require a clear integration plan with leadership appointments, and obtain regulatory clearance before proceeding.

DECISION: NO-GO
