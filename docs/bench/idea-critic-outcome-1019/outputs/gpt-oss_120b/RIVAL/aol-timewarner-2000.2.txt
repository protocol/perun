The deal pairs a massive, but rapidly eroding, dial‑up business with a diversified media empire. While the strategic logic—content plus a distribution platform, cross‑promotion, and the promise of high‑speed cable broadband—looks compelling, the fundamentals are mis‑aligned for a successful merger in early 2000.

**Valuation risk** – The ISP’s market cap is driven by a speculative dot‑com premium. Its stock price will be highly volatile, and a sudden correction would transfer a disproportionate loss to the media‑conglomerate’s shareholders, who would become a minority.

**Business‑model mismatch** – Dial‑up revenue is on a steep decline as broadband spreads. The ISP’s core cash flow will disappear before the combined entity can monetize the cable‑based broadband vision, especially since the cable network is owned by the target and not guaranteed to be dedicated to the new company.

**Integration challenges** – Cultural and operational differences between a fast‑moving internet firm and a legacy media group are profound. Past mergers of similar scale have stumbled on decision‑making speed, product‑roadmap alignment, and talent retention. No concrete integration plan is presented.

**Regulatory hurdle** – A vertical combination that gives the new firm control over both content creation and a major broadband conduit will attract intense antitrust scrutiny. Approval is far from assured and could impose costly divestitures or conditions.

**Strategic gaps** – The proposal does not address how the new company will acquire or upgrade broadband capacity, how it will protect the media assets from the volatility of the internet business, or what governance safeguards will balance the minority shareholders.

Given the high valuation risk, the imminent decline of the ISP’s core business, uncertain access to the cable infrastructure, cultural integration uncertainty, and the likelihood of a protracted regulator review, the transaction is premature. The board should require a revised structure that caps exposure to the ISP’s volatile equity, secures a firm, long‑term broadband commitment, and outlines a detailed integration and governance plan before proceeding.

DECISION: NO-GO
