The plan adds a low‑quality, small‑catalog streaming service for free to an already profitable DVD‑rental base. In 2007 broadband penetration is still limited, so many subscribers cannot use streaming reliably. The streaming library consists mainly of older titles and will not attract new customers or increase engagement. Licensing fees for streaming are high, adding a cost burden without a clear revenue stream. Offering it at no extra charge erodes the margin of the core DVD business, which is the company’s primary profit engine. The risk of cannibalisation is therefore significant: existing subscribers may shift from DVDs to the inferior streaming option, reducing DVD shipments and associated revenue. Moreover, large technology and retail players are expected to enter the streaming market, bringing deep pockets, superior infrastructure, and broader content deals that this company cannot match. Competing directly would likely force a price war and further margin compression.

Given these factors—limited broadband, poor video quality, high licensing costs, no incremental revenue, and imminent competition—the proposal threatens the current profitable model without delivering a clear strategic advantage. A more prudent approach would be to defer streaming until broadband penetration, content licensing terms, and competitive dynamics improve, or to launch a limited pilot with a clear monetisation plan.

DECISION: NO-GO
