The target is the leading video‑sharing platform, but its business fundamentals are weak. Its staff is minimal, it generates virtually no revenue, and it incurs massive bandwidth expenses that will balloon under a larger user base. The buyer already operates a smaller video service, so the acquisition does not add a proven, complementary product line—rather it adds a costly, unprofitable operation.

Key risk factors:

1. **Price** – $1.65 billion in stock for a cash‑flow‑negative asset is steep. The buyer would be paying a premium for brand and traffic without a clear path to monetize that traffic at a scale that justifies the cost.
2. **Copyright litigation** – A large share of user‑uploaded content is infringing TV and film clips. Media companies have already threatened lawsuits, exposing the buyer to costly legal defenses and potential damages that could outweigh any future ad revenue.
3. **Unproven ad model** – The market for video advertising was nascent in 2006. There is no evidence the site can generate sufficient ad revenue to offset bandwidth and legal costs.
4. **Bandwidth at scale** – Existing high bandwidth bills suggest that scaling the service will dramatically increase operating expenses, eroding any margin even if ad revenue improves.

Given the combination of an inflated valuation, significant legal exposure, and an untested monetization strategy, the acquisition presents a high‑risk, low‑return scenario. The prudent course is to decline the deal until a viable, revenue‑generating model and a clear legal compliance framework can be demonstrated.

DECISION: NO-GO
