**Proposal Summary**  
In 2013 a software company that runs a low‑share mobile OS wants to buy the handset division of the principal OEM that ships devices with that OS for €5.4 bn. The goal is to own both hardware and software, preventing the OEM from moving to the dominant rival platform. The OEM’s phone sales are already declining, while the rival OS enjoys a massive app and developer ecosystem.

**Key Benefits**  
- **Vertical integration** could streamline firmware updates, improve performance, and ensure tighter security between OS and hardware.  
- **Strategic control** prevents the OEM from switching to the competitor, preserving the software company’s existing user base.  
- **Brand alignment** may create a more cohesive user experience, potentially attracting niche customers who value an integrated ecosystem.

**Major Risks**  
- **Integration complexity:** Merging a large, culturally distinct workforce into a software‑centric organization is costly and time‑consuming, with a high chance of talent loss and operational disruption.  
- **Industry backlash:** Competing handset makers may accelerate their own moves toward the dominant platform, reducing OEM market share further and limiting the combined entity’s bargaining power with carriers and suppliers.  
- **App‑ecosystem gap:** Ownership of hardware does not inherently solve the fundamental problem of a sparse app catalogue; developers gravitate to the platform with the largest user base, and the €5.4 bn outlay does not guarantee a turnaround in app availability.  
- **Financial exposure:** The acquisition price is large relative to the OEM’s declining sales, increasing the risk of a poor return on investment if market share does not rebound.

**Recommendation**  
Given the steep integration hurdles, the limited upside of merely owning hardware, and the core competitive disadvantage—an under‑populated app ecosystem—the proposed acquisition is unlikely to deliver the intended strategic benefit. A more prudent path would be to focus on strengthening the software platform’s developer incentives, explore strategic partnerships, or consider smaller, targeted acquisitions that address specific technology gaps.

**Conclusion**  
Approve a pause and revision of the plan rather than proceeding as outlined.

DECISION: NO-GO
