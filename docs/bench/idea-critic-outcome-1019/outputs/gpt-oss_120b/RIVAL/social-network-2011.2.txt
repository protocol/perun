The proposal seeks to launch a new social network in 2011 by embedding a profile and sharing layer across existing search, mail, video, and photo services, with an invite‑only beta and executive bonuses tied to adoption. While the integration could expose the network to a large existing user base, several red flags outweigh potential upside.

1. **Historical performance** – The company has already launched three social‑network attempts that failed, indicating a pattern of misreading market demand or execution challenges.
2. **Network‑effect barrier** – The market leader already enjoys hundreds of millions of users. Gaining critical mass without a compelling, differentiated value proposition is exceedingly difficult.
3. **Forced integration risk** – Embedding the new layer into core products may alienate current users who value those services for their primary functions, leading to churn or reduced engagement.
4. **Incentive misalignment** – Executive bonuses tied directly to adoption could pressure teams to prioritize short‑term metrics over sustainable growth, encouraging aggressive tactics that damage brand trust.
5. **Differentiators insufficiently proven** – “Friend‑grouping circles” and “video hangouts” are modest enhancements that competitors can replicate quickly and do not address the fundamental barrier of user migration.

Given the high probability of low adoption, the potential for user backlash, and the company’s track record, proceeding as described poses significant strategic and financial risk. A more prudent path would be to conduct focused experiments, validate demand, and redesign incentives before committing to a full‑scale launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
