The proposal aims to replace a century‑old flagship cola with a sweeter formula that outperforms both the current product and the main rival in blind taste tests, surveys, and focus groups. The data show clear short‑term taste preference, which could boost market share against diet and non‑cola competitors.

However, several high‑impact risks are ignored:

1. **Emotional attachment and brand heritage** – The original formula is a cultural icon; removing it could alienate long‑time customers and erode brand equity built over generations.
2. **No fallback product** – If the new formula fails to resonate in the broader market or encounters supply issues, the company would have no alternative to re‑enter the market quickly.
3. **Bottler and consumer backlash** – Loyal bottlers have invested in the existing product line and may resist a sudden switch, potentially causing distribution disruptions.
4. **Testing limitation** – Participants were not informed that the original would be discontinued, so the tests did not capture reactions to loss of the legacy product.

Given these unresolved strategic and operational concerns, the proposal should be paused for a deeper analysis that includes brand‑impact studies, a phased rollout with a legacy “classic” line retained, and contingency planning for supply and bottler alignment.

DECISION: NO-GO
