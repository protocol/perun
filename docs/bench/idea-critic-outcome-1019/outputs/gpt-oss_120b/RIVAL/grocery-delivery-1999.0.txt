The proposal calls for a $1 billion, capital‑intensive build‑out of massive, highly automated warehouses and a fleet of delivery trucks before the company has proven the economics of its core service. Grocery delivery operates on thin margins, and profitability depends on very high order density per square foot and per vehicle. Without an operating track record, the firm cannot yet demonstrate that it can attract enough customers in a single metropolitan area to cover the fixed costs of such facilities or to achieve the 30‑minute delivery promise that drives operational complexity.

Spending a large, irreversible sum on infrastructure across a dozen cities creates a “one‑way” commitment: the assets cannot be repurposed easily if demand falls short. The risks the team has identified—capital intensity, thin margins, required customer density, and lack of historical performance—are precisely the factors that should be validated through a modest pilot before scaling. A disciplined approach would involve:

1. Operating the first warehouse at full capacity to establish unit economics.  
2. Using the data to size subsequent facilities and fleet size.  
3. Expanding incrementally, funding each new location with cash flow or modest equity raises tied to proven performance.

Premature, large‑scale expansion threatens cash burn, dilutes investor confidence, and could lock the company into an unsustainable cost structure. The prudent course is to defer the $1 billion spend until the first market’s economics are validated.

**Recommendation:** Hold the massive capital deployment and adopt a staged rollout that learns and proves the model before committing further resources.
