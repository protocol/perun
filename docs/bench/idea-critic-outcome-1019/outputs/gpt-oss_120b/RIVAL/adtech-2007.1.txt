The deal would be the company’s biggest cash outlay, a $6 billion premium‑priced purchase of a digital‑ad agency and its ad‑serving platform. While acquiring technology that serves display ads could give the firm a foothold against the search leader, the strategic upside is uncertain for three reasons:

1. **Price risk** – Paying a large premium in cash strains balance‑sheet flexibility and forces a high return‑on‑investment threshold. The market leader’s simultaneous bid suggests the target is already priced near the top of the market; matching that premium offers little bargain‑price advantage.

2. **Customer conflict** – The buyer already runs an agency business. Owning an ad‑serving platform that competes with the buyer’s existing agency clients creates a direct conflict of interest, potentially driving key agency accounts away and eroding recurring revenue.

3. **Technology volatility** – Display‑ad serving is evolving rapidly (e.g., programmatic buying, real‑time bidding, privacy regulations). An acquired platform could become obsolete within a few years, making the cash investment a sunk‑cost liability unless the buyer can continuously innovate.

Given the magnitude of the cash commitment, the integration friction with existing agency customers, and the fast‑changing tech landscape, the proposal carries excessive financial and operational risk at this stage. A more measured approach—such as strategic partnerships, minority stakes, or a smaller, lower‑priced acquisition—would mitigate these risks while still providing a path to display‑ad capability.

DECISION: NO-GO
