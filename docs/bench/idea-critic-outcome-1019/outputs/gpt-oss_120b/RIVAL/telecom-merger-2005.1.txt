The merger would instantly add a valuable business‑customer segment, but it also ties the buyer to a legacy, non‑data‑centric network that cannot be merged without massive engineering effort. The two carriers run fundamentally different radio standards, meaning the combined firm would have to either maintain two parallel infrastructures or invest heavily in a network conversion—both options drive up capex and OPEX far beyond the $36 B purchase price.  

Goodwill on the balance sheet adds financial risk, especially if the integration stalls and the legacy network erodes customer confidence. In 2005, data services are beginning to drive growth; inheriting a network that “is limited for data” threatens the combined company’s ability to compete on emerging mobile broadband offerings.  

Without a clear, funded plan to harmonize the radio technologies, de‑risk the goodwill exposure, and guarantee service continuity for the high‑value business base, the upside does not outweigh the integration and operational hazards. The proposal should be revisited after a detailed technical integration roadmap and a financial model that isolates the cost of network convergence.

DECISION: NO-GO
