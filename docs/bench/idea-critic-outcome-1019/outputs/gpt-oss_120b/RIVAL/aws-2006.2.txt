The proposal pivots a thin‑margin retailer into a capital‑intensive, high‑skill developer‑platform business for which it has no track record. The core retail operation already faces “spiky” infrastructure costs; adding a cloud service will amplify those spikes and likely erode margins further, contradicting the current need to tighten spending.  

Key risk factors are:

* **Experience gap** – No team has built, operated, or scaled a pay‑per‑use compute/storage platform. Achieving the reliability and performance expected by developers typically requires deep expertise in hardware provisioning, networking, billing, and support, which must be acquired from scratch.  

* **Market uncertainty** – In 2006 the hourly‑rented server market is unproven. Early adopters are limited, and demand forecasts are speculative. Competing against entrenched hardware/software vendors would demand aggressive pricing that further squeezes margins.  

* **Investor confidence** – Capital‑hungry investors already question the retailer’s heavy spending. Launching a parallel, capital‑heavy line could exacerbate concerns and jeopardize existing financing.  

* **Strategic distraction** – Diverting engineering, product, and sales focus away from the retailer’s core e‑commerce value chain threatens execution on the primary revenue source.  

Given the combination of a nascent market, lack of internal capability, and the potential to weaken the retailer’s existing business, the proposal presents a high probability of margin dilution and operational failure without a clear path to offsetting revenue. The prudent course is to defer or redesign the initiative until the retailer can build requisite expertise, secure dedicated capital, and validate market demand.

DECISION: NO-GO
