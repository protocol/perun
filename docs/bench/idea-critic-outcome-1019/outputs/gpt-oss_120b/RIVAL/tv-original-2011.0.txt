**Overview**  
The service plans to spend a large portion of its content budget on a two‑season, 26‑episode original drama, bypassing the usual pilot test and releasing all episodes at once. This would be the first prestige scripted series produced by an online‑only platform, with a well‑known director and film star attached.

**Risks**  
1. **No pilot de‑risking** – Without a pilot, the team cannot gauge audience reaction, leading to a binary “all‑in” gamble.  
2. **Uncertain audience** – Viewing data shows strong consumption of licensed content, but there is no evidence that the subscriber base will migrate to long‑form prestige drama, especially when released in bulk.  
3. **Cost exposure** – Licensing costs are already rising; allocating a large share of the budget to a single untested property could strain cash flow and limit future acquisitions.  
4. **Market precedent** – No online‑only service has successfully launched a prestige series in this manner, increasing execution uncertainty.

**Potential Upsides**  
- Differentiation from competitors that rely solely on licensed libraries.  
- Ability to attract new subscribers seeking exclusive, high‑profile content.  
- Long‑tail revenue from binge‑watching and international licensing.

**Recommendation**  
Given the high financial exposure, lack of pilot validation, and absence of market precedent, the prudent path is to first test the concept with a pilot or limited‑run series. This would provide concrete audience data, reduce sunk‑cost risk, and preserve budget flexibility for continued licensing. Proceeding with the full two‑season order now is too risky.

**Conclusion**  
Approve a scaled‑down pilot approach before committing to the full production.

DECISION: NO-GO
