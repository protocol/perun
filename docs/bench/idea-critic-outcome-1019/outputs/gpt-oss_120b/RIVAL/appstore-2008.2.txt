The proposal mirrors the successful model that launched a year earlier on a rival platform, showing that a curated native‑app store can drive a vibrant ecosystem while protecting the user experience. By keeping the distribution channel single‑sourced and requiring company review, the firm can enforce security standards, maintain stability, and preserve product polish—issues that would be far harder to control with an open‑distribution model. The 30 % revenue share aligns incentives for developers and the company, and a vetted marketplace can become a differentiator rather than a liability.

The highlighted risks are real but manageable:

* **Security & stability** – A rigorous review process plus sandboxing can limit exposure from untrusted code. The company should invest in automated testing and static analysis to keep the workload tractable.
* **Carrier objections** – Early engagement with carriers to define permissible app categories and to integrate carrier‑specific billing where needed will mitigate push‑back.
* **Review workload** – Scaling the review team, leveraging triage automation, and setting clear submission guidelines will keep turnaround times reasonable.
* **Polish impact** – Curated approval preserves the brand’s reputation for quality; the store can enforce UI/UX guidelines and performance metrics.

Given that rival platforms already benefit from large app ecosystems, entering the market now avoids falling further behind and positions the phone as a first‑class platform for native experiences. The strategic upside—greater user engagement, new revenue streams, and a defensible ecosystem—outweighs the manageable operational challenges.

**Recommendation:** Approve the launch of the company‑run app store, with the conditions that the firm invests in a robust security sandbox, expands the review team, and secures carrier agreements before the store goes live.
