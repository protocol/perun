The product targets a premium consumer segment, yet its $699 price point places it well above typical kitchen appliances and rivals high‑end juicers that cost half as much while offering similar functionality without a subscription lock‑in. The recurring cost of pre‑packed bags must compete directly with the raw price of fresh produce; most consumers can obtain comparable nutritional value by buying fruit and vegetables outright and juicing at home for a fraction of the per‑bag price, especially given seasonal price fluctuations. Requiring Wi‑Fi connectivity adds friction: users without reliable internet, those concerned about data privacy, or renters unable to install permanent devices will be excluded, shrinking the addressable market. Moreover, the business hinges on a subscription model whose adoption rates are historically low for consumable food services, and no market sizing data is provided to demonstrate sufficient demand to sustain tens of millions of dollars in funding. Without clear evidence that the combined hardware‑plus‑subscription offering delivers a compelling value proposition and a sizable, reachable market, the risk outweighs the potential reward.

DECISION: NO-GO
