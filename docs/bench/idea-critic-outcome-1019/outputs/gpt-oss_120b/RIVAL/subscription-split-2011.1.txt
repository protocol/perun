The proposal tries to solve two divergent trends with a single structural change: a declining DVD‑by‑mail business and a fast‑growing streaming service. Splitting the brands will let each unit pursue its own product roadmap, pricing, and partnerships without being held back by the other’s constraints. That separation can improve focus, enable distinct marketing messages, and allow the streaming side to experiment with free‑tier or ad‑supported models while the DVD side can negotiate with distributors and retailers on its own terms.

However, the plan also introduces several customer‑experience risks that are not addressed:

1. **Two accounts and two bills** – Existing customers who currently pay a single combined price will now have to manage separate logins and payment flows. In 2011, many users still preferred simple, unified experiences; forcing a split will likely increase support tickets, churn, and negative sentiment, especially after a recent 60 % price hike.

2. **Loss of cross‑service recommendations** – The current recommendation engine benefits from combined viewing and rental data. Removing that shared signal reduces the relevance of suggestions for both DVD and streaming, weakening a key differentiator for the service.

3. **Reaction to the price increase** – The price hike alone already risks churn. Adding brand fragmentation could amplify the backlash, as customers may perceive the move as a “nickel‑and‑dime” tactic rather than a strategic realignment.

4. **Operational overhead** – Running two separate websites, billing systems, and support channels raises cost and complexity. The proposal assumes the DVD side “needs different management,” but it does not outline how the additional overhead will be justified against the expected decline in DVD revenue.

Mitigation paths (single sign‑on, shared recommendation data, a transition period with bundled pricing, clear communication) are absent. Without concrete steps to address these friction points, the strategic benefit of separation is outweighed by the immediate risk of customer loss and brand dilution.

**Conclusion:** Approve only after a detailed mitigation plan is added to handle account unification, recommendation sharing, and communication of the price change.

DECISION: NO-GO
