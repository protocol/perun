**Executive Summary**  
The proposal calls for a legacy film‑TV conglomerate to abandon lucrative licensing deals, launch a low‑price subscription streaming service, and fund the venture with sustained operating losses while it builds an audience. The target is “tens of millions” of subscribers in five years, versus a market leader already above one hundred million.

**Key Risks**  

1. **Revenue Cannibalisation** – Pulling content from rival platforms eliminates a proven, high‑margin licensing stream. Early cash flow will depend entirely on subscriber acquisition, which is uncertain at the projected scale.  
2. **Cash‑Burn Sustainability** – Funding a multi‑year loss‑making operation requires deep pockets. If subscriber growth stalls, the company must continue subsidising the service, risking balance‑sheet strain and reduced investment capacity elsewhere.  
3. **Technology Execution** – The firm has no track record operating a streaming platform. Building, scaling, and securing a global service entails substantial engineering, content‑delivery, and data‑privacy challenges.  
4. **Cannibalisation of Existing Channels** – Removing content from cable and theatrical releases may erode those revenue pillars, especially if consumers shift to the new service before it proves profitable.

**Mitigating Factors**  

- Industry precedent (e.g., Disney+, HBO Max) shows that strong brand libraries can attract rapid subscriber growth when paired with aggressive pricing and deep marketing.  
- A low‑price tier can lower the barrier to entry and accelerate churn from rivals, but only if the user experience, device compatibility, and content discovery are competitive.  
- Multi‑year loss tolerance is viable only if the balance sheet can absorb the burn without jeopardising other strategic initiatives.

**Strategic Assessment**  

The upside—capturing a share of the growing streaming market and owning the direct consumer relationship—aligns with long‑term industry trends. However, the proposal lacks concrete mitigation plans for the four high‑impact risks. Without a phased rollout, clear break‑even milestones, and a technology partnership or acquisition strategy, the initiative is likely to jeopardise existing cash‑generating businesses.

**Recommendation**  

Defer approval until the company delivers a detailed risk‑mitigation roadmap: (a) phased licensing withdrawal with fallback revenue guarantees, (b) a cash‑burn model tied to subscriber milestones, (c) a technology execution plan (partner or build‑buy analysis), and (d) a cannibalisation impact study. Only after these are validated should the launch move forward.

DECISION: NO-GO
