The proposal tries to extend a profitable DVD‑rental model by adding a low‑quality, small‑catalog streaming service at no extra charge. In 2007 broadband penetration was far from universal, and many subscribers would experience buffering or unwatchable video. Licensing the streaming rights that studios demand is far more expensive per title than the DVD rights that already fund the core business, so the cost base would rise sharply without a commensurate revenue stream.

Streaming directly competes with the existing DVD offering. Since the service is free for current members, a sizable share of them will shift to streaming whenever bandwidth allows, eroding the high‑margin DVD rentals that sustain the company’s cash flow. The cannibalisation risk therefore threatens the very profit engine that makes the business attractive.

Furthermore, the market is about to be flooded by large technology and retail players (e.g., Apple, Amazon, Google) with deep pockets, superior infrastructure, and the ability to secure premium content at scale. Entering now forces the company to invest heavily in technology and licensing just to keep pace, while offering a sub‑par experience that could damage the brand.

Given the high licensing cost, technology investment, limited broadband reach, poor user experience, and imminent competition, the strategic upside does not outweigh the financial and operational risks. The plan should be postponed, refined, or abandoned until the market and technology mature.

DECISION: NO-GO
