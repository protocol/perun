The proposal isolates two diverging businesses at a moment when the DVD‑by‑mail model is already on a steep decline while streaming is gaining rapid market traction. By giving each line its own brand, product team, and billing cycle, the company can tailor pricing, feature roadmaps, and marketing without the constraints of a single‑service compromise. This should improve operational focus, allow the streaming division to experiment with freemium or tiered models, and prevent the DVD side from pulling down the streaming brand’s perception of value.

**Key Risks & Mitigations**

1. **Customer confusion / dual login** – Introduce a unified authentication layer (single‑sign‑on) that lets users access both accounts with one credential, and provide a clear migration portal that explains the split and the benefits. A concise, step‑by‑step email campaign at launch will reduce friction.

2. **Loss of cross‑service recommendations** – Keep the recommendation engine shared in the backend and surface combined suggestions on each site, labeling the source (DVD vs. streaming) so users still benefit from their full consumption history.

3. **Reaction to the 60 % price hike** – Offer a limited‑time loyalty discount for existing dual‑subscribers who transition to the new DVD brand, and bundle a free trial of streaming for a month to soften the price shock.

4. **Brand dilution** – Choose a distinct but related brand name that signals “premium physical media” while preserving the streaming brand’s modern image, avoiding direct competition between the two.

Overall, the strategic upside (focused growth for streaming, clean cost structure for a shrinking DVD line) outweighs the manageable execution risks. With the outlined mitigations, the split should preserve customer goodwill and position the company for long‑term digital growth.

DECISION: GO
