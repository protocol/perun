**Recommendation:** Proceed with the 3-month pilot starting 1 Oct 2024, but explicitly cap the initial rollout to 50 tutors (not 400) and revise the revenue model to treat group classes as a distinct, lower-margin product line rather than a pure additive lift, given the high risk of cannibalization.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Cannibalization of High-Margin Revenue:** The 70% adoption assumption ignores that students may switch from $40–$60/hr private lessons to the $25/hr group option, reducing overall revenue per tutor even if total student hours increase.
2.  **Operational Collapse at Scale:** Managing scheduling, payment splitting, and technical stability for 90-minute multi-party video sessions introduces support costs and churn risks not present in 1-on-1 models, potentially eroding the projected $320k margin.
3.  **Tutor Capacity Illusion:** Tutors filling "4 groups per week" assumes perfect scheduling alignment; in reality, no-shows in an 8-person group drastically reduce effective hourly earnings compared to private lessons, leading to tutor drop-off before the 70% adoption target is met.

**Halt Trigger:** Stop the rollout immediately if, by the end of Month 2 of the pilot, the **net incremental revenue** (group revenue minus lost private lesson revenue minus additional support costs) is less than **$150 per tutor per week**.

**Hidden Risks Missed:**
*   **Quality Control:** A single bad experience in a group setting (e.g., one disruptive student) can cause 7 other students to churn simultaneously, whereas 1-on-1 issues are isolated.
*   **Technical Debt:** The current platform likely lacks native multi-party breakout rooms or group billing logic; the $120k dev estimate may be insufficient if it requires rebuilding the core session architecture.

*Note: The 15% revenue lift projection assumes zero cannibalization, which is historically unlikely in education marketplaces; a sensitivity analysis showing break-even cannibalization rates should accompany the proposal.*
*The $80k marketing budget should be contingent on achieving a >40% fill rate in the first 2 weeks of the pilot to avoid burning cash on empty seats.*
