**Recommendation: MODIFY (Hold Branch Expansion; Pivot to Digital Capacity & Pilot)**

Do not proceed with the $1.2M branch rollout. The math is flawed: a 15% growth on a $12M book yields $1.8M in *total new principal*, not $2M in *revenue*. Assuming a net interest margin of ~6%, the actual gross revenue gain is only ~$108k annually, which cannot cover the $360k+ increase in staff overhead alone, let alone the $1M technology capex. The proposal currently projects a guaranteed loss.

Shift strategy immediately:
1.  **Freeze physical expansion.** Cancel the 5 new branches and $1M tech spend.
2.  **Reallocate capital.** Invest the $200k solely in upgrading digital underwriting to compete with the fintech "instant-approve" threat.
3.  **Hire selectively.** Hire 2–3 senior loan officers focused on complex deals machines can't handle, rather than 12 generalists.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **Unit Economics Collapse:** As calculated above, the revenue model confuses principal volume with income. Even with perfect execution, the cost of 12 new officers and 5 branches exceeds the interest income from the projected growth, burning cash rather than generating profit.
2.  **Regulatory & Compliance Breach:** The assumption that "current underwriting capacity can handle extra volume" without new compliance staff is dangerous. A 15% volume spike typically triggers heightened regulatory scrutiny and requires proportional compliance headcount; skipping this invites fines or audit failures that dwarf loan profits.
3.  **Competitive Obsolescence:** Opening physical branches while a competitor launches "instant-approve" products is a strategic mismatch. Small businesses seeking speed will bypass your new branches for the fintech solution, leaving your new expensive infrastructure underutilized.

**Abort Trigger (Kill Criteria)**
If, by **September 30, 2025**, the pilot digital upgrade does not result in a **20% increase in application conversion rates** OR if **net new loan volume** fails to reach **$450,000** (25% of the annual target) by Q3, immediately halt all remaining hiring and capital deployment.

**Note:** The "flat interest rate" assumption is a secondary risk; if rates drop, your margin compresses further, worsening the loss. The primary blocker is the fundamental error in calculating revenue vs. principal.
