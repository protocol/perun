**Primary Failure Modes**
1.  **Arithmetic & Logic Slip:** Your projection assumes a linear scale from the Centennial campaign (4x budget = 1.5x return), which contradicts the law of diminishing returns in fundraising. Additionally, your math is internally inconsistent: 12,500 alumni × 20% participation = 2,500 donors. At $48/donor, this yields **$120,000**, not $1.2M. To reach $1.2M with 2,500 donors, the average gift must be **$480**, not $48. One of these variables is off by a factor of 10.

3.  **Missing Cost:** The "unpriced" third-party data vendor is a major risk. If this costs more than the projected margin (which is already negative based on the corrected $120k revenue vs. $350k+ spend), the campaign loses money on every donor acquired.

**Kill Criteria**
Terminate the initiative immediately if:
*   **Pilot Response Rate:** A test batch of 500 records yields a participation rate below **2%** (not 20%) after two weeks.
*   **Cost Per Acquisition (CPA):** The blended CPA (total spend / total donors) exceeds **$150** (three times your assumed average gift of $48, or 31% of the required $480 average gift) within the first 30 days.
*   **Data Vendor Pricing:** The fixed-price quote for the data vendor exceeds **$40,000**, rendering the projected ROI impossible even under optimistic participation scenarios.
