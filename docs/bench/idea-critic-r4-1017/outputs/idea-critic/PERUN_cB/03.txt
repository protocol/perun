**Recommendation: Proceed with a Go-If Decision**
Move forward with the pilot only if the licensing cost for the "Chronicles of Alder" series is confirmed to fit within the existing $15,000 contingency before any funds are committed on June 1, 2025.

**Primary Failure Modes & Hidden Risks**
1.  **Budget Collapse via Licensing:** The unpriced "Chronicles of Alder" licensing could exceed the $15,000 cushion. If this series is essential for the projected 30% adoption, failing to secure it cheaply invalidates the entire benefit case while consuming funds needed for the core 1,600 titles.
2.  **Adoption Overestimation:** Assuming 30% adoption (3,600 patrons) in year one is aggressive for a new platform without historical baseline data for this specific demographic. If adoption lands at 15%, the cost-per-checkout doubles, making the $85,000 allocation inefficient compared to renewing existing high-demand licenses.
3.  **Timeline Compression:** A three-month pilot (June–September) coincides with summer reading programs. Staff bandwidth may be too diverted to properly market the new platform or gather clean data, leading to a false negative on performance.
4.  **Catalog Math Error:** The proposal states adding 1,600 titles brings the total from 2,400 to 4,000. This arithmetic is correct, but it assumes zero attrition of existing titles during the transition. If the new platform requires dropping old vendors to fit the budget, the net gain may be significantly lower than 1,600.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Criteria**
Terminate the project immediately if, by May 15, 2025:
*   The total cost of the "Chronicles of Alder" license plus the base platform fee exceeds $85,000; OR
*   Preliminary patron surveys indicate less than 10% interest in the specific new titles offered, suggesting the 30% adoption target is unachievable.

**Leading Metric for Review**
Track "Active Unique Users" on the new platform weekly. If the count does not reach 600 (5% of total patrons) by July 15, 2025 (mid-pilot), trigger an immediate scope review to reallocate remaining funds to proven high-demand categories.
