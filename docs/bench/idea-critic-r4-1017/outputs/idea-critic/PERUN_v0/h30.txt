**Recommendation:** Do not launch the Family Plan on July 15 as proposed. The current financial model contains a fatal arithmetic error that turns the projected profit into an immediate loss, and the support costs are unpriced. Revise the proposal to correct the revenue calculation and define a support capacity limit before presenting to the CEO.

**How this plan could fail:**
1.  **Revenue Miscalculation:** The proposal claims $150,000 in annual revenue from 2,400 families. The actual math is 2,400 families × $4.99/month × 12 months = **$143,712**. Since development costs are $150,000, the plan loses at least $6,288 in year one before accounting for marketing ($25,000) or support. The claim of a "20% revenue increase" is false; this specific launch scenario results in a net negative cash flow relative to the investment.
2.  **Support Bankruptcy:** A family plan multiplies support tickets (multiple users, billing disputes, parental controls) without a budgeted increase in staff. If just 10% of families generate one extra ticket per month, and your current team is sized for individual users, response times will crash, driving churn for both new families and existing individual users.
3.  **Cannibalization Risk:** If the 2,400 "upgrades" come from existing individual subscribers consolidating accounts rather than new logos, total revenue drops. Four individuals paying separately generate ~$240/year; one family plan generates ~$60/year. Without a strict "new logo" acquisition strategy, you could shrink revenue while increasing load.

**Kill Criteria & Metric:**
*   **Stop/Pivot Trigger:** If the pilot conversion rate is **≤8%** (not the assumed 20%) OR if the average support ticket volume per family account exceeds **2.5×** the volume of an individual account.
*   **Action:** If either metric is hit after the 3-month pilot, halt the global rollout and pivot to a "Family Add-on" pricing tier ($2.99 per additional member) instead of a flat capped rate.
