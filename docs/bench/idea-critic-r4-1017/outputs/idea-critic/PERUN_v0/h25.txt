**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Arithmetic Slip:** Your projection assumes $1.1M revenue from 75,000 diners (30% of 250k) spending $20 total ($12 ticket + $8 food). However, $20 × 75,000 equals **$1.5 million**, not $1.1 million. Conversely, to hit $1.1M with a $20 average spend, you only need a **22% uptake** (55,000 diners), which matches the competitor's reported conversion. If your 30% assumption is correct, you are significantly underestimating revenue; if the competitor's 22% is the realistic ceiling, your revenue target is achievable but leaves no room for error in ticket pricing or food attach rates.
2.  **Contingency Insufficiency:** The $700k contingency covers 22% of the base cost, which is healthy, but the "unknown" licensing fee could add $120k. If construction encounters the typical 10–15% overrun common in retrofits ($110k–$165k) *and* the licensing fee hits its max ($120k), you face a potential $285k gap that eats directly into the contingency, leaving only ~$415k for operational delays or permit issues.
3.  **Market Cannibalization vs. Incrementality:** The 30% uptake assumption presumes these are new spenders. If 40% of these diners are simply shifting from existing standard seating to the premium auditorium without increasing their total spend (cannibalization), the *incremental* revenue drops drastically, potentially failing to cover the $3.2M capital outlay within a reasonable payback period (currently projected at ~2.9 years based on the flawed $1.1M figure).
