**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **The Productivity Illusion:** Your linear productivity assumption ignores route density; if optimized routes cluster stops in high-traffic zones rather than reducing highway miles, the 15% mileage cut may vanish while driver stress spikes, causing the projected $2,640/month labor savings to fail materializing.
2.  **Hidden Variable Costs:** The model assumes static fuel efficiency, but tighter windows often force aggressive driving (hard braking/acceleration) and idling in traffic, which can increase fuel consumption per mile by up to 20%, potentially eroding the entire $1,800 mileage saving.
3.  **Human Friction Tax:** Without data on morale, introducing tighter windows may trigger immediate resistance or turnover; replacing even one experienced driver costs ~$4,000 (recruiting + training + ramp-up), instantly wiping out two months of theoretical software savings.

**Abort/Redesign Triggers:**
*   **Financial:** Net operational cost (software fee + fuel + labor) exceeds current baseline by **>$300/month** for two consecutive months.
*   **Operational:** Driver turnover exceeds **1 driver (2.2%)** during the 90-day evaluation or average on-time delivery rate drops below **92%**.
