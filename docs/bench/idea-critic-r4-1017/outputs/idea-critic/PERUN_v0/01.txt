**Recommendation:** Proceed with the 3-month pilot starting 1 Oct 2024, but **do not** assume 70% tutor adoption or $320k monthly revenue in your baseline forecast. Instead, present a conservative "Base Case" assuming only 20% tutor adoption and 50% seat fill-rate, which reduces projected monthly incremental revenue to ~$68,000. This lowers the ROI timeline but protects credibility if uptake is slow. The $200k upfront investment is justified only if the pilot validates that group classes do not cannibalize higher-margin 1-on-1 sessions.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Cannibalization:** Existing 1-on-1 students migrate to the cheaper $25/hr group option, reducing overall revenue per student despite higher volume.
2.  **Tutor Bottleneck:** Tutors fail to recruit 8 students per session, leading to low-density classes that feel low-value to students while wasting tutor time, causing tutor churn rather than adoption.
3.  **Operational Drag:** Scheduling 8 students simultaneously creates high no-show rates and coordination friction, increasing support costs and degrading the user experience compared to flexible 1-on-1 booking.

**Halt Trigger (Kill Criterion):**
Stop the rollout immediately if, by the end of Month 2 of the pilot, the **net revenue contribution** (group revenue minus lost 1-on-1 revenue minus incremental support costs) is less than **$15,000 per month** across the entire pilot cohort, or if the average class fill-rate remains below **4 students** per session.

**Hidden Risks & Costs Missed:**
*   **Cannibalization Accounting:** Your current math treats group revenue as purely additive. You must track if 1-on-1 bookings drop concurrently; if a student switches from $40/hr (1-on-1) to $25/hr (group), you lose $15/hr even if the seat is filled.
*   **Technical Latency:** Live video for 9 people (1 tutor + 8 students) requires significantly more bandwidth and server stability than 1-on-1; expect higher infrastructure costs and potential quality complaints not included in the $120k dev budget.
*   **Tutor Training:** Tutors skilled in 1-on-1 instruction often struggle to manage group dynamics without specific training; lack of preparation could lead to poor reviews that damage the brand beyond just this feature.

*Note: The 70% adoption assumption within one month is historically unrealistic for behavioral changes; stress-test your cash flow against a 6-month ramp-up.*
*Ensure your tracking system can attribute individual students to both group and 1-on-1 histories to accurately measure cannibalization.*
