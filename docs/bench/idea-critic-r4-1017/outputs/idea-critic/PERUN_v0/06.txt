**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" condition.**
Move forward only if you secure binding fixed-price labor contracts in Dallas that cap installation costs at no more than 15% above Arizona rates before committing to the $500k inventory purchase. Without this, the projected 40% gross margin is mathematically unsustainable given the unadjusted labor variance.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Margin Collapse:** The plan assumes Texas per-kW costs ($2.0k) are lower than Arizona ($2.5k) while ignoring higher Dallas labor rates. If labor costs rise 20% above Arizona levels, your gross margin drops from 40% to ~28%, turning the projected $3.2M gross profit into barely enough to cover the $755k in new fixed costs (salaries, licensing, inventory financing), leaving negligible net income.
2.  **Cash Flow Strangulation:** You are fronting $650k in hard costs (inventory + licensing + first-year salaries) against a revenue stream delayed by unknown county permitting timelines. If permitting adds 6–8 weeks per job as feared, revenue recognition slips while salary burn continues, creating a cash trough that the current $4.2M Arizona revenue base may not support without external financing.
3.  **Incentive Cliff:** The entire $8M revenue model relies on the 30% federal/state incentive remaining static. Any legislative reduction to this incentive before your first 12 months concludes would immediately depress demand, leaving you with $500k in stranded inventory and three underutilized installers.

**Kill Criteria:**
Stop or freeze the expansion immediately if, after the first 50 installs, the **verified net margin falls below 12%**. This threshold accounts for the higher operating leverage in Texas; dropping below this indicates the labor cost assumption was false and the path to profitability is broken. Review this metric quarterly, with a hard decision gate at Month 6.
