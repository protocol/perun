**Recommendation: Proceed with a controlled pilot at 3 locations only; do not launch chain-wide yet.**

The current proposal contains a fatal capacity flaw that makes a full rollout dangerous. Your math assumes existing bays can absorb a **36% increase in total wash volume** (129,600 extra washes/year ≈ 355 extra washes/day across the chain) without new capital expenditure. With a hard limit of 8 washes per bay per day, you have likely already hit physical throughput ceilings during peak hours. Adding unlimited users who average 5 washes/month will create bottlenecks, increasing wait times and driving away your existing high-margin $12 single-visit customers. The $176k revenue projection is irrelevant if you lose more than that in displaced regular traffic.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Capacity Collapse & Cannibalization:** The "no new drying bays" assumption is the weakest link. If unlimited members cluster on weekends, queue times will spike. Regular customers (1.2 washes/month) value speed; if they leave due to delays, you lose high-margin revenue to subsidize low-margin unlimited users. The model fails if the net revenue (New Subscription Revenue – Lost Regular Revenue) turns negative.
2.  **Adverse Selection on Usage:** You assumed 5 washes/month, but "unlimited" plans attract the heaviest users (e.g., rideshare drivers, muddy tradespeople) who may average 8–10 washes. At $0.50 variable cost, a user washing 10 times costs you $5.00/month just in variables, leaving only $44.00 margin before fixed costs. If the average creeps to 8 washes, your margin halves. If it hits 10+, you are paying them to wash their cars.
3.  **Churn After Promotion:** If the initial 10% sign-up is driven by a "first month free" or launch hype, churn could exceed 20% in quarter two. If you hired staff or extended hours based on the 10% projection, you will be stuck with fixed labor costs for a shrinking subscriber base, turning the program into a loss leader immediately.

**Kill Criteria:**
Terminate the program immediately if, after 60 days of the pilot:
*   **Average wait time** during peak hours exceeds 15 minutes (indicating capacity breach).
*   **Average washes per subscriber** exceeds 7 (indicating adverse selection destroying margins).
*   **Net Revenue per Bay** (Total Bay Revenue / Total Bay Operating Hours) drops below the pre-pilot baseline.

**Note:** Run the 3-location pilot for 8 weeks to gather real usage data before calculating chain-wide CAPEX needs. Do not commit to the $176k revenue figure until variable costs are verified against actual heavy-user behavior.
