Your current math significantly understates the true annual cost. You calculated a flat $30,000/year by simply adding the principal to estimated maintenance, ignoring interest and omitting insurance and fuel. A corrected estimate shows a much higher burden:

*   **Insurance:** Add your estimated **$2,500/year**.
*   **Maintenance:** **$4,000/year**.
*   **Revised Total Annual Cash Outflow:** ~$43,700–$48,500 (depending on fuel efficiency), not $30,000.

**Main Failure Points:**
1.  **Financial Underestimation:** Presenting a $30k cost when the reality is nearly $45k+ erodes board trust and risks a budget shortfall.
2.  **Fuel Price Volatility:** Assuming flat fuel prices for five years is risky; a 20% spike adds ~$1,000 annually to your costs.
3.  **Resale Value Unknown:** Ignoring depreciation could mean writing off the entire $145,000 asset value if the truck has zero resale value in five years.
4.  **Capacity Utilization:** The plan relies on serving 1,200 extra meals monthly. If demand falls short, the cost-per-meal skyrockets, making the truck inefficient.
