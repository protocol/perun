**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" pilot, not a full rollout.**
Approve the installation of three lockers only if the city permit cost is confirmed under $1,500 and a dedicated labor budget is secured. Do not commit to a broader expansion until the pilot validates the 80% utilization rate without cannibalizing existing in-store pickup efficiency.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Labor Cost Erosion:** The model ignores the cost of staffing the locker area during peaks. If labor hours required exceed the margin gained from the projected 10% sales lift, the unit economics turn negative immediately.
2.  **Permit Variance:** The $0–$3,000 permit range creates a binary risk. A fee above $1,500 extends the break-even period beyond six months, violating the investment thesis.
3.  **Utilization Overestimation:** Assuming 80% utilization (200 pickups) based on a 250-order in-store baseline is aggressive. If actual adoption tracks closer to 50%, the fixed costs ($650 initial + permits) will not be covered by the incremental sales lift.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Change Triggers):**
*   **Financial Trigger:** Halt immediately if the final city permit invoice exceeds **$1,500**.
*   **Operational Trigger:** Suspend the program if, after 60 days, the average weekly labor hours spent managing lockers exceed **10 hours** without a corresponding 5% increase in total weekly order volume.
*   **Adoption Trigger:** Reevaluate the footprint if utilization remains below **40%** (80 pickups/week) by May 1, 2024.

**Leading Metric to Watch:**
Track **Net Margin per Locker Pickup** (Revenue from associated sale minus allocated labor and permit amortization) weekly. The target must remain positive by Month 2; if it trends negative for two consecutive weeks, execute the operational trigger.
