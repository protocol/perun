**Recommendation: Do not push ahead with the current financial model; revise the revenue projection immediately before presenting to the COO.**

The plan contains a critical arithmetic failure in the revenue logic. You projected $360,000 in monthly sales by multiplying total foot traffic (3,000/week) directly by average spend ($30). This assumes a **100% conversion rate**, meaning every single person walking past or entering the store buys something. In retail, even high-performing boutiques typically see conversion rates between 20% and 30%.

If we apply a realistic 25% conversion rate:
*   **Actual Buyers:** 3,000 visitors × 25% = 750 transactions/week.
*   **Realistic Revenue:** 750 × $30 × 4 weeks = **$90,000/month**.
*   **Monthly Deficit:** $90,000 (Revenue) - $45,000 (Rent) - ~$15,000 (Staff/Utilities estimate) = **Negative Cash Flow**.

At your projected $360,000 revenue, the store looks profitable. At a realistic $90,000, you cannot cover the $45,000 rent alone, let alone staff, utilities, inventory replenishment, or the $250,000 capex payback. Presenting the 100% conversion figure to the COO will destroy credibility immediately.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Illusion:** The plan relies on a physical impossibility (100% conversion). If the actual conversion is below 37.5%, you cannot even cover rent and inventory costs, ignoring labor entirely.
2.  **Hidden Labor Costs:** The budget lists Capex and launch costs but omits monthly operating expenses (OpEx) for staff. A 2,500 sq. ft. store requires at least 3–4 staff members per shift. At ~$4,000/month per employee fully loaded, this adds $15,000–$20,000/month in fixed costs, widening the loss.
3.  **Cash Burn Rate:** With a $250,000 capex and potential monthly losses of $20k–$40k (once labor is included), the store could burn through its entire startup capital in less than 6 months if the conversion rate does not exceed 40%.

**Kill/Pivot Criteria:**
Establish a **Month 3 Go/No-Go Gate**. If the store fails to achieve a **15% conversion rate** and **$110,000 in gross sales** by the end of September 2024 (Q3), trigger an immediate pivot to a lower-cost pop-up model or cease operations to preserve remaining capital. Do not wait for a "breakeven month" that may never arrive under the current cost structure.
