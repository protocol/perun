**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" pilot, not a full launch.**
Launch a limited 20-unit pilot immediately to validate the unknown insurance costs and temperature degradation rates before committing to the full Q3 2025 revenue target. Do not scale beyond the pilot until these two variables are quantified, as they directly threaten the 15% gross margin and the 4.2-year customer payback calculation.

**Three Most Likely Failure Modes:**
1.  **Margin Collapse from Hidden Costs:** The unquantified insurance premium combined with permitting delays exceeding the two-week assumption will erode the $18,000 gross profit per unit, pushing margins below the 10% viability threshold.
2.  **Payback Period Drift:** If seasonal temperature swings accelerate battery degradation beyond standard estimates, the actual energy savings will fall short of the projected 20% reduction in grid charges, extending the customer payback period beyond 4.2 years and stalling sales.
3.  **Operational Bottleneck:** Scaling to 60 upsells per quarter may strain current installation crews if battery integration adds complexity, causing permitting or installation backlogs that delay revenue recognition and increase labor costs.

**Kill/Redesign Triggers:**
*   **Kill Condition:** If the verified all-in cost per unit (including actual insurance and adjusted labor for delays) exceeds $109,200, resulting in a gross margin below 10%.
*   **Redesign Condition:** If pilot data shows battery degradation reduces customer savings by more than 15% (extending payback beyond 5 years) or if churn/cancellation rates among upsell customers exceed 12% within the first six months due to delivery delays.

*Note: The current model treats unknown insurance and degradation rates as zero-risk variables; the pilot must specifically measure these before any broader rollout.*
