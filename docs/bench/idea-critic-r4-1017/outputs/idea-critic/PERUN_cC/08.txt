**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed only with a capped pilot tranche of **$50k spend** (not the full $350k) targeting **2,500 alumni** to validate the 20% participation rate and secure the third-party vendor pricing before committing to the full scale.

**Why this could fail (Top 3 Risks):**
1.  **Arithmetic & Linearity Trap:** Your model assumes linear scaling from the 2022 Centennial campaign (25% ROI), but fundraising response rates typically decay as you expand beyond core donors. Reaching 12,500 alumni (vs. a smaller 2022 pool) with a cold "Class-Together" theme may drop participation well below 20%. If participation falls to 10%, revenue halves to $600k while costs remain fixed at ~$350k+, destroying the margin.
2.  **Unpriced & "Free" Costs:** The $100k line item for an unpriced vendor is a critical gap; if actual quotes exceed $60k, the budget breaks. Additionally, assuming social media ads are "$0" is dangerous; organic reach for alumni groups is historically near-zero without paid boost, meaning your "free" channel likely yields no gifts, invalidating the volume needed to hit $1.2M.
3.  **Matching Grant Cap Risk:** The plan hinges on a 2:1 match. If the Kestrel Foundation has a total cap (e.g., $500k total match) rather than an open-ended ratio, and you raise $1.2M in gifts, the effective match rate drops to <1:1 on the overage, drastically reducing the net revenue per donor and blowing up the ROI calculation.

Terminate the full rollout immediately if the pilot tranche (first 4 weeks) shows a **participation rate below 12%** OR if the **all-in cost per acquired donor exceeds $35** (which would make the $48 average gift unprofitable after matching adjustments).
