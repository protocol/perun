**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **capped 6-week pilot** limited to 20 volunteer tutors (not 400), with a hard spend cap of $50,000. Do not commit to the full $200,000 budget or the October 1st launch date until the pilot proves that students will cannibalize existing 1-on-1 revenue by less than 10% and that tutors can consistently fill 4 groups. The goal is to validate demand and pricing elasticity, not to hit the $320k/month projection immediately.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Cannibalization & Margin Erosion:** Your model assumes group classes are pure增量 (incremental) revenue. If existing high-paying 1-on-1 students switch to the cheaper $25/hr group option, your net revenue could drop despite high enrollment. If a tutor converts just three 1-on-1 students ($60/hr each = $180/hr) to one 8-student group ($200/hr), the gross gain is negligible while operational complexity spikes.
2.  **The "Empty Seat" Economics:** The projection relies on 100% capacity (8 students). Because revenue is linear per student but the tutor's time is fixed, falling to 4 students cuts revenue by 50% but costs the same in tutor payouts. If average fill rates are below 6 students/session, the unit economics likely turn negative relative to tutor opportunity cost.
3.  **Adoption Friction:** Assuming 70% tutor adoption in month one is aggressive. Tutors may resist the format due to increased classroom management difficulty without extra pay, or they may lack the specific skills to manage group dynamics, leading to poor student retention and refund requests.

**Hidden Risks & Costs Missed:**
*   **Technical Latency/Quality:** Group video calls require significantly higher bandwidth and lower latency than 1-on-1. If your current infrastructure isn't optimized for 8-way audio/video, quality degradation will churn customers immediately.
*   **Scheduling Complexity:** Coordinating 8 students across time zones for a specific 90-minute window is exponentially harder than matching pairs. This friction will lower conversion rates at the signup stage.
*   **Support Load:** Group dynamics introduce new support tickets (e.g., one disruptive student ruining the class for seven others) that your current support playbook may not cover.

**Pre-Agreed Halt Trigger (Kill Criterion):**
Halt the rollout immediately if, at the 4-week mark of the pilot:
*   **Net Revenue Contribution** (Group Revenue minus Cannibalized 1-on-1 Revenue minus Additional Support Costs) is **<$5,000** across the 20-tutor cohort; OR
*   **Average Fill Rate** per session is **<5 students** for two consecutive weeks.

*Note: The original $320k projection assumes zero cannibalization and perfect capacity, which is statistically improbable for a first launch. The reduced pilot scope protects the downside while testing the core hypothesis.*
