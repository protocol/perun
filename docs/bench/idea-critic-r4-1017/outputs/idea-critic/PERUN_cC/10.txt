**Recommendation: Do not launch the full package yet; run a capped, 90-day pilot with a hard spend limit of $25k.**

Your current plan relies on unverified assumptions that create a high risk of capital loss. The math for $675k ARR is arithmetically correct based on your inputs, but the inputs themselves are fragile. Specifically, assuming a 30% conversion rate from free to a $15k/year product without historical data on price elasticity is speculative. Furthermore, the assumption that current support staff can absorb a 50% ticket increase without degradation or burnout is a single point of failure; if this breaks, churn will spike immediately, invalidating the revenue model.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Conversion Collapse:** The 30% conversion target is not met because solo practitioners are price-sensitive or do not perceive $15k/year in value, resulting in revenue far below the $195k needed just to cover your stated $195k in initial costs (licensing + UI + marketing), let alone generate profit.
2.  **Support Saturation:** The 50% increase in tickets overwhelms existing staff, leading to slow response times, customer dissatisfaction, and immediate churn before the first annual renewal, destroying the Lifetime Value (LTV) calculation.
3.  **Usage Mismatch:** The assumption that lawyers will spend 3 hours/month on the tool is incorrect; if actual usage is significantly lower, the perceived value drops, making the $15k price point indefensible and increasing cancellation rates.

**Kill Criterion:**
Terminate the project if, by **Day 45 of the pilot**, you have not secured **15 paid commitments** (10% of the total addressable market, not 30% of the free tier) at the full $15k price point, or if support ticket volume exceeds a **20% increase** over baseline for two consecutive weeks.

*Note: Recalculate your unit economics using a conservative 5–10% conversion rate to see if the business model survives under realistic stress. Validate the support capacity constraint with a simulated load test before accepting any paid users.*
