**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **capped pilot** launching 1 March 2025, limited to **$650,000 total spend** (marketing + logistics) and a **hard cap of 1,000 subscribers** for the first 90 days. Do not commit to the full 8,000-subscriber scale or the $500k influencer spend until the pilot proves unit economics.

**How This Plan Could Fail:**
1.  **Revenue Arithmetic Error:** Your projection claims $2M revenue, but 8,000 subscribers × $25 × 12 months equals **$2.4M**, not $2M. More critically, at a 20% gross margin ($5/box), you generate $40k/month at scale. This cannot cover the amortization of the $650k upfront investment within a reasonable timeframe, nor does it account for the unpriced $0.08/mile fuel surcharge which will directly erode that thin 20% margin.
2.  **Churn vs. Acquisition Math:** At 5% monthly churn, you lose 400 subscribers/month at steady state. To reach 8,000, you must acquire significantly more than 400/month just to break even on retention, let alone grow. Without data on upgrade conversion from your existing 22,000 customers, the "elite" market size is an unverified assumption.
3.  **Logistics Cost Leakage:** The promised 15-minute delivery improvement has no priced value attached to retention, while the fuel surcharge is a guaranteed cost increase. If fuel costs rise or routes lengthen, the 20% margin turns negative immediately.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Pivot Triggers):**
*   **Margin Trigger:** If verified gross margin drops below **12%** after accounting for the $0.30 packaging and actual fuel surcharges in the pilot.
*   **Churn Trigger:** If monthly churn exceeds **7%** among the pilot group (indicating the "elite" value prop is not sticking).
*   **Conversion Trigger:** If fewer than **3%** of the targeted existing customers upgrade to the Performance Pack within the first 45 days.

*Note: The current math shows the project likely loses money even at full projected scale due to the low margin relative to upfront CAPEX. The pilot must validate a path to higher margins or lower acquisition costs before expanding.*
*Ensure the fuel surcharge is explicitly modeled in the pilot P&L; do not treat it as negligible.*
