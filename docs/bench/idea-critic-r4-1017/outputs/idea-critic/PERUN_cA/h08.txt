**Recommendation: Proceed with a capped, one-gym pilot only (Go-If).**
Do not roll out to all five gyms. Launch the youth tier at the downtown location for 6 months with a hard cap of $15,000 in total operational spend. Proceed to full chain rollout only if the pilot achieves a net positive cash flow by month 4 and retains >60% of youth members past month 3.

**Three most plausible failure modes:**
1.  **Unit Economics Collapse:** The fixed cost of the part-time instructor ($2,800/mo per gym) creates a break-even point of 112 youth members per location just to cover labor. If any single gym attracts fewer than 112 members, that location loses money every month regardless of total chain sign-ups.
2.  **The "Leaky Bucket" Retention:** The assumption that 30% convert to adult members is unverified. If the actual conversion is <10% (industry average for youth sports), the lifetime value of a youth member is too low to ever recoup the $15,500 upfront hardware cost across the chain.
3.  **Cannibalization without Growth:** The 400 projected "new" members are actually existing adult members downgrading their accounts to the cheaper youth tier for their children while canceling their own adult memberships, resulting in lower total revenue despite higher headcount.

**Kill Criterion:**
Terminate the initiative immediately if, at the 90-day review, the **net revenue per youth member** (after deducting the proportional share of the instructor's salary) is less than $10/month, or if the **instructor-to-member ratio** exceeds 1:40 (violating safety protocols or requiring unplanned hiring).

*Note: Your current math shows a single gym needs ~112 youth members just to pay the instructor; ensure the pilot tracks this specific break-even weekly. The $12k wall cost is a sunk risk if the labor model fails, so negotiate modular equipment before committing.*
