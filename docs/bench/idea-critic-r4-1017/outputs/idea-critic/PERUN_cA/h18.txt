**Recommendation: Do not launch company-wide yet. Run a 90-day pilot at 3 locations with a hard cap of 500 subscribers total.**

The current proposal relies on a fatal assumption: that existing infrastructure can absorb a 36% increase in total wash volume (129,600 extra washes) without capital expenditure. Your current capacity is roughly 87,600 washes/year (30 locations × 8 bays × 365 days × 1 wash/day effective utilization). The projected demand exceeds your total theoretical maximum capacity, guaranteeing severe bottlenecks, longer wait times, and customer dissatisfaction before you even realize the revenue gain. Furthermore, the revenue math assumes subscribers replace single-visit customers one-to-one; in reality, heavy users (5+ washes) will clog bays, displacing high-margin single visits and potentially lowering net profit despite higher top-line revenue.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Capacity Collapse:** The "no CapEx" assumption is mathematically impossible. At 5 washes/subscriber, the system hits 140%+ utilization during peak hours, causing queues that drive away both new subscribers and existing high-value single-visit customers.
2.  **Adverse Selection & Margin Erosion:** The plan attracts only the heaviest users (e.g., fleet drivers or enthusiasts washing 10+ times). If the average rises from 5 to 8 washes, your variable cost ($0.50/wash) eats 80% of the $49 fee, leaving no margin for fixed costs, while single-visit revenue drops due to bay congestion.
3.  **Cannibalization Without Growth:** If 10% of current customers switch but reduce their spend from $14.40/year (1.2 visits × $12) to $49, you gain revenue. However, if these users were already visiting 2–3 times, you may simply be discounting existing behavior while adding massive operational load, resulting in a net loss per square foot of bay space.

**Kill Criterion:**
Terminate the pilot immediately if **average wait time during peak hours exceeds 15 minutes** OR if **variable cost per subscriber exceeds $25 in any single month** (implying >50 washes/subscriber or inefficiency). These are leading indicators of capacity breach and margin collapse, appearing weeks before churn data would reveal the failure.
