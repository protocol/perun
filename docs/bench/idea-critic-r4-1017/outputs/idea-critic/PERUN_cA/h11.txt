**Recommendation:** Proceed with the pilot, but cap the initial launch at **one subject (Math)** and **two weekly sessions** rather than the full three-subject, twelve-session plan. This reduces the upfront operational risk and allows you to validate the technical load and instructor readiness before committing the full $120,000 budget.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Cannibalization:** High-value private students ($30/session) downgrade to group classes ($15), reducing total revenue per user despite higher seat volume.
2.  **Quality Dilution:** Instructors trained for 1-on-1 fail to manage group dynamics, leading to poor student experiences and higher churn than private tutoring.
3.  **Break-Even Miscalculation:** If actual fixed costs exceed estimates or attendance drops below 60% capacity, the unit economics may never support the platform upgrade costs.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
Terminate or pivot the program if **net revenue retention (NRR) for group-class students falls below 85%** after the first 6 weeks, or if **more than 10% of existing private clients cancel their 1-on-1 sessions to switch exclusively to group classes**.

*Note: Your break-even calculation of 800 seats appears correct based on $120k fixed costs and $150 contribution margin per 10-seat class average, but verify if "platform upgrades" are truly one-time costs. The 30% adoption assumption is aggressive; a 10% actual adoption rate would still yield positive cash flow but delay ROI significantly.*
