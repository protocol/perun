**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with the reserved-slot system only if a binding regulatory approval date is secured before committing the $2.75M capital expenditure, and the pilot is capped at a $500k spend over 8 weeks to validate the 85% load factor assumption.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **Regulatory Limbo:** The "few weeks" timeline slips to months, incurring soft costs (legal, idle project management) and delaying revenue while the $2.2M software license or development clock ticks without operational clearance.
2.  **Cannibalization vs. Incrementality:** The 30 premium slots do not represent new demand but are taken by existing customers who previously paid standard rates, destroying margin without achieving the projected $3,240/day *incremental* revenue.
3.  **Operational Friction:** The reservation system introduces bottlenecks at check-in or boarding, violating the "no turnaround time impact" assumption, causing missed tides, increased fuel costs, and customer churn that offsets premium pricing gains.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Pivot Trigger)**
Stop or pivot if **net occupancy** (premium + standard) falls below **72%** for two consecutive months post-launch, or if **average vehicle turnaround time** increases by more than **4 minutes** per vessel, indicating the system is degrading core throughput.

**Hidden Costs & Arithmetic Slips**
*   **Arithmetic Error:** Your annualized revenue calculation is incorrect. $3,240/day × 365 days = **$1,182,600**, not $1,180,800 (a minor $1,800 slip, but worth correcting for board precision).
*   **Hidden Cost – Maintenance & Support:** The budget lists implementation ($2.2M + $400k + $150k) but omits ongoing annual software maintenance, hosting, and support fees, typically 15–20% of the software cost annually (~$330k–$440k/year), which significantly impacts net present value.
*   **Hidden Risk – Load Factor Contradiction:** You state current occupancy is 78% but assume it rises to 85% *after* launching a system that reserves 25% of capacity (30/120) for a specific segment. If premium demand is weak, you may sail with empty reserved slots while turning away standard traffic, actually *lowering* total load factor unless dynamic release mechanisms are built (adding complexity/cost).
