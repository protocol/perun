**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with a capped pilot at **3 locations only** (not 12) for 90 days. Release the remaining 9 locations only if the pilot hits **40% of the enrollment target** (70 patients/store) and **zero critical POS failures** occur by Day 45.

**How This Could Fail**
1.  **Revenue Overestimation:** Your math assumes 100% of the 525 patients stay for the full month immediately. If enrollment ramps up over 30 days, Month 1 revenue will be roughly half your projection (~$31.5k chain-wide if rolled out everywhere), potentially failing to cover the $12k staff cost plus the hidden $8.5k IT risk.
2.  **Hidden IT & POS Costs:** Assuming the existing POS handles the volume without testing is a critical gap. If the subscription logic requires a backend integration ($8,500) or causes transaction latency during peak hours, the pilot could disrupt core pharmacy operations, costing far more than the subscription revenue.
3.  **Staff Capacity Bottleneck:** Budgeting $12k for "additional staff time" across 12 stores is vague. If enrollment takes 10 minutes per patient, enrolling 525 patients requires ~87 hours. If this distracts staff from filling prescriptions, wait times will rise, causing churn in the core business.
4.  **Churn Cliff:** Without a marketing budget, you rely on counter traffic. If patients sign up for one month to sync a specific prescription and then cancel (high churn), the administrative cost of processing the cancellation may exceed the $9.99 revenue.

**Pre-Agreed Stop/Pivot Triggers**
*   **Enrollment Trigger:** If total enrollments across the 3 pilot stores are **<210 patients** (70/store) by Day 45, **STOP** the rollout. Do not expand to the remaining 9 locations.
*   **Operational Trigger:** If **any** single store reports a POS slowdown >2 minutes per transaction attributed to the new subscription logic, **PAUSE** immediately for IT review before continuing.
*   **Financial Trigger:** If net revenue (after staff costs) in the pilot group is **<$1,500/store** by Day 60, **PIVOT** to a higher price point or reduce service scope before considering further expansion.

*Note: The "go-if" structure protects the chain from the unverified IT integration cost and inflated revenue assumptions while validating the churn hypothesis on a smaller scale.*
*Ensure the $8,500 IT contingency is formally reserved before Day 1, even if not spent.*
