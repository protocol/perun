Approve the licensing ($150k) and inventory for only **80 installs** (approx. $160k), hiring **one** lead installer initially. Do not commit to the full $500k inventory or three hires until the first 20 jobs are completed. The current plan assumes a 47% gross margin in Texas ($8M revenue vs. ~$4.2M projected direct costs), which is dangerously higher than your proven 40% in Arizona, despite admitting Texas labor rates are higher and permitting is slower. Your calculation uses a lower material cost ($2.0k vs $2.5k) but fails to quantify the labor delta or the cash-flow drag of permitting delays. If you hire three people and buy full inventory before validating the speed of installation, fixed costs will consume cash before revenue lands.

**Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Margin Compression:** If Texas labor costs exceed the $0.5k/kW material savings, your gross margin drops below the Arizona baseline. With $255k in new fixed salaries, a 5% margin slip turns a projected $3.8M operating profit into a loss.
2.  **Cash Flow Starvation:** If permitting takes 6–8 weeks per county (vs. your implicit assumption of speed), you cannot bill upon completion. You will burn cash on salaries and inventory carrying costs while waiting to invoice, potentially running out of liquidity before the 250th install.
3.  **Policy Cliff:** Relying on the 30% incentive for three years is a single point of failure. If legislation changes in Year 2, demand could collapse while you are still locked into leases and inventory purchases.

**Kill Criteria (Stop/Rethink Trigger):**
Stop the expansion immediately if, after the first **20 completed installs**, the **net margin per job is below 15%** (after accounting for actual Texas labor and permitting delays) OR if the **average time from contract to installation exceeds 75 days**. These metrics must be reviewed 90 days from launch. If either is breached, freeze hiring and liquidate remaining Texas-specific inventory.
