**Recommendation: Proceed with a capped, conditional pilot ("Go-If").**
Commit only the first $40,000 (design and core build) to achieve a private beta by 1 Oct 2025. Do not commit the full $120,000 or launch the public pilot until the state health board provides written confirmation of the certification timeline. The revenue model is viable only if regulatory approval arrives before the pilot burns 50% of the budget; without this, the project risks becoming a compliant but uncertifiable asset.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Regulatory Limbo:** The state board takes >6 months to define certification requirements. You spend the full budget building features that may need costly re-architecture to meet compliance, delaying revenue indefinitely.
2.  **Adherence-Revenue Decoupling:** The assumed 70% adherence rate does not correlate to retention. Patients may use the app intensely for 3 weeks post-injury then churn, failing to sustain the $30/month subscription needed to hit the $15,000 monthly revenue target.
3.  **Clinic Cannibalization:** A 10% reduction in in-clinic visits reduces high-margin revenue (hands-on therapy) more than the app subscription replaces it, causing net revenue loss for the practice despite user growth.

**Pre-Agreed Kill-Switch Condition:**
If certified regulatory guidelines are not published by **15 August 2025**, or if **fewer than 150 patients** (30% of the 500 target) are actively subscribed and paying by **1 January 2026**, terminate the project immediately. Do not spend the remaining budget.

**Hidden Costs & Risks:**
*   **Liability Insurance:** Standard malpractice policies often exclude uncertified digital therapeutics; expect a 15–20% premium increase or a separate rider cost not in the $120k.
*   **Integration Debt:** The plan assumes standalone usage; connecting to existing Electronic Health Records (EHR) for seamless prescription will likely require custom API work, adding ~$25,000 in unplanned engineering costs.

**Note:** The $15,000 monthly revenue projection assumes 100% of the 500 patients pay simultaneously; realistic ramp-up suggests breaking even will take 6–8 months longer than modeled.
**Note:** Verify if the $30 price point exceeds patient out-of-pocket caps for non-certified tools in your jurisdiction, which could artificially suppress sign-ups.
