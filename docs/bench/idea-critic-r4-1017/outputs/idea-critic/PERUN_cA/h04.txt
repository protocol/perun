**Recommendation: Proceed with a capped pilot (Go-If)**
Approve the installation of three lockers only if the total upfront exposure (hardware, installation, and maximum estimated permit fee) remains under **$5,000**. Do not commit to a full rollout or marketing push until this pilot validates that the "free" locker advantage actually converts browser traffic into sales without eroding margin via unstaffed labor costs.

**Primary Failure Modes**
1.  **The Permit Trap:** The city assesses the maximum $3,000 fee, increasing the break-even period from six months to over nine, destroying the ROI before operations begin.
2.  **Labor Leakage:** The model assumes 80% utilization based on in-store rates but ignores the specific labor cost of staffing the locker zone during peaks. If even 15% of projected pickups require manual intervention or restocking during rush hours, the $0 fee becomes a loss leader rather than a sales driver.
3.  **False Equivalence:** Comparing a same-day locker to a competitor's two-day shipping is a category error. Customers needing items immediately may not wait two days regardless of price; the true competitor is likely your own existing in-store pickup, meaning this may just cannibalize existing high-margin foot traffic rather than generating the projected 10% new sales lift.

**Kill Criterion (Stop Condition)**
Halt the rollout immediately if, by **April 15, 2024** (6 weeks post-launch), the **net margin per locker pickup** (sales price minus COGS minus allocated labor cost) is less than **50% of the net margin of a standard in-store pickup**. This metric proves whether the operational friction of the lockers is consuming the value of the "free" convenience. If the permit cost exceeds **$1,500**, pause the project entirely to renegotiate site placement or terms before spending further.

*Note: The 10% sales lift assumption is highly aggressive for a convenience feature; ensure the pilot tracks "new customer acquisition" separately from "channel shift" to verify actual growth.*
