**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **capped 6-week pilot** limited to 40 tutors (10% of base) starting October 1, 2024, with a strict **go/no-go decision** on November 15 based on net revenue contribution, rather than the originally proposed 3-month, 400-tutor rollout.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Cannibalization Risk:** The 70% adoption assumption ignores that students may switch from higher-margin 1-on-1 sessions ($40–$60/hr) to the $25 group rate, reducing overall revenue per tutor despite higher volume.
2.  **Operational Collapse:** Tutors lack training in group dynamics, leading to poor student experiences, churn, and brand damage if class sizes hit 8 without adequate engagement tools.
3.  **False Revenue Math:** The $800/week projection assumes 100% fill rates (32 students/tutor/week); if average attendance drops to 4 students due to scheduling friction or lack of demand, revenue halves while fixed development costs remain.

**Halt Trigger (Kill Criterion):** If the pilot's **net revenue contribution** (group revenue minus cannibalized 1-on-1 revenue minus incremental support costs) falls below **$150 per tutor per week** by Week 4, halt the rollout immediately.

**Hidden Risks Missed:**
*   **Platform Stability:** Live video for 8 students × 40 tutors concurrently may exceed current bandwidth/server limits, causing outages during peak hours.
*   **Refund Liability:** Group classes have higher no-show and dissatisfaction rates; without a clear refund policy, chargebacks could erase thin margins.
*   **Tutor Burnout:** Managing 8 students simultaneously is cognitively heavier than 1-on-1; without higher pay per hour for tutors, adoption will stall regardless of targets.

The original 70% adoption target within one month is unsupported by historical feature uptake data and should be treated as a maximum stretch goal, not a baseline for financial modeling.
