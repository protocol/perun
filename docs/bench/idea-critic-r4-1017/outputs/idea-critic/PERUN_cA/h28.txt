**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **capped pilot of 10 installations** (not the full 60) before committing to the Q3 2025 rollout. Do not launch broadly until the unknown insurance premiums and temperature-dependent degradation rates are verified with actual data, as these two variables alone could erase the projected 15% gross margin.

**Three Most Likely Failure Modes:**
1.  **Margin Collapse from Hidden Costs:** The model assumes a hard $102,000 hardware cost but explicitly admits ignorance on insurance premiums. If specialized battery insurance adds even $3,000 per unit annually, or if permitting delays exceed the assumed two-week window (incurring labor carrying costs), the 15% gross margin will likely drop below the break-even point.
2.  **Degradation-Driven Churn:** The 4.2-year payback relies on a stable 20% reduction in grid charges. If seasonal temperature swings accelerate battery degradation faster than vendor specs predict, performance will drop, the customer's savings will vanish, and churn among upsell customers will spike, destroying the lifetime value calculation.
3.  **Permitting Bottlenecks:** The plan assumes a uniform two-week permitting cycle. If local jurisdictions treat battery storage differently than standard solar—requiring new fire inspections or structural reviews—the timeline could stretch to months, stalling revenue recognition and ballooning installation labor costs.

**Kill/Redesign Triggers:**
*   **Margin Trigger:** Kill or redesign if the **actual realized gross margin falls below 10%** after accounting for final insurance premiums and true permitting labor costs on the first 10 units.
*   **Performance Trigger:** Halt expansion if **average battery efficiency degradation exceeds 5% within the first six months** of operation during peak seasonal temperature swings.
*   **Adoption Trigger:** Stop the program if **churn or cancellation requests from upsell customers exceed 10%** in the first six months post-installation.

*Note: The current model treats unknown variables as zero-cost; the pilot must specifically measure insurance and thermal performance before scaling.*
*Do not sign long-term hardware purchase orders until the pilot confirms the $102,000 cost basis holds under real-world permitting conditions.*
