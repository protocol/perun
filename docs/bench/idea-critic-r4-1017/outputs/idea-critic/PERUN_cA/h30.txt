**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **restricted pilot only** (no full July 15 launch), capped at $50k development spend and 6 weeks duration. Do not commit to the full $150k budget or national rollout until the pilot proves families do not cannibalize individual subscriptions.

**How This Plan Could Fail:**
1.  **Cannibalization Over Growth:** The math assumes 2,400 *new* paying units, but if existing individual users (paying ~$10.83/mo inferred from $130k/12k/12mo) switch to the $4.99 family plan, revenue per user drops by >50%. You could gain 2,400 families while losing $60k in annual recurring revenue from broken individual accounts.
2.  **Support Cost Explosion:** The plan explicitly omits pricing for support load. Family plans typically generate 3x more support tickets (billing disputes, child account management, password resets) than individual plans. At $25/ticket, just 4 tickets/family/year erases 16% of your projected profit.
3.  **Flawed Conversion Math:** The $150k projection assumes 100% of the $4.99 fee is pure profit from new users. If only 50% of the 2,400 families are *new* money and the rest are downgrades, your actual net revenue gain is near zero, making the $175k total investment (dev + marketing) unrecoverable in year one.

**Kill Criterion:**
Stop or pivot if the pilot shows **<8% net revenue lift** (not just conversion count) after 6 weeks, or if **>30% of family plan sign-ups** come from existing individual subscribers who cancel their higher-tier accounts.

*Note: Your current revenue math implies individual users pay ~$10.83/month; switching them to $4.99 requires nearly double the user count just to break even on those specific accounts.*
*Note: The "20% increase" claim is invalid unless you prove these 2,400 families are entirely incremental users, not consolidated individual accounts.*
