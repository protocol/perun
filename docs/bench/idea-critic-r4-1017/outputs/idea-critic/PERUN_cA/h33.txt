**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" Pilot, Not a Full Launch.**
Do not commit the full $250,000 upfront. Instead, authorize a capped pilot tranche of $50,000 to validate demand before leasing the satellite kitchen or buying the mobile unit. The plan fails if it assumes 12% food costs and 20% operational load capacity without proof; catering logistics differ fundamentally from restaurant service. A "Go-If" decision requires securing 40 committed corporate events (approx. $80,000 revenue) via letters of intent before releasing funds for the satellite lease. This reduces exposure while testing the core unknown: local demand.

**Three Most Likely Failure Modes:**
1.  **Operational Collapse:** The existing kitchen cannot absorb a 20% load during peak dinner hours without degrading restaurant quality or causing staff burnout, leading to churn and reputational damage in the core business.
2.  **Margin Erosion:** Catering food costs exceed 12% due to bulk waste, transport spoilage, and distinct packaging needs, while labor costs spike because event staffing is less efficient than stationary service, turning the projected profit into a loss.
3.  **Demand Overestimation:** The market supports fewer than 80 events annually rather than 150, leaving the expensive mobile unit and satellite kitchen underutilized and unable to cover their fixed costs.

**Kill Criterion:**
Stop or pivot immediately if fewer than **40 confirmed corporate events** are secured within the first **six months** of operation. This threshold represents less than 50% of the projected annual volume required to break even; failing to hit this pace by month six indicates the total addressable market is insufficient to support the $250,000 investment structure.

*Note: The current plan treats unverified assumptions about capacity and margins as facts; the pilot must measure these specifically.*
*Ensure any "committed events" used to trigger the next funding tranche require non-refundable deposits to count as valid evidence.*
