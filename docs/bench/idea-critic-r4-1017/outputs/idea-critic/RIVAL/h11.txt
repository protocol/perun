**Recommendation: Proceed with the pilot, but cap initial marketing spend at 40% of the budget until conversion data is validated.**

The unit economics are compelling ($15 vs. $30 price point with significantly higher tutor utilization), and the break-even point of 800 seats is only 21% of your projected volume, providing a healthy safety margin. However, your revenue projection relies entirely on the unproven assumption that 30% of private students will convert to group settings. By proceeding but throttling marketing spend, you validate demand without risking the full $120,000 before proving the churn model.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Cannibalization without Net Growth:** Existing private students downgrade to the $15 option rather than new users filling seats, reducing total revenue per tutor despite higher utilization.
2.  **Quality Dilution:** Tutors accustomed to 1:1 dynamics fail to manage group engagement, leading to poor student outcomes and brand damage that affects the core private business.
3.  **Technical Friction:** The platform cannot handle synchronous multi-user video or scheduling logic for 15-person cohorts, causing session drop-offs and support overload.

**Stop/Pivot Triggers:**
*   **Stop Condition:** If **net revenue per active tutor** drops by more than 10% compared to the pre-pilot baseline after Month 1, indicating severe cannibalization.
*   **Pivot Condition:** If **student retention** (students booking a second session) is below 40% after the first month, pivot from open enrollment to curated "cohorts" with fixed curricula to increase stickiness.
*   **Scale Condition:** Only unlock the remaining 60% of the marketing budget if **conversion from private to group** exceeds 15% *and** **new student acquisition** (users who were not previously paying) represents at least 50% of group seat fill.
