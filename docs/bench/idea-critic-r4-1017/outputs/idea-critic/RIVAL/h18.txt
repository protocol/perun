**Recommendation: Do not launch "Brightfin Unlimited" in its current form.**

The proposal contains a critical arithmetic error and a dangerous capacity assumption that threaten profitability. Your revenue projection of $176,400 assumes 1,200 subscribers (10% of 12,000), but your wash volume calculation incorrectly applies that 10% to the total annual transaction count rather than the subscriber base. The real math: 1,200 subscribers × 5 washes/month = 6,000 washes/month. Across 30 locations, that is 200 extra washes per location monthly, or ~6.7 extra washes per day.

While 6.7 washes sounds manageable against an 8-wash-per-bay capacity, you have assumed **zero capital expense** for drying. Unlimited users will cluster visits (e.g., rainy days, weekends), creating peak-hour bottlenecks your current drying bays cannot handle. If a subscriber waits 15 minutes to dry because bays are full, the "unlimited" value proposition collapses, driving immediate churn. Furthermore, at $0.50 variable cost, your margin per subscriber is $46.50/month. If heavy users average 8 washes instead of 5, your cost jumps to $4.00, slashing margin by 16%. One bad month of heavy usage wipes out the projected annual profit buffer.

**The Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **The "Peak-Load" Capacity Trap:** Your model assumes linear distribution of washes. In reality, unlimited users wash frequently after rain or on weekends. If 20% of your subscribers show up on a Saturday morning, your drying bays will gridlock. Without adding drying lanes (CapEx), service times spike, customer satisfaction plummets, and the brand promise breaks.
2.  **Adverse Selection (The "Whale" Problem):** You are pricing for the average user (5 washes), but the product attracts the extremes. If just 15% of subscribers are "whales" washing 12+ times a month, your blended variable cost rises sharply. At 12 washes, your cost is $6.00, leaving only $43 margin. If acquisition costs are high, these users are net negative from Day 1.
3.  **The Revenue Cannibalization Blind Spot:** You calculated revenue based on *new* volume, ignoring that existing high-frequency customers (currently paying $12 × 4 = $48) will downgrade to the $49 plan but wash significantly more. If 200 existing heavy users switch and increase frequency to 8 washes, you gain $200 in revenue but incur $7,200 in *extra* variable costs annually, actually reducing total profit.

**Kill Switch Metric:**

Terminate the program immediately if **the ratio of variable costs to subscription revenue exceeds 15% in any single month** (roughly 7.5 washes per subscriber average). This accounts for both heavy usage and peak-time inefficiencies before they erode the entire profit margin.
