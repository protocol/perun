Approve the immediate expansion of the teledentistry follow-up pilot to the full 350-patient cohort. The corrected budget of $125,000 ($100k platform, $10k training, $15k marketing) is sufficient given the licensing cap of 5,000 sessions far exceeds our projected volume. The jump to 8% conversion post-July 1 (28 sessions) remains well within capacity. However, do not commit the full marketing spend until IT confirms the bandwidth cost per session to prevent margin erosion on the variable infrastructure side.

**Top Three Failure Modes**

1.  **Hidden Infrastructure Overage Costs:** The current budget assumes fixed IT costs. If high-definition video streams exceed the corporate bandwidth cap, variable overage fees could spike unexpectedly, turning a low-volume pilot into a high-cost liability without clinical revenue to offset it.
2.  **Clinical-Legal Liability Gap:** A 15-minute video window is insufficient for diagnosing pediatric dental issues without tactile examination. A missed diagnosis (e.g., early abscess or enamel defect) during a brief video call could lead to malpractice claims that exceed the pilot's entire budget, especially if consent forms do not explicitly limit the scope of video-only advice.
3.  **Low Parental Engagement (Sunk Marketing Cost):** The 5–8% conversion assumption relies on busy parents prioritizing a $45 optional add-on. If uptake stays below 3% despite the $15k marketing push, the cost per acquired engagement will exceed $500, rendering the marketing spend inefficient and signaling a product-market fit issue for paid virtual follow-ups in this demographic.

**Kill Trigger**

Terminate the program immediately if, after 90 days or 100 offered sessions:
*   **Net Loss > $15,000** (excluding sunk training costs) **OR**
*   **Parent Satisfaction Score < 3.5/5** specifically regarding "resolved concern" (indicating the video format is clinically ineffective).
