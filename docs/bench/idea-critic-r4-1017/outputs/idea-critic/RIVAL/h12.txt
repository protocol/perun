**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Soft Launch" pilot, not the full rollout.**
Do not commit the full $200k loan or 12,000-case volume immediately. Your unit economics are dangerously thin, and the revenue error in your deck signals a lack of financial rigor that will alarm Maya. Run a 2,000-case pilot to validate the West Coast freight costs and the competitor's price elasticity before scaling.

**The Three Biggest Ways This Could Fail:**

1.  **Negative Contribution Margin:** At $1.80 revenue per case, you have almost no room for error. If your production cost exceeds $1.40 and freight to the West Coast (30% of volume) exceeds $0.30, you are losing money on every case sold. The competitor's $2.50 price point suggests the market supports higher pricing; your $1.80 target may signal "low quality" rather than "value," or simply leave no profit buffer.
2.  **The Distribution Bottleneck:** You assume 70% of volume moves through existing channels without extra fees. If the distributor charges storage or handling fees for this new SKU, or if they prioritize their own higher-margin brands, your effective revenue per case drops further. If they cannot absorb the volume, you face emergency logistics costs that wipe out margins.
3.  **Cash Flow Suffocation:** With $350k cash and a $200k loan, you have ~$550k total. The loan payment is ~$6,100/month. If sales ramp slowly or freight costs are higher than expected, you could burn through cash reserves within 6–8 months. A single month of unsold inventory (12,000 cases sitting in a warehouse) ties up roughly $21,600 in potential revenue plus storage costs, creating a liquidity crunch.

If **Month 2 net revenue per case falls below $1.50** (after accounting for all freight, fees, and discounts), **kill the project immediately.**
