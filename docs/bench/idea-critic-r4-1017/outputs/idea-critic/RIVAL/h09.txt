**Recommendation: Do not launch chain-wide on July 1. Approve a single-store pilot first.**

Your current plan risks a net loss in the pilot's first 90 days due to two unbudgeted realities: the likely $8,500 IT integration cost and the omission of marketing spend required to hit your 15% enrollment target. By restricting the initial launch to one location, you cap your exposure to ~$1,500 in integration costs while validating whether the $9.99 price point actually drives retention before scaling to all 12 stores.

**Primary Failure Modes & Hidden Risks**

*   **The "Free Rider" Margin Crush:** If your POS cannot automatically distinguish subscription refills from standard copays, staff will manually waive fees for eligible patients who haven't formally enrolled. At 3,500 monthly scripts, even a 5% error rate destroys profitability.
*   **Integration Debt:** Assuming existing POS handles this without upgrades is your highest technical risk. If the $8,500 integration is required *after* launch, you will face emergency downtime or double-entry workarounds that spike labor costs beyond your $12,000 budget.
*   **Marketing Void:** You have no budget to acquire the 525 patients. Organic uptake is rarely sufficient for new fee-based services; without a $2,000–$3,000 targeted push (SMS, in-store signage), enrollment will likely miss the 15% target by half, extending the break-even point indefinitely.
*   **Regulatory Friction:** In pharmacy, bundling services for a fee can sometimes trigger insurance auditing or compliance questions regarding "inducements." A chain-wide rollout amplifies this regulatory surface area before legal has reviewed the specific $9.99 structure.

**Pre-Agreed Stop/Pivot Triggers**

Commit to these metrics before day one. If any are breached, halt expansion immediately:

1.  **Enrollment Shortfall:** If Store #1 fails to enroll **40 patients** (approx. 8% of volume, not 15%) by Day 45, **STOP** chain-wide rollout. Re-evaluate pricing or value proposition.
2.  **Revenue/Churn Breach:** If net revenue (after labor) is **<$7,000** in Month 2, or if churn exceeds **20%** before Day 60, **PIVOT**. The unit economics do not support the labor overhead.
3.  **Labor Overflow:** If staff spends **>15 hours/week** on manual coordination (vs. the budgeted ~10), **PAUSE**. This indicates the POS integration is insufficient and requires the $8,500 fix before proceeding.
