**Recommendation: Proceed with the pilot, but freeze the full $650k rollout budget.**
The unit economics for existing customers are strong ($2.4M revenue vs. $650k cost over 18 months), but the "new customer" growth assumption is currently a guess. Use the 90-day pilot to validate the no-show reduction technically and, more importantly, to generate a case study that proves market pull before committing to the full marketing spend.

**Main Failure Modes**
1.  **Integration Friction:** The 30% no-show drop relies on seamless calendar integration. If the setup takes clinic staff >15 minutes, adoption will stall regardless of ROI.
2.  **Revenue Leakage:** A 30% drop in no-shows increases provider utilization. If your current scheduling logic doesn't account for buffer times or provider burnout, service quality may drop, causing churn among your *best* patients.
3.  **The "New Logo" Mirage:** You are banking on new sign-ups to justify the $200k marketing spend. If the feature is seen as a "nice-to-have" rather than a deal-closer by new prospects, your CAC will skyrocket, destroying the 18-month projection.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Trigger**
**Stop the full rollout if, by Day 60 of the pilot:**
*   **Adoption < 40%** within the three pilot clinics (indicates UX friction), OR
*   **Net Revenue Lift < $40,000/month** across the three clinics combined (indicates the no-show math doesn't translate to cash).

**Hidden Risks Not Covered**
*   **Cannibalization:** Existing customers might demand this module be included in their base subscription rather than paying extra, eroding your $66k/month projection.
*   **Support Load Spike:** A new module often doubles support ticket volume initially. If your support team is already at capacity, response times for *all* products could slip, threatening the core business.
*   **Data Privacy Liability:** Accessing real-time patient scheduling data increases your security surface area. A breach here is more damaging than a standard login leak.
