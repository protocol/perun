**Recommendation: Do not launch the Family Plan as currently structured.**

The proposal contains a critical mathematical error and a dangerous financial asymmetry. You are projecting $144,000 in annual revenue (2,400 families × $4.99 × 12 months), not $150,000. More importantly, you are budgeting $175,000 in upfront costs (development + marketing) against a first-year return of $144,000. Even if you hit 100% of your adoption targets, the product loses money in year one before accounting for the unpriced support load or payment processing fees. You are effectively paying users to test a feature with undefined churn characteristics.

**Primary Failure Modes:**

1.  **Cannibalization Without Net Growth:** If existing individual subscribers ($10–$15/month estimated) downgrade to the $4.99 family plan and simply add low-usage children, your Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) will collapse. You could gain 2,400 families but lose total revenue if only 15% of existing users downgrade.
2.  **Support Cost Overrun:** Parents with children under 12 generate disproportionately high support tickets (account management, content filtering, password resets). Without a specific budget for this, the $25,000 marketing allocation will be consumed by operational drag, turning a margin product into a loss leader.
3.  **The "Free Rider" Churn:** If one family member stops using the app, the primary payer may not notice immediately, but once they do, they cancel the entire account for four users at once. Family plans often have higher "all-or-nothing" churn volatility than individual accounts.

**Pivot/Stop Metric:**

**Stop or Pivot if: Pilot Conversion < 8% OR Net Revenue Impact < 0.**

Do not wait for the full 20% target. If the two-region pilot does not convert at least **8% of eligible existing users** (not just total base) within 45 days, the value proposition is too weak to justify the $150k dev spend. Furthermore, if the **Net Revenue Impact** (New Family Revenue minus Lost Individual Subscription Revenue) is negative after the first 60 days, immediately halt the rollout. You cannot afford to buy market share with discounted tiers when the development cost exceeds the first year's gross return.
