**Recommendation: Proceed with a conditional pilot launch, but immediately revise the pricing model to include a storage cap.**

Do not launch with the current "pay-per-page" structure against Competitor X's unlimited storage. Solo lawyers are highly sensitive to unpredictable costs; a variable fee creates anxiety that will suppress conversion regardless of time savings. Instead, bundle a generous allowance (e.g., 5,000 pages/month) into the $29 fee and charge overage rates only for extreme outliers. This aligns your value proposition (time saved) with predictable billing.

**Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Price Perception Friction:** Lawyers may perceive the variable cost as a "tax on work," causing them to stick with the cheaper, predictable Competitor X despite your superior efficiency.
2.  **Churn Underestimation:** If the 5-hour time-saving claim relies on complex contracts that only appear quarterly, users will cancel during months with low volume, seeing no recurring value.
3.  **Unit Economics Collapse:** If the average user exceeds 2,000 pages/month, your storage costs will erode the margin on the $29 subscription faster than anticipated, turning new customers into liabilities.

**Abort/Pivot Triggers:**
Execute an immediate strategic pivot if **either** of the following occurs within the first six months:
*   **Churn Rate > 12%:** This indicates the product is failing to deliver sticky, recurring value or the pricing model is driving users away.
*   **Monthly Burn > $22,000:** This signals that customer acquisition costs or infrastructure usage (page storage) are scaling unsustainably relative to revenue.
