**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **conditional go**, but restructure the pilot to de-risk the regulatory uncertainty before committing the full $120k. Do not build the full "remote prescription" feature set yet. Instead, launch a "compliance-lite" MVP focused purely on adherence tracking and patient engagement, which requires no state board certification. Use the first $40k and 3 months to validate the 500-patient signup assumption and the 70% adherence rate. If those metrics hold, *then* trigger the remaining budget for the regulatory certification track. This prevents burning cash on a feature (remote prescription) that might be blocked by the board indefinitely.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Regulatory Limbo:** The state health board takes 18+ months to certify remote prescriptions, rendering the core value proposition (cutting clinic visits via prescribed remote exercise) illegal to market as a medical tool, forcing a pivot to a generic wellness app with lower pricing power.
2.  **Adherence Illusion:** The 70% adherence rate is a fantasy. Industry standard for home exercise apps is often <20% after 30 days. If patients don't do the exercises, they won't see results, won't value the $30/mo subscription, and churn will spike well above 5%, destroying the unit economics.
3.  **Clinic Cannibalization Resistance:** Your own physiotherapists may resist an app that cuts their billable visits by 10%. If the staff isn't incentivized to prescribe the app, patient signup will stall regardless of patient demand.

**Pre-agreed Kill-Switch:**
Terminate the pilot and wind down development if, by **1 January 2026** (3 months post-launch), you have not secured **either** (a) at least 250 active paying subscribers (50% of target) **or** (b) a written preliminary approval timeline from the state health board.

**Hidden Risks & Costs:**
*   **Liability Insurance:** You likely need a specialized rider on your malpractice insurance for "digital therapeutics," which could add $15k–$25k annually, unaccounted for in the $120k.
*   **Integration Debt:** The plan assumes patients just "use the app." If you need to integrate with existing Electronic Health Records (EHR) so therapists can see the data, that integration alone could consume 40% of your dev budget, not just design and testing.
*   **Support Load:** A 70% adherence goal implies high touch. Expect a hidden operational cost of 1 FTE (Full Time Employee) for customer success/support to manually nag patients, costing ~$60k/year, effectively doubling your burn rate if not automated perfectly.
