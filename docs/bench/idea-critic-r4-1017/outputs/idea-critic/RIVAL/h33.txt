**Recommendation: Proceed, but only with a conditional, phased launch.**
Do not commit the full $205,000 in fixed assets (prep unit + lease) immediately. Instead, validate the demand hypothesis by running a 60-day "ghost catering" pilot using the existing kitchen during off-hours and rented equipment for delivery. If you secure 40 paid bookings in that window, greenlight the full build-out. If not, you have saved $185,000 in sunk costs. The current plan bets the company's liquidity on an unproven demand curve; de-risk the capital expenditure before locking it in.

**Three Most Likely Failure Modes:**
1.  **Operational Collapse via Capacity Saturation:** The assumption that the existing kitchen can absorb a 20% load without new staff is fragile. Catering peaks (lunch rushes, large weekend events) often clash with restaurant primes. One major service failure due to slowed restaurant tickets will damage the core brand's reputation faster than the catering arm can build its own.
2.  **Margin Erosion from Logistics:** The 12% food-cost margin ignores the "hidden" costs of off-site service: transport fuel, vehicle maintenance, staffing for setup/breakdown, and compostable packaging required for off-premise events. These variable costs often eat 5–8% of revenue, turning a projected profit into a break-even or loss scenario at 150 events.
3.  **Sales Cycle Mismatch:** Corporate procurement moves slowly. Securing 150 events in year one requires a sales pipeline that likely needs 6–9 months to mature. If the sales cycle averages 4 months, you will face a "revenue desert" for the first two quarters while lease and loan payments are already due, creating an immediate cash-flow crisis.

**Specific Pivot Trigger:**
**Stop or pivot if fewer than 45 confirmed, paid corporate events are secured in the first six months.**
At this volume, the annualized run rate is only 90 events ($180,000 revenue), which fails to cover the $250,000 cost basis and fixed overheads. Hitting this threshold by Month 6 indicates the market density or price point is insufficient to support the dedicated infrastructure, necessitating an immediate halt to the lease/unit investment and a shift to a low-overhead partnership model.
