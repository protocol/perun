**Three Most Likely Failure Points:**
1.  **Margin Collapse from Hidden Costs:** Your $102k hardware cost likely excludes the unknown insurance premium and potential thermal management upgrades. If insurance adds just $4k per unit and heat degradation forces an extra $2k in cooling hardware, your margin drops from 15% to 4.6%, making the venture operationally breakeven at best.
2.  **Permitting Bottlenecks:** The model assumes a hard 2-week permitting cap. If even 30% of sites hit a 6-week municipal backlog (common in energy upgrades), your installation velocity drops, carrying costs rise, and the "30% attach rate" assumption fails because customers abandon the upsell due to delays.
3.  **Flawed Payback Economics:** The 4.2-year payback relies on a static 20% grid charge reduction. If utility rate structures shift to time-of-use pricing that doesn't favor your specific battery discharge profile, or if actual degradation exceeds 2% annually due to temperature swings, the customer ROI stretches to 6+ years, killing demand.

**Kill/Redesign Triggers:**
*   **Kill the program** if the pilot reveals a **gross margin below 10%** after accounting for actual insurance premiums and thermal mitigation costs. At that level, the risk-adjusted return does not justify the capital tie-up.
*   **Redesign the program** if **churn among signed upsell customers exceeds 12% in the first six months** (specifically due to permitting delays or revised ROI calculations). This indicates the value proposition or operational timeline is misaligned with customer reality, requiring a pivot to a lease model or a revised service contract structure.
