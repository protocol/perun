**Recommendation: Do not approve the build in its current form.**

The financial model is dangerously optimistic and hides a fatal flaw in unit economics. At $800,000 monthly gross revenue with a 15% commission, your *net* revenue is actually $120,000, not $800,000. If you treat $800k as net, you are misrepresenting the top line by 85%. Even assuming $120k net revenue, your monthly burn ($30k ops + amortized dev costs) leaves a razor-thin margin that cannot absorb the 12% IRR target given the $535k upfront capex. The competitor's off-the-shelf tool, even with unknown subscription fees, likely offers a superior risk-adjusted return since it avoids the $450k development sinkhole. Pivot to a "buy then build" strategy: license the competitor's tool for 12 months to validate load volume assumptions before committing to proprietary development.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Unit Economics Collapse:** The model conflates gross load value with net commission revenue. If the $800k figure is gross load value, your actual revenue is $120k/month. Against $30k/month ops + $30k/month dev amortization, you have zero cushion for carrier API failures, data feed cost spikes ($25k vs $10k), or load volume dips below 3,200. One 15% volume drop pushes you into negative cash flow.

2.  **Integration Debt Underestimation:** The $85k integration budget for three carrier APIs is likely 60-70% too low. Carrier APIs are notoriously unstable, poorly documented, and require constant maintenance. Real-world integration often reveals hidden costs in error handling, rate limiting, and schema changes that can double the budget and extend the timeline past the 18-month break-even point.

3.  **Data Feed Dependency Risk:** The $10k-$25k licensing variance for the national carrier data feed is a 150% swing on an unpriced critical dependency. If the provider raises fees post-integration or changes terms, you have no leverage. Worse, if the feed experiences downtime, your entire matching engine becomes useless, creating a single point of failure that the model ignores.

**Kill Trigger:**

Terminate the project immediately if, by Month 9 (midway through development), the validated carrier API integration count is fewer than 2 out of 3, OR if the actual monthly load volume (from any pilot testing) falls below 2,400 loads (75% of target) for two consecutive months. These metrics prove the technical feasibility and market demand assumptions before you burn the remaining $250k+ of development budget.
