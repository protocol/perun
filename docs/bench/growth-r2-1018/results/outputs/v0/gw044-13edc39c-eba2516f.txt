**North Star Metric: Weekly Active Users Completing a 5-Day Streak**

For FitQuest's pilot stage, your single definitive metric must be the count of unique users who complete a **5-day workout streak within a rolling 7-day window**.

This specific action matters more than Daily Active Users (DAU), weekly challenges, or social shares because it is the only metric that directly proxies the **habit formation** you identified as your value driver. Your own data shows that hitting five days predicts return; therefore, the product's success is not defined by how many people open the app (DAU) or how many times they click "share" (vanity), but by how many successfully cross that specific threshold of consistency.

Here is why the alternatives fail as your North Star:
*   **Daily Active Users (DAU):** This is a vanity metric in this context. A user can open the app daily to check leaderboards without exercising. DAU can rise while actual health outcomes and retention flatline. It measures attention, not value.
*   **Weekly Completed Challenges:** This is too broad. If a challenge requires only one workout, a user can "complete" it without forming the 5-day habit that drives your retention. It dilutes the specific behavior you need to reinforce.
*   **Social Shares Generated:** This measures advocacy, not core value. Users may share without working out, or work out without sharing. Optimizing for shares might lead you to design pop-ups that annoy users and disrupt the workout flow, hurting the very retention you seek.

**Action for the Product Design Team (Home Screen Redesign):**
The home screen must not prioritize "total workouts" or "friends online." Instead, every primary UI element should drive the user toward **Day 5 of their current streak**.
1.  **Visualize the Gap:** The dominant visual should be a progress tracker showing "X of 5 days" for the current week, not lifetime stats.
2.  **Contextual Nudges:** If a user is on Day 4, the primary Call-to-Action (CTA) should be aggressively optimized to start a workout *today*. If they are on Day 1, the UI should educate them on the "5-Day Rule" benefit.
3.  **Social Context:** Display social competition data (leaderboards) only if it directly motivates the *next* workout needed to hit the streak, not as a passive feed.

By optimizing for "Weekly Users hitting 5 Days," you align the interface with the specific mechanism that keeps users returning next month.

**Note:** This metric is derived strictly from your observed correlation between 5-day streaks and monthly retention; no external benchmarks were used. Ensure your event taxonomy instruments "streak_completed" as a distinct event to track this accurately without relying on raw session counts.
