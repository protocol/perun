### North Star & Input Metrics
**North Star:** Weekly Active Agencies generating a "Qualified Lead" (defined as a prospect contacted or saved) using Advanced Filters.
*Reject vanity metrics:* Do not track "Filter Views" or "Total Searches." These rise regardless of value. Track only the core action where value is realized.

**Input Metrics (The Levers):**
1.  **Activation Rate:** % of new subscribers who use Advanced Filters within their first 3 sessions.
2.  **Retention Cohort:** % of users who use Advanced Filters in Week 1 and return to use them in Week 2.
3.  **Conversion to Expansion:** % of users in the "High Filter Usage" cohort who upgrade or add seats within 60 days, compared to the "Non-User" baseline.

### The Funnel: Read Bottom-Up (Retention First)
Do not pour more acquisition spend into this feature until retention is proven. Your flat expansion revenue suggests a leak in the bucket, not a lack of awareness.
1.  **Retention Check:** Of the 40% who tried the feature, how many used it twice? If drop-off is high after the first use, the feature is likely too complex (time-to-value issue) or irrelevant (problem-fit issue).
2.  **Activation Check:** Are users finding the feature early enough? If time-to-first-filter-use > 3 days, onboarding is the blocker.
3.  **Revenue Link:** Only after confirming retention should you correlate usage volume to expansion.

### Experiment Rigor: Validate the Assumption
Your assumption ("filters → better leads → higher spend") is currently **UNVERIFIED**. You cannot claim correlation without isolating the variable.
*   **Do not peek:** Do not stop analysis because "p < 0.05" today.

*   **Guardrail:** Monitor "Time to First Qualified Lead." If filter users take *longer* to find a lead than non-users, the feature is increasing complexity without adding value, explaining the flat revenue.
