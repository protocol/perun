**Deliverable: Conversion Definition & Signal Framework**

**1. Define the North Star: "Sales-Qualified Submission"**
Do not define conversion as "page view" or "partial entry." Your North Star must be **"Completed Form Submitted."**
*   **Reasoning:** Your sales team has a hard capacity constraint (15 calls/week = ~60 qualified leads/month). Counting partial submissions as conversions inflates your metric without delivering business value, creating a "vanity metric" that rises while sales capacity remains bottlenecked.
*   **Action:** Instrument two distinct events: `form_started` (any field interaction) and `form_submitted` (all 12 fields + budget completed). Only `form_submitted` counts toward your primary KPI.

**2. The Diagnostic Signal: Quality vs. Friction**
To determine if the drop-off (18% → 9%) is due to "unqualified traffic filtering out" or "excessive friction killing good leads," analyze the **drop-off point relative to the budget questions**.

*   **Signal A (Genuine Disinterest/Friction):** If >60% of drop-offs occur *before* or *exactly at* the new budget fields, the length is the blocker. Users are willing to give contact info but refuse the specific effort of estimating budget. This suggests the form is too demanding for the current traffic temperature.
*   **Signal B (Higher Quality Filtering):** If drop-offs remain consistent with historical patterns across all 12 fields (i.e., users leave randomly throughout the form, not clustering at the new questions), the lower rate likely reflects a stricter self-selection. The remaining 9% are highly motivated.

**3. The Capacity Test (The "This-Week" Step)**
You do not need to guess; run the numbers against your sales capacity immediately.
*   **Current Math:** 200 visits/day × 9% = 18 submissions/day.
*   **Weekly Volume:** 18 × 5 days = 90 submissions/week.
*   **The Conflict:** Your team can handle 15 calls. You are generating 90 leads.
*   **Conclusion:** Your problem is **not** low conversion; it is **over-generation**. The 9% rate is already producing 6x more leads than sales can contact. Shortening the form to increase conversion to 15% would generate ~150 leads/week, worsening the bottleneck and wasting marketing spend on uncontacted leads.

**4. Recommendation for Q4 KPIs**
*   **Primary Metric:** `form_submitted` count (capped at sales capacity).
*   **Guardrail Metric:** `sales_contact_rate` (percentage of submissions actually called). If this drops below 80%, stop increasing top-of-funnel volume.
*   **Decision:** Do not shorten the form yet. The current friction is acting as a necessary filter. Instead, instrument a `budget_field_abandonment` event. If abandonment there is disproportionately high compared to other fields, consider making the budget question optional or multiple-choice to reduce cognitive load without removing the qualifier entirely.

**Note:** The 9% rate is a feature, not a bug, given your 15-call/week limit. Focus on lead scoring rather than form length.
