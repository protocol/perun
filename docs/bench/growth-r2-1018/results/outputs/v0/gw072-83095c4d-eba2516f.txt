**Recommendation: Reject Both Proposed Metrics as Your North Star**

Neither "average calories burned per session" nor "number of friends invited per user" should be your North Star metric for the beta launch. Both are vanity metrics in this context: they can rise while your business fails.

*   **Average Calories:** This measures workout intensity, not product value or habit formation. A user can burn high calories in one intense session and never return. It does not correlate with your stated goal of six-month retention.
*   **Friends Invited:** This measures viral potential, not core value. Users may invite friends out of curiosity without ever completing a class themselves. High invites with low actual usage creates a "leaky bucket" where acquisition costs rise but retention stays flat.

**The Correct North Star: "Weekly Active Users Completing 3+ Classes"**

Your core assumption is that users who complete three classes in their first week retain for six months. Therefore, your single North Star must be the count of unique users who hit this specific threshold within a seven-day window.

**Why This Wins for Your Operations Director:**
1.  **Directly Ties to Retention:** It measures the exact behavior you believe drives long-term value. If this number goes up, your six-month retention projection improves. If it stays flat, no amount of calories burned or friends invited will save the business.
2.  **Filters Noise:** It ignores one-off users who try the VR headset once and quit, focusing only on those building the habit.
3.  **Aligns Hardware Marketing:** It gives your hardware partners a clear target: marketing should drive users who are likely to complete three classes, not just anyone who signs up.

**How to Measure the Social Variable (The Unknown)**
Do not make "friends invited" the North Star. Instead, treat it as an **input metric** to test your hypothesis.
*   **Segment your North Star:** Compare the "3+ Classes" rate between two cohorts: users who worked out with a friend vs. users who worked out solo.
*   **The Decision Rule:** If the social cohort shows a significantly higher rate of hitting the 3-class threshold, *then* you double down on social features. If not, you pivot to improving solo onboarding. This answers your unknown variable without distorting your primary goal.

**Immediate Action Plan for May 20th Deadline:**
1.  **Define the Event:** Instrument a specific event triggered only when a user finishes their third class in a rolling 7-day window.
2.  **Set the Baseline:** With 500 waitlist signups, your initial goal is simply to measure what percentage of early adopters hit this mark. Do not guess a target; establish your own baseline from the first 50 users.
3.  **Present the Strategy:** Tell the director: "We are optimizing for habitual users, not intense workouts or viral invites. Our success metric is the number of users completing three classes in week one. We will use social data to refine *how* we get them there, not to define *if* we are succeeding."

This approach ensures you measure real value creation, not just activity that looks good on a slide.

*Note: Ensure user identifiers are pseudonymous to comply with privacy standards when tracking social connections. Do not instrument "calories" or "invites" unless you have a specific question they answer; otherwise, they are just noise.*
