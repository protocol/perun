### Board Briefing: TalentBridge Marketplace Health & Bottleneck Analysis

**1. Defining Success for Both Sides**
To measure health, we must stop tracking "matches" as the primary goal and start tracking **value realization**.
*   **For Agencies (Demand):** Success is **Speed to Qualified Hire**. They do not just want a match; they want a designer who starts work quickly. The metric is the average days from "Job Posted" to "Contract Signed."
*   **For Freelancers (Supply):** Success is **Time to First Earnings**. They care about how quickly they can move from seeing a job to securing paid work. The metric is the average days from "Profile Active" to "First Contract Signed."

**2. The Single North Star Metric**
Replace "Total Matches" with **Weekly Active Successful Pairs**.
*   *Definition:* The number of unique agency-freelancer pairs that both logged in and completed a contract within the last 7 days.
*   *Why:* Total matches is a vanity metric that can rise even if the platform is slowing down (e.g., more low-quality matches). This metric only rises when real value is delivered to both sides simultaneously.

**3. Diagnosing the Bottleneck (The 7-Day Delay)**
Your assumption that "demand outpaces supply" is unverified. To find the true friction point without technical jargon, we must measure the **drop-off rate** at three specific stages. We will track the percentage of jobs that fail to move forward at each step:

*   **Stage A: Visibility Friction.** Of the jobs posted, what percentage receive *zero* proposals within 24 hours?
    *   *If high:* Freelancers aren't seeing the jobs or the scope is so unclear they ignore it immediately.
*   **Stage B: Negotiation Friction.** Of the jobs that receive proposals, what percentage fail to reach a "Contract Sent" status?
    *   *If high:* The friction is in the negotiation phase (pricing, terms, or communication delays).
*   **Stage C: Closing Friction.** Of the contracts sent, what percentage are not signed within 48 hours?
    *   *If high:* The final agreement process is broken.

**Actionable Insight:** If Stage A is low (jobs get proposals) but Stage B is high, your problem is not a lack of freelancers; it is a breakdown in how agencies and freelancers agree on terms. Pouring more marketing into agency acquisition will only worsen the delay if the negotiation pipe is clogged.

**4. Measuring Supply-Demand Balance**
Do not use raw counts of users. Use the **Proposals-per-Job Ratio**.
*   *Healthy Range:* Establish a baseline (e.g., 3–5 qualified proposals per job).
*   *Imbalance Signal:* If this ratio drops below 2, you have a supply shortage. If it spikes above 10, you have too much supply, leading to freelancer frustration (spammy low-ball offers).
*   *Current Status:* Since your time-to-hire doubled, check if the *quality* of proposals dropped even if the *count* remained steady. Agencies may be ignoring 20 weak proposals to find one good fit, artificially inflating the time-to-hire.

**5. Immediate Next Steps for the Board**
1.  **Stop guessing the cause.** Pull the data for the last 30 days to calculate the failure rate at Stage A (Visibility) vs. Stage B (Negotiation).
2.  **Segment the delay.** Compare the time-to-hire for "Clear Scope" jobs (those with detailed descriptions) vs. "Vague Scope" jobs. If vague jobs take 10 days and clear jobs take 3, the solution is product guidance, not recruitment.
3.  **Report the trend, not the snapshot.** Next week, present the *trend* of the "Weekly Active Successful Pairs" alongside the "Proposals-per-Job Ratio." If matches go up but active pairs go down, the platform is becoming less efficient.
