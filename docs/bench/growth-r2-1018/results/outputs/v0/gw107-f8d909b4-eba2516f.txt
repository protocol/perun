**Primary Metric Recommendation: "Successful Express Bookings per Active User (Weekly)"**

Do not select "Time to Book" as your North Star. While 60% of users *say* they value speed, your own data proves that maximizing speed in isolation destroys value by driving cancellations from 5% to 12%. A metric that rises while cancellations spike is a vanity metric; it measures activity, not customer value.

Your true North Star must capture the completion of the core value proposition: a cleaning service booked quickly *and* fulfilled without regret. Define a "Successful Express Booking" as a booking made via the express flow that is **not cancelled within 24 hours** and where the service provider **arrives as scheduled**. This single metric forces the product team to optimize for speed only insofar as it does not compromise accuracy. If you make the flow faster but users pick the wrong time slot, this metric drops. If you make the flow slower but more accurate, this metric rises. It aligns the team to the actual business outcome: retained revenue and satisfied customers.

**Immediate Diagnostic Steps (This Week):**
To resolve the uncertainty between "wrong time slot" vs. "feeling rushed," instrument two specific events immediately. Do not track everything; track only what answers these questions:
1.  **Event:** `express_booking_cancelled` with property `cancel_reason_category` (mapped from user selection or support tags).
    *   *Question Answered:* Is the leakage due to selection errors (e.g., wrong date) or buyer's remorse (e.g., "changed mind," "too fast")?
2.  **Event:** `express_flow_step_duration` for each step in the new checkout.
    *   *Question Answered:* Is there a specific threshold where duration correlates with lower cancellation? (e.g., users who spend <3 seconds on the time-slot selection cancel at 20%, while those taking >5 seconds cancel at 4%).

**Decision Framework for the Regional Director:**
Present the OKR as: "Increase Weekly Successful Express Bookings per Active User by X%."
*   **Input Metric 1:** Median Time-to-Book (Guardrail: Must not exceed current baseline by >10%).
*   **Input Metric 2:** 24-Hour Cancellation Rate (Guardrail: Must remain <6%).

If the cancellation rate remains elevated after segmenting by `cancel_reason_category`, the hypothesis that "speed causes regret" is verified, and the solution is not a slower flow, but a "friction-for-accuracy" intervention (e.g., a mandatory confirmation summary or a smart-default time slot) before the final click. Do not rely on the survey's stated preference; rely on the revealed preference in your cancellation data.

**Note:** The survey indicates intent, but the 12% cancellation rate reveals the actual constraint; prioritize the behavioral data over self-reported preferences.
