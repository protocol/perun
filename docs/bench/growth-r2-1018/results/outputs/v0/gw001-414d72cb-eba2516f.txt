**Recommendation: Adopt "Weekly Active Buyers Completing a Verified Sustainable Action" as your North Star Metric.**

Do not select "Carbon Footprint Saved" or "Total Pageviews" as your North Star. These are vanity metrics; they can rise while your business stagnates because they measure output, not customer value. A user can view a carbon score without buying, or buy once and never return. Your current data proves this: traffic is up 15%, yet repeat purchases are stalled at 18%. This indicates a leak in retention, not acquisition.

Your North Star must rise *only* when a user derives real, recurring value. For GreenCart, value is not seeing a number; it is the act of choosing sustainability repeatedly. Therefore, define your North Star as the count of unique users per week who complete a purchase where the "sustainable choice" was active (e.g., buying an item with a displayed footprint, choosing green shipping, or purchasing from a verified eco-brand). This metric ties engineering (display accuracy, checkout flow) and marketing (campaigns driving specific behaviors) directly to retained revenue.

**Analysis Structure to Validate the Choice**

To convince your CEO and Head of Sustainability, you must prove that this specific behavior correlates with long-term retention better than simple traffic or one-off purchases. Do not look for external benchmarks; compare your own user cohorts against your internal baseline.

1.  **Segment by Behavior, Not Demographics:**
    Pull your user data from March 12 to present. Create three cohorts:
    *   **Cohort A:** Users who viewed carbon footprints but did not purchase.
    *   **Cohort B:** Users who purchased items *without* engaging with sustainability features.
    *   **Cohort C:** Users who purchased items *with* verified sustainability features (your proposed North Star behavior).

2.  **Read Retention Bottom-Up:**
    Plot the Week-2, Week-4, and Week-8 return rates for each cohort.
    *   If Cohort C shows a significantly higher return rate than Cohort B, your hypothesis is validated: the sustainability feature drives loyalty, not just clicks.
    *   If Cohort C and Cohort B have identical retention rates, the carbon footprint display is merely a "nice-to-have" that influences initial clicks (acquisition) but fails to drive loyalty. In this case, your roadmap must shift from *displaying* data to *incentivizing* the repeat behavior (e.g., rewards for consecutive green purchases).

3.  **Trace the Input Metrics:**
    Once the North Star is set, identify the two or three input metrics that move it. For example: "Percentage of product pages where the carbon score is visible above the fold" or "Checkout completion rate for green-shipping options." These are the levers your engineering team builds and your marketing team optimizes. If the North Star dips, you check these inputs to find the break.

**Immediate Next Step**
This week, query your database to calculate the 30-day retention rate for Cohort C versus Cohort B. If the difference is less than 5 percentage points, pause plans to expand carbon display features and instead investigate friction in the post-purchase experience. If the difference is greater than 5 percentage points, align the Q3 roadmap to maximize the frequency of Cohort C behaviors.
