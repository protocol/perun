**Decision: Do not approve the national rollout yet. The current data is `UNVERIFIED` for scaling decisions because it lacks a retention baseline and confounds promotional mechanics with demographic traits.**

Your pilot shows high adoption (10%) but low Average Order Value (AOV). However, interpreting this as a permanent demographic trait ("students only buy small items") is a rationalization error. You are comparing a **promotional pilot** (likely discounted) against a **standard customer base** (full price). This is an apples-to-oranges comparison. The lower AOV is likely driven by your initial offer structure, not necessarily the students' inherent lifetime value (LTV).

To assess the risk of scaling to ten campuses, you must answer three specific questions using your own data before asking for budget:

1.  **Is Retention Holding?**
    Adoption (acquisition) is a vanity metric if users do not return. You stated the pilot ran for two months. Do not look at total signups. Look at **Week-4 and Week-8 retention cohorts**: of the students who bought in September, what percentage bought again in October *without* a new deep discount? If retention drops to near zero once the initial promo fades, the 10% adoption is a leaky bucket. Scaling acquisition into a leaky retention funnel wastes capital.

2.  **Separate Price Sensitivity from Promo Mechanics**
    You cannot know if low AOV is permanent until you test it. Did all students receive the same discount? If yes, you have no data on price elasticity. You need to instrument an event taxonomy that tracks **purchase behavior by discount tier**. Before expanding, run a localized A/B test on this campus: offer Group A the current deep discount and Group B a moderate discount. Measure if the moderate group converts at a similar rate but with higher AOV. Without this, you are assuming price sensitivity rather than measuring it.

3.  **Define the North Star for Expansion**
    Stop optimizing for "Adoption Rate." Your North Star for this program must be **Repeat Purchase Rate** or **Net Revenue per Active Student**. If the CFO needs to approve budget, present a projection based on *conservative* retention (e.g., if only 20% of the pilot users return at the current AOV, does the unit economics still work?). If the math only works assuming 100% of the pilot users stay and spend, the risk is too high.

**This-Week Step:**
1.  **Query your analytics** for the specific cohort of students who signed up in Week 1 of September: calculate their % who made a second purchase in October. If this number is <15%, halt expansion plans immediately.
2.  **Design a guardrailed experiment** for the next campus: pre-commit to testing two discount levels (current vs. 50% of current depth) with a primary metric of *Revenue per User* and a guardrail of *Conversion Rate*. Do not peek at results before the pre-committed sample size is reached.
