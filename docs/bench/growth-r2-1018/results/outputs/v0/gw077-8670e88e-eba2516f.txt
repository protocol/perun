**Recommendation: Adopt "Weekly Active Contributors Completing a Review Cycle" as your North Star Metric.**

Do not choose between "time to first review" (speed) or "number of unique contributors" (breadth) in isolation. Optimizing solely for speed risks cementing a power-user elite, where fast feedback loops exclude newcomers who need more guidance. Optimizing solely for unique contributors risks vanity growth—signups that never merge code—creating a hollow ecosystem.

Your mission is *democratizing* development. This requires a metric that only rises when a **new or existing user successfully contributes value**. Define your North Star as: **The count of unique users who submit a pull request and receive a merged contribution (or substantive review feedback) within a 7-day window.**

This metric forces the system to balance your competing tensions:
1.  **It demands retention:** A user who contributes once and leaves does not count next week. This addresses your fear that fast reviews might only serve a static group of experts. If the "power users" dominate without onboarding new blood, this metric stagnates.
2.  **It validates speed indirectly:** If "time to first review" drags beyond 24 hours, new contributors often abandon the process before merging. Speed is an *input* metric to drive the North Star, not the goal itself.
3.  **It measures real value:** Unlike raw signups or total PR counts, this counts only completed cycles of value exchange.

**Implementation Strategy for Q3:**

*   **Primary Metric (North Star):** Weekly Active Contributors completing a merge/feedback cycle.
*   **Input Metric A (Speed):** Median "Time to First Review" for first-time contributors vs. repeat contributors. Track these separately. If the gap widens, your democratization is failing.
*   **Input Metric B (Breadth):** Ratio of first-time contributors to total active contributors.
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Percentage of PRs closed without feedback. Ensure speed efforts don't lead to dismissive rejections.

**Rationale for the Community Manager:**
Tell your moderators: "Our goal is not just fast reviews, nor just more names on a list. Our goal is **successful contribution cycles**. If you speed up reviews for experts but newcomers still drop off, we fail. If we recruit thousands who never merge code, we fail. We win only when a diverse set of users repeatedly ships code."

**Immediate Next Steps:**
1.  Instrument the event `contribution_cycle_completed` (user ID, repo ID, timestamp, contributor_status: new/returning).
2.  Segment this metric by "first-time contributor" vs. "repeat contributor" immediately. If the retention rate for new contributors is not rising, the 24-hour hypothesis is insufficient on its own.
3.  Set a Q3 target to increase the *ratio* of new contributors completing a cycle, not just the raw volume.

*Note: Verify your data pipeline can distinguish between a "review comment" and a "merged contribution" to avoid inflating the metric with noise.*
*Note: Do not set a fixed benchmark for "twice the rate" until you establish your own baseline from July 1st data.*
