**Recommendation: Merchant Repeat Rate (Cohort-Based)**

For CargoUnite, the single North Star metric must be **Merchant Repeat Rate**, defined specifically as the percentage of merchants who book a second shipment within 30 days of their first successful delivery.

This metric alone aligns your operations team with long-term sustainability because it directly measures the core assumption you stated: that lifetime value is driven by reliability, not price. In a fragmented logistics market like Ho Chi Minh City, where trust is scarce and switching costs are low, a merchant returning is the only definitive proof that you delivered value.

**Trade-off Analysis**

1.  **Total Gross Merchandise Value (GMV):**
    *   *The Trap:* GMV is a vanity metric in your context. It can be inflated by a few large, one-off shipments or by subsidizing prices to win volume.
    *   *The Risk:* Optimizing for GMV encourages sales to chase big, difficult orders that may strain your fragmented trucker network, leading to late deliveries. If those merchants never return, you have burned cash and operational capacity for zero sustainable growth. GMV rises even while your business rots from the inside.

2.  **Number of Successful Deliveries:**
    *   *The Trap:* This measures output, not outcome. It counts the transaction but ignores the customer's satisfaction or future intent.
    *   *The Risk:* Your team could maximize this by focusing on easy, short-haul routes while neglecting complex, high-value lanes that merchants actually need. It creates a "churn and burn" dynamic where you constantly replace lost merchants with new acquisitions, driving up customer acquisition costs without building network density.

3.  **Merchant Repeat Rate (The Winner):**
    *   *The Alignment:* This metric forces every department to focus on the "reliability" lever.
        *   **Operations:** Must prioritize on-time performance over sheer volume, as a late delivery kills the repeat probability.
        *   **Supply Side:** Must recruit and retain truckers who are reliable, not just cheap, because unreliable truckers cause merchant churn.
        *   **Product:** Must build features that reduce friction for re-booking, not just for sign-ups.
    *   *The Sustainability:* In a two-sided marketplace, supply follows demand. High repeat rates create predictable demand, which allows you to optimize trucker utilization and earnings, stabilizing the supply side without heavy subsidies.

**Why This Prevents Local Optimization**
If you choose GMV, sales will oversell capacity. If you choose Delivery Count, ops will cherry-pick easy jobs. If you choose Repeat Rate, *no one* can succeed locally at the expense of the whole. Sales cannot sell to merchants who won't stay; ops cannot cut corners that ruin reliability; finance cannot slash trucker pay if it causes no-shows. The metric ties the entire chain to the merchant's actual experience of value.

**Immediate Action**
For your Thursday meeting, define the baseline: Calculate the current % of merchants from 90 days ago who have booked again since. Set a target improvement for the next quarter based solely on moving that percentage up. Discard GMV and raw delivery counts as primary decision drivers; use them only as secondary guardrails to ensure you aren't achieving retention by ignoring scale entirely.

*Note: Ensure your data tracking distinguishes between a "successful" delivery (on-time, undamaged) and a mere "completed" one, as the nuance drives the repeat behavior.*
*Do not fabricate a target percentage; use your own historical cohort data to set the baseline.*
