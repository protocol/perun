### Recommended Metrics for MedInsight ROI Demonstration

To demonstrate value without accessing protected health information (PHI) or Electronic Health Records (EHR), you must shift from tracking **vanity metrics** (logins, clicks) to **actionable workflow metrics** that proxy operational efficiency. These metrics measure whether your dashboard successfully reduces the administrative burden, which logically precedes improvements in patient outcomes.

Instrument the following four metrics, ensuring each answers a specific business question:

**1. Time-to-Insight (Activation & Efficiency)**
*   **The Question:** "How quickly can a nurse or administrator find the specific operational bottleneck they logged in to solve?"
*   **Instrumentation:** Measure the elapsed time from `session_start` to the first `export_report`, `share_alert`, or `drill_down_to_unit_level` event.
*   **ROI Argument:** A reduction in Time-to-Insight directly correlates to reduced administrative labor hours. If your dashboard cuts search time from 15 minutes to 2 minutes, you can calculate the annual labor cost savings per user. This is a direct efficiency gain the CMO can validate without patient data.

**2. Actionable Alert Acknowledgment Rate (Core Value)**
*   **The Question:** "Are users acting on the anomalies our dashboard identifies, or are they ignoring them?"
*   **Instrumentation:** Track the ratio of `alert_viewed` events to `alert_acknowledged` or `task_assigned` events within the same session.
*   **ROI Argument:** High login frequency with low acknowledgment rates indicates the data is noisy or irrelevant. A high acknowledgment rate proves your tool is driving operational workflow changes. This proxies "operational efficiency" by showing that your software triggers real-world administrative actions.

**3. Cross-Departmental Data Sharing Frequency (Adoption & Stickiness)**
*   **The Question:** "Is the dashboard breaking down silos between nursing, administration, and finance?"
*   **Instrumentation:** Count `report_shared` events where the recipient belongs to a different user cohort (e.g., Nurse → Administrator) compared to same-cohort shares.
*   **ROI Argument:** Hospital inefficiency often stems from communication gaps. Demonstrating that your tool facilitates cross-functional alignment provides a qualitative proxy for improved organizational coordination, a key precursor to better patient flow and reduced wait times.

**4. Weekly Active Users Completing Core Workflow (North Star)**
*   **The Question:** "How many users are deriving actual value weekly, rather than just logging in?"
*   **Instrumentation:** Define a "Core Workflow" as a sequence: `login` → `filter_data` → `export/share`. Count unique users completing this sequence per week.
*   **ROI Argument:** Reject raw "logins" as a vanity metric; a user can log in and find nothing useful. This metric isolates users who actually completed a value-generating task. Retention of this specific behavior is your strongest predictor of renewal.

### Privacy & Implementation Constraints
*   **Identifier Seam:** Use a stable, **pseudonymous** user ID to track these workflows across sessions. Do not use names or employee IDs in your analytics stack. Route any mapping of these IDs to real identities strictly to your internal HR systems, not the product analytics database, to comply with privacy standards.
*   **No Benchmarks:** Do not claim "Industry average Time-to-Insight is X." Instead, establish a **baseline** for each hospital network before your deployment and measure the **delta** (improvement) over time. Your ROI case rests on *their* improvement, not an external standard.
