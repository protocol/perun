**Evaluation of Monetization Strategy for PixelQuest Summer Update**

**Current Status:** The recent 20% revenue lift accompanied by a 3-minute session reduction is an **UNVERIFIED** long-term win. Without comparing this against your own prior retention baselines for the specific cohort exposed to higher ad frequency, this is a snapshot, not a trend. The assumption that "7-day consecutive login" drives purchase is a hypothesis until validated by your internal cohort data; do not treat it as a benchmark.

**Option A: Prioritize Immediate Revenue (Scale Ad Frequency)**
*   **Benefit:** Maximizes short-term cash flow, potentially meeting immediate board expectations for the June 10th meeting.
*   **Risk:** You are testing the elasticity of your retention bucket. If the 3-minute session drop pushes users below the "activation" threshold required to form the 7-day habit, you are trading Lifetime Value (LTV) for current quarter revenue. In freemium models, retention leaks make acquisition spend wasteful; if players churn before day 7, the assumed 50% purchase uplift never materializes.
*   **Verdict:** High risk of eroding the user base required for future monetization. Only proceed if your data confirms that the specific users dropping off had near-zero LTV potential anyway.

**Option B: Prioritize Engagement (Protect Session Length)**
*   **Benefit:** Protects the "core action" (playing the game) required to drive the 7-day streak. If the streak correlates to purchase, preserving session length preserves the funnel's top.
*   **Risk:** Leaves immediate revenue on the table. If the 20% lift was "free money" from users who would have churned regardless, reducing ads now is an opportunity cost.
*   **Verdict:** Safer for long-term health, but requires proof that the session length reduction is the *cause* of potential churn, not just a correlation.

**The Decision Framework**
Do not choose based on "engagement vs. revenue" as abstract concepts. Choose based on **retention of the core value**.
1.  **Reject Vanity:** Total DAU (100k) and raw revenue are vanity if the retention cohort is leaking.
2.  **Diagnose Bottom-Up:** Before scaling ads (Acquisition/Revenue), verify if the users exposed to the test are retaining at Day 7 and Day 30 compared to your pre-May 5th baseline. If retention is flat, the revenue gain is likely sustainable. If retention dips, the revenue gain is a loan against future LTV.
3.  **Experiment Rigor:** Do not peek at daily results to declare a winner. If you run an A/B test for the summer update, pre-commit to a sample size or duration. A result observed after only a few days of the new ad strategy is `UNVERIFIED` due to novelty effects and cannot justify a permanent strategy shift.

**This-Week Step:**
1.  **Query:** Segment your users by the new ad-frequency exposure and calculate **Day-7 and Day-30 retention rates** compared to the control group (pre-May 5th or low-ad cohort).
2.  **Decision Rule:** If Day-7 retention drops by >5% relative to baseline, revert ad frequency immediately (Guardrail tripped). If retention is stable, the revenue lift is likely safe to scale.
3.  **Instrumentation:** Ensure you are tracking a stable, pseudonymous user ID to follow these cohorts across sessions; do not rely on aggregate daily totals.

**Note:** No external benchmarks for "good" retention were used; this analysis relies strictly on your internal baseline comparison. The 50% purchase likelihood assumption must be validated against actual PixelQuest conversion data before driving strategy.
