**Recommendation: Adopt "Number of Completed Lesson Plans" as your primary metric.**

"Hours of connection time" is a vanity metric in this context. It measures infrastructure availability and passive presence, not educational engagement. A student can remain connected while distracted, multitasking, or experiencing technical idle time without any learning occurring. Since your specific concern is whether passive time leads to learning, this metric fails to distinguish between genuine tutoring and mere internet access. It risks inflating your impact report with numbers that rise even if student outcomes stagnate.

"Number of Completed Lesson Plans" is a superior North Star metric because it directly proxies the delivery of your core value: structured curriculum. A lesson plan represents a defined unit of learning with a beginning, middle, and end. Counting completed plans ensures that the time spent was purposeful and followed your pedagogical design. It aligns with your assumption that frequency (two sessions per week) drives improvement, as lesson plans are discrete units you can count per week.

**Addressing the Data Gap (40% Missing Exam Scores)**
You cannot fabricate the missing exam scores, and claiming a blanket "10% improvement" without data for 40% of users would undermine credibility with donors. Instead, reframe your impact narrative around the **leading indicator** (lesson completion) while transparently reporting the **lagging indicator** (exam scores) only for the verified cohort.

1.  **Primary Claim:** "Students who completed ≥2 lesson plans per week demonstrated high engagement with the core curriculum." This is 100% verifiable with your current data.
2.  **Secondary Correlation:** "Among the 60% of students with reported exam data, those completing ≥2 lesson plans per week saw an average score improvement of X%."
3.  **The Gap:** Explicitly state that exam outcome tracking is limited by irregular school schedules for 40% of users, and frame the upcoming year's goal as improving this data collection mechanism. Donors often value this level of honesty and clear identification of operational bottlenecks over inflated, unverifiable success rates.

**Immediate Actions for the August 30th Report**
*   **Instrumentation Check:** Verify that your system explicitly logs "Lesson Plan Started" and "Lesson Plan Completed" as distinct events. Do not infer completion from time spent.
*   **Cohort Analysis:** Segment your users into three groups: (a) <1 plan/week, (b) 1–2 plans/week, (c) >2 plans/week. Compare the verified exam score improvements across these three groups. If the metric is valid, Group C should show significantly higher gains than Group A.
*   **Narrative Shift:** Move the donor conversation from "hours logged" to "curriculum units mastered." This shifts the focus from input (time) to output (learning activities).

By choosing "Completed Lesson Plans," you measure the *activity that causes learning* rather than the *environment where learning might happen*. This provides a defensible, logical link to student improvement that survives scrutiny even when final exam data is incomplete.
