**Deliverable: The Two Activation Metrics for SwiftWrap**

Stop tracking forty events immediately. For a two-engineer team at the MVP stage, instrument only these two metrics. They directly test your hypothesis that "3+ summaries in week one" drives revenue.

**1. The "Value Realization" Rate (Core Action Completion)**
*   **Definition:** The percentage of new signups who generate **three or more property summaries within their first 7 days**.
*   **Why this indicates value:** Real estate agents do not pay for "AI"; they pay for time saved on listing descriptions. An agent who creates one summary is curious; an agent who creates three has integrated your tool into their actual workflow (likely for multiple listings or revisions). This specific threshold moves the metric from "usage" to "habit formation." If this number is low, your product isn't solving a painful enough problem to warrant repeated use, regardless of how many pages they view.
*   **Action:** Build one query: `Count(users) WHERE event = 'summary_generated' >= 3 AND timestamp < signup + 7 days`. Ignore all other click events until this rate stabilizes.

**2. The "Week-2 Return" Rate (Early Retention)**
*   **Definition:** The percentage of users who generated at least one summary in Week 1 and return to generate **at least one more** in Week 2.
*   **Why this indicates value:** Activation is useless without retention. In local real estate, inventory and client needs are continuous. If an agent uses the tool intensely in Week 1 (perhaps due to onboarding novelty) but fails to return in Week 2, the "aha moment" was temporary. This metric proves the tool survives the initial hype and fits into the agent's weekly listing cycle. It is the leading indicator of your long-term revenue assumption.
*   **Action:** Build one cohort query: `Users active in Week 1` filtered by `Active again in Week 2`.

**What to Ignore (The Noise)**
Discard the other 38+ events for now. Specifically ignore:
*   **Page views:** Agents may load pages without finding value. This is vanity.
*   **Clicks on non-core features:** Unless it leads to a summary, it doesn't predict revenue yet.
*   **Cumulative signups:** This number only goes up and hides the fact that users might be churning immediately.

**Board Meeting Narrative**
Tell your board: "We are shifting from measuring 'activity' to measuring 'value.' We have identified that agents who create 3+ summaries in week one and return in week two are our only true leading indicators of revenue. We are deprioritizing all other data collection to focus engineering entirely on improving these two rates."
