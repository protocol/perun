### Measurement Plan for EduStart Advisory Board

**1. The North Star Metric: Weekly Active Learners Completing Lesson 3**
Reject "total signups" (800 waitlist) and "time spent" as your primary success indicators. Signups are vanity; they rise regardless of value. Time spent is noisy; a confused student may linger longer than an engaged one.
Your single North Star must be **the count of unique students who complete Lesson 3 within 7 days of starting**.
*Why:* You hypothesized that finishing the first three lessons is the "aha moment" predicting long-term retention. This metric measures *value delivered*, not just interest shown. If this number grows week-over-week, you are solving a real problem. If it stagnates while signups rise, you have a leaky bucket.

**2. The Funnel: Diagnose Bottom-Up (Retention First)**
Do not focus on acquisition yet. With 800 waitlist users, acquisition is currently solved. Your risk is building a product nobody sticks with. Analyze your funnel in this specific order:
*   **Retention (The Core):** Of the students who started last week, how many returned to finish Lesson 3? This is your only true product-market fit signal right now.
*   **Activation:** Of those who signed up, what percentage finished Lesson 1? If this is low, your onboarding is broken.
*   **Acquisition:** Only optimize referral sources once the above two are stable.

**3. Event Taxonomy: Instrument Only What Answers a Question**
Do not "track everything." Instrument exactly three events to answer specific questions:
*   `lesson_started` (Properties: `lesson_id`, `user_id`): *Question: Are students entering the flow?*
*   `lesson_completed` (Properties: `lesson_id`, `user_id`, `duration_seconds`): *Question: Are they reaching the end?*
*   `return_visit` (Properties: `user_id`, `days_since_last_active`): *Question: Are they coming back without a push notification?*

**4. Validating Your Hypothesis**
You cannot confirm the "Lesson 3 threshold" yet because you have no historical data. That is fine.
*   **Action:** Track the correlation between completing Lesson 3 and returning in Week 2.
*   **Success Criteria:** If students who hit Lesson 3 have a significantly higher Week 2 return rate than those who stop at Lesson 2, your hypothesis is validated. If not, you must find the new "magic moment."

**5. Experiment Rigor**
As you tweak the curriculum to improve completion:
*   Pre-commit to a test duration (e.g., 2 weeks). Do not stop early because "Lesson 2 completion looks good today."
*   Define a guardrail: Ensure that speeding up lessons to boost completion doesn't reduce the quality of code submitted (if measurable) or cause confusion spikes.

**This Week's Steps**
1.  **Define the Query:** Write the SQL/analytics query today to calculate "Percentage of starters who complete Lesson 3 within 7 days."
2.  **Set the Baseline:** Run this query on Day 7 post-launch. This number is your baseline; do not compare it to industry averages.
3.  **The Decision Rule:** If <20% of starters hit Lesson 3, pause all marketing/referral efforts and focus entirely on fixing the first three lessons.

*Note: Ensure user IDs are pseudonymous for cohort tracking; route any PII handling to your privacy compliance review. Since you are in the MVP stage, ignore revenue metrics until the retention loop is proven.*
