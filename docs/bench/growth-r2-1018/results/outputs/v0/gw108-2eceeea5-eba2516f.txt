**Recommendation: Do not roll out strict privacy as the global default yet.**

A 2% retention gain is statistically positive, but a 15% surge in breakage reports represents a critical functional failure that threatens the core value proposition of a browser: accessing the web. In a bottom-up funnel analysis, if the "Activation" layer (sites loading) is compromised, long-term "Retention" gains are unsustainable. Users who cannot access their frequent destinations will churn regardless of privacy benefits. The current data suggests you are trading immediate utility for a theoretical trust benefit, which is a net negative for a mass-market product.

The ambiguity regarding *where* breakage occurs is the deciding factor. If the 15% increase stems from a few high-traffic sites (e.g., major banking, email, or news portals), the actual number of frustrated users is massive, and the retention lift is likely an artifact of a specific cohort that doesn't care about those sites. If the breakage is distributed across thousands of obscure sites, the impact per user is lower, but the volume of support tickets indicates a scalability issue for your engineering team.

**Required Data Slices for a Confident Decision:**

1.  **Breakage Concentration Analysis:** Segment the breakage reports by domain. Determine if the top 10 domains account for >50% of the errors. If yes, the risk is concentrated and potentially fixable via allow-listing; if no, the protection logic is too broad for general release.
2.  **Cohort Overlap Verification:** Cross-reference the users who reported breakage with the users who were retained. It is highly probable that the "retained" group consists entirely of users who did *not* experience breakage. You need to calculate the retention rate specifically for the "breakage reporter" cohort. If their retention dropped, the aggregate 2% lift is masking a severe leak in a specific user segment.
3.  **Task Completion Rates:** Move beyond binary "site load" metrics. Measure the completion rate of core actions (login, checkout, form submit) on affected sites. A site may "load" visually but fail functionally due to blocked scripts, creating a silent friction point that doesn't generate a crash report but kills conversion.
4.  **Support Ticket Sentiment & Volume:** Quantify the operational cost. A 15% increase in breakage reports likely correlates with a spike in support costs and negative app store reviews. Model the cost of support against the lifetime value (LTV) gain from the 2% retention lift.

**This Week's Action Plan:**
*   **Query:** Run a cohort analysis joining `breakage_report_event` with `weekly_active_user` status to isolate the retention rate of users who experienced breakage vs. those who did not.
*   **Instrument:** Add a `privacy_shield_bypass_count` property to your breakage events to identify if specific rule-sets are causing the majority of failures.
*   **Decision Threshold:** If retention among users experiencing breakage is lower than the control group's baseline, the feature causes net harm to those users. Do not proceed with a global default until breakage is reduced to <1% or confined to non-critical domains.

*Note: The 2% lift may be driven by privacy-conscious early adopters who are less representative of the general population; ensure your 1M user sample matches your global demographic mix before generalizing.*
