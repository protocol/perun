**Decision: Do not deploy. The result is `UNVERIFIED` as a win.**

The 35% increase in time-on-site is a vanity metric in this context; it rises regardless of value. Because your primary goal is content consumption and the Click-Through Rate (CTR) to destination pages showed no lift, the new algorithm has failed to move the North Star. The divergence implies one of two failure modes: either users are confused by irrelevant results (friction), or the results page now satisfies intent directly (cannibalization). Without distinguishing these, deploying risks degrading the core user journey.

**Immediate Analyses to Decode Intent:**

1.  **Search Exit Rate & Bounce Analysis:** Segment sessions where users spent >30 seconds on the results page but did not click. Calculate the exit rate for this cohort compared to the control. A high exit rate here suggests users found their answer on the results page (success for query resolution, failure for content consumption) or gave up entirely.
2.  **Query Reformulation Rate:** Measure how often users modify their search query immediately after viewing the new results page. A spike in reformulations indicates confusion and poor relevance (failure). Stable or lower reformulation rates suggest the results were clear, even if not clicked.
3.  **Zero-Click Satisfaction Survey:** Trigger a micro-survey ("Did you find what you needed?") specifically for users who lingered on the results page without clicking. This qualitative signal is the only way to distinguish between "satisfied" and "stuck."
4.  **Result Snippet Interaction:** If available, track hovers or expansions of search snippets. If users are expanding snippets to read full answers without clicking through, the algorithm is successfully answering queries but cannibalizing your content funnel. You must then decide if "answering on-page" is an acceptable business outcome.

**This Week's Step:**
Run the **Exit Rate** and **Query Reformulation** queries on the test cohort data immediately. If reformulation is up >10% or exit rate is up without a corresponding drop in negative feedback, the experiment is a failure due to confusion. If reformulation is flat/down and exit rate is high, the model is too effective at summarizing, requiring a product strategy pivot rather than an algorithmic rollback.
