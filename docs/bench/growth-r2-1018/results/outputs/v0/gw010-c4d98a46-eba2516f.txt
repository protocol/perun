**Executive Summary: UrbanMove Austin Pilot (Weeks 1–3)**

The new e-scooter fleet is delivering its primary operational value: battery depletion incidents have dropped 40%, confirming improved reliability. However, a critical behavioral shift has emerged. Unauthorized zone endings have risen 20%, contradicting the assumption that higher GPS accuracy would automatically improve parking compliance. This suggests users are not failing to find zones due to technical error, but are actively choosing to end rides outside them, likely perceiving the stricter geofencing as too restrictive or identifying loopholes.

**Decision Framework: Adjust Rules vs. Educate**
Do not launch an educational campaign yet. Education solves ignorance; this data suggests intentional behavior or friction. If users know where the zones are but find them inconvenient, education will fail and damage sentiment. The immediate priority is to validate user intent before altering city agreements.

**Required Data Points to Determine Intent**
To distinguish between "confused users" and "frustrated users," gather these specific metrics this week:

1.  **Pre-End Hover Time:** Measure the time a scooter remains stationary *just outside* an authorized zone before the ride ends. Long hovers suggest users are trying to find a spot but cannot reach one without excessive walking (friction). Immediate ends suggest indifference to the rule (intent).
2.  **Walk-Distance Distribution:** Calculate the straight-line distance from the unauthorized end-point to the nearest valid zone. If 80% of violations occur within 10 meters of a zone, the geofence is too tight. If violations are scattered widely, it is a compliance culture issue.
3.  **App Interaction Logs:** Track how often users open the "parking map" view in the final 2 minutes of a ride. Low interaction implies users are ignoring the map entirely; high interaction followed by unauthorized parking implies the available options are unacceptable.
4.  **Repeat Offender Cohorts:** Identify if the 20% increase is driven by a small group of repeat violators or spread evenly across all users. A concentrated cohort requires targeted enforcement; a broad trend requires systemic rule adjustment.

**Narrative for City Liaison**
"Our new fleet has immediately reduced street clutter caused by dead batteries by 40%. We are currently investigating a 20% rise in boundary exceptions. Early data indicates this is not a GPS failure, but a signal that our current no-parking buffers may be tighter than necessary for safe pedestrian flow. We are analyzing precise stop-location telemetry to propose a data-driven adjustment to zone boundaries that maintains safety while reducing user friction, ensuring high compliance without needing punitive measures."

**Next Step**
Pull the "Pre-End Hover Time" and "Walk-Distance" distributions by Friday. If the median walk distance to a valid zone exceeds 40 meters, propose a geofence expansion to the city. If the distance is negligible but violations remain high, pivot to a targeted in-app warning system for repeat offenders.

*Note: All metrics must be aggregated to protect user privacy; do not share individual trip IDs with city officials.*
*Baseline comparison: Ensure the 20% increase is normalized against the total ride volume growth to confirm it is a rate issue, not just a volume artifact.*
