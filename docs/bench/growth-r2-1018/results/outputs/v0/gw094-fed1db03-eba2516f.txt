**SUBJECT:** Strategic Metrics Framework for February Launch: Balancing Data Integrity and Value Demonstration

To secure enterprise adoption despite Scope 3 data gaps, we must shift our North Star from "Total Emissions Reduced" (a vanity metric in this context due to integrity risks) to **"Verified Actionable Coverage."** This metric rises only when a client successfully ingests, validates, and acts upon supplier data, proving value regardless of perfect precision.

We will measure success using a bottom-up retention framework, prioritizing data trust over raw volume. The following three indicators form our core dashboard:

**1. North Star: Verified Actionable Coverage (%)**
*Definition:* The percentage of a client's top 20 suppliers (by spend) providing data that passes our integrity checks and triggers a specific decarbonization action.
*Rationale:* Raw ingestion numbers are vanity; they rise even with garbage data. This metric proves we are solving the core problem: turning noisy supplier inputs into trusted decisions. If this number stagnates, no amount of total emission reduction claims will retain clients.

**2. Input Metric: Data Confidence Score (DCS) by Region**
*Definition:* A calculated score (0–100) for each regional dataset based on verification methods (e.g., third-party audit vs. self-reported estimate) and historical variance.
*Rationale:* This directly addresses the credibility risk. Instead of hiding data gaps, we visualize them. A client can see, "Region A has a DCS of 90 (High Confidence), while Region B is 45 (Estimate)." This transparency builds trust rather than damaging it. We instrument this to answer: "Does low confidence in smaller vendors skew the aggregate report?" If the aggregate trend holds even when low-DCS vendors are excluded, the insight is robust.

**3. Retention Signal: Supplier Engagement Rate**
*Definition:* The percentage of invited suppliers who not only upload data but correct flagged errors within 7 days.
*Rationale:* In a supply chain model, our "users" are also the suppliers. If suppliers disengage due to complex reporting, our data integrity collapses. High engagement correlates directly with improved DCS over time.

**Implementation Strategy for February Launch**
We will not instrument "everything." We will track only events that answer specific questions:
*   *Question:* Do clients trust the dashboard enough to share it with their board?
    *   *Event:* `report_exported_to_pdf` (filtered by DCS > 70).
*   *Question:* Does data quality improve as clients use the tool?
    *   *Event:* `supplier_data_correction_submitted`.

**Addressing the Data Integrity Risk**
We will not fabricate benchmarks for "acceptable" error rates. Instead, we will compare each client's current DCS against their own baseline from the pilot. The narrative to prospects is not "perfect data," but "progressive assurance." We demonstrate ROI by showing how the platform moves suppliers from "Self-Reported" to "Verified" over quarters, reducing the risk premium associated with Scope 3 reporting.

**Immediate Next Steps**
1.  **Define the DCS Algorithm:** Finalize the weighting of verification methods (audit vs. estimate) by Nov 10 to ensure the February build calculates this dynamically.
2.  **Instrument the "Correction" Event:** Ensure the engineering team tags `supplier_data_correction_submitted` with region and vendor size to analyze if smaller vendors are the primary source of friction.
3.  **Pilot Re-analysis:** Re-run the pilot data excluding vendors with a projected DCS < 50. If the "10% reduction" claim holds, we have a defensible marketing message; if it collapses, we must adjust the value proposition before launch.

By focusing on the quality of verified actions rather than the quantity of raw data, we turn our biggest weakness (data gaps) into a managed variable, proving credibility through transparency.

*Note: All metric thresholds must be derived from the February pilot baseline; no external industry benchmarks will be cited to avoid misalignment with client-specific supply chains.*
*Privacy compliance for supplier identifiers must be routed to the legal team before instrumentation begins.*
