### Diagnosis Plan: Dissecting the Churn Signal

**Status:** The root cause is currently **UNVERIFIED**. The 18% rise in zero-active organizations and the 4-minute drop in session duration are strong signals of value leakage, but without segmenting this data, we cannot distinguish between a product failure, a pricing mismatch, or natural project completion. Do not attribute this to market shifts or competitors yet; those are hypotheses to test, not conclusions.

**Immediate Investigation Steps (This Week):**

1.  **Segment the "Zero-Active" Cohort by Tenure and Plan**
    *   **Action:** Query your analytics to split the 18% increase into: (a) New customers (<90 days) vs. Mature customers, and (b) Enterprise tiers vs. lower tiers.
    *   **Why:** If the spike is in *new* enterprise customers, this is likely an **activation/onboarding failure** (they never found value). If it is *mature* customers, this suggests **retention decay** (value eroded over time) or **project completion**. If it is isolated to a specific pricing tier, it may be a **pricing/value mismatch**.
    *   **Decision Trigger:** If >60% of the churn is from customers <90 days old, the root cause is onboarding, not pricing.

2.  **Correlate Session Duration Drop with Specific Features**
    *   **Action:** Break down the 4-minute session duration drop by feature usage (e.g., video calls, file sharing, task boards). Did the drop happen everywhere, or only in core workflows?
    *   **Why:** A uniform drop suggests a **technical outage** or a **UX regression** (e.g., a slow load time causing early exits). A drop only in specific features suggests those features are no longer meeting needs or have become unusable.
    *   **Decision Trigger:** If the drop correlates exactly with a recent deployment date, treat this as a **product bug/incident** first.

3.  **Validate the "Project Completion" Hypothesis**
    *   **Action:** Cross-reference the churned organizations with their known project lifecycles (if available in your CRM) or survey 5–10 of the churned accounts immediately.
    *   **Why:** B2B tools often see natural churn when specific projects end. If these organizations had defined end-dates in Q1, this is expected behavior, not a crisis.
    *   **Decision Trigger:** If churned accounts had no defined end-date or renewed contracts recently, reject the "project ending" hypothesis.

4.  **Rule Out Technical Observability Issues**
    *   **Action:** Check system health logs (error rates, latency) for the period starting March 1st specifically for enterprise clusters.
    *   **Why:** You mentioned a possible missed outage. If error rates spiked for enterprise IPs while global metrics looked fine, this is a **silent failure**.
    *   **Decision Trigger:** If error rates >1% or latency >2s for enterprise segments during the drop, the cause is technical reliability.

**Recommended Next Step for CEO Update:**
Present a **"Segmented Leak Report"** rather than a single churn number. State: *"We have isolated the 18% increase to [Segment X]. Our leading hypothesis is [Onboarding/Bug/Project End] based on [Data Point Y]. We are validating this via [Specific Query/Survey] and will have a confirmed root cause by [Date]."* Do not commit to a pricing change or market pivot until the segment is identified.
