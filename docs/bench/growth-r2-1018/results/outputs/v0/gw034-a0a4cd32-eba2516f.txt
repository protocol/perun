### North Star Metric: "Value-Weighted Migration Events"
Reject "successful API calls per day" as your North Star; it is a vanity metric that penalizes high-value enterprise users who process massive datasets infrequently. Instead, define your North Star as **Value-Weighted Migration Events**, calculated as: *(Number of Successful Migrations) × (Complexity Weight)*. The "Complexity Weight" must be derived from the **data volume processed** or **schema complexity** per job, not the request count. This ensures one massive enterprise migration counts more than 1,000 small test runs, aligning the metric with actual customer value rather than network noise.

### Balanced Scorecard Draft (Enterprise Tier)
To satisfy the VP of Sales and track churn risk, structure your dashboard into three layers. Do not present uptime alone; it is a hygiene factor, not a value driver.

**1. Outcome Metrics (The "Why" – Primary for ROI)**
*   **Engineering Hours Saved:** The direct business outcome. Estimate this by multiplying *Data Volume Processed* by an industry-standard baseline for manual migration speed (e.g., "X GB migrated = Y hours saved"). This answers the ROI question directly.
*   **Time-to-Production:** The average duration from migration start to successful completion. Enterprise clients care about speed of delivery, not just throughput.

**2. Input Metrics (The "How" – Leading Indicators)**
*   **Active Core Accounts:** Count teams performing at least one *weighted* migration event per week (fixing your 120/500 activation leak).
*   **Data Volume Processed (TB/Week):** A raw proxy for trust and dependency. If this drops for a top-tier client, churn risk spikes regardless of call count.

**3. Guardrail Metrics (The "Risk" – Reliability)**
*   **Critical Error Rate:** Percentage of migrations failing due to platform errors (exclude user configuration errors).
*   **P95 Latency for Large Jobs:** Specifically track performance for jobs >1GB. General uptime % is insufficient; you must prove performance under heavy load.

### Diagnostic Questions for Pilot Customers
Do not ask generic satisfaction questions. Ask these specific questions to calibrate your "Complexity Weight" and validate the "Hours Saved" model:
1.  "For your last major migration, how many engineering hours would this have consumed if done manually?" (Validates the ROI formula).
2.  "What specific schema complexity factors (e.g., number of foreign keys, data types) make a migration 'hard' for your team?" (Defines the weighting algorithm).
3.  "At what point does a delay in migration completion block your release cycle?" (Defines the SLA threshold for the dashboard).
