**Recommendation: Adopt "Verified Dollars Distributed to Beneficiaries" as your 30-Day North Star.**

Do not optimize for "Total Dollars Raised" (Volume) or "Average Donation Size" (Value) in isolation. In a crisis, cash sitting in your platform is a liability, not an asset. Your North Star must be the rate at which funds move from donor intent to verified relief on the ground. This metric forces alignment between engineering (speed of payout rails), marketing (messaging around immediate impact), and operations (verification throughput).

**Analysis of Strategic Tensions**

**1. Volume (Total Dollars Raised)**
*   **Pros:** Maximizes immediate cash flow; satisfies the urgent emotional need of donors to "do something"; easy to communicate to the board as a headline number.
*   **Cons:** Creates a "vanity trap." Your data already shows the danger: transactions doubled while average donation dropped 42% ($55 to $32). If you optimize only for volume, you risk accumulating a backlog of unverified small donations that cannot be distributed quickly. This creates a false sense of success while actual relief stalls. It ignores the operational cost of processing high volumes of low-value transactions.

**2. Value (Average Donation Size / LTV)**
*   **Pros:** Protects long-term platform health and donor retention; ensures unit economics remain positive; reduces processing friction.
*   **Cons:** Dangerous during a crisis. Prioritizing high-value donors now may alienate the surge of micro-donors motivated by immediate empathy. Optimizing for average size often involves friction (upsells, recurring prompts) that slows down the checkout process, directly contradicting the need for speed. Focusing on retention (a long-term metric) is secondary when the immediate goal is saving lives.

**3. Speed (Time-to-Distribution)**
*   **Pros:** Directly correlates with mission impact; builds trust with beneficiaries and donors who want to see immediate results; clears the balance sheet liability.
*   **Cons:** Without a quality guardrail, rushing distribution can lead to fraud or misallocation, causing reputational damage that destroys long-term health. Speed alone does not account for the *amount* moved.

**Why "Verified Dollars Distributed" Resolves the Conflict**
This metric synthesizes the three tensions. It requires **Volume** (you must raise it to distribute it), respects **Value** (you must verify it to count it), and demands **Speed** (it measures the flow, not the stock).

Your drop in average donation size suggests a shift in donor persona from "planned giver" to "impulse responder." Chasing the old $55 average is fighting the current reality. Instead, engineer your funnel to convert this high volume of $32 donations into distributed aid as fast as possible. If your distribution pipeline cannot handle the surge, your "Total Raised" number is misleading leadership about your actual effectiveness.

**Immediate Actions for the Board**
1.  **Instrument the Flow:** Stop measuring "Signups" or "Total Raised" as the primary dashboard header. Replace it with a real-time counter of "Dollars Verified and Sent."
2.  **Define the Guardrail:** Set a fraud/error rate threshold (e.g., <0.5%). If speed increases but errors breach this, the North Star reading becomes invalid.
3.  **Align Engineering:** Priority shifts from "checkout optimization" to "payout automation and verification throughput."

**Note:** Do not fabricate a "good" distribution speed benchmark; compare your current daily distribution rate against your own baseline from last week to measure improvement. Ensure user identifiers used for tracking distribution remain pseudonymous to comply with privacy standards.
