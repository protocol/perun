**Diagnostic Report: Localized Carousel Experiment (March 1–21)**

**Status:** `UNVERIFIED` causal claim. The hypothesis that "higher initial engagement predicts better retention" is contradicted by your own data (CTR +12%, Day-7 Retention -4%). This indicates a "false activation" or a negative downstream experience, not a measurement error. Do not iterate on the assumption that engagement equals value.

**Root Cause Hypotheses to Investigate**

1.  **The "Bait-and-Switch" Effect (Content Quality Mismatch)**
    The carousel successfully drove clicks (high CTR) but failed to deliver satisfying viewing sessions. Users may be clicking localized titles expecting specific quality or relevance, only to churn when the content does not meet expectations.
    *   *Investigation:* Correlate carousel clicks with **completion rates** and **session duration** for the clicked content. Compare abandonment rates of carousel-driven sessions vs. control group sessions. If completion drops significantly, the content recommendation logic is flawed, not the interface.

2.  **Cohort-Specific Friction (The "Older User" Confounder)**
    With older users comprising 30% of the base, the new interface may have introduced navigation friction that specifically impacts this demographic, masking gains in younger segments.
    *   *Investigation:* Slice the Day-7 retention metric by **age cohort** (e.g., <40 vs. 40+). Isolate the retention delta for the 40+ group in the treatment arm. If the drop is concentrated entirely in this segment, the issue is usability/accessibility, not content quality. If the drop is uniform across ages, the issue is likely content relevance.

3.  **Cannibalization of Core Value**
    The carousel may be diverting attention from high-retention "core actions" (e.g., continuing a watched series) to low-retention discovery behavior. Users are busy clicking new things but not finishing what they started.
    *   *Investigation:* Measure the **ratio of new content starts vs. continued series plays** for the treatment group. Check if the "time to first play" increased (indicating decision paralysis) or if the "return rate" within the same session decreased.

**Required Data Queries for This Week**

To decide between killing or iterating, run these three specific analyses on your existing analytics platform. Do not collect new data yet; query what you have.

*   **Query A (Content Validation):** Filter for users who clicked the carousel. Calculate the **percentage of those sessions that resulted in >50% content completion**. Compare this rate to the control group's completion rate for organic clicks.
    *   *Decision Trigger:* If completion is >10% lower in treatment, the content algorithm is the failure point.
*   **Query B (Demographic Slice):** Segment Day-7 retention by **age bracket** for Treatment vs. Control.
    *   *Decision Trigger:* If the 40+ cohort shows a retention drop >8% while <40 is flat or positive, the UI design is the failure point for that segment.
*   **Query C (Behavioral Flow):** Track the **next action** after a carousel click. Did the user watch the selected item, or did they bounce/exit the app immediately?
    *   *Decision Trigger:* High immediate bounce rates post-click indicate the interface or metadata was misleading (clickbait).

**Conclusion on Current State**
The experiment result is valid as a signal of failure, but the *cause* remains `UNVERIFIED` without these slices. The increase in CTR is a vanity metric in this context because it did not translate to the North Star (Retention). Do not ship this feature to 100% of users. The decision to kill or iterate depends entirely on whether Query A (content) or Query B (usability) identifies the leak.
