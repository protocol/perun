**Recommendation: Do not kill the project yet; kill the current testing method and audience fit hypothesis.**

The data indicates a severe **activation-to-retention disconnect**, not necessarily a broken feature. Your activation rate (30%) is strong, suggesting the value proposition resonates initially. However, your Day-3 retention (6% of total invited, or ~7% of activated users) is a critical failure signal that outweighs the activation success. In growth analytics, we diagnose bottom-up: pouring more acquisition (forum invites) into a bucket with this level of leakage is wasted effort.

**Interpretation of the Signal**
The 6% retention figure is statistically meaningful enough to act upon *for this specific cohort*, even with N=150. It tells you that while the "hook" works, the "habit" does not form. This is rarely noise; it is usually a mismatch between the user's expectation (set by the forum context) and the product's immediate reality, or a lack of value in the core action after the first use.

Your assumption that the "niche audience is not a fit for a general tool" is likely correct, but it is an unverified hypothesis. The rapid churn suggests these specific users did not find the "aha moment" required to return. Before pivoting segments, you must verify if the feature delivers value to *anyone* consistently. If the feature itself lacks stickiness, a new segment will yield the same result.

**Immediate Actions for the CPO Review**
1.  **Stop the Forum Channel:** Cease acquisition from this forum immediately. The Cost of Acquisition is burning against near-zero Lifetime Value.
2.  **Instrument the "Core Action" Drop-off:** You currently know *that* they left, not *why*. Define the single event that correlates with long-term retention (e.g., "posted a second comment," "invited a peer"). Instrument this specifically. Did the 3 returners perform a different action than the 42 who churned? Without this event taxonomy, you are guessing.
3.  **Qualitative Loop:** With only 3 returning users and 42 churning, do not rely on quantitative significance testing yet. Interview 5 of the 42 who activated but churned. Ask: "What did you expect to happen after day one that didn't?" This is product discovery, not growth measurement, and is required before declaring the feature dead.

**Decision Framework**
Do not pivot segments until you have isolated whether the leak is **Product-Market Fit** (wrong audience) or **Product-Value Fit** (broken core loop).
*   **If** qualitative feedback reveals the forum users expected a different workflow entirely, **then** pivot the segment.
*   **If** feedback reveals they tried the core action and found it empty/broken, **then** the feature requires iteration before any new audience is tested.

**Verdict:** The signal is strong enough to halt the current experiment, but too incomplete to kill the project. The high activation proves interest; the low retention proves the current delivery fails to sustain it. Fix the retention leak or validate the audience mismatch qualitatively before allocating roadmap resources to a new segment.

*Note: No benchmarks were used; this analysis relies solely on your internal activation/retention delta. The "6%" is treated as a directional signal for this specific cohort, not a universal industry standard.*
