**Executive Summary: IronLink Q3 Growth & Measurement Strategy**

**The Verdict:** The 20% rise in submissions is an **unverified trend**, not a confirmed win. With a 45-day decision cycle and a 14-day attribution window, we are currently blind to the actual source of growth (content vs. seasonality) and the quality of those leads. Do not extend the window yet; first, fix the instrumentation to match the business reality.

**1. Redefine the North Star**
Stop optimizing for "Request-for-Quote (RFQ) submissions." In B2B, volume is a vanity metric if it doesn't lead to revenue.
*   **New North Star:** "Weekly Qualified Opportunities Created" (an RFQ that passes initial vetting *and$45k potential).
*   **Input Metrics:** Content engagement depth (time on spec sheets), supplier response time, and vetting pass rate.
*   **Action:** Immediately tag every RFQ with its source and initial deal size estimate. If the 20% volume spike consists of low-quality leads, the North Star will remain flat, revealing the content strategy's true impact.

**2. Fix the Attribution Seam (The 45-Day Reality)**
Your current 14-day window is structurally broken for a 45-day sales cycle. It attributes late conversions to "Direct" or "Unknown," hiding the value of your content.
*   **Solution:** Do not aggregate quarters (too slow). Instead, implement **cohort-based attribution**. Tag the user at the *first* touchpoint (content view) with a persistent, pseudonymous ID. Measure conversion rates based on the "Week of First Touch" cohort, regardless of when the deal closes.
*   **Result:** You can evaluate Q3 content effectiveness in Q4 without waiting for the full sales cycle to close for every single lead.

**3. Statistical Rigor with Sparse Data (N=150/week)**
Standard A/B testing (p<0.05) will fail here; you cannot achieve significance on weekly traffic with long cycles.
*   **Method:** Abandon fixed-horizon significance testing for now. Use **Bayesian estimation** or **Sequential Testing** with wide confidence intervals to detect directional shifts rather than binary "wins."
*   **Guardrail:** Monitor "Supplier Response Rate." If RFQ volume rises but suppliers stop responding (due to lead fatigue or low quality), stop the campaign immediately. This is your early warning system before revenue data arrives.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the "Qualified Opportunity" rate drops below your Q2 baseline while raw RFQs rise, the content strategy is attracting noise. Cut it.

**This Week's Steps:**
1.  **Instrument:** Add a hidden field to the RFQ form capturing the `first_touch_campaign` and `first_touch_date` to enable cohort analysis.
2.  **Query:** Run a manual cohort report: "Of the RFQs submitted in Week 1 of Q3, how many converted to a qualified opportunity by Week 7?" Compare this to the same cohort from Q2.
3.  **Decide:** If the qualified rate is flat or down despite the 20% volume increase, pause the new content strategy. If up, double down.

**Note:** We cannot determine if the spike is seasonal without comparing this cohort data to the same weeks in the previous year. Please provide last year's weekly RFQ counts for a true baseline comparison.
**Note:** Ensure the persistent user ID used for cohort tracking is pseudonymized to comply with privacy standards for B2B data.
