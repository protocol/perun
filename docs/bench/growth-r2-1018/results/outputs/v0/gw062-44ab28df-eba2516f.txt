### Proposed Metrics for Enterprise Customer Health

To distinguish between feature adoption gaps and pricing sensitivity, replace "Total Signatures Sent" with these three metrics. Each is designed to be pulled from your existing event logs and CRM data without new instrumentation, focusing on *how* the value is delivered rather than *how much* volume exists.

**1. Workflow Embedding Rate (Adoption vs. Utility)**
*Definition:* The percentage of signed documents that originate from an automated workflow (API, template merge, or bulk send) versus a manual, one-off upload.
*Rationale:* High transactional volume driven by manual uploads indicates a "tool" usage pattern, which is highly replaceable and price-sensitive. High volume driven by embedded workflows indicates a "platform" usage pattern, where the software is woven into the client's operations. If your churned accounts had a low Embedding Rate despite high signature counts, the issue is **feature adoption**; they are using the product superficially and will leave for a cheaper competitor. If embedded users are churning, the issue may be **pricing** or specific workflow friction.

**2. Multi-Stakeholder Activation Depth (Stickiness)**
*Definition:* The average number of unique internal user roles (e.g., Legal, Sales, HR, Procurement) per account that have initiated a signing action in the last 30 days.
*Rationale:* In B2B, single-department usage is a churn risk; if the Sales team leaves, the contract goes with them. Deep integration requires cross-functional reliance. A decline in this metric precedes renewal failure because it signals the product is failing to expand its internal footprint. If renewing accounts show high depth while churned accounts show low depth (even with high total signatures), the problem is **adoption breadth**, not price. This metric directly informs Customer Success directors to shift check-in calls from "How many docs did you send?" to "Which teams are still manually emailing PDFs?"

**3. Time-to-First-Value (TTFV) for New Workflows (Integration Health)**
*Definition:* The median time elapsed between a customer creating a new template/workflow and the first successful signature generated via that specific automation.
*Rationale:* This measures the friction of deep integration. A long TTFV suggests the enterprise client is struggling to configure the tool into their complex legacy systems. If churned accounts exhibited high TTFV prior to cancellation, the root cause is **implementation friction** (adoption), leading to perceived low value regardless of price. If TTFV is low (integration was easy) but they still churned, the conversation shifts to **pricing sensitivity** or lack of ongoing strategic value. This helps CS directors identify at-risk accounts *during* the onboarding phase rather than waiting for renewal signals.

**Implementation Note:**
Do not benchmark these against industry averages; compare each account's current performance against its own 90-day baseline to identify negative trends. Present these metrics to your directors with a directive to audit the top 10 churned accounts: if their "Workflow Embedding Rate" was below 20%, the playbook must shift from pricing discounts to technical integration support.
