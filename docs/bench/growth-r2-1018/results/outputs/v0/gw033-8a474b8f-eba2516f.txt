**Recommendation: Optimize bidding for "Verified 18-Month Cohort Value," not raw Acquisition Cost (CAC) or theoretical Lifetime Value (LTV).**

Your current 8% repeat purchase rate invalidates the finance team's assumption that sofa buyers reliably return for chairs within 18 months. Optimizing for low CAC today will likely scale one-time buyers, while optimizing for a theoretical LTV based on unproven assumptions risks overspending on acquisition with no return. You must treat the repeat purchase rate as **UNVERIFIED** until proven by cohort data, not finance models.

**Immediate Trade-off Analysis for Tomorrow's Meeting:**
1.  **The Risk of Low CAC:** If you bid purely for lowest cost, you attract price-sensitive, one-time shoppers. Given your current 8% retention, this maximizes immediate cash flow but destroys long-term unit economics.
2.  **The Risk of Projected LTV:** Bidding against a hypothetical LTV (assuming >50% repeat rates) is dangerous. If the actual rate stays at 8%, you will overpay for every customer, burning the remaining $300k budget faster than revenue can replenish it.
3.  **The Solution:** Shift the North Star from "Total Revenue" or "Raw Signups" to **"Retention of Core Action."** In your case, the core action is not the first purchase, but the *second* purchase within 18 months. Until you prove this happens, your bidding strategy must prioritize acquiring users who exhibit behaviors correlated with that second purchase, even if their initial CAC is higher.

**Data Points to Gather Before Final Commitment:**
Do not rely on aggregate averages. You need **cohort-specific data** to validate the repeat purchase assumption:
*   **Cohort Retention Curve:** Segment customers by purchase date (e.g., Q1 2023 vs. Q3 2023). What percentage of *sofa-only* buyers purchased add-ons at month 6, 12, and 18? If the curve flattens early, the 18-month assumption is false.
*   **Behavioral Correlates:** Identify events preceding a second purchase. Do repeat buyers engage with specific content (e.g., "care guides," "matching collection emails") or buy specific accessories (e.g., cushions) within the first 30 days? These are your leading indicators.
*   **Channel-Specific Retention:** Break down the 8% repeat rate by acquisition channel. If Facebook ads bring 5% repeaters but organic search brings 15%, your bidding strategy must favor the latter, even if the initial CAC is higher.
