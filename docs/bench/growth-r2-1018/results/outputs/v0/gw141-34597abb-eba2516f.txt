**Top Five Behaviors to Measure Immediately**

1.  **Week-2 Core Action Retention:** The percentage of users who complete a *second* video consultation within 14 days of sign-up.
    *   *Interpretation:* This is your true North Star. High sign-ups with low Week-2 retention indicates your video quality or triage flow fails to deliver repeat value, regardless of marketing spend. Do not optimize acquisition until this stabilizes.

2.  **Activation "Aha" Completion:** The percentage of new users who successfully connect to a vet and complete a call lasting >3 minutes within 24 hours of sign-up.
    *   *Interpretation:* This validates your core assumption. If users drop off before the 3-minute mark, video quality or booking friction is blocking value delivery. Compare this rate against your "total calls" metric; a high drop-off here means your current tracking is masking failure.

3.  **Subscription Conversion by Interaction Type:** The conversion rate to paid subscription for users who used *video* vs. those who only used *chat/triage* during their first session.
    *   *Interpretation:* This directly answers your steering committee's decision. If chat-only users convert at equal or higher rates than video users, your assumption that "video quality is paramount" is false, and resources should shift to triage efficiency.

4.  **Booking-to-Call Success Rate:** The percentage of initiated appointment bookings that result in a connected call without user abandonment or technical error.
    *   *Interpretation:* This isolates "booking speed" and technical reliability. A low rate here suggests friction in the handoff process, which may matter more to users than raw video resolution.

5.  **Feature Utilization Depth:** The average number of distinct video features (e.g., screen share, file upload, multi-person call) used per successful session.
    *   *Interpretation:* Low utilization suggests the extra video capabilities are unnecessary complexity. If most successful outcomes happen with basic video only, advanced features are cost centers, not value drivers.

**How to Interpret Without Historical Data**
Since you lack benchmarks, do not ask "Is 20% good?" Instead, establish a **baseline trend** starting March 18th. Compare each day's metrics to the previous day and the weekly average. A declining trend in *Activation Completion* or *Week-2 Retention* while *Sign-ups* rise is a critical warning signal of a leaky bucket. For the April 1st meeting, present these internal trends rather than external comparisons. If *Subscription Conversion* is significantly higher for one interaction type (Video vs. Chat) after just two weeks of data, that divergence is your signal to pivot resources.

**Note:** Stop tracking "page views" for this decision; they are vanity metrics that rise even when the product fails. Ensure all user tracking uses a pseudonymous stable ID to link sessions without violating privacy compliance.
