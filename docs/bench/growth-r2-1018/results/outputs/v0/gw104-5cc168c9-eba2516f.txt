**Diagnosis: Your data collection is likely intact; the "noise" is a signal of shared-device usage patterns and network intermittency, not a bug.**

The fluctuation you describe (60% drops, 200% spikes) uncorrelated with weather forecasts strongly suggests your current **North Star metric (Daily Active Users)** is a vanity metric in this specific context. In rural Ethiopia, "Daily Active Users" often measures "devices connected," not "farmers served." When one farmer shares a phone with five neighbors, your DAU drops by 80% while your actual value delivered remains constant. Conversely, a spike may represent a single community leader downloading forecasts for a whole village, not organic growth.

**Immediate Actions to Isolate Signal:**

1.  **Redefine the Metric (Stop counting devices):**
    Stop optimizing for DAU immediately. Switch your primary measurement to **"Weekly Active Households"** or **"Forecast Requests per Village Cluster."**
    *   *Action:* Group events by a stable, pseudonymous identifier (e.g., a hashed SIM card ID or a generated household ID if login exists) rather than session IDs. If you lack this, instrument a "Household ID" prompt at the next app open.
    *   *Why:* This smooths out the "shared device" noise. One device used by five people on different days should count as one consistent unit of value, not five erratic spikes.

2.  **Segment by Network Quality (The "Offline" Factor):**
    The 60% drops likely correlate with local network tower outages or electricity failures, not app bugs.
    *   *Action:* Instrument an event called `connection_attempt_result` with properties `network_type` (2G/3G/4G/Offline) and `error_code`. Cross-reference your usage drops with local telecom outage reports or electricity grid schedules, not just weather.
    *   *Why:* If usage drops when the network is down but users return immediately when it's up, your retention is fine; your infrastructure dependency is the variable.

3.  **Validate the "Core Action" Retention:**
    Ignore the spikes. Focus strictly on **Retention of the Core Action**: Did the user who requested a forecast on April 5th return to check the outcome or request a new one within 7 days?
    *   *Action:* Run a cohort analysis on users who performed `forecast_request` between April 5–10. Measure how many performed `forecast_request` again in the following 7 days.
    *   *Why:* If retention is stable despite the DAU volatility, your product value is secure, and the server capacity planning should be based on *peak concurrent household load*, not average DAU.

**Verdict for Server Capacity Planning:**
Do not size servers based on the April 5–25 average or peak DAU. Size them based on the **maximum concurrent "Forecast Requests" per minute** observed during the spikes, assuming those spikes represent legitimate community aggregation. The "noise" is actually your system handling bursty, communal usage correctly.
