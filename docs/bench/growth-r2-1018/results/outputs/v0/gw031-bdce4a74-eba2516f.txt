**Recommendation: Adopt "Gross Merchandise Value (GMV) of Completed Jobs" as your North Star Metric.**

"Total completed repairs" is a vanity metric in your context; it rises even if you only facilitate low-value bulb replacements, decoupling growth from actual business health and revenue. GMV aligns directly with the value delivered to both sides of the marketplace: homeowners get significant problems solved, and technicians earn meaningful income. Since you hypothesize that higher job value drives six-month retention, GMV is the only candidate that tests this hypothesis. If GMV rises while retention falls, your hypothesis is false, and you will know immediately. If you optimize for volume, you may fill the dashboard with noise while the business bleeds on low-margin transactions.

**Framework for Decision & Leading Indicators**

Do not choose based on opinion; choose based on which metric correlates with **Retention of the Core Action**. Read your funnel bottom-up. Before scaling acquisition or volume, verify that users who complete high-GMV jobs return within 90 days at a higher rate than those completing low-GMV jobs.

1.  **Define the Core Action:** A "completed job" where the homeowner rates the service ≥4 stars and the technician is paid.
2.  **Segment by Value:** Cohort users by job value tiers (e.g., <$50, $50–$200, >$200).
3.  **Measure Retention First:** Calculate the percentage of users in each tier who book a second job within 6 months.
    *   *If high-GMV cohorts retain significantly better:* GMV is your North Star.
    *   *If retention is flat across tiers:* Volume may be acceptable, but re-evaluate your unit economics.
4.  **Input Metrics (The Levers):** Track these weekly to move GMV:
    *   **Activation Rate:** % of new signups who complete a job >$100 within 14 days.
    *   **Technician Fill Rate:** % of high-value job requests matched within 2 hours (supply constraint).
    *   **Repeat Booking Rate:** % of homeowners booking a second job within 90 days (demand health).
