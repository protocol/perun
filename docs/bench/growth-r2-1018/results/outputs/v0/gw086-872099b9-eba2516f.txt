**Primary Goal: Week-2 Retention of the "Fully Verified" Onboarding State**

Do not choose between speed (completion time) or accuracy (data quality) as your primary metric; choosing one invites the team to optimize for it at the expense of the other. Instead, define your North Star as the **percentage of new hires who reach a "Fully Verified" state within their first 14 days**.

A user is only "Fully Verified" when they have both **submitted** all required fields (speed) and those fields have **passed validation** without requiring HR admin correction (accuracy). This single metric captures the true business value: an onboarding process that is fast enough to satisfy employees but accurate enough to avoid costly downstream fixes for your enterprise clients.

**Supporting Input Metrics**
Track these two inputs to diagnose why the North Star moves:
1.  **Median Time-to-Submit:** Measures the friction in the user interface. If this rises, speed is the blocker.
2.  **First-Pass Acceptance Rate:** The percentage of submissions requiring zero manual corrections by HR. If this falls, accuracy is the blocker.

**Rationale for the B2B Pilot Context**
In a six-week enterprise pilot with only ~1,500 total users across five clients, vanity metrics like "total signups" or "average completion time" are dangerous. A fast completion time means nothing if the data is wrong and requires your client's HR team to spend hours fixing it, which will destroy customer satisfaction and kill renewal chances. Conversely, perfect accuracy achieved over three days frustrates new hires and increases support tickets.

By measuring the **retention of the verified state**, you align with the client's ultimate goal: a workforce that is ready to work with compliant data. This metric forces the product team to balance the trade-off internally rather than exposing it to the client. It also respects the pilot constraints; with a small sample size, you cannot rely on broad satisfaction surveys alone. You need a behavioral signal that proves the module works before the six-week window closes.

**This Week's Steps**
1.  **Instrument the "Verified" Event:** Define the exact database state that constitutes "Fully Verified" (all fields present + validation flags clear) and ensure your analytics can track this specific transition per user.
2.  **Establish the Baseline:** Before August 12th, confirm you can currently measure the "time-to-complete" and "error-rate" separately so you can calculate the combined "Verified" rate manually if automated tracking isn't ready.
3.  **Set the Pilot Tripwire:** Agree with the CEO that if the "Fully Verified" rate is below 80% by Week 3, the pilot pauses for a hotfix, regardless of how fast users are submitting forms.

*Note: Do not fabricate a "good" retention benchmark; compare your Week 3 results strictly against your own Week 1 baseline. Ensure user identifiers used for this tracking are pseudonymous to comply with data privacy standards.*
