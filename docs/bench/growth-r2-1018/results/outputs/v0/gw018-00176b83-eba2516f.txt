**Recommendation: Optimize for Completion Rate, not Session Duration.**

For a structured course like 'Data Basics,' **Completion Rate** is the superior North Star metric for predicting long-term success and renewal. Session duration is a vanity metric in this context; while 25 minutes appears high, coupled with an 18% completion rate, it strongly indicates friction, confusion, or students getting stuck rather than "deep engagement." In learning products, value is realized when a user finishes a module or course and achieves the learning outcome, not merely when they spend time on the platform.

Your historical baseline confirms this: a drop from 30% to 18% completion is a critical retention leak. Pouring marketing effort into acquisition while 82% of users fail to finish the core product is inefficient. The high session time likely represents users struggling to navigate content or re-watching segments due to lack of clarity, which correlates negatively with satisfaction and renewal.

**Immediate Action Plan (This Week):**

1.  **Instrument the "Stuck" Event:** Do not guess where users drop off. Define and instrument a specific event for "Last Active Module" and "Exit Point." Map the funnel step-by-step (Module 1 → Module 2 → ... → Certificate). Identify the exact lesson where the drop-off spikes. This answers the question: "Is there a specific conceptual barrier causing the exit?"
2.  **Validate the "Offline" Hypothesis:** Your open question about PDF downloads is a data gap, not a benchmark. Instrument a `content_download` event immediately. Create a cohort analysis comparing users who *only* watch video vs. those who *download and watch*. If the "downloaders" have high completion rates offline but low video completion, your video player is the friction point, not the content quality. If they also drop out, the content pacing is the issue.
3.  **Redefine "Engagement":** Shift the team's definition of engagement from "Time Spent" to "Progress Made." A user finishing in 15 minutes is more successful than one lingering for 45 minutes without completing.

**Decision Logic:**
If the data shows users are downloading PDFs and completing the course objectives (verified via assessment scores or self-reporting), then optimize for multi-format accessibility. However, if users are spending 25 minutes and still dropping out without consuming alternative formats, the instructional design is failing to facilitate progress. Fix the completion leak first; only then does acquisition matter.
