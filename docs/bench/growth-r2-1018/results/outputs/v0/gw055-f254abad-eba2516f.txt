**Deliverable: Retention Measurement Plan for MelodyStream Social Feature**

Do not rely on the current 5% DAU lift or the session duration drop as final verdicts. The short test window (since Nov 10) and the conflicting signals (more users, less time) indicate a classic "novelty vs. value" ambiguity. To determine if this trade-off is acceptable for the board, you must shift from vanity metrics (raw DAU, average session length) to **cohort-based retention of the core value action**.

**1. Define the North Star and Input Metrics**
Your North Star is not "Daily Active Users" but **"Weekly Active Listeners completing a full track."** Session duration is a poor proxy for value here; a user finding a song in 30 seconds and leaving is higher value than a user idling for 20 minutes.
*   **Primary Metric:** Week-over-week retention of users who perform the **core action** (listening to ≥1 full track) after interacting with the social feature.
*   **Guardrail Metric:** Total listening hours per cohort. If DAU rises but total hours flatline or drop, the feature is cannibalizing core value.

**2. The Measurement Framework (Bottom-Up AARRR)**
Diagnose retention before acquisition. The 5% DAU increase is irrelevant if those users do not return in Week 2, 4, and 8.
*   **Cohort Definition:** Segment the 10,000 participants into "Social Exposed" vs. "Control" (if available) or pre-test baseline.
*   **Time-Based Measurements (The 3-Month View):**
    *   **Day 1, Day 7, Day 30, Day 90 Retention Curves:** Plot the percentage of users who return to listen to a full track on these specific days post-exposure. Compare the slope of the "Social" curve against your historical baseline.
    *   **Frequency of Core Action:** Measure the average number of *full tracks listened to per week* per user. Does the social group listen more frequently, even if sessions are shorter? High frequency + short duration often indicates a successful habit formation (quick hits), whereas low frequency + long duration may indicate passive background noise.
    *   **Churn Rate by Week:** Calculate the % of users who stop listening entirely after Week 1, Week 2, etc. If the social feature accelerates churn after the novelty fades (e.g., a sharp drop at Day 21), it is a net negative.

**3. Event Taxonomy to Instrument Immediately**
Stop measuring "session duration" as a primary success indicator. Instrument these specific events to answer named questions:
*   `social_share_clicked`: Did they engage with the feature?
*   `track_played_full`: Did they derive core value after the social interaction?
*   `return_visit_day_7`: Did they come back a week later without a social prompt?
*   **Question answered:** "Does social interaction predict higher Week-4 retention of core listening?"

**4. Executive Decision Criteria**
For the board, present the **Net Retention Lift**:
*   **Acceptable Trade-off:** If the "Social" cohort shows a higher Day-30 retention rate of *full track listens* compared to baseline, even with shorter sessions, the feature drives sustainable growth. The efficiency of value delivery has increased.
*   **Unacceptable Trade-off:** If Day-30 retention is flat or lower, and total listening hours per cohort have dropped, the 5% DAU bump is a novelty effect. The feature distracts from the core product without building loyalty.

**This Week's Step:**
Pull the Day-7 and Day-14 retention curves for the 10,000 participants, segmented by those who clicked "share" vs. those who only viewed the interface. If the "clickers" show a lower Day-14 return rate than non-clickers, halt expansion and investigate the friction; if higher, proceed to a 90-day cohort lock.

**Note:** The drop in session duration may signal increased efficiency (users finding music faster via social recs) rather than distraction; verify this by correlating session length with `track_played_full` events. Do not fabricate a "good" retention benchmark; compare these curves strictly against MelodyStream's historical pre-test baselines.
