**Decision: Adopt "Research Output Quality" (citations per active user) as the North Star Metric for the next six months, with "Weekly Active Users performing deep dives" as the primary input metric.**

Do not optimize for frequency of use. Your data indicates that while daily searchers renew 10% more often, diverse explorers drive the core value proposition: 30% more citations. For institutional clients who fund your revenue, research output quality is the retention driver, not login frequency. Optimizing for daily short searches risks creating a "vanity metric" trap where dashboards look busy (high logins) while the actual business value (institutional renewals based on research impact) stagnates or declines.

**The Measurement Strategy**

1.  **Redefine the North Star:** Shift from "Daily Active Users" to **"Citations Generated per Active Institutional Seat."** This aligns directly with your clients' mission and your long-term revenue security. Frequency is a habit; citations are the *outcome* that justifies the contract.
2.  **Diagnose the Funnel Bottom-Up:** Before acquiring more users or pushing daily logins, verify retention of the *core value action*.
    *   *Question:* Do users who perform "deep dives" in Month 1 return to perform another deep dive in Month 2?
    *   *Action:* Instrument a specific event `deep_dive_session` (defined as >X topics explored or >Y papers viewed in one sitting) and measure its Week-2 and Month-2 retention cohorts. If this retention is strong, the "breadth" strategy is safe. If it is weak, fixing this leak is more urgent than driving daily frequency.
3.  **Resolve the Trade-off via Experimentation:** You fear breadth might reduce frequency. Do not guess; test.
    *   *Design:* Run an A/B test on a segment of users. Group A sees features promoting daily quick searches (current state). Group B sees features promoting topic exploration and diverse discovery.
    *   *Pre-commitment:* Define a fixed duration (e.g., 8 weeks) and a minimum sample size before starting. Do not peek at results on day 2.
    *   *Metrics:* Primary metric = `deep_dive_sessions per user`. Guardrail metric = `overall renewal probability` (or a proxy like `weekly_active_status`).
    *   *Success Criteria:* If Group B increases deep dives without causing the guardrail (renewal proxy) to drop below a statistically significant threshold, the "breadth" strategy wins. If frequency drops but citations rise significantly, the trade-off is acceptable because citations drive the institutional contract.

**This Week's Steps**
1.  **Define and Instrument:** Create the `deep_dive_session` event definition and ensure it captures the "diverse topics" behavior you value. Verify you can link this event to a stable, pseudonymous user ID to track cohorts over time.
2.  **Baseline Analysis:** Query your historical data to calculate the retention curve specifically for users who performed a `deep_dive_session` in their first week. Compare this to the retention of "daily searchers." If deep-divers retain equally well or better, your fear of losing revenue is unfounded.
3.  **Draft the Experiment Plan:** Write the one-page design for the A/B test described above, explicitly stating the primary metric, the guardrail, and the fixed end-date. Present this to the Board as the method to de-risk the strategic shift before full rollout.

Your roadmap should not choose between revenue and mission; by anchoring on citations, you align them. Frequency is only valuable if it leads to depth.
