**North Star Metric: Weekly Core-Action Retention Rate (Referred vs. Organic)**

Do not use "cumulative sign-ups" or "revenue" as your primary metric; these are vanity figures in this context because they rise even while your business leaks value. Your single North Star must be the **percentage of referred users who complete a "Meal Cooked" event in their second, third, and fourth weeks**, compared directly to the organic baseline.

"Sign-up" is not value; "cooking" is value. If a user signs up via referral but never cooks, they are not a customer; they are a cost center driven by discount arbitrage. To validate your assumption about commitment, you must instrument a specific event called `meal_cooked_confirmed` (triggered when a user marks a meal as prepared in the app or website). This event answers the specific question: *"Did the user derive core product value this week?"*

**The Narrative for the CFO**
The current 25% higher churn suggests a leak, but it does not explain the *cause*. By shifting focus to **Weekly Core-Action Retention**, you separate two distinct failure modes:
1.  **Low Engagement:** If referred users have significantly lower `meal_cooked_confirmed` rates than organic users *before* they churn, your assumption is correct. They are discount-seekers with low product commitment. The referral incentive is attracting the wrong profile, and funding should be paused or restructured to reward *cooking* rather than *signing up*.
2.  **Friction/Forgetfulness:** If referred users cook at the *same* rate as organic users but still churn at higher rates, the issue is not commitment. It is likely billing friction, forgotten cancellations, or a mismatch in the renewal notification flow. In this case, cutting the referral program would be a mistake; the fix lies in the retention lifecycle (e.g., better "skip a week" prompts), not acquisition.

**Measurement Plan**
Stop measuring "referral sign-ups" as a success metric immediately. Instead, build a cohort analysis comparing the **Week-2, Week-3, and Week-4 retention curves** of the March 15 referral cohort against the organic baseline.
