**Diagnosis: The "Curiosity Leak" Hypothesis**

Your data indicates a classic funnel decoupling: the redesign successfully optimized for **Acquisition** (clicks up 12%) but degraded **Activation/Conversion** (enrollments down 15.5% relative to baseline). The rise in clicks paired with a drop in final conversion suggests the new layout is generating "vanity traffic"—users intrigued by the visual prominence of the premium plan but deterred once they encounter the actual price or terms. This is not necessarily "lower-quality traffic"; it is likely the *same* user profile reacting to a mismatch between the promise of the landing page and the reality of the checkout page.

**Segmentation Strategy to Isolate Intent**

To distinguish between a layout failure and a pricing objection, segment your existing data by these three dimensions before collecting new information:

1.  **New vs. Returning Visitors:**
    *   *Logic:* If the conversion drop is isolated to **New Visitors**, the design is attracting curious browsers who lack prior intent. If **Returning Visitors** (who already know the price) are also dropping off, the issue is likely friction in the new checkout flow or confusing copy, not "curiosity."
    *   *Action:* Compare the pre-Feb 20 and post-Feb 20 conversion rates for these two cohorts separately.

2.  **Traffic Source Quality:**
    *   *Logic:* Did the redesign change how different ad channels perform? If organic search conversion held steady but paid social conversion plummeted, the new design may be misaligned with the expectations set by your ads.
    *   *Action:* Break down the conversion rate drop by channel. A uniform drop suggests a site-wide issue; a skewed drop points to a message mismatch.

3.  **Device-Type Behavior:**
    *   *Logic:* Making a plan "more prominent" often breaks mobile layouts or obscures critical details on smaller screens.
    *   *Action:* Segment by desktop vs. mobile. If mobile conversion crashed while desktop remained stable, the "prominence" feature is likely creating UI friction, not intent issues.

**Additional Context Required**

You cannot solve this with aggregate rates alone. You need to instrument or extract two specific behavioral signals to confirm the hesitation point:

*   **Time-on-Page & Scroll Depth on the Pricing Section:** If users are clicking through but spending significantly less time on the checkout page before bouncing, they are rejecting the price immediately. If they spend *more* time but don't convert, they are confused by the copy or terms.
*   **Exit-Point Heatmaps:** Identify exactly where users drop off in the checkout flow. Is it immediately upon seeing the total cost, or after interacting with specific form fields? This distinguishes between "price shock" and "usability friction."

**Recommendation for the Marketing Director**

Do not revert the design yet, as the 12% increase in top-of-funnel interest is a valuable asset. Instead, **iterate on the checkout copy** to align with the new expectations set by the landing page. The data suggests the "promise" (landing page) is working, but the "delivery" (checkout) is causing hesitation.

**Immediate Next Step:** Run a cohort analysis comparing "Time on Checkout Page" pre- and post-redesign. If time-on-page decreased, the price is the barrier; if it increased, the copy is confusing. Use this finding to justify retaining the ad spend while tweaking the checkout messaging to close the leak.

*Note: All comparisons must be made against your own pre-Feb 20 baseline; do not benchmark against industry averages. Ensure user identifiers used for cohort tracking remain pseudonymous to comply with privacy standards.*
