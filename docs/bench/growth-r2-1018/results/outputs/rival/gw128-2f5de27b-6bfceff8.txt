**Analysis: Search Relevance Experiment Divergence**

The divergence between increased time-on-site (+35%) and flat click-through rates (CTR) suggests the new algorithm is altering *how* users interact with the Search Results Page (SERP), but not necessarily driving them toward your primary goal of content consumption. This behavior typically indicates one of two opposing realities:

1.  **The "Answer Engine" Success (Positive):** The new model surfaces highly relevant snippets, summaries, or direct answers on the SERP. Users find their solution immediately without needing to click through. In this scenario, time-on-site increases because users are reading the high-quality result, but CTR drops or stays flat because the "click" is no longer necessary. This is a satisfaction win but a traffic loss for destination pages.
2.  **The "Confusion" Failure (Negative):** The results appear relevant based on keywords (increasing initial engagement time) but fail to provide a clear path forward. Users may be scanning repeatedly, confused by the layout, or unable to distinguish the best link. Here, time-on-site represents friction, not satisfaction.

**Recommended Follow-Up Analyses**

To distinguish between satisfaction and confusion before making a deployment decision, execute these four analyses:

*   **Zero-Click Search Rate:** Calculate the percentage of sessions where a search occurs but no subsequent page view happens within 30 seconds. If this rate spiked alongside time-on-site, users are likely finding answers directly on the SERP. Cross-reference this with query type (e.g., navigational vs. informational).
*   **SERP Scroll Depth & Heatmaps:** Analyze how far users scroll on the results page.
    *   *Shallow scroll + High time:* Suggests deep reading of a top result (likely success).
    *   *Deep/Erratic scroll + High time:* Suggests hunting and confusion (likely failure).
*   **Query Reformulation Rate:** Measure how often users modify their search query within the same session without clicking a result. A spike here indicates the new results were insufficient, forcing users to try different keywords.
*   **Qualitative Session Replay:** Watch 20–30 recordings of users exhibiting this specific behavior (high time, zero clicks). Look for "rage clicks" on non-links, rapid scrolling up and down, or mouse hovering that indicates hesitation versus confident reading.

**Decision Framework**

*   **Deploy if:** Zero-click searches are high for informational queries, scroll depth is shallow, and reformulation rates are low. This confirms the algorithm is efficiently solving user problems. You must then redefine "success" metrics to value "resolved queries" over "page views."
*   **Do Not Deploy if:** Reformulation rates are high, scroll depth is erratic, or session replays show hesitation. This confirms the interface is confusing or the snippets are misleading.

**Additional Data to Gather**

Implement a "Was this helpful?" thumbs-up/down micro-survey specifically on the SERP for the test variant. This provides direct intent data that behavioral metrics cannot fully resolve.

*Note: Ensure your analytics window matches the two-week test period exactly when recalculating these rates to avoid skewing data with pre-test behavior.*
*Do not rely on average time-on-site alone; it is a vanity metric without the context of click intent.*
