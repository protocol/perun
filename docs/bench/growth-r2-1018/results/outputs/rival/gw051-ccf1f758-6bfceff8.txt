### Recommended Data Breakdowns for JobFind Dublin Funnel Analysis

To validate your hypothesis that the identity verification step is causing friction, specifically for users without local bank accounts, request the following specific data slices from your data team. These metrics are designed to isolate the bottleneck before your Friday executive meeting.

**1. Step-Level Drop-Off Within Identity Verification**
Do not treat "Identity Verification" as a single black box. Request a micro-funnel breakdown of every distinct action within that section:
*   **Entry Rate:** Users entering the verification section vs. total funnel starters.
*   **Document Upload Initiation:** Users who click "Upload ID."
*   **Document Upload Success:** Users who successfully complete the upload.
*   **Bank Account Selection:** Users who reach the bank selection screen.
*   **Local vs. Non-Local Bank Selection:** Split users who select an Irish/UK bank vs. those selecting "Other/International" or abandoning at this specific dropdown.
*   **Verification Submission:** Users who hit final submit in this section.
*   **Exit Rate per Step:** Calculate the % drop between each of these specific micro-steps.

**2. Segmentation by User Attributes (The "Who")**
Cross-tabulate the drop-off points above with these dimensions to confirm if the pain point is specific to non-local users:
*   **Bank Country Selected:** Compare completion rates for users selecting local banks vs. international banks. If your hypothesis is correct, the drop-off should be negligible for local banks and massive (>60%) for international selections.
*   **Device Type:** Mobile vs. Desktop vs. Tablet. Uploading documents and navigating complex forms is often significantly harder on mobile. If mobile drop-off is 2x desktop, the UI may be the culprit, not just the policy.
*   **Traffic Source:** Organic search vs. Paid ads vs. Direct. Sometimes paid traffic (expecting a quick apply) has lower patience for friction than organic seekers.
*   **New vs. Returning:** Did returning users (who might have saved data) fare better?

**3. Time-Based Comparison (The "When")**
*   **Pre-Launch vs. Post-Launch:** Compare the completion rate of the *entire* application flow for the 2 weeks prior to October 12th against the period from October 12th to present.
*   **Day-of-Week Trends:** Ensure the drop-off isn't correlated with specific days (e.g., if verification services are slower on weekends).

**4. Error and Technical Metrics**
*   **Error Message Frequency:** Count occurrences of specific error codes related to document rejection or bank validation failures.
*   **Time-on-Step:** Average time spent on the bank selection screen. A spike in time followed by an exit indicates confusion or inability to find a matching option.

**What to Look For in the Report**
*   **The "Cliff":** Identify the single step with the highest percentage drop. If >50% of users selecting "International Bank" exit before the next step, your hypothesis is confirmed.
*   **Device Disparity:** If mobile users abandon the document upload step at 3x the rate of desktop users, the issue is likely UX complexity on small screens, requiring an immediate mobile-specific fix.
*   **Uniformity:** If the drop-off is uniform (e.g., 40% loss) across *all* bank types and devices, the issue may be the mere presence of the step (friction fatigue) rather than the specific content, suggesting a need to streamline the whole section or move it post-registration.

**Executive Summary Focus:** For Friday, lead with the comparison between "Local Bank Completion Rate" and "Non-Local Bank Completion Rate." This single metric directly validates or invalidates your core hypothesis regarding local account friction.

*Ensure your data team excludes test accounts and internal IPs from these figures to prevent skewing the conversion rates.*
