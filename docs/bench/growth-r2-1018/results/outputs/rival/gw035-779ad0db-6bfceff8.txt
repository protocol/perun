### Executive Summary: Checkout Flow A/B Test (March 1–15)

**Recommendation:** Do not approve a global launch yet. Proceed with a **mobile-only rollout** pending validation of the Average Order Value (AOV) drop. The current data presents a classic "volume vs. value" trade-off that requires segmentation to resolve before committing to a global change.

#### 1. Required Segmentation Analysis
To isolate the mobile effect and diagnose the AOV drop, run the following splits immediately:

*   **Device-Type Split (Mobile vs. Desktop):**
    *   Hypothesis: The conversion lift is driven entirely by mobile (60% of traffic), while desktop remains flat or negative.
    *   Action: Calculate conversion rate and AOV delta separately for mobile and desktop. If mobile shows high conversion lift with *stable* AOV, the global AOV drop is likely diluted by desktop friction or a specific desktop tracking error.
*   **Cart Size & Category Split:**
    *   Hypothesis: Removing survey steps reduced "pause time," causing users to skip impulse add-ons (e.g., accessories, warranties) that typically appear between checkout steps.
    *   Action: Compare the attachment rate of add-on products and the distribution of cart sizes (1 item vs. 2+ items) between control and variant.
*   **New vs. Returning User:**
    *   Hypothesis: Returning users navigate faster and benefit most from the simplified flow, while new users may need the survey context to build trust or discover products.
    *   Action: Segment metrics by user status to see if the AOV drop is concentrated among new customers.

#### 2. Critical Validation Steps
Before recommending a launch, rule out technical artifacts:

*   **Data Integrity Check:** Verify that the removal of survey steps did not accidentally strip UTM parameters or data layer variables used to calculate AOV. If the tracking script fires on a "survey complete" event that no longer exists, revenue might be under-reported rather than actually lost.
*   **Funnel Drop-off Analysis:** Examine where users abandon the cart in the variant. If they are leaving *later* in the process (e.g., at payment) compared to the control, the simplified flow may be attracting low-intent traffic that fails to convert fully, skewing AOV.
*   **Cohort Holdout:** If possible, keep 5% of mobile traffic on the old flow for another 48 hours to see if the AOV trend persists or was a two-week anomaly.

#### 3. Stakeholder Presentation Strategy
Present the findings as a **strategic optimization opportunity** rather than an inconclusive failure. Use this framework:

*   **Acknowledge the Trade-off Explicitly:** "The test successfully reduced friction, driving a 2.3% conversion lift. However, this speed appears to correlate with a $4.50 reduction in basket size, likely due to reduced exposure to impulse prompts."
*   **Propose a Phased Rollout:** "We recommend launching the simplified flow **only on mobile devices** immediately. This captures the conversion gain where friction matters most while protecting desktop AOV. We will monitor mobile AOV closely for 14 days."
*   **Define the Success Metric:** "Our goal is not just higher conversion, but higher *total revenue per session*. We will approve a global rollout only when mobile AOV stabilizes within 2% of the baseline."
*   **Quantify the Risk:** "A global launch today risks a net revenue loss if the AOV drop outweighs the volume gain. A mobile-only launch limits this risk to 60% of traffic while we engineer a solution to recover impulse buys (e.g., moving add-ons to the post-purchase page)."

This approach demonstrates command of the data, protects revenue, and offers a clear path forward without demanding absolute certainty before acting.
