### Proposed Success Metrics for Enterprise Customer Health

To distinguish between feature adoption gaps and pricing sensitivity, and to better predict renewal outcomes for contracts over €10,000, replace "Total Signatures Sent" with the following three metrics:

**1. Workflow Integration Depth (WID)**
*   **Definition:** The average number of distinct internal user roles (e.g., Legal, HR, Sales, Procurement) within a single customer account who have initiated or completed a signature request in the last 30 days.
*   **Rationale:** Total volume can be skewed by a single power user spamming documents, masking a lack of organizational buy-in. Renewals for enterprise contracts are rarely decided by one user; they require cross-departmental reliance. If volume is high but WID is low (e.g., only one department uses the tool), the account is vulnerable to churn if that specific champion leaves or if the tool is perceived as a niche utility rather than core infrastructure. A declining WID despite stable volume is a leading indicator of pricing sensitivity, as the perceived value is concentrated rather than distributed.

**2. Feature-Weighted Activity Score (FWAS)**
*   **Definition:** A composite score where basic actions (sending a doc) equal 1 point, but "sticky" enterprise features (API integrations, custom branding, advanced audit logs, bulk send, SSO usage) equal 5 points. Calculate the monthly average FWAS per account.
*   **Rationale:** This metric directly isolates the "feature adoption" variable. If an account has 45,000 signatures but a low FWAS, they are using SignFast as a commodity transactional tool, making them highly price-sensitive and likely to churn for a cheaper competitor. Conversely, a high FWAS indicates the customer has embedded SignFast into their specific workflows. Customers who configure custom branding or use the API face higher switching costs, making them less sensitive to price increases and more likely to renew regardless of raw volume fluctuations.

**3. Critical Path Completion Rate (CPCR)**
*   **Definition:** The percentage of signature workflows initiated by an enterprise account that are fully executed (signed by all parties and archived) within 48 hours of initiation.
*   **Rationale:** High volume with low completion speed suggests friction in the user experience or that the tool is being used for low-priority, non-critical documents. Enterprise customers pay a premium for speed and reliability in critical deals. If CPCR drops, it indicates the tool is failing to deliver on its core value promise of efficiency, regardless of how many documents are sent. A decline in CPCR often precedes churn because it signals that the tool is becoming a bottleneck rather than an accelerator, a qualitative failure that raw volume numbers completely obscure.

**Implementation Note:**
Direct Customer Success Directors to segment accounts by these new metrics immediately; accounts with high volume but low WID/FWAS are your highest immediate churn risks. These metrics shift the conversation from "How much did you use us?" to "How deeply are we embedded in your critical operations?"
