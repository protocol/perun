### Recommended Metrics for Driver Incentive Evaluation

To determine if the long-distance incentive program is sustainable, you must shift focus from **acceptance volume** to **net driver profitability**. The current 5% drop in hourly earnings suggests the incentive covers the trip fare but fails to compensate for the "deadhead" (empty return) time and extra fuel.

Implement the following three metric categories to balance volume with driver welfare before the city-wide rollout:

#### 1. Net Profitability Metrics (The "True Earnings")
These metrics reveal if drivers are actually making money after costs.
*   **Net Earnings Per Active Hour:** Calculate `(Trip Fare + Incentive - Estimated Fuel Cost) / (Trip Time + Return Time)`.
    *   *Why:* Your current "earnings per hour" likely divides income by total online time. This metric isolates the efficiency of the long-distance trip itself, including the unpaid return leg.
*   **Deadhead Ratio:** The percentage of kilometers driven without a passenger specifically after completing a long-distance incentive trip.
    *   *Why:* If this exceeds 40-50%, the incentive is effectively paying drivers to drive empty. You need to know the average distance drivers travel empty before securing their next fare.
*   **Contribution Margin per Trip:** `(Total Payout to Driver) - (Variable Costs: Fuel + Vehicle Wear)`.
    *   *Why:* Ensures the payout exceeds the driver's marginal cost. If this is lower than their average short-trip margin, they will eventually churn despite higher acceptance rates.

#### 2. Efficiency & Utilization Metrics
These measure how the incentive impacts overall fleet productivity.
*   **Re-booking Rate within 5km of Drop-off:** The percentage of drivers who secure a new passenger within 5km of the long-distance drop-off point within 30 minutes.
    *   *Why:* This validates your hypothesis about empty returns. If this rate is low, the drop-off zone is a "graveyard" for demand, and the incentive must include a "return guarantee" or higher base fare.
*   **Total Online Time vs. Paid Time Ratio:** Compare the time spent on incentivized trips versus standard trips.
    *   *Why:* Long trips might be locking drivers into low-yield hours, preventing them from taking multiple shorter, higher-frequency trips during peak demand.

#### 3. Driver Sentiment & Sustainability
Quantitative data must be paired with qualitative feedback to predict long-term retention.
*   **Incentive Participation Churn:** The percentage of the 1,200 pilot drivers who accepted one long-distance trip but refused subsequent ones.
    *   *Why:* High initial acceptance followed by refusal indicates drivers realized the trip was unprofitable after experiencing the return journey.
*   **Net Promoter Score (NPS) on Trip Profitability:** A simple post-trip survey asking: "Was this trip profitable for you?" (Yes/No/Barely).
    *   *Why:* Direct feedback on perceived fairness is often faster and more accurate than inferring satisfaction from earnings data alone.

### Strategic Recommendation for the Policy Team
Do not roll out the current formula city-wide. The 25% increase in acceptance is a "vanity metric" if it drives a 5,000 IDR/hour loss for partners.

**Immediate Action:** Adjust the payout formula to include a **dynamic return bonus**. Instead of a flat fee for acceptance, offer a guaranteed minimum earning rate that accounts for the average deadhead time in the specific drop-off zone. Re-run the pilot measuring **Net Earnings Per Active Hour** as the primary success criterion, aiming for parity with or a 10% premium over standard short-trip earnings.

*Note: Since you lack precise fuel data, use a standardized fuel-cost-per-km estimate based on average vehicle types in your fleet for immediate calculations.*
*Note: Prioritize the "Re-booking Rate" analysis; if drivers can't get return fares, the incentive structure must change from "acceptance-based" to "completion-and-rebooking-based."*
