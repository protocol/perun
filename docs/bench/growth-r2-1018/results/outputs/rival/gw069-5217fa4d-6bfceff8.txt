**Recommended Primary Goal Metric: "Validated Retention Rate"**

**Definition:** The percentage of new volunteers recruited during the August 1 drive who submit at least **three** map edits that pass community validation within their first **14 days** of signing up.

**Formula:**
`(Number of new volunteers with ≥3 validated edits in first 14 days) ÷ (Total new volunteers recruited) × 100`

**Target Benchmark:** Aim for **15%** (up from last year's effective retention of ~4%).

### Why This Metric Balances Your Needs

1.  **Filters for Quality (Accuracy):** By requiring edits to be "validated" (accepted by experienced mappers or automated quality checks), you exclude spam or erroneous data. This assures NGO partners that funding drives *usable* map data, not just noise.
2.  **Ensures Meaningful Engagement (Volume + Retention):** Requiring **three** edits distinguishes between a user who accidentally clicked "save" once and one who understood the workflow. Last year, 300 users submitted *something*, but only 50 stayed. A single edit is a trial; three edits indicate a habit forming.
3.  **Diagnoses the Drop-off:** The 14-day window is critical.
    *   If users sign up but make **zero** edits: The barrier to entry (technical setup) is too high.
    *   If users make **one** edit but not three: They likely lacked feedback or found the second step confusing.
    *   If users make **three+** edits: They have successfully onboarded.

### Implementation for Funding Proposals

Present this metric to partners as a shift from "Vanity Metrics" (total sign-ups) to "Value Metrics" (active, qualified contributors).

*   **Last Year's Reality:** 1,200 sign-ups → 25% activation (1 edit) → 4% retention (regulars).
*   **New Goal:** 1,200 sign-ups → **15% Validated Retention** (180 high-quality active mappers).

While the total number of "active" users might appear lower than the 300 who made one edit last year, the **quality and longevity** of these 180 users will be significantly higher, providing a more stable base for long-term mapping. This demonstrates fiscal responsibility by focusing resources on converting users into lasting contributors rather than chasing raw registration numbers.

To track this without complex tools, simply tag new users in your database with their signup date and count their validated edits at Day 14.

*Note: Ensure your validation team processes new user edits within 48 hours during the drive; delayed feedback is a primary cause of early drop-off.*
*Note: If "validation" is currently slow, use "submitted and not reverted" as a temporary proxy for the first month.*
