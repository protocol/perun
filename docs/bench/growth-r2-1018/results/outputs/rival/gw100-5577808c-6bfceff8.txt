# Experimental Design: Last-Minute Discount Elasticity Test

## Objective
Determine the optimal discount depth (10% vs. 20%) for bookings made within 48 hours of departure that maximizes total net revenue without significantly cannibalizing full-price bookings made >14 days prior.

## Methodology
**Design:** A/B/n Split-URL or Server-Side Experiment
**Duration:** February 1 – March 31 (8 weeks)
**Scope:** Five major domestic routes; traffic split randomly at the user session level.

### Test Variants
*   **Control (A):** Current standard 10% discount for <48h bookings.
*   **Variant (B):** Increased 20% discount for <48h bookings.
*   **Holdout (C):** No discount offered (5% of traffic) to measure true incremental lift vs. organic demand.

### Segmentation Strategy
To isolate business vs. leisure elasticity, we will stratify randomization and analyze results by:
1.  **Booking Window:** <48 hours (Target) vs. >14 days (Cannibalization Check).
2.  **Traveler Type:** Proxy identified via purchase attributes (e.g., Saturday night stay = Leisure; One-way/Weekday = Business; Corporate domain email = Business).
3.  **Route Density:** High-frequency business routes vs. leisure-heavy routes.

## Required Data Points
Capture the following at the transaction and session level:

**Primary Metrics (Revenue Impact)**
*   **Net Revenue Per Session (NRPS):** Total ticket value minus discount cost, divided by total sessions in variant.
*   **Incremental Bookings:** Bookings in the <48h window attributed to the test vs. holdout baseline.
*   **Margin Contribution:** (Ticket Price - Discount - Variable Costs) per booking.

**Secondary Metrics (Cannibalization & Behavior)**
*   **Cannibalization Rate:** % change in full-price (>14 days) bookings on test routes compared to non-test control routes.
*   **Pull-Forward Rate:** % of bookings shifted from the 3–14 day window into the <48h window.
*   **Segment Mix:** Ratio of Business vs. Leisure profiles in the <48h bucket.

**Guardrail Metrics**
*   **Load Factor:** Ensure we do not sell out too early, blocking higher-yield last-minute business demand.
*   **Customer Support Tickets:** Spike in inquiries regarding pricing discrepancies.

## Criteria for Success
The 20% discount (Variant B) is declared the winner **only if**:
1.  **Net Revenue Lift:** NRPS increases by ≥2% compared to Control (statistically significant at 95% confidence).
2.  **Cannibalization Limit:** Full-price bookings (>14 days) do not decrease by more than 3% relative to the Holdout group.
3.  **Segment Validation:** The lift is driven primarily by the identified "Leisure" or "Price-Sensitive" segment, not by shifting existing Business travelers to a lower fare.

If Variant B increases volume but fails the Net Revenue test, the 10% discount (Control) remains optimal.

## Risk Mitigation Strategies
1.  **Dynamic Stop-Loss:** Implement an automated kill-switch if net revenue per route drops >5% below the 7-day rolling average for two consecutive days.
2.  **Capacity Caps:** Limit the number of discounted seats available per flight to prevent premature sell-outs that block high-yield business bookings closer to departure.
3.  **Route Isolation:** Run the test on specific routes only; keep comparable routes as pure controls to account for external factors (e.g., holidays, competitor sales).
4.  **Blind Pricing:** Ensure discount visibility is restricted to the test group to prevent PR backlash or brand devaluation among full-price payers.

*Note: Ensure tracking tags distinguish between "new demand" and "shifted demand" by analyzing user cookies for prior price checks.*
*Analysis must use intention-to-treat (ITT) principles to avoid bias from users switching devices before booking.*
