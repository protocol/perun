### Diagnostic Segmentation Plan: CodeLeap Pricing Page

To resolve the ambiguity between "curiosity clicks" and "checkout hesitation," you must segment the existing data to isolate where the friction occurs. The 12% increase in click-through rate (CTR) paired with a 0.7 percentage point drop in enrollment conversion indicates a breakdown in message match or value perception, not necessarily traffic quality.

**1. Segment by Traffic Source and Campaign**
Isolate performance by `utm_source` and `utm_medium`. If the conversion drop is uniform across all channels (organic, paid search, social), the issue is likely the page design or copy itself. However, if the drop is concentrated in broad-awareness channels (e.g., social display ads) while high-intent channels (e.g., "coding bootcamp" search terms) remain stable, the new layout may be successfully attracting curious browsers who were previously filtering themselves out. This suggests the traffic mix has changed, not that the page is broken for qualified leads.

**2. Segment by Plan Selection (Premium vs. Standard)**
Since the goal was to drive higher-value sales, compare the conversion rates of the Premium plan specifically against the Standard plan.
*   **Scenario A:** If Premium clicks increased but Premium conversions plummeted while Standard remained flat, the prominence of the Premium plan is creating hesitation (e.g., price shock or unclear value prop).
*   **Scenario B:** If both plans dropped equally, the friction is likely in the general checkout flow or a confusing element introduced in the redesign that affects all users.

**3. Segment by Device Type**
Redesigns often introduce layout issues on mobile that are invisible on desktop. If the conversion drop is disproportionately high on mobile devices, the "prominent" premium placement may be pushing critical trust signals or pricing details below the fold, causing users to abandon before checkout.

**4. Analyze the "Click-to-Checkout" Drop-off**
You noted an increase in clicks to the checkout page, but a drop in final enrollment. You must calculate the conversion rate *from* the checkout page view *to* enrollment.
*   If users click the CTA, land on the checkout page, and immediately bounce, the pricing details or unexpected costs (fees, taxes) revealed at the final stage are causing rejection.
*   If they fill out half the form and stop, the friction is likely form complexity or lack of payment options.

**Additional Context Required**
To finalize the recommendation for the marketing director, you need two specific data points not yet mentioned:
*   **Average Order Value (AOV):** Has the revenue per enrolled student increased? If the 3.8% conversion rate yields higher total revenue due to more Premium sign-ups, the design is a success despite the lower volume.
*   **Time-on-Page and Scroll Depth:** Did users spend more time reading the new Premium details before clicking? High time-on-page with low conversion suggests they are interested but unconvinced; low time-on-page suggests confusion or mismatch.

**Recommendation Logic**
Do not revert the design yet. If the AOV has risen enough to offset the volume loss, the strategy is working financially. If AOV is flat and the drop is universal across devices and sources, the issue is likely value communication on the Premium tier. In that case, iterate on the copy to clarify ROI rather than reverting the layout, as the increased CTR proves the new design successfully captures attention.

*Note: Ensure your observation windows for pre- and post-Feb 20 data are identical (e.g., 14 days prior vs. 14 days post) to avoid seasonal skew.*
*Calculate the net revenue impact before presenting to the director; conversion rate alone is an incomplete metric for pricing changes.*
