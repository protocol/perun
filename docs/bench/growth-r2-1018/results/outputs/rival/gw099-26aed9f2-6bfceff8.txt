# Executive Summary: Autonomous Delivery Pilot Analysis

**Recommendation: Pause wider rollout for targeted improvements.**
Proceeding with the current handoff and package handling protocols risks brand damage and higher churn in new markets. The data indicates that speed gains are being negated by failures in the final delivery experience.

## Root Cause Analysis: The "Last Ten Yards" Problem

The disconnect between operational efficiency (22-minute average delivery, 5 minutes faster than human couriers) and customer sentiment (3.8/5 stars) suggests that speed is not the primary driver of satisfaction for grocery deliveries. Instead, the feedback points to two critical failure points:

1. **Handoff Procedures**: Customers report confusion or friction during the robot-to-customer transfer process.
2. **Package Condition**: Items are arriving damaged or improperly secured, likely due to robot movement dynamics or loading procedures.

## Required Data Sources to Combine

To isolate the root cause, integrate these data streams:

| Data Source | Key Metrics | Purpose |
|-------------|-------------|---------|
| **Operational Logs** | Delivery completion time, route deviations, stop duration | Identify if handoffs are taking longer than expected despite faster transit |
| **Customer Feedback Tags** | Categorized comments (handoff, damage, novelty, other) | Quantify frequency of specific complaint types |
| **Package Integrity Reports** | Damage claims, item type, packaging method | Correlate damage rates with specific routes, times, or package types |
| **Handoff Completion Time** | Time from arrival confirmation to customer retrieval | Measure actual handoff friction vs. assumed seamless transfer |
| **Repeat Order Rates** | Customer retention post-robot delivery | Determine if low satisfaction translates to churn |
| **Comparative Human Courier Data** | Same metrics for human deliveries in same district | Establish baseline for handoff time and damage rates |

## Specific Investigations Needed

1. **Handoff Friction Analysis**: Cross-reference delivery completion timestamps with customer app interactions (e.g., time to unlock robot, retrieve items). If handoffs average >3 minutes, the speed advantage erodes.

2. **Damage Pattern Mapping**: Segment damage reports by:
   - Item type (fragile vs. sturdy)
   - Distance traveled
   - Time of day (traffic conditions affecting robot movement)
   - Packaging method used

3. **Novelty vs. Functionality Split**: Analyze feedback sentiment by customer tenure:
   - First-time robot users vs. repeat users
   - If scores improve with repeat usage, novelty is a factor
   - If scores remain low or worsen, functional failures dominate

4. **Geographic Hotspots**: Map low satisfaction scores to specific delivery zones. Certain sidewalk conditions, building access points, or weather exposures may correlate with problems.

## Immediate Actions Before Scaling

1. **Implement Handoff Improvements**:
   - Add clearer customer instructions via app notifications
   - Test alternative unlock mechanisms (QR code vs. app button)
   - Consider attended delivery option for first-time users

2. **Package Handling Protocol Review**:
   - Audit loading procedures and securing methods
   - Test additional padding or compartmentalization for fragile items
   - Implement pre-delivery image capture for damage dispute resolution

3. **Pilot Extension with Controls**:
   - Run a 2-week modified pilot addressing handoff and packaging
   - A/B test improved protocols against current baseline
   - Target 4.2+ satisfaction score before scaling

## Decision Framework

**Proceed with scaling if:**
- Handoff time averages <2 minutes
- Damage rates fall below human courier baseline
- Satisfaction scores reach 4.2+ in modified pilot
- Repeat order rates match or exceed human delivery retention

**Pause and iterate if:**
- Handoff friction remains >3 minutes after improvements
- Damage rates exceed human couriers by >20%
- Satisfaction scores remain below 4.0
- Customer churn increases post-robot delivery

---

*Note: The 3.8 satisfaction score represents a critical risk signal that outweighs the 5-minute speed advantage. Grocery delivery is a trust-based service where item condition and ease of receipt matter more than marginal speed gains.*
