**Recommendation: Adopt "Sustainable Repeat Purchase Rate" as the Q3 North Star Metric**

**Definition:** The percentage of customers who make a second purchase within 90 days of their first, where at least one item in the *second* transaction has a verified carbon footprint saving displayed and clicked/viewed by the user during that session.

**Rationale for Selection**
Your current 18% repeat purchase rate is a lagging indicator that aggregates all behaviors, masking whether your sustainability value proposition actually drives loyalty. Leadership assumes carbon data drives retention, but this is currently an unvalidated hypothesis. If you optimize for general repeat purchases, engineering might improve shipping speed or price matching, while marketing might push generic discounts—neither of which validates your core brand differentiator.

By shifting to **Sustainable Repeat Purchase Rate**, you align both teams to a specific behavioral loop:
1.  **Engineering** must ensure carbon data is visible, accessible, and performant on product pages and the cart.
2.  **Marketing** must craft messaging that reminds users of their accumulated impact, driving them back to purchase *sustainably* again, not just purchase *anything*.

This metric isolates the specific mechanism you believe creates loyalty. If this rate rises while the general repeat rate stays flat, you have proof that sustainability is your retention engine. If this rate remains low while general repeats rise, you have evidence that sustainability is a acquisition hook (nice-to-have) but price or convenience drives actual loyalty, requiring a strategic pivot.

**Analysis Structure for Validation**
To validate this choice without complex statistical testing, structure your data review around three clear observational windows:

1.  **Cohort Segmentation by Exposure**
    Divide your March 12+ launch cohort into two distinct groups based on their first purchase behavior:
    *   **High-Exposure Group:** Users who viewed carbon footprint details on ≥2 items in their first order.
    *   **Low-Exposure Group:** Users who purchased but never clicked/viewed carbon details.
    *   *Reading the Trend:* Track the 60-day and 90-day return rates for both groups weekly. If the High-Exposure group returns at a significantly higher rate than the Low-Exposure group, the hypothesis holds. If the lines are parallel, the feature is not a retention driver.

2.  **The "Second Buy" Composition Check**
    Analyze the second transaction of every returning customer. Categorize them as:
    *   **Sustainable Repeat:** Second order includes interaction with carbon data.
    *   **Commodity Repeat:** Second order contains no interaction with carbon data.
    *   *Reading the Trend:* Plot the ratio of Sustainable Repeats vs. Commodity Repeats over time. A growing ratio indicates the brand identity is sticking. A declining ratio suggests users are returning for other reasons (price/habit), and the sustainability angle is fading in importance.

3.  **Regional Variance Scan (Sweden vs. Norway)**
    Since you operate in two distinct markets, run the above two analyses separately for each country.
    *   *Reading the Trend:* If Sweden shows a strong correlation between carbon data exposure and retention, but Norway does not, your roadmap should not be uniform. You may need to adjust the feature's prominence or the marketing narrative specifically for the Norwegian market, rather than assuming a universal driver.

**Decision Logic for the CEO**
*   **Green Light:** If High-Exposure users return 20%+ more often than Low-Exposure users, double down. Make carbon data the primary UI element.
*   **Yellow Light:** If return rates are similar, but "Sustainable Repeats" are growing slowly, the feature is neutral. Keep it, but do not prioritize it over friction-reduction initiatives.
*   **Red Light:** If High-Exposure users return *less* often (perhaps due to analysis paralysis or higher prices associated with green items), the hypothesis is false. The roadmap must shift to treating sustainability as an acquisition tool only, while focusing retention efforts on convenience or loyalty rewards.

*Note: Ensure your analytics implementation tracks "view_item_eco_data" as a distinct event to accurately segment these cohorts.*
*This approach avoids vanity metrics by tying the sustainability mission directly to revenue-generating behavior.*
