### Top 5 Behaviors to Measure for VetConnect Launch

To validate your value proposition and inform the April 1st board decision, immediately instrument tracking for these five specific behaviors. These metrics directly test your assumption that video quality drives retention versus booking speed.

**1. Video Stability Score (Pre-Call & During Call)**
*   **What to track:** Count of connection drops, resolution downgrades (e.g., HD to SD), and audio lag events (>200ms) per session.
*   **Why it matters:** This objectively measures "quality." If your core assumption is correct, users experiencing >1 stability event should have significantly lower conversion to subscription than those with flawless streams.
*   **Interpretation:** A strong negative correlation between stability events and Day-7 retention validates investing in video infrastructure. No correlation suggests users tolerate glitches if the vet is helpful.

**2. Time-to-Connected-Vet (Booking Speed)**
*   **What to track:** Seconds elapsed from "Find a Vet" click to the moment the video stream initializes.
*   **Why it matters:** This measures your competing hypothesis: that speed matters more than quality.
*   **Interpretation:** If users who connect in <30 seconds convert to subscriptions at a higher rate than those waiting >60 seconds (regardless of video quality), your assumption is false, and resources should shift to triage/chat efficiency.

**3. The "Re-booking" Event (True Retention)**
*   **What to track:** A boolean flag for any user who books a *second* consultation within 14 days of their first.
*   **Why it matters:** Total calls are a vanity metric; re-booking proves long-term value. One-off consultations indicate a failure to demonstrate ongoing utility.
*   **Interpretation:** Segment this by the first call's quality and speed. If high-quality/low-speed users re-book more than low-quality/high-speed users, double down on video features.

**4. Feature Utilization Depth During Call**
*   **What to track:** Specific interactions with video-enhanced tools (e.g., "Screen Share Activated," "Photo Upload During Call," "Zoom/Pause Video Used").
*   **Why it matters:** Passive video watching may not be the differentiator; active collaboration might be.
*   **Interpretation:** Low usage of these tools suggests the "video" aspect is merely a commodity. High usage correlated with subscription indicates the *interactive* nature of video is the value driver, not just the stream quality.

**5. Post-Call Resolution Sentiment**
*   **What to track:** A single-click micro-survey immediately post-call: "Was your pet's issue resolved?" (Yes/No) + optional comment.
*   **Why it matters:** Quality and speed are means to an end. If the medical outcome isn't perceived as successful, neither metric matters.
*   **Interpretation:** Cross-reference "Resolved" answers with stability and speed data. If users report "Resolved" despite poor video quality, your assumption that they prioritize quality is likely incorrect; they prioritize outcome, which chat might achieve cheaper.

**Immediate Implementation Note:**
Since you lack historical data, establish a baseline during launch week (March 18–24). Do not wait for statistical significance before the April 1st meeting; report early trends using cohort analysis (e.g., "Users with stable video are currently 20% more likely to re-book, though N is small").

**Strategic Recommendation:**
If data shows video stability drives re-booking, present a roadmap for infrastructure investment. If speed or resolution sentiment drives it, propose a pilot for chat-based triage to reduce cost-per-acquisition.
