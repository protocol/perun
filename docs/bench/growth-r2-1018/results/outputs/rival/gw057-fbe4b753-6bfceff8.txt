### Decision Framework: Evaluating Mixed A/B Test Results

Your test presents a classic growth dilemma: a significant gain in activation (first project created) paired with a significant increase in friction (support tickets). To determine if the tooltips are net positive, you must move beyond the top-line percentages and analyze the **magnitude of impact** and **user lifecycle stages**.

**1. Quantify the Trade-off (The "Net Value" Calculation)**
Before refining copy, calculate the actual business cost versus benefit.
*   **Benefit:** How many *additional* projects were created due to the 20% lift? Multiply this by your known conversion rate from "first project" to "paid subscription" and your Customer Lifetime Value (LTV). This gives you the **Revenue Uplift**.
*   **Cost:** Calculate the cost of the 15% increase in support tickets. Include agent time per ticket and any potential churn risk from frustrated users.
*   **Comparison:** If the projected LTV gain significantly outweighs the support cost (e.g., by 5x or 10x), the feature is likely a net positive despite the friction. If the margins are thin, the friction is too high.

**2. Analyze the "When" and "Who" of the Friction**
Your hypothesis suggests tooltips help initially but confuse users later. You need to validate this timing:
*   **Ticket Timing:** Do the support tickets come from users on Day 1 (during onboarding) or Day 3+ (exploring advanced features)? If tickets spike only after the onboarding window closes, the tooltips may be failing to dismiss properly or are triggering contextually inappropriate later.
*   **Segmentation:** Are the users creating projects the *same* users filing tickets? Or did you acquire a new segment of users who successfully start projects but lack the sophistication to navigate advanced tools?

**3. Missing Data Points Needed**
You cannot make a final call without these three specific data points:
*   **Retention Curve (Day 7 & Day 14):** Does the group with higher initial project creation *stay* active? If they churn faster because of the confusion, the 20% lift is a "false start" and the feature is harmful long-term.
*   **Feature Adoption Depth:** Beyond the *first* project, does the tooltip group use *advanced* features (e.g., dependencies, automations) at lower rates than the control? This would confirm your fear that tooltips overwhelm users from deeper exploration.
*   **Qualitative Sentiment:** Since you lack sentiment data, immediately deploy a micro-survey (one question) to users who opened a support ticket: "Was this issue caused by confusion with the onboarding guide?" Also, sample users who *did not* file tickets to see if they found the tooltips helpful or ignored them.

**Recommendation for the Design Team**
Do not remove the tooltips entirely yet; the 20% activation lift is too valuable to discard without proof of long-term churn. Instead, recommend a **targeted iteration**:
1.  **Limit Scope:** Restrict tooltips strictly to the "Create Project" flow and ensure they permanently dismiss after completion.
2.  **Contextual Triggers:** Ensure tooltips do not fire when users are navigating advanced menus unless explicitly requested.
3.  **Follow-up Test:** Run a follow-up A/B test with these refined constraints, measuring **Day 7 retention** as the primary metric and support tickets as a guardrail.

**Note:** Prioritize checking Day 7 retention immediately; if retention drops, the activation gain is illusory.
**Note:** Implement a "dismiss forever" option on tooltips to reduce navigation friction for power users.
