### Analysis of Time-Dependent Email Frequency Behavior

**The Core Dynamic: Engagement Decay vs. Fatigue Accumulation**
Your six-week test reveals a classic "fatigue curve" rather than a static performance metric. The data indicates two distinct phases:
1.  **Phase 1 (Weeks 1–3):** High frequency (5x/week) successfully capitalized on initial subscriber interest, yielding a 10% open rate lift. This suggests your content quality is high enough to sustain short-term attention.
2.  **Phase 2 (Weeks 4–6):** The marginal utility of additional emails turned negative. The unsubscribe rate in the test group (2.5%) exceeded the control (0.8%) by a factor of over 3x. This inflection point confirms that user fatigue is not linear; it accelerates once a specific threshold of inbox saturation is crossed.

**Interpretation of the "Unknown"**
Your hypothesis—that reducing frequency after Month 1 could retain engagement while curbing churn—is statistically sound based on these trends. The initial 10% open rate boost proves the *content* is desired; the later unsubscribe spike proves the *cadence* was unsustainable. The churn was not driven by disinterest in BookWorm Press, but by the operational burden of processing five weekly decisions.

By week four, the "cost" of opening an email (time/attention) began to outweigh the perceived value for a segment of your audience, leading them to opt-out entirely rather than just ignore the messages.

**Strategic Recommendation: The "Ramp-Down" Dynamic Schedule**
For your Q4 proposal to the CRO, do not present a static frequency change. Instead, propose a **Dynamic Frequency Strategy** that mirrors the observed user behavior:

*   **New Subscriber/Re-engagement Phase (Days 1–30):** Maintain the **5 emails/week** cadence. This captures the high-intent window where the 10% open rate lift was observed. This maximizes immediate revenue potential and content consumption when interest is peaked.
*   **Retention Phase (Day 31+):** Automatically transition these users to **3 emails/week**. This reduction occurs *before* the historical fatigue inflection point (week 4), theoretically preserving the engagement habits formed in Month 1 while removing the friction that caused the 2.5% unsubscribe spike.

**Projected Outcome**
This approach aims to secure the "best of both worlds": the high top-of-funnel engagement of the test group during the critical onboarding period, combined with the long-term list health and lower churn of the control group. It treats email frequency not as a fixed setting, but as a lifecycle variable that adapts to user tolerance.

**Next Steps for Validation**
To validate this before a full Q4 rollout, recommend an A/B test in August where the "Ramp-Down" group is compared against a static 5x/week and static 3x/week group over 8 weeks. Measure the *cumulative* revenue per user, not just open rates, to ensure the reduced frequency in month two does not disproportionately impact sales.
