# Recommendation: North Star Metric for FitVerse Beta Launch

**Recommended Metric:** Number of Friends Invited Per User
**Alternative Metric (Monitor Only):** Average Calories Burned Per Session

## Analysis for Local Operations Director

### Option A: Number of Friends Invited Per User (Recommended)

**Pros:**
- **Directly tests your core unknown:** This metric measures the social hypothesis head-on. If social features drive retention, this number will correlate strongly with your six-month retention goal.
- **Predicts community health:** In VR fitness, working out with friends creates accountability and fun, which are stronger drivers of habit formation than solitary performance data. A user inviting friends signals they find enough value to share it.
- **Drives viral growth:** Unlike calories burned, every increment in this metric potentially brings new users into the ecosystem at low acquisition cost, critical for a beta launch with only 500 waitlist signups.
- **Actionable for product teams:** If this number is low, you know immediately to improve social features, onboarding flows for inviting friends, or in-class social interactions.

**Cons:**
- **Delayed feedback loop:** You may need 2-3 weeks to see if invitations actually convert to active users and whether those users retain.
- **Potential for spam:** Users might invite many people who never join, inflating the number without real value. Must track "accepted invitations" as a secondary check.
- **Less relevant for solo-preferring users:** Some users may become highly retained without ever inviting anyone, potentially skewing your view of overall product health.

### Option B: Average Calories Burned Per Session

**Pros:**
- **Immediate data availability:** You can measure this from day one of the beta without waiting for social connections to form.
- **Validates core fitness value:** Confirms that classes are physically effective, which is table stakes for any fitness platform.
- **Easy to communicate:** Hardware partners and stakeholders instantly understand "calories burned" as a fitness outcome.

**Cons:**
- **Does not test your key hypothesis:** This metric tells you nothing about whether social features drive retention. You could have high calories burned but still fail to build a habitual community if users workout alone and churn.
- **Weak predictor of long-term retention:** In fitness apps, initial intensity (calories) often correlates poorly with six-month habits. Users frequently start strong then quit when motivation fades without social accountability.
- **No growth mechanism:** High calories burned doesn't naturally bring in new users or create network effects. It's an individual outcome, not a community builder.
- **Risk of optimizing wrong behavior:** Focusing here might push teams to make classes harder rather than more engaging or social, potentially increasing injury risk or discouraging beginners.

## Strategic Recommendation

For a beta launch where your primary learning goal is validating whether **social features drive six-month retention**, "Number of Friends Invited Per User" is the superior north star metric. It directly measures the behavior you hypothesize creates habitual users in the VR space: social accountability and shared experiences.

Calories burned should be tracked as a **guardrail metric** to ensure classes remain effective, but it should not drive product decisions during this beta phase. If social invitations correlate with retention in your beta data, you have validated your core assumption and can scale social features aggressively for the full launch. If not, you've learned critical information before committing significant marketing spend.
