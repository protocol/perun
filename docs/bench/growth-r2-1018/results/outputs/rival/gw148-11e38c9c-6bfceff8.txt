**Recommendation: Do Not Segment by Age Yet; Diagnose Trust vs. Layout via Low-Cost Signals First**

Do not immediately remove payment options for users aged 65+. Segmenting the experience by age introduces technical debt, potential legal/compliance risks regarding age discrimination, and fragments your data long-term. The 20% drop-off is significant, but your assumption that "visual confusion" is the sole cause is unproven. The root cause is likely a combination of **trust deficits** (unfamiliar branding) and **cognitive load** (layout complexity), which require different fixes.

To distinguish between a **Layout Issue** (usability) and a **Trust Issue** (credibility) without running a new expensive multivariate test, analyze the following existing signals immediately:

**1. Quantitative Funnel Analysis (The "Where" and "When")**
*   **Drop-off Point:** Did users abandon *before* clicking a payment method, or *after* selecting one but before completing the transaction?
    *   *Abandonment before selection:* Suggests **Layout/Confusion**. The user cannot decipher the options or feels overwhelmed by choice.
    *   *Abandonment after selection:* Suggests **Trust/Friction**. The user tried to use the method but got scared by a redirect, unfamiliar login screen, or security warning.
*   **Time-on-Step:** Compare the time spent on the payment selection step for the 65+ cohort vs. the control group.
    *   *Significantly higher time:* Indicates hesitation and cognitive struggle (Layout/Comprehension).
    *   *Normal or lower time:* Indicates a quick decision to reject the options (Trust/Preference).
*   **Method Selection Distribution:** Did the 65+ group still select "Credit Card" at higher rates than younger cohorts, or did they abandon even when Credit Card was available?
    *   If they abandoned despite Credit Card being present, the new buttons likely obscured the primary option (Layout).
    *   If they selected new methods but didn't complete, it is a Trust issue with those specific providers.

**2. Qualitative & Behavioral Signals (The "Why")**
*   **Session Recordings/Heatmaps:** Filter recordings for the 65+ demographic (if identifiable via login data) or general high-dwell-time sessions.
    *   *Look for:* "Rage clicks" on non-interactive elements, rapid scrolling up and down (confusion), or mouse hovers over the new logos without clicking (uncertainty).
    *   *Trust Indicator:* If users hover over the logo and then immediately move the cursor to a "Security" link or FAQ, they are seeking validation.
*   **Customer Support Logs:** Search for tickets containing keywords like "payment," "scam," "unknown," "couldn't find," or specific names of the new providers during the test window. An uptick in "Is this safe?" queries confirms a Trust issue.
*   **Exit Survey Data:** If you have an exit-intent survey on the checkout page, filter responses from the test period. Look for verbatim comments mentioning "confusing," "didn't recognize," or "prefer Visa/Mastercard."

**Immediate Action Plan for the UX Director:**
1.  **Hypothesis Validation:** If data shows abandonment *after* selection, prioritize **Trust** fixes (e.g., adding "Secure" badges, displaying familiar logos alongside new ones, or shortening the redirect flow). If abandonment is *before* selection, prioritize **Layout** fixes (e.g., moving new methods below Credit Cards, increasing icon size, or simplifying the grid).
2.  **Interim Mitigation:** Instead of age-gating, reorder the payment methods. Place "Credit Card" as the default, expanded option, and list alternative methods in a collapsible "More Options" accordion. This reduces visual clutter for all users while maintaining access.
3.  **Targeted Follow-up:** If possible, recruit 5–10 users aged 65+ for a 30-minute moderated usability test (remote or in-person) specifically observing their reaction to the new buttons. This is cheaper and faster than a full A/B test and will definitively answer the layout vs. trust question.

**Note:** Avoid making permanent structural changes based on a single two-week window without validating the specific friction point.
**Note:** Ensure any age-based filtering complies with local non-discrimination laws before implementation.
