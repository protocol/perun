### Data Slices for Root Cause Analysis

To distinguish between a rendering/usability failure and a content preference issue before Tuesday, isolate the following data segments immediately. Do not rely on aggregate mobile averages.

**1. Interaction Depth & Error Rates (Usability vs. Preference)**
*   **Slice:** Mobile users who opened the new dashboard vs. those who immediately navigated away (bounce) within 5 seconds.
*   **Metric:** Compare " Rage Clicks" (rapid repeated taps) and "Error Console Logs" (JavaScript failures) on the new dashboard view.
*   **Interpretation:** High rage clicks or JS errors indicate a **rendering/technical block**. High immediate bounce without errors suggests the layout is **visually overwhelming** or users cannot find their primary metric quickly (preference/usability).

**2. Task Completion by Feature Usage (Content Relevance)**
*   **Slice:** Segment mobile sessions by the *first action* taken: Viewing a chart vs. Checking a single KPI vs. Exporting data.
*   **Metric:** Time-to-First-Interaction and Task Completion Rate for each action type.
*   **Interpretation:** If "Checking a single KPI" time increased significantly while "Viewing a chart" engagement rose, mobile users likely prefer the old view for **quick checks** (supporting your hypothesis). If all tasks show increased time with lower completion, the layout is likely **broken or unreadable** on small screens.

**3. Scroll Depth and Viewport Visibility**
*   **Slice:** Mobile users by device screen height (e.g., iPhone SE vs. iPhone Pro Max vs. Android equivalents).
*   **Metric:** Percentage of users who scroll past the fold vs. those who do not. Heatmap data if available.
*   **Interpretation:** If critical navigation or key metrics are pushed below the fold on smaller screens and users do not scroll, this confirms a **layout cramming issue**. If users scroll but disengage, the content density may be the problem.

**4. Cohort Retention (New vs. Power Users)**
*   **Slice:** Mobile Power Users (daily active > 5 times/week) vs. Casual Users.
*   **Metric:** Session frequency and duration change post-rollout.
*   **Interpretation:** If Power Users show the steepest drop in duration, the new view disrupts an established, efficient workflow (preference/usability). If Casual Users are unaffected or engage more, the new view may be too complex for expert mobile workflows.

**5. Exit Point Analysis**
*   **Slice:** Specific page elements where mobile sessions end.
*   **Metric:** Exit rate on specific dashboard widgets or navigation buttons.
*   **Interpretation:** Concentrated exits on specific interactive elements suggest those elements are **non-functional or confusing** on mobile (rendering/usability).

### Recommended Immediate Action
If data shows high error rates or rage clicks, prioritize a **hotfix** for rendering. If data shows users abandoning specific tasks due to layout density but no technical errors, consider **reverting the mobile layout** to the previous version while keeping the desktop update, then redesign the mobile view specifically for "quick check" workflows.

*Note: Ensure your analytics events distinguish between "app backgrounding" and "session end" to avoid falsely inflating drop-off rates if users are simply switching apps.*
*Verify that the 50% rollout was truly random across device types to rule out sampling bias.*
