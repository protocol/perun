# Diagnostic Analysis: StreamFlix Argentina Content Carousel Experiment

## Executive Summary
The experiment (March 1–21) presents a classic "engagement trap": a 12% increase in click-through rate (CTR) coupled with a 4% decline in Day-7 retention. This divergence suggests the new carousel successfully drives initial curiosity but fails to deliver sustained value, or actively introduces friction that accelerates churn. The assumption that "higher engagement predicts better retention" has been invalidated for this specific feature; the quality of engagement, not just the quantity, is the determining factor.

## Potential Root Causes

### 1. The "Clickbait" Quality Mismatch
The localized content may be highly effective at attracting clicks (high CTR) due to familiar cultural cues, but the actual content quality or relevance may not meet user expectations once playback begins.
*   **Hypothesis:** Users click expecting premium local hits but encounter lower-quality library content, leading to early session abandonment and a negative perception of the platform's value proposition.
*   **Indicator to Check:** Compare "Play Rate" (clicks resulting in >1 min watch) and "Completion Rate" between variants. If the variant has high clicks but low play/completion rates, the issue is content promise vs. delivery.

### 2. Interface Friction for Older Demographics
With older users comprising 30% of the subscriber base, the new interface may introduce navigational confusion that outweighs the benefit of localized recommendations.
*   **Hypothesis:** The carousel disrupts established viewing habits for users over 50, causing frustration that leads to churn, even if they initially click out of curiosity.
*   **Indicator to Check:** Segment retention drop-off by age cohort. If the 4% global drop is driven entirely by the 50+ segment (e.g., a 15% drop in that group masked by stability in younger groups), the issue is UX accessibility, not content quality.

### 3. Discovery vs. Satisfaction Paradox
The algorithm may be optimizing for "novelty" rather than "satisfaction."
*   **Hypothesis:** The carousel pushes users to try new genres they don't actually enjoy, exhausting their interest faster than the standard recommendation engine which likely favors safe, proven hits.
*   **Indicator to Check:** Analyze "Time to Churn" and "Number of Titles Sampled." If variant users sample more titles but watch fewer total minutes before cancelling, the feature is encouraging inefficient browsing.

## Recommended Investigative Segments & Patterns

To isolate the root cause without further testing, the following data slices must be examined immediately:

### A. Age-Based Cohort Analysis
*   **Action:** Break down Day-7 retention by age brackets (18–34, 35–49, 50+).
*   **Goal:** Determine if the retention drop is universal or concentrated in the 50+ demographic. If concentrated, the issue is interface complexity. If universal, the issue is content relevance.

### B. The "Click-to-Watch" Funnel
*   **Action:** Compare the conversion rate from *Carousel Click* → *Video Start* → *10-Minute Mark* for Variant vs. Control.
*   **Goal:** Identify the exact drop-off point. A drop after the click but before the 10-minute mark indicates a content quality mismatch. A drop after 10 minutes suggests the content failed to sustain interest long-term.

### C. Content Genre Affinity
*   **Action:** Correlate the specific titles clicked in the carousel with subsequent retention.
*   **Goal:** Identify if specific genres within the localized carousel are driving the churn. It is possible that while "Local News" drives clicks, it correlates with low retention, whereas "Local Drama" does not.

### D. Session Depth Analysis
*   **Action:** Measure the average number of titles watched per session for users who interacted with the carousel.
*   **Goal:** Verify if the carousel encourages "binging" (positive) or "grazing" (negative). If users click multiple carousel items but watch nothing fully, the interface is promoting indecision.

## Next Steps for Diagnosis
Do not proceed with feature iteration until the data confirms whether the failure is **demographic** (UX issue for older users) or **content-based** (quality mismatch). The decision to kill or iterate depends entirely on which segment is driving the 4% loss. If the loss is isolated to the 50+ group, a UI toggle or simplified view may solve it. If the loss is across all ages due to poor content completion, the content sourcing strategy requires a full audit.
