### Recommended North Star Metric: **Net Client Cash Flow Improvement (NCCFI)**

Replace "Volume of Invoices Financed" with **Net Client Cash Flow Improvement**. This metric measures the actual liquidity gain for the client after costs, ensuring you are solving their cash flow problems rather than just generating fee revenue or encouraging risky micro-borrowing.

**Formula:**
$$NCCFI = (\text{Total Invoice Value Financed}) - (\text{Total Fees Charged}) - (\text{Value of Invoices Financed < \$X Threshold})$$

*Note: The threshold ($X$) should be set based on your minimum viable loan size that covers operational costs and indicates genuine business need.*

### Supporting Guardrail Metrics

To present a complete picture to your CFO and diagnose the pilot issues, track these three guardrails alongside NCCFI:

1.  **Fee-to-Liquidity Ratio**
    *   **Formula:** $\frac{\text{Total Fees Paid}}{\text{Net Cash Received by Client}}$
    *   **Purpose:** Directly addresses the doubling of support queries. If this ratio spikes, clients feel "nickel-and-dimed," indicating the pricing model erodes the value proposition. A healthy ratio ensures the cost of capital doesn't outweigh the liquidity benefit.

2.  **Repeat Borrowing Velocity**
    *   **Formula:** $\text{Average Days Between Consecutive Financed Invoices per Client}$
    *   **Purpose:** Distinguishes between "testing the waters" and "cash-strapped distress."
        *   *Healthy:* Moderate velocity matching their natural invoice cycle (e.g., every 30–45 days).
        *   *Risky:* Extremely high velocity (e.g., every 2–3 days) suggests clients are using instant payouts to plug daily operational holes, increasing default risk.

3.  **Loan Size Stability Index**
    *   **Formula:** $\frac{\text{Median Loan Size (Current Month)}}{\text{Median Loan Size (Pre-Pilot Baseline)}}$
    *   **Purpose:** Monitors the 15% drop in average loan size. If this index drops below 0.90 without a corresponding increase in client count, it signals a shift toward smaller, potentially less profitable, or higher-risk segments.

### Strategic Rationale for the CFO

The current focus on **Volume** created a perverse incentive: it encouraged users to break large invoices into smaller chunks or finance marginal invoices to game the "instant" feature, driving up support costs and lowering average loan quality.

By shifting to **NCCFI**, you align FinFlow's success with the client's financial health:
*   **If volume rises but fees eat all the liquidity:** NCCFI stays flat or drops, signaling a pricing problem.
*   **If loan sizes shrink to micro-transactions:** The threshold exclusion prevents these from inflating your success metrics, highlighting the need for a minimum ticket size.
*   **If clients are genuinely helped:** They receive meaningful net cash, leading to higher retention and lower support friction.
