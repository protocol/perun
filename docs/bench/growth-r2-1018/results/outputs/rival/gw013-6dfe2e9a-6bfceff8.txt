**Recommended North Star Metric: Weekly Active Organizations Hosting Hybrid Events**

For your investor update and strategic pivot to hybrid events, do not select retention, session length, or total events hosted as your primary health metric. Instead, track **Weekly Active Organizations Hosting Hybrid Events**.

This metric uniquely satisfies the requirements of your new business model and investor narrative for three critical reasons:

1.  **It Validates the Hybrid Pivot:** Total "events hosted" includes legacy virtual-only events, which may be declining. "Active organizations" filters out noise from dormant accounts. By requiring the event to be *hybrid*, you directly measure adoption of your new strategic direction rather than legacy usage.
2.  **It Correlates to Revenue Better Than Engagement:** Your data shows session length increased (70 mins) while DAU dropped (−25%). This suggests users are engaging deeply but less frequently. Session length is a vanity metric in this context; a user can stay for 70 minutes without generating value. Revenue in B2B SaaS is driven by organizational utility, not individual screen time. An organization hosting a hybrid event is a leading indicator of contract renewal and expansion, whereas a long session might just indicate a lack of alternative tools.
3.  **It Solves the Lagging Billing Data Issue:** Since billing lags by a month, you cannot wait for revenue data to assess the June feature release. The act of an organization successfully configuring and launching a hybrid event is an immediate behavioral signal that precedes the invoice. It bridges the gap between product usage and financial outcome.

**Framework for Metric Selection**

To defend this choice in your deck, apply the **Sustainable Growth Filter**. A valid North Star metric in a pivot must pass three tests:

*   **The Strategy Test:** Does an increase in this metric prove our new hypothesis is working?
    *   *Result:* Yes. If organizations host more hybrid events, the pivot to hybrid is succeeding. If session length increases but hybrid events stay flat, you are just a better virtual tool, not a hybrid platform.
*   **The Leading Indicator Test:** Does this move before revenue?
    *   *Result:* Yes. Hosting behavior happens weeks before the billing cycle closes. Retention is lagging (you only know it after they leave); session length is concurrent but ambiguous.
*   **The Value Test:** Does this capture value creation, not just activity?
    *   *Result:* Yes. "Active Organizations" captures the B2B buyer unit. "Events" captures activity. Combining them ensures you are measuring *paying units* deriving *core value*.

**Implementation Note for the Deck**
Present this metric alongside a secondary guardrail: **Average Session Duration per Hybrid Event**. This acknowledges your finding that quality/duration is up, framing it as a feature of the hybrid experience (e.g., longer Q&A, extended networking) rather than a standalone goal. This tells investors: "We are driving more organizations to adopt our new hybrid core (North Star), and when they do, they engage deeper than ever (Guardrail)."

Do not focus on DAU or raw session time; these reflect the old "constant connectivity" model you are moving away from. Focus on the organizational commitment to the new format.
