### Post-Mortem Analysis Plan: DailyBrief Dark Mode Experiment

**Executive Summary**
The aggregate data indicates no statistically significant impact on day-30 retention (0.5% difference). However, visual preference often influences *engagement quality* (session duration, reading depth) rather than *retention frequency*. To determine if the feature warrants investment for specific use cases (e.g., night reading) versus dropping entirely, you must move beyond aggregate retention and analyze engagement metrics across specific behavioral and temporal cohorts.

**Recommended Data Slices & Hypotheses**

Execute the following four analyses to uncover hidden signals before your Tuesday presentation:

**1. Temporal Cohort Analysis (The "Night Owl" Hypothesis)**
*   **Slice:** Segment users by their primary reading window: *Morning (6 AM–12 PM)*, *Commute (12 PM–6 PM)*, and *Night (6 PM–12 AM)*.
*   **Metric:** Compare average session duration and articles-per-session between variants within the *Night* cohort only.
*   **Rationale:** Dark mode primarily reduces eye strain in low-light environments. If the feature works, the lift will be concentrated here. A neutral aggregate result often masks a strong positive in this segment diluted by indifferent daytime users.

**2. Device & Environment Context**
*   **Slice:** Separate iOS vs. Android and OLED vs. LCD screens (if device data is available). Further segment by "System Default Dark Mode Enabled" vs. "Manual App Toggle."
*   **Metric:** Adoption rate of the dark mode toggle and subsequent session depth.
*   **Rationale:** Users who manually override system settings demonstrate higher intent. If users with system-wide dark mode enabled show higher retention *only* when the app matches their system setting, the value is in seamless integration, not the theme itself.

**3. Engagement Depth vs. Frequency**
*   **Slice:** Quartiles of "Power Users" (top 25% by session count) vs. "Casual Readers."
*   **Metric:** Reading depth (scroll depth % or time-on-page) rather than just session count.
*   **Rationale:** Your hypothesis suggests content drives loyalty. If dark mode increases *reading depth* for Power Users (who consume more content per session), it supports a "quality of life" investment even if it doesn't acquire new users.

**4. The "Toggle" Behavior**
*   **Slice:** Users who switched themes ≥1 time during the experiment vs. those who never touched the setting.
*   **Metric:** Retention and session duration of "Switchers" compared to "Static" users.
*   **Rationale:** If "Switchers" have significantly higher engagement, the *ability to choose* is the value driver, suggesting you should keep the feature but perhaps stop promoting it as a primary acquisition hook.

**Strategic Recommendation Framework**

Present your final recommendation based on these outcomes:

*   **Invest Further:** If Night Cohort or Power Users show >5% lift in engagement depth. *Action:* Optimize night-specific UI elements, not just colors.
*   **Maintain Status Quo:** If only "Switchers" show benefit. *Action:* Keep the feature for completeness but de-prioritize future theme customization resources.
*   **Drop Feature:** If no segment shows positive movement in engagement depth or duration. *Action:* Confirm your hypothesis that content relevance is the sole loyalty driver; reallocate design resources to content discovery features.

**Immediate Next Steps**
Run these four queries immediately. If the "Night" cohort shows no lift in session duration, your assumption is correct: visual theme is not a lever for this audience, and you should recommend dropping further customization to focus on content algorithms.

*Note: Ensure your statistical significance calculator accounts for the smaller sample sizes within these sliced cohorts before declaring a winner.*
