**Recommendation: Optimize for Completion Rate, Not Session Duration**

For the 'Data Basics' course and your broader business goals, **completion rate** is the superior metric to optimize. While the 25-minute average session time suggests initial interest, it is a "vanity metric" in this context because it does not correlate with the ultimate value proposition: finishing the course.

**Why Completion Rate Predicts Success Better:**
1.  **Outcome Alignment:** Students renew subscriptions because they achieve learning outcomes, not because they spend time on the platform. A student who spends 25 minutes stuck on a confusing module without finishing has had a negative experience, despite the high time-on-site.
2.  **The "Stuck" Signal:** The discrepancy between high session time and low completion (18% vs. 30% historical) strongly indicates friction. Students are likely struggling with specific concepts, encountering technical barriers, or finding the pacing mismatched to their needs. They are expending effort (time) without achieving progress (completion).
3.  **Revenue Correlation:** Historical data in ed-tech consistently shows that course completion is a leading indicator of retention and renewal. Students who finish courses feel a sense of accomplishment and are more likely to enroll in the next one. High session time without completion often precedes churn.

**Addressing the "Hidden Consumption" Hypothesis:**
Your hypothesis that dropouts are consuming content via PDFs is plausible but currently unverified. Even if true, optimizing for completion remains the priority because:
*   **Structured Learning:** The course curriculum is designed as a sequence. Skipping video modules for PDFs may work for some, but if the majority are dropping out, the structured path is failing them.
*   **Measurement Gap:** If PDF downloads are a valid completion path, they must be integrated into your definition of "completion." Currently, your tracking defines completion as finishing video modules. If you want to validate alternative learning paths, you must track those specific actions (e.g., "resource_downloaded" + "quiz_passed") as alternative completion events.

**Immediate Action Plan:**
1.  **Funnel Analysis:** Map the drop-off points within the course. Identify exactly which modules cause the session time to spike and completion to halt. This is where your instructional design updates should focus.
2.  **Redefine Completion:** Investigate the PDF hypothesis. If students are downloading resources and passing assessments without watching every video, update your tracking to count this as a valid completion path. Do not assume this is happening; measure it.
3.  **Shift Design Goals:** Instruct the content team to reduce friction. Aim to maintain learning quality while reducing the time required to reach milestones. High engagement should look like steady progress, not prolonged stagnation.

**Conclusion:**
Do not optimize for session duration. It is currently masking a retention problem. Focus entirely on improving the completion rate by removing barriers to progress. Once completion rates recover toward the 30% historical average, then evaluate if session times are healthy.

*Note: Ensure your analytics team validates whether PDF downloads or other non-video interactions are occurring for non-completers before finalizing the Q3 design roadmap.*
*This recommendation assumes the course content itself is sound; if completion rises but renewal stays flat, further investigation into content-market fit will be needed.*
