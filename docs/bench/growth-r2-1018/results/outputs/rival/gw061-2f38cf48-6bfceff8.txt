To validate whether the Level 4 difficulty spike is driving your 18% Day-30 retention deficit, you must isolate player behavior specifically around the hypothesized churn point using existing telemetry. Do not rely on aggregate averages; instead, segment your October 1–15 cohort by progression depth to confirm if drop-offs correlate with Level 4 entry.

**1. Progression Funnel Drop-off Rates**
Calculate the conversion rate between completing Level 3 and attempting Level 4, and then between attempting Level 4 and completing it. Compare this specifically against the drop-off rates of Levels 1–3. If the "Attempt Level 4 → Complete Level 4" conversion is significantly lower (e.g., >15% delta) than the average of previous levels, this confirms a friction point. Crucially, filter your Day-30 retained users to see what percentage actually passed Level 4. If retained users overwhelmingly cleared Level 4 while churned users stalled there, the correlation is strong.

**2. Session Length and Frequency Pre-Churn**
Analyze the session duration and frequency for users in their final 48 hours before churn.
*   **The "Wall" Pattern:** If difficulty is the cause, you should see a spike in session frequency (repeated attempts) with decreasing session length (rage-quitting after repeated failures) immediately preceding churn.
*   **The "Boredom" Pattern:** If onboarding is the issue, you will likely see lengthening sessions with no progression or a steady decline in login frequency without a spike in attempt repetition.
Cross-reference this with the "Time to Level 4." If users are churning *before* reaching Level 4 but exhibiting the "Wall" pattern on Level 3, your difficulty spike may be misplaced.

**3. In-Game Resource Consumption at Failure Points**
Examine the usage of consumables (health potions, revive tokens, power-ups) per attempt at Level 4 versus Level 3. A sharp increase in resource consumption without a corresponding increase in success rate indicates players are struggling mechanically but lack the tools to overcome the gap. If resource usage is low and churn is high, players may be confused by mechanics (onboarding issue) rather than overwhelmed by difficulty.

**4. Feature Engagement Depth**
Segment churned users by whether they unlocked core RPG systems (e.g., skill trees, guilds, crafting) before quitting.
*   If churn happens *before* unlocking these systems, the barrier is likely early-game difficulty or onboarding clarity.
*   If churn happens *after* unlocking but during Level 4, the difficulty curve is disrupting the core loop engagement.
Compare the Day-7 retention of users who reached Level 4 versus those who didn't. If Day-7 retention plummets exactly at the Level 4 threshold, the difficulty spike is cannibalizing your mid-term retention, which directly impacts Day-30 numbers.

**Recommendation Framework for the Steering Committee**
Present the data as a binary decision tree:
*   **Allocate to Rebalancing IF:** Level 4 completion rates are statistical outliers (<50% of Level 3 completion), session data shows repeated short failures, and resource usage spikes at failure.
*   **Allocate to Onboarding IF:** Drop-offs are distributed evenly across Levels 1–4, session lengths decay gradually without repetition spikes, or users churn before engaging with core mechanics regardless of level reached.

Do not propose a split investment; the data slices above will point to the single biggest lever. Rebalancing yields faster ROI if the "Wall" pattern is confirmed, as onboarding overhauls require longer development cycles and A/B testing validation.
