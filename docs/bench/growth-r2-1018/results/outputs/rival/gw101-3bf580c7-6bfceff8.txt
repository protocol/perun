### Recommended Metrics for Retention Impact Analysis

To determine if the profile prompt harms long-term engagement or creates temporary noise, you must move beyond the aggregate 5% drop and analyze behavior through cohort-based retention and friction metrics. The following specific measurements will isolate the prompt's true effect:

**1. Cohort-Based Retention Curves (Days 1, 3, 7, 14, 30)**
Do not compare total daily active users (DAU) across the whole period, as new user volume fluctuations can skew this. Instead, track retention by signup date:
*   **Metric:** Percentage of users from each daily cohort who return on Day 1, Day 3, Day 7, and Day 14.
*   **Action:** Plot separate curves for the "Prompt" vs. "No Prompt" groups. If the prompt causes harassment, the Day 1 and Day 3 retention will show a sharp divergence that widens over time. If it is merely a temporary distraction, the curves should converge by Day 7.

**2. "Completed Profile" Retention Differential**
The aggregate drop might be driven entirely by users who *saw* the prompt but *ignored* it, while those who completed it might be more engaged.
*   **Metric:** Compare Day 7 retention for three distinct segments:
    1.  Control Group (No prompt)
    2.  Treatment Group: Saw prompt → Completed profile
    3.  Treatment Group: Saw prompt → Dismissed/Ignored
*   **Insight:** If Segment 2 has significantly higher retention than the Control, the prompt works for converters. If Segment 3 has drastically lower retention than the Control, the prompt actively alienates non-converters.

**3. Time-to-First-Value (TFV) and Connection Rate**
Your hypothesis links profile completion to connection rates. Verify if the faster completion actually translates to core product value.
*   **Metric:** Average time from signup to first connection made, and percentage of users making ≥1 connection within 48 hours.
*   **Insight:** If the prompt group completes profiles faster but makes *fewer* connections, the prompt may be driving low-quality, "box-ticking" completions that do not improve network effects.

**4. Dismissal Friction Metrics**
Quantify the "harassment" factor directly.
*   **Metric:** Average number of times the prompt was dismissed before completion or permanent dismissal; percentage of users who closed the app immediately after the prompt appeared (bounce rate).
*   **Insight:** High immediate bounce rates or excessive dismissal clicks indicate aggressive UX that correlates with the DAU drop.

**5. Long-Term L7/L14 Lift on Core Actions**
Since the test was only 15 days, you cannot measure full monthly retention yet. Use leading indicators.
*   **Metric:** Compare the average number of sessions per user and total time spent in-app over the first 14 days of life for both groups.
*   **Decision Rule:** If the "Prompt" group shows higher profile completion but lower total sessions/time spent by Day 14, the feature drives vanity metrics at the cost of genuine engagement. Do not roll out globally.

**Immediate Next Step:** Segment the 5% DAU drop by "Completed" vs. "Did Not Complete" within the treatment group. If the drop is isolated entirely to those who ignored the prompt, consider making the prompt dismissible permanently after one view rather than removing the feature entirely.

Ensure your analysis window matches user age (e.g., only compare Day 7 retention for users who signed up at least 7 days ago) to avoid counting pending users as churned.
