**Recommendation: Do Not Make the Price Increase Permanent Across All Locations**

Based on the specific data from your January 5–February 2 experiment, the 18% price increase is financially inefficient and poses a risk to long-term demand. While RevPAR increased by 9%, the underlying elasticity reveals that you sacrificed too much volume for too little gain.

**The Math Behind the Decision**
Your data shows an 18% price hike resulted in a ~26% drop in conversion rate (from 4.2% to 3.1%). In hospitality, a drop in conversion of this magnitude typically signals that you have crossed the "value threshold" for your target demographic.

Critically, your RevPAR only grew by 9%. This means you gave up more than a quarter of your booking volume to achieve a single-digit revenue gain. In the boutique luxury segment, this trade-off is rarely sustainable because:
1.  **Fixed Cost Leverage:** Hotels have high fixed costs. A 26% drop in occupied rooms significantly increases your cost per occupied room, likely eroding a substantial portion of that 9% revenue gain from actual profit margins.
2.  **Long-Tail Damage:** Luxury travel relies heavily on repeat guests and word-of-mouth. A sharp price shock without a perceived increase in value damages brand affinity. Guests who bounce due to price rarely return, even if rates normalize, because they perceive the brand as "overpriced" rather than "premium."
3.  **Seasonality Context:** January and February are historically low-demand months in Prague. Price sensitivity is naturally higher during off-peak periods. Assuming brand loyalty would buffer this ignores the reality that leisure travelers in Q1 are often deal-hunters, not your core high-yield corporate or peak-season luxury segment.

**Strategic Narrative for Ownership**
For the Friday meeting, frame the experiment not as a failure, but as a successful identification of your **price ceiling**. The data proves that while VelvetStay has pricing power, an 18% blanket increase exceeds what the market will absorb during low-season weekends without severe volume penalties.

**Actionable Next Steps for Q2**
Instead of a permanent, across-the-board hike, adopt a **segmented dynamic pricing strategy**:
*   **Reject the Blanket Hike:** Revert base weekend rates to pre-experiment levels to restore volume and protect brand perception.
*   **Targeted Premiums:** Apply the 18% premium *only* to specific high-demand dates (e.g., local events, holidays) or specific room categories (suites) where elasticity is known to be lower.
*   **Value-Add Testing:** If you need to raise effective rates, test bundling the price increase with perceived value (e.g., included breakfast, late checkout) rather than a naked rate hike. This protects conversion rates while maintaining yield.

The 9% RevPAR gain is a "vanity metric" in this context; the 26% conversion collapse is the "sanity check." Prioritize occupancy stability and guest lifetime value over short-term yield optimization that alienates your base.
