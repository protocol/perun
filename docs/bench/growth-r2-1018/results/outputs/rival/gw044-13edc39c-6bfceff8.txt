**Recommended Core Loop Metric: Weekly Completed Challenges**

For FitQuest's product design team, **Weekly Completed Challenges** is the definitive metric to optimize for the home screen redesign. This metric directly captures the habit-forming loop you are trying to build, whereas Daily Active Users (DAU) and Social Shares are either too broad or too superficial for your specific goals.

**Why "Weekly Completed Challenges" Wins**

Your data indicates that a **five-day workout streak** is the critical threshold for long-term retention. A "Weekly Completed Challenge" inherently requires users to hit this 5-day frequency within a 7-day window. By optimizing for this metric, you are mathematically aligning your UI with the specific behavior (consistency) that drives your business outcome (monthly retention).

1.  **It Measures the Habit, Not Just Presence:**
    *   **DAU** is a vanity metric in this context. A user can open the app daily to check leaderboards or browse content without actually exercising. High DAU with low workout completion creates a false sense of health and engagement. It tells you they are *visiting*, not *building a habit*.
    *   **Weekly Completed Challenges** forces the measurement of the *completion* of the core value proposition: the exercise itself. It validates that the user didn't just show up; they finished the loop.

2.  **It Validates the Social Hypothesis Without Distraction:**
    *   **Social Shares** are an output, not the core loop. A user might share a workout once for clout but never return. Optimizing for shares might encourage UI that makes sharing easy but makes working out hard.
    *   Your assumption is that social competition *drives* consistency. If this is true, it will manifest as higher rates of **Weekly Completed Challenges**. If you optimize for shares, you might get viral noise without the underlying retention. If you optimize for completed challenges, and the social feature is working, the metric will rise naturally. If it doesn't rise, you know the social feature isn't driving the *right* behavior, even if share counts are high.

3.  **It Provides Actionable UI Direction:**
    *   Optimizing for **DAU** leads to UI that encourages endless scrolling or notification spam.
    *   Optimizing for **Shares** leads to UI that prioritizes post-workout pop-ups over workout initiation.
    *   Optimizing for **Weekly Completed Challenges** directs the home screen to answer three specific questions for the user every time they open the app:
        *   *What do I need to do today to keep my streak alive?*
        *   *How close am I to completing this week's challenge?*
        *   *Who am I competing against right now?*

**Implementation for the Redesign**
The home screen should visually prioritize the user's progress toward the current week's challenge. The primary Call-to-Action (CTA) should be "Start Today's Workout" (to secure the streak), with secondary visual cues showing the weekly progress bar and the immediate social standing. This focuses the user entirely on the action required to convert them into a retained, healthy user.

*Note: Ensure your analytics event `challenge_completed` fires only when the 5-day threshold is met within the 7-day window to maintain data integrity.*
*Avoid tracking "streaks" longer than 7 days as a primary metric for now; weekly cycles provide faster feedback loops for UI iteration.*
