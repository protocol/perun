**Recommendation: Merchant Repeat Rate**

For CargoUnite's current stage and strategic assumption, **Merchant Repeat Rate** is the correct North Star metric.

In a fragmented logistics market like Ho Chi Minh City, supply (truckers) is abundant but inconsistent, while demand (merchants) is sticky only when trust is established. Your core hypothesis—that lifetime value is driven by reliability rather than price—dictates that your primary goal must be proving that reliability repeatedly.

Here is the trade-off analysis for your leadership team:

**1. Total Gross Merchandise Value (GMV)**
*   **The Trap:** GMV incentivizes volume at any cost. In a low-margin aggregator model, chasing GMV often leads to subsidizing shipments or accepting low-reliability carriers to fill capacity.
*   **The Risk:** This encourages local optimization where the sales team pushes volume while operations struggle with failed deliveries. If you grow GMV by 20% but on-time performance drops, you are effectively scaling churn. In a fragmented market, one bad experience causes a merchant to revert to their old, familiar trucker permanently. GMV measures activity, not sustainability.

**2. Number of Successful Deliveries**
*   **The Trap:** This is an output metric that ignores customer retention. It treats every shipment as an isolated event.
*   **The Risk:** Your team could maximize this by focusing entirely on acquiring new merchants or one-off shipments, neglecting the service quality required to keep them. It fails to capture the "aggregator" advantage: network effects. If a merchant uses you once successfully but never returns, you have incurred acquisition costs without securing lifetime value. This metric aligns operations with throughput, not with building a recurring revenue base.

**3. Merchant Repeat Rate (Recommended)**
*   **The Alignment:** This metric directly validates your hypothesis. It forces the entire organization to prioritize the *quality* of the match between trucker and merchant.
    *   **Operations:** Must focus on on-time performance and issue resolution, because a failed delivery kills the second transaction.
    *   **Sales:** Must target merchants with recurring needs and set accurate expectations, rather than over-promising to close a one-time deal.
    *   **Product/Tech:** Must build features that improve matching algorithms and visibility, as these drive reliability.
*   **The Strategic Fit:** In a fragmented market, trust is the scarcest resource. Repeat rate measures trust. If merchants return, it proves your network is more reliable than their informal alternatives. This metric naturally balances supply and demand: you cannot have high repeat rates if you don't have enough reliable trucks (supply) or if you aren't solving real merchant pain points (demand).

**Implementation Note:**
Define "Repeat" clearly (e.g., a second shipment within 45 days) to ensure consistency. Track GMV and successful deliveries as secondary guardrail metrics to ensure you aren't sacrificing total scale while optimizing for retention.

By choosing Merchant Repeat Rate, you align CargoUnite around the only outcome that guarantees long-term sustainability: a merchant who trusts your network enough to make it their default logistics partner.
