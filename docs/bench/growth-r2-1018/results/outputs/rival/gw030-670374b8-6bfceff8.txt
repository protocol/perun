**Executive Summary: Pilot Data Interpretation & Rollout Recommendation**

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **controlled expansion** to 3–5 additional campuses rather than a full 10-campus national rollout immediately. The 10% adoption rate validates demand, but the 30% lower Average Order Value (AOV) presents a margin risk that requires validation before committing full budget.

**Data Assessment & Risk Analysis**

Your pilot data contains two conflicting signals: high volume (adoption) but low value (AOV). Before scaling, you must isolate the cause of the lower AOV to determine if it is a permanent demographic trait or a temporary pilot artifact.

1.  **Isolate the "Promo Effect" vs. "Demographic Effect":**
    *   **Hypothesis A (Temporary):** The lower AOV is driven solely by the introductory discount structure. If students bought full-price items elsewhere but used the code only for small add-ons, the low AOV is a measurement error of the *program*, not the *student*.
    *   **Hypothesis B (Permanent):** Students are inherently price-sensitive with lower disposable income. Even at full price, their basket size will remain 30% lower than your standard base.
    *   **Action:** Segment the pilot data. Compare AOV of items purchased *with* the discount vs. any full-price items bought by the same user IDs. If full-price baskets are also small, Hypothesis B is likely true.

2.  **Campus Specificity Check:**
    *   A single campus (15k students) is a statistically insufficient sample for a national rollout. Campus culture (e.g., a wealthy private university vs. a large state school) heavily influences spending power.
    *   **Risk:** Scaling to 10 diverse campuses based on one data point risks diluting margins if the pilot campus was an outlier in either adoption or spending.

3.  **Lifetime Value (LTV) Projection:**
    *   Do not evaluate students on first-order AOV alone. Students have a defined lifecycle (3–4 years).
    *   **Calculation Required:** Estimate LTV = (Average Order Frequency × Average Margin per Order × Retention Months).
    *   If student retention is 2x higher than your standard base, a 30% lower AOV may still yield higher total profit over time. If retention is equal, the lower AOV directly threatens profitability.

**Required Next Steps for CFO Approval**

To justify the budget for 10 campuses, present a **Phased Rollout Plan** instead of a binary "go/no-go":

*   **Phase 1 (Validation):** Launch in 3 distinct campus types (e.g., one large public, one private, one community college) for 60 days.
*   **Metric to Watch:** Track **Repeat Purchase Rate** and **Full-Price Conversion** in this phase. If students return to buy full-price items, the low initial AOV is an acceptable acquisition cost.
*   **Financial Guardrail:** Set a minimum blended margin target. If the 30% lower AOV cannot be offset by volume or frequency within 90 days, pause expansion.

**Conclusion:** The high adoption proves the *offer* works; the low AOV questions the *economics*. Validate the economics on a small, diverse sample before scaling to 10 campuses.

*Note: Ensure your tracking distinguishes between "discounted first order" and "subsequent full-price orders" to accurately calculate LTV. Do not blend these metrics in your CFO presentation.*
