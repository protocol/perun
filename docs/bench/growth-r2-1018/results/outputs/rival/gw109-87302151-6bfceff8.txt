**Recommendation: Prioritize "Breadth of Use" (Research Output Quality) as the Primary North Star Metric, with "Frequency" as a Critical Guardrail.**

For ScholarDB's Board meeting on July 15th, do not choose between frequency and breadth as mutually exclusive goals. Instead, structure your six-month roadmap around **maximizing research output quality (breadth)** while enforcing a **minimum threshold for usage frequency** to protect revenue.

**Strategic Rationale**
Your biggest institutional clients—the decision-makers for renewal contracts—value research output quality (citations) over login frequency. While daily users show a 10% higher renewal rate individually, institutional renewals are driven by demonstrated ROI in the form of published research. If optimizing for breadth increases citations by 30%, it directly addresses the core value proposition for your largest revenue sources. Frequency is a *leading indicator* of habit, but citations are the *lagging indicator* of mission success and contract justification.

**The Balanced Approach: "Quality at Scale"**
Adopt a strategy that treats breadth as the growth engine and frequency as the retention safety net.

1.  **Primary Objective (North Star):** Increase the average "Topic Diversity Score" per active user by 15% over six months. This aligns product development with the 30% citation lift, directly serving your mission and institutional clients' needs.
2.  **Revenue Guardrail:** Maintain a "Active Daily User" rate within 5% of current baselines. This ensures you do not sacrifice the habitual usage that drives individual renewal likelihood.

**Roadmap Implications (Next 6 Months)**
*   **Feature Focus:** Enhance discovery tools, cross-disciplinary recommendation algorithms, and "deep dive" session aids rather than quick-search optimizations.
*   **Measurement:** Track "Citations per Institutional Seat" as the primary success metric for the Board.
*   **Risk Mitigation:** Implement A/B tests on new "breadth" features to ensure they do not increase friction for daily searchers. If a feature boosts diversity but drops daily active users by >5%, it fails the guardrail and requires iteration.

**Addressing the Board's Concerns**
Explicitly acknowledge the risk: "We recognize that pushing for depth could reduce habitual logins. Therefore, our roadmap includes a strict 'frequency guardrail.' We will not launch any breadth-enhancing feature that causes daily active usage to drop below our defined threshold. This allows us to pursue higher-value outcomes (citations) without jeopardizing the habit-based renewal model."

This approach satisfies revenue goals by protecting the frequency baseline while aligning the product's strategic direction with the high-value outcomes that secure large institutional contracts.
