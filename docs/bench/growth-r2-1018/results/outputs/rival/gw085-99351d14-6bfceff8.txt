# FreshMart Loyalty Program Holdout Test Design

## Executive Summary
To measure the true lift of the new loyalty program launching February 1st in Texas, we will execute a randomized controlled trial (RCT) with a 90/10 split. This design isolates program impact from external factors like Super Bowl promotions and seasonal shifts by ensuring both test and control groups experience identical market conditions.

## 1. Group Structure & Randomization
*   **Unit of Randomization:** Randomize at the **customer/household ID level**, not the store level. This prevents store-specific variables (e.g., local demographics, staffing) from skewing results.
*   **Allocation:**
    *   **Test Group (90%):** Receives loyalty program invitation and full benefits.
    *   **Control Group (10%):** Does not receive the invitation. They continue shopping under standard pricing/promotions.
*   **Stratification:** Before random assignment, stratify customers by **historical average basket size** and **purchase frequency** (based on Jan 2023–Jan 2024 data). This ensures both groups have identical pre-test spending profiles, reducing variance and increasing statistical power.
*   **Exclusion Criteria:** Exclude customers with <2 visits in the past 6 months to focus on active shoppers where the "$5 lift" hypothesis is relevant.

## 2. Addressing Seasonality & External Events
Since we cannot change the calendar, we rely on the **parallel trends assumption**: if the program did not exist, the Test and Control groups would have moved in lockstep from January through April.
*   **Super Bowl & Seasonality:** Because both groups shop in the same Texas stores during the same weeks, the "Super Bowl bump" and seasonal shifts affect both equally. The *difference* in performance between the groups isolates the program's effect.
*   **Baseline Correction:** Use January 2024 data (pre-launch) as a covariate. If the Control group's basket size naturally rises 8% in February due to seasonality, we expect the Test group to rise 8% *plus* the program lift.

## 3. Key Covariates to Monitor
To ensure statistical validity and adjust for any minor randomization imbalances, track these covariates in your regression model:
*   **Pre-test Basket Size:** Average spend per trip (Jan 1–31).
*   **Purchase Frequency:** Visits per month (last 90 days).
*   **Store Location:** Fixed effects for specific Texas regions to account for local economic variations.
*   **Day-of-Week/Time:** To control for shopping pattern shifts.
*   **Promotion Exposure:** Flag transactions occurring during major circular drops or Super Bowl weeks to test for interaction effects (e.g., does the program amplify promo response?).

## 4. Maintaining Group Integrity
*   **Contamination Prevention:** Ensure POS systems strictly enforce the 10% block. Control customers must *never* accidentally receive loyalty pricing or points. If a control customer calls support, scripts must prevent manual enrollment.
*   **Consistent Communication:** Do not send "You were not selected" messaging to the control group. They should simply experience business-as-usual. Any awareness of exclusion could alter behavior (resentment bias).
*   **Duration:** Run the test for a full 12 weeks (Feb 1 – Apr 24). This covers the Super Bowl, post-holiday lull, and early spring, capturing a full cycle of shopping behaviors.
*   **Stopping Rules:** Do not peek at results before the 4-week mark to avoid false positives from early volatility. Monitor only for technical failures (e.g., control group accidentally getting points).

## 5. Success Metric Definition
*   **Primary Metric:** Incremental Average Basket Size (Test Avg – Control Avg).
*   **Statistical Threshold:** Detect a $5 lift with 95% confidence. Given typical grocery variance, ensure your sample size calculation accounts for a standard deviation of ~$15–$20 per basket.

By holding the environment constant and varying only the program access, this design attributes any divergence in basket size directly to the loyalty initiative, satisfying the VP's need for causal proof.
