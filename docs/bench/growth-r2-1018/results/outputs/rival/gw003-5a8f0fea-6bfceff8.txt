# ChefMate Behavioral Measurement Plan

To infer satisfaction and content quality without explicit feedback, shift focus from "what users say" to "what users do." In a hands-free cooking context, completion and flow continuity are the primary proxies for success. Implement tracking for the following four behavioral signal categories.

### 1. Recipe Completion & Abandonment Signals
**What to Track:**
*   **Step Completion Rate:** Percentage of users who trigger the "next step" command versus those who stop mid-recipe.
*   **Abandonment Point:** The specific step number where the session ends prematurely.
*   **Session Duration vs. Estimated Cook Time:** Compare actual active session time against the recipe's stated total time.

**How to Interpret:**
*   **High Drop-off at Specific Steps:** If 40% of users abandon a recipe at Step 3, the instruction is likely unclear, too complex, or missing an ingredient check. This is a content quality issue.
*   **Short Sessions (<2 mins):** Indicates immediate rejection; the recipe didn't match the user's intent (e.g., wrong dietary restriction or missing ingredients).
*   **Long Sessions with Low Step Count:** Suggests the user is stuck, confused, or struggling to trigger the next command, indicating friction in the conversation flow.

### 2. Interaction Friction & Repetition
**What to Track:**
*   **Repeat Command Rate:** Frequency of users repeating the same voice command (e.g., "Next," "Repeat") within a 10-second window.
*   **Error/No-Match Rate:** Instances where the skill responds with "I didn't catch that" or fails to trigger an intent.
*   **Help Trigger Frequency:** How often users explicitly ask "What can I say?" or "Help" during an active recipe.

**How to Interpret:**
*   **High Repeat Rates:** Indicates the system isn't recognizing voice input (perhaps due to kitchen noise) or the confirmation prompt is unclear. This is a technical/UX friction point.
*   **Spikes in Help Requests:** Users are lost in the flow. If this happens consistently at the ingredient list phase, the list may be too long to hold in working memory.

### 3. Engagement & Value Signals
**What to Track:**
*   **Save/Bookmark Rate:** Percentage of sessions where a user asks to "Save this" or "Send to phone."
*   **Re-cook Rate:** Users returning to the exact same recipe ID within 30 days.
*   **Variant Exploration:** Users who finish one recipe and immediately ask for "something similar" or "another like this."

**How to Interpret:**
*   **High Save/Re-cook Rate:** Strong proxy for satisfaction. Even without a 5-star rating, returning behavior confirms value.
*   **High Completion + Low Re-cook:** The recipe worked once, but wasn't memorable or delicious enough to repeat. Content may be functional but not delightful.

### 4. Post-Recipe Flow
**What to Track:**
*   **Immediate Exit:** Session ends immediately after the "Enjoy your meal" prompt.
*   **Continued Engagement:** User asks for dessert, wine pairing, or cleanup tips after the main recipe concludes.

**How to Interpret:**
*   **Continued Engagement:** Indicates a highly successful, engaging session where the user trusts the assistant.
*   **Immediate Exit:** Neutral signal; could be satisfaction (they are now cooking) or relief that the difficult interaction is over. Correlate this with completion rates to differentiate.

### Implementation Priorities for Design Team
1.  **Instrument Step-Level Events:** Ensure every voice intent (`next_step`, `repeat_step`, `ingredient_check`) logs the `recipe_id` and `step_number`.
2.  **Define "Active" Sessions:** Filter out sessions where the app is open but no voice interaction occurs for >5 minutes to avoid skewing duration data.
3.  **Create Friction Heatmaps:** Visualize drop-off rates by step number across all recipes to identify systemic conversation flow issues versus isolated bad content.

By monitoring these behaviors, you can identify exactly where users struggle and which recipes deliver value, turning silent usage into actionable design data.

*Note: Establish baseline metrics for these signals over 2 weeks before implementing design changes to measure improvement accurately.*
