### Conversion Event Framework for B2B Lead Gen

**Recommendation: Define "Conversion" as a Fully Completed Form Only**

For your Q4 KPIs and sales capacity planning, you must define a conversion strictly as a **fully completed form submission**. Counting partial submissions (e.g., contact info entered but budget unanswered) as conversions will inflate your metrics, misalign with your sales team's actual capacity, and obscure the root cause of your drop-off.

**The Capacity Constraint Logic**
Your sales team can handle 15 qualified calls per week (approx. 2–3 per day).
*   **Current State:** With 200 daily visits and a 9% completion rate, you generate ~18 completed forms daily. Even if only 50% are truly "sales-ready," this exceeds your team's capacity.
*   **Risk of Partial Counting:** If you count partials (where users enter email but skip budget), your "conversion rate" might artificially rise to 12–14%. This creates a false signal of success while flooding your team with unqualified leads who explicitly refused to share budget data.
*   **Alignment:** Since your team is already capacity-constrained, your marketing goal is not "more leads" but "more *qualified* leads." The budget questions act as a necessary friction filter. A user unwilling to answer them is unlikely to be a qualified opportunity for a sales call.

**Analyzing Drop-Off Signals: Disinterest vs. Friction**

To determine if the drop-off indicates genuine disinterest (good filtering) or excessive friction (bad UX), analyze the following signals in your analytics:

1.  **Drop-Off Location Specificity**
    *   **Signal for Disinterest (Good):** If 80%+ of drop-offs occur *exactly* at the two new budget fields, the form is working as intended. Users self-select out because they don't meet the budget criteria or aren't serious.
    *   **Signal for Friction (Bad):** If drop-offs are distributed evenly across all 12 fields, or spike at early fields (name/email), the form length itself is the barrier. Users are abandoning due to fatigue before even reaching the qualification questions.

2.  **Time-on-Page vs. Completion**
    *   **Signal for Disinterest:** Users who drop off at the budget questions have high time-on-page (they read and considered the question) but leave. This indicates a deliberate decision not to proceed.
    *   **Signal for Friction:** Users bounce immediately (low time-on-page) or exhibit "rage clicks" (rapid clicking) on the form fields. This suggests the interface is confusing or the ask feels overwhelming immediately.

3.  **Lead Quality Correlation (The Ultimate Test)**
    *   Compare the **Sales Qualified Lead (SQL) rate** of leads generated before August 20th (18% rate, no budget Qs) vs. after (9% rate, with budget Qs).
    *   **Winning Signal:** If the post-August 20th leads have a 2x higher SQL-to-Close rate, the drop-off is healthy filtering. Keep the long form.
    *   **Losing Signal:** If the SQL rate is identical but volume is halved, the budget questions are creating unnecessary friction without improving quality.

**Next Steps for Q4 Planning**
1.  **Set KPI:** Track "Completed Form Submissions" as the primary conversion.
2.  **Monitor:** Create a funnel visualization showing drop-off at each specific field.
3.  **Validate:** Request a report from Sales on lead quality for August/September vs. July. If quality hasn't improved, A/B test a shorter form (6 fields) against the current 12-field version to see if you can recover volume without sacrificing qualification.

*Note: Do not optimize for "partial conversions" until your sales team has excess capacity. Your current bottleneck is sales bandwidth, not lead volume.*
*Ensure your analytics tool tracks field-level abandonment to pinpoint exactly where users quit.*
