**Recommendation: Optimize for "Number of Unique Contributors per Project"**

For CodeCollab's mission of democratizing software development, "Number of Unique Contributors per Project" is the superior primary metric for Q3. While "Time to First Review" is a critical operational lever, it is a means to an end, not the end itself.

Your data already highlights the risk of optimizing solely for speed: fast reviews can correlate with a concentration of work among power users, creating a bottleneck rather than a broad base. If you prioritize speed alone, moderators may inadvertently favor familiar, high-velocity contributors to keep metrics low, excluding new voices who need more guidance. This contradicts the goal of democratization.

By making "Unique Contributors" the primary north star, you align the Community Manager's goals directly with ecosystem health. This metric forces the team to ask: "Are we bringing new people in?" rather than just "Are we moving fast?" Speed remains essential, but it becomes a secondary guardrail. You can set a target (e.g., "Maintain median review time under 48 hours") to ensure efficiency doesn't collapse, but the primary victory condition is broadening participation.

This approach directly addresses your unknown factor. If broadening participation is indeed more valuable for long-term health, this metric will reveal it by rewarding projects that successfully onboard diverse contributors, even if their initial review cycle is slightly slower due to the learning curve. It shifts the focus from servicing experts to cultivating new ones, which is the essence of democratization.

**Note:** Ensure moderators are trained to balance speed with inclusivity, as rushing new contributors can be as damaging as ignoring them. Track "Time to First Review" weekly as a health check to prevent it from degrading significantly.
