**Recommendation: Prioritize the "Upload-to-View" Journey Stage**

To maximize enterprise contract value (ECV) for CaseMate, engineering resources must be allocated to eliminating friction between document upload and analysis viewing. While the "share with colleague" step is a strong indicator of viral growth and team adoption, the "upload-to-view" transition is the fundamental predictor of individual user retention and immediate value realization.

**Rationale**

1.  **Magnitude of Leakage:** Your data indicates a 30% drop-off between upload and viewing results, compared to a 20% drop-off between viewing and sharing. Addressing the larger leak yields a higher immediate impact on the total addressable user base. Recovering even half of this lost segment (15% of total users) represents a significantly larger revenue opportunity than optimizing the secondary sharing step.

2.  **Prerequisite for Value:** A lawyer cannot perceive the tool's value proposition—"analysis within two minutes"—if they never see the results. The "share" action is a secondary behavior that presupposes the primary value has been delivered and validated. If the initial analysis is delayed, confusing, or fails to load, the user has no incentive to share, rendering the sharing step irrelevant for those 30% of users. You cannot optimize for team collaboration (sharing) before securing individual utility (viewing).

3.  **Enterprise Contract Value Driver:** Enterprise deals are sold on reliability and speed. The core promise of CaseMate is rapid contract review. If 30% of users experience a breakdown in this core promise during their first critical interaction, trust in the platform erodes immediately. High churn at this stage signals a failure in the product's primary function, which directly undermines renewal rates and expansion revenue in enterprise accounts. Fixing this ensures the baseline product works as advertised, which is the non-negotiable foundation for any upsell or seat expansion.

4.  **Technical Feasibility and Speed:** Since the hypothesis centers on the "two-minute" delivery window, the drop-off likely stems from performance latency, processing errors, or UI confusion regarding result availability. These are typically engineering-centric issues (backend processing speed, error handling, frontend state management) that can be diagnosed and resolved within a single sprint cycle. In contrast, low sharing rates often require product design changes, cultural shifts within law firms, or feature education, which take longer to influence.

**Measurement Strategy**

To validate this prioritization, rigorously measure the time-to-result latency for every upload attempt and correlate specific latency thresholds with the drop-off rate. Segment this data by file size and complexity to identify if specific document types are causing the bottleneck. Additionally, implement event tracking to distinguish between users who abandon due to technical failure versus those who simply navigate away, ensuring engineering efforts target the root cause of the attrition.

By securing the "upload-to-view" flow, you ensure every user experiences the core value, creating a stable foundation upon which sharing behaviors and enterprise expansion can naturally grow.

*Note: Ensure the "two-minute" SLA is technically enforced and visible to the user to reinforce trust.*
*Focus initial debugging on server-side processing queues and frontend loading states.*
