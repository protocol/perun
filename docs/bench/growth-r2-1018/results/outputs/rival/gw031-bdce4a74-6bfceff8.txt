**Recommendation: Prioritize Gross Merchandise Value (GMV) as your Q3 North Star**

For a two-sided marketplace like FixItNow in the early growth stage, optimizing for "total completed repairs" creates a dangerous misalignment. It incentivizes low-value, one-off transactions (e.g., bulb replacements) that inflate volume but fail to build a sustainable business model or prove the retention hypothesis. GMV directly correlates with revenue take-rate, technician earnings potential, and the complexity of jobs that typically drive higher homeowner retention.

**Framework for Decision: The Unit-Economic Viability Matrix**

Do not choose based on intuition; choose based on which metric drives sustainable unit economics. Use this framework to validate the shift to GMV:

1.  **Hypothesis Validation:** Explicitly test the assumption that "Higher Job Value = Higher 6-Month Retention."
    *   *Action:* Segment your existing 4,500 jobs into quartiles by job value. Calculate the 30-day and 90-day repeat booking rate for each quartile. If the top quartile does not show significantly higher retention than the bottom quartile, GMV is still the correct north star, but your retention strategy needs a pivot, not a metric change.
2.  **Supply-Demand Equilibrium:** Ensure optimizing for GMV doesn't starve the platform of liquidity.
    *   *Constraint:* High-value jobs are less frequent. If you prioritize GMV exclusively, technician utilization might drop, causing churn on the supply side.
    *   *Check:* Monitor "Technician Earnings Per Active Hour." If GMV rises but earnings/hour falls (due to idle time waiting for big jobs), the model breaks.
3.  **Leading Indicators to Track:**
    *   **Average Order Value (AOV) by Category:** Track this weekly to ensure the mix is shifting toward complex repairs without manual intervention.
    *   **Repeat Booking Rate (30/60/90 days):** The ultimate lagging indicator of product-market fit.
    *   **Technician Utilization Rate:** Percentage of active technicians completing at least one job per week.
    *   **Request-to-Fill Time:** Segmented by job value. If high-value jobs sit unfilled >24 hours, you have a supply gap, not a demand problem.

**Pros and Cons Analysis**

| Metric | Pros | Cons | Verdict for FixItNow |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Total Completed Repairs** | • Easy to understand.<br>• Drives rapid user acquisition.<br>• Good for initial liquidity. | • Encourages low-margin work.<br>• Masks revenue stagnation.<br>• Weak correlation to LTV. | **Reject.** Dangerous for Q3. It rewards activity, not value. |
| **Gross Merchandise Value** | • Aligns with revenue.<br>• Incentivizes complex, sticky jobs.<br>• Better proxy for technician livelihood. | • May slow total transaction count.<br>• Requires higher trust/quality control. | **Select.** Directly tests your core business hypothesis. |

**Implementation Plan for Q3**
Shift the roadmap to features that increase average job size: bundling services (e.g., "Full Bathroom Refresh" vs. "Fix Leak"), verified pro tiers for complex plumbing, and dynamic pricing that rewards technicians for high-skill tasks. Do not optimize for the number of clicks; optimize for the dollar value of the problem solved.

*Note: Ensure your analytics track "Job Value" as a distinct property on every transaction event immediately to enable the segmentation above.*
*Do not set GMV targets without a floor on technician utilization to prevent supply-side collapse.*
