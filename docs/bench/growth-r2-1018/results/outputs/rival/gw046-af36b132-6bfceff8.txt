**Recommendation: Do Not Build Features to Push Early Phone Number Exchange**

Based on the data provided, you should **not** prioritize engineering resources to force or incentivize phone number exchange within the first five messages. The correlation you observed is almost certainly a symptom of high user intent, not the cause of retention.

**Analysis of the Retention Curve**
Your cohort data shows a standard but steep decay: 40% retention at Week 2 dropping to 15% by Week 8. This 25-point drop indicates a failure to transition users from "initial matching" to "sustained engagement."

The users exchanging numbers early are self-selecting. They have already achieved the core value proposition of a dating app (finding a match they trust) and are naturally migrating off-platform. Pushing hesitant users to share numbers early will not replicate this success; instead, it will likely:
1.  **Increase Privacy Friction:** Users not ready to share personal data will churn immediately due to safety concerns.
2.  **Decrease Match Quality:** It encourages low-effort interactions aimed solely at extracting contact info rather than building rapport.
3.  **Fail Causality:** Users who would have churned by Week 3 regardless will simply churn at Message 5.

**Strategic Direction for Engineering Sprint**
The engineering team should focus on **extending high-quality in-app conversation duration** between Message 2 and Message 10. The goal is to help users build enough rapport to *want* to exchange numbers naturally, or to keep them engaged if they aren't ready yet.

**Prioritized Feature Interventions:**
1.  **Conversation Starters & Prompts:** Implement context-aware icebreakers based on profile data to reduce the friction of the first message. In Buenos Aires culture, where conversation and wit are highly valued, generic openers often fail.
2.  **Engagement Gamification:** Introduce lightweight interactive elements (e.g., "This or That" questions, photo challenges) that can only be done in-app to deepen the connection before moving off-platform.
3.  **Safety & Trust Signals:** Instead of pushing for numbers, build features that verify identity or highlight mutual interests, increasing the comfort level for eventual off-app movement.

**Interpretation of User Intent**
The 15% Week 8 retention represents your "power users" who found genuine long-term connections or highly engaging ongoing conversations. The drop-off between Week 2 and Week 8 suggests that for the majority, the initial excitement fades because the conversation stalls or the match proves superficial.

In the local dating culture, trust is built through sustained interaction. By trying to shortcut this process, you risk alienating the very users you need to retain. The metric to watch is not "time to phone number," but "messages exchanged per successful match" and "conversation depth score."

**Next Steps for Monday's Sprint Planning:**
*   **Deprioritize:** Any ticket related to prompting, gating, or rewarding early phone number sharing.
*   **Prioritize:** Features that increase message reply rates and conversation length within the first 48 hours of a match.
*   **Measurement:** Define success as an increase in Week 4 retention, not Week 2 contact exchange rates.
