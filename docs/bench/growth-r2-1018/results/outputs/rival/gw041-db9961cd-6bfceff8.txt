### Recommended Activation Metrics for SwiftWrap

Given your constraint of two engineers and a board meeting focused on revenue justification, you should immediately stop analyzing 40+ events. For an AI productivity tool targeting real estate agents, volume of clicks is noise; **completion of valuable output** is the signal.

Here are the only two metrics you need to track for activation:

#### 1. The "Aha" Metric: Property Summaries Generated (Week 1)
**Definition:** The count of agents who generate **3 or more complete property summaries** within their first 7 days.
**Why this predicts revenue:**
Real estate agents do not pay for "AI"; they pay for time saved on listing descriptions. Your assumption is correct: the value proposition only clicks when an agent sees the tool successfully draft multiple listings. One summary might be a test; three indicates the agent has integrated SwiftWrap into their actual workflow for multiple properties. This directly correlates to the "time-to-value" moment where the agent realizes, "This tool just saved me two hours of writing." If they don't hit this threshold, they will churn regardless of how many buttons they clicked.

#### 2. The "Commitment" Metric: Exported or Saved Summaries
**Definition:** The count of agents who **copy, download, or save** at least one generated summary to their device or CRM.
**Why this predicts revenue:**
Generation is passive; export is active intent. An agent might generate ten summaries to test the AI's quality but only export the ones they deem good enough to use professionally. This action proves the output met the quality bar for a real transaction. It bridges the gap between "trying the tool" and "relying on the tool." If an agent generates summaries but never exports them, your AI quality is likely the bottleneck, not the feature set. This metric filters out curious browsers from serious users.

### What to Ignore (The "Do Not Build" List)
To save engineering time, **ignore** the following immediately:
*   **Page views:** Irrelevant for a functional tool.
*   **Button clicks (unless it's "Generate" or "Export"):** Clicking "Settings" or "Profile" does not predict revenue.
*   **Time on site:** Agents want speed, not longevity.
*   **Total events:** Aggregate counts hide the specific behavior of your power users.

### Implementation Plan for Two Engineers
1.  **Strip Tracking:** Remove 38 of your 40 current events. Keep only `summary_generated` and `summary_exported` (plus basic signup info).
2.  **Simple Query:** Instead of a dashboard, write one SQL query or use a simple spreadsheet to calculate: *"Of the 150 beta users, what % hit 3+ summaries in week 1, and what % of those converted to paid?"*
3.  **Board Narrative:** Present this binary: "Agents who hit 3 summaries convert at X%. Our Q2 goal is to move 40% of new signups to this threshold by improving the generation flow."

**Note:** Validate your current data quickly by manually checking if any of the 150 users have already paid; see if they all hit the "3 summaries" mark.
**Note:** If "Export" is technically difficult to track right now, substitute it with "Summary viewed for >30 seconds" as a proxy for quality validation.
