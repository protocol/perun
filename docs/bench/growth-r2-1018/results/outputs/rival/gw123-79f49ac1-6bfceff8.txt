### Executive Summary: Resolving the Variant B Contradiction

**Recommendation: No-Go for Full Rollout.**
Proceeding with Variant B in its current state is high-risk. The 22% increase in session duration is likely a "friction metric" (users struggling to find data) rather than an "engagement metric" (users delighting in discovery). The 15% rise in support tickets and qualitative reports of "clutter" confirm that the extra time spent is inefficient, not valuable. Rolling this out will increase support costs and churn, negating any perceived engagement gains.

### Strategic Analysis: Why Data and Feedback Conflict

The contradiction arises because **session duration is being misinterpreted as a positive signal**. In complex dashboards, longer sessions often indicate navigation failure, not deeper exploration. Users are likely clicking through multiple menus or scanning repeatedly to locate the same data points they previously accessed quickly. The quantitative "retention" may simply be users persisting through frustration because they have no alternative, not because they prefer the experience.

### Immediate Action Plan (Pre-Steering Committee)

To bridge the gap between behavior and sentiment before next week's meeting, gather these three specific data points:

**1. Task Completion Time & Success Rate (Efficiency Metrics)**
Stop measuring "time on page" and start measuring "time to value."
*   **Action:** Isolate the top 3 critical tasks users perform on the dashboard (e.g., "Find monthly revenue," "Export user list").
*   **Measurement:** Compare the average time to complete these specific tasks in Variant B vs. Control.
*   **Hypothesis:** Variant B will show significantly *slower* completion times despite higher overall session duration. This proves the layout adds friction.

**2. Rage Clicks and Dead Clicks (Behavioral Frustration)**
*   **Action:** Use session replay or heatmapping tools to identify "rage clicks" (rapid repeated clicking) or "dead clicks" (clicking elements that aren't interactive) on Variant B.
*   **Measurement:** Calculate the rate of frustration clicks per session.
*   **Hypothesis:** Variant B will show a higher concentration of frustration clicks around the new data points, validating the "cluttered" feedback.

**3. Support Ticket Categorization (Qualitative Quantification)**
*   **Action:** Manually tag the increased support tickets from the last two weeks.
*   **Measurement:** Determine the percentage of new tickets specifically citing "cannot find X," "confusing layout," or "too much information."
*   **Hypothesis:** If >50% of the 15% increase relates to navigation confusion, the cost of the redesign outweighs the engagement lift.

### Decision Framework for the CDO

Present the findings using this matrix to the steering committee:

| Metric | Control | Variant B | Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | : |
| **Session Duration** | Baseline | +22% | **Negative:** Indicates friction, not engagement. |
| **Task Completion Time** | Baseline | Likely Higher | **Critical:** Users are less efficient. |
| **Support Volume** | Baseline | +15% | **Cost:** Direct increase in operational expense. |
| **User Sentiment** | Neutral | Negative | **Risk:** High churn potential if forced. |

**Final Strategy:** Do not roll out Variant B. Instead, initiate a rapid iteration cycle:
1.  **Pause** the test or revert to Control for 50% of traffic to halt support ticket growth.
2.  **Redesign** Variant B to hide secondary data points behind "expandable" sections rather than exposing them upfront.
3.  **Re-test** with "Task Completion Time" as the primary success metric, replacing "Session Duration."

The goal of a dashboard is efficiency, not prolonged exposure. Users saying it is "difficult" while staying longer is the classic signature of a usability failure, not a success.

*Note: Ensure your analytics tool is configured to track specific button clicks for critical tasks, not just page views, to get the efficiency data required.*
