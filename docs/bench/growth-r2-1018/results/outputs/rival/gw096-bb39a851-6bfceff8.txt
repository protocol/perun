# Executive Briefing: Enterprise Churn Post-Pricing Adjustment

**To:** Chief Revenue Officer
**From:** Analytics Lead
**Date:** October 26, 2023
**Subject:** Divergent Response to New Pricing Tiers – Root Cause Analysis & Action Plan

### Current Situation
Following our recent pricing tier adjustment, we are observing a sharp divergence in customer behavior:
*   **SMB Segment:** Plan upgrades increased by **25%**, indicating strong alignment with the new value proposition for smaller teams.
*   **Enterprise Segment:** Churn rate increased by **10%** within the first three weeks.

Our initial hypothesis—that enterprises are uniquely sensitive to per-seat pricing while SMBs focus on feature limits—is not fully supported by current aggregate data. We cannot yet distinguish if this churn is a temporary reaction to the announcement or a fundamental mismatch in value perception for large teams.

### Immediate Data Collection Requirements
To isolate the root cause, we must move beyond aggregate churn numbers and analyze specific usage patterns of departing accounts versus retained ones. We need to collect and segment the following data points immediately:

1.  **Seat Utilization Rates:** Compare the percentage of paid seats actively used in the 30 days prior to cancellation for churned accounts vs. retained accounts. Low utilization suggests price sensitivity; high utilization suggests a value mismatch.
2.  **Feature Adoption Depth:** Identify if churned enterprises were heavy users of specific features that may have been deprioritized or capped in the new tiers.
3.  **Admin vs. End-User Activity:** Analyze if the cancellation decision correlates with a drop in Admin-level logins (indicating internal advocacy loss) or purely end-user activity.
4.  **Support Ticket Sentiment:** Tag and analyze support tickets from enterprise accounts in the three weeks post-announcement for keywords related to "cost," "seats," and "budget approval."
5.  **Time-to-Churn Distribution:** Map the exact day of cancellation relative to the announcement date. A spike immediately following the announcement suggests shock/reaction; a steady drip suggests a calculated decision based on renewal cycles.

### Preliminary Interpretation
The divergent behavior suggests our new pricing model successfully unlocked value for SMBs (likely through clearer feature tiers) but may have inadvertently disrupted the economic model for enterprises.

If data shows churned enterprises had **high seat utilization**, the issue is likely **total contract cost shock** rather than per-seat sensitivity alone. They may be hitting a "cliff" where the new tier forces them into a significantly higher price bracket for marginal additional value. Conversely, if utilization was **low**, the churn may be a strategic cleanup of underused licenses triggered by the price hike, rather than a loss of faith in the product.

### Recommended Next Steps

**1. Launch "Save" Interviews (Immediate)**
Sales Leadership must conduct exit interviews with the top 10 churned enterprise accounts this week. The goal is not to win them back immediately, but to validate the quantitative data: Was the decision driven by budget caps, perceived unfairness in seat pricing, or a specific missing capability?

**2. Segment Retention Offers (48 Hours)**
Based on early data, prepare two distinct retention paths:
*   **For High-Utilization Accounts:** Offer a "legacy grace period" or a custom volume discount to smooth the transition, acknowledging their loyalty.
*   **For Low-Utilization Accounts:** Propose a rightsizing consultation to optimize their seat count under the new model, reducing their bill while maintaining the relationship.

**3. Pause Broad Enterprise Outreach**
Until we understand the specific friction point, halt proactive upsell campaigns to the enterprise segment to avoid triggering further churn.

**4. Establish a Daily Churn Dashboard**
Implement a daily view of enterprise cancellations tagged by "reason code" (if available) and seat utilization to monitor if the 10% rate stabilizes or accelerates.

### Conclusion
The SMB success proves the new tiers have merit, but the enterprise churn indicates a specific friction point in how large teams consume and pay for value. By correlating usage depth with cancellation timing, we can determine if this is a fixable communication/pricing gap or a structural issue requiring a dedicated enterprise tier adjustment.

***

*Note: This analysis relies on the assumption that the 10% churn figure is net of new enterprise acquisitions; if gross churn is higher, the urgency increases. Immediate access to raw usage logs is required to validate the seat utilization hypothesis.*
