### Executive Diagnosis: TeamSync Churn Signal

Based on the data provided, the simultaneous 18% rise in zero-activity organizations and the 4-minute drop in average session duration strongly indicates a **product engagement or utility issue**, rather than a pure pricing problem or broad market shift.

**Why this is likely not Pricing:**
Pricing friction typically manifests *before* usage drops (at renewal or upgrade points) or results in "zombie" accounts where users log in briefly to check status but don't collaborate. A sharp decline in session duration suggests users are logging in but failing to complete workflows, or finding less value per visit. If this were purely price-driven, you would likely see stable session durations until the moment of cancellation, not a gradual erosion of engagement.

**Why this is likely not Seasonal/Market:**
You correctly noted the trend persisted past typical holiday windows into April. A general market shift usually affects new sales pipelines first; a sudden drop in *existing* enterprise usage suggests an internal trigger.

**Primary Hypotheses to Investigate:**

1.  **The "Broken Workflow" Hypothesis (Highest Probability):**
    The 4-minute session drop suggests a specific feature or integration critical to enterprise workflows may be degraded, slow, or broken. Users log in, encounter friction, fail to complete their task, and leave.
    *   *Action:* Correlate the March 1st start date with your release history. Did a deployment occur on Feb 28th or March 1st? Check error logs for spikes in failed API calls or latency in core features (e.g., file syncing, video loading, search) specifically for enterprise segments.

2.  **The "Project Cliff" Hypothesis:**
    Enterprise usage is often project-based. If several major customer implementations concluded simultaneously in late February, usage would naturally plummet.
    *   *Action:* Segment the "zero active user" list by customer tenure and contract start date. Are these customers who started 6 or 12 months ago (typical project cycles)? If the churn is concentrated in specific cohorts, it is a natural lifecycle event, not a product failure.

3.  **The "Competitor Displacement" Hypothesis:**
    If a competitor launched a feature that makes your tool redundant for a specific use case, users might migrate mid-contract.
    *   *Action:* Review support tickets and sales feedback from March onwards. Look for keywords like "missing feature," "switching," or specific competitor names. Check if the drop is isolated to a specific industry vertical.

### Immediate Next Steps for Investigation

1.  **Technical Audit (Immediate):** Isolate the session duration drop. Filter analytics by feature usage. Did *all* features drop 4 minutes, or did one specific module (e.g., "Whiteboard" or "Gantt Chart") collapse? If one module dropped significantly, you have found the broken workflow.
2.  **Cohort Analysis:** Separate the 18% of struggling organizations into two groups: those who canceled vs. those who are still paying but inactive. Contact 5 customers from the "paying but inactive" group immediately for a "health check" call. Ask: "We noticed usage patterns changed; is there a blocker we can remove?"
3.  **Release Rollback Review:** If a specific release coincides with March 1st, prepare a rollback plan or a hotfix while the investigation continues.

**Recommendation to CEO:**
Frame this as a **Product Health Incident** rather than a Revenue Churn crisis *for now*. The leading indicator is engagement (session time), not cancellation. If we fix the engagement drop, we likely stop the churn. Allocate engineering resources to identify the friction point causing the 4-minute drop before launching discount campaigns, which will not solve a utility problem.

*Note: Ensure your "zero active users" definition excludes API-only integrations, as some enterprise clients may have shifted to automated background syncs which wouldn't register as "sessions" but still derive value.*
*Prioritize the "health check" calls over surveys; qualitative feedback will reveal the "why" faster than quantitative data in this specific crisis.*
