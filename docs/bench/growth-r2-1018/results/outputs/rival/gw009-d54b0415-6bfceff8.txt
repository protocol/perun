# TalentBridge Marketplace Health Dashboard

## Executive Summary
Since the January 10 relaunch, while total matches have grown to 450, the time-to-hire has doubled from 4 to 7 days. To address board concerns on scalability, we must move beyond "total matches" and track the efficiency of our two-sided engine. The goal is to identify whether delays stem from a lack of freelancer supply or friction in the negotiation process.

## Defining Success for Both Sides

To measure health, we must define what a "good experience" looks like for each user group:

**For Creative Agencies (The Buyers):**
*   **Success Definition:** Posting a job results in a hired designer quickly, with minimal back-and-forth clarification.
*   **Key Metric:** **Time-to-First-Qualified-Proposal.** Instead of just counting applications, we measure how many hours pass before an agency receives a proposal that clearly addresses the project scope. If this number is high, freelancers are ignoring posts due to unclear scopes.

**For Freelance Designers (The Sellers):**
*   **Success Definition:** Finding paid work that matches their skills without wasting time on vague inquiries or prolonged negotiations that go nowhere.
*   **Key Metric:** **Proposal-to-Hire Conversion Rate.** This measures how many proposals actually turn into jobs. A low rate indicates that freelancers are spending time negotiating deals that eventually fall through, suggesting friction in the negotiation phase or mismatched expectations.

## Diagnosing the Bottleneck

To determine if the delay is caused by supply shortages or negotiation friction, we will track these three specific diagnostics:

1.  **The "Silence" Ratio (Supply Check):**
    *   *Measurement:* The percentage of job posts that receive zero proposals within 24 hours.
    *   *Diagnosis:* If this number is rising, our assumption is correct: there are not enough freelancers for the volume of jobs. The bottleneck is **Supply**.

2.  **The "Ghosting" Rate (Negotiation Check):**
    *   *Measurement:* The percentage of conversations where a proposal is sent, but no contract is signed within 5 days.
    *   *Diagnosis:* If agencies are getting proposals quickly (low Silence Ratio) but deals aren't closing (high Ghosting Rate), the bottleneck is **Negotiation Friction**. This suggests project scopes are unclear, or terms are stalling the deal.

3.  **The "Clarity" Score (Root Cause Check):**
    *   *Measurement:* Track how often freelancers ask clarifying questions in the first message versus submitting a direct proposal.
    *   *Diagnosis:* A high volume of "Can you explain more?" messages indicates that agency job posts are too vague, causing delays before negotiations even begin.

## Measuring Market Balance

Scalability depends on balancing supply and demand. We will measure this using **Fill Rate by Category**.

*   **How it works:** We calculate the percentage of jobs posted that are successfully filled within 7 days, broken down by design specialty (e.g., UX, Graphic, Motion).
*   **Why it matters:** This prevents us from looking at averages that hide problems. We might have too many graphic designers (surplus) but a critical shortage of UX designers (bottleneck).
*   **Target State:** A healthy market has a Fill Rate above 80% across all major categories. Any category dropping below 60% requires immediate recruitment focus for that specific freelancer type.

## Recommended Immediate Actions

1.  **Segment the Data:** Break down the 7-day average by design category to see if the delay is universal or isolated to specific skills.
2.  **Audit Job Posts:** Review the 20% of jobs with the longest time-to-hire to check for vague descriptions.
3.  **Introduce a "Scope Checklist":** Require agencies to answer three specific questions about budget and deliverables before posting, reducing back-and-forth clarification time.

By shifting focus from total volume to these efficiency metrics, we can pinpoint exactly where the process breaks and prove to the board that we have a clear path to restoring the 4-day hire cycle.
