# SwiftRide Referral Program Test: Findings & Recommendations
**Period:** April 10 – April 24 | **Incentive:** $10 Double-Sided Credit

## Executive Summary
The referral test successfully drove volume (+150% referrals) but failed to ensure quality. With 35% of new accounts flagged as potentially synthetic, the program is currently subsidizing fraud rather than organic growth. The double-sided $10 incentive creates an immediate arbitrage opportunity ($20 value) that exceeds the cost of acquiring synthetic identities (e.g., burner phones, temporary emails). While some flags may be false positives from shared households, the scale suggests systematic exploitation. **Recommendation: Pause the city-wide expansion immediately.** Proceed only after implementing stricter verification and adjusting the reward structure to delay gratification for high-risk behaviors.

## Data Evaluation: Fraud vs. Genuine Growth

### 1. The Fraud Signal (High Confidence)
The 35% flag rate is statistically anomalous for a genuine urban mobility user base.
*   **Arbitrage Logic:** Bad actors can generate infinite $20 value pairs if the cost of identity < $20. Current verification likely costs them less.
*   **Velocity:** A 150% spike in two weeks without a corresponding marketing push usually indicates bot activity or coordinated "farming" rather than viral organic sharing.
*   **Conclusion:** The majority of the flagged 35% are likely fraudulent. Treating them as genuine would inflate CAC and drain the marketing budget before the city-wide launch.

### 2. The False Positive Risk (Moderate Confidence)
Urban mobility users do share devices (family plans, roommates in dense cities).
*   **Shared IPs/Wi-Fi:** Legitimate users in apartments or offices share IPs.
*   **Device Sharing:** Families may share a tablet or older phone.
*   **Mitigation:** If we tighten rules too aggressively (e.g., blocking shared IPs), we will penalize our highest-value dense urban clusters. We must distinguish between *identity* fraud and *device* sharing.

## Strategic Recommendations

### Immediate Actions (Before City-Wide Launch)
1.  **Pause Expansion:** Do not scale until the fraud rate is <5%. Scaling now amplifies loss.
2.  **Audit the 35%:** Manually review a sample of 50 flagged accounts.
    *   *Check:* Did they take a real ride? Accounts with $0 ride revenue but $10 credit usage are 99% fraud.
    *   *Check:* Device fingerprinting. Are 100 accounts coming from 5 device IDs?
3.  **Clawback Mechanism:** Implement a rule where credits are "pending" until the referee completes a paid ride of at least $15. This kills the incentive for empty account creation.

### Structural Adjustments
1.  **Shift from Double-Sided to Asymmetric/Reward-on-Action:**
    *   *Current:* $10 Referrer + $10 Referee on signup.
    *   *Proposed:* $5 Referee on signup + $15 Referrer *only after* referee's first paid ride.
    *   *Why:* This forces the fraudster to actually use the service (incurring cost/risk) to get the full reward, reducing arbitrage margins.
2.  **Tiered Verification:**
    *   *Low Risk:* Email/Phone verification only for small credits.
    *   *High Risk:* Require payment method validation (valid card check) before releasing *any* credit. Fraudsters rarely use traceable payment methods for farming.
3.  **Cap Velocity:** Limit referrals per user to 5 per month initially. Genuine users rarely refer >5 people in a week; bots do.

## Decision Matrix for Leadership

| Option | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Launch as Is** | Fast execution | High financial loss; skewed data | **Reject** |
| **Pause & Tighten** | Protects budget; ensures real growth | Delays launch by 1-2 weeks | **Recommended** |
| **Reduce Reward** | Lowers arbitrage margin | May reduce genuine referrals too | **Secondary Option** |

**Next Steps:** Freeze the program for 5 days to implement the "Credit on First Ride" logic and payment validation. Re-run a pilot with these controls before approving the city-wide rollout.

*Note: False positives can be reduced by whitelisting known residential IP ranges or allowing manual appeal for flagged accounts with verified ride history.*
