# EduStart Pre-Launch Success Framework

## Primary Metric: Lesson 3 Completion Rate (Activation Rate)

**Definition:** The percentage of users who start Lesson 1 and successfully complete Lesson 3 within 7 days of signup.

**Why this is your "One Key Metric":**
You hypothesized that completing the first three lessons predicts long-term retention. In pre-revenue edtech, **activation** (experiencing core value) is the only valid proxy for product-market fit. Time spent can be inflated by confusion; social shares are vanity until the core product sticks. If students cannot get through the initial learning curve, they will never pay. This metric directly validates whether your content resonates and your UX is functional.

**Target:** Aim for **25–30%** of starters reaching Lesson 3. If below 15%, you have a product/usability problem, not a marketing problem.

---

## Supporting Metrics (The "Why" and "How")

Track only these three secondary metrics to diagnose issues with your primary metric:

1.  **Step-by-Step Drop-off Rate:** Identify exactly where students quit (e.g., between Lesson 1 and 2). This tells you *what* to fix.
2.  **Time-to-First-Value:** Average time from signup to completing Lesson 1. Long delays indicate onboarding friction.
3.  **Return Rate (Day 3 & Day 7):** Percentage of users who come back after their first session. This confirms initial interest isn't a one-off spike.

*Ignore:* Total time spent, page views, and social shares for now. They do not correlate with retention at this stage.

---

## Measurement Plan for Advisory Board

### 1. The Hypothesis
"We believe that students who complete the first three coding lessons will return weekly. We will validate product-market fit by achieving a 25% activation rate among our waitlist converts."

### 2. Tracking Implementation (Immediate Action)
Since you launch next week, implement this immediately:
*   **Event 1:** `lesson_started` (Property: `lesson_number`)
*   **Event 2:** `lesson_completed` (Property: `lesson_number`, `time_spent`)
*   **Event 3:** `user_registered` (Property: `source`, `school_name`)

*Tooling:* Use Google Analytics 4 (GA4) with Enhanced Measurement or a lightweight product tool like PostHog/Mixpanel if engineering resources allow. Ensure events fire on the *success* screen of each lesson, not just the click.

### 3. The Validation Window
*   **Cohort:** Users who sign up in Week 1.
*   **Observation Period:** 14 days. (Allow 7 days for them to start, 7 days to finish).
*   **Calculation:** (Users completing Lesson 3 by Day 14) ÷ (Users starting Lesson 1).

### 4. Decision Framework
Present this logic to your board:
*   **If Rate > 25%:** Product-market fit signal is strong. Focus shifts to acquisition (inviting more waitlist users).
*   **If Rate 15–25%:** Promising but fragile. Investigate drop-off points; iterate content/UX before scaling ads.
*   **If Rate < 15%:** Stop acquisition. The product does not yet deliver value. Pivot content or onboarding immediately.

### 5. Qualitative Loop
For every user who drops off before Lesson 3, trigger an automated email (or manual check-in for the first 50 users): *"We noticed you stopped at Lesson 2. Was it too hard, too easy, or a technical bug?"* This qualitative data is critical to interpret the quantitative drop-off.

**Summary for Board:** We are not measuring "traffic" or "buzz." We are measuring **value delivery**. Until we prove students can successfully learn from our first module, no other metric matters.

---
*Note: Ensure your tracking distinguishes between "clicked start" and "actually completed" to avoid false positives.*
*Re-evaluate this threshold after 100 completed activations; your historical data may refine the "Lesson 3" rule.*
