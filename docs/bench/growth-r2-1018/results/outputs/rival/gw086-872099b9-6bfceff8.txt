**Recommendation: Primary Goal**

**Primary Metric:** **Onboarding Completion Rate within 24 Hours** (Percentage of new hires who fully complete the module with valid data within one business day of invitation).

**Supporting Metrics:**
*   **Data Quality Score:** Percentage of records passing automated validation rules (e.g., valid ID formats, mandatory fields populated correctly) without requiring HR admin intervention.
*   **Admin Time Saved:** Average time spent by client HR teams correcting errors or chasing incomplete profiles (measured via weekly client check-ins).
*   **Time-to-First-Value:** Days from contract start to the first employee successfully processed through the full workflow.

**Rationale for B2B Pilot Context**

In a six-week enterprise pilot with only five clients, statistical significance on "speed alone" or "satisfaction scores" is insufficient. Your assumption that speed drives satisfaction is likely correct, but in B2B HR software, **speed without accuracy creates technical debt for the client.** If new hires finish in one hour but enter incorrect tax numbers or banking details, the HR team's workload increases, destroying the value proposition regardless of completion speed.

Therefore, the primary goal must be a **compound metric** that enforces both constraints. By defining success as "Completion within 24 Hours *with Valid Data*," you align the product's performance with the client's actual operational reality.

1.  **Reflects True Business Value:** For an HR director, a fast onboarding process that requires manual cleanup is a failure. This metric ensures TalentFlow is measured on *outcomes* (ready-to-work employees), not just *output* (forms submitted).
2.  **Fits the Pilot Constraints:** With only ~1,500–2,500 total users across five clients, you cannot rely on broad satisfaction surveys (which are lagging and subjective) to make go/no-go decisions. A binary success rate (Did they finish correctly in <24h?) provides immediate, actionable data every week.
3.  **De-risks the Expansion:** If you prioritize speed alone, you risk scaling a product that breaks downstream payroll integrations due to bad data. If you prioritize accuracy alone, you may build a cumbersome flow that users abandon. The compound metric forces the engineering team to optimize the UX for clarity and speed simultaneously.
4.  **Aligns Stakeholders:** The CEO sees a clear adoption metric (completion rate), while Customer Success sees a reduction in support tickets (data quality). This prevents internal conflict over whether the pilot is "working."

**Implementation Note:** Track this daily per client. If the rate drops below 85%, investigate immediately whether the bottleneck is UX confusion (speed issue) or validation errors (accuracy issue). Do not wait for the six-week conclusion to analyze trends.

**Caution:** Do not use "Average Time to Complete" as your primary metric, as it can be skewed by outliers and does not account for data validity.
