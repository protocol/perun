### Seasonality Adjustment Framework for GreenThumb

To separate structural trends from seasonal noise in your growth models, you must treat the April–May surge and the August dip not as anomalies, but as predictable baseline modifiers. Your current approach of assuming a direct repeat of last year's intensity carries significant risk; instead, apply a **Seasonal Index** to your forecasting.

**1. Establishing the Baseline (The "Non-Seasonal" Trend)**
Before applying seasonality, calculate the underlying growth trend excluding the extreme months (April, May, August). Use a moving average of the remaining months to determine your organic growth rate. This represents what your app would do without weather-driven behavior.

**2. Calculating Seasonal Indices**
Derive specific multipliers for your key periods based on the historical data provided:
*   **Spring Surge Index:** If April/May installs are 40% higher than the baseline trend, your index is roughly **1.40**. For 2024 planning, do not simply add 40% to your current numbers. Apply this index to your *projected* 2024 baseline. If your organic baseline has grown since last year, the absolute surge will be larger; if it has shrunk, the surge will be smaller.
*   **August Retention Index:** A 25% drop in Daily Active Users (DAU) suggests an index of **0.75**. However, you must distinguish between *churn* (users deleting the app) and *dormancy* (users keeping the app but not opening it).

**3. Distinguishing Churn vs. Dormancy (The August Problem)**
To answer the retention question, segment your August drop-off into two cohorts:
*   **Hard Churn:** Users who uninstall or cancel subscriptions. This is a structural loss requiring acquisition spend to replace.
*   **Soft Churn (Dormancy):** Users who remain installed but stop logging in. This is seasonal behavior.
*   **Action:** Analyze reactivation rates in September. If 60-70% of "lost" August users return in September, the drop is primarily dormancy. Do not budget for re-acquiring these users; instead, budget for **reactivation campaigns** (push notifications, email) which are cheaper than new acquisition. If reactivation is low, the drop is structural churn, requiring increased Q3 acquisition spend to maintain user count.

**4. Budgeting Implications for Q2 and Q3**
*   **Q2 (The Surge):** Do not overspend trying to "create" the surge; it is largely organic. Shift Q2 budget toward **conversion rate optimization (CRO)** and onboarding. Since traffic is high and cheap (organic), ensure the funnel captures maximum value. Allocate only incremental paid media budget to test if you can extend the seasonality window into late May or early June.
*   **Q3 (The Dip):**
    *   **July:** Pre-empt the August drop. Launch engagement campaigns to build habit strength before the season ends.
    *   **August:** Reduce paid acquisition spend if the drop is primarily dormancy. Reallocate these funds to **retention mechanics** (content updates, community challenges) to keep the app top-of-mind.
    *   **September:** Prepare a "Welcome Back" budget spike. If the data confirms high dormancy rather than churn, your highest ROI spend will be re-engaging the August sleepers in early September.

**5. Adjusting Expectations**
Stop measuring success in August against July. Compare August 2024 only against August 2023 (Year-Over-Year) using the seasonal index. If DAU drops 25% but your baseline trend is up 10%, you have actually outperformed expectations despite the raw number decline. Report metrics as **"Seasonally Adjusted"** to prevent the finance team from panicking over natural cyclical dips.

*Note: Validate the "mid-March" start date early in Q1 2024; if weather patterns shift, the surge window may slide, requiring agile budget reallocation.*
*Ensure your tracking distinguishes between "uninstall" events and simple "no session" to accurately price the cost of reactivation vs. new acquisition.*
