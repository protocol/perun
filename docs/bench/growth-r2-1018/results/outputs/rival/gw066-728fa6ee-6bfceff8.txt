### Diagnostic Approach: Home Goods Funnel Leakage Analysis

To isolate the critical drop-off stage without user surveys, you must decompose the macro-funnel (View → Purchase) into a granular micro-conversion chain. Given your low bounce rate (15%) but low conversion (<1%), the friction is likely occurring post-engagement, specifically during value validation or transactional commitment.

**1. Define the Micro-Conversion Chain**
Implement or extract event data for these specific intermediate steps to create a step-by-step conversion rate view:
*   **Product Detail View (PDV) → Add to Cart (ATC):** Measures intent and initial price/shipping acceptance.
*   **Add to Cart → Checkout Initiated:** Measures friction in moving from cart to the first checkout step.
*   **Checkout Initiated → Shipping Info Entered:** Isolates friction caused by account creation requirements or early shipping cost reveals.
*   **Shipping Info Entered → Payment Info Entered:** The critical "shipping shock" checkpoint. If drop-off spikes here, late-stage shipping costs are the primary culprit.
*   **Payment Info Entered → Purchase Completed:** Isolates technical payment failures or final trust issues.

**2. Segment Analysis for Pattern Recognition**
Aggregate data alone will hide the root cause. Slice the micro-conversion rates by these dimensions:
*   **Device Type:** Mobile checkout flows often suffer from UI complexity or input friction that desktop does not.
*   **Traffic Source:** Paid traffic may have mismatched expectations compared to organic or direct traffic.
*   **Cart Value Tiers:** Segment by basket size (e.g., <$50, $50-$100, >$100). If conversion drops disproportionately at lower tiers, fixed shipping costs are likely eroding value perception.
*   **New vs. Returning Users:** High drop-off for new users at the "Account Creation" gate suggests forced registration is the blocker.

**3. The "Shipping Shock" Stress Test**
Since shipping costs are a suspected variable, analyze the **Checkout Initiated → Shipping Info Entered** and **Shipping Info Entered → Payment Info Entered** steps specifically.
*   If the drop-off between *Shipping Info* and *Payment Info* exceeds 40-50%, users are abandoning after seeing the final total with shipping included.
*   Compare this rate against your site-wide average or a mature category (e.g., Electronics) to establish a baseline. A significant delta confirms shipping as the friction point.

**4. Technical & Form Friction Audit**
Review the time-on-step and error rates for form fields in the checkout flow.
*   **Form Abandonment:** Identify which specific field (e.g., phone number, address line 2) correlates with session ends.
*   **Error Rates:** High validation error rates on payment fields indicate technical flaws or confusing UI instructions.

**5. Competitive Benchmarking (Proxy Metric)**
If internal benchmarks are unavailable, use your own "Add to Cart" rate as a health metric. For home goods, an ATC rate below 5-7% suggests the product page (price/value prop) is the issue, while an ATC rate >8% with low purchase completion confirms the friction is strictly in the checkout/shipping phase.

**Immediate Action Plan:**
1.  Query your analytics platform for the 5-step conversion rates listed above for the last 30 days.
2.  Create a visualization showing the % drop-off between each consecutive step.
3.  Highlight the single largest percentage drop; this is your primary optimization target for the director.

This data-driven approach isolates whether the leak is value-based (price/shipping) or process-based (checkout UX), enabling precise inventory and UX recommendations.

*Ensure your observation windows match for all metrics to avoid skewing rates with partial-period data.*
*If using GA4, verify that "checkout_start" and "add_shipping_info" events are firing correctly before drawing conclusions.*
