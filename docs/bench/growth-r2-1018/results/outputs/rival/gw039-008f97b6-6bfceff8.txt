### Decision Framework: Do Not Launch Permanently Yet

Based on the data provided, the signal is **too noisy to justify making the program permanent** for your investor update. While the treatment group showed a 33% increase in raw referrals (12 vs. 9), the extreme variance driven by three "super-referrers" indicates the incentive attracts inconsistent behavior rather than reliable organic growth. With only 200 users per variant, a single outlier skews the entire dataset, making the average conversion rate statistically unreliable for forecasting.

**Recommended Action:** Frame this to investors as a **"Validated Hypothesis, Optimizing Structure"** phase. You have proven that cashback *can* trigger referrals, but the current 1% flat rate attracts volume over quality. Proceed with a targeted pilot rather than a full launch.

### How to Interpret the Skewed Results
The three users who referred 20 people each are likely professional referrers or "power users" gaming the system, not typical market vendors. In a network of 400, relying on finding more of these unicorns is a high-risk strategy. The fact that 197 other treatment users generated only 9 referrals combined suggests the incentive is ineffective for the average merchant. If you launch permanently based on the average, you will likely overspend on referrals that never materialize from the broader base.

### Qualitative Checks & Leading Indicators for Quality
Since you cannot run a larger quantitative test, shift immediately to deep qualitative validation of the **12 referred merchants** in the treatment group. Your goal is to determine if the "super-referrers" brought in low-quality churn risks.

Execute these four checks within 48 hours:

1.  **Cohort Retention Check (The Churn Test):**
    Compare the 7-day and 14-day active usage rates of the 12 referred merchants against your organic baseline. If the referred merchants signed up but haven't processed a transaction in 5 days, the incentive is driving "bonus hunters," not viable businesses. *Action:* Manually review transaction logs for these 12 IDs.

2.  **Network Integrity Audit:**
    Investigate the three super-referrers. Are the 20 people they referred distinct, legitimate vendors, or fake accounts/friends creating dummy profiles to claim the cashback? *Action:* Call the top 3 referrers directly. Ask, "How did you explain PayQuick to the 20 vendors you invited?" Their answer will reveal if they are selling your value prop or just the cash bonus.

3.  **Unit Economics Spot-Check:**
    Calculate the Lifetime Value (LTV) potential of the referred group so far. If the cost of the 1% cashback payout exceeds the transaction fees generated by the new merchants in their first month, the model is unsustainable. *Action:* Sum the transaction fees from the 12 new merchants and compare against the cashback liability.

4.  **The "Why" Interview:**
    Conduct 5-minute interviews with 5 of the referred merchants. Ask: "What made you sign up?" If the answer is solely "My friend said I'd get money/free processing," your value proposition is weak. If they cite specific features (speed, reliability), the referral channel has quality potential.

### Strategic Pivot for the Investor Update
Instead of asking for approval to launch the 1% program broadly, propose a **Tiered Pilot**:
*   **Cap the Reward:** Limit the cashback to the first 3 referrals per merchant to prevent gaming.
*   **Delay Payout:** Release the bonus only after the referred merchant processes 5 successful transactions (aligns incentive with quality).
*   **Narrative:** Tell investors, "We found high-variance demand. We are now optimizing the unit economics to ensure sustainable CAC before scaling." This shows data maturity rather than reckless growth.
