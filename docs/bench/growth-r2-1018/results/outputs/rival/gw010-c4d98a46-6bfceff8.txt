**Executive Summary: UrbanMove Austin Pilot (March 1–21)**

The new e-scooter fleet is delivering on its primary operational goal: battery depletion incidents have dropped by 40% across 12,000 rides, significantly improving fleet availability and reducing maintenance costs. However, this operational gain is accompanied by a 20% rise in unauthorized parking reports. While the new GPS hardware offers greater precision, early data suggests users are not necessarily parking more recklessly, but rather interacting differently with stricter geofencing boundaries. The increase in reports likely reflects a combination of users testing the limits of the new "no-park" zones and genuine confusion over reduced tolerance margins, rather than a systemic failure of compliance.

To distinguish between intentional exploitation and user confusion before adjusting rules or launching campaigns, we must gather the following specific data points regarding user intent:

1.  **End-of-Ride Hover Time:** Analyze the time elapsed between a user opening the app to end a ride and the actual lock confirmation. Long delays followed by an unauthorized zone lock suggest the user was searching for a valid spot but failed to find one (friction/confusion). Immediate locks in red zones suggest intentional disregard.
2.  **Proximity to Valid Zones:** Measure the average distance of unauthorized parks from the nearest designated corrals. If users are parking 5–10 feet outside a zone, the geofence may be too tight or physically obstructed. If parks are scattered randomly, it indicates behavioral non-compliance.
3.  **Warning Dismissal Rates:** Track how often users receive the "You are outside a designated zone" warning and whether they move the scooter after the alert. High dismissal rates without movement indicate the warning is being ignored or the alternative options are unclear.
4.  **Repeat Offender Segmentation:** Determine if the 20% increase is driven by a small segment of repeat offenders or distributed broadly across the user base. A concentrated source points to specific bad actors; a broad distribution points to a systemic UX or clarity issue.

**Narrative for City Liaison:**
The narrative for local officials should frame this as a successful calibration phase. The 40% reduction in battery failures proves the new fleet is more reliable and less likely to strand users. The rise in parking reports is a direct byproduct of higher-accuracy technology revealing previously uncounted minor infractions, alongside a necessary period of user adaptation to stricter boundaries. We are currently analyzing the specific data points above to determine if the solution requires minor geofence adjustments to match physical reality or a targeted educational push. This data-driven approach ensures that any policy changes are based on verified user intent rather than raw report counts, guaranteeing a solution that maintains high compliance without sacrificing accessibility.

**Note:** Do not commit to rule changes until the "Hover Time" and "Proximity" data distinguishes between confusion and defiance.
**Note:** Frame the GPS accuracy increase as a feature that allows for *better* enforcement, not just more citations.
