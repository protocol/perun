### Recommended Primary Conversion Funnel: "Search-to-Booking"

For your immediate UX research and usability testing next week, define your primary optimization funnel as **First Search → Completed Booking**.

Do not start at "Landing Page" or "App Download" for this specific study. Those are *acquisition* metrics. Your stated goal is to reduce friction in the *rental* process, specifically around identity verification. Users who download the app but never search are not experiencing the verification friction; they are experiencing a value proposition or onboarding friction. To validate your hypothesis that simplifying verification increases bookings, you must isolate the users who have already expressed intent to rent.

#### Funnel Steps for Usability Testing

Instruct your UX researchers to map and observe these five specific steps. This scope captures the critical path where identity verification occurs while filtering out non-intent traffic.

| Step | Action | Why This Matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. First Search** | User enters location/dates and hits "Search Cars." | **Funnel Start.** Confirms genuine intent to rent. Filters out browsers who just downloaded the app. |
| **2. Vehicle Selection** | User clicks on a specific car listing from results. | Validates inventory appeal and listing clarity. Drop-off here suggests pricing/photo issues, not verification friction. |
| **3. Booking Initiation** | User clicks "Book Now" or "Request to Book." | The commitment point. This is where the system typically triggers the identity check requirement. |
| **4. Identity Verification** | User uploads docs/takes selfie (Current 4-min bottleneck). | **Primary Friction Point.** This is the specific step your hypothesis targets. Measure time-on-task and abandonment here precisely. |
| **5. Booking Confirmation** | User receives "Booking Confirmed" screen. | **Funnel End.** A completed rental. Account creation alone is not a success metric for this goal. |

#### Why This Scope Works for Your Hypothesis

1.  **Isolates the Variable:** Your hypothesis is about *verification speed* impacting *bookings*. If you include "App Download" as step one, you dilute your data with users who dropped off because of poor onboarding or lack of immediate need, masking the verification drop-off rate.
2.  **Aligns with P2P Mechanics:** In peer-to-peer models, identity verification is often required *after* a user selects a car but *before* the host accepts or the booking is finalized. Starting at "First Search" ensures you capture the context of *which* car they wanted, which may influence their willingness to endure verification (e.g., high-demand cars justify more friction).
3.  **Actionable for UX Researchers:** This gives your team a clear script. They can recruit participants who have previously searched but not booked, or observe new users specifically from the moment they type in a destination. They can then time the verification step accurately against your "under two minutes" target.

#### Secondary Funnel: "Signup-to-Search"

While "Search-to-Booking" is your primary focus for the verification hypothesis, maintain a separate, secondary view of **Account Creation → First Search**.
*   **Purpose:** To determine if users are dropping off *before* they even see a car because the initial signup (email/password) is too heavy.
*   **Relevance:** If this secondary funnel has high drop-off, your verification problem might actually be two problems: a hard signup *and* a hard verification. However, do not let this confuse the primary test scope for next week.

#### Measurement Note for Researchers

When observing the **Identity Verification** step, do not just measure completion. Measure:
*   **Time to Complete:** Validate the current 4-minute average vs. the target <2 minutes.
*   **Error States:** How many users fail the first upload attempt?
*   **Abandonment Point:** Do they quit when asked for the ID photo, or the selfie? This granularity tells developers exactly which part of the flow to simplify.

By strictly defining the funnel from **Intent (Search)** to **Value (Booking)**, you ensure your usability tests directly address the revenue-leaking friction point rather than general browsing behavior.
