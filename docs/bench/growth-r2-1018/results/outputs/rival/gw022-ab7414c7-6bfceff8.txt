To validate which metric drives contract renewals for your logistics beta, structure your evaluation around **Leading Indicators of Retention** rather than choosing one optimization goal immediately. Since your fuel data is spotty and the pilot is ongoing (started September 1st), relying solely on "fuel cost reduction" risks building your value proposition on incomplete data. Instead, track the following specific outcomes to determine correlation with satisfaction:

**1. The "Realized Value" Ratio (Primary Validation Metric)**
Do not track "time saved" or "fuel saved" in isolation. Track the **percentage of suggested savings actually realized** by the driver.
*   **Measurement:** Compare the tool's predicted savings vs. the driver's actual post-route log.
*   **Why:** Fleet managers do not pay for theoretical optimization; they pay for executed efficiency. If the tool saves 12 minutes on paper but drivers ignore the route due to traffic patterns the algorithm missed, the "time saved" metric is vanity. High realization rates correlate directly with trust and renewal.

**2. The "Extra Delivery" Conversion Rate**
Your hypothesis regarding time savings hinges on whether 12 minutes translates to revenue. You must measure **incremental capacity utilization**.
*   **Measurement:** Track how often the time saved allowed a fleet to add an extra stop or complete a route earlier than the cutoff window.
*   **Calculation:** (Number of routes where time savings enabled an additional billable stop) / (Total optimized routes).
*   **Why:** If 12 minutes rarely results in an extra delivery (e.g., due to loading dock bottlenecks), then "time saved" has low economic value. If it frequently enables an extra drop, this metric outweighs volatile fuel savings.

**3. Manager Intervention Frequency**
This is a proxy for trust and satisfaction.
*   **Measurement:** Count how often a fleet manager or dispatcher manually overrides the tool's recommended route.
*   **Why:** High override rates indicate the algorithm is optimizing for the wrong variable (e.g., saving fuel but causing late arrivals). Low override rates suggest the tool aligns with the manager's operational reality, a strong predictor of renewal.

**4. Qualitative "Peace of Mind" Score**
Since you need this for an investor deck by quarter-end, quantitative data alone may be insufficient given the small sample (500 routes).
*   **Measurement:** Conduct structured interviews with the three pilot managers asking: "Did this tool reduce your daily planning stress?" and "Would you revert to your old method if this subscription ended tomorrow?"
*   **Why:** In mid-sized firms, the buyer (owner/manager) often values reliability and reduced cognitive load over marginal percentage gains in fuel.

**Recommendation for Your Value Proposition:**
Do not commit to "Fuel" vs. "Time" yet. Position the tool as **"Guaranteed Realized Efficiency."** Your data collection should focus on proving that your recommendations are *actionable* and *trusted*.

For the investor deck, present the "Extra Delivery Conversion Rate" as the primary economic driver if the data shows time savings are being utilized. If the data shows drivers cannot utilize the time, pivot the narrative to "Fuel Cost Stability" but explicitly state that you are enhancing data collection methods to prove this in the next phase. Investors prefer a clear hypothesis on *how* value is captured (extra deliveries) over a vague promise of "saving time."

**Note:** Ensure your 500-route sample is segmented by route type (long-haul vs. last-mile) as the value of time vs. fuel differs significantly between them.
**Note:** Do not calculate average savings across the whole period; match the observation window for each specific fleet to avoid skewing results with early adoption friction.
