**Analysis of Homepage Personalization A/B Test (Feb 1–14)**

The conflicting metrics suggest the personalization engine is successfully filtering for high-intent buyers while alienating browsers, rather than failing technically. The 5% lift in Average Order Value (AOV) alongside a 10% drop in session duration confirms your hypothesis: the feature makes shopping more efficient for ready-to-buy users. However, the 8% increase in bounce rate indicates a segmentation issue where "bargain hunters" or explorers feel the homepage no longer serves their discovery needs.

To determine if this trade-off supports a global rollout, you must slice the data by the following four dimensions to isolate value from friction:

**1. Purchase Intent Segmentation (New vs. Returning vs. High-LTV)**
Since the test targeted returning visitors, further segment this group by historical Lifetime Value (LTV) and purchase frequency.
*   **Hypothesis:** High-LTV users drove the AOV lift with reduced browsing time (efficiency gain). Low-LTV or infrequent buyers likely drove the bounce rate increase (relevance failure).
*   **Action:** Compare metrics for users with >3 past purchases versus those with only 1. If the bounce rate is concentrated in the low-frequency group, the engine is correctly prioritizing high-value efficiency over low-value engagement.

**2. Entry Point and Navigation Depth**
Analyze users who landed directly on the homepage versus those who navigated there from category pages or search results.
*   **Hypothesis:** Users arriving with a specific intent (e.g., via search) benefited from immediate recommendations (higher AOV, lower duration). Users arriving to browse (direct traffic) found the personalized wall restrictive, leading to bounces.
*   **Action:** Isolate bounce rates by traffic source. If direct traffic bounces spiked while search-referral traffic converted higher, the issue is context, not the algorithm itself.

**3. Category Affinity Match**
Cross-reference the recommended products against the user's historical category affinity.
*   **Hypothesis:** The "overwhelmed" feeling may stem from low-confidence recommendations. If the engine suggested unrelated high-margin items to bargain hunters, they bounced.
*   **Action:** Segment by "Recommendation Click-Through Rate." Did users who ignored the widget bounce immediately? If so, the placement or relevance threshold is too aggressive for cold-start or price-sensitive segments.

**4. Device Type (Mobile vs. Desktop)**
Screen real estate significantly impacts how "overwhelming" recommendations feel.
*   **Hypothesis:** The 8% bounce increase may be entirely mobile-driven if the personalization module pushes critical navigation below the fold.
*   **Action:** Split all metrics by device. A desktop efficiency gain paired with a mobile usability failure requires a responsive design fix, not a feature rollback.

**Recommendation for the VP of Merchandising**
Do not roll out globally yet. The data suggests a "efficiency vs. discovery" conflict. The feature works for high-intent shoppers but fails browsers. Recommend a **segmented rollout**: enable personalization only for users with a defined purchase history (e.g., 2+ orders) or high predicted intent scores, while serving a standard discovery layout to new or low-frequency visitors. This captures the AOV lift without sacrificing the engagement of bargain hunters who may convert later.

*Note: Ensure the observation window accounts for any delayed conversions from bounced users who returned via direct search later in the period.*
*Verify that the "bounce" definition excludes single-page purchases, which might artificially inflate the bounce rate if the new flow allows faster checkout.*
