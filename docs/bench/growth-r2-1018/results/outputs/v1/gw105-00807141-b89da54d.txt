**North Star Recommendation: Net Client Cash-Flow Improvement (NCCI)**

Reject "volume of invoices financed" as your North Star. It is a vanity metric that rose 25% while masking a 15% drop in loan size and a doubling of support friction. Volume counts activity, not value. Your new North Star must be **Net Client Cash-Flow Improvement (NCCI)**: the actual liquidity gained by the client after fees, relative to their baseline cash position.

**The Metric Definition**
NCCI = (Total Payout Amount) − (Total Fees Charged) − (Baseline Cash Shortfall Cost).
*Why this works:* It forces the product to optimize for the client's bottom line. If users take smaller loans but pay disproportionate fees (driving up support queries), NCCI stagnates or drops, correctly signaling that "growth" is illusory. This aligns with your CFO's requirement for healthy unit economics and prevents encouraging risky, fee-heavy borrowing for trivial amounts.

**Input Metrics to Move NCCI**
To drive NCCI, track these three specific inputs instead of raw volume:
1.  **Fee-Adjusted Retention Rate:** The % of pilot companies returning in Month 2 *with a stable or increased average loan size*. This filters out "testing the waters" (one-off small loans) from genuine reliance. If retention is high but loan size shrinks, you have a distress signal, not a success.
2.  **Cost-of-Capital Delta:** The difference between your effective fee rate and the client's alternative cost of capital (e.g., late payment penalties or overdraft rates). If your fees exceed their alternative costs, you are destroying value, regardless of volume.
3.  **Friction-to-Value Ratio:** Support queries about fees divided by total successful payouts. A rising ratio indicates the fee structure is confusing or perceived as unfair, directly threatening long-term retention.

Your current data (Volume +25%, Loan Size -15%, Support x2) suggests a **leaky activation bucket**. Users are likely fragmenting large invoices into smaller ones to test the speed, hitting fee minimums that make the service expensive, hence the support spikes.
