**Recommended North Star Metric: Weekly Active Buyers Completing a "Verified Sustainable" Purchase**

Do not measure raw traffic, cumulative sign-ups, or pageviews; these are vanity metrics that rise even when the business fails. Do not measure "Carbon Footprint Saved" as your primary metric; while central to your mission, it is an output attribute, not a behavior. A user can view a carbon score without deriving value or returning.

Your North Star must be a **behavior** that only occurs when a customer receives real value and returns to do it again. For GreenCart, that behavior is a **repeat purchase where the sustainability attribute was visible and actionable**. Specifically, track the weekly count of unique users who complete a purchase of an item where the carbon footprint data was displayed and interacted with (e.g., tooltip viewed or comparison used) within the same session.

This metric aligns Engineering (ensuring the data is visible and performant) and Marketing (driving traffic likely to care about this specific signal) toward the same outcome: loyal customers who actively choose sustainability, not just those who happen to buy green items by accident.

**Analysis Structure to Validate the Choice**

To prove this metric correlates with long-term retention and validates your leadership's hypothesis, structure your analysis as follows:

1.  **Segment by Interaction, Not Just Purchase:**
    Split your existing customer base into two cohorts for the period since March 12:
    *   **Cohort A (Engaged):** Users who purchased an item *and* interacted with the carbon footprint feature (clicked, hovered, or compared).
    *   **Cohort B (Passive):** Users who purchased items with carbon data available but did *not* interact with the feature.

    Compare the **Week-4 Retention Rate** (did they buy again in the fourth week after signup/first purchase?) between these two groups. If your hypothesis is correct, Cohort A's retention must be significantly higher than Cohort B's. If both cohorts retain at the same 18% rate, the carbon feature is a "nice-to-have" that does not drive loyalty, and your roadmap must pivot to other retention drivers (e.g., price, delivery speed, product quality).

2.  **Read the Funnel Bottom-Up:**
    Your current 18% repeat purchase rate is the leak. Do not pour more marketing budget into acquiring new traffic (the 15% increase) until you understand why the bucket is leaking.
    *   **Retention First:** Analyze if the users who *did* return (the 18%) share a common "activation" event. Was it the first carbon-labeled purchase? Was it a specific category?
    *   **Activation Second:** Once the retention driver is confirmed, measure how many new users hit that specific activation event.
    *   **Acquisition Last:** Only after fixing retention and activation should you scale acquisition channels that deliver users likely to hit that activation event.

3.  **Trend Against Baseline, Not External Benchmarks:**
    Do not look for an industry "good" retention rate. Compare your weekly North Star number strictly against your own pre-March 12 baseline and your week-over-week trend since launch. If the North Star rises while the 18% aggregate stays flat, you have identified the specific behavior that drives loyalty. If the North Star is flat, the feature is not working as intended.

**Immediate Next Step:**
Query your analytics this week to calculate the Week-4 retention delta between "Carbon Feature Engagers" and "Non-Engagers." If the delta is less than 5 percentage points, the carbon footprint display is not your loyalty driver, and the Q3 roadmap must shift to testing other value propositions.
