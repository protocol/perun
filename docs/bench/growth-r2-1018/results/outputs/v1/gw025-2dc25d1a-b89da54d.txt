**Recommendation: Do not kill the project yet; pivot the testing approach immediately.**

The data indicates a severe **activation-to-retention leak**, not necessarily a lack of market fit. Your assumption that the audience is wrong is premature because you cannot validate "fit" without first validating "value delivery." If the feature fails to deliver value in the first three days, no audience segment will retain, regardless of how specialized they are.

**Interpretation of the Signal**
You observed a 30% activation rate (45/150) but a ~6.7% retention rate (3/45) post-day three.
*   **Activation is strong:** 30% suggests the promise of the feature resonates with this niche. The "hook" works.
*   **Retention is critical:** The drop-off after day three implies the **core value loop is broken** or the "aha moment" occurs too late. Users are trying the feature, finding it lacks immediate utility or clarity, and leaving.
*   **Statistical Meaning:** While N=150 is small for precise conversion benchmarks, a 93% churn rate within three days is a **qualitative failure signal** that is statistically sufficient to halt scaling. You do not need a larger sample size to know that losing 42 of 45 users is a problem. The noise is not in the churn rate; the noise would be in trying to generalize this to a revenue forecast.

**Diagnosis: Retention Before Acquisition**
Following the principle of reading the funnel bottom-up, pouring more acquisition (testing new segments) into a bucket with a hole this large is waste. The issue is likely **product mechanics**, not **audience demographics**. If the feature worked for 3 users, it is technically functional, but the *onboarding* or *initial value proposition* is failing for the other 42.

**Action Plan for the CPO**
1.  **Halt Segment Testing:** Do not test new audiences until the Day-3 retention leak is plugged. Changing the user segment now confounds the variable; you won't know if improvements are due to the audience or the product fix.
2.  **Qualitative Deep Dive (Immediate):** Contact the 3 retainers and the 42 churners. Ask the churners specifically: "What did you expect to happen after you activated that didn't?" and ask retainers: "What specific action did you take on Day 2 or 3 that made you come back?" This is faster and more accurate than running another quantitative cohort test.
3.  **Instrument the "Cliff":** Define the exact event where users drop off. Is it a missing feature? A confusing UI? A lack of content? Instrument only the events that answer why they left between Hour 24 and Hour 72.
4.  **Iterate on Value, Not Reach:** Run a rapid iteration cycle to improve the Day-3 experience for *this* specific group. If you can move retention from 6% to 20% with this specialized group, *then* expand to broader segments.

**Verdict:** The signal is strong enough to **stop scaling** but not strong enough to **kill the project**. The high activation proves interest; the low retention proves an execution gap in immediate value delivery. Fix the leak, then re-test.
