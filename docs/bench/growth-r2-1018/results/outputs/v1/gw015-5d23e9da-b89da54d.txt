### Diagnostic Approach: Funnel Leakage & Cohort Integrity

Your flat revenue despite a 35% spend increase indicates a breakdown in **Retention** or **Activation**, rendering the additional **Acquisition** spend inefficient. Per the bottom-up diagnostic rule, we must verify if the core action (purchase) is being retained before analyzing traffic sources. Do not assume the new creatives are "working" until retention is proven stable.

Here are the specific hypotheses and data cuts to request from engineering immediately for Thursday's sync:

#### 1. Hypothesis: The "Leaky Bucket" (Retention Collapse)
**Premise:** Increased spend acquired low-intent users who churned immediately, masking the performance of high-value segments. The flat revenue suggests the *volume* of buyers hasn't changed, only the cost to acquire them.
*   **Data Cut A (Cohort Retention):** Request a weekly retention cohort table for the last 6 months.
    *   *Rows:* Week of First Purchase (Jan–Present).
    *   *Columns:* % of that cohort making a 2nd purchase in Week 2, Week 4, and Week 8.
    *   *Goal:* Identify if the Q2 cohorts (post-spend increase) have a statistically lower repeat purchase rate than Q1.
*   **Data Cut B (Returning vs. New Revenue Split):**
    *   *Metric:* Monthly Revenue split by "First-time Buyer" vs. "Returning Customer."
    *   *Goal:* If total revenue is flat but "New Buyer" revenue rose while "Returning" revenue fell, your new traffic is cannibalizing loyalists or failing to convert them again.

#### 2. Hypothesis: Activation Friction (The "Almost" Buyer)
**Premise:** Traffic is arriving (high intent or not), but the site experience or pricing is blocking the conversion *after* the click.
*   **Data Cut C (Funnel Drop-off by Source):**
    *   *Metrics:* Session → Product View → Add to Cart → Checkout Initiated → Purchase.
    *   *Segmentation:* Break down by "New Campaign Traffic" vs. "Organic/Existing Traffic."
    *   *Goal:* Pinpoint the exact step where the new traffic diverges. If they drop at "Add to Cart," it's likely pricing/shipping shock. If they drop at "Product View," the creative promised something the landing page didn't deliver.

#### 3. Hypothesis: Low-Intent Traffic (Acquisition Quality)
**Premise:** The new creatives are optimizing for clicks, not buyers, inflating top-of-funnel metrics while delivering empty sessions.
*   **Data Cut D (Session Quality Metrics):**
    *   *Metrics:* Bounce Rate, Average Session Duration, and Pages Per Session.
    *   *Segmentation:* Compare "New Ad Creative" traffic vs. "Control/Old Creative" traffic.
    *   *Goal:* If the new traffic has significantly lower engagement depth, the audience targeting is misaligned regardless of the landing page quality.

#### Required Event Taxonomy Check
Ensure your data team confirms these events are instrumented with a **stable, pseudonymous user ID** to link sessions across devices. Without a stable ID, you cannot accurately measure the "Returning Customer Rate" or attribute multi-session journeys. If this ID is missing or inconsistent, mark the retention analysis as `UNVERIFIED` until fixed.

### This Week's Step
1.  **Request:** The 6-month weekly retention cohort table and the New vs. Returning revenue split for Jan–Present.
2.  **Query:** Run the funnel drop-off analysis segmented by the new campaign source ID.
3.  **Decision Trigger:** If the Q2 cohort retention is >10% lower than Q1, the diagnosis is a **Retention/Quality leak** (stop scaling spend). If retention is flat but funnel drop-off is high at "Checkout," the diagnosis is **Experience/Pricing friction**.

*Note: Do not present "Signups" or "Site Visits" to the CEO; these are vanity metrics that rise with spend regardless of business health. Only present conversion and retention rates.*
*All benchmarks must be derived from your own Q1 baseline; do not compare against industry averages.*
