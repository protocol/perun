**Diagnosis & Immediate Actions**
Your current data reveals a classic activation leak: you optimized for acquisition/conversion (speed) while retention (completion) degraded. The survey indicated a *desire* for speed, but the behavioral data proves that unchecked speed destroys value. Do not revert to the slow flow; instead, instrument the specific friction points to find the "sweet spot" where speed does not compromise accuracy.

Execute these three steps this week:

1.  **Instrument the "Regret Event" Taxonomy:** Stop guessing why cancellations happen. Add properties to the `booking_cancelled` event to capture the *reason code* (e.g., "wrong_time_slot", "changed_mind", "accidental_double_book") and the *time-to-cancel* delta.
    *   *Question answered:* Is the 12% cancellation rate driven by users picking the wrong slot (a UI/clarity issue) or by the psychological pressure of rushing (a flow design issue)?
    *   *Decision trigger:* If >50% are "wrong_time_slot," the fix is better slot selection UI, not slower checkout. If >50% are "changed_mind" with <60 second time-to-cancel, the flow is too aggressive.

2.  **Define the "Quality-Speed" Guardrail:** For your next A/B test, set the primary metric as the North Star defined above. Set a hard guardrail: "Cancellation rate must not exceed 7%."
    *   *Question answered:* Can we achieve faster checkout times without breaching the acceptable error threshold?
    *   *Decision trigger:* Any variant that increases speed but pushes cancellations above 7% is a fail, regardless of conversion lift. This prevents the "peeking" trap of shipping a feature that looks good on day 1 (high conversions) but fails by day 2 (high churn).

3.  **Cohort Analysis on "Rushed" vs. "Deliberate" Users:** Segment users by their time-to-book in the new flow. Compare the 24-hour retention of users who booked in <30 seconds versus those who took 30–90 seconds.
