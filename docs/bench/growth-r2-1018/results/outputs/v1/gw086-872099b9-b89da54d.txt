**Primary Goal: Week-2 Retention of the "Fully Verified" Onboarding State**

Do not choose between speed (time-to-complete) or accuracy (data quality) as your primary metric; choosing one invites gaming the other. Instead, define your North Star as the **percentage of new hires who reach a "Fully Verified" state within their first 14 days**.

A user is "Fully Verified" only when:
1.  **Completion:** All mandatory onboarding steps are finished.
2.  **Accuracy:** Zero critical data errors requiring HR admin intervention to fix.
3.  **Time:** The process was completed within the target window (e.g., 48 hours).

This single metric captures the *real value*: a fast process that produces bad data creates downstream work for your clients (eroding satisfaction), while a perfect process that takes three days fails the efficiency hypothesis. In a B2B pilot with only five clients and ~1,500 users, aggregate "average time" or "satisfaction scores" are too noisy and lagging. Retention of the *correct* state proves the module works end-to-end.

**Supporting Input Metrics (to diagnose the North Star):**
*   **Activation Rate:** % of invited users who start the process within 48 hours.
*   **Error Injection Rate:** % of submissions flagged by validation rules (measures accuracy friction).
*   **Admin Touch Rate:** Number of times client HR staff must manually correct a record (the true cost of poor accuracy).
*   **Drop-off Point:** The specific step where users abandon the flow.

**Rationale for B2B Pilot Constraints:**
With only five enterprise clients, you cannot rely on statistical significance for A/B testing or broad benchmarks. Your baseline is the client's *previous* manual process. If "Fully Verified" retention is low, you immediately know if the leak is due to users quitting (speed issue) or HR rejecting submissions (accuracy issue). This aligns the CEO (business value via reduced support costs) and Customer Success (client satisfaction via seamless adoption) on a single truth: value is delivered only when the job is done *right* and *fast*.

**This Week's Steps:**
1.  **Instrument the "Verified" Event:** Define the exact database state that constitutes "Fully Verified" (all fields valid + no admin flags) and ensure your analytics can track this per-user ID pseudonymously.
2.  **Query the Baseline:** Ask your five pilot clients for their historical "time-to-ready" and "data-error correction hours" from their old process to establish a comparative baseline, not an invented benchmark.
3.  **Set the Tripwire:** If <60% of new hires reach "Fully Verified" status by Week 3, pause feature additions and focus entirely on the highest-friction step identified in the drop-off data.

*Note: Ensure user identifiers are pseudonymous to comply with POPIA (South Africa) before ingesting data into your analytics platform.*
*Do not track "total logins" or "page views"; these are vanity metrics that rise even when the product fails.*
