**Diagnostic Analysis: StreamFlix Argentina Recommendation Carousel Experiment**

**Status:** The experiment result (4% retention drop) is **verified** based on your provided data. The hypothesis that "higher CTR predicts better retention" is **rejected** for this specific intervention; the data indicates a "leaky bucket" where initial engagement does not translate to sustained value.

**Root Cause Hypotheses (To Investigate, Not Assume)**
1.  **The "Bait-and-Switch" Effect:** The 12% CTR increase may be driven by clickbait-style thumbnails or titles that fail to deliver satisfying content, causing early churn. Users click, dislike the content, and do not return by Day 7.
2.  **Cohort-Specific Friction:** The interface change may have degraded the experience for the 30% older subscriber base. If this segment relies on familiar navigation patterns, the new carousel could increase cognitive load, leading to abandonment despite higher overall clicks from younger demographics.
3.  **Content Quality Mismatch:** The localized algorithm may be surfacing high-volume but low-satisfaction content specific to the Buenos Aires region, inflating clicks while depressing long-term viewing sessions.

**Required Segmentation & Behavioral Patterns**
To isolate the cause without fabricating benchmarks, query your analytics for the following specific cuts. Do not look at aggregate numbers; the signal is in the variance.

*   **Segment by Age Cohort:** Split the retention curve into "Under 40" and "Over 40."
    *   *Question to answer:* Did retention drop exclusively in the older cohort? If the older cohort retention dropped >10% while the younger cohort remained flat or improved, the interface usability is the primary driver.
*   **Segment by "Click-to-Completion" Rate:**
    *   *Question to answer:* Of the users who clicked the carousel, what percentage watched >50% of the title? If CTR is up but completion rate is down, the recommendation relevance (content quality) is the failure point, not the interface.
*   **Segment by Return Frequency (The "Binge" Check):**
    *   *Question to answer:* Did the treatment group have fewer *subsequent* sessions within the first 48 hours compared to control? A drop here suggests the initial experience was frustrating enough to prevent immediate re-engagement.
*   **Navigation Path Analysis:**
    *   *Question to answer:* For users who exited immediately after clicking the carousel, did they attempt to search manually or browse other categories? If they exited the app entirely, the content was the issue. If they navigated elsewhere successfully, the carousel placement may be disrupting the flow.

**Decision Framework for Regional Director**
Do not decide to "kill" or "iterate" until these specific queries return results.
*   **If the drop is isolated to the >40 cohort:** The issue is likely UI confusion. The feature may be viable with an accessibility toggle or simplified view for that segment.
*   **If the drop is universal but completion rates are low:** The recommendation logic is flawed. The feature requires algorithmic retraining before any UI iteration.
*   **If the drop is universal and completion rates are normal:** Investigate external factors (e.g., content licensing expirations during the test window) or novelty effects that wore off by Day 7.
