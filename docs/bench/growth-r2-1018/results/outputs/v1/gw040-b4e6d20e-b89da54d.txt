### Measurement Plan for EduStart Advisory Board

**1. The North Star Metric: Weekly Active Learners Completing the Core Loop**
Reject "cumulative signups" (800 waitlist) and "time spent" as your primary success indicators. Signups are vanity; they rise regardless of value. Time spent is noisy; a confused student stuck on a page racks up minutes without learning.
Your single North Star must be: **The count of unique students per week who complete Lessons 1, 2, and 3.**
This metric only rises when a user receives the core value (the "aha" moment you hypothesized). It directly measures whether you are solving the problem of engaging students in coding, not just attracting them.

**2. The Funnel Strategy: Read Bottom-Up (Retention First)**
Do not focus on acquiring more of the 800 waitlist users until you prove the bucket doesn't leak. Diagnose your funnel in this specific order:
*   **Retention (The Leak Check):** Of the students who start Lesson 1, what percentage return to start Lesson 2 within 48 hours? If this is low, pouring marketing effort into the waitlist is wasted capital.
*   **Activation (The Threshold Test):** You hypothesize that finishing three lessons predicts long-term stickiness. Since you have no historical data, this is currently **UNVERIFIED**. Your immediate goal is to instrument the drop-off between Lesson 1→2 and Lesson 2→3. If 90% finish Lesson 1 but only 10% start Lesson 2, your "three lesson" threshold is irrelevant; the friction is at step two.
*   **Acquisition:** Only optimize converting the waitlist once the retention of the core loop is stable.

**3. Event Taxonomy: Instrument Only What Answers a Named Question**
Avoid "tracking everything." Define exactly three events for launch week, each tied to a specific decision:
*   `lesson_started`: *Question:* Are students initiating the experience?
*   `lesson_completed`: *Question:* Where exactly do they drop off? (Properties: `lesson_id`, `time_spent`).
*   `return_visit`: *Question:* Did they come back within 48 hours? (Requires a stable, pseudonymous user ID to link sessions).

*Note on Identity:* To track retention, you must link a user's activity across days. Use a stable, pseudonymous ID for this analysis. Do not use raw PII (names/emails) in your analytics logs; route privacy compliance to your legal/privacy review process.

**4. Success Framework for the Board**
Since you cannot track revenue or LTV yet, "Product-Market Fit" is defined strictly by **behavioral retention**, not sentiment.
*   **Hypothesis:** Students who complete 3 lessons will return weekly.
*   **Validation Method:** Cohort Analysis. Group users by their start date. Measure what % of the "Week 1 Cohort" completes Lesson 3. Then, measure what % of *those* users return in Week 2 without email nudges.
*   **The Verdict:** If the retention curve flattens above a sustainable baseline (your own internal benchmark, not an industry average), you have fit. If it drops to zero, you have a product problem, not a marketing problem.

**This Week's Actions**
1.  **Instrument:** Implement tracking for `lesson_completed` with `lesson_id` before launch. Do not launch without verifying this event fires correctly.
2.  **Define the Baseline:** Calculate the conversion rate from Lesson 1 → Lesson 2 for your first 100 users. This is your baseline.
3.  **The Decision Trigger:** If <20% of starters reach Lesson 2 by next Tuesday, pause acquisition efforts and interview the drop-offs to fix the activation friction. Do not expand the waitlist until this leak is plugged.

*Note: Do not assert a "good" completion rate (e.g., "50% is good") as you have no market benchmark; compare only against your own week-over-week improvement.*
*Note: Social shares are a secondary "Referral" metric; ignore them until the core retention loop is proven.*
