**North Star Metric:** Weekly Active Agents completing **3+ Property Summaries**.

This replaces your forty tracked events. For a real estate agent, value is not "visiting the site"; it is "having marketing copy ready to send." Your hypothesis states that three summaries unlock the paid conversion. Therefore, an agent who hits this threshold has received tangible value (time saved, listings prepared). If this number rises, your business grows. If it stays flat while pageviews rise, you are merely generating noise.

**Two Activation Metrics to Measure:**

1.  **Day-7 Retention of the "3+ Summary" Cohort**
    *   **The Question:** Of the agents who generated 3+ summaries in week one, how many return to generate at least one more summary in week two?
    *   **Why it matters:** This validates your core value promise. If agents get value once but never return, the product is a novelty, not a tool. Real estate is a recurring workflow; if the tool works, they must come back. This metric tells you if you are building a habit or just a one-off utility.
    *   **Action:** Ignore all other retention curves. Focus solely on whether the users who hit your "aha moment" actually stick around.

2.  **Activation Rate (Signups → 3 Summaries in 7 Days)**
    *   **The Question:** What percentage of new signups successfully generate three summaries within their first seven days?
    *   **Why it matters:** This measures the friction in your onboarding. With only two engineers, you cannot fix everything. If this rate is low, your problem is not acquisition; it is that users aren't reaching the value threshold. Improving this by even 5% directly impacts future revenue more than acquiring 100 new users who churn immediately.
    *   **Action:** Instrument *only* the events required to calculate this funnel: `signup`, `summary_generated` (with count), and `day_7_timestamp`.

**What to Ignore (The "Do Not Build" List):**
*   **Pageviews, Clicks, and Time-on-Site:** These are vanity metrics. An agent can click wildly out of frustration or leave a tab open while showing a house. These numbers can rise while your business dies. Do not build dashboards for them.
*   **Features unrelated to the core loop:** Unless a click directly contributes to generating a property summary, do not track it individually right now. You are in the MVP stage; instrument only what answers the two questions above.

**Board Meeting Narrative:**
Tell the board: "We are stopping the tracking of 40 noise events. We are focusing entirely on moving the needle on 'Agents hitting 3 summaries' and ensuring they return next week. Our engineering capacity for the next quarter will be dedicated exclusively to removing friction from this specific path. We will not optimize for signups until activation is stable."

**Note:** Your current "40 events" approach is a classic retention leak; you are likely drowning in data while missing the signal.
**Note:** Ensure your user identifier is stable across sessions to track the same agent from Week 1 to Week 2, but keep it pseudonymous to comply with privacy standards.
