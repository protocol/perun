**Recommendation: North Star Metric for the Next 30 Days**
**"Total Verified Dollars Distributed to Relief Efforts within 48 Hours of Donation."**

Do not select "Total Dollars Raised" (Volume) or "Average Donation Size" (Value) as your sole North Star. In a crisis context like the Delta floods, donor intent shifts from "supporting a platform" to "solving an immediate problem." Your data shows a classic crisis signature: transaction volume doubled while average donation size dropped 42% ($55 to $32). This indicates an influx of small, urgent donors who care about speed and impact, not large institutional donors optimizing for tax efficiency or long-term engagement.

If you optimize only for **Volume (Total Raised)**, you risk creating a bottleneck where cash accumulates faster than your operational capacity to distribute it. This creates a "vanity metric" trap: the dashboard looks green while trust erodes because funds aren't reaching victims. If you optimize only for **Value (Average Donation)**, you will inadvertently deprioritize the small-dollar surge that is currently driving your liquidity, effectively turning away the majority of your current user base.

The proposed metric—**Verified Dollars Distributed within 48 Hours**—forces alignment between engineering (speed of processing), operations (verification speed), and marketing (messaging about immediacy). It treats "raised" money as inventory liability until it is "distributed" value.

**Analysis of Tensions:**

*   **Volume (Total Raised):**
    *   *Pros:* Maximizes immediate cash flow; satisfies urgent liquidity needs for relief partners.
    *   *Cons:* Ignores fulfillment capacity. If you raise $1M but only distribute $200k in a month, donors perceive inefficiency. It encourages acquiring donors who may never return if the funds sit idle.
*   **Value (Average Donation/Retention):**
    *   *Pros:* Protects long-term LTV and platform health; focuses on high-intent donors.
    *   *Cons:* Misaligned with current crisis behavior. The 400% traffic surge is driven by small, emotional gifts. Optimizing for higher averages now would suppress the total volume needed for immediate relief. Retention is secondary to survival in the first 30 days of a disaster.
*   **Speed (Distribution Velocity):**
    *   *Pros:* Directly validates donor intent ("my money helped now"); builds immense trust for post-crisis retention.
    *   *Cons:* Requires rigorous backend verification to prevent fraud, which can slow processing if not automated.

**Executive Decision Logic:**
Adopting "Distributed within 48 Hours" as the North Star resolves the board's division by making "speed of impact" the proxy for both volume and health. If distribution lags, marketing must pause acquisition to prevent a backlog (protecting brand health). If distribution is fast, marketing scales acquisition (maximizing volume). This metric ensures that every dollar raised is treated as a promise kept, not just a number on a graph.

**Note:** Do not fabricate a "good" distribution rate; compare your current 48-hour rate against your own baseline from last week to measure improvement. Ensure user identifiers used for tracking distribution cohorts are pseudonymized to comply with privacy standards.
