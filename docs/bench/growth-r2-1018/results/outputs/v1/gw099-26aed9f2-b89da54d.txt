**Executive Summary: Pilot Disconnect & Recommendation**

**Verdict:** **Pause wider rollout.** Proceed only after resolving "last ten yards" friction. Speed is a hygiene factor; failed handoffs and damaged goods are critical failures that speed cannot offset. The 3.8/5 score indicates the core value proposition (reliable grocery delivery) is broken despite operational efficiency.

**Root Cause Analysis Plan**
To distinguish between "novelty anxiety" and "functional failure," combine these specific data sources immediately:

1.  **Event Taxonomy (The "Last Ten Yards"):** Instrument and query three specific events per delivery: `handoff_initiated`, `handoff_completed`, and `package_integrity_confirmed`.
    *   *Hypothesis:* High latency between `initiated` and `completed` correlates with low scores, indicating handoff confusion.
    *   *Action:* Filter satisfaction scores by deliveries where `handoff_completed` took >2 minutes. If scores remain low here, the issue is functional, not novelty.

2.  **Qualitative Tagging vs. Operational Metrics:** Cross-reference open-text feedback tags ("hard to find," "damaged," "scary") against the `package_integrity` sensor logs (tilt/shock events) and GPS stop precision.
    *   *Hypothesis:* If "damaged" comments align with high shock events, the issue is package condition. If "confusing" aligns with normal sensor logs but low scores, the issue is UX/handoff design.

3.  **Cohort Retention (Novelty Test):** Segment users into "First Time" vs. "Repeat" (if any existed in pilot) or compare early-week vs. late-week scores.
    *   *Logic:* If scores rise as the district gets used to the robots, it is a novelty issue. If scores remain flat or decline regardless of exposure, it is a functional failure. Do not assume novelty without this trend line.

**North Star Correction**
Your current North Star appears to be "Average Delivery Time." This is a vanity metric in this context because it rises while customer value falls.
*   **New North Star:** "Successful Handoff Rate" (deliveries completed without user support intervention or damage report).
*   **Input Metrics:** Handoff duration, package shock events, CSAT.

**Decision Framework for VP**
Do not scale until the "Successful Handoff Rate" exceeds 98% and CSAT stabilizes above 4.2. Scaling now pours acquisition (new cities) into a leaky retention bucket; you will acquire customers faster only to churn them due to poor experience.
