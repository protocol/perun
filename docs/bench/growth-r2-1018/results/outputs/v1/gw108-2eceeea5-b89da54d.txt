**Recommendation: Do not ship globally yet. The 15% breakage increase is a critical guardrail failure that invalidates the 2% retention win until the scope of breakage is quantified.**

A 2% retention lift is valuable, but a 15% surge in breakage reports suggests a severe degradation of the core product utility (browsing the web). If "breakage" means users cannot access news, banking, or work tools, the retention gain is likely an artifact of a specific cohort or short-term novelty, not sustainable value. You cannot trade a broken core function for a secondary metric without knowing the *concentration* of that breakage.

**Immediate Data Slices Required (This Week):**

1.  **Breakage Concentration (Pareto Analysis):**
    *   **Query:** Plot the cumulative distribution of breakage reports against the top 1,000 domains.
    *   **Decision Trigger:** If >80% of breakage comes from the top 50 domains, the issue is fixable via allow-listing. If breakage is evenly distributed across long-tail sites, the strict default is fundamentally incompatible with the current web ecosystem and will cause churn at scale.

2.  **Retention vs. Breakage Cohort Overlap:**
    *   **Query:** Segment the 2% retention gain by users who *did not* file a breakage report vs. those who did.
    *   **Decision Trigger:** If the retention gain exists *only* among users who experienced zero breakage, the metric is fragile. If users experiencing breakage still retained at higher rates, the privacy value proposition is genuinely strong enough to overcome friction.

3.  **Severity-Weighted Breakage Rate:**
    *   **Query:** Recalculate the 15% increase weighting by session duration lost or task abandonment (e.g., did they leave the site immediately?).
    *   **Decision Trigger:** A 15% increase in minor CSS glitches is acceptable; a 15% increase in total page load failures is not. You need a "critical breakage" rate, not just raw report volume.

**The Trade-off Logic:**
Your experiment violated the "guardrail" principle. You observed a primary metric win (retention) while a critical guardrail (functionality) collapsed. In this state, the result is `UNVERIFIED` as a net positive. The "win" may disappear once the broken sites (which drive daily utility) cause users to abandon the browser entirely in Month 2 or 3, a lag not visible in a 28-day test.

**Action Plan:**
1.  **Pause Global Rollout:** Maintain the status quo or limit strict defaults to a voluntary opt-in segment.
2.  **Execute the Pareto Slice:** Identify the top 20 sites causing breakage. Work with engineering to create compatibility shims or exceptions for these specific domains.
3.  **Re-run or Extend:** Once the top breakage sources are mitigated, re-measure the retention lift. If the lift persists with a near-zero critical breakage rate, you have a valid case for the CPO.

Without the concentration data, recommending a global default is a gamble that prioritizes a small statistical gain over the reliability of the browser's primary function.
