**Recommendation: Optimize for Completion Rate, not Session Duration.**

For a structured course like 'Data Basics,' **Completion Rate** is your North Star metric for customer value; **Session Duration** is currently a vanity metric masking a retention leak. High session time with low completion indicates friction (students getting stuck, confused, or unable to progress), not deep engagement. In the AARRR funnel, you must fix **Retention** (getting users to finish) before optimizing for time-on-site. Pouring effort into making sessions longer when users aren't finishing will increase frustration, not learning.

**Decision Framework:**
1.  **Reject Session Duration as the Primary Goal:** Long sessions without completion suggest users are re-watching confusing segments or struggling with broken flows. Optimizing for this risks making content more verbose or complex, worsening the drop-off.
2.  **Adopt Completion Rate as the Proxy for Success:** A student who finishes the course receives the full value proposition. Historical data (30% avg) confirms this is the baseline for a healthy product. The current 18% is a critical leak that invalidates the "high engagement" signal from session time.
3.  **Address the "Hidden Consumption" Unknown:** Your hypothesis that dropouts are consuming PDFs is `UNVERIFIED`. You cannot weigh this signal until you instrument it. If students are learning via PDFs, they are still "completing" the learning objective, just not via the video player.

**Immediate Actions (This Week):**
*   **Instrument the "Alternative Format" Event:** Add tracking for `pdf_download` and `resource_access` events tied to the user ID. Define the question: "Do users who drop out of the video player convert to PDF consumers?"
*   **Redefine "Completion":** Create a composite metric: `Course_Completed = Video_Finished OR (All_Modules_Accessed_via_PDF + Quiz_Passed)`. Re-calculate the 18% rate against this new definition.
*   **Analyze the Drop-off Point:** Segment the 82% who didn't finish. Identify the exact module where session duration spikes (struggle) or where exit rates peak. This tells you *where* the content is failing, not just *that* it is failing.

**Verdict:** Do not optimize for session duration. It is a lagging indicator of confusion in this context. Focus instructional design on removing barriers to **Completion**. If the redefined completion rate (including PDFs) remains near 18%, the content or flow is broken. If it rises to ~25-30%, your video player was simply the wrong medium for some users, and the product is healthy.

**Note:** Ensure user IDs used for cross-format tracking are pseudonymous and routed to your privacy compliance protocol; do not use raw PII.
**Note:** Do not compare your 18% to external industry benchmarks; rely solely on your internal 30% historical baseline to measure recovery.
