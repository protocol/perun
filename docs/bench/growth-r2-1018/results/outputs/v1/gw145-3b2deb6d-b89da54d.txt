### Prioritized Telemetry Events for SkyDrop Customer Interface

To ensure transparency without clutter, instrument only events that answer the specific question: **"Has the delivery status materially changed for the recipient?"** Avoid raw telemetry streams; instead, derive the following four customer-facing states from your internal data.

**1. "Out for Delivery" (Activation)**
*   **Trigger:** Drone departs the local distribution hub with the specific package loaded.
*   **Value:** Confirms the process has started. This replaces vague "processing" statuses.
*   **Instrumentation Question:** "Did the package leave the ground toward the user?"

**2. "Approaching Neighborhood" (Contextual Awareness)**
*   **Trigger:** Drone enters a geofence ~1km from the drop zone.
*   **Value:** Provides a meaningful time horizon (approx. 2–3 minutes) for the user to prepare, without exposing noisy intermediate waypoints.
*   **Instrumentation Question:** "Is the drone close enough that the user should look up?"

**3. "Final Approach Initiated" (The Core Value Moment)**
*   **Trigger:** Drone begins the specific 3-minute descent sequence identified in your hypothesis.
*   **Value:** Validates your assumption that this phase matters. It signals imminent arrival.
*   **Instrumentation Question:** "Is the drone executing the final landing protocol?"

**4. "Package Secured on Ground" (Completion)**
*   **Trigger:** Weight sensors confirm release and stability on the ground.
*   **Value:** Definitive proof of delivery completion. Do not show "Landing" as a final state; show "Delivered."
*   **Instrumentation Question:** "Is the package safely out of the drone's possession?"

### Determining Anxiety vs. Reassurance

You cannot guess which updates cause anxiety; you must measure user behavior against your own baseline once the pilot launches.

*   **Define the Metric:** Track **"Session Churn During Final Approach."** If a user opens the tracker during the 3-minute final approach and immediately closes it (or refreshes repeatedly within 5 seconds), flag this as a potential anxiety signal.
*   **The Test:** For the Seattle pilot, run a simple A/B test on the *frequency* of location updates during the "Final Approach" phase.
    *   *Group A:* Updates every 10 seconds.
    *   *Group B:* Updates only on significant altitude changes (e.g., every 15 meters).
*   **The Readout:** Compare the churn rate and support ticket volume between groups. If Group A has higher churn, the high-frequency updates are creating noise/anxiety. If Group B users refresh manually more often, they lack reassurance.
*   **Constraint:** Do not declare a winner until the pre-committed pilot volume (500 daily deliveries) is reached. Peeking at results after day 2 of the April 12th launch will yield false positives.
