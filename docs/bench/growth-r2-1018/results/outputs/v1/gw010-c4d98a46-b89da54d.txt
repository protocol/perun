**Executive Summary: UrbanMove Austin Pilot (Weeks 1–3)**

The new e-scooter fleet is delivering its primary operational value: battery depletion incidents have dropped 40%, confirming improved reliability over the legacy fleet. However, this gain is accompanied by a 20% rise in unauthorized zone endings. While total volume stands at 12,000 rides with a 14-minute average duration, the parking signal suggests the stricter geofencing intended to improve compliance is currently driving unintended user behavior.

**Behavioral Narrative & Operational Impact**
The data indicates a friction point between system precision and user convenience. The assumption that higher GPS accuracy would automatically correct parking behavior has not held; instead, users appear to be reacting to the tighter constraints. Two distinct behaviors are likely driving the 20% increase:
1.  **Exploitation:** Users may be identifying "loopholes" where the new GPS allows a ride end just outside the technical boundary while remaining visually close to prohibited zones.
2.  **Restriction Frustration:** Legitimate users attempting to park near destinations find the allowed zones too restrictive or inaccessible, forcing them to end rides in the nearest available (but unauthorized) spot.

For the city liaison, the narrative must shift from "GPS failure" to "calibration phase." The operational impact is a temporary spike in compliance reports, but the underlying asset health (battery) is significantly improved. The risk is not system error, but a mismatch between the digital fence and physical reality.

**Decision Framework: Adjust Rules vs. Educate**
Do not launch a broad educational campaign yet. If the root cause is restrictive geofencing, education will not solve the inability to park legally. If the cause is exploitation, education is insufficient without enforcement. The decision depends on distinguishing intent.

**Required Data Points to Determine Intent**
To decide between relaxing geofences or enforcing penalties, gather these specific metrics immediately:
*   **Distance-to-Zone Delta:** Measure the average distance users travel from their intended destination to the nearest valid parking zone. A high average suggests the zones are too restrictive (supporting rule adjustment).
*   **End-Ride Precision Heatmap:** Overlay unauthorized end points against the new GPS accuracy margin. If clusters form exactly at the boundary edge, users are exploiting the tolerance (supporting stricter logic or penalties).
*   **Repeat Offender Rate:** Calculate the percentage of unauthorized ends coming from unique users versus distributed across the base. A high concentration implies deliberate exploitation; a wide distribution suggests systemic confusion or restriction.
*   **Session Abandonment Time:** Analyze the time spent circling or idling before an unauthorized end. Long durations indicate users trying to find a valid spot and failing (restriction issue); instant ends suggest pre-meditated non-compliance.

**Immediate Next Step**
Pause any public messaging. Run the **Distance-to-Zone Delta** analysis on the 12,000 rides by Friday. If the average detour to a valid zone exceeds 40 meters, propose a geofence expansion to the city. If the detour is minimal but violations persist at boundary edges, prepare a targeted warning system for repeat offenders rather than a general campaign.

*Note: All metrics above must be calculated from existing telemetry; no new user surveys are required at this stage.*
*Baseline comparison for "unauthorized zones" must use the old fleet's data to ensure the 20% increase is not simply better detection of existing behavior.*
