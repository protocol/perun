### Recommended Metrics & Narrative for Series A Update

Given your high-value, low-volume B2B context (12 deals in Q1), standard volume-based funnels are indeed vanity metrics that obscure real progress. Your North Star must shift from "number of signups" to **Depth of Integration During Trial**. Since you hypothesize that document uploads predict contracts, validate this not with statistical significance (which is impossible with N=12), but with **consistency of behavior** across your tiny cohort.

Present these three leading indicators to demonstrate momentum without overstating data:

**1. The "Core Action" Density Rate (Not Conversion Rate)**
Instead of a binary "Trial → Paid" conversion rate, measure the **percentage of trial accounts that hit a specific "activation threshold"** (e.g., uploading >5 documents or connecting >1 carrier) within the first 14 days.
*   **The Story:** "While total volume is low, 83% of teams that reach our 'Integration Threshold' proceed to a signed contract. Our focus is now on driving more trials to this specific depth."
*   **Why it works:** It proves you have identified the *mechanism* of value (the "aha" moment) even if the total sample size is small. It shifts the investor question from "Why so few users?" to "How fast can you drive users to the threshold?"

**2. Sales Cycle Compression & Stage Velocity**
Track the **average days spent in each sales stage** for the 12 closed deals versus industry benchmarks (if known) or your own internal baseline from Q4.
*   **The Story:** "Our average sales cycle is 4 months, but for deals where the champion uses the document upload feature weekly, the cycle compresses to 2.5 months. We are seeing a 35% velocity increase in engaged accounts."
*   **Why it works:** In enterprise sales, time-to-close is a stronger proxy for product-market fit than raw logo count. It demonstrates that the product is actively removing friction in a complex buying process.

**3. Qualitative "Signal Strength" Score**
Create a composite score for active trials based on binary signals: *Did they upload a doc? Did they invite a second user? Did they book an onboarding call?*
*   **The Story:** "We track a 'Readiness Score' for all active trials. Currently, 4 accounts are scoring in the 'High Intent' band (matching the behavior profile of our 12 closed deals). These represent $Xk in potential ARR currently in the pipeline."
*   **Why it works:** It turns sparse behavioral data into a forward-looking revenue forecast. It shows investors you can *predict* future revenue based on current behavior, which is the definition of a scalable engine.

**Framing the Narrative**
Do not apologize for the low N. Frame it as **intentional scarcity** due to high contract value. Explicitly state: "We are not optimizing for top-of-funnel vanity; we are optimizing for the *quality* of the handshake between shipper and carrier." Present the 12 deals not as a small number, but as a **validated cohort** where the correlation between your hypothesized metric (uploads) and revenue is 100% (or whatever your actual data shows). If even one deal closed *without* uploads, note that as a critical learning to refine the hypothesis.
