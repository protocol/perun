**Decision: Do not roll out globally yet. Extend the analysis with segmented cohorts to isolate the "efficiency" win from the "overwhelm" signal.**

1.  **Purchase Intent Cohorts (High vs. Low):**
    Segment users by their historical behavior prior to this test. Compare "high-intent" users (those who typically search for specific SKUs) against "browsers" (those who historically have long sessions and low immediate conversion).

    *   *Action:* If bounce rate is concentrated entirely in the "browser" cohort, the feature is working as intended for its target audience but needs a different fallback experience for explorers.

2.  **Recommendation Relevance (Click-Through Rate on Recs):**

    *   *Hypothesis:* Users who engaged with recs should show higher AOV and *lower* bounce rates. If users who ignored the recs also bounced at higher rates, the placement or visual weight of the recommendations is likely disrupting the core navigation, creating the "overwhelm" effect.
    *   *Action:* If the "ignored" cohort drives the negative metrics, the issue is UI intrusion, not the algorithm itself.

3.  **Return Visitor Frequency (New Returners vs. Loyalists):**
    Slice by how often the "returning visitor" comes back. Distinguish between someone returning after 30 days versus someone returning daily.
    *   *Hypothesis:* Daily loyalists might find the efficiency helpful, while monthly returners might need the discovery aspect of a longer session. The 10% drop in session duration might be a net positive for loyalists (friction removed) but a net negative for lapsed users (discovery failed).

The increased bounce rate suggests that for a subset of users, the homepage failed to provide the discovery environment they expected.
