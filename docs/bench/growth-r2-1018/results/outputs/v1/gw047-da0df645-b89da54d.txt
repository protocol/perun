**Recommendation: Merchant Repeat Rate (Cohort-Based)**

For CargoUnite, the single North Star metric must be **Merchant Repeat Rate** (specifically, the percentage of merchants who book a second shipment within 30 days of their first).

This metric alone aligns your operations team with long-term sustainability because it directly measures whether your core assumption—that reliability drives lifetime value—is true. In a fragmented logistics market like Ho Chi Minh City, where trust is scarce and switching costs are low, a merchant returning is the only definitive proof that you delivered value.

**Trade-off Analysis**

1.  **Total Gross Merchandise Value (GMV): Reject.**
    *   *The Trap:* GMV is a vanity metric in your context. It can be inflated by a few large, one-off shipments or by subsidizing prices to win volume.
    *   *The Misalignment:* If operations chases GMV, they may prioritize large, complex jobs that strain your fragmented trucker network, leading to delays and failed deliveries. This burns cash and damages reputation without building a sustainable base. Rising GMV with flat retention means you are pouring water into a leaky bucket.

2.  **Number of Successful Deliveries: Reject.**
    *   *The Trap:* This measures output, not outcome. It counts the transaction but ignores the customer's satisfaction or future intent.
    *   *The Misalignment:* Operations could maximize this by focusing on easy, short-haul routes while neglecting harder, high-value lanes that merchants actually need. It encourages "churning and burning" shipments rather than building reliable merchant relationships. You can have 1,200 successful deliveries and still go bankrupt if those 1,200 belong to 1,200 different merchants who never return.

3.  **Merchant Repeat Rate: Select.**
    *   *The Value:* This metric forces the entire organization to focus on the *quality* of the service. For a merchant to return, the trucker must arrive on time, the cargo must be safe, and the price must be fair.
    *   *The Alignment:* It prevents local optimization. Sales cannot just sign up anyone; they must find merchants who fit your reliability profile. Operations cannot just move boxes; they must ensure the specific conditions that cause a merchant to book again are met. It directly ties your operational execution (on-time delivery) to your financial future (LTV).

**Why This Fits Your Market Dynamics**
In a fragmented supply market, your inventory (truckers) is variable. You cannot guarantee capacity the way a fleet owner can. Therefore, your product is not "moving boxes"; it is "reliable access." Only a repeat merchant validates that you have solved the reliability problem. If this number rises, your network health is improving. If it falls, no amount of new acquisition will save the business.

**Immediate Next Steps**
1.  **Define the Event:** Instrument a `merchant_booking_completed` event linked to a stable, pseudonymous merchant ID to track the 30-day return window.
2.  **Establish Baseline:** Calculate your current repeat rate from the last 1,200 shipments before Thursday's meeting; do not guess.
3.  **Set the Guardrail:** Monitor "On-Time Delivery Rate" as a leading input metric to ensure the Repeat Rate isn't being gamed by lowering standards.

*Note: Do not fabricate a "good" benchmark percentage; compare your future performance strictly against the baseline you calculate this week. Ensure merchant IDs are pseudonymized to comply with privacy standards.*
