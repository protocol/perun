**Executive Summary: IronLink Q3 Growth & Measurement Strategy**

**The Verdict:** The 20% rise in Request-for-Quote (RFQ) submissions is an **UNVERIFIED** win. Without extending the attribution window, we cannot distinguish between a successful content strategy, seasonal noise, or merely earlier intent capture. Do not aggregate quarters yet; instead, **extend the attribution window to 60 days immediately** to match the 45-day buyer cycle. Aggregating quarters masks causality; extending the window reveals it.

**1. North Star Metric: Qualified RFQs Converted (Not Submitted)**
Reject "Total RFQs" as a vanity metric; it rises regardless of business health if lead quality drops. Your North Star must be **Weekly Active Buyers Completing a Transaction** or, given the long cycle, **RFQs Submitted that Convert to Deal within 60 Days**. Until the window extends, label current conversion data as `UNVERIFIED`. The input metric to watch is **RFQ Quality Score** (e.g., specificity of part numbers, budget attached), not raw volume.

**2. Funnel Diagnosis: Retention/Conversion Before Acquisition**
You are pouring acquisition effort (content) into a measurement bucket with a hole (14-day window).
*   **The Leak:** A 14-day window for a 45-day cycle guarantees false negatives. You are likely attributing wins to "Direct" or "Last Click" incorrectly, starving your content strategy of credit.
*   **The Fix:** Shift analysis bottom-up. Before celebrating the 20% volume spike, verify if the *conversion rate* of those RFQs holds steady. If volume rises but 60-day conversion drops, the content is attracting low-intent traffic.

**3. Event Taxonomy: Answer Named Questions Only**
Instrument only what resolves the "Content vs. Seasonality" debate. Do not track "everything."
*   **Event:** `rfq_submitted` with property `content_source_id`.
*   **Question:** "Do users from the new content cohort convert at a higher rate than historical seasonal baselines?"
*   **Identifier Seam:** Use a stable, pseudonymous User/Organization ID to track the same entity across the 45+ day journey. Do not use raw PII; route identity governance to privacy compliance protocols. This stable ID is critical to invert the short-window ops rule.

**4. Experiment Rigor for Sparse Data (N=150/week)**
Standard A/B testing (p<0.05 fixed horizon) will fail here; you cannot reach significance quickly without peeking errors.
*   **Design:** Use **Sequential Testing** (e.g., mSPRT or confidence sequences) which allows continuous monitoring without inflating false positives, or pre-commit to a **quarterly readout** based on a Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE) calculated for your specific N.
*   **Guardrail:** Monitor **Average Deal Size** ($45k). If RFQ volume spikes but deal size dips, stop the content experiment immediately.
*   **No Peeking:** A result observed at day 14 of a 45-day cycle is `UNVERIFIED`. Do not report interim wins to the CEO.

**5. This-Week Steps**
1.  **Reconfigure Analytics:** Change the attribution lookback window from 14 to 60 days today. Re-run Q3 RFQ-to-Deal attribution; if the lift disappears, the spike was timing, not value.
2.  **Define the Cohort Query:** Isolate users who touched the new content vs. those who did not. Compare their 60-day conversion rates, not just submission counts.
3.  **Set the Tripwire:** If the 60-day conversion rate for the new content cohort falls below the historical baseline by >5%, pause the content push. If it holds steady or improves, scale.

**Note:** Never assert a "good" conversion rate benchmark; compare only against IronLink's own historical baseline. Rising signups with flat revenue is a classic retention/quality leak, not growth.
