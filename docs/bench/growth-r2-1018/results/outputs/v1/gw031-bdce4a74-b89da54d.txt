**Recommendation: Adopt "Gross Merchandise Value (GMV) of Retained Jobs" as your North Star.**

Reject "Total Completed Repairs" immediately. As you suspected, it is a vanity metric that rises even if you only facilitate low-value bulb replacements, decoupling growth from actual business health. "Total Transactions" optimizes for volume, not value, and risks flooding your platform with low-margin noise that strains technician supply without driving revenue.

However, raw GMV alone is also dangerous; it can be inflated by one-off expensive jobs from customers who never return. Therefore, your single guiding metric must be **GMV generated by users who have completed a second job within 90 days**. This forces the roadmap to balance three critical levers simultaneously: attracting high-value demand, ensuring technician capacity for complex jobs, and delivering an experience good enough to trigger retention.

**Leading Indicators to Track (Input Metrics)**
Do not instrument "everything." Track only these three inputs that move your North Star:
1.  **Complex Job Activation Rate:** The percentage of new homeowners whose *first* completed job exceeds $200 (or your local median complex job threshold). This validates demand quality immediately, rather than waiting six months.
2.  **Technician Acceptance Rate for High-Value Jobs:** The percentage of complex job requests accepted within 2 hours. If this drops, your supply side is misaligned (technicians preferring quick, low-value tasks), choking the North Star.
3.  **Day-30 Return Rate:** The percentage of users booking a second job within 30 days. This is your earliest proxy for the six-month retention assumption. If this is flat, your hypothesis that "value drives retention" is unverified.

**Decision Framework: Volume vs. Value**
To finalize your Q3 roadmap by next Friday, run this analysis on your existing 4,500 jobs using your current data:
*   **Cohort Split:** Separate users into "High-Value First Job" vs. "Low-Value First Job."
*   **Retention Check:** Calculate the Day-30 and Day-90 return rates for both cohorts.
    *   *If High-Value retention is <2x Low-Value retention:* Your assumption is **UNVERIFIED**. Do not optimize for GMV yet. Your Q3 focus must be discovery: interview users to understand why high spenders aren't returning.
    *   *If High-Value retention is significantly higher:* Proceed with GMV-focused North Star. Your Q3 roadmap must prioritize features that increase the *mix* of complex jobs (e.g., better categorization, pricing tools for plumbers) rather than features that simply increase total booking volume.
