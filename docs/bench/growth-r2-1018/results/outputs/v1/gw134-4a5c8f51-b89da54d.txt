# LogiTrack Pilot Success Metrics (April 5 – June 4)

## 1. North Star Metric: Verified Delay Detection Time
**Definition:** The median time elapsed between a real-world shipment deviation occurring and the system generating a verified alert to the logistics manager.
**Target:** < 6 hours (Baseline: 48 hours).
**Why:** This directly measures the core value promise. Unlike "alerts sent" (vanity), this only rises when the system correctly identifies a problem faster than the status quo.
**Input Metrics:**
*   *Data Ingestion Latency:* Time from carrier update to LogiTrack ingestion.
*   *False Positive Rate:* % of alerts flagged as delays that were not actual delays (critical for trust).

## 2. The Funnel (Read Bottom-Up: Retention First)
Do not measure "number of factories onboarded" yet. Measure if they *stay* and *act*.
*   **Retention (Core Action):** % of logistics managers who log in or acknowledge an alert within 24 hours of receipt, weekly.
    *   *Question:* Are they relying on us, or reverting to phone calls/emails?
*   **Activation:** % of pilot factories that execute a documented operational change (e.g., rerouting, expediting) based *specifically* on a LogiTrack alert within the first 30 days.
*   **Acquisition:** N/A for this pilot (factories are pre-selected).

## 3. Qualitative Indicator: The "Trust Proxy"
You cannot instrument "trust" directly. Instrument the *behavior* that proves it.
*   **Metric:** **Manual Override Rate**.
*   **Definition:** The frequency with which a logistics manager ignores a LogiTrack alert to make a decision based on external info, vs. the frequency they escalate an issue *without* an alert.
*   **Method:** Weekly 15-minute structured interviews with the 5 factory leads. Ask: "Did you act on an alert this week? Did you miss an issue we didn't catch?"
*   **Success Signal:** Managers cite LogiTrack data in their internal reports to leadership. (Collect 3 distinct examples by Day 45).

## 4. Resolution to Your Trade-off: Speed vs. Frequency
**Decision:** Prioritize **Speed of Detection Accuracy** over Frequency.
*   **Reasoning:** In electronics manufacturing, alert fatigue causes managers to ignore the system. A high frequency of low-confidence alerts destroys the "Trust Proxy."
*   **Rule:** Do not instrument "Total Alerts Sent" as a success metric. Instrument "Actionable Alerts" (alerts that led to a verified operational change). If speed drops but accuracy rises, trust holds. If speed rises but false positives spike, trust collapses.

## 5. Event Taxonomy (Instrument Only These)
Every event below must answer a named question. Do not log "page views."
| Event Name | Question Answered | Properties |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `delay_detected` | Did we find the delay faster than 48h? | `shipment_id`, `detection_latency_minutes`, `confidence_score` |
| `alert_acknowledged` | Did the human see and accept the risk? | `user_id` (pseudonymous), `time_to_acknowledge` |
| `action_taken` | Did the alert drive real value? | `action_type` (reroute/expedite), `estimated_cost_saved` |
| `false_positive_flag` | Are we crying wolf? | `reason_code`, `user_id` |

## 6. Verification & Constraints
*   **Inventory Cost Claim:** The "15% reduction in holding costs" is **UNVERIFIED** at this stage. You cannot measure this in 60 days without baseline cost data from the factories.
    *   *Action:* Add a field to the `action_taken` event for the manager to estimate "Hours of delay prevented." Use this as a leading indicator for cost savings; do not promise the 15% figure until post-pilot analysis.
*   **Identifier Seam:** Use a stable, pseudonymous `factory_unit_id` to track cohorts across sessions. Do not use personal emails or names in analytics logs; route PII to your privacy compliance protocol.
*   **No Benchmarks:** Do not compare your detection time to global averages. Compare April's median time to May's median time for *these specific five factories*.
