**North Star Metric: Weekly Active Users Completing a 5-Day Streak**

For FitQuest, the definitive metric to optimize is not daily active users (DAU), weekly completed challenges, or social shares. It is **Weekly Active Users who complete a 5-day workout streak**.

This specific action matters more than the alternatives because it directly measures the habit-forming threshold you identified in your pilot: users who hit five days tend to return the following month. In growth analytics, this is your "activation" or "aha" moment—the point where a user derives recurring value rather than just sampling the product.

Here is why the other candidates fail as your primary North Star:
*   **Daily Active Users (DAU):** This is a vanity metric in your context. A user can open the app daily to check leaderboards without ever exercising. DAU can rise while actual health outcomes and long-term retention flatline, misleading your team into thinking the product is healthy when it is not.
*   **Weekly Completed Challenges:** This measures volume, not consistency. A user could binge three challenges on Saturday and remain inactive for the rest of the week, failing to build the daily habit required for fitness outcomes. It ignores the "streak" mechanic that drives your retention.
*   **Social Shares Generated:** This measures virality, not core value. While social competition is your hypothesized driver, a share is a secondary action. Optimizing for shares might encourage users to spam friends without completing workouts themselves, decoupling the metric from actual fitness results.

**Why the 5-Day Streak is the Core Loop**
Your product design team must redesign the home screen to drive *this specific behavior*. The core loop of a fitness gamification app is not "open app → see content"; it is "commit → execute → maintain streak → feel progress." The 5-day streak is the proxy for "feel progress." If the UI makes the streak status invisible, hard to track, or easy to break without recovery options, you break the loop regardless of how engaging the social features are.

By making "Users completing a 5-day streak" your North Star, every UI change on the home screen can be evaluated against a single question: "Does this make it more likely for a user to log their fifth workout this week?" This aligns your design efforts directly with the behavior that predicts long-term retention and actual health outcomes, avoiding the trap of optimizing for noise.

**Note:** Instrument this event immediately with a stable, pseudonymous user ID to track cohorts over time; do not use raw PII. Since you have not yet A/B tested the social hypothesis, treat the "social drives consistency" assumption as unverified until a controlled experiment isolates it from the exercise content.
