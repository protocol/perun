**Top Five Behaviors to Measure Immediately**

1.  **Week-2 Core Action Retention:** The percentage of users who complete a *second* video consultation within 14 days of sign-up.
    *   *Interpretation:* This is your true North Star candidate. If this is low, video quality or booking speed is failing to deliver repeat value, regardless of initial sign-up volume. High sign-ups with low Week-2 retention indicates a "leaky bucket" where marketing spend is wasted.

2.  **Activation Rate (The "Aha" Moment):** The percentage of sign-ups who successfully complete a *full* video call (duration > 5 mins) within 24 hours.
    *   *Interpretation:* This validates if users can overcome friction to experience the core value. If sign-ups are high but activation is low, the barrier is likely booking speed or technical onboarding, not feature preference.

3.  **Call Quality vs. Abandonment Correlation:** The rate of call drops or user-initiated exits during the first 2 minutes, segmented by connection type.
    *   *Interpretation:* Directly tests your assumption about video quality priority. A high early-abandonment rate suggests quality is the primary blocker. If abandonment is low but repeat usage is low, the issue may be utility, not quality.

4.  **Booking Flow Completion Time:** The median time from "Find a Vet" click to "Call Started," tracked per user session.
    *   *Interpretation:* Measures the "booking speed" variable. If this time correlates negatively with activation (i.e., slower booking = lower activation), your assumption about quality over speed is incorrect, and resources should shift to triage/booking efficiency.

5.  **Feature Interaction Depth:** The frequency of specific in-call actions (e.g., screen share usage, file upload, chat toggle) during successful calls (>10 mins).
    *   *Interpretation:* Identifies which specific interaction points predict long-term subscription. If users who utilize screen sharing have 3x higher retention than those who don't, that specific behavior predicts value better than generic "total calls."

**How to Interpret Without Historical Data**
Since you lack benchmarks, do not ask "Is 20% retention good?" Instead, establish your own baseline immediately upon launch. Compare **Cohort A** (users exposed to high-friction booking) vs. **Cohort B** (users with streamlined booking) internally. Your only valid benchmark is your own trend: if Week-2 retention rises from 10% to 15% after a product tweak, that is a win. Any metric not tied to a specific question above (like raw page views) should be ignored to avoid vanity noise.

**Note:** Ensure all user tracking uses a stable, pseudonymous ID to link sessions without violating privacy compliance; do not use raw PII for analytics. Do not stop measurement early to check for "significance" before your pre-defined review date; peeking at data inflates false positives.
