Neither "average calories burned" nor "friends invited" should be your North Star metric for the beta launch. Both are vanity metrics in this context: calories measure session intensity but not habit formation, and invites measure viral potential but not whether the core product delivers value. If users burn calories once and quit, or invite friends who never return, your business fails despite these numbers rising.

Your North Star must be **Weekly Active Users completing 3+ classes**.

This metric directly tracks your core assumption: that completing three classes in the first week predicts six-month retention. It rises only when users derive real value (habit formation) and aligns with your goal of building a habitual user base. Unlike calories (which can be gamed by one intense session) or invites (which can be spammy), this metric proves users are returning to the platform repeatedly.

**Analysis of your proposed options:**

*   **Average calories burned per session:**
    *   *Pros:* Easy to capture; appeals to hardware partners' fitness narrative.
    *   *Cons:* A user can burn 1,000 calories in a single session and never return. This metric ignores retention entirely. High averages could mask a churn problem where only power users remain.
*   **Number of friends invited per user:**
    *   *Pros:* Indicates social engagement; useful for viral growth modeling.
    *   *Cons:* Invites do not equal activation. Users may invite friends who never complete a class. Focusing here risks optimizing for acquisition while ignoring the "leaky bucket" of retention.

**Addressing the social vs. solo unknown:**
Do not make social features the North Star yet. Instead, treat "social vs. solo" as an input variable to your North Star. Segment your **Weekly Active Users (3+ classes)** by those who worked out with friends versus those who tracked solo. If the "friends" segment shows significantly higher retention, you have your answer without distorting your primary goal.

**Recommendation for the May 20th presentation:**
Present **Weekly Active Users completing 3+ classes** as the definitive goal. Tell the hardware partners that marketing spend should target users likely to complete three sessions in week one, not just those who sign up or burn calories. This aligns their spend with actual habit formation, ensuring the 500 waitlist signups convert into a sustainable community.

**This week's steps:**
1. Define the event "class_completed" and ensure it links to a stable, pseudonymous user ID to track the 3-class threshold across sessions.
2. Prepare a cohort query for June 15th comparing retention rates of users who hit 3 classes with friends versus solo; the result that changes your roadmap is if the solo cohort retains equally well, in which case you delay heavy social investment.

Note: Ensure user IDs are pseudonymous to comply with privacy standards; do not use raw PII for cohort analysis. Do not fabricate retention benchmarks; compare your June data only against your own July/August trends.
