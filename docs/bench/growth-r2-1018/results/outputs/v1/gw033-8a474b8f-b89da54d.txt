**Recommendation: Optimize bidding for "Verified Repeat Intent" rather than raw CAC or theoretical LTV.**

Do not allocate the remaining $300,000 to minimize Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) or maximize projected Lifetime Value (LTV) yet. Your current data invalidates the core assumption driving your LTV model: with an 8% repeat purchase rate against a finance team expectation of high sofa-to-chair conversion, your projected LTV is likely inflated by 5–10x. Bidding against an inflated LTV will cause you to overpay for customers who behave like one-time buyers, while bidding strictly on CAC will attract low-intent traffic that never converts to your high-margin core items.

**The Trade-Off Analysis Structure**
Present this framework to your CEO and Head of Marketing:

1.  **The Baseline Reality:** Your actual unit economics are currently driven by single-transaction revenue ($950 AOV). The "sofa-to-chair" upsell is a hypothesis, not a metric. Until verified, treat LTV as equal to AOV for bidding purposes to avoid overspending.
2.  **The Risk of CAC Optimization:** Minimizing CAC often lowers lead quality. In furniture, cheap clicks frequently come from bargain hunters who cannot afford a $950 sofa, let alone a matching set later. This depresses your already low 8% retention further.
3.  **The Risk of LTV Optimization:** Bidding on the *assumed* LTV (sofa + chairs) assumes a conversion rate you do not have. If you bid $200 per acquisition expecting $1,500 in return but only get $950, you will burn the $300k budget faster than planned.
4.  **The Solution – "Verified Repeat Intent":** Shift the bidding target to users who exhibit behaviors correlated with your *actual* 8% repeaters, not the theoretical majority. Use your existing data to identify what the 8% did differently in their first 30 days (e.g., viewed care guides, bought add-ons, signed up for specific newsletters) and bid to acquire *those* profiles.

**Immediate Data Points to Gather (Before Final Commitment)**
You cannot validate the 18-month assumption tomorrow, but you can validate the *leading indicators* tonight. Gather these three specific cuts from your analytics:

*   **Cohort Retention by Product Type:** Segment your 8% repeaters. Did they buy dining sets first, or sofas? If sofa buyers never return but dining set buyers do, your LTV model is segment-specific, not brand-wide.
*   **Time-to-Second-Purchase Distribution:** Plot the days between purchase 1 and 2 for your repeaters. If the cluster is at 3 months, not 18, your finance team's timeline is wrong, affecting cash flow planning.
*   **Pre-Purchase Behavior Correlation:** Identify the top 3 events completed by the 8% before their first purchase (e.g., "downloaded fabric guide," "used AR viewer"). These become your proxy metrics for "high LTV potential" to guide Q4 bidding.

**Decision for Tomorrow:**
Allocate the $300k to test **creative and audience segments** that target the behaviors of your known 8% repeaters. Set a hard guardrail: if the repeat rate on this new cohort does not exceed 12% within 90 days, revert to AOV-based bidding. Do not commit the full year's strategy to an 18-month hypothesis without a 90-day validation sprint.

*Note: Your North Star for Q4 should be "Repeat Purchase Rate of New Cohorts," not total revenue or raw CAC. This forces the organization to prove the LTV model before scaling spend.*
