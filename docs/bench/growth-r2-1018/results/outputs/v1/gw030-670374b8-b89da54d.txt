**Analysis of Risk & Required Steps**

Your assumption that students are inherently low-value is a hypothesis, not a fact. The 30% lower Average Order Value (AOV) likely stems from two confounders inherent to a pilot: **promotional pricing** (did you discount the items themselves?) and **novelty/activation behavior** (new users often start with small "test" purchases). Without separating these, you cannot distinguish between a permanent demographic trait and a temporary pilot artifact.

To assess the risk of scaling, you must shift from vanity metrics (10% adoption) to value metrics (Retention and Net Revenue) using your own data:

1.  **Isolate the Price Effect:** Segment the pilot data by users who bought full-price items vs. discounted items. If the AOV gap disappears among full-price buyers, the issue is your pricing strategy, not the student demographic. If the gap persists, the low spend may be structural.
2.  **Read Retention Before Acquisition:** Adoption (10%) is an acquisition metric. It tells you nothing about value. You must analyze **Week-4 and Week-8 retention cohorts**. Did the students who bought in September return in October? If retention is high despite low AOV, the Lifetime Value (LTV) may still be viable through frequency. If retention drops off, the low AOV is compounded by churn, making the cohort unprofitable.
3.  **Define the North Star for this Segment:** Reject "Total Signups" as your success metric. Your North Star must be **Weekly Active Buyers** or **Repeat Purchase Rate**. If students only buy once during the promo window, scaling acquisition will only increase marketing waste.
