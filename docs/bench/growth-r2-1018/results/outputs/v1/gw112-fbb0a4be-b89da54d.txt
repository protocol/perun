### Recommended KPIs for Welcome Email Delay Experiment

To validate your hypothesis without falling into vanity metrics, the dashboard must track **retention of the core action** and **guardrail risks** before optimizing for clicks. The following metrics answer specific questions about user value and risk:

**1. Primary Outcome: Day-7 Core Action Retention**
*   **Question:** Do users who wait 24 hours actually perform the product's core value action (e.g., reading an article, setting a preference) at a higher rate than those contacted immediately?
*   **Definition:** Percentage of users in each cohort who complete the defined "core action" within 7 days of signup.
*   **Why:** Open/click rates are vanity if they don't lead to value. If the delay increases clicks but decreases core action retention, the hypothesis fails.

**2. Guardrail Metric: Day-1 Unsubscribe Rate**
*   **Question:** Does the 24-hour silence cause users to lose interest or forget why they signed up, leading to immediate churn?
*   **Definition:** Percentage of users who unsubscribe within the first 24 hours post-signup.
*   **Why:** This is your stated unknown variable. Set a tripwire: if the treatment group's Day-1 unsubscribe rate exceeds the control group by >0.5% (absolute), stop the experiment early regardless of engagement lifts.

**3. Activation Latency**
*   **Question:** Does delaying the email shift *when* users activate, or does it prevent activation entirely?
*   **Definition:** Median time (in hours) from signup to first core action.
*   **Why:** You hypothesize users need time to explore. If the median latency increases in the treatment group but the final 7-day retention remains flat, the delay successfully changed behavior without harming outcomes. If latency increases and retention drops, the silence was harmful.

**4. Secondary Outcome: Click-to-Value Conversion**
*   **Question:** Are the clicks we do get higher quality?
*   **Definition:** Percentage of users who click the welcome email AND subsequently complete the core action within 24 hours of that click.
*   **Why:** A lower click-through rate (CTR) is acceptable if the users who *do* click are more likely to find value. This measures the efficiency of the traffic you drive.

**5. Long-term Signal: Week-2 Return Rate**
*   **Question:** Does the initial delay impact the habit formation required for a newsletter business?
*   **Definition:** Percentage of users who return to the platform or open a subsequent email in Week 2.
*   **Why:** Welcome sequences set the cadence expectation. A win in Week 1 that causes a drop in Week 2 indicates you trained users to ignore you after the first interaction.

**Experiment Governance Requirements**
*   **Pre-commitment:** Do not check these metrics daily. Define the sample size (10,000) and duration required to reach statistical significance for the *primary* metric (Day-7 Core Action Retention) before launch. Reading the data on Day 3 because "it looks good" invalidates the result due to peeking bias.
*   **Identifier Seam:** Ensure your analytics tool links these events via a stable, pseudonymous user ID to track the journey from signup → email send → click → core action. Do not use raw PII in the analytics platform; route consent management to your privacy compliance protocol.
