**Post-Mortem Recommendation: Stop Investment in Theme Customization**

The aggregate data provides a verified verdict: the 0.5% difference in day-30 retention is statistically negligible. Since retention is the primary indicator of long-term loyalty in your AARRR funnel, the hypothesis that visual preference drives loyalty is unsupported. Do not invest further in theme customization based on loyalty gains.

To address your specific uncertainty about hidden segment benefits before finalizing the decision, execute these three targeted slices on your existing dataset. These are not new experiments but forensic queries to confirm whether "night owls" or specific cohorts derive value that the aggregate masked:

1.  **Time-of-Day Cohort Split:** Segment users by their primary reading window (e.g., 6 PM–6 AM vs. 6 AM–6 PM). Compare **session duration** and **articles-per-session** for the dark mode group within the night cohort against the light mode night cohort.
    *   *Decision Trigger:* If night-session duration increases by >5% for dark mode users while retention remains flat, consider retaining dark mode as a niche accessibility feature, not a growth lever. If no difference exists, the feature adds no behavioral value.

2.  **Activation vs. Retention Check:** Isolate users who *manually toggled* to dark mode versus those who stayed on default. Did the "toggling" group show higher activation (core action completion) in week 1, even if day-30 retention converged?
    *   *Decision Trigger:* If activators show no early engagement lift, the feature is invisible to user value. If they show early lift but no retention gain, the novelty wears off, confirming content relevance as the true driver.

3.  **Guardrail Metric Review:** Check if the mandatory option increased support tickets or negative app store reviews regarding visibility or battery usage.
    *   *Decision Trigger:* Any increase in friction costs without a corresponding retention gain is a definitive signal to drop the feature entirely.

**Conclusion for Head of Product:**
Unless the time-of-day slice reveals a dramatic (>5%) engagement lift for night readers, the data confirms your assumption: content relevance drives loyalty, not themes. The recommended action is to **drop further investment** in theme customization. Maintain the current implementation only if engineering maintenance costs are near zero; otherwise, revert to the standard light theme to reduce interface complexity. The "hidden signal" you seek is likely noise; the flat retention curve is the true signal.
