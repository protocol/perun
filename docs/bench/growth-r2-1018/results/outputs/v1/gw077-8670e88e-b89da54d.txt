**Recommendation: Optimize for "Weekly Active Contributors Completing a Review Cycle"**

Do not choose between "time to first review" (speed) or "unique contributors per project" (breadth) as your single North Star. Both are vanity metrics in isolation. Speed without breadth creates an exclusive club for power users; breadth without successful completion creates a graveyard of abandoned pull requests.

Your mission is "democratizing software development." This value is only realized when a contributor—new or existing—successfully lands code. Therefore, your North Star must be **Weekly Active Contributors Completing a Review Cycle**. Define "completing" as a user who opens a PR and sees it merged (or closed with a valid reason) within a defined window.

**Why this metric wins:**
1.  **It captures value, not just activity:** A fast review time means nothing if the PR is never merged. A high count of unique contributors means nothing if they all churn after one rejected attempt. This metric rises only when a human being successfully contributes.
2.  **It resolves your tension:** If you optimize for this, you are forced to improve "time to first review" *because* speed drives completion. Simultaneously, you are forced to broaden participation *because* the metric counts unique users, not total events. It naturally balances the power-user efficiency against the need for new blood.
3.  **It aligns with retention:** Your hypothesis states that fast reviews double retention. This metric measures the *outcome* of that retention (a completed cycle) rather than the *input* (speed). If speed increases but completion does not, your hypothesis is false, and you will know immediately.

**Input Metrics to Watch:**
To move this North Star, track these two inputs:
*   **Median Time to First Review:** To ensure the workflow remains fast enough to prevent drop-off.
*   **New Contributor Activation Rate:** The percentage of first-time submitters who complete a second cycle. This ensures you aren't just serving the experts.

**This Week's Step:**
1.  **Query:** Run a cohort analysis on Q2 data grouping users by "time to first review" buckets (<24h, 24-48h, >48h) and measure the % who completed a merge within 14 days. Do not assume the 2x retention rate; verify it against your own baseline.
2.  **Instrument:** If you cannot currently link a specific user ID across sessions to count "completed cycles" per unique human, tag this as `UNVERIFIED`. Implement a pseudonymous stable user ID to track individual contributor journeys, ensuring PII routing complies with your privacy standards.

The goal for your Community Manager is not "faster reviews" or "more users," but "more successful contributions per week."

*Note: Avoid tracking cumulative total contributors; it rises regardless of health and masks churn.*
*Note: If the data shows fast reviews correlate with lower unique completion, prioritize onboarding flows over review speed.*
