SRM: The session split is 50.5% control vs 49.5% treatment. A chi-squared test on these counts yields p < 0.001, indicating a statistically significant deviation from the 50/50 plan, suggesting assignment or logging bias.

PEEK: The readout date (July 2) is only 30 days after the start (June 2), but the planned duration was 28 days. While close, the daily lift data shows monitoring occurred daily. However, since the final readout is past the planned 28 days, strict "peeking" before the horizon isn't the primary error here, but the SRM invalidates the randomization assumption entirely.

POWER: With ~270k users per arm, the test is vastly overpowered for a 5% lift detection; power is not the issue.

SIMPSON: Calculating segment rates: Desktop Control (6.11%) vs Treatment (6.47%); Mobile Control (2.29%) vs Treatment (2.43%). Both segments show a positive lift consistent with the aggregate. No paradox.

DENOM: Randomization was by user ID, but metrics are reported per session. If users have multiple sessions, this violates the independence assumption of the unit of analysis.

NOVELTY: The daily lift fluctuates (4.7% to 8.2%) but shows no clear fading trend over the 30 days; the average remains stable around 6%.

The critical failures are SRM (broken randomization) and DENOM (wrong denominator).

ERRORS: SRM, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
