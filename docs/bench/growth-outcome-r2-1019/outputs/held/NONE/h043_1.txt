The analysis contains multiple critical errors. Second, DENOM applies because users were randomized by user ID, but the primary arm counts and baseline metrics are reported in sessions, mismatching the unit of randomization. Third, SIMPSON's paradox occurs: while the pooled data shows treatment winning (6.9% vs 5.9%), calculating per-user conversion reveals control wins on both desktop (12.8% vs 13.4%) and mobile (3.8% vs 4.0%). The pooled result is reversed within segments due to the session-count imbalance. Finally, PEEK is likely given the "p=0.0" precision and early stop at day 21 without mentioning sequential correction.
