The data shows a severe Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). With 50/50 randomization, expected splits are ~335k each, but observed counts are 357,855 (control) vs 313,758 (treatment). This ~6.5% deviation is statistically impossible by chance, indicating broken assignment or logging. Additionally, the denominator is "sessions" while randomization was by "user id," violating the unit of analysis (DENOM). Multiple users per session or uneven session generation per user biases the conversion rate. While daily lift fluctuates, it doesn't clearly fade to zero, so NOVELTY is not definitively flagged by numbers alone. The test ran nearly the full 28 days (July 20 to Aug 18 is 29 days), so PEEK is unlikely. Segment data aligns with the overall direction, ruling out SIMPSON. Given SRM and DENOM, the results are invalid.

ERRORS: SRM, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
