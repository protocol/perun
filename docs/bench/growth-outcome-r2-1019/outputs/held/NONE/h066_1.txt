The arm sizes (91,761 vs 96,656) deviate significantly from the planned 50/50 split, indicating Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). Additionally, the metric denominator is "sessions," while randomization occurred by "user id," violating the unit of analysis (DENOM). Although the test stopped early (18 days vs 28 planned), the p-value (0.35) is not significant, so PEEK is not the primary driver of a false positive here. However, SRM and DENOM invalidate the statistical integrity of the results regardless of significance. The daily lift appears stable, ruling out NOVELTY. Segment data does not show a reversal of the aggregate trend, ruling out SIMPSON. Due to broken randomization assumptions and incorrect denominators, the results cannot be trusted for a rollout decision.
