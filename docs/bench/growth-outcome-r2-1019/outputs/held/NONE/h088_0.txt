The data reveals multiple critical errors. First, SRM is present: the split is ~51.9% control vs ~48.1% treatment, a significant deviation from the 50/50 plan indicating assignment issues. Second, DENOM applies because randomization was by user ID, but the primary arm counts and conversion rates use "sessions" as the denominator, violating the unit of analysis. Third, SIMPSON's paradox occurs: while the pooled data shows treatment winning (3.77% vs 3.35%), the desktop segment alone shows treatment losing (5,584/67,550 ≈ 8.27% vs 4,910/56,689 ≈ 8.66%). The daily lift data appears stable, ruling out NOVELTY, and the duration matches the plan, ruling out PEEK. Due to broken randomization and inverted segment results, the result is unreliable.

ERRORS: SRM, DENOM, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
