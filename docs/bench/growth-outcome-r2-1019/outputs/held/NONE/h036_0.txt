The test ran for 14 days against a planned 21-day duration, indicating early stopping (PEEK), which inflates false positive risk. The arm sizes (53,888 vs 55,416) differ by ~2.8%; while potentially significant, the early stop is the clearer violation. The metric denominator is "sessions," but randomization was by "user id," violating the unit of analysis (DENOM). Regarding Simpson's paradox, desktop conversion is ~11.9% (control) vs ~12.6% (treatment), and mobile is ~4.1% vs ~4.4%; both segments show positive lift consistent with the pooled result, so no reversal exists. Daily lift fluctuates but shows no clear fading trend to suggest novelty wear-off within this short window. Power appears sufficient given the significant p-value and large sample sizes.
