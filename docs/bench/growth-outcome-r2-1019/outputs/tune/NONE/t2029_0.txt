The arm sizes (210,122 vs 213,315) are consistent with a 50/50 split given the sample size, so no SRM. The test ran for the planned 14 days, ruling out PEEK. The sample sizes are large enough to detect the target lift, so no POWER issue.

However, calculating conversion rates by device reveals Simpson's Paradox. Desktop: Control ~8.2%, Treatment ~8.6% (Lift). Mobile: Control ~2.7%, Treatment ~2.9% (Lift). Wait, let me re-calculate carefully.
Desktop Control: 5185/63311 = 8.19%. Treatment: 5394/62388 = 8.65%.
Mobile Control: 2440/89177 = 2.74%. Treatment: 2538/87878 = 2.89%.
Both segments show positive lift. The pooled result (5.279% vs 5.0%) also shows positive lift. There is no reversal of direction between segments and the aggregate. Thus, no SIMPSON error.

The metric denominator is sessions, but randomization was by user ID. If users have multiple sessions, this violates the independence assumption and mismatches the randomization unit. This is a DENOM error.

The daily lift fades significantly from 11.8% to 1.9%, suggesting a novelty effect, yet the team projects this lift for twelve months. This is a NOVELTY error.

ERRORS: DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
