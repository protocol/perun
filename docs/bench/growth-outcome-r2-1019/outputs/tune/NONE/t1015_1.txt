The user split (58,375 vs 58,719) is statistically consistent with a 50/50 randomization, ruling out SRM. The test ran exactly 21 days as planned, so PEEK is not indicated. While the p-value (0.1251) shows the result is not statistically significant, the sample sizes appear sufficient to detect the target lift had it existed; the issue is lack of significance, not necessarily lack of power based on provided counts.

However, Simpson's Paradox is clearly present. In the pooled data, Treatment appears better (4.178% vs 4.0%). Yet, within segments: Desktop Conversion is Control ~6.6% vs Treatment ~6.2%, and Mobile Conversion is Control ~2.1% vs Treatment ~1.9%. The treatment loses in both devices but wins in aggregate due to imbalanced segment sizes (more high-converting desktop users in treatment). Additionally, projecting a non-significant result (p > 0.05) as a lasting lift is methodologically flawed.
