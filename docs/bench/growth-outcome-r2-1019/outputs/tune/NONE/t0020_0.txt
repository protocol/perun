The user split is nearly 50/50 (56,320 vs 56,362), ruling out SRM. The test ran 22 days against a 21-day plan, so PEEK is not indicated. Power appears sufficient given the significant p-value. However, Simpson's Paradox is present: while the pooled data shows a treatment lift, the segmented data reveals the treatment lost on mobile (1,017/26,736 ≈ 3.8%) compared to control (1,394/33,719 ≈ 4.1%), and won on desktop. The aggregate positive result is driven by the uneven device split between arms, not a universal improvement. Additionally, projecting a short-term test lift over twelve months without evidence of persistence risks a NOVELTY error, though the daily data looks stable. The primary fatal flaw is the misleading aggregate due to segmentation reversal.
