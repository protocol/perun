The test ran for 8 days against a planned 14-day duration, indicating early stopping (PEEK). While the overall user split is nearly 50/50, the device segments show severe imbalance: desktop is 43% control/57% treatment, while mobile is 55% control/45% treatment. This suggests broken randomization within segments or segmentation issues, but the primary user-level SRM is acceptable (59,288 vs 59,368). However, looking at conversion rates by device: Desktop Control = 6.43%, Treatment = 6.05% (negative lift); Mobile Control = 2.52%, Treatment = 2.37% (negative lift). Both segments show negative lift, yet the pooled result shows positive lift (4.5%). This is a clear case of Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON), likely driven by the skewed device mix between arms. The projection assumes lasting lift despite the test being stopped early and showing paradoxical results.
