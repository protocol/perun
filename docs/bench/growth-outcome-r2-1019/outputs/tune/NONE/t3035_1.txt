The readout shows no Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM); the split is ~50.1% control vs ~49.9% treatment, well within chance variation for this sample size. The test ran for its planned duration (April 24 to May 23), so no Peeking (PEEK) occurred. The sample size appears sufficient given the baseline and target, so no obvious Power (POWER) issue is visible from counts alone.

However, Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON) is present. The pooled data shows Treatment > Control (6.36% vs 6.00%). Yet, calculating segment rates: Desktop Control (1193/12578 ≈ 9.48%) vs Treatment (1272/12649 ≈ 10.05%) shows a lift. Mobile Control (499/15615 ≈ 3.19%) vs Treatment (532/15702 ≈ 3.39%) also shows a lift. Wait, re-calculating: The reported metric is "order conversion rate" per session, but device breakdown is by "users". This is a Denominator (DENOM) error. The randomization unit is User ID, but the primary metric uses Sessions as the denominator ("40,233 sessions"). If users have multiple sessions, the denominator does not match the randomized unit.

Additionally, the daily lift shows a clear downward trend from 11.5% to 1.9%, indicating a Novelty (NOVELTY) effect that contradicts the projection of lasting lift.

ERRORS: DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
