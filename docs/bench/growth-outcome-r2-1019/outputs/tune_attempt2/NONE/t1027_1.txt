**SRM Check**: The total users are 310,202 (Control) and 310,748 (Treatment). Total N = 620,950. Expected per arm = 310,475.
Difference = 546 users.
Standard Deviation for binomial split $\approx \sqrt{N \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.5} = \sqrt{155,237} \approx 394$.
Z-score $\approx 546 / 394 \approx 1.38$. This is within normal chance variation (typically < 1.96 for 95% confidence). No SRM.

**PEEK Check**: Planned duration was 14 days. The readout date is 2026-06-15, and the start was 2026-06-07. This is only 8 days of data. The test was stopped 6 days early. Stopping before the planned duration based on significance inflates false positives. **PEEK flagged.**

**POWER Check**: The sample sizes are large (~310k per arm). With a 3% baseline, this is easily sufficient to detect a 5% lift with 80% power. No POWER issue.

**SIMPSON Check**:
Control Rate Desktop: $5,753 / 110,331 \approx 5.21\%$
Treatment Rate Desktop: $6,879 / 140,152 \approx 4.91\%$
(Lift is negative: -5.8%)

Control Rate Mobile: $3,553 / 199,871 \approx 1.78\%$
Treatment Rate Mobile: $2,855 / 170,596 \approx 1.67\%$
(Lift is negative: -6.2%)

Both segments show a negative lift (treatment worse than control), yet the pooled result reports a positive lift (+4.4%). This is a classic reversal caused by uneven weighting between segments (Simpson's Paradox). **SIMPSON flagged.**

**DENOM Check**: Metric is order conversion rate. Randomization is by user ID. The counts provided for the metric calculation (Orders / Users) align with the randomized unit. No DENOM issue.

**NOVELTY Check**: Daily lift values (4.9, 3.6, 3.9, 4.0, 4.2, 4.7, 4.6, 5.2) do not show a fading trend; they fluctuate but remain positive in the daily breakdown (though the segment math above suggests the daily *pooled* numbers might be masking the segment reversal, the trend itself isn't clearly fading to zero). However, since SIMPSON and PEEK are already definitive, this is secondary. The projection assumes the lift lasts 12 months, but given the paradoxical data, the lift itself is questionable.

The presence of Simpson's Paradox indicates the aggregate result is misleading; the treatment is actually performing worse in both desktop and mobile segments individually. Additionally, stopping the test early (PEEK) invalidates the p-value.
