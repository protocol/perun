First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 152,488 (control) + 150,266 (treatment) = 302,754. The expected split is 50%, so 151,377 per arm. The difference is |152,488 - 151,377| = 1,111. The standard error for the difference in proportions is approx $\sqrt{N \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.5} \approx \sqrt{75688} \approx 275$. A deviation of 1,111 is roughly 4 standard deviations away ($1111/275 \approx 4.04$), which is highly unlikely by chance ($p < 0.0001$). This indicates broken randomization or logging. **SRM is flagged.**

Next, I check the Denominator (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate." Typically, this is Orders / Users or Orders / Sessions. The readout provides both. If the denominator were sessions, the rates would be: Control $7,625/210,122 \approx 3.6\%$, Treatment $7,932/213,315 \approx 3.7\%$. The reported rates are 5.0% and 5.279%. Let's check Orders / Users: Control $7,625/152,488 \approx 5.00\%$. Treatment $7,932/150,266 \approx 5.28\%$. These match the reported numbers exactly. Since users were the randomized unit, using users as the denominator is correct. **DENOM is not flagged.**

I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON).
Desktop Control Rate: $5,185/63,311 \approx 8.19\%$.
Desktop Treatment Rate: $5,394/62,388 \approx 8.65\%$. (Lift positive)
Mobile Control Rate: $2,440/89,177 \approx 2.74\%$.
Mobile Treatment Rate: $2,538/87,878 \approx 2.89\%$. (Lift positive)
Both segments show a positive lift, consistent with the pooled result. **SIMPSON is not flagged.**

I check for Power (POWER). The sample sizes (~150k per arm) are massive compared to the baseline (5%) and target lift (5%). A quick mental check confirms this is vastly overpowered, not underpowered. **POWER is not flagged.**

I check for Peeking (PEEK). The test ran from March 1 to March 15 (14 days), matching the planned duration. There is no evidence of early stopping. **PEEK is not flagged.**

I check for Novelty (NOVELTY). The daily lift starts at 11.8% and decays steadily to 1.9% by day 14. The team projects the current aggregate lift (5.6%) for the next twelve months. This ignores the clear fading trend, assuming the initial spike (novelty effect) represents the long-term steady state. **NOVELTY is flagged.**

ERRORS: SRM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
