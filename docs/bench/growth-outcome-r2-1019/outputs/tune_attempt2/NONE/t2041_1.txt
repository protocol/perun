First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 59,497 (control) + 58,111 (treatment) = 117,608. Expected per arm is 58,804. The treatment arm has 693 fewer users than expected. A chi-square calculation: $\chi^2 = \frac{(59497-58804)^2}{58804} + \frac{(58111-58804)^2}{58804} \approx 8.16 + 8.16 = 16.32$. With 1 degree of freedom, a value > 3.84 indicates $p < 0.05$. This significant deviation suggests broken randomization or logging. **Flag SRM.**

Next, I check for Denominator errors (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate." Users were randomized by user ID. The counts provided for the metric calculation appear to be Orders / Users (e.g., $3570/59497 \approx 6.0\%$). Since the denominator (users) matches the randomization unit, there is no DENOM error.

I check for Power (POWER). The readout states a baseline of 6.0% and target lift of 5%. The actual sample sizes (~58k per arm) are generally sufficient to detect a 5% relative lift on a 6% baseline with 80% power (typically requiring ~25k-30k per arm depending on variance). The test achieved significance ($p=0.0107$), so it was not underpowered for the observed effect. No POWER error.

I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON).
Desktop Control Rate: $2709/29662 \approx 9.13\%$.
Desktop Treatment Rate: $2804/28971 \approx 9.68\%$. (Lift positive)
Mobile Control Rate: $861/29835 \approx 2.89\%$.
Mobile Treatment Rate: $891/29140 \approx 3.06\%$. (Lift positive)
Both segments show a positive lift, consistent with the pooled result. No SIMPSON error.

I check for Peeking (PEEK). The test ran from May 18 to June 2. This is exactly 15 days inclusive (or 14 full days), matching the "planned duration 14 days" with a final readout on the end date. There is no evidence in the numbers that it was stopped early before the planned duration elapsed. No PEEK error.

I check for Novelty (NOVELTY). The daily lift drops steadily from 12.1% on Day 1 to 2.1% on Day 15. The reported aggregate lift is 6.0%, but the trend shows a sharp decay, suggesting the effect may be temporary (novelty) rather than persistent. However, the team projects this lift for twelve months. While the fading trend strongly suggests a novelty effect, the definition requires the lift to be *fading* (which it is) and *projected as lasting* (which it is). The numbers clearly show the fade. **Flag NOVELTY.**

ERRORS: SRM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
