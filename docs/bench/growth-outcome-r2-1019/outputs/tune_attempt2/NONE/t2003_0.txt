First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 48,249 (control) + 48,289 (treatment) = 96,538. The expected split is 48,269 per arm. The observed difference is only 40 users. This is well within the range of random chance for this sample size, so SRM is not present.

Next, I check the Denominator (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate," and the arms report users and orders. The calculation implies orders divided by users (e.g., 1,930 / 48,249 ≈ 4.0%). Since users were the randomized unit, the denominator matches the randomization unit. No DENOM error.

I check for Power (POWER). The baseline is 4.0%, target lift 10% (to 4.4%). With ~48k users per arm, the standard error for the difference in proportions is roughly $\sqrt{\frac{0.04(0.96)}{48000} + \frac{0.04(0.96)}{48000}} \approx 0.00126$. The detectable effect at 80% power and alpha 0.05 is roughly $1.96 + 0.84 \times SE \approx 0.0028$ (absolute), or ~7% relative lift. The study is adequately powered to detect a 10% lift. The fact that the result is not significant (p=0.17) is due to the observed lift being only 4.4%, not because the sample was too small to detect the *target* lift. No POWER error.
