First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 18,840 (control) + 18,084 (treatment) = 36,924. Expected per arm is 18,462. The difference is 378 users. Using a quick chi-square approximation: $\chi^2 \approx \frac{(378)^2}{18462} + \frac{(-378)^2}{18462} \approx 15.5$. With 1 degree of freedom, a value > 3.84 indicates significant mismatch ($p < 0.05$). The split is statistically significantly different from 50/50, indicating broken randomization or logging. **SRM flagged.**

Next, I check the denominator. The metric is "order conversion rate." Users were randomized by user ID. The counts provided are users and orders. The reported rates (e.g., control $942/18840 \approx 5.0\%$? Wait, $942/18840 = 0.05$. But the readout says 3.664%. Let's re-calculate: $942 / 25711$ (sessions) $\approx 0.0366$. The reported rate uses *sessions* as the denominator, not *users*. Since randomization was by user, using sessions violates the unit of analysis, risking inflated significance due to correlated sessions per user. **DENOM flagged.**
