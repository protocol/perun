**SRM Check**: Total users = 24,105 (Control) + 23,213 (Treatment) = 47,318. Expected per arm = 23,659.
Difference = 24,105 - 23,659 = 446.
Standard Error for proportion $p=0.5$ is $\sqrt{n \cdot p \cdot (1-p)} = \sqrt{47318 \cdot 0.25} \approx 108.8$.
Z-score = $446 / 108.8 \approx 4.1$. This exceeds the threshold of ~1.96 (95% CI), indicating a significant Sample Ratio Mismatch. Assignment or logging is likely broken.

**PEEK Check**: Planned duration was 14 days (June 5 to June 19). The readout date is June 21, and daily data is provided for 16 days. The test ran longer than planned, not shorter. No evidence of early stopping inflating false positives.

**POWER Check**: The smaller arm (Treatment, 23,213) is very close to the expected size. Given the large sample sizes (~23k per arm) and a baseline of 6%, the study is likely sufficiently powered to detect an 8% lift if it existed, though the observed effect was small. The primary issue is not underpowering but the validity of the split.

**SIMPSON Check**:
Control Rate Desktop: $1066/10902 \approx 9.78\%$
Treatment Rate Desktop: $1131/12121 \approx 9.33\%$ (Treatment worse)
Control Rate Mobile: $380/13203 \approx 2.88\%$
Treatment Rate Mobile: $305/11092 \approx 2.75\%$ (Treatment worse)
Overall Reported: Treatment (6.186%) > Control (5.999%).
The treatment performs worse in both segments but better in the aggregate. This is a clear case of Simpson's Paradox, driven by the treatment arm having a higher proportion of high-converting desktop users (likely due to the SRM).

**DENOM Check**: Randomization was by user ID. The metric is order conversion rate. The readout lists users and orders. Assuming "conversion rate" is Orders/Users, the denominator matches the randomization unit. If it were Orders/Sessions, it would be an error, but standard conversion usually implies users here. Without explicit session-based rate definition contradicting user randomization, we assume alignment, though the SRM makes the user counts unreliable.

**NOVELTY Check**: Daily lift fluctuates between 2.5% and 3.7% with no clear fading trend over the 16 days. The projection assumes the lift lasts, but the data does not show fading, so "Novelty" (fading effect) is not demonstrated by the numbers provided.

The critical errors are SRM (invalidating the experiment integrity) and SIMPSON (the aggregate result is misleading compared to segments).

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
