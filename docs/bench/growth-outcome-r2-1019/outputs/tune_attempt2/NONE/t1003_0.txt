First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 146,520 (control) + 146,792 (treatment) = 293,312. The expected split is 50%, so 146,656 per arm. The treatment deviation is $146,792 - 146,656 = 136$. The standard error for the difference is roughly $\sqrt{N \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.5} \approx \sqrt{73328} \approx 270$. A deviation of 136 is well within 2 standard deviations ($136 < 540$), so the split is statistically consistent with 50/50. No SRM.

Next, I check for Denominator errors (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate." The randomization unit is "user id." The counts provided for the metric calculation appear to be Orders / Users ($8,792 / 146,520 \approx 6.001\%$ and $9,223 / 146,792 \approx 6.283\%$). Since the denominator (users) matches the randomization unit (users), there is no DENOM error.

I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON).
Desktop Control Rate: $6,462 / 66,941 \approx 9.65\%$.
Desktop Treatment Rate: $6,779 / 67,066 \approx 10.11\%$. (Lift positive)
Mobile Control Rate: $2,330 / 79,579 \approx 2.93\%$.
Mobile Treatment Rate: $2,444 / 79,726 \approx 3.07\%$. (Lift positive)
Since the lift is positive in both segments and positive in the aggregate, the direction is consistent. No SIMPSON error.

I check for Peeking (PEEK). The planned duration was 14 days. The dates are May 28 to June 13. Counting inclusive: May 28–31 (4 days) + June 1–13 (13 days) = 17 days? Wait, May has 31 days. May 28, 29, 30, 31 is 4 days. June 1 to 13 is 13 days. Total 17 days. However, the daily lift data lists 16 days of data. The readout says "final readout 2026-06-13". If started May 28, day 14 is June 10. The test ran for 16 days according to the daily data list. While it ran *longer* than planned, PEEK specifically refers to stopping *early* or calling significance before the duration is met based on interim looks. The text does not state they stopped early; they have 16 days of data vs 14 planned. This is not a clear PEEK violation based on the numbers alone (running long doesn't inflate false positives in the same way stopping early does).

I check for Power (POWER). The sample size (~146k per arm) is very large for a 6% baseline. With 80% power to detect 5% lift, the required sample is much smaller than 146k. The test is not underpowered.

I check for Novelty (NOVELTY). The daily lift shows: 5.2, 5.3, 5.4... dropping to 3.4, 3.9 in the final days (days 15-16). There is a visible downward trend in the last few days compared to the start. However, the projection applies the *overall* reported lift (4.7%) to the next twelve months. If the effect is decaying (novelty wearing off), projecting the average lift forward is an error. The drop from ~5.4% to ~3.4% suggests fading. The team projects the current average as lasting. This fits the NOVELTY definition: "lift is fading over the test but is projected as lasting."

ERRORS: NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
