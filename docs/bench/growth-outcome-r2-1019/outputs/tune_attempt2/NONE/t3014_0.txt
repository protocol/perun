First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 176,974 (control) + 177,400 (treatment) = 354,374. The expected split is 50%, so 177,187 per arm. The difference is only 213 users, which is well within the bounds of random chance for this sample size. No SRM.

Next, I check for Denominator errors (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate," and the counts provided are orders divided by users (e.g., 7,079 / 176,974 ≈ 4.0%). Since users were the randomized unit, the denominator is correct. No DENOM.

I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON).
Desktop lift: Control 5,035/77,203 ≈ 6.52%; Treatment 5,352/77,389 ≈ 6.92%. Lift is positive.
Mobile lift: Control 2,044/99,771 ≈ 2.05%; Treatment 2,172/100,011 ≈ 2.17%. Lift is positive.
Since both segments show a positive lift consistent with the pooled result, there is no reversal. No SIMPSON.

I check for Power (POWER). The sample sizes (~177k per arm) are massive compared to the baseline (4%) and target lift (5%). A quick mental estimate confirms this is vastly overpowered, not underpowered. No POWER issue.

I check for Peeking (PEEK). The test started March 5 and ended April 3. This is exactly 29-30 days, matching the "planned duration 28 days" (allowing for start/end date inclusion). There is no evidence in the numbers that the test was stopped early based on interim results; it ran the full course. No PEEK.

Finally, I check for Novelty (NOVELTY). The daily relative lift starts high (8.7%, 10.8%) and trends downward significantly over the 29 days, ending near 3.0%. The final reported lift is 6.0%, an average heavily skewed by the early spike. However, the team's projection applies this 6.0% lift to the "next twelve months." This ignores the clear fading trend where the effect halved by the end of the test. Projecting the initial inflated average as a permanent steady-state lift is a classic novelty error.

ERRORS: NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
