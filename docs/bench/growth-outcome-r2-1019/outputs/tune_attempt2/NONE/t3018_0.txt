First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 54,307 (control) + 54,033 (treatment) = 108,340. The expected split is 54,170 per arm. The difference is 137 users. The standard error for a 50/50 split is $\sqrt{108340 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 164.5$. The deviation is less than 1 standard error ($137 < 164.5$), so the split is consistent with random chance. No SRM.

Next, I check for PEEKing. The planned duration was 28 days (Sep 12 to Oct 10). The readout date is Oct 12, and daily data is provided for 30 days. The test ran longer than planned, not shorter. There is no evidence of early stopping inflating false positives. No PEEK.

Next, I check for POWER. The baseline is 5.0%, target lift 8%. The actual sample size (~54k per arm) is substantial. While the observed lift (2.0%) was not significant (p=0.46), the study was designed to detect an 8% lift. The failure to find significance is due to the effect size being smaller than the target, not necessarily an underpowered design relative to the *planned* parameters. However, checking the segment data reveals a more critical issue.

I check for SIMPSON's paradox by calculating conversion rates per device.
Desktop Control: $1929 / 26683 \approx 7.23\%$.
Desktop Treatment: $2082 / 29672 \approx 7.02\%$. (Treatment is lower).
Mobile Control: $786 / 27624 \approx 2.85\%$.
Mobile Treatment: $672 / 24361 \approx 2.76\%$. (Treatment is lower).
In both segments, the treatment conversion rate is lower than the control. Yet, the pooled reported rates show Treatment (5.097%) higher than Control (4.999%). This reversal of direction when aggregating data is the definition of Simpson's Paradox. This occurred because the treatment group had a much higher proportion of high-converting desktop users (29,672 vs 26,683) compared to the control, skewing the overall average upward despite performing worse in both individual segments.

Regarding DENOM: The metric is order conversion rate. The randomization unit is user ID. The readout lists users and orders. If "conversion rate" is orders/users, the denominator matches the randomization unit. If it is orders/sessions, it would be a mismatch. Given the counts (e.g., 2715/54307 ≈ 5.0%), the reported rate matches the orders/users calculation. No DENOM error.

NOVELTY: The daily lift fluctuates between 1.3% and 2.5% but shows no clear fading trend over the 30 days; it remains stable around 2%. No NOVELTY error.

The critical error is SIMPSON. Aggregating the data hides that the treatment is actually performing worse in both desktop and mobile segments. Rolling this out based on the pooled number would be a mistake.

ERRORS: SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
