First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 46,101 (control) + 45,871 (treatment) = 91,972. The expected split is 45,986 per arm. The difference is 115 users. The standard error for a 50/50 split is $\sqrt{N \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx \sqrt{23000} \approx 151$. The deviation (115) is less than 2 standard deviations ($2 \times 151 = 302$), so the split is statistically consistent with 50/50. No SRM.

Next, I check for PEEKing. The dates are 2026-09-12 to 2026-09-27. This is exactly 15 days inclusive, or 14 days duration depending on interpretation, but the readout says "planned duration 14 days" and lists 15 data points (Day 1 to Day 15). If the test ran for 15 days against a 14-day plan, it might be a minor over-run, but "PEEK" usually implies stopping *early* upon seeing significance. Here, the result is not significant ($p = 0.0907 > 0.05$). The team did not stop early to claim a win; they ran the full duration (or slightly longer) and still failed to reach significance. Thus, PEEK is not indicated by the numbers.

I check POWER. The baseline is 6.0%, target lift 5%. The sample size per arm is ~46k. Using a standard approximation, detecting a 5% relative lift (from 6.0% to 6.3%) with 80% power and alpha 0.05 requires roughly $N \approx 100,000$ per arm (variance $\approx 0.06$, effect size $\approx 0.003$). With only ~46k per arm, the test is likely underpowered to detect the *target* 5% lift if it existed, though the observed lift is 4.5%. However, the error code POWER applies if the arm is "too small to detect the target lift". Given the large gap between 46k and the ~100k typically needed for small conversion lifts, this fits the definition of underpowered for the stated design goals.

I check SIMPSON.
Desktop CR: Control $1693/16371 \approx 10.34\%$, Treatment $2023/20790 \approx 9.73\%$. Lift is negative.
Mobile CR: Control $1073/29730 \approx 3.61\%$, Treatment $852/25081 \approx 3.40\%$. Lift is negative.
Both segments show negative lift, yet the pooled result shows positive lift (6.0% vs 6.268%). This is a classic reversal. The treatment group has a much higher proportion of Desktop users (high baseline conversion) than the control group (20790/45871 ≈ 45% vs 16371/46101 ≈ 35%). The aggregation masks the negative performance in both segments. This is SIMPSON.

I check DENOM. Randomized by user id, metric is order conversion (orders/users). Denominator matches randomization unit. No error.

I check NOVELTY. The daily lift fluctuates (3.6% to 5.4%) but shows no clear fading trend over the 15 days. The projection assumes lasting lift, but the data doesn't explicitly show fading to flag NOVELTY based *only* on the numbers provided.

The critical errors are SIMPSON (aggregated lift is positive while all segments are negative) and POWER (sample size insufficient for target). SIMPSON makes the reported positive lift entirely misleading.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, POWER
DECISION: HOLD
