First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total user count is 43,721 (control) + 44,777 (treatment) = 88,498. The expected split is 50%, so 44,249 per arm. The treatment arm has 528 more users than expected. The standard deviation for a binomial distribution here is $\sqrt{88498 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 148.7$. The difference is $528 / 148.7 \approx 3.55$ standard deviations. This exceeds the typical threshold of 2 (or even 3) sigma, indicating a significant deviation from the 50/50 plan. SRM is present.

Next, I check for PEEKing. The planned duration was 14 days, but the readout date is June 19, starting June 9. This is exactly 10 days of data. Stopping at day 10 instead of the planned day 14 constitutes early stopping without mentioned correction, inflating false positive risk. PEEK is present.

I check for POWER. The smaller arm (control) has 43,721 users. With a 4% baseline and 10% target lift, standard calculators usually require ~30k-40k users per arm for 80% power at alpha 0.05. Since 43,721 > required sample size, the test is not underpowered based on these rough estimates. No POWER error.

I check for SIMPSON's paradox by calculating segment conversion rates:
Desktop Control: $1,193 / 18,983 \approx 6.28\%$. Desktop Treatment: $1,309 / 19,441 \approx 6.73\%$. (Lift positive)
Mobile Control: $556 / 24,738 \approx 2.25\%$. Mobile Treatment: $610 / 25,336 \approx 2.41\%$. (Lift positive)
Both segments show positive lift, matching the pooled result. No SIMPSON error.

I check DENOM. The metric is order conversion rate. The denominator used in calculations above was users (randomized unit). The readout lists users and orders. If the rate was sessions, it would be an error, but "order conversion rate" typically implies orders/users or orders/sessions. However, since randomization is by user, using sessions as a denominator can be risky if session counts differ significantly. Sessions per user: Control $55,249/43,721 \approx 1.26$; Treatment $56,909/44,777 \approx 1.27$. These are very close. Assuming the reported rate uses users as the denominator (standard for user-randomized tests), there is no obvious DENOM error visible from the counts alone without explicit formula confirmation, but the SRM and PEEK are definitive.

I check NOVELTY. The daily lift fluctuates (7.8, 7.0, 8.2, 5.8, etc.) but shows no clear fading trend; the last few days are 6.3, 8.4. There is no evidence of fading.

The projection to 12 months assumes the lift lasts, but without fading data, NOVELTY isn't strictly provable from the numbers provided, though the PEEK and SRM are critical failures.
