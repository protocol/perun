**SIMPSON Check**: Let's calculate conversion rates (Orders/Users) by segment.
Desktop Control: $757 / 15,846 \approx 4.78\%$. Desktop Treatment: $801 / 15,928 \approx 5.03\%$. (Lift positive).
Mobile Control: $360 / 21,386 \approx 1.68\%$. Mobile Treatment: $381 / 21,496 \approx 1.77\%$. (Lift positive).
Overall Control: $1,117 / 37,232 \approx 3.00\%$. Overall Treatment: $1,182 / 37,424 \approx 3.16\%$.
Wait, the reported overall rates in the text are 2.188% and 2.738%.
Let's re-calculate using the text's reported overall rates vs my sum.
My sum: Control $1117/37232 = 3.00\%$. Text says $2.188\%$.
Why the discrepancy? The text defines the metric as "order conversion rate" but the denominator used for the reported % might be sessions, not users.
Control Sessions: 51,043. Orders: 1,117. Rate = $1,117 / 51,043 = 2.188\%$. Matches text.
Treatment Sessions: 43,167. Orders: 1,182. Rate = $1,182 / 43,167 = 2.738\%$. Matches text.
The randomization unit is **User ID**. The metric denominator is **Sessions**.
Since users can have multiple sessions, and the treatment arm has significantly fewer sessions per user (43,167/37,424 ≈ 1.15) than control (51,043/37,232 ≈ 1.37), the denominator is not the randomized unit. This biases the rate if the treatment affects session frequency. This is a DENOM error.

Does SIMPSON apply?
Desktop Lift: $(5.03-4.78)/4.78 \approx 5.2\%$.
Mobile Lift: $(1.77-1.68)/1.68 \approx 5.3\%$.
Overall Lift (using users): $(3.16-3.00)/3.00 \approx 5.3\%$.
The pooled result (positive) matches the segments (positive). The massive 25% lift reported is purely due to the denominator mismatch (sessions), not a reversal of direction between segments. So, no SIMPSON paradox, just a wrong denominator.

**DENOM Confirmation**: Randomized by User, Metric = Orders / Sessions. Since session counts differ drastically between arms (likely an effect of the treatment), the ratio is biased.
