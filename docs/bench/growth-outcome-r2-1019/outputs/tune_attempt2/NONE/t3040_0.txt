**SRM Check**: Total users = 13,336 + 13,512 = 26,848. Expected per arm = 13,424.
Observed difference = |13,512 - 13,336| = 176.
Standard Error (SE) for binomial split ≈ $\sqrt{N \times 0.5 \times 0.5} = \sqrt{26848 \times 0.25} \approx 82$.
Z-score = $176 / 82 \approx 2.15$. This exceeds the typical threshold of 2 (approx. p < 0.05), indicating the split is statistically significantly different from 50/50. Assignment or logging is likely broken. **Flag SRM.**

**PEEK Check**: Planned duration was 21 days. The readout date is Oct 16, start date Oct 1. Duration = 15 days. The test was stopped 6 days early. While early stopping often inflates false positives, the reported p-value is 0.6416 (not significant). The error definition states PEEK inflates false positives (leading to erroneous rollouts). Since the result is not significant, the team isn't rolling out based on a false positive caused by peeking; they are rolling out despite non-significance based on a projection. However, the act of stopping early violates the design. But strictly, does the *numbers* show the error of "inflated false positive leading to bad decision"? The decision is bad, but the p-value admits no significance. Let's look closer at the definition: "the test was stopped... which inflates false positives." The condition of early stopping is met (15 vs 21 days). Does this constitute the error category? Yes, the protocol was violated.

**POWER Check**: Baseline 8%, target 10% lift (to 8.8%). Alpha 0.05, Power 80%.
Required sample size per arm (approx) for detecting 10% relative lift on 8% base:
Using standard approximation $n \approx 16 \times (p_1(1-p_1) + p_2(1-p_2)) / (p_1-p_2)^2$.
$p_1=0.08, p_2=0.088$. Diff = 0.008.
$n \approx 16 \times (0.0736 + 0.0802) / 0.000064 \approx 16 \times 0.1538 / 0.000064 \approx 38,450$ per arm.
Current n ≈ 13,400 per arm. The study is severely underpowered to detect the target lift. **Flag POWER.**

**SIMPSON Check**:

**Summary**:
SRM is present (Z > 2).
POWER is present (N too small for target).
SIMPSON is present (reversal in segments).
PEEK is present (stopped early), though the primary driver of the bad decision here is the misinterpretation of non-significant data and the paradox. However, the prompt asks to flag if the numbers show the error. The numbers show 15 days vs 21 planned.

The presence of SRM invalidates the randomization assumption, making any causal inference unreliable. Simpson's paradox indicates a confounding variable (device) distribution shift or interaction that makes the pooled number misleading. Power issues mean the non-significant result doesn't prove equivalence, but the team is projecting a lift anyway.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK, POWER, SIMPSON
