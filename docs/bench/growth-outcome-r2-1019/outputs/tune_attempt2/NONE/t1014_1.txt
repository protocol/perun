First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 38,106 (control) + 38,230 (treatment) = 76,336. The expected split is 50%, so 38,168 per arm. The difference is only 62 users, which is well within statistical noise for this sample size. No SRM.

Next, I check the Denominator (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate." The readout defines arms by "users" and reports orders per user count (e.g., 2,286 / 38,106). Since users were randomized by user ID and the denominator is users, the unit of analysis matches the unit of randomization. No DENOM error.

I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON).
Control Desktop Rate: 1,581 / 15,169 ≈ 10.42%
Treatment Desktop Rate: 1,679 / 15,218 ≈ 11.03% (Lift present)
Control Mobile Rate: 705 / 22,937 ≈ 3.07%
Treatment Mobile Rate: 749 / 23,012 ≈ 3.25% (Lift present)
Both segments show a positive lift, matching the pooled result. No SIMPSON.

I check Power (POWER). The target was a 10% lift on a 6% baseline. The observed lift is ~5.9%. While the test failed to detect the *target* lift, the question asks if the arm is too small to detect the lift *at the stated alpha and power*. The test actually found a significant result (p=0.0434) for a smaller lift. The sample size was sufficient to find *a* difference, even if not the specific target. Usually, POWER flags refer to inability to detect the effect size of interest leading to a false negative, but here we have a positive. However, strictly speaking, the design targeted 10%. Did they have power for 5.9%? Likely not 80%. But is this an "error" in the readout analysis or just a design outcome? The prompt asks to flag if the arm is "too small to detect the target lift". They didn't detect the target lift (10%), they detected 5.9%. This is a failure to meet the design goal, but often "underpowered" implies we can't trust the negative. Here the result is positive. I will not flag POWER as the primary issue because the test *did* yield a significant result, just not for the pre-specified effect size. The more glaring error lies elsewhere.

I check PEEK. The planned duration was 21 days. The readout date is Feb 25, start Feb 2. That is 23 days. They ran *longer* than planned, not shorter. No PEEK.

I check NOVELTY. The daily lift starts at 13.4% and decays steadily to 1.8% by day 23. The trend is clearly fading. However, the team's projection applies the *reported* aggregate lift (5.9%) to the next twelve months. This ignores the decay trend visible in the daily data, assuming the initial/average lift is permanent. This is a classic Novelty effect error where a transient bump is projected as steady state.

ERRORS: NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
