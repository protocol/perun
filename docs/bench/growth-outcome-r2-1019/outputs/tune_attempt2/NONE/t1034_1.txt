**SRM Check**: Total users = 43,273 (Control) + 41,845 (Treatment) = 85,118. Expected per arm = 42,559. Difference = 714 users. Standard error for proportion $p=0.5$ is $\sqrt{0.5 \times 0.5 / 85118} \approx 0.0017$. The observed treatment proportion is $41845/85118 \approx 0.4916$. The Z-score is $|0.5 - 0.4916| / 0.0017 \approx 4.9$. A Z-score > 1.96 indicates a significant deviation from the 50/50 split. **SRM flagged.**

**PEEK Check**: Planned duration was 21 days. The readout date (Aug 10) is exactly 21 days after the start (July 20). The daily data covers day 1 to 21. There is no evidence the test was stopped early; it ran the full planned duration. **PEEK not flagged.**

**POWER Check**: The test detected a 4.5% lift with a p-value of 0.0544, very close to the 0.05 threshold. While the target was 8%, the sample size was sufficient to detect a smaller effect with near-significance. The issue here is the result isn't significant, not necessarily that the arm was too small to *run* the analysis, though it was underpowered for the *target* 8% if the true effect is lower. However, SRM is a more fundamental breakdown. Strictly speaking, "underpowered" usually refers to pre-test planning or failing to detect an existing large effect. Here we have a non-significant result. But SRM invalidates the randomization assumption required for power calculations anyway. I will focus on the clear errors.

**SIMPSON Check**:
Control Desktop Rate: $2300/18780 \approx 12.25\%$

Control Mobile Rate: $1162/24493 \approx 4.74\%$
Treatment Mobile Rate: $1174/23684 \approx 4.96\%$ (Lift positive)
Both segments show positive lift, consistent with the pooled positive lift. No reversal. **SIMPSON not flagged.**

**DENOM Check**: Randomization was by user id. The metric is order conversion rate. The counts provided are "users" and "orders". The rates are calculated as Orders/Users. The denominator matches the randomization unit. **DENOM not flagged.**

**NOVELTY Check**: Daily lift starts at 9.0% and trends downward to 0.7% by day 21. The team projects this reported average lift (4.5%) for the next twelve months. This ignores the clear fading trend (novelty effect), assuming the initial higher lift or the current average will persist indefinitely despite the drop to near-zero. **NOVELTY flagged.**

ERRORS: SRM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
