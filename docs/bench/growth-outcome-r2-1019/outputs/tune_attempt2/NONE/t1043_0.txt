First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total user count is 18,148 (control) + 18,012 (treatment) = 36,160. The expected split is 18,080 per arm. The difference is only 68 users. A standard deviation check ($\sqrt{36160 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 95$) shows this deviation is less than 1 standard deviation, so SRM is not present.

Next, I check the Denominator (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate," and the randomized unit is "user id." The reported rates align with users as the denominator: Control $726 / 18,148 \approx 4.00\%$? Wait, the readout says 3.094%. Let's re-calculate: $726 / 23,461$ (sessions) $\approx 3.094\%$. The treatment rate $767 / 19,464 \approx 3.941\%$. The reported percentages use **sessions** as the denominator, but users were randomized. This violates the unit of randomization, introducing bias if session counts differ per user. This is a DENOM error.

I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON).
Desktop Control Rate (users): $505 / 7,524 \approx 6.71\%$.
Desktop Treatment Rate (users): $534 / 7,467 \approx 7.15\%$. (Lift positive)
Mobile Control Rate (users): $221 / 10,624 \approx 2.08\%$.
Mobile Treatment Rate (users): $233 / 10,545 \approx 2.21\%$. (Lift positive)
Since the lift is positive in both segments, there is no reversal. No SIMPSON error.

I check for Novelty (NOVELTY). The daily lift starts at 64.9% and steadily declines to 9.3% by day 15. The team projects the current reported aggregate lift (27.4%) for the next twelve months. Ignoring the clear fading trend and projecting the inflated early lift constitutes a NOVELTY error.

I check for Peeking (PEEK). The test ran from July 11 to July 26, which is 15 days. The planned duration was 14 days. Since the test ran slightly longer than planned, not shorter, there is no evidence of early stopping based on the dates provided.

Finally, Power (POWER). The sample size (~18k per arm) is sufficient to detect a 10% lift on a 4% baseline with 80% power (required n is roughly 3,000 per arm for this effect size). The test is not underpowered.

The identified errors are DENOM (wrong denominator used for the rate) and NOVELTY (ignoring fading lift in projection).

ERRORS: DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
