Several critical analysis errors are evident in the provided readout numbers.

First, there is a clear **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch). While the total user counts (54,863 vs 54,733) appear balanced, the breakdown by device shows a severe imbalance.
Desktop: 25,429 (Control) vs 33,609 (Treatment).
Mobile: 29,434 (Control) vs 21,124 (Treatment).
Total users match the sum of segments, meaning the randomization itself was skewed by device type or logging failed to attribute device types correctly across arms. A 50/50 split by user ID should not result in such drastic differences in segment sizes unless the assignment mechanism is broken.

Second, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is present. The aggregate data shows the Treatment winning significantly (7.23% vs 5.59%). However, looking at the segments:
Desktop Control Rate: 3,146 / 25,429 ≈ 12.37%
Desktop Treatment Rate: 3,826 / 33,609 ≈ 11.38%
Mobile Control Rate: 1,243 / 29,434 ≈ 4.22%
Mobile Treatment Rate: 821 / 21,124 ≈ 3.89%
In both Desktop and Mobile segments individually, the Control group has a higher conversion rate than the Treatment group. Yet, the pooled result claims Treatment is better. This reversal confirms Simpson's Paradox, driven by the SRM where the Treatment arm is heavily weighted toward the higher-converting Desktop segment.

Third, **PEEK** is indicated. The planned duration was 14 days, but the final readout occurred on Nov 6th, only 6 days after the Oct 31st start date. Stopping early based on significant results inflates the false positive rate.

Fourth, **NOVELTY** cannot be confirmed as the lift is not fading; the daily lift numbers (29.1 to 32.3) are stable or increasing. **POWER** is not an issue as the sample sizes are large. **DENOM** is not explicitly violated as the metric (orders/users) aligns with the randomized unit (user), though the SRM complicates this.

The combination of SRM and Simpson's Paradox makes the reported lift invalid; the treatment actually performs worse in every segment.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
