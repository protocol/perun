**SRM Check**: Total users = 159,640. Expected per arm = 79,820. Observed: 79,761 (Control) and 79,879 (Treatment). The difference is negligible (~0.07%), well within chance variation for this sample size. No SRM.

**PEEK Check**: Planned duration was 14 days. The readout date is 2026-01-30, exactly 8 days after the start (2026-01-22). The test was stopped early (8 vs 14 days). Stopping before the planned duration based on interim results inflates false positive rates. This is a clear PEEK error.

**POWER Check**: Baseline 3.0%, target lift 10% (to 3.3%). With ~80k users per arm, standard power calculations suggest this sample is sufficient to detect a 10% lift at 80% power/0.05 alpha. The observed lift is smaller (4.6%) and non-significant (p=0.1125), but the design itself wasn't necessarily underpowered for the *target* lift. The issue is the result, not the pre-test power calculation relative to the target.

**SIMPSON Check**:
Control Rate Desktop: 1,422 / 28,592 ≈ 4.97%
Treatment Rate Desktop: 1,749 / 37,431 ≈ 4.67% (Lift is negative)
Control Rate Mobile: 971 / 51,169 ≈ 1.90%
Treatment Rate Mobile: 757 / 42,448 ≈ 1.78% (Lift is negative)
Both segments show a negative lift (Treatment < Control), yet the pooled data shows a positive lift (3.137% > 3.0%). This is a classic reversal: Simpson's Paradox. The treatment arm has a higher proportion of Desktop users (who convert higher) than the control arm, skewing the aggregate.

**DENOM Check**: Randomized by user ID. Metric is order conversion rate (Orders/Users). Denominator matches randomization unit. No DENOM error.

**NOVELTY Check**: Daily lift values (4.1 to 4.9%) fluctuate but do not show a clear fading trend over the 8 days. The projection assumes longevity, but the data doesn't explicitly show fading yet. No clear NOVELTY error based solely on the provided numbers.

The presence of PEEK (early stopping) and SIMPSON (paradoxical reversal where aggregates contradict segments) invalidates the current conclusion. The negative lift in both device segments suggests the popup may actually be harmful, despite the misleading aggregate positive number caused by imbalance in device mix.

ERRORS: PEEK, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
