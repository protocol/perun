**SRM Check**: The total users are 83,615 (Control) and 83,197 (Treatment). Total N = 166,812. Expected per arm = 83,406.
Difference = 209 users.
Standard Error for proportion $p=0.5$ is $\sqrt{N \cdot p \cdot (1-p)} = \sqrt{166812 \cdot 0.25} \approx 204$.
The deviation is roughly 1 standard error ($209/204 \approx 1.02$). This is well within the range of chance (typically < 1.96 SE for 95% confidence). **No SRM.**

**PEEK Check**: Planned duration was 14 days. The readout date is 2026-10-29, and the start was 2026-10-19. This is exactly 10 days of data. The test was stopped 4 days early. Stopping before the planned duration based on looking at the results inflates the false positive rate. **Flag PEEK.**

**POWER Check**: We need to verify if the sample size supports detecting a 5% lift on a 5% baseline.
Target absolute lift = $0.05 \times 0.05 = 0.0025$.
Approximate sample size per arm needed $n \approx \frac{16 \cdot p(1-p)}{\Delta^2}$.
$n \approx \frac{16 \cdot 0.05 \cdot 0.95}{0.0025^2} = \frac{0.76}{0.00000625} = 121,600$ users per arm.
Actual users per arm are ~83,000.
$83,000 < 121,600$. The study is underpowered to detect the target lift defined in the design. **Flag POWER.**

**SIMPSON Check**:
Desktop Control Rate: $2788/34921 \approx 8.0\%$.
Desktop Treatment Rate: $2896/34747 \approx 8.3\%$. (Lift positive)
Mobile Control Rate: $1393/48694 \approx 2.9\%$.
Mobile Treatment Rate: $1446/48450 \approx 3.0\%$. (Lift positive)
Both segments show a positive lift; the direction does not reverse. **No SIMPSON.**

**DENOM Check**: Randomization was by user ID. The metric is "order conversion rate." The counts provided for the metric calculation (Orders/Users) align with the randomized unit (users). While session counts differ, the primary metric uses users as the denominator implicitly or explicitly matches the randomization unit. **No DENOM.**

**NOVELTY Check**: The daily lift figures (16.0, 10.5, ... 15.6) fluctuate but do not show a consistent fading trend (e.g., starting high and dropping to near zero). The projection assumes the current average holds, but the data does not explicitly demonstrate a "fading" pattern that contradicts the projection yet. **No NOVELTY.**

ERRORS: PEEK, POWER
DECISION: HOLD
