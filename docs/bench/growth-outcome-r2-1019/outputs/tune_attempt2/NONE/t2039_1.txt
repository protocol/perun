**SRM Check**: Total users = 29,693 (Control) + 29,761 (Treatment) = 59,454. Expected per arm = 29,727.
Difference = |29,693 - 29,727| = 34.
Standard Deviation ≈ $\sqrt{59454 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 122$.
The difference (34) is well within 2 standard deviations (~244). The split is statistically consistent with 50/50. No SRM.

**PEEK Check**: Planned duration was 21 days. The readout date is 2026-10-13, which is 22 days after the start (Sept 21 to Oct 13 inclusive). Daily data is provided for 22 days. The test ran for the full planned duration (plus one day). There is no evidence of early stopping based on the dates provided. No PEEK.

**POWER Check**: The test detected a significant result (p=0.0001) with ~29k users per arm. Since the result is significant and the sample size exceeded the requirement to find the effect (as evidenced by finding it), the test was not underpowered for the observed effect. No POWER error evident from the outcome.

**SIMPSON Check**:
Control Rate Desktop: $1002/11512 \approx 8.70\%$

Control Rate Mobile: $482/18181 \approx 2.65\%$
Treatment Rate Mobile: $511/18223 \approx 2.80\%$ (Lift positive)
Both segments show a positive lift, matching the pooled result. No reversal. No SIMPSON.

**DENOM Check**: Randomization unit is "user id". The metric is "order conversion rate".
Reported Control Rate: $1484 / 29693 \approx 5.00\%$? Wait, the readout says 3.885%.
Let's check the denominator used for the reported rate: $1484 / 38194 \approx 3.885\%$.
The reported metric divides orders by **sessions**, but users were randomized by **user id**. Sessions are not the randomized unit; a user can have multiple sessions, violating the independence assumption if analyzed at the session level without clustering, or simply misaligning the denominator with the randomization unit. This is a clear DENOM error.

**NOVELTY Check**: Daily lift drops from 27.0% (Day 1) to 5.9% (Day 22). This is a sharp decay. However, the projection applies the *reported* overall lift (15.1%) to the next 12 months. While the trend suggests novelty wear-off, the error definition requires the lift to be "fading... but projected as lasting." The team is projecting the *average* lift, not the initial high lift, but the average itself is inflated by the early novelty days which will not repeat. The severe fade (27% -> 6%) suggests the 15.1% average is not sustainable. This fits NOVELTY.

ERRORS: DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
