Several analysis errors are evident in the provided readout based on the raw numbers.

First, there is a clear **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch). The planned allocation was 50/50. The total user count is 97,253 (control) + 99,997 (treatment) = 197,250. The expected count per arm is 98,625. The treatment arm has 1,372 more users than expected. Using a standard chi-square check: $\chi^2 = \frac{(97253-98625)^2}{98625} + \frac{(99997-98625)^2}{98625} \approx 19.1 + 19.1 = 38.2$. With 1 degree of freedom, a value > 3.84 indicates significance at p<0.05. A value of 38.2 is massive, indicating the randomization or logging is broken.

Second, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is present. The aggregated data shows the treatment winning (3.182% vs 2.999%). However, looking at the segments:
*   Desktop Control Rate: $1,929 / 42,412 \approx 4.55\%$
*   Desktop Treatment Rate: $2,522 / 60,229 \approx 4.19\%$ (Treatment loses)
*   Mobile Control Rate: $988 / 54,841 \approx 1.80\%$
*   Mobile Treatment Rate: $660 / 39,768 \approx 1.66\%$ (Treatment loses)
The treatment performs worse in both device segments individually but appears better when pooled. This reversal confirms Simpson's Paradox, likely driven by the SRM shifting the mix toward the higher-converting desktop group in the treatment arm.

Third, **PEEK** is indicated. The test planned for 14 days but the readout is dated August 4th, only 6 days after the July 29th start. Stopping early because results look significant inflates the false positive rate.

Finally, **NOVELTY** is a concern. The daily lift shows a peak at day 3 (6.8%) followed by a consistent decline to 5.4% by day 6. Projecting the current average lift over twelve months ignores this fading trend, suggesting a novelty effect rather than a permanent behavioral change.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
