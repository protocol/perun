**SRM Check**: Total users = 27,946 + 28,008 = 55,954. Expected per arm = 27,977. Observed difference is minimal (Control -31, Treatment +31). A chi-square approximation: $(31^2/27977) \times 2 \approx 0.07$. This is well within chance limits ($p \gg 0.05$). No SRM.

**PEEK Check**: Start date May 25, end date June 9. Duration = 16 days (inclusive). Planned duration = 21 days. The test was stopped 5 days early. Stopping before the planned duration without adjustment inflates false positive rates. **PEEK flagged**.

**POWER Check**: Baseline 6.0%, target lift 8% (absolute 0.48%). Target rate 6.48%. With $N \approx 28,000$ per arm, standard error for difference $\approx \sqrt{\frac{0.06(0.94)}{28000} \times 2} \approx 0.002$. Detectable lift at 80% power $\approx 2.8 \times 0.002 \approx 0.56\%$. The target (0.48%) is slightly below this, but the sample size is very close to requirements. However, the observed p-value is 0.544, indicating no significance. While potentially underpowered for the *target*, the primary error here is the early stop. Let's re-evaluate strict underpowering. Required $n$ per arm for 6% base, 8% lift, $\alpha=0.05, \beta=0.2$ is roughly 33,000. We have 28,000. It is technically underpowered for the *target* lift, but usually, POWER is flagged when the result is negative/non-significant and the sample is clearly too small to have found the effect. Given the explicit "planned duration" violation, PEEK is the stronger signal. Is it strictly underpowered? Yes, $28k < 33k$. **POWER flagged**.

The direction reverses: Treatment wins overall, but Control wins in both segments. **SIMPSON flagged**.

**DENOM Check**: Randomized by user id. Metric is order conversion rate (orders/users). Denominator matches randomization unit. No DENOM.

**NOVELTY Check**: Daily lift: 2.0 to 2.7, then drops to 1.5 by day 15. There is a clear fading trend (2.7 -> 1.5). The team projects this lift for 12 months despite the fade. **NOVELTY flagged**.
