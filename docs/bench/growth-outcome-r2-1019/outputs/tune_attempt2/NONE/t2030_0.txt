First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 128,033 (control) + 128,379 (treatment) = 256,412. The expected split is 50%, so 128,206 per arm. The difference is |128,379 - 128,206| = 173. The standard deviation for a binomial distribution here is $\sqrt{256412 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 253$. The difference is less than 1 standard deviation, so the split is consistent with random chance. No SRM.

Next, I check for Denominator errors (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate." Users were randomized by user ID. The reported rates are calculated as orders divided by users?
Control: $3,841 / 128,033 \approx 3.00\%$. Wait, the readout says 2.347%.
Let's try orders divided by sessions.
Control: $3,841 / 163,623 \approx 0.02347$ (2.347%).
Treatment: $4,057 / 147,190 \approx 0.02756$ (2.756%).
The calculation matches the reported percentages. However, the denominator used is **sessions**, while the unit of randomization is **users**. Since a single user can have multiple sessions, and the treatment might affect session frequency (treatment has fewer sessions per user: $147,190/128,379 \approx 1.15$ vs control $163,623/128,033 \approx 1.28$), using sessions as the denominator biases the result. This is a classic DENOM error.

I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON).
Desktop Control Rate: $2,663 / 53,214 \approx 5.00\%$.
Desktop Treatment Rate: $2,813 / 53,358 \approx 5.27\%$. (Lift positive)
Mobile Control Rate: $1,178 / 74,819 \approx 1.57\%$.
Mobile Treatment Rate: $1,244 / 75,021 \approx 1.66\%$. (Lift positive)
Both segments show a positive lift, consistent with the pooled direction. No Simpson's paradox.

Regarding Power (POWER): The sample size (~128k per arm) is massive compared to the baseline (3%) and target lift (5%). It is not underpowered.

Regarding Peeking (PEEK): The test ran from March 5 to March 26 (21 days), matching the planned duration. No evidence of early stopping.

Regarding Novelty (NOVELTY): The daily lift fluctuates (13.1% to 20.6%) but shows no clear fading trend toward zero over the 21 days. The projection assumes the lift lasts, but the data doesn't explicitly show fading yet.

The critical error is DENOM: analyzing session-level conversion when users were randomized.
