First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 74,697 (control) + 75,087 (treatment) = 149,784. The expected split is 50%, so 74,892 per arm. The treatment has 195 more users than expected. The standard error for the difference in proportions is roughly $\sqrt{N \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.5} \approx \sqrt{37446} \approx 193$. The deviation (195) is approximately 1 standard deviation away from the mean. This is well within the range of chance (typically we look for >3 or 4 standard deviations, or a p-value < 0.001). Thus, SRM is not flagged.

Next, I check for PEEK. The planned duration was 21 days, but the readout date is July 16, starting July 7. This is only 9 days of data. Stopping the test early (less than half the planned duration) because the results look significant is a classic "peeking" error that inflates false positives. The daily lift data is provided day-by-day, suggesting monitoring occurred. This flags PEEK.
