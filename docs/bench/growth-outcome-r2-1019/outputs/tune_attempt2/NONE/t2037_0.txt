**SRM Check**: Total users = 36,822 (Control) + 36,568 (Treatment) = 73,390. Expected per arm = 36,695.
Difference = 36,822 - 36,695 = 127.
Standard Error (SE) for binomial split $\approx \sqrt{N \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.5} = \sqrt{18,347.5} \approx 135$.
The difference (127) is less than 2 SEs (270). The split is within chance expectations. No SRM.

**PEEK Check**: Planned duration was 21 days. The readout covers dates from Oct 31 to Nov 22.
Oct 31 to Nov 22 inclusive is exactly 23 days? Let's count: Oct (1 day) + Nov (22 days) = 23 days of data collection? Or did it end on the 22nd?
Start: Oct 31. End: Nov 22.
Days: Oct 31 (1), Nov 1-22 (22). Total = 23 days.
Wait, the daily lift lists 22 data points ("day 1 to day 22"). The planned duration was 21 days. The test ran for 22 days.
Running *longer* than planned does not inflate false positives in the same way stopping early does. PEEK specifically refers to stopping *before* the planned duration or peeking repeatedly without correction. Since the test exceeded the planned 21 days (22 days of data), this is not a premature stop. No PEEK.

**POWER Check**: The test reported a p-value of 0.4179, meaning it was not statistically significant.
Did the sample size allow detecting the target lift?
Target lift = 8% relative on 4.0% baseline = 0.32% absolute lift (from 4.0% to 4.32%).
Approximate sample size per arm needed for 80% power, alpha 0.05, baseline 0.04, detect 0.0432:
$N \approx 16 \cdot \bar{p}(1-\bar{p}) / \delta^2$. $\bar{p} \approx 0.0416$, $\delta = 0.0032$.
$N \approx 16 \cdot 0.04 / (0.0032)^2 \approx 0.64 / 0.00001024 \approx 62,500$ per arm.
Actual N per arm $\approx$ 36,700.
36,700 < 62,500. The study is underpowered to detect the *target* 8% lift. However, the metric observed was only 3% lift. The error code POWER applies if the arm is too small to detect the *target* lift at stated alpha/power. Since $N_{actual} < N_{required}$ for the design parameters, this fits the definition of an underpowered test relative to its design goals. Flag POWER.

**SIMPSON Check**:
Desktop Conversion:
Control: 1,137 / 18,128 $\approx$ 6.27%
Treatment: 1,231 / 20,539 $\approx$ 6.00% (Treatment < Control)
Mobile Conversion:
Control: 336 / 18,694 $\approx$ 1.80%
Treatment: 275 / 16,029 $\approx$ 1.72% (Treatment < Control)
Both segments show Treatment < Control (negative lift).
Pooled result: Control 4.0%, Treatment 4.118% (positive lift).
Since the pooled result (positive) is reversed compared to both segments (negative), this is a clear case of Simpson's Paradox. This likely stems from the denomination issue (see below) or weighting, but the reversal is present. Flag SIMPSON.

**DENOM Check**: Randomization unit is "user id". The metric is "order conversion rate".
Usually, conversion rate is Orders / Users or Orders / Sessions?
If Orders / Users:
Control: 1,473 / 36,822 = 4.00%. Matches reported.
Treatment: 1,506 / 36,568 = 4.118%. Matches reported.
The denominator matches the randomization unit (Users). No DENOM error based on the definition "denominator is not the unit that was randomized". The math works out perfectly with users.

**NOVELTY Check**: Daily lift starts at 6.0% and trends down to 1.5%.
The team projects the "reported lift" (aggregate 3.0%) for the next 12 months.
The trend shows a clear decay (novelty effect), yet the projection assumes the average (which includes the high early days) will persist. This ignores the fading lift. Flag NOVELTY.

ERRORS: POWER, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
