First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 83,039 (control) + 83,411 (treatment) = 166,450. The expected split is 50%, so 83,225 per arm. The treatment has 186 more users than expected. The standard deviation for a binomial distribution here is $\sqrt{166450 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 204$. The difference (186) is less than 2 standard deviations ($186 < 408$), so the split is within chance expectations. No SRM.

Next, I check for PEEKing. The planned duration was 21 days. The readout date is 2026-11-05, and the start was 2026-10-14. Counting inclusive, October has 31 days: $31 - 14 + 1 = 18$ days in October. Plus 5 days in November equals 23 days of data. However, the daily lift data lists 22 days. The test ran for at least 22 days, exceeding the planned 21 days. While close, stopping one day late or analyzing on day 22 when 21 were planned is often acceptable, but strictly speaking, if they stopped *early* it would be peeking. Here they ran longer. However, looking at the daily lift, the test seems to have been monitored daily. But the primary flag for PEEK is stopping *before* planned duration. They did not stop before 21 days; they have 22 days of data. So, no clear PEEK based on duration alone.

Now, check SIMPSON's paradox.
Control Conversion:
Desktop: $4,163 / 29,990 \approx 13.88\%$
Mobile: $2,480 / 53,049 \approx 4.67\%$
Treatment Conversion:
Desktop: $4,554 / 33,809 \approx 13.47\%$
Mobile: $2,250 / 49,602 \approx 4.54\%$
In both segments, Control > Treatment (13.88% > 13.47% and 4.67% > 4.54%).
However, the reported aggregate shows Treatment (6.49%) > Control (6.085%).
The direction reverses when pooled. This is a classic Simpson's Paradox caused by the imbalance in device traffic (Treatment has more high-converting Desktop users). The aggregate result is misleading.

Check NOVELTY. The daily lift starts at 11.0% and trends downward to 3.7% by day 22. The team projects the current reported lift (6.7%) for twelve months. Since the lift is clearly fading over the test duration, projecting the average or current lift as a permanent effect ignores the decay trend. This is a Novely effect error.

Regarding POWER: The sample size is large (~83k per arm), baseline 8%, target 5%. With these numbers, the test is well-powered. The issue is the validity of the metric, not the power.
Regarding DENOM: Randomized by user id, metric is order conversion rate (orders/users). This matches.

The critical errors are SIMPSON (aggregate lies) and NOVELTY (fade ignored).

ERRORS: SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
