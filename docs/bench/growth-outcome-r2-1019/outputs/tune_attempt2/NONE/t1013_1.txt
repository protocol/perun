First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 177,993 (control) + 177,565 (treatment) = 355,558. The expected split is 50%, so 177,779 per arm. The difference is roughly 214 users. The standard error for a 50/50 split with this N is $\sqrt{355558 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 298$. The deviation is less than 1 standard error, so the split is statistically consistent with 50/50. No SRM.

Next, I check the Denominator (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate." The readout provides users and orders. Conversion rate is typically Orders/Users. The randomization unit is "user id." The calculation uses users as the denominator ($8,900/177,993 \approx 5.0\%$). Since the denominator matches the randomization unit, there is no DENOM error.

Then, I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON). I calculate the conversion rates per segment:
Desktop Control: $6,145 / 80,291 \approx 7.65\%$. Desktop Treatment: $6,378 / 80,098 \approx 7.96\%$. (Lift positive).
Mobile Control: $2,755 / 97,702 \approx 2.82\%$. Mobile Treatment: $2,859 / 97,467 \approx 2.93\%$. (Lift positive).
Both segments show a positive lift, matching the pooled result. No SIMPSON.

I check Power (POWER). The baseline is 5.0%, target lift 5%. The sample size per arm is ~177k. Standard rules of thumb suggest detecting a 5% relative lift on a 5% baseline requires roughly 30k–50k users per arm for 80% power. With 177k, the study is well-powered. No POWER error.

I check Peeking (PEEK). The test ran from Jan 13 to Feb 10. This is 28 days (Jan has 31 days: $31-13+1 = 19$ days in Jan + 10 days in Feb = 29 days? Or strictly 28 days duration). The planned duration was 28 days. The readout says "final readout 2026-02-10," implying the test ran its full course. There is no evidence in the numbers that it was stopped early based on interim results. No PEEK.

Finally, I check Novelty (NOVELTY). The daily lift data shows a clear decay: starting at 9.0% and dropping to roughly 1.2% by day 28. The trend is consistently downward. However, the team's projection applies the *reported* average lift (4.0%) to the next twelve months. This ignores the fading effect evident in the daily data, assuming the initial high lift (or the average inflated by early novelty) will persist indefinitely. This is a classic Novelty error where temporary uplift is projected as permanent.

ERRORS: NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
