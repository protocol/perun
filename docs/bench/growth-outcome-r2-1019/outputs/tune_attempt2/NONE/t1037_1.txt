Several critical analysis errors are evident in the provided readout numbers.

First, there is a clear **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch). The experiment randomized users 50/50, yet the final counts are 44,634 (control) and 44,378 (treatment). While close, the segment breakdown reveals severe imbalance: Desktop has 22,263 vs 27,848 users, and Mobile has 22,371 vs 16,530. The treatment arm has significantly more desktop users and fewer mobile users than expected, indicating broken assignment or logging logic that correlates with device type.

Second, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is present. The aggregated report claims a massive lift (24%), but looking at the segments reveals the opposite or null effects when normalized:
- Desktop Control Rate: $2,573 / 22,263 \approx 11.56\%$
- Desktop Treatment Rate: $3,020 / 27,848 \approx 10.84\%$ (Treatment is worse)
- Mobile Control Rate: $998 / 22,371 \approx 4.46\%$
- Mobile Treatment Rate: $692 / 16,530 \approx 4.19\%$ (Treatment is worse)
The treatment performs worse in both device segments, yet the pooled data shows a huge gain. This is caused by the SRM shifting the treatment mix toward the higher-converting desktop users.

Third, **DENOM** is an issue. The metric is "order conversion rate," but the reported percentages ($3,571/59,617 \approx 5.99\%$) use *sessions* as the denominator. Since randomization occurred by *user id*, the denominator must be users to maintain statistical validity. Using sessions violates the unit of randomization.

Fourth, **PEEK** is indicated. The planned duration was 14 days, but the readout date is August 11th, only 8 days after the August 3rd start. Stopping early based on a significant p-value inflates the false positive rate.

Finally, **NOVELTY** cannot be confirmed as the daily lift does not show a fading trend; it fluctuates between 19% and 31%. **POWER** is not an issue as the sample sizes are large.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK, SIMPSON, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
