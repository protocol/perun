First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total user count is 43,721 (control) + 44,777 (treatment) = 88,498. The expected split is 50%, so 44,249 per arm. The treatment has 528 more users than expected. The standard deviation for a binomial distribution here is $\sqrt{88498 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 148.7$. The difference is $528 / 148.7 \approx 3.55$ standard deviations. This exceeds the typical threshold of 2 (or even 3) for chance variation, indicating a broken randomization or logging issue. **SRM is flagged.**

Next, I check for PEEKing. The planned duration was 14 days, but the final readout date (June 19) is only 10 days after the start date (June 9). The test was stopped 4 days early. Stopping early based on significance inflates false positive rates. **PEEK is flagged.**

I check for POWER. The smaller arm (control) has 43,721 users. With a 4% baseline and 10% target lift, the required sample size per arm for 80% power at $\alpha=0.05$ is roughly calculated using the formula $n \approx 16 \times p(1-p) / (p \times \text{lift})^2$. Here, $p=0.04$, lift=0.004. $n \approx 16 \times 0.0384 / 0.000016 \approx 38,400$. Since 43,721 > 38,400, the test is adequately powered. No POWER error.
