**SRM Check**: Total users = 234,533 (Control) + 234,169 (Treatment) = 468,702. Expected per arm = 234,351. Difference = 364 users. Standard Error ≈ $\sqrt{N \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.5} \approx 342$. The difference is roughly 1.06 standard deviations, which is well within chance expectations ($|Z| < 1.96$). No SRM.

**PEEK Check**: Planned duration was 14 days. The readout date is 2026-05-18, and the start date was 2026-05-12. This is only 6 days of data. The test was stopped early before the planned duration. This constitutes peeking/early stopping, inflating false positive risk. **Flag PEEK**.

**POWER Check**: We must verify if the sample size is sufficient for the target lift. Target relative lift is 5% on a 5.0% baseline (absolute difference = 0.25%). Using standard approximation for $n$ per arm: $n \approx \frac{16 \cdot p(1-p)}{\Delta^2}$. Here $p=0.05$, $\Delta=0.0025$. $n \approx \frac{16 \cdot 0.05 \cdot 0.95}{0.00000625} \approx 121,600$. The actual sample size (~234k per arm) is nearly double the requirement. The test is adequately powered. No POWER error.

**SIMPSON Check**: Calculate conversion rates (Orders/Users) by segment.
Desktop Control: $7,770 / 87,091 \approx 8.92\%$. Desktop Treatment: $8,096 / 86,956 \approx 9.31\%$. Lift is positive.
Mobile Control: $3,957 / 147,442 \approx 2.68\%$. Mobile Treatment: $4,123 / 147,213 \approx 2.80\%$. Lift is positive.
Both segments show a positive lift, consistent with the pooled result. No Simpson's paradox.

**DENOM Check**: Randomization unit is "user id". The metric is "order conversion rate". The readout implies orders per user ($11,727/234,533 \approx 5.0\%$). The denominator matches the randomization unit. No DENOM error.

**NOVELTY Check**: Daily lifts are: 4.2, 4.6, 3.9, 4.4, 5.3, 4.0. There is no clear fading trend; the values fluctuate around the mean without a monotonic decrease. The projection applies the current average, not assuming a fade that isn't visible. No NOVELTY error evident from the numbers provided.

The primary issue is **PEEK**: the test ran for 6 days instead of the planned 14. Stopping early because results looked significant invalidates the reported p-value ($p=0.0007$ is likely inflated). The decision to roll out cannot be made until the test runs its full course or the alpha spending is corrected.
