First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total user count is 18,454 (control) + 19,244 (treatment) = 37,698. Expected per arm is 18,849. The treatment arm has 395 extra users. The standard error for the difference in proportions is $\sqrt{N \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.5} \approx \sqrt{37698}/2 \approx 97$. The deviation is $395$, which is $395/97 \approx 4.07$ standard deviations. This is far beyond the threshold for chance (typically 2 SDs), indicating a broken assignment or logging mechanism. **SRM is present.**

Next, I check the denominator. The metric is "order conversion rate." The readout provides users and orders. Conversion is calculated as Orders/Users ($923/18454 \approx 5.00\%$). Since users were the randomized unit, the denominator matches the randomization unit. **No DENOM error.**

I check for Simpson's Paradox by calculating segment rates:
Desktop Control: $641/7525 \approx 8.52\%$. Desktop Treatment: $711/7848 \approx 9.06\%$. (Lift positive)
Mobile Control: $282/10929 \approx 2.58\%$. Mobile Treatment: $313/11396 \approx 2.75\%$. (Lift positive)
Both segments show a positive lift, consistent with the pooled result. **No SIMPSON error.**

Regarding Power and Peeking: The test ran for 28 days as planned (Oct 3 to Oct 31), so there is no evidence of early stopping based on the dates provided. **No PEEK error.** The sample size (~37k) is generally sufficient for a 5% baseline, though the SRM makes power calculations unreliable; however, the primary failure here is the allocation mismatch, not necessarily an a priori underpowered design based on the counts alone. **No clear POWER error** distinct from the SRM consequence.

Regarding Novelty: The daily lift fluctuates (7.0% down to 4.9%) but does not show a clear, monotonic decay to zero that would invalidate the projection as "fading." It remains volatile around the mean. **No clear NOVELTY error.**

The critical failure is SRM. When the split is not 50/50 by chance, the randomization assumption is violated, making the p-value (0.1611) and the lift estimate untrustworthy. We cannot determine if the difference is due to the feature or the bias in user assignment.
