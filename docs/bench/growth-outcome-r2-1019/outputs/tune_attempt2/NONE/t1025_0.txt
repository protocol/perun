First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total user count is 37,232 (control) + 37,424 (treatment) = 74,656. The expected split is 50%, so 37,328 per arm. The treatment deviation is 37,424 - 37,328 = +96 users. The standard error for the difference in proportions is roughly $\sqrt{N \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.5} \approx \sqrt{18664} \approx 136$. A deviation of 96 is well within 2 standard errors (~272), so the split is statistically consistent with 50/50. No SRM.

Next, I check the denominator (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate." Users were randomized by user ID. The counts provided are orders per users (e.g., 1,117 orders / 37,232 users). The denominator matches the randomization unit. No DENOM error.

I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON).
Control Desktop Rate: $757 / 15,846 \approx 4.78\%$.
Treatment Desktop Rate: $801 / 15,928 \approx 5.03\%$. (Lift positive)
Control Mobile Rate: $360 / 21,386 \approx 1.68\%$.
Treatment Mobile Rate: $381 / 21,496 \approx 1.77\%$. (Lift positive)
Both segments show a positive lift, consistent with the pooled result. No SIMPSON error.

I check Power (POWER). The baseline is stated as 3.0%, but the observed control rate is $1,117/37,232 \approx 3.0\%$. Wait, the readout says "Reported conversion rate: control 2.188%". Let's re-calculate: $1,117 / 37,232 = 0.02999 \approx 3.0\%$. The readout claims the rate is 2.188%. This is a discrepancy. $1,117 / 51,043$ (sessions) = 2.188%. The reported metric uses **sessions** as the denominator, but users were randomized by **user ID**. This is a denominator mismatch. The analysis compares session-level conversion while randomizing users, violating independence assumptions if sessions are correlated, or simply misaligning the unit of analysis. This flags DENOM.

Regarding PEEK: The test ran from June 21 to July 14. That is 23 days (June has 30 days: 30-21+1 = 10 days in June + 14 in July = 24 days? Or simply counting the daily lift data points provided: there are 23 data points). The planned duration was 21 days. Running 23 days instead of 21 is slightly over, not stopped early. No PEEK.

Regarding NOVELTY: The daily lift fluctuates (28.9 down to 20.5, up to 30.2, etc.) but does not show a clear, monotonic fading trend indicative of novelty wear-off that contradicts the projection. The projection assumes the lift lasts 12 months; while risky, the numbers themselves don't explicitly show the "fading" pattern required to flag NOVELTY based strictly on the provided list.

The critical error is DENOM. The randomization unit is User, but the metric calculated is Orders/Sessions (2.188% = 1117/51043). Using sessions as the denominator when users are randomized inflates the sample size artificially and ignores within-user correlation, invalidating the p-value.

ERRORS: DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
