**SRM Check**: Total users = 27,946 (Control) + 28,008 (Treatment) = 55,954. Expected per arm = 27,977.
Difference = 31 users. Standard Error ≈ $\sqrt{55954 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 118$.
The deviation (31) is well within 2 standard deviations ($2 \times 118 = 236$). No SRM.

**PEEK Check**: Started May 25, readout June 9. Duration = 16 days (inclusive). Planned = 21 days.
The test was stopped 5 days early. Stopping before the planned duration based on interim results inflates false positive rates. This is a PEEK error.

**POWER Check**: Baseline 6%, target lift 8% (absolute 0.48%).
Observed counts are ~28k per arm. Required sample size for 80% power at these parameters is roughly $2 \times (1.96+0.84)^2 \times 0.06 \times 0.94 / (0.0048)^2 \approx 190,000$ per arm.
The actual sample (~28k) is far below the required ~190k. The test is severely underpowered to detect the target lift. This is a POWER error.

**SIMPSON Check**:
Desktop Conversion: Control $1142/11868 \approx 9.62\%$, Treatment $1237/13236 \approx 9.35\%$. (Treatment < Control)
Mobile Conversion: Control $534/16078 \approx 3.32\%$, Treatment $477/14772 \approx 3.23\%$. (Treatment < Control)
Pooled Result: Control $5.997\%$, Treatment $6.12\%$. (Treatment > Control)
The direction of the effect reverses when pooling segments (negative in both segments, positive in aggregate). This is Simpson's Paradox. This is a SIMPSON error.

**DENOM Check**: Randomized by user ID. Metric is order conversion rate (orders/users). The denominator matches the randomization unit. No DENOM error.

**NOVELTY Check**: Daily lift: 2.0% to 2.7% in first half, dropping to 1.5% in the last two days. While there is a slight dip at the very end, the trend is not a clear, consistent fade from a high novelty spike to zero; it fluctuates. However, projecting a non-significant (p=0.544) result as a lasting lift is the primary issue, but strictly looking for "fading over the test": the drop from 2.7% (day 5) to 1.5% (day 15) suggests some decay, but the p-value indicates the whole effect is likely noise. Given the explicit definition requires "fading... but projected as lasting", and the lift is statistically indistinguishable from zero, the projection is flawed, but the "fading" pattern is weak compared to the other errors. I will stick to the clear arithmetic violations.

The critical errors are stopping early (PEEK), insufficient sample size (POWER), and the paradoxical segment data (SIMPSON).

ERRORS: PEEK, POWER, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
