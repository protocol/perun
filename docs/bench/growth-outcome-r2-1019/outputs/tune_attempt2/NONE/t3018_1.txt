First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total user count is 54,307 (control) + 54,033 (treatment) = 108,340. The expected split is 54,170 per arm. The difference is 137 users. A quick chi-square approximation: $(137^2 / 54170) \times 2 \approx 0.7$. This is well below the critical threshold (~3.84 for p=0.05), so the split is statistically consistent with 50/50. No SRM.

Next, I check for Denominator errors (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate." Typically, this is Orders / Users or Orders / Sessions. Since randomization was by user ID, the denominator must be users.
Control Rate: $2,715 / 54,307 \approx 5.00\%$.
Treatment Rate: $2,754 / 54,033 \approx 5.097\%$.
The reported rates match the user-based calculation. No DENOM error.

Now, I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON). I calculate the conversion rates within the device segments:
Desktop Control: $1,929 / 26,683 \approx 7.23\%$.
Desktop Treatment: $2,082 / 29,672 \approx 7.02\%$.
Here, Control > Treatment (Negative lift).

Mobile Control: $786 / 27,624 \approx 2.85\%$.
Mobile Treatment: $672 / 24,361 \approx 2.76\%$.
Here, Control > Treatment (Negative lift).

In both segments, the treatment performs worse than the control. However, the pooled result reports a positive lift (5.097% vs 4.999%). This reversal occurs because the treatment arm has a higher proportion of high-converting desktop users (29,672 vs 26,683) compared to the control, skewing the aggregate. This is a clear case of Simpson's Paradox. The aggregate lift is misleading; the actual effect within segments is negative.

Regarding Power (POWER): The test ran for 30 days (slightly over the planned 28), and sample sizes are large (~54k per arm). With a 5% baseline, detecting an 8% lift usually requires ~20k-30k per arm. The study is not underpowered; the high p-value (0.46) simply confirms no significant positive effect exists, especially given the paradox.

Regarding Novelty (NOVELTY): The daily lift fluctuates between 1.3% and 2.5% without a clear fading trend toward zero or negative over the 30 days. While the aggregate is wrong due to Simpson's paradox, the daily data doesn't explicitly show a novelty decay pattern that contradicts the projection method itself, though the projection is invalid because the base lift is illusory.

Regarding Peek (PEEK): The readout states the final readout was after the planned duration (30 days vs 28 planned). There is no evidence of early stopping.

The critical error is SIMPSON. The overall positive lift is an artifact of imbalanced segmentation, not a real treatment effect. Since the segmented data shows the treatment is actually worse in both groups, rolling out would likely harm conversion.

ERRORS: SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
