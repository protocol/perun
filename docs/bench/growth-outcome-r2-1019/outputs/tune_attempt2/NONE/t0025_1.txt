First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 30,459 (control) + 30,531 (treatment) = 60,990. The expected split is 30,495 per arm. The difference is only 36 users, which is well within random chance for this sample size. No SRM.

Next, I check the Denominator (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate." The readout reports control conversion as 4.326% and treatment as 5.603%.
Calculation using Users as denominator:
Control: 1,827 orders / 30,459 users ≈ 6.00%.
Treatment: 1,907 orders / 30,531 users ≈ 6.25%.
These calculated rates do not match the reported rates.
Calculation using Sessions as denominator:
Control: 1,827 / 42,232 ≈ 4.326%.
Treatment: 1,907 / 34,037 ≈ 5.603%.
The reported rates match the session-based calculation. However, users were randomized by user ID, not session ID. Using sessions as the denominator violates the unit of randomization, introducing bias if users in one arm generate more sessions than the other. This is a DENOM error.

I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON) by calculating lift within segments using the correct user-based denominator to see if the direction flips, though the DENOM error already invalidates the pooled result.
Desktop Lift (users): (1253/12587) - (1201/12557) ≈ 9.96% - 9.56% = +0.4% (Positive).
Mobile Lift (users): (654/17944) - (626/17902) ≈ 3.64% - 3.50% = +0.14% (Positive).
The direction is consistent; no Simpson's paradox.

Regarding Power (POWER): The target was a 5% lift on a 6% baseline. The observed effect (using correct user denominators) is roughly 4% relative lift ((6.25-6.00)/6.00). While the observed effect is smaller than the target, the sample size (~30k per arm) is generally sufficient to detect a 5% lift with 80% power at alpha 0.05. The test is not clearly underpowered based on the counts alone.

Regarding Peek (PEEK) or Novelty: The test ran from April 14 to April 30 (17 days inclusive), exceeding the planned 14 days. The daily lift fluctuates but does not show a clear fading trend indicative of novelty wear-off that contradicts the projection.

The critical error is DENOM. The reported 29.5% lift is an artifact of dividing by sessions rather than users. The true lift based on users is approximately 4.2% ((6.25-6.00)/6.00), which is significantly lower than reported and changes the business case. The decision cannot be made on flawed metrics.
