First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 90,610 (control) + 90,936 (treatment) = 181,546. The expected split is 50%, so 90,773 per arm. The treatment has 163 more users than expected. The standard deviation for a binomial split is $\sqrt{181546 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 213$. The difference (163) is less than 2 standard deviations ($163 < 426$), so the split is within chance expectations. No SRM.

Next, I check for PEEKing. The planned duration was 21 days. The readout date is 2026-11-03, and the start was 2026-10-11. October has 31 days. Days in October: $31 - 11 + 1 = 21$. Days in November: 3. Total duration = 24 days. The test ran longer than planned, not shorter. No PEEK.

Next, I check POWER. The smaller arm is ~90k. With a 4% baseline and 8% target lift, standard calculators usually require ~100k-150k per arm for 80% power at alpha 0.05. However, the test actually ran and produced a p-value. "Underpowered" usually refers to the design before running or if the sample is so small no signal could be found. Here, the sample is large, and the p-value (0.0818) is close to significance. While the lift (4%) missed the target (8%), the sample size itself isn't trivially small enough to flag as a fatal "POWER" error based solely on counts without a specific power calculation table, but more importantly, the test completed its extended duration. I will not flag this as the primary error compared to others.

Next, SIMPSON's paradox.
Desktop Control Rate: $2376 / 33934 \approx 7.00\%$.
Desktop Treatment Rate: $2481 / 34056 \approx 7.29\%$. (Lift positive)
Mobile Control Rate: $1248 / 56676 \approx 2.20\%$.
Mobile Treatment Rate: $1303 / 56880 \approx 2.29\%$. (Lift positive)
Both segments show a positive lift. The aggregate is positive. No reversal. No SIMPSON.

Next, DENOM. Randomization is by user_id. The metric is "order conversion rate". Usually, this is Orders / Users or Orders / Sessions. If it is Orders / Users, the denominator matches the randomization unit. The numbers provided (Orders vs Users) allow this calculation. There is no evidence the denominator is mismatched (e.g., using pageviews). No DENOM.

Next, NOVELTY. The daily lift starts at 8.8% and 9.0% (Days 1-2) and steadily decays to 0.4% and 1.0% by the end (Days 22-23). The reported overall lift is 4.0%. The team projects this 4.0% lift for the next twelve months. This ignores the clear downward trend indicating the effect is fading (novelty wearing off). Projecting a sustained 4% when the most recent data shows ~1% is a clear NOVELTY error.

DECISION: HOLD
