**SRM**: The planned split is 50/50. Total users = 233,136 + 237,254 = 470,390. Expected per arm = 235,195.
Control deviation: |233,136 - 235,195| = 2,059.
Treatment deviation: |237,254 - 235,195| = 2,059.
Standard Error for proportion $p=0.5$ with $N=470,390$ is $\sqrt{0.5 \times 0.5 / 470,390} \approx 0.00073$.
Expected count SD = $470,390 \times 0.00073 \approx 343$.
The deviation (2,059) is roughly $2,059 / 343 \approx 6$ standard deviations away from expected. This is statistically impossible by chance; assignment or logging is broken.

**PEEK**: Planned duration was 21 days. The readout date (June 13) is only 9 days after the start (June 4). The test was stopped early based on significant results, inflating false positive risk.

**DENOM**: Users were randomized by user ID. The reported metric is "order conversion rate."
Control rate calculation: $11,656 \text{ orders} / 233,136 \text{ users} \approx 5.0\%$.
However, the readout reports control conversion as **3.48%**.
Let's check sessions: $11,656 / 334,910 \text{ sessions} \approx 3.48\%$.
The reported rate uses **sessions** as the denominator, but randomization occurred at the **user** level. This violates the unit of analysis.

**SIMPSON**: Let's check segment lifts.
Desktop Control Rate: $8,770 / 111,403 \approx 7.87\%$.
Desktop Treatment Rate: $9,537 / 113,371 \approx 8.41\%$. (Lift ~6.8%)
Mobile Control Rate: $2,886 / 121,733 \approx 2.37\%$.
Mobile Treatment Rate: $3,139 / 123,883 \approx 2.53\%$. (Lift ~6.7%)
Both segments show ~6-7% lift. The aggregate reported lift is 36.4%. While the aggregate is inflated due to the denominator error (mixing sessions/users), the direction of the effect is consistent across segments (Treatment > Control). Simpson's paradox requires a *reversal* of direction within segments compared to the whole. No reversal exists here.

**POWER**: With ~233k users per arm, baseline 5%, and target 5% lift, the study is vastly overpowered, not underpowered.

**NOVELTY**: Daily lift fluctuates (29.9% to 43.8%) but shows no clear fading trend over the 9 days; it remains high.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
