First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 21,474 (control) + 21,656 (treatment) = 43,130. The expected split is 50%, so 21,565 per arm. The difference is roughly 90 users. A standard deviation check ($\sqrt{43130 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 104$) shows the deviation is less than 1 standard deviation. This is well within chance; no SRM.

Next, I verify the denominator (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate." The randomization unit is "user id." The reported rates use users as the denominator:
Control: $1,718 / 21,474 \approx 0.0800$ (8.00%).
Treatment: $1,820 / 21,656 \approx 0.0840$ (8.40%).
However, the readout reports "Control 5.348%" and "Treatment 6.216%."
Let's check sessions as the denominator:
Control: $1,718 / 32,127 \approx 0.05347$ (5.35%).
Treatment: $1,820 / 29,279 \approx 0.06216$ (6.22%).
The reported percentages match the session-based calculation, not the user-based calculation. Since users were randomized, using sessions as the denominator violates the unit of randomization, risking bias if session counts differ per user. This is a DENOM error.

I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON) by calculating lift within segments:
Desktop Control: $1,286 / 10,714 \approx 12.00\%$. Desktop Treatment: $1,362 / 10,804 \approx 12.61\%$. (Lift positive).
Mobile Control: $432 / 10,760 \approx 4.01\%$. Mobile Treatment: $458 / 10,852 \approx 4.22\%$. (Lift positive).
Both segments show a positive lift, consistent with the pooled direction. No Simpson's paradox.

Regarding Power (POWER): The baseline used in calculations appears to be ~8% (user-based), matching the design. The sample size (~21k per arm) is generally sufficient for an 8% baseline to detect a 10% lift with 80% power. No obvious underpowering based on counts.

For Peeking (PEEK): The test ran from Sep 25 to Oct 10. This is exactly 15 days inclusive, or 14 days duration. The planned duration was 14 days. The test finished on schedule. No PEEK.

For Novelty (NOVELTY): The daily lift fluctuates (18.9% down to 13.4%) but shows no clear, consistent fading trend to zero; it remains high throughout. The projection assumes lasting lift, but the data doesn't show a decay pattern characteristic of novelty wear-off that contradicts the projection strongly enough to flag solely on these numbers without a statistical trend test.

The primary error is DENOM.

ERRORS: DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
