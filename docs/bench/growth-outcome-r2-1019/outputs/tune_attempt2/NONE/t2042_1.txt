**SRM Check**: Total users = 125,747 (Control) + 123,691 (Treatment) = 249,438. Expected per arm = 124,719.
Difference = 125,747 - 124,719 = 1,028.
Standard Error ≈ $\sqrt{N \times 0.5 \times 0.5} = \sqrt{62,359.5} \approx 250$.
Z-score = $1,028 / 250 \approx 4.1$. A Z-score > 2 indicates a significant deviation from the 50/50 split. The assignment or logging is broken.

**PEEK Check**: Planned duration was 14 days. The readout date is 2026-02-25, and data is shown for 9 days (Feb 16–24). The test was stopped 5 days early. Stopping before the planned duration based on interim results inflates false positive rates.

**SIMPSON Check**:
Control Rate Desktop: $5,747 / 62,802 \approx 9.15\%$.
Treatment Rate Desktop: $6,211 / 71,104 \approx 8.73\%$. (Treatment is lower).
Control Rate Mobile: $1,797 / 62,945 \approx 2.86\%$.

Both segments show a negative lift for treatment, yet the pooled result reports a positive lift (6.18% vs 5.999%). This is a clear reversal, indicating Simpson's Paradox caused by the unequal segment sizes between arms.

**POWER Check**: With ~124k users per arm, a 6% baseline, and 80% power, the test should easily detect a 5% lift. The sample size is sufficient; the issue is not underpowering but invalid data composition.

**DENOM Check**: The metric is order conversion rate. The denominator used in my segment checks was users, which matches the randomization unit (user id). No error here.

**NOVELTY Check**: Daily lifts fluctuate (3.3% down to 2.4%) but do not show a consistent fading trend to zero. While the projection assumes longevity, the primary errors are structural.

The presence of SRM, PEEK, and SIMPSON makes the reported lift unreliable. The positive pooled result is mathematically impossible given the negative performance in both sub-segments, driven entirely by the skewed user distribution (SRM) and early stopping (PEEK).
