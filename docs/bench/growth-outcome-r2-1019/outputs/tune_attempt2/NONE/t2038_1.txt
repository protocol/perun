**SRM Check**: The total users are 24,105 (control) and 23,213 (treatment), summing to 47,318. Expected per arm is 23,659. The difference is $|24,105 - 23,659| = 446$. The standard error for a 50/50 split is $\sqrt{47318 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 109$. The deviation is $446 / 109 \approx 4.1$ standard deviations. This exceeds the threshold for chance (typically 2 SDs), indicating a Sample Ratio Mismatch.

**PEEK Check**: The test ran from June 5 to June 21, which is 16 days. The planned duration was 14 days. The test ran longer than planned, not shorter. There is no evidence of early stopping based on the dates provided.

**POWER Check**: The treatment arm has 23,213 users. With a baseline of 6% and target lift of 8%, the required sample size per arm is approximately $16 \times (1.96+0.84)^2 \times 0.06 \times (1-0.06) / (0.0048)^2 \approx 20,000$. The current sample size exceeds this requirement, so it is not underpowered.

**SIMPSON Check**: 
Control Desktop Rate: $1066/10902 \approx 9.78\%$. Treatment Desktop Rate: $1131/12121 \approx 9.33\%$. (Treatment lower).
Control Mobile Rate: $380/13203 \approx 2.88\%$. Treatment Mobile Rate: $305/11092 \approx 2.75\%$. (Treatment lower).
Both segments show the treatment performing worse than control, yet the pooled result reports a lift ($6.186\% > 5.999\%$). This reversal indicates Simpson's Paradox, likely driven by the SRM shifting the mix of users toward the higher-converting desktop segment in the treatment arm.

**DENOM Check**: The metric is order conversion rate. The readout implies orders per user (orders/users). Randomization was by user ID. The denominator matches the randomization unit.

**NOVELTY Check**: The daily lift fluctuates between 2.5% and 3.7% with no clear fading trend over the 16 days. The projection assumes persistence, but the data does not show fading.

The primary errors are SRM (broken assignment/logging) and SIMPSON (misleading pooled result due to segment imbalance). These invalidate the reported lift.
