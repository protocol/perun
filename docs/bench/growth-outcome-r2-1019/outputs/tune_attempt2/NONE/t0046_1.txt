**SRM**: The total users are 286,382. Expected per arm is 143,191. Observed: 143,252 (Control) and 143,130 (Treatment). The difference is negligible (~122 users), well within chance for this sample size. No SRM.

**PEEK**: Planned duration was 14 days. The readout date is 2026-04-28, exactly 6 days after the start (2026-04-22). The test was stopped early before the planned duration. This constitutes peeking/early stopping. **Flag PEEK.**

**POWER**: Both arms have ~143k users. With a baseline of 8% and target lift of 5%, standard calculators indicate ~60k-70k users per arm are needed for 80% power at alpha 0.05. The sample size (143k) exceeds the requirement. No POWER issue.

**SIMPSON**: Let's check segment conversion rates.
Desktop Control: 7,927 / 63,652 ≈ 12.45%
Desktop Treatment: 9,761 / 85,253 ≈ 11.45% (Treatment is lower)
Mobile Control: 3,533 / 79,600 ≈ 4.44%
Mobile Treatment: 2,361 / 57,877 ≈ 4.08% (Treatment is lower)
In both segments, the treatment performs worse than control. However, the pooled result claims treatment is better (8.47% vs 8.0%).
Pooled Control: 11,460 / 143,252 ≈ 8.00%
Pooled Treatment: 12,122 / 143,130 ≈ 8.47%
The direction reverses when pooling because the treatment arm has a much higher proportion of Desktop users (who convert at higher rates) compared to the control arm, despite randomization claims (likely due to the device split mismatch noted in Simpson's setup, though the root cause is the skewed device distribution across arms). The aggregate shows a lift, while every segment shows a loss. **Flag SIMPSON.**

**DENOM**: Randomization was by user id. The metric is order conversion rate (orders/users). The denominator matches the randomization unit. No DENOM error.

**NOVELTY**: Daily lift drops from 18.7% to -0.0% over 6 days. The trend shows the effect vanishing. The team projects this transient lift over 12 months. **Flag NOVELTY.**
