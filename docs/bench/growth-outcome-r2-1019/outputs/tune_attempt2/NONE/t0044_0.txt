First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 15,768 (control) + 15,678 (treatment) = 31,446. Expected per arm is 15,723. The difference is only 45 users, which is well within chance variation for this sample size. No SRM.

Next, I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON). I calculate the conversion rates by device:
Desktop Control: 323 / 6,954 ≈ 4.64%
Desktop Treatment: 401 / 9,385 ≈ 4.27% (Treatment is lower)
Mobile Control: 150 / 8,814 ≈ 1.70%
Mobile Treatment: 98 / 6,293 ≈ 1.56% (Treatment is lower)
In both segments, the treatment performs worse than the control. However, the pooled data shows Treatment (499/15,678 ≈ 3.18%) beating Control (473/15,768 ≈ 3.00%). Since the direction reverses when aggregating, this is a clear case of Simpson's Paradox, likely driven by the imbalance in device traffic between arms.

I check Denominator (DENOM). The metric is order conversion rate. The readout implies orders per user (randomized unit). Even if it were orders per session, the paradox exists regardless, but the primary error is the aggregation issue. The denominator issue is secondary to the paradox here, but the paradox is the definitive flag.

I check Novelty (NOVELTY). The daily lift starts at 15.8% and decays steadily to 1.3% by day 29. The reported aggregate lift is 6.1%. Projecting this 6.1% lift for twelve months ignores the clear decay trend, suggesting the effect is temporary (novelty) rather than permanent. The team's projection assumes the average holds, which the trend contradicts.

I check Power (POWER). The sample sizes are roughly equal and large (~15k each). With a 3% baseline, detecting a 10% lift usually requires ~14k per arm. The study is adequately powered; the non-significant p-value (0.3484) is likely due to the conflicting segment data canceling out the signal or the effect being null/negative in reality.

I check Peek (PEEK). The test ran from Aug 29 to Sep 27 (29 days), matching the planned 28 days closely. There is no evidence of early stopping.

The critical errors are SIMPSON (reversed trends in segments) and NOVELTY (fading lift projected as permanent). These invalidate the recommendation to roll out.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
