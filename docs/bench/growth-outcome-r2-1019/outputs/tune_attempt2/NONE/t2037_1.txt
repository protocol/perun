**SRM Check**: Total users = 36,822 (Control) + 36,568 (Treatment) = 73,390. Expected per arm = 36,695.
Difference = |36,822 - 36,695| = 127.
Standard Error ≈ $\sqrt{73390 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 135$.
The deviation (127) is less than 2 standard errors (~270). The split is statistically consistent with 50/50. No SRM.

**PEEK Check**: Planned duration was 21 days (Oct 31 to Nov 20). The readout date is Nov 22, and daily data is provided for 22 days. The test ran slightly longer than planned, not shorter. Stopping early inflates false positives; running late does not trigger this specific error code based on the provided definition. No PEEK.

**POWER Check**: The test detected a 3% lift with a p-value of 0.4179 (not significant). The target was an 8% lift. While the result is not significant, "underpowered" usually refers to the design phase calculation. With ~36k users per arm and a 4% baseline, the detectable effect size at 80% power/5% alpha is roughly 1.5-2%. The study was large enough to detect the *target* 8% lift easily. The fact that the observed lift was smaller (3%) and insignificant is a result, not a design flaw in sample size relative to the target. No POWER error flagged by the numbers alone.

**SIMPSON Check**:
Control Rate Desktop: $1,137 / 18,128 \approx 6.27\%$
Treatment Rate Desktop: $1,231 / 20,539 \approx 6.00\%$ (Lift is negative)
Control Rate Mobile: $336 / 18,694 \approx 1.80\%$
Treatment Rate Mobile: $275 / 16,029 \approx 1.72\%$ (Lift is negative)
Both segments show a negative lift (Treatment < Control). However, the pooled result reports a positive lift (4.118% > 4.0%).
Pooled Control: $1,473 / 36,822 \approx 4.00\%$
Pooled Treatment: $1,506 / 36,568 \approx 4.12\%$
Since the direction reverses from negative in all segments to positive in the aggregate, this is Simpson's Paradox. This likely stems from the treatment group having a much higher proportion of Desktop users (who convert higher) compared to the control group, despite the randomization claim (which might hold for users but session weighting or specific sub-randomization issues could cause this). The numbers strictly show the paradox.

**DENOM Check**: Metric is order conversion rate. Randomization is by user id. The counts provided for the metric calculation (Orders / Users) align with the randomized unit. No DENOM error.

**NOVELTY Check**: Daily lift drops from 6.0% to 1.5%. The trend is clearly fading. The team projects the current reported average (which includes the high early days) for the next 12 months. This ignores the decay trend evident in the daily data. This is a NOVELTY error.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
