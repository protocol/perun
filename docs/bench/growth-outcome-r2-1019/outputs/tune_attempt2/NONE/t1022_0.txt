First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 149,820 (control) + 149,334 (treatment) = 299,154. The expected split is 149,577 per arm. The difference is roughly 486 users. Given the large sample size, a deviation of this magnitude is well within the bounds of random chance for a 50/50 split; no SRM is evident.

Next, I examine the Denominator (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate," and the calculation uses orders divided by users (e.g., 8,989 / 149,820 ≈ 6.0%). Since users were the randomized unit, the denominator matches the randomization unit. No DENOM error exists.

Then, I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON).
Desktop Control Rate: 6,410 / 72,024 ≈ 8.90%
Desktop Treatment Rate: 6,869 / 71,791 ≈ 9.57% (Lift positive)
Mobile Control Rate: 2,579 / 77,796 ≈ 3.31%
Mobile Treatment Rate: 2,764 / 77,543 ≈ 3.56% (Lift positive)
Both segments show a positive lift consistent with the pooled result. No SIMPSON error.

Regarding Power (POWER): The test has ~150k users per arm, a 6% baseline, and targets a 5% lift. Standard power calculations indicate this sample size is more than sufficient to detect such an effect with 80% power. No POWER error.

However, two critical errors are present. First, PEEK: The planned duration was 28 days, but the readout date (Aug 12) is only 16 days after the start (July 27). The test was stopped early. Furthermore, the p-value is reported as "0.0", which is statistically impossible for finite data and suggests premature stopping or reporting error without correction.

Second, NOVELTY: The daily lift data fluctuates (ranging from 5.4% to 10.1%) without a clear monotonic fade, but the projection applies the current lift to the "next twelve months." Assuming a short-term spike (potentially driven by novelty or early volatility) will persist unchanged for a year is a classic novelty/forecasting error. The readout explicitly projects this transient lift indefinitely.

ERRORS: PEEK, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
