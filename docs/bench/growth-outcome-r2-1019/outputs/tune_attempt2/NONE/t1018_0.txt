First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 109,632 (control) + 109,376 (treatment) = 219,008. The expected split is 50%, so 109,504 per arm. The treatment deviation is $|109,376 - 109,504| = 128$. The standard error for the difference in proportions is $\sqrt{N \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.5} \approx \sqrt{54,752} \approx 234$. The deviation (128) is well within 2 standard deviations (~468), so the split is statistically consistent with 50/50. No SRM.

Next, I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON) by calculating conversion rates per device segment.
Desktop Control: $6,322 / 52,960 \approx 11.94\%$.
Desktop Treatment: $7,392 / 65,955 \approx 11.21\%$.
Here, Control > Treatment (negative lift).

Mobile Control: $2,449 / 56,672 \approx 4.32\%$.
Mobile Treatment: $1,761 / 43,421 \approx 4.06\%$.
Here, Control > Treatment (negative lift).

In both segments, the control group outperforms the treatment group. However, the reported aggregate result claims the treatment (8.368%) beats the control (8.0%). This reversal of direction when pooling data confirms Simpson's Paradox. This occurred because the treatment arm received a disproportionately high number of desktop users (who have higher baseline conversion) compared to the control arm, skewing the aggregate.

Regarding Power (POWER): The sample sizes are ~109k per arm. With a baseline of 8% and target lift of 5%, the required sample size is typically much lower than 100k per arm for 80% power. The test is not underpowered.

Regarding Denominator (DENOM): The metric is order conversion rate. The readout implies orders per user (orders/users). Since randomization was by user ID, using users as the denominator is correct.

Regarding Peek (PEEK) and Novelty (NOVELTY): The test ran for 22 days against a planned 21 days. There is no evidence of early stopping. The daily lift fluctuates (4.0% to 5.3%) but shows no clear fading trend that contradicts the projection.

The critical error is SIMPSON. The aggregate lift is an artifact of imbalanced segment sizes, not a true treatment effect. In fact, the treatment appears harmful in both major segments.

ERRORS: SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
