First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 90,610 (control) + 90,936 (treatment) = 181,546. The expected split is 50%, so 90,773 per arm. The difference is |90,936 - 90,773| = 163. The standard error for the difference in proportions is roughly $\sqrt{N \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.5} \approx \sqrt{45386} \approx 213$. A difference of 163 is well within 2 standard deviations (~0.76 SD), so SRM is not present.

Next, I check for PEEKing. The planned duration was 21 days. The readout dates are Oct 11 to Nov 3. Counting inclusive: Oct 11–31 is 21 days; Nov 1–3 adds 3 days. Total duration is 24 days. However, the daily lift data lists 23 days of data. The test ran longer than the planned 21 days, not shorter. Stopping late generally reduces false positives (increases power) rather than inflating them, unless specific sequential testing boundaries were crossed earlier and ignored, which isn't stated. The prompt defines PEEK as stopping *before* planned duration. Since 23/24 days > 21 days, PEEK is not flagged based on the definition provided.

Next, I check POWER. The baseline is 4.0%, target lift 8% (absolute 0.32%). With ~90k users per arm, the standard error for the difference in proportions ($p_1 - p_2$) is $\sqrt{\frac{0.04(0.96)}{90000} + \frac{0.04(0.96)}{90000}} \approx \sqrt{2 \cdot 4.26 \cdot 10^{-7}} \approx 0.00092$. The detectable lift at 80% power (Z=0.84) and alpha 0.05 (Z=1.96) requires a difference of $(1.96+0.84) \times 0.00092 \approx 0.0026$ (0.26%). The target was 0.32%. Since 0.32% > 0.26%, the study is adequately powered.

Next, SIMPSON's paradox.
Desktop Control Rate: $2376/33934 \approx 7.00\%$.
Desktop Treatment Rate: $2481/34056 \approx 7.29\%$. (Lift positive)
Mobile Control Rate: $1248/56676 \approx 2.20\%$.
Mobile Treatment Rate: $1303/56880 \approx 2.29\%$. (Lift positive)
Both segments show a positive lift. The aggregate also shows positive lift. No reversal occurs.

Next, DENOM. Randomization is by user id. The metric is order conversion rate. The readout implies orders/users. Control: $3624/90610 = 4.00\%$. Treatment: $3784/90936 = 4.16\%$. The denominator matches the randomization unit.

Next, NOVELTY. The daily lift starts at 8.8% and trends downward to 1.0% by day 23. The reported overall lift is 4.0%. The team projects this 4.0% lift for the next twelve months. This ignores the clear fading trend where the effect nearly vanished by the end of the test. This is a classic novelty effect error.

ERRORS: NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
