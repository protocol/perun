First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total user count is 21,282 (control) + 21,066 (treatment) = 42,348. The expected split is 21,174 per arm. The difference is 108 users. The standard error for a 50/50 split is $\sqrt{42348 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 103$. The deviation is roughly 1 standard error ($108/103 \approx 1.05$), which is well within the range of chance (typically < 1.96 for 95% confidence). No SRM.

Next, I check for Denominator errors (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate." Users were randomized by user ID. The reported rates align with orders divided by users: Control $1,703 / 21,282 \approx 8.002\%$; Treatment $1,768 / 21,066 \approx 8.393\%$. The denominator matches the randomization unit. No DENOM error.

I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON).
Desktop Conversion: Control $1,174/8,412 \approx 13.96\%$, Treatment $1,219/8,326 \approx 14.64\%$. (Treatment > Control)
Mobile Conversion: Control $529/12,870 \approx 4.11\%$, Treatment $549/12,740 \approx 4.31\%$. (Treatment > Control)
Since the treatment performs better in both segments and in the aggregate, there is no reversal. No SIMPSON error.

I check for Power (POWER). The target lift was 10% on an 8% baseline (detecting 8.8%). The observed lift is ~4.9%. While the test did not reach significance (p=0.1429), the sample size (~21k per arm) is generally sufficient to detect a 10% lift with 80% power. The failure to find significance is likely due to the true effect being smaller than the target, not necessarily an underpowered design relative to the *planned* parameters. However, strictly speaking, we cannot confirm the power calculation without the variance formula used, but usually, ~42k users is adequate for these baseline rates. I will not flag this based solely on the non-significant result, as that is a valid outcome.

I check for Peeking (PEEK). The planned duration was 28 days. The readout date is March 27, and the start was March 6. This is 21 days of data ($27-6=21$). The test was analyzed and a recommendation made after 21 days, before the planned 28 days elapsed. Stopping early to check results inflates the false positive rate. This is a clear PEEK error.

I check for Novelty (NOVELTY). The daily lift values fluctuate between 4.2% and 5.7% over the 21 days. There is no clear fading trend (e.g., starting high and dropping to near zero). The projection applies the current lift to 12 months, but without evidence of fading in the data provided, we cannot confirm a NOVELTY error from the numbers alone.

The primary error is analyzing the test before the pre-specified duration ended.

ERRORS: PEEK
DECISION: HOLD
