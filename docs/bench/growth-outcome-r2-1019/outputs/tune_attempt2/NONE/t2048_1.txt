**SRM Check**: Total users = 18,840 (Control) + 18,084 (Treatment) = 36,924. Expected per arm = 18,462.
Difference = |18,840 - 18,462| = 378.
Standard Error (SE) for binomial split ≈ $\sqrt{n \cdot p \cdot (1-p)} = \sqrt{36924 \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.5} \approx 96$.
Z-score = $378 / 96 \approx 3.94$. A Z-score > 1.96 indicates a significant deviation from the 50/50 split. The assignment or logging is likely broken.

**DENOM Check**: The metric is "order conversion rate." Users were randomized by user ID.
Reported Control Rate: $942 \text{ orders} / 18,840 \text{ users} = 0.0500$ (5.00%).
However, the readout reports 3.664%.
If we divide by sessions: $942 / 25,711 \approx 0.03664$ (3.664%).
The reported metric uses *sessions* as the denominator, but the unit of randomization was *users*. This violates the independence assumption of the test because multiple sessions from one user are correlated.

**SIMPSON Check**:
Desktop Control Rate (users): $625/8005 \approx 7.81\%$. Desktop Treatment: $625/7683 \approx 8.13\%$. (Lift ~4.1%)
Mobile Control Rate (users): $317/10835 \approx 2.93\%$. Mobile Treatment: $317/10401 \approx 3.05\%$. (Lift ~4.1%)
Pooled Control (users): $942/18840 = 5.00\%$. Pooled Treatment: $942/18084 = 5.21\%$.
The direction of the effect is consistent across segments and the whole (Treatment > Control). No reversal observed. Note: If calculated by session (the erroneous denominator), the rates are identical within segments ($625/625$ orders vs sessions logic doesn't hold for rates without session counts per segment, but the user-based check confirms no paradox).

**PEEK/POWER/NOVELTY**:
Dates: Feb 13 to Mar 6 is 21 days (inclusive counting roughly matches planned duration). No evidence of early stopping (PEEK).
Power: With ~18k users and 5% baseline, detecting 10% lift is feasible; the sample is not obviously too small (POWER).
Daily lift fluctuates (13.2% to 20.8%) but shows no clear fading trend toward zero; it remains high at day 21 (14.7%). No clear evidence of NOVELTY decay invalidating the projection based solely on these numbers.

The critical errors are the Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM) and the Denominator mismatch (DENOM).

ERRORS: SRM, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
