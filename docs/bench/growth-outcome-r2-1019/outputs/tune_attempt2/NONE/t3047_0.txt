First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total user count is 35,473 (control) + 36,445 (treatment) = 71,918. The expected split is 50%, so 35,959 per arm. The treatment arm has 486 more users than expected. The standard deviation for a binomial distribution here is $\sqrt{71918 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 134$. The difference is $486 / 134 \approx 3.6$ standard deviations. This exceeds the typical threshold of 2 (or even 3) standard deviations, indicating a significant deviation from the 50/50 plan. **SRM is flagged.**

Next, I check for PEEKing. The planned duration was 14 days, but the readout date is January 18th, starting January 8th. This is exactly 10 days of data. Stopping early at day 10 out of 14 without adjustment inflates false positives. **PEEK is flagged.**

I check POWER. The smaller arm (control) has 35,473 users. With a baseline of 5% and target lift of 10% (to 5.5%), standard power calculators suggest roughly 30,000–35,000 users per arm are needed for 80% power at alpha 0.05. Since 35,473 > required sample size, the test appears adequately powered. No error here.

I check SIMPSON's paradox by calculating segment conversion rates.
Desktop Control: $1,214 / 14,313 \approx 8.48\%$. Desktop Treatment: $1,310 / 14,705 \approx 8.91\%$. Lift is positive.
Mobile Control: $559 / 21,160 \approx 2.64\%$. Mobile Treatment: $603 / 21,740 \approx 2.77\%$. Lift is positive.
Since both segments show a positive lift consistent with the pooled result, there is no reversal. No Simpson's paradox.

I check DENOM. The metric is "order conversion rate." The randomization unit is "user id." The readout provides user counts and order counts. However, the reported conversion rates (3.7% and 4.2%) do not match orders/users ($1,773/35,473 \approx 5.0\%$). They match orders/sessions ($1,773/47,869 \approx 3.7\%$). Since sessions were not the randomized unit (users were), using sessions as the denominator violates the unit of randomization. **DENOM is flagged.**

I check NOVELTY. The daily lift values (12.1% to 15.9%) fluctuate but do not show a clear fading trend; they remain stable or increase slightly. There is no evidence of fading to flag this.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
