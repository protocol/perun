First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 67,449 (control) + 66,099 (treatment) = 133,548. The expected split is 66,774 per arm. The difference is roughly 675 users. Using a quick chi-square approximation: $\chi^2 \approx \frac{(675)^2}{66774} + \frac{(-675)^2}{66774} \approx 6.8 + 6.8 = 13.6$. With 1 degree of freedom, a value > 3.84 indicates significance at p < 0.05. 13.6 is far above this threshold, indicating the 50/50 randomization failed or logging is broken.

Next, I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON).
Control Conversion Rate (Desktop): $1,996 / 31,282 \approx 6.38\%$
Treatment Conversion Rate (Desktop): $2,090 / 33,790 \approx 6.19\%$
Result: Treatment is lower (-0.19%).

Control Conversion Rate (Mobile): $702 / 36,167 \approx 1.94\%$
Treatment Conversion Rate (Mobile): $608 / 32,309 \approx 1.88\%$
Result: Treatment is lower (-0.06%).

In both segments, the treatment performs worse than the control. However, the reported aggregate numbers show:
Control Aggregate: $2,698 / 67,449 \approx 4.00\%$
Treatment Aggregate: $2,698 / 66,099 \approx 4.08\%$
Result: Treatment appears higher (+0.08%).

Since the direction of the effect reverses when pooling the data (negative in segments, positive in aggregate), this is a clear case of Simpson's Paradox, likely driven by the unequal distribution of users across devices combined with the SRM.

Regarding Power (POWER): The test ran for 28 days as planned, so PEEK is not evident from the dates. The sample size (~133k) is generally sufficient for a 4% baseline, but the SRM and paradox make power calculations on the current data unreliable. The Denominator (DENOM) matches the randomization unit (users), so that is correct. The daily lift shows fluctuation but no obvious fading trend to suggest NOVELTY issues specifically overriding the other errors.

The presence of SRM invalidates the causal inference, and Simpson's Paradox confirms the aggregate metric is misleading. The recommendation to roll out is based on flawed data.
