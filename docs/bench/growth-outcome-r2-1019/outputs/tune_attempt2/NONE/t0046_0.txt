Several analysis errors are evident from the provided numbers.

**SRM (Sample Ratio Mismatch):** The total users are 143,252 (control) and 143,130 (treatment), which is a near-perfect 50/50 split globally. However, looking at the device segments reveals a massive imbalance. Desktop users are 63,652 (control) vs 85,253 (treatment), while mobile users are 79,600 (control) vs 57,877 (treatment). The treatment arm has significantly more desktop users and fewer mobile users than expected under random assignment. This indicates broken assignment or logging correlated with device type.

**PEEK (Peeking):** The test started on 2026-04-22 with a planned duration of 14 days. The final readout date is 2026-04-28, which is only 6 days later. The test was stopped and a decision recommended before the planned duration ended. The daily lift data shows the effect decaying rapidly (from 18.7% to -0.0%), suggesting the team stopped early because the initial spike looked significant, inflating the false positive rate.

**SIMPSON (Simpson's Paradox):** Let's calculate the conversion rates per device.
Desktop Control: $7,927 / 63,652 \approx 12.45\%$.
Desktop Treatment: $9,761 / 85,253 \approx 11.45\%$. (Treatment is worse).
Mobile Control: $3,533 / 79,600 \approx 4.44\%$.
Mobile Treatment: $2,361 / 57,877 \approx 4.08\%$. (Treatment is worse).
In both segments, the treatment conversion rate is lower than the control. However, the pooled reported result claims a lift (8.469% vs 8.0%). This reversal occurs because the treatment arm is overloaded with high-converting desktop users, artificially inflating the aggregate average despite the feature performing worse in every segment.

**NOVELTY:** The daily relative lift drops from 18.7% on day 1 to -0.0% by day 6. The team is projecting the current aggregate lift (driven by early days) over twelve months, ignoring the clear fading trend indicative of a novelty effect.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
