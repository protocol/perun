**SRM**: The total users are 15,768 (control) and 15,678 (treatment). Total $N = 31,446$. Expected per arm is 15,723. The difference is 90 users. The standard error for the split is $\sqrt{N \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.5} \approx 88$. A deviation of 90 is roughly 1.02 standard errors, which is well within the range of chance ($p > 0.05$). No SRM.

**PEEK**: The test started 2026-08-29 and ended 2026-09-27. This is exactly 29 days inclusive (or 28 full days depending on timezones), matching the "planned duration 28 days." There is no evidence of early stopping based on the dates provided. No PEEK.

**POWER**: The reported p-value is 0.3484, which is not significant. However, "underpowered" specifically means the sample size was insufficient to detect the *target* lift if it existed. With ~15k users per arm and a 3% baseline, the detectable lift at 80% power is typically around 10-12%. The study observed a 6.1% lift. Failing to find significance for a 6% lift when targeting 10% does not prove the study was underpowered for its *target*. It simply means the effect wasn't there or was smaller than targeted. No POWER error flagged strictly by numbers.

**SIMPSON**: Let's check the segment conversion rates.
Desktop Control: $323 / 6,954 \approx 4.64\%$. Desktop Treatment: $401 / 9,385 \approx 4.27\%$. (Treatment is lower).
Mobile Control: $150 / 8,814 \approx 1.70\%$. Mobile Treatment: $98 / 6,293 \approx 1.56\%$. (Treatment is lower).
In both segments, Treatment < Control.
Yet, the pooled result reports Treatment (3.183%) > Control (3.0%).
Since the direction reverses when aggregating, this is Simpson's Paradox. The imbalance in device mix (Treatment has more Desktop users, who convert higher generally) is driving the false positive aggregate. **SIMPSON** flagged.

**DENOM**: Randomized by user id. Metric is order conversion rate. The readout implies orders/users. $473/15768 = 3.00\%$. $499/15678 = 3.18\%$. The denominator matches the randomization unit. No DENOM.

**NOVELTY**: The daily lift drops from 15.8% to 1.3% over the month. The team projects this fading lift for 12 months. This assumes the initial high lift persists or averages out, ignoring the clear decay trend. **NOVELTY** flagged.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
