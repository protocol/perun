**SRM Check**: The total users are 61,691 (Control) + 59,599 (Treatment) = 121,290. Expected per arm is 60,645. The difference is roughly 1,046 users. Using a chi-square approximation: $\chi^2 \approx \frac{(61691-60645)^2}{60645} + \frac{(59599-60645)^2}{60645} \approx 18 + 18 = 36$. With 1 degree of freedom, a value > 3.84 indicates significant mismatch at p<0.05. 36 is far greater, indicating a Sample Ratio Mismatch.

**PEEK Check**: The test started Feb 9 and ended Feb 22. Duration = 14 days (inclusive). Planned duration was 28 days. The test was stopped exactly halfway through the planned timeline. Stopping early based on interim results inflates false positive rates. This is Peeking.

**POWER Check**: We assess if the sample size supports detecting the target lift. Baseline = 5%, Target Lift = 10% (absolute change 0.5%). Approximate sample size per arm needed $n \approx \frac{16 \cdot p(1-p)}{\Delta^2}$. Here $p=0.05$, $\Delta=0.005$. $n \approx \frac{16 \cdot 0.05 \cdot 0.95}{0.000025} = \frac{0.76}{0.000025} = 30,400$. Both arms have >59,000 users. The study is adequately powered; this error is not present.

**SIMPSON Check**:
Control Desktop Rate: $2180/26217 \approx 8.31\%$. Treatment Desktop: $2206/25328 \approx 8.71\%$. (Lift positive).
Control Mobile Rate: $905/35474 \approx 2.55\%$. Treatment Mobile: $916/34271 \approx 2.67\%$. (Lift positive).
Both segments show a positive lift, consistent with the pooled result. No Simpson's Paradox.

**DENOM Check**: Randomization was by user ID. The metric is order conversion rate (Orders/Users). The denominator matches the randomization unit. No error.

**NOVELTY Check**: The daily lift values (4.5 to 5.0) fluctuate but do not show a clear fading trend (e.g., starting high and dropping to near zero). The projection assumes the current average holds, but the data does not explicitly show a "fading" pattern characteristic of novelty effects wearing off within this short window. No clear error based strictly on the provided numbers.

The readout contains critical flaws: the user allocation is statistically broken (SRM), invalidating the comparison, and the test was stopped early (PEEK), invalidating the p-value.
