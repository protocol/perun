**SRM Check**: Total users = 42,259 (Control) + 42,525 (Treatment) = 84,784. Expected per arm = 42,392.
Difference = |42,259 - 42,392| = 133.
Standard Deviation ≈ $\sqrt{84784 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 145.6$.
The difference (133) is less than 2 standard deviations (~291). The split is within chance expectations. No SRM.

**PEEK Check**: Planned duration was 14 days (Aug 3 to Aug 17). The readout date is Aug 18, and daily data is provided for 15 days. The test ran past its planned duration, not stopped early. No PEEK.

**POWER Check**: Baseline 4.0%, target lift 8% (to 4.32%). Alpha 0.05, Power 80%.
Required sample size per arm ≈ $16 \times p(1-p) / (p_{diff})^2$.
$p \approx 0.04$, $p_{diff} = 0.0032$.
$n \approx 16 \times 0.04 \times 0.96 / (0.0032)^2 \approx 0.6144 / 0.00001024 \approx 60,000$.
Actual users per arm (~42,400) are significantly lower than the ~60k required to detect an 8% lift with 80% power. The test is underpowered to detect the *target* lift, though it detected a smaller 5.1% lift with low significance (p=0.136). However, the error definition specifies "too small to detect the target lift". Since $42,400 < 60,000$, this fits the POWER definition strictly regarding the design parameters.

**NOVELTY Check**: Daily lift values: 5.5, 5.9, 5.3, 4.7, 4.6, 5.3, 5.7, 4.7, 3.7, 5.5, 5.7, 4.5, 4.6, 4.8, 6.0.
The values fluctuate but do not show a consistent fading trend (e.g., starting high and dropping to near zero). The final days are not lower than the start. No NOVELTY.

The primary issue is POWER. The test was designed to detect an 8% lift but lacks the sample size to do so with the stated power. While a 5.1% lift was observed, it is not statistically significant (p=0.136), and the test was not sized to validate this smaller effect reliably. Rolling out based on an underpowered test with a non-significant result is risky.
