First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 28,009 (control) + 28,269 (treatment) = 56,278. The expected split is 28,139 per arm. The difference is 130 users. The standard error for a 50/50 split is $\sqrt{56278 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 118.6$. The deviation is $130 / 118.6 \approx 1.1$ standard deviations, which is well within the range of chance (typically < 1.96 for 95% confidence). SRM is not present.

Next, I check for Denominator errors (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate." The randomization unit is "user id." The counts provided for the metric calculation appear to be Orders / Users (e.g., $1,121 / 28,009 \approx 4.002\%$). Since the denominator matches the randomization unit, there is no DENOM error.

I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON).

Mobile lift: Control $264/14091 \approx 1.87\%$, Treatment $284/14222 \approx 2.00\%$. Lift is positive.
Since both segments show a positive lift and the aggregate shows a positive lift, the direction is consistent. No SIMPSON error.

I check for Power (POWER). The baseline is 4.0%, target lift 8%. The sample size per arm is ~28,000. Standard power calculators for these parameters typically require ~20,000–25,000 users per arm to detect an 8% lift with 80% power. With ~28,000, the study is adequately powered. No POWER error.

I check for Peeking (PEEK). The test started June 22 and ended July 20. This is exactly 28 days, matching the planned duration. There is no evidence of early stopping. No PEEK error.

I check for Novelty (NOVELTY). The daily relative lift starts at 13.8% on Day 1 and trends downward consistently to roughly 2.9% by Day 28. The reported aggregate lift is 6.5%. However, the recommendation projects this 6.5% lift for the next twelve months. Given the clear decay trend (from ~14% to ~3%), the aggregate average is inflated by early novelty effects that are already fading. Projecting the current average as a sustained long-term lift ignores this decay, constituting a Novelty error.
