First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 44,288 (control) + 45,356 (treatment) = 89,644. The expected split is 50%, so 44,822 per arm. The treatment has 534 more users than expected. The standard deviation for a binomial split is $\sqrt{89644 \times 0.5 \times 0.5} \approx 150$. The difference is $534 / 150 \approx 3.56$ standard deviations. This exceeds the typical threshold of 2 (or even 3) sigma, indicating a significant deviation from the 50/50 plan. SRM is present.

Next, I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON). I calculate the conversion rates per device segment:
Desktop Control: $1,536 / 15,518 \approx 9.90\%$.
Desktop Treatment: $1,747 / 18,183 \approx 9.61\%$. (Treatment is lower).
Mobile Control: $1,122 / 28,770 \approx 3.90\%$.
Mobile Treatment: $1,028 / 27,173 \approx 3.78\%$. (Treatment is lower).
In both segments, the treatment performs worse than the control. However, the aggregated reported result claims the treatment (6.118%) is higher than the control (6.002%). Since the direction of the effect reverses when pooling the data compared to the segments, Simpson's Paradox is present. This is likely driven by the SRM shifting more high-converting desktop users into the treatment arm.

Regarding Power (POWER): The test ran for 28 days as planned, but the effect size observed (1.9%) is far below the target (8%). While the test might be underpowered to detect such a small lift, the primary errors here are the data integrity issues (SRM) and the aggregation artifact (Simpson). The p-value (0.46) correctly indicates non-significance for the observed lift, so claiming a win based on power alone isn't the primary error; the data itself is corrupted.

The other categories (PEEK, DENOM, NOVELTY) do not show definitive proof in the numbers provided. The duration matches the plan. The denominator (users) matches the randomization unit. While daily lift fades, the team projects the *current* reported lift, not the initial high lift, so NOVELTY is less clear than the structural errors.

The presence of SRM invalidates the randomization, and Simpson's Paradox confirms the aggregate metric is misleading. The recommendation to roll out is unsupported.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
