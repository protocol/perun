First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 8,581 (control) + 8,451 (treatment) = 17,032. The expected split is 8,516 per arm. The observed difference is 65 users. Using a quick chi-square approximation: $\chi^2 \approx \frac{(65)^2}{8516} + \frac{(-65)^2}{8516} \approx 0.99$. With 1 degree of freedom, the critical value at $\alpha=0.05$ is 3.84. Since $0.99 < 3.84$, the split is within chance expectations. No SRM.

Next, I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON). I calculate the conversion rates per device:
Desktop Control: $464 / 3,736 \approx 12.42\%$. Desktop Treatment: $571 / 5,005 \approx 11.41\%$. (Treatment is lower).
Mobile Control: $222 / 4,845 \approx 4.58\%$. Mobile Treatment: $145 / 3,446 \approx 4.21\%$. (Treatment is lower).
In both segments, the treatment performs worse than the control. However, the reported pooled data shows Treatment (8.47%) beating Control (7.99%). The direction reverses when aggregating. This is a clear case of Simpson's Paradox, likely driven by the treatment arm having a much higher proportion of high-converting desktop users (59% vs 44% in control).

I check Power (POWER). The target lift was 8% on an 8% baseline (to 8.64%). The observed lift is 6%. While the result is not significant ($p=0.2565$), the sample size (~8.5k per arm) is generally sufficient to detect an 8% lift given the baseline. The lack of significance is likely due to the true effect being smaller than the target or negative (as seen in segments), not necessarily an underpowered design relative to the *planned* effect. The numbers don't definitively prove the design was underpowered for the *target*, only that the *observed* effect wasn't detected.

I check Denominator (DENOM). Randomization was by user ID. The metric is order conversion rate (orders/users). The denominator matches the randomization unit. No error.

I check Novelty (NOVELTY) and Peek (PEEK). The daily lift fluctuates but doesn't show a clear fading trend (starts 5.9%, ends 5.4%, with peaks later). The test ran for 21 days as planned (March 6 to March 27 is 21 days inclusive). No obvious peeking or novelty decay evident from the numbers alone.

The critical error is SIMPSON. Aggregating the data masks that the treatment actually reduced conversion rates in both desktop and mobile segments individually. Rolling this out based on the pooled number would be a mistake.

ERRORS: SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
