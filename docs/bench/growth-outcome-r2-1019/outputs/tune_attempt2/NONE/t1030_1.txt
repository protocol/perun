First, I check for Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The total users are 39,472 (control) + 39,392 (treatment) = 78,864. The expected split is 39,432 per arm. The difference is only 40 users. A standard chi-square check: $(40^2 / 39432) + (40^2 / 39432) \approx 0.08$. This is far below the critical value of 3.84 for p=0.05. No SRM.

Next, I check the denominator (DENOM). The metric is "order conversion rate." The readout provides orders and users. Calculating the rates: Control $2,369 / 39,472 \approx 6.002\%$. Treatment $2,502 / 39,392 \approx 6.352\%$. These match the reported percentages exactly. The denominator is users, which matches the randomization unit. No DENOM error.

I check for Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON) by calculating segment lifts.
Desktop Control: $1,630 / 17,143 \approx 9.51\%$. Desktop Treatment: $1,722 / 17,108 \approx 10.07\%$. Lift is positive.
Mobile Control: $739 / 22,329 \approx 3.31\%$. Mobile Treatment: $780 / 22,284 \approx 3.50\%$. Lift is positive.
Since both segments show a positive lift consistent with the aggregate, there is no reversal. No SIMPSON error.

I check for Power (POWER). The smaller arm has ~39k users. With a 6% baseline and 10% target lift, standard calculators indicate ~27k users per arm are needed for 80% power at alpha 0.05. Since 39k > 27k, the test is adequately powered. No POWER error.

I check for Peeking (PEEK). The planned duration was 21 days. The readout date is Nov 8, and the start was Oct 28. This is 11 days of data. The test was stopped and analyzed at day 11, well before the planned 21 days. Stopping early based on significance inflates false positives. This is a clear PEEK error.

I check for Novelty (NOVELTY). The daily lift drops from 13.6% on day 1 to 1.6% on day 11. This is a sharp, consistent decay. However, the recommendation projects the current reported lift (5.8%) over the next twelve months. Ignoring the fading trend to project a static long-term lift constitutes a NOVELTY error.

ERRORS: PEEK, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
