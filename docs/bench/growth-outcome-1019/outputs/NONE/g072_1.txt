The readout indicates multiple critical flaws. First, although the total arm sizes (50,080 vs 49,920) appear balanced, the analysis explicitly states "each arm has 120 users," which contradicts the total counts and suggests a severe data inconsistency or logging error potentially related to SRM, but more clearly points to POWER issues given the admission that "nobody sized the test for the lift it reports." The small effective sample size (120 users) renders the test underpowered for reliable detection of the reported effects.

Second, the team "stopped the test the first day p fell below 0.05" after checking daily. This is a classic case of PEEKING, which invalidates the p-value and inflates the false positive rate.

Third, the forecast relies on the day-one lift of 12%, ignoring the drop to 1% by day fourteen. This indicates a NOVELTY effect that is being incorrectly projected as a lasting benefit.

While the readout confirms the denominator matches the randomized unit and Simpson's paradox is not present (treatment leads in all segments), the combination of peeking, underpowering, and misinterpreting a novelty effect makes the current recommendation unsafe.

ERRORS: PEEK, POWER, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
