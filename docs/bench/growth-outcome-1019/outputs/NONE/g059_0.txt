The readout indicates a Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The planned split was 50/50, yet the arms received 52,300 and 47,700 users respectively. This deviation (roughly 52.3% vs 47.7%) suggests the assignment or logging mechanism may be broken, violating the assumption of randomization required for valid causal inference.

Additionally, the readout describes a PEEKing error. The team explicitly states they "checked the dashboard every day and stopped the test the first day p fell below 0.05." Stopping an experiment based on repeated interim significance checks without statistical correction inflates the false positive rate, rendering the reported p-value and confidence in the lift invalid.

While the readout claims the denominator matches the randomized unit (ruling out DENOM), notes stability over four weeks (ruling out NOVELTY), and shows consistent direction across platforms (ruling out SIMPSON), the presence of SRM and PEEKing fundamentally compromises the validity of the results. The mention of a power calculation addresses sample size targets but does not negate the error introduced by early stopping based on uncorrected daily looks.

Because the randomization integrity is questionable and the statistical significance is likely inflated due to optional stopping, the current results cannot support a rollout decision.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK
DECISION: HOLD
