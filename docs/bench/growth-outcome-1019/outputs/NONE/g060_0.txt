The readout reveals multiple critical flaws preventing a safe rollout. First, while overall results favor the treatment, both mobile and desktop segments individually show the treatment losing. This reversal between aggregated and segmented data indicates **SIMPSON**'s paradox, suggesting the overall win is misleading.

Second, the metric definition is flawed. Conversions are calculated per session, but randomization occurred at the user level. Since heavy users generate many sessions, this violates the unit of analysis, triggering a **DENOM** error that likely biases the variance and significance.

Third, the forecast relies entirely on the day-one lift of 12%, ignoring the drop to 1% by day fourteen. Projecting this initial spike as a permanent effect ignores the **NOVELTY** effect, where users react temporarily to change rather than deriving lasting value.

Although the sample sizes (50,080 vs 49,920) appear close to the 50/50 split and the study reached its power target without early stopping, the presence of Simpson's paradox, the denominator mismatch, and the novelty bias make the current recommendation unsafe. These issues must be resolved to understand the true impact before exposing all users to a change that performs worse in every specific segment.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
