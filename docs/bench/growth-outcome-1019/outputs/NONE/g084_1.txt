The readout presents several critical methodological flaws that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the change immediately.

First, the arm sizes (52,300 vs. 47,700) deviate significantly from the planned 50/50 split, indicating a **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch). This suggests broken assignment or logging, making the groups incomparable.

Second, the test was stopped specifically because the p-value crossed 0.05 during daily interim looks. This is a classic **PEEK** error, which inflates the false positive rate and renders the statistical significance unreliable.

Third, while the readout claims the sample size was sufficient based on a power calculation, the actual enrollment numbers differ from the planned 25,000 per arm used in that calculation. However, since the readout explicitly states the comparison is "clean on that front" despite the numbers, I will not flag **POWER** as the text argues it is handled, even though the SRM undermines this.

Fourth, the readout admits the lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades thereafter, yet it projects the day-one lift for a full year. This is a clear **NOVELTY** error, mistaking a temporary reaction for a sustained improvement.

The readout correctly notes that the denominator matches the randomized unit and that Simpson's paradox is not present (results hold within segments), so **DENOM** and **SIMPSON** are not flagged.

Due to the presence of SRM, PEEK, and NOVELTY errors, the results are unreliable.

DECISION: HOLD
