The readout indicates two critical methodological flaws. First, there is a **NOVELTY** error: the lift dropped sharply from 12% on day one to 1% by day fourteen, yet the forecast for the rollout relies on the initial 12% spike rather than the stabilized rate. This suggests the effect is a short-lived novelty that will not persist. Second, there is a **DENOM** error: the metric divides conversions by sessions, but randomization occurred at the user level. Since heavy users generate multiple sessions, this mismatch biases the results and violates the assumption of independent observations.

Other categories are explicitly ruled out in the text. SRM is not present as the split (50,200 vs 50,100) is consistent with chance. PEEK is excluded because there was only a single analysis at the planned end date. POWER is sufficient as the sample size matched the pre-calculated requirement. SIMPSON is not an issue because the treatment led in both the pooled total and within every platform segment.

Because the forecast overstates the benefit due to novelty and the metric definition is statistically invalid, the recommendation cannot be accepted as is.

ERRORS: NOVELTY, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
