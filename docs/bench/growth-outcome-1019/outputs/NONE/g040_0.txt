The readout presents several critical issues preventing a safe rollout. First, while the text claims each arm got 25,000 users, it simultaneously states the arms received 52,300 and 47,700 users against a planned 50/50 split. This massive discrepancy between reported counts and the planned ratio indicates a **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch), suggesting broken assignment or logging.

Second, the overall result shows the treatment winning, yet it loses within both the mobile and desktop segments. This is a classic case of **SIMPSON** (Simpson's Paradox), where aggregated data masks negative performance across all specific user groups, making the overall "win" misleading.

Third, the forecast relies on the 12% lift observed on day one, ignoring the drop to 1% by day fourteen. Projecting this initial spike as a lasting effect without accounting for the rapid decay indicates a **NOVELTY** error, as the lift appears to be a short-lived reaction to change rather than a sustainable improvement.

Although the readout mentions the analysis occurred at the planned end date (avoiding PEEK) and the denominator matches the randomization unit (avoiding DENOM), the presence of SRM, Simpson's paradox, and novelty effects makes the current recommendation invalid.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
