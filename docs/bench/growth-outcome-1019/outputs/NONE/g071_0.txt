The readout presents several critical analytical errors that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the feature immediately.

First, there is a clear **DENOM** error. The metric divides conversions by sessions, yet randomization occurred at the user level. Since heavy users generate many sessions, this mismatch biases the results and violates the unit of randomization.

Second, the readout exhibits a **NOVELTY** error. It explicitly states the lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades thereafter, yet the team projects this short-term spike as a lasting annual benefit. This overstates the long-term value of the change.

Other categories appear handled: the split (50,200 vs 50,100) is close enough to rule out **SRM**; the single analysis at the planned end date avoids **PEEK**; the sample size matches the power calculation, addressing **POWER**; and consistent results across platforms suggest no **SIMPSON** paradox.

However, the denominator mismatch and the unjustified projection of a novelty effect are fatal flaws. The team must reanalyze the data using the correct denominator (users) and adjust projections to reflect the decaying lift before making a rollout decision.

ERRORS: DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
