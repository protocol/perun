The readout presents multiple critical flaws preventing a safe rollout. First, the arm sizes (52,300 vs. 47,700) deviate significantly from the planned 50/50 split despite the claim that each arm got 25,000 users for power; this contradiction and the actual imbalance indicate **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch), suggesting broken assignment or logging. Second, the overall result shows a win for the treatment, yet both mobile and desktop segments individually show a loss. This is a classic case of **SIMPSON** (Simpson's Paradox), where aggregated data masks negative performance in all underlying groups, making the overall "win" misleading. Third, the forecast relies on the 12% lift observed on day one, ignoring the drop to 1% by day fourteen. Projecting the initial spike as a lasting effect indicates **NOVELTY**, as the benefit appears to be a short-lived reaction rather than a sustainable improvement.

Because the aggregated win is an artifact of Simpson's Paradox hiding segment-level losses, and the forecast ignores the decay of the effect, the recommendation to roll out is unsupported. The SRM further casts doubt on the validity of the assignment mechanism itself.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
