The readout presents two critical statistical errors. First, although the arm sizes (50,080 vs. 49,920) appear close to the planned 50/50 split, the results exhibit **SIMPSON**'s paradox: the treatment wins overall but loses within both the mobile and desktop segments. This reversal indicates that pooling the data hides the true negative effect across all user types, making the aggregate lift misleading.

Second, the test was stopped early based on daily looks when the p-value first crossed 0.05. This constitutes **PEEK**ing, which inflates the false positive rate and invalidates the reported significance. While the sample size met the power requirement and the denominator matches the randomized unit, the combination of a reversed trend in segments and early stopping based on interim analysis renders the current conclusion unreliable. The novelty effect is explicitly ruled out by the stability over four weeks, and SRM is not indicated as the split deviation is likely within chance limits given the large N, but the other two errors are fatal to the decision.

ERRORS: PEEK, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
