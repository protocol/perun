The readout presents two critical methodological flaws. First, it explicitly states that "treatment wins" overall but "loses within both the mobile segment and the desktop segment." This is a textbook description of **SIMPSON**'s paradox, where aggregated data masks contradictory trends in subgroups, making the overall lift misleading.

Second, the metric definition is incorrect relative to the experimental design. The text notes that "users were the unit randomized," yet "conversion is computed per page view." This constitutes a **DENOM** error, as the denominator (page views) does not match the unit of randomization (users), violating the assumptions of standard statistical tests and invalidating the p-values.

Other categories are explicitly ruled out by the text: the arm sizes (50,080 vs 49,920) are close enough to rule out **SRM**; the analysis occurred once at the planned end date, avoiding **PEEK**; the sample size exceeded the power calculation, avoiding **POWER**; and the lift remained stable for 28 days, suggesting **NOVELTY** is not the primary driver.

Because the results are driven by Simpson's paradox and an incorrect metric denominator, the claimed lift is unreliable.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
