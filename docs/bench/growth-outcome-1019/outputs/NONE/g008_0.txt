The readout reveals multiple critical flaws preventing a safe rollout decision. First, the test was stopped early based on daily interim looks once significance was reached, which is a classic **PEEK** error that inflates false positive rates. Second, while the total sample size matches the power calculation, the metric definition is incorrect: conversion is computed per page view, but users were the unit of randomization. This **DENOM** error violates the alignment between the randomized unit and the analysis unit, invalidating the statistical inference. Third, the forecast relies on the day-one lift of 12% while ignoring the drop to 1% by day fourteen, indicating a **NOVELTY** effect that is incorrectly projected as a lasting benefit.

Although the arm sizes (50,080 vs 49,920) are close to the 50/50 split and likely within chance variation, and the results are consistent across mobile and desktop segments (ruling out SRM and SIMPSON), the presence of peeking, denominator mismatch, and novelty bias makes the current recommendation unsafe. These issues must be resolved before proceeding.
