The readout indicates two critical methodological errors. First, there is a **DENOM** error: the analysis explicitly states that "users were the unit randomized," yet the primary metric (add-on conversion) was "computed per page view." Using a denominator different from the randomization unit violates the independence assumption required for standard statistical tests, invalidating the p-values and confidence intervals.

Second, there is a **POWER** issue. The readout admits that "only 120 users were enrolled per arm" and "no power calculation was done." While the team claims the effect is "directionally strong," a sample size of 240 total users is generally insufficient to detect stable conversion lifts with adequate statistical power, making the result highly susceptible to noise and false positives. The claim of consistency over 28 days does not compensate for the fundamental lack of sample size relative to the variance inherent in conversion metrics.

Although the readout confirms the stopping rule was pre-set (avoiding PEEK), the split is balanced (avoiding SRM), and segment trends align with the total (avoiding SIMPSON), the denominator mismatch and severe underpowering render the statistical evidence unreliable for a rollout decision.

ERRORS: DENOM, POWER
DECISION: HOLD
