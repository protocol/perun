The readout explicitly identifies three critical analysis errors. First, **SRM** is present because the observed split (60,400 control vs. 50,100 treatment) deviates significantly from the planned even split, indicating broken assignment or logging. Second, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is occurring: while the pooled data shows a lift, the treatment actually trails within every platform segment; the aggregate gain is an artifact of the treatment group receiving disproportionately more high-converting desktop traffic. Third, **DENOM** is violated because the conversion metric is calculated per page view, whereas the randomization unit was the user, creating a mismatch that biases the results.

The readout confirms that other potential issues are handled: the sample size exceeded the power calculation requirements (not **POWER**), the analysis occurred only once at the planned end date (not **PEEK**), and the lift remained stable over four weeks without fading (not **NOVELTY**). However, the presence of sample ratio mismatch, Simpson's paradox, and denominator errors renders the current +6.2% lift claim unreliable. These fundamental flaws in data integrity and aggregation must be resolved before any business decision can be made.
