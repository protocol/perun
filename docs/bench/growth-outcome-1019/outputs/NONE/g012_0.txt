The readout presents multiple critical flaws preventing a rollout decision. First, the traffic split is severely skewed (60,400 vs. 50,100) despite a planned even split, indicating **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch), which suggests broken assignment or logging. Second, the aggregated result shows a lift while every individual platform segment shows a decline; this is a textbook case of **SIMPSON** (Simpson's Paradox), driven here by an imbalance in high-converting desktop traffic. Third, the metric divides conversions by sessions while randomization occurred at the user level. Since heavy users generate many sessions, this creates **DENOM** (denominator mismatch), violating the unit of randomization.

While the readout confirms adequate sample size (**POWER** is handled) and a fixed stopping rule (**PEEK** is handled), and the sustained lift over 28 days argues against a **NOVELTY** effect, the presence of SRM, Simpson's Paradox, and denominator errors renders the 6.8% lift statistic invalid. The observed "lift" is likely an artifact of traffic composition and metric definition rather than a true treatment effect. These issues must be investigated and corrected before any business decision can be made.

DECISION: HOLD
