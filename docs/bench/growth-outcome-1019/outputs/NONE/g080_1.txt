The readout reveals multiple critical flaws preventing a safe rollout. First, although the sample sizes (50,200 vs 50,100) appear balanced, the team explicitly stopped the test the first day the p-value dropped below 0.05 after checking daily. This constitutes **PEEK**, as repeated interim looks without correction inflate false positive rates.

Second, the forecast relies entirely on the 12% lift observed on day one, ignoring the drop to 1% by day fourteen. Projecting the initial spike as a lasting effect indicates a **NOVELTY** error, where short-term user curiosity is mistaken for sustained value.

Third, while the overall result favors the treatment, the readout states that the treatment loses within both the mobile and desktop segments. This is a classic case of **SIMPSON**'s paradox, where the aggregated data misleadingly reverses the trend found in every subgroup, suggesting the overall "win" is a statistical artifact rather than a real effect.

Finally, the power calculation required 25,000 users per arm, and the test exceeded this, so **POWER** is not an issue. The denominator matches the randomization unit, ruling out **DENOM**. The split is close enough to expected that **SRM** is not indicated by the text. However, the presence of peeking, novelty bias, and Simpson's paradox makes the current conclusion invalid.

ERRORS: PEEK, NOVELTY, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
