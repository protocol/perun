The readout indicates a clear Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). While the planned split was 50/50 with 25,000 users per arm, the actual assignment resulted in 50,080 and 49,920 users. Although the absolute difference seems small, the text explicitly contrasts the "planned" numbers with the "received" numbers, and the specific counts (totaling 100,000) suggest a deviation from the intended equal allocation that warrants investigation into assignment or logging integrity.

More critically, the readout describes a PEEKing error. It states the team "checked the dashboard every day and stopped the test the first day p fell below 0.05." Stopping a test based on interim looks without statistical correction inflates the false positive rate, making the reported +4.2% lift unreliable.

Regarding other categories: POWER is not an issue as the sample size matched the power calculation. SIMPSON's paradox is ruled out because the treatment won both overall and within segments. DENOM is correct as conversion is computed per randomized user. NOVELTY is addressed by the stability of the lift over four weeks.

Because the PEEKing error fundamentally invalidates the statistical significance of the result, and the SRM suggests potential data integrity issues, the rollout cannot be approved based on this data alone.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK
DECISION: HOLD
