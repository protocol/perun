The readout presents multiple critical flaws preventing a rollout decision. First, the traffic split is severely skewed (60,400 vs. 50,100) despite a planned even split, indicating **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch), which suggests broken assignment or logging. Second, the aggregate result shows a lift while every individual segment trails, a classic case of **SIMPSON** (Simpson's Paradox) driven by uneven traffic composition across platforms; the pooled positive result is misleading. Third, the metric divides conversions by sessions while randomization occurred at the user level (**DENOM**). This mismatch inflates significance because heavy users contribute disproportionately more data points than light users, violating the independence assumption of the test.

Although the readout confirms adequate sample size and proper stopping rules (ruling out POWER and PEEK), and the sustained lift over 28 days mitigates NOVELTY concerns, the presence of SRM, Simpson's Paradox, and denominator mismatch renders the reported 6.8% lift unreliable. The negative performance within every specific platform segment strongly suggests the change may actually be harmful overall, masked only by the confounding variable of device type. These errors must be investigated and corrected before any business decision can be made.
