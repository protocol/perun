The readout reveals several critical analytical errors. First, while the sample sizes are nearly equal (50,200 vs 50,100), the primary metric uses page views as the denominator despite users being the randomized unit, violating the unit of analysis (DENOM). Second, the pooled result shows a lift, but the treatment actually trails within every platform segment; the aggregate gain is driven entirely by an imbalance in traffic composition (more high-converting desktop users in the treatment arm), which is a classic case of Simpson's paradox (SIMPSON). Third, the observed lift is explicitly described as concentrated in the first three days and fading thereafter, yet the team projects this short-term spike as a permanent annual gain, indicating a novelty effect error (NOVELTY).

Although the test avoided sample ratio mismatch (SRM), peeking (PEEK), and underpowering (POWER), the presence of DENOM, SIMPSON, and NOVELTY errors invalidates the claimed +8.3% lift and the financial projection. Rolling out based on these flawed metrics risks implementing a change that performs worse across all user segments and provides no long-term value.

ERRORS: DENOM, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
