The readout presents multiple critical flaws preventing a safe rollout. First, while arm sizes are nearly equal, the result exhibits **SIMPSON**'s paradox: the treatment leads in the pooled total only because it attracted more high-converting desktop traffic, yet it actually trails within every specific platform segment. Aggregating these results masks the negative performance across all individual groups.

Second, the analysis suffers from **NOVELTY**. The lift is explicitly described as concentrated in the first three days and fading thereafter, yet the recommendation projects this transient day-one lift over a full year. This overestimates long-term value.

Third, the test is severely **POWER** constrained. With only 120 users per arm and no prior power calculation, the sample size is likely insufficient to detect meaningful effects or ensure the observed differences are not due to random noise, despite the large percentage lift claimed.

The readout confirms that **DENOM** is handled correctly (users randomized and used as the denominator) and **PEEK** was avoided (stopped at the planned date). However, the combination of Simpson's paradox, unverified novelty effects, and inadequate statistical power makes the current recommendation unreliable.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, NOVELTY, POWER
DECISION: HOLD
