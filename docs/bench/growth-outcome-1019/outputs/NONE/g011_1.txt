The readout presents two critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the change.

First, there is a clear **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch). The test planned for an even split, yet the results show 60,400 users in control versus only 50,100 in treatment. This significant deviation suggests a broken assignment or logging mechanism, meaning the groups are not comparable.

Second, the data exhibits **SIMPSON** (Simpson's Paradox). While the aggregated result shows a +3.1% lift for the treatment, the readout explicitly states that the treatment loses within both the mobile and desktop segments. When a trend appears in different groups of data but disappears or reverses when these groups are combined, the overall "win" is a statistical artifact and not a real effect.

Although the readout confirms adequate power, correct denominators, no peeking, and stable lifts over time (addressing POWER, DENOM, PEEK, and NOVELTY), the presence of SRM and Simpson's paradox makes the primary result unreliable. You cannot trust the aggregate lift when the underlying segments all performed worse, especially when the sample sizes are suspect.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
