The readout reveals multiple critical flaws. First, the arm sizes (52,300 vs. 47,700) deviate significantly from the planned 50/50 split, indicating **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch), which suggests broken assignment or logging. Second, the metric definition divides conversions by sessions while randomization occurred at the user level. Since heavy users generate many sessions, this creates a denominator mismatch (**DENOM**), biasing the results toward high-frequency users rather than reflecting the true impact per randomized unit. Third, the forecast relies on the 12% lift observed on day one, ignoring the drop to 1% by day fourteen. This projects a short-lived **NOVELTY** effect as a permanent gain, leading to an inflated expectation of value.

Although the test avoided early stopping (no PEEK), achieved sufficient sample size (no POWER issue), and showed consistent direction across segments (no SIMPSON), the presence of SRM, DENOM, and NOVELTY errors invalidates the current recommendation. The SRM alone questions the integrity of the experiment, while the denominator mismatch and novelty projection render the effect size estimate unreliable. These issues must be resolved before any rollout decision can be made.
