The readout explicitly identifies several critical flaws in the analysis. First, while the sample sizes are balanced, the metric denominator is page views while the randomization unit was users, violating the unit of analysis (DENOM). Second, the pooled result shows a treatment lead, but the treatment actually trails within every platform segment; the aggregate lift is an artifact of the treatment group receiving disproportionately more high-converting desktop traffic, a classic case of Simpson's paradox (SIMPSON). Third, the observed lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades subsequently, yet the projection assumes this initial spike will persist for a full year, indicating an unaddressed novelty effect (NOVELTY).

The readout confirms that sample ratio mismatch (SRM), peeking (PEEK), and power (POWER) issues were anticipated and handled correctly, so those are not flagged. However, the presence of DENOM, SIMPSON, and NOVELTY errors renders the current recommendation to roll out invalid. The projected long-term value is unsupported, and the direction of the effect is ambiguous due to the paradox.

ERRORS: DENOM, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
