The readout presents multiple critical flaws preventing a safe rollout decision. First, the traffic allocation is severely skewed: Control received 60,400 visitors while Treatment received only 50,100, despite a planned even split. This indicates a Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM), suggesting broken assignment or logging. Second, the team explicitly stopped the test the moment significance was reached ("first day p fell below 0.05"), which is a classic case of Peeking (PEEK) that invalidates the p-value by inflating false positive rates. Third, the readout admits "nobody sized the test for the lift it reports," confirming the study is Underpowered (POWER) and the results may be due to noise rather than a true effect.

While the readout notes stability over four weeks (addressing Novelty) and consistent results across platforms (addressing Simpson's paradox), the presence of SRM, PEEK, and POWER errors renders the statistical claims unreliable. The metric denominator correctly matches the randomization unit, so DENOM is not an issue. However, the combination of broken randomization, early stopping, and insufficient sample size means the reported 12.4% lift cannot be trusted.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK, POWER
DECISION: HOLD
