The readout indicates a Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The planned split was 50/50, yet the observed arms received 52,300 and 47,700 users respectively. This deviation (approximately 52.3% vs 47.7%) suggests a potential issue with assignment or logging that exceeds what chance typically allows, violating the assumption of equal probability assignment.

Additionally, the readout describes a Peeking (PEEK) error. The team explicitly states they "checked the dashboard every day and stopped the test the first day p fell below 0.05." Conducting repeated significance tests and stopping immediately upon crossing a threshold without statistical correction inflates the false positive rate, rendering the reported p-value and confidence invalid.

While the readout claims the sample size met power requirements and the denominator matches the randomized unit, the presence of SRM and PEEK fundamentally compromises the validity of the results. The stability over four weeks addresses novelty concerns, and consistent lifts across platforms mitigate Simpson's paradox risks, but the methodological errors in data collection and stopping rules are critical.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK
DECISION: HOLD
