The readout presents several critical statistical flaws preventing a safe rollout. First, while the user split (50,080 vs 49,920) appears balanced, the analysis suffers from **DENOM** because conversion is computed per page view despite users being the randomized unit. This mismatch invalidates the variance calculations and p-values.

Second, the results exhibit **SIMPSON** paradox: the overall treatment wins, yet the treatment loses within both the mobile and desktop segments. Aggregating these losing segments to find a winner indicates a severe confounding variable or data aggregation error that makes the overall lift unreliable.

Third, the test is severely **POWER** compromised. The readout explicitly states, "Each arm has 120 users and nobody sized the test for the lift it reports." This sample size is incredibly small compared to the user counts mentioned earlier (implying a data reporting contradiction or a specific subset analysis that was not power-calculated), rendering the reported 3.1% lift statistically untrustworthy.

Although the stopping rule was pre-defined (avoiding PEEK) and the lift was stable over 28 days (mitigating NOVELTY concerns), the presence of denominator mismatch, Simpson's paradox, and lack of power makes the results invalid.

ERRORS: DENOM, SIMPSON, POWER
DECISION: HOLD
