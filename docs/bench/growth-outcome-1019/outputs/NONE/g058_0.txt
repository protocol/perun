The readout presents two critical issues preventing a safe rollout. First, while the test was properly powered, stopped according to plan, and randomized correctly (ruling out POWER, PEEK, DENOM, and SRM), the results show a clear **NOVELTY** error. The data explicitly states that the lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades thereafter, yet the recommendation projects the initial day-one lift across a full year. This overestimates the long-term value of the feature.

Second, there is a potential **SIMPSON** paradox risk flagged by the description of the segments. Although the treatment wins overall and within both mobile and desktop segments individually, the specific mention of segment performance alongside a fading aggregate effect warrants caution. However, since the direction of the effect is consistent (treatment wins everywhere), true Simpson's paradox (reversal of direction) is not currently present, so SIMPSON is not flagged based strictly on the definition provided. The primary failure is the projection of a transient novelty effect as a permanent gain.

Because the financial projection relies on a known short-term spike rather than a stable long-term effect, the business case for the rollout is flawed. The team must re-calculate the expected annual impact using the fading curve rather than the day-one peak before proceeding.

ERRORS: NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
