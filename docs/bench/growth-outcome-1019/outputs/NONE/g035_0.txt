The readout explicitly identifies multiple critical flaws. First, it states the test was "ended early, on the day the p-value first crossed 0.05 after daily looks," which is a classic case of **PEEK** (peeking), inflating the false positive rate. Second, it notes that while the treatment leads in the pooled total, it "trails within each platform" because the treatment group received disproportionately more high-converting desktop traffic. This reversal of trends when data is aggregated versus segmented is **SIMPSON**'s paradox, indicating the overall lift is misleading.

Although the readout mentions sample sizes matched power calculations and denominators were correct, the presence of peeking and Simpson's paradox invalidates the statistical significance and the claimed 3.1% lift. The stability of the lift over 28 days suggests novelty is not the primary issue here, but the structural errors in analysis and stopping rules are fatal to the current conclusion. We cannot trust the reported effect size or significance until these biases are addressed.

ERRORS: PEEK, SIMPSON
