The readout presents multiple critical flaws preventing a safe rollout. First, although the arm sizes (50,200 vs 50,100) appear balanced, the team explicitly stopped the test the first day the p-value dropped below 0.05 after checking daily. This constitutes **PEEK**, as repeated interim looks without correction inflate the false positive rate.

Second, the overall result shows a treatment win, yet the treatment loses within both the mobile and desktop segments. This is a classic case of **SIMPSON** paradox, where the aggregated data misleadingly reverses the trend found in every subgroup, suggesting a data aggregation error or confounding variable.

Third, the forecast relies entirely on the 12% lift observed on day one, ignoring the decay to 1% by day fourteen. Projecting the initial spike as a lasting effect indicates a **NOVELTY** error, as users often react strongly to new UI elements initially before the effect wears off.

While the sample size exceeded the power calculation and the denominator matches the randomization unit, the presence of peeking, Simpson's paradox, and novelty bias makes the current recommendation unreliable. The contradiction between segment and overall results specifically requires investigation before any decision can be made.

ERRORS: PEEK, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
