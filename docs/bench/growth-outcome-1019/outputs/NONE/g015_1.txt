The readout explicitly identifies three critical analysis errors. First, **PEEK** is present because the test was stopped early based on daily looks when the p-value first crossed 0.05, a practice known to inflate false positives. Second, **POWER** is an issue; the readout admits "nobody sized the test" and notes only 120 users per arm, which is insufficient to reliably detect the reported effect size. Third, **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch) is indicated. While the visitor counts (50,200 vs 50,100) look balanced, the analysis relies on a sample of only 120 users per arm for the metric calculation. This massive discrepancy between assigned visitors and analyzed users suggests severe data loss or filtering that breaks the randomization integrity relative to the analysis set.

Although the readout confirms the denominator matches the randomized unit (ruling out DENOM), shows consistent results across segments (ruling out SIMPSON), and reports stable lift over time (ruling out NOVELTY), the combination of peeking, severe underpowering, and potential sampling issues makes the results unreliable. The claim that "Treatment wins" cannot be trusted given these methodological flaws.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK, POWER
DECISION: HOLD
