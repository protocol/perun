The readout presents several critical issues preventing a safe rollout decision. First, while the visitor counts (50,200 vs 50,100) appear balanced, the analysis explicitly states "Each arm has 120 users," indicating a massive discrepancy between assigned traffic and analyzed sample size. This suggests a severe **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch) or logging failure where the vast majority of data is missing, rendering the statistical validity questionable.

Second, the text admits "nobody sized the test for the lift it reports." With only 120 users per arm, the test is almost certainly **POWER** deficient to detect an 8.3% lift with statistical significance, making the result likely noise.

Third, the readout notes the lift is "concentrated in the first three days and fades," yet the team "projects the day-one lift for a full year." This is a classic **NOVELTY** error, where a temporary user reaction to change is incorrectly modeled as a permanent improvement.

Although the stopping rule was followed (no PEEK), the denominator matches the randomization unit (no DENOM), and results are consistent across platforms (no SIMPSON), the combination of potential data loss, lack of power, and erroneous long-term projection makes the current recommendation unsafe.
