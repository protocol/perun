The readout presents two critical issues preventing a safe rollout decision. First, the assignment numbers show a severe Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The planned split was even, yet the control group received 60,400 visitors while the treatment group received only 50,100. This discrepancy exceeds what chance allows, indicating a broken assignment or logging mechanism that invalidates the comparison.

Second, the results exhibit Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON). While the aggregated data shows a +3.1% lift for the treatment, the readout explicitly states that the treatment loses within both the mobile and desktop segments. When a trend appears in different groups but disappears or reverses when combined, the overall "win" is a statistical artifact, likely driven by an imbalance in the mix of high-performing versus low-performing user types between arms rather than the feature itself.

Although the test avoided early stopping (no PEEK), achieved sufficient sample size (no POWER issue), used the correct denominator (no DENOM error), and showed stable performance over time (no NOVELTY concern), the presence of SRM and SIMPSON makes the current results unreliable. The fundamental integrity of the randomization and the interpretation of the aggregate metric are compromised.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
