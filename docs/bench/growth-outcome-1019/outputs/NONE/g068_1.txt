The readout explicitly identifies several critical methodological flaws. First, the metric denominator is incorrect: conversion is computed per page view, yet users were the randomized unit (DENOM). Second, the test was stopped the first day the p-value dropped below 0.05 after daily checks, which constitutes peeking and inflates false positives (PEEK). Third, while the planned split was 50/50, the actual arms received 52,300 and 47,700 users; this 4,600-user discrepancy suggests a potential sample ratio mismatch where assignment or logging may be broken (SRM).

Although the readout notes that power targets were met and the lift was consistent across platforms and time (addressing POWER, SIMPSON, and NOVELTY concerns), the presence of DENOM, PEEK, and SRM errors invalidates the statistical significance of the reported +3.1% lift. The metric definition error alone prevents accurate effect estimation, while peeking and potential SRM undermine the validity of the hypothesis test. These issues must be resolved before any rollout decision can be made.

ERRORS: DENOM, PEEK, SRM
DECISION: HOLD
