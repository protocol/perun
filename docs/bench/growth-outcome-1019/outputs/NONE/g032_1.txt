The readout presents multiple critical flaws preventing a safe rollout. First, the traffic allocation is severely skewed: 60,400 visitors in control versus 50,100 in treatment deviates significantly from the planned even split, indicating a Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). This suggests broken assignment or logging, invalidating the comparison.

Second, the results exhibit Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON). While the aggregated data shows a treatment win, the treatment actually loses within both the mobile and desktop segments individually. Relying on the pooled result masks the negative performance across all user types.

Third, there is a clear Novelty effect (NOVELTY). The lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades subsequently, yet the readout incorrectly projects the initial day-one lift over a full year. This overestimates the long-term value of the change.

Although the test avoided peeking and met power requirements, the presence of SRM, Simpson's paradox, and unadjusted novelty effects makes the current recommendation unreliable. The contradictory segment data and broken randomization ratio must be investigated before any decision is made.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
