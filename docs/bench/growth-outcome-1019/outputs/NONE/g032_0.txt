The readout presents several critical issues that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the change.

First, the visitor counts (60,400 vs. 50,100) deviate significantly from the planned even split, indicating a **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch). This suggests broken assignment or logging, making the comparison unreliable.

Second, while the overall result favors the treatment, the treatment loses in both mobile and desktop segments. This reversal of trends when data is aggregated versus segmented is a classic case of **SIMPSON** (Simpson's Paradox), implying the overall "win" is a statistical artifact rather than a real effect.

Third, the readout admits the lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades, yet it projects this day-one lift for a full year. This is a clear **NOVELTY** error, where a temporary user reaction to a new feature is mistakenly treated as a permanent gain.

Although the test avoided peeking and met power requirements, the presence of SRM, Simpson's Paradox, and the novelty effect projection renders the current results unusable for a rollout decision. The fundamental integrity of the experiment is compromised by the assignment mismatch and the contradictory segment data.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
