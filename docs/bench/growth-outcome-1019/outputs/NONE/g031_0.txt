The readout indicates two critical methodological flaws. First, while the sample sizes (50,200 vs 50,100) appear balanced, the analysis unit mismatches the randomization unit. Users were randomized, but the conversion metric divides by sessions. Since heavy users generate multiple sessions, this violates the independence assumption and biases the results; this is a **DENOM** error.

Second, the forecast relies entirely on the 12% lift observed on day one, ignoring the drop to 1% by day fourteen. Animated changes often suffer from initial curiosity that fades quickly. Projecting the short-term spike as a permanent gain without adjusting for this decay constitutes a **NOVELTY** error.

Although the test avoided early stopping (no PEEK), had adequate sample size (no POWER), showed consistent directional effects across segments (no SIMPSON), and maintained a valid split (no SRM), the presence of denominator mismatch and unadjusted novelty effects makes the current recommendation unreliable. The forecasted impact is likely significantly overstated.

ERRORS: DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
