The readout presents several critical statistical flaws that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the change.

First, there is a clear **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch). The planned split was even, yet the observed counts are 60,400 for control and 50,100 for treatment. This significant deviation suggests broken assignment or logging logic.

Second, the data exhibits **SIMPSON** (Simpson's Paradox). While the aggregated data shows a +9.2% lift for the treatment, the treatment actually loses within both the mobile and desktop segments individually. The overall positive result is a mathematical artifact of pooling, not a genuine effect.

Third, there is a **DENOM** error. The metric "conversion" is computed per page view, but the unit of randomization was the user. This mismatch invalidates the statistical inference because page views are not independent observations.

Although the readout correctly notes that power requirements were met, the analysis was done at the planned end date (no PEEK), and the effect was stable over time (no NOVELTY), the presence of SRM, Simpson's Paradox, and denominator mismatch makes the current results unreliable. The contradictory segment data and ratio mismatch must be investigated before any business decision can be made.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
