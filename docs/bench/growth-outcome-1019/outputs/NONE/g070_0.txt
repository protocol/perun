The readout presents a mostly robust analysis, but one critical issue prevents an immediate rollout decision. The planned split was even (50/50), yet the observed traffic is 60,400 for control and 50,100 for treatment. This significant deviation suggests a Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM), indicating potential bugs in assignment or logging that invalidate the comparison.

Other error categories appear to be explicitly handled rather than present. The test ran to the planned end date with a pre-set stopping rule, ruling out peeking (PEEK). Both arms exceeded the 25,000-user power requirement, addressing power concerns (POWER). The metric denominator matches the randomized unit (users), avoiding denominator errors (DENOM). The lift remained stable over 28 days, suggesting the effect is not merely a short-lived novelty (NOVELTY). Finally, the treatment won in both mobile and desktop segments, showing no evidence of Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON).

However, because SRM implies the fundamental integrity of the experiment is compromised, the results cannot be trusted until the cause of the traffic imbalance is identified and fixed.

ERRORS: SRM
DECISION: HOLD
