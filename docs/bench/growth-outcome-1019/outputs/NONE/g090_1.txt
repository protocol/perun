The readout explicitly describes a classic case of Simpson's Paradox. Although the treatment shows a 3.1% lift in the pooled total, it actually trails the control within each specific platform segment. The aggregate positive result is an artifact of the treatment group receiving a disproportionate amount of high-converting desktop traffic, not a genuine improvement in the button design. Because the pooled result hides and reverses the negative performance seen in every segment, the headline lift is misleading.

The readout indicates other potential issues are handled: the sample sizes (50,200 vs 50,100) are consistent with an even split, ruling out SRM. There was only a single analysis at the planned end date, excluding PEEK. The sample size exceeded the power calculation requirement, addressing POWER. The metric denominator matches the randomized unit, avoiding DENOM. Finally, the lift remained stable over 28 days, suggesting the effect is not a short-lived NOVELTY. However, the presence of Simpson's Paradox is a critical analytical error that invalidates the conclusion that the treatment is superior.

ERRORS: SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
