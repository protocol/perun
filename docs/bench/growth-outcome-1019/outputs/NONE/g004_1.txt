The readout presents several critical statistical flaws preventing a safe rollout. First, while the user split (50,080 vs 49,920) appears balanced, the analysis explicitly states each arm has only "120 users" for the calculation, which contradicts the total traffic and indicates a severe **POWER** issue; the sample is far too small for the claimed lift. Second, the metric definition triggers a **DENOM** error: conversion is computed per page view, yet users were the randomized unit. This mismatch invalidates the statistical inference. Third, the results exhibit **SIMPSON**'s paradox: the overall treatment wins, yet it loses within both the mobile and desktop segments. This reversal suggests the aggregate result is misleading and likely driven by unequal segment weighting rather than a true effect.

Although the test avoided **PEEK** by adhering to a pre-set stopping rule and reading results once, and the sustained lift over 28 days argues against a **NOVELTY** effect, the presence of Simpson's paradox, the denominator mismatch, and the underpowered sample size make the current conclusion unreliable. The recommendation to roll out cannot be accepted until these fundamental analytical errors are resolved.

ERRORS: POWER, DENOM, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
