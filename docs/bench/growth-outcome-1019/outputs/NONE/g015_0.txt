The readout explicitly identifies three critical analysis errors. First, **PEEK** is present because the test "was ended early, on the day the p-value first crossed 0.05 after daily looks," which inflates the false positive rate. Second, **POWER** is an issue as the text admits "nobody sized the test for the lift it reports" and notes a contradictory sample size ("Each arm has 120 users") that is insufficient for the claimed effect. Third, **SRM** is indicated by the statement that "Each arm has 120 users," which drastically contradicts the earlier visitor counts of ~50,000 per arm; this discrepancy suggests a broken assignment or logging mechanism where the analyzed sample does not match the traffic.

While the readout notes that Simpson's paradox is not occurring (treatment wins in both segments) and the denominator matches the randomization unit, the confirmed presence of peeking, underpowering, and sample ratio mismatch invalidates the statistical significance of the +7.3% lift. The stability of the lift over four weeks mitigates the novelty concern, but the fundamental integrity of the experiment is compromised by the other errors. Therefore, the rollout cannot proceed based on this data.
