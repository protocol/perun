The readout presents a clear case of denominator mismatch. The unit of randomization was the user, yet the primary metric (booking conversion) is defined as completed bookings divided by page views. Since a single user can generate multiple page views, analyzing data at the page-view level violates the independence assumption of the statistical test and constitutes a DENOM error.

Other potential issues appear addressed. The arm sizes (50,080 vs. 49,920) are consistent with a 50/50 split given the sample size, ruling out SRM. The test ran to a pre-set date with a single analysis, avoiding PEEK. The sample size exceeded the power calculation requirements, addressing POWER. The treatment lead persists across the pooled total and within each platform segment, indicating SIMPSON is not present. Finally, the lift remained stable over 28 days without decay, suggesting NOVELTY is not a factor.

However, the DENOM error is critical. Because the analysis unit differs from the randomization unit, the reported p-values and confidence intervals are invalid. We cannot trust the claimed +3.1% lift until the metric is recalculated using the user as the denominator (e.g., users with a completed booking divided by total users).

ERRORS: DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
