The readout reveals several critical methodological flaws. First, the metric denominator is incorrect: conversion is computed per page view, but users were the unit of randomization (DENOM). Second, the team engaged in optional stopping by checking the dashboard daily and halting the test the moment significance was reached, which inflates the false positive rate (PEEK). Third, while the power calculation required 25,000 users per arm, the actual split resulted in 52,300 and 47,700 users. This 4,600-user deviation from the planned 50/50 split suggests a potential Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM), indicating broken assignment or logging.

Although the readout notes consistent lifts across platforms and over time (addressing SIMPSON and NOVELTY concerns) and confirms sample size targets were met regarding raw counts, the presence of DENOM, PEEK, and likely SRM invalidates the statistical conclusion. The +3.1% lift cannot be trusted without correcting how the metric is calculated, adhering to a fixed stopping rule, and investigating the traffic imbalance.
