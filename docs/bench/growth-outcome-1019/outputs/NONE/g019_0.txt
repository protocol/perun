The readout indicates several critical issues preventing a safe rollout. First, the team stopped the test immediately upon seeing p < 0.05 during daily checks, which is a clear case of **PEEK** (peeking), inflating the false positive rate. Second, while the treatment leads in the pooled total, it actually trails within every specific platform segment. The aggregate lift is driven entirely by the treatment group receiving a disproportionate amount of high-converting desktop traffic. This is a textbook example of **SIMPSON** (Simpson's paradox), meaning the overall positive result is misleading and the change likely performs worse for all user types.

Although the sample sizes appear balanced (ruling out SRM), the denominator matches the randomization unit (ruling out DENOM), and the sample size met power requirements (ruling out POWER), the presence of peeking and Simpson's paradox invalidates the statistical conclusion. The claim that the lift held steady argues against a NOVELTY effect, but the structural flaws in analysis remain. Because the data suggests the treatment is actually inferior within segments and the p-value is unreliable due to early stopping, the recommendation to roll out cannot be accepted.
