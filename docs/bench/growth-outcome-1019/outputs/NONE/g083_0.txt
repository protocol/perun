The readout presents several critical issues that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the change immediately.

First, while the visitor counts (50,200 vs 50,100) appear balanced, the analysis explicitly states "Each arm has 120 users." This massive discrepancy between assigned visitors and analyzed users indicates a severe data integrity issue or logging failure, fitting the **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch) category where assignment or logging is broken.

Second, the text admits "nobody sized the test for the lift it reports," indicating the study is **POWER** (underpowered). The sample size of 120 users is insufficient to reliably detect or confirm the reported 8.3% lift, making the result statistically unreliable.

Third, the readout notes that the lift "is concentrated in the first three days and fades each day after," yet the team "projects the day-one lift for a full year." This is a classic **NOVELTY** error, where a short-term behavioral change due to curiosity is incorrectly modeled as a permanent improvement.

Although the stopping rule was followed (no PEEK), the denominator matches the randomization unit (no DENOM), and results are consistent across platforms (no SIMPSON), the combination of data mismatch, lack of statistical power, and flawed projection logic makes the current conclusion unsafe.

ERRORS: SRM, POWER, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
