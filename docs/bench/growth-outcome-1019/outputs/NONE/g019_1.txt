The readout explicitly describes several critical flaws. First, the team "stopped the test the first day p fell below 0.05," which is a classic case of optional stopping that inflates false positives (PEEK). Second, while the total sample size exceeded the power requirement, the result shows the treatment leading in the pooled total but trailing within every individual platform segment. This reversal occurs because the treatment group received a disproportionate amount of high-converting desktop traffic, a clear instance of Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON). Finally, the readout states the lift held steady for 28 days but provides no evidence distinguishing this from a short-term novelty effect, yet the recommendation assumes the lift is permanent (NOVELTY).

The readout indicates that SRM is not an issue (sizes are nearly equal), DENOM is correct (denominator matches randomization unit), and the raw sample size met the power calculation, so POWER is not flagged based on the text provided, despite the other errors invalidating the result. Because PEEK, SIMPSON, and NOVELTY are present, the results are unreliable for a rollout decision.
