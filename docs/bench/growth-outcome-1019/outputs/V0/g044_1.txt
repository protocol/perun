The readout contains multiple critical flaws preventing a rollout decision. First, the arm sizes (52,300 vs 47,700) deviate significantly from the planned 50/50 split, indicating **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch), which suggests broken assignment or logging. Second, the team "stopped the test the first day p fell below 0.05" after checking daily; this is classic **PEEK** (peeking), which invalidates the p-value by inflating the false-positive rate. Third, the primary metric divides conversions by sessions while randomization occurred at the user level (**DENOM** error); although unique-user conversion was tracked as secondary, the primary claim relies on the wrong denominator. While the effect persisted for 28 days (mitigating **NOVELTY**) and held across segments (no **SIMPSON** paradox), the combination of SRM, peeking, and denominator mismatch renders the statistical significance claims unreliable. The power calculation is irrelevant because the sequential testing violation and data integrity issues (SRM) compromise the result regardless of sample size.
