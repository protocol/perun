Second, **DENOM** error exists because the metric divides conversions by sessions, while randomization occurred at the user level. Since heavy users generate many sessions, this violates the independence assumption of the test and biases the variance estimates.

Third, **NOVELTY** is evident. The lift dropped from 12% on day one to 1% by day fourteen, yet the forecast relies on the initial spike. This suggests a short-lived curiosity effect rather than sustained value, making the projection inaccurate.

Although the sample size meets power requirements (**POWER** is not flagged) and the split is acceptable (**SRM** is not flagged), the combination of reversed segment results, incorrect denominators, and reliance on novelty-driven forecasts makes the current "win" illusory.
