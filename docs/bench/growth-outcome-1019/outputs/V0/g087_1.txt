The readout describes a classic case of **Simpson's paradox (SIMPSON)**: the treatment wins overall but loses within both the mobile and desktop segments. This mathematical impossibility indicates a severe confounding variable (likely an imbalance in the mix of high-vs-low converting traffic between arms) that invalidates the aggregate "win."

Additionally, the test suffers from a **power (POWER)** failure. The readout states each arm has only 120 users (contradicting the earlier visitor counts, implying the analysis sample is tiny) and explicitly admits "nobody sized the test for the lift it reports." Without a pre-committed sample size based on a minimum detectable effect, the reported significance is unreliable.

While the readout claims a single analysis at the end (ruling out PEEK) and correct denominators (ruling out DENOM), the presence of Simpson's paradox and the lack of power calculation make the result untrustworthy. You cannot ship a change that performs worse in every specific user segment based on a distorted aggregate number.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, POWER
DECISION: HOLD
