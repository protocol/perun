The readout contains multiple critical statistical flaws that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, **SRM** is present: the planned split was even, yet the observed arms are 60,400 (control) vs. 50,100 (treatment). This ~17% deviation suggests broken assignment or logging, making the groups incomparable.

Second, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is explicit: while the aggregated data shows a +12.3% lift, the treatment loses within *both* mobile and desktop segments. Aggregating contradictory segment results creates a misleading positive headline.

Third, **PEEK** occurred: the test was stopped the moment daily looks showed p < 0.05. This "peeking" inflates the false-positive rate, rendering the significance claim unreliable regardless of the pre-calculated sample size.

Although the denominator matches the randomized unit (no DENOM) and the lift showed stability over time (no NOVELTY), the combination of sample ratio mismatch, reversed segment trends, and sequential testing without correction makes the result untrustworthy. The claim that "each arm got 25,000 users" contradicts the total visitor counts provided, further confusing the power analysis.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, PEEK
DECISION: HOLD
