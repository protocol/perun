The readout describes a critical instance of Simpson's Paradox. While the aggregated data shows a 3.2% lift for the treatment, the result reverses within every specific segment: the treatment loses on both mobile and desktop. This indicates that the overall "win" is a statistical artifact caused by an imbalance in traffic composition between arms (e.g., the treatment arm received a disproportionately high volume of high-converting desktop users), rather than a genuine improvement in the user experience.

Rolling out a change that performs worse for every actual user segment would likely decrease total conversion once the traffic mix normalizes. The other potential errors are explicitly ruled out in the text: the arm sizes (50,080 vs 49,920) are consistent with chance, ruling out SRM; the analysis occurred only at the planned end date, ruling out PEEK; the sample size exceeded the power calculation, ruling out POWER; the denominator matches the randomized unit, ruling out DENOM; and the lift was stable over 28 days, making NOVELTY unlikely.

Because the aggregate positive result is entirely driven by segmentation bias and the change is detrimental to all user subgroups, the recommendation to ship is invalid. The team must investigate the traffic allocation mechanism and analyze the segments independently before making a deployment decision.
