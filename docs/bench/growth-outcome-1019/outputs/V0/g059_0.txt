The readout contains two critical flaws. First, the arm sizes (52,300 vs 47,700) deviate significantly from the planned 50/50 split, indicating **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch). This suggests broken assignment or logging, invalidating the comparison. Second, the team stopped the test immediately upon seeing p < 0.05 during daily checks. This is **PEEK** (peeking); repeated looks inflate the false-positive rate, meaning the "win" is likely noise despite reaching the target sample size at that moment. A fixed-horizon test must be read only once at the pre-committed end, or a valid sequential design must be used.

While the readout claims stability over four weeks (addressing NOVELTY) and consistent results across platforms (addressing SIMPSON), the fundamental validity of the experiment is compromised by the assignment imbalance and the stopping rule. You cannot trust the +6.2% lift figure.
