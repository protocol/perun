The readout explicitly states the test "was ended early, on the day the p-value first crossed 0.05 after daily looks." This is a classic case of **peeking** (PEEK). Repeated significance testing without a pre-committed sample size or a valid sequential stopping rule inflates the false-positive rate far above the nominal 5%. Consequently, the observed "win" is statistically unverified; the result could easily be noise rather than a true effect.

While the readout confirms adequate power was reached (25,000 users per arm), the sample ratio is balanced, the denominator matches the randomization unit, segments align with the aggregate (no Simpson's paradox), and the lift showed stability over four weeks (no novelty fade), the methodological failure in how the test was stopped invalidates the conclusion. You cannot treat a peeked result as a confirmed win. The team must either extend the test to a pre-determined endpoint based on the original power calculation without further interim looks, or re-analyze using a proper sequential testing framework that accounts for the multiple looks already taken. Until the statistical validity is restored, rolling out the change is premature.
