The readout presents a critical contradiction: while the overall treatment arm shows an 8.2% lift, it loses within both the mobile and desktop segments. Since the total population is composed entirely of these two segments, an overall win alongside losses in both parts indicates **Simpson's paradox** (SIMPSON), likely driven by a shift in the mix of users between arms rather than a true treatment effect. This makes the aggregate lift unreliable.

Additionally, the test was stopped on the day the p-value first crossed 0.05 after daily looks. This is classic **peeking** (PEEK). Repeated significance testing without a sequential correction inflates the false-positive rate, meaning the observed "win" is statistically unverified. Although the sample size exceeded the power calculation, the early stopping rule invalidates the significance claim.

Because the aggregate result contradicts the segment results and the statistical significance is compromised by optional stopping, the evidence does not support a rollout. The team must investigate the segment-level loss and re-evaluate the data using a proper sequential testing framework or a fixed-horizon analysis without peeking.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, PEEK
DECISION: HOLD
