The readout contains multiple critical flaws preventing a rollout decision.

First, **DENOM**: The metric divides conversions by sessions while randomization occurred at the user level. Heavy users generate more sessions, skewing the denominator and invalidating the causal claim.

Second, **NOVELTY**: The lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades thereafter, yet the readout projects this transient spike as a permanent annual gain. This misrepresents the long-term value.

Third, **POWER**: While the readout claims the sample size matches a power calculation, the rapid decay of the effect suggests the observed signal may be noise or a short-term artifact rather than the sustained target lift the power analysis assumed. However, since the text explicitly states the sample size matched the requirement, I will not flag POWER as a definitive error based *only* on the text's own claims, but the NOVELTY and DENOM errors are explicit contradictions in the data description.

The denominator mismatch alone breaks the statistical validity, and the novelty effect invalidates the business case projection.

ERRORS: DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
