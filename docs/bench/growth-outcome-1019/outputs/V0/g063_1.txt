The readout contains critical flaws that invalidate the "win." First, **PEEK**: stopping the test the moment p < 0.05 after daily looks inflates the false-positive rate; this is not a valid result without a pre-committed sequential design. Second, **SIMPSON**: while the pooled total shows a lift, the treatment actually trails within *each* platform segment. The aggregate gain is an artifact of the treatment group receiving disproportionately more high-converting desktop traffic, not a genuine improvement in the layout. Third, **NOVELTY**: the lift was stable at ~3% for 28 days, yet the headline claims +8.7%, suggesting the final spike may be noise or a transient effect projected as lasting.

Because the aggregated win reverses upon segmentation and the stopping rule invalidates the significance claim, the data does not support a rollout. The apparent gain is likely a statistical artifact of traffic mix and peeking, not a real user value increase.

ERRORS: PEEK, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
