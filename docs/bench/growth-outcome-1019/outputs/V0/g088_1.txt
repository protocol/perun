The readout contains two critical flaws. First, the arm sizes (60,400 vs 50,100) deviate significantly from the planned 50/50 split, indicating **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch), which suggests broken assignment or logging. Second, while the pooled total shows a lift, the treatment actually trails within every platform segment; the aggregate win is driven entirely by an over-representation of high-converting desktop traffic in the treatment group. This is **SIMPSON**'s paradox. Additionally, the metric denominator (page views) does not match the randomization unit (users), flagged as **DENOM**, risking inflated significance due to correlated observations.

Although the test reached the planned sample size (**POWER** satisfied), ran to the pre-committed end date (**PEEK** avoided), and showed no fade (**NOVELTY** avoided), the presence of SRM and Simpson's paradox makes the headline lift unreliable. The apparent win is an artifact of traffic mix and potential assignment bias, not genuine user preference.
