The readout presents a strong case for the pause button, but one critical data integrity issue prevents a safe rollout decision.

The planned assignment was an even split, yet the observed traffic is heavily skewed: 60,400 users in control versus 50,100 in treatment. This ~55/45 split deviates significantly from the expected 50/50 distribution. With sample sizes this large, such a discrepancy is statistically improbable under random chance, indicating a **Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM)**. This suggests a fundamental breakdown in the randomization logic or data logging pipeline (e.g., users dropping out of the treatment arm due to a bug). When SRM is present, the treatment and control groups are no longer comparable, rendering the observed lift invalid regardless of the positive results in sub-segments or the adherence to stopping rules.

While the readout correctly notes that power targets were met, the stopping rule was pre-committed, and Simpson's paradox was checked (treatment wins in both segments), the SRM invalidates the entire experiment's internal validity. You cannot trust the conversion rates if the assignment mechanism failed.
