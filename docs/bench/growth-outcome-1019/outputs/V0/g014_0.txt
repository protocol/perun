The readout describes a critical Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The experiment planned an even split, yet the observed traffic is 60,400 (Control) versus 50,100 (Treatment). This ~17% deviation is far too large to be random chance, indicating a broken assignment mechanism or logging error that invalidates the comparison. Because the groups are not comparable, the reported +18.4% lift is unreliable.

Other error categories are explicitly ruled out by the text: Power is sufficient (both arms exceeded the 25,000 user requirement); Peeking did not occur (read once at the planned end); the Denominator matches the randomization unit (users); Simpson's paradox is absent (treatment wins in both mobile and desktop segments); and Novelty effects are unlikely (lift was stable across four weeks with no fade).

However, the presence of SRM is fatal to the validity of the results. You cannot make a rollout decision based on data where the treatment and control groups were not generated correctly. The engineering team must investigate why the traffic split failed before any statistical conclusions can be trusted.
