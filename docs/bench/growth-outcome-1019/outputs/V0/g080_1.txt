The readout contains multiple critical flaws that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, the team stopped the test the moment significance was reached after daily checks. This is **PEEK**ing; repeated looks inflate the false-positive rate, making the "win" likely noise. A fixed-horizon test must be read once at the end, or a valid sequential design used.

Second, the forecast relies on the 12% day-one lift while ignoring the 1% result at day fourteen. This attributes a transient **NOVELTY** effect to long-term value. Users often react strongly to new UI elements initially, then habituate. The mature rate (1%) is the only valid basis for impact.

Third, the overall treatment win reverses within both mobile and desktop segments. This is **SIMPSON**'s paradox. If the change loses in every segment but wins overall, the aggregate result is a statistical artifact driven by uneven traffic mixing, not a true treatment effect. You cannot ship a feature that performs worse for every user type.

Finally, while the power calculation was met (**POWER** is not flagged) and the denominator matches the randomization unit (**DENOM** is not flagged), the combination of peeking, novelty bias, and Simpson's paradox makes the current conclusion unsafe.

ERRORS: PEEK, NOVELTY, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
