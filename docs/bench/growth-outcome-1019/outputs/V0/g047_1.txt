The readout contains three critical errors preventing a rollout decision.

First, **DENOM**: The metric divides conversions by *sessions*, but randomization occurred at the *user* level. Heavy users generate more sessions, skewing the denominator and invalidating the statistical test. The unit of analysis must match the unit of randomization.

Second, **NOVELTY**: The forecast relies on the 12% lift observed on day one, ignoring the decay to 1% by day fourteen. This projects a short-term novelty effect as a permanent gain, vastly overstating long-term value.

Third, **PEEK** (implicit in the forecast logic): While the *test* ran to completion without early stopping, the *decision logic* effectively "peeked" at the interim day-one result to select the effect size for forecasting, violating the pre-commitment to the full-period outcome.

The traffic split is valid (ruling out SRM), power was achieved (ruling out POWER), and results are consistent across segments (ruling out SIMPSON). However, the mismatched denominator and the reliance on decaying novelty data make the current conclusion unreliable.
