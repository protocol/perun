The readout describes a single denominator mismatch error. Conversions are divided by sessions while randomization occurred at the user level. This violates the unit of analysis, as heavy users contribute multiple sessions, artificially compressing variance and potentially biasing the effect estimate (DENOM). The text explicitly acknowledges this ("compresses the observed effect slightly") but proceeds with the recommendation anyway.

Other categories are cleared by the text: arm sizes (50,080 vs 49,920) are within chance expectations for 100k users, ruling out SRM. The test ran the full 28 days and was read once at the end, ruling out PEEK. Sample size exceeded the pre-calculated requirement (25k/arm), ruling out POWER. Results were consistent across platforms and traffic mix, ruling out SIMPSON. The lift remained stable over 28 days rather than decaying, arguing against a pure NOVELTY effect.

Despite the consistent direction and adequate power, the denominator error means the reported magnitude and significance levels are technically invalid without re-aggregation to the user level. A rollout decision based on biased metrics carries unknown risk.
