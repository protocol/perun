The readout confirms proper randomization (arms are balanced), adequate power (both arms exceeded the 25k requirement), and correct denominator alignment (users). There was no peeking, as analysis occurred only at the planned end date.

However, two critical errors prevent a rollout decision. First, the readout exhibits **NOVELTY**: the lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades, yet the team projects this day-one lift over a full year. This assumes a temporary reaction to the new reminder is a permanent behavior change. Second, while not a full paradox, the concentration of effects suggests the need to verify if the pooled result masks long-term neutral or negative trends in specific cohorts, though the primary flag here is the unjustified projection of a fading effect.

Because the business case relies on projecting a short-lived novelty effect as a permanent annual gain, the financial justification is flawed. The team must re-calculate the projected value based on the stabilized (post-fade) retention rate or extend the measurement window to capture the true steady-state impact before rolling out.

ERRORS: NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
