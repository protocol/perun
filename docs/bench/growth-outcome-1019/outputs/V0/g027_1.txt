The readout contains three critical flaws that invalidate the "win" claim. First, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is explicitly present: while the aggregated data favors treatment, the effect reverses within both mobile and desktop segments, indicating the overall lift is a statistical artifact of segment mixing rather than a true causal effect. Second, **DENOM**inator error exists because conversion was computed per page view despite users being the randomized unit; this violates the principle of analyzing at the level of randomization and biases variance estimates. Third, although the test ran for 28 days with stable lift, recommending a permanent rollout based on this duration without evidence of long-term behavior risks **NOVELTY**, as the "gentle animation" may lose effectiveness over time, yet the readout projects the lift as lasting without verification beyond four weeks.

Because the aggregate win is contradicted by segment-level losses and the metric definition is mismatched to the experimental design, the result is unreliable. The reversal in all known segments suggests the change may actually be harmful to specific user experiences, masked only by traffic composition. You cannot ship a feature that loses in every observed subgroup based on a flawed aggregate number.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
