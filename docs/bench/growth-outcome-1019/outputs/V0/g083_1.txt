The readout contains three critical flaws preventing a rollout decision. First, **POWER**: while each arm has 120 users for the success rate metric, the test was never sized for the reported +8.3% lift, making the result statistically unreliable. Second, **NOVELTY**: the lift is explicitly concentrated in the first three days and fades thereafter, yet the recommendation projects this transient spike as a permanent annual gain. Third, **PEEK**: although a stopping rule existed, reading the result based on the early, novelty-driven surge without accounting for the fade suggests the "win" relies on the initial noise rather than sustained value.

The sample ratio (50,200 vs 50,100) is acceptable, the denominator matches the randomization unit, and segment results align with the total, so SRM, DENOM, and SIMPSON do not apply. However, the combination of an underpowered design and a novelty effect masquerading as a long-term win invalidates the current recommendation.
