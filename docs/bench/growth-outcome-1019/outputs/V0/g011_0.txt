The readout contains two critical flaws. First, the arm sizes (60,400 vs. 50,100) deviate significantly from the planned even split despite both exceeding the power requirement. This indicates **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch), suggesting broken assignment or logging that invalidates the comparison. Second, while the overall treatment wins, it loses within both mobile and desktop segments. This is a textbook case of **SIMPSON** (Simpson's Paradox), where aggregated data masks negative performance in all underlying subgroups, likely driven by an imbalance in segment sizes between arms.

Although the test avoided early stopping (no PEEK) and met sample size targets (no POWER), the presence of SRM and Simpson's Paradox means the observed "+3.1%" lift is statistically unreliable and potentially directionally wrong. The claim that the lift "held" over 28 days does not resolve the structural bias indicated by SRM. You cannot ship a change that performs worse in every user segment based on a distorted aggregate.
