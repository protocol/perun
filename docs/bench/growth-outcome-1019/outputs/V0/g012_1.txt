The readout contains multiple critical flaws preventing a rollout decision.

First, the arm sizes (60,400 vs 50,100) deviate significantly from the planned even split despite both exceeding the power requirement, indicating **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch). This suggests broken assignment or logging, invalidating the comparison.

Second, the aggregate result shows a lift while every segment trails, a classic case of **SIMPSON**'s paradox caused by a traffic mix shift (treatment drew more high-converting desktop users). The pooled "win" is an artifact of composition, not treatment effect.

Third, the metric divides conversions by sessions while randomization occurred at the user level (**DENOM**). Heavy users generate multiple sessions, violating the independence assumption and skewing variance.

Although the test avoided early stopping (no PEEK) and met sample size targets (no POWER), the combination of SRM, Simpson's paradox, and denominator mismatch means the reported 6.8% lift is unreliable. The stability of the lift over 28 days does not cure these structural errors; it merely confirms a consistent bias. Do not ship based on contaminated data.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
