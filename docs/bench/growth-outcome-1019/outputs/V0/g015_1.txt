The readout contains two critical flaws. First, it explicitly states the test was "ended early, on the day the p-value first crossed 0.05 after daily looks." This is **PEEK**ing; repeated significance testing without a sequential design inflates the false-positive rate, making the "win" statistically unreliable. Second, the readout admits "nobody sized the test for the lift it reports" while claiming a specific +7.3% lift with only 120 users per arm. This indicates a **POWER** issue; the sample is likely too small to detect the claimed effect reliably, rendering the magnitude of the lift untrustworthy.

Although the split is balanced (no SRM), the denominator matches the randomization unit (no DENOM), results are consistent across segments (no SIMPSON), and the lift did not fade (no NOVELTY), the statistical validity of the result is compromised by the early stopping and lack of power calculation. You cannot confidently roll out a change based on a p-value derived from peeking and an underpowered sample.

ERRORS: PEEK, POWER
DECISION: HOLD
