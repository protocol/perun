The readout contains multiple critical flaws preventing a rollout decision.

First, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is present: the treatment wins overall but loses within both mobile and desktop segments. This indicates a skew in traffic composition (e.g., the treatment group received disproportionately more high-converting traffic types), making the aggregate result misleading.

Second, **DENOM**inator mismatch exists. The unit of randomization was the *user*, but the metric divides conversions by *sessions*. Since heavy users generate many sessions, this violates the independence assumption of the test and biases the variance, rendering the significance calculation invalid.

Third, **NOVELTY** effect is evident. The lift dropped from 12% on day one to 1% by day fourteen, yet the forecast relies on the initial spike. Projecting the day-one lift as a lasting effect ignores the rapid decay typical of UI novelty.

While the sample size meets power requirements and the split (50,080 vs 49,920) appears acceptable without a specific statistical test for SRM in the text, the structural errors in segmentation, metric definition, and temporal projection are fatal. The recommendation to roll out based on these figures is unsupported.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
