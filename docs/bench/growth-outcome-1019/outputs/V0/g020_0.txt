The readout contains two critical flaws. First, the arm sizes (60,400 vs 50,100) deviate significantly from the planned even split, indicating **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch). This suggests broken assignment or logging, invalidating the comparison. Second, the team "stopped the test the first day p fell below 0.05" after checking daily. This is **PEEK** (peeking), which inflates the false-positive rate; a fixed-horizon test cannot be stopped early based on interim significance without a sequential testing design.

While the readout claims stability over four weeks (mitigating NOVELTY) and consistent lifts across platforms (mitigating SIMPSON), the fundamental validity of the experiment is compromised by the assignment imbalance and the statistical error of early stopping. The note about "120 users" contradicts the visitor counts but reinforces the **POWER** concern if those were the actual analyzable units; however, SRM and PEEK are the definitive blockers described. You cannot ship based on a result where the randomization mechanism appears broken and the significance threshold was breached via repeated testing.
