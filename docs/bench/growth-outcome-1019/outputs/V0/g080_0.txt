The readout contains multiple critical flaws that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, the test suffers from **PEEK** (peeking). The team checked the dashboard daily and stopped immediately upon hitting p < 0.05 on day one. This sequential looking without a pre-committed sequential testing design (like mSPRT) inflates the false-positive rate, making the "win" statistically unreliable.

Second, there is clear evidence of **SIMPSON** (Simpson's paradox). While the aggregated data shows a win for the treatment, the readout explicitly states that the treatment *loses* within both the mobile and desktop segments. An overall win driven by aggregate math while losing in every constituent segment indicates a severe aggregation error or confounding variable, rendering the top-line result meaningless.

Third, the forecast relies on **NOVELTY**. The lift was 12% on day one but decayed to 1% by day fourteen. Using the day-one spike (likely driven by user curiosity about the new thumbnail/timer) to forecast long-term impact ignores the rapid decay, overestimating the true value.

Because the primary result is statistically invalid due to peeking, logically impossible due to Simpson's paradox, and based on a transient novelty effect, the rollout cannot proceed.

ERRORS: PEEK, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
