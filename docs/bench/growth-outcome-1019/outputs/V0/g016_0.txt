The readout contains multiple critical flaws preventing a rollout decision.

First, the arm sizes (52,300 vs 47,700) deviate significantly from the planned 50/50 split, indicating **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch), which suggests broken assignment or logging.

Second, the text explicitly states the treatment leads in the total but trails within every platform segment because it attracted more high-converting desktop traffic. This is a textbook case of **SIMPSON** (Simpson's Paradox), meaning the aggregate lift is misleading and the variant likely performs worse for all user types.

Third, the claim of "120 users enrolled per arm" contradicts the tens of thousands reported earlier. If the effective sample for the analysis was truly ~120, the test is severely **POWER** (underpowered) to detect the claimed 3.4% lift reliably, especially without a pre-stated power calculation.

While the readout correctly notes a single analysis at the end (avoiding PEEK) and uses the correct denominator (avoiding DENOM), the presence of SRM, Simpson's Paradox, and potential power issues makes the results invalid. The contradictory data regarding sample size further undermines trust in the metrics. You cannot ship based on aggregated data that reverses upon segmentation, especially when assignment integrity is questionable.
