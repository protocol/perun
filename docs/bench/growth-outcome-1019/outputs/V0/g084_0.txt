The readout contains multiple critical flaws preventing a rollout decision. First, the arm sizes (52,300 vs 47,700) deviate significantly from the planned 50/50 split, indicating **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch), which suggests broken assignment or logging. Second, the test was stopped immediately upon crossing p < 0.05 after daily checks; this is classic **PEEK** (peeking), which invalidates the significance claim by inflating the false-positive rate. Third, while the sample size matched the power calculation for 25,000 users, the actual imbalance and early stop compromise that validity. Finally, the readout admits the lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades, yet projects this short-term **NOVELTY** effect as a permanent annual gain. Although the denominator matches the randomization unit and Simpson's paradox is ruled out, the combination of SRM, peeking, and unjustified novelty projection makes the reported +6.3% lift unreliable.
