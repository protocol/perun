The readout contains multiple critical flaws preventing a rollout decision.

First, **SRM**: The planned even split resulted in 60,400 control vs. 50,100 treatment visitors. This significant deviation suggests broken assignment or logging, invalidating the comparison.

Second, **SIMPSON**: While the pooled total shows a lift, the treatment trails within *each* platform segment. The aggregate win is an artifact of the treatment group receiving disproportionately more high-converting desktop traffic, masking the fact that the change actually performs worse on every individual platform.

Third, **DENOM**: The analysis divides conversions by sessions, but randomization occurred at the user level. Heavy users generating many sessions skew the denominator, violating the unit of randomization and biasing the metric.

The readout correctly notes that power targets were met and the stopping rule was followed (no PEEK). The sustained lift over 28 days argues against a simple NOVELTY effect, though the structural errors above make the lift figure unreliable.

Because the result reverses upon segmentation and the sample ratio is broken, the observed "win" is statistically invalid.
