The readout contains critical methodological flaws that invalidate the +6.2% lift claim.

First, **SRM** is present: the planned even split resulted in 60,400 control vs. 50,100 treatment users. This significant deviation suggests broken assignment or logging, making the groups incomparable.

Second, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is explicitly admitted: while treatment leads in the pooled total, it trails within every platform segment. The aggregate win is an artifact of treatment receiving disproportionately more high-converting desktop traffic, not a genuine improvement.

Third, **DENOM** is violated: users were randomized, but conversion was computed per page view. This mismatch inflates variance and biases results if users generate different page-view counts across arms.

Although the readout correctly notes adequate power, pre-committed stopping rules (no peeking), and stable lifts (no novelty effect), the combination of sample ratio mismatch, Simpson's paradox, and denominator errors renders the primary metric unreliable. The observed lift is driven by traffic mix, not product value.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
