The readout contains multiple critical flaws that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, the arm sizes (60,400 vs 50,100) deviate significantly from the planned 50/50 split despite sufficient power, indicating **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch). This suggests broken assignment or logging, making causal inference impossible.

Second, while the overall result is positive, the treatment loses in both mobile and desktop segments. This is a textbook case of **SIMPSON**'s paradox, where aggregated data masks negative performance across all underlying groups. The overall "win" is likely a mathematical artifact of the SRM rather than a real effect.

Third, the lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades, yet the projection assumes this day-one lift persists for a year. This is an unjustified extrapolation of a **NOVELTY** effect.

Although the test avoided peeking (read once at the end) and had sufficient sample size (POWER ok), the combination of SRM, Simpson's paradox, and novelty projection renders the result unreliable. You cannot ship a feature that performs worse in every user segment based on a distorted aggregate.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
