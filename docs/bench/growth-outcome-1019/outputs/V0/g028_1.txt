The readout contains multiple critical flaws that invalidate the "treatment wins" conclusion.

First, **SRM** is present: the planned even split resulted in 60,400 control vs. 50,100 treatment visitors, a discrepancy indicating broken assignment or logging. Second, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is explicitly described: while the pooled data shows a +9.2% lift, the treatment actually loses within both mobile and desktop segments individually. This suggests the aggregate win is a statistical artifact, likely driven by a shift in traffic mix rather than true efficacy. Third, **DENOM** is violated: users were randomized, but conversion was computed per page view, mismatching the unit of analysis and risking biased variance estimates.

Although the test avoided early stopping (no PEEK) and met sample size requirements (no POWER), the combination of sample ratio mismatch, reversed segment results, and incorrect denominator calculation makes the headline lift unreliable. The stability over time rules out NOVELTY, but the structural errors are fatal. You cannot ship a change that performs worse in every user segment based on a flawed aggregate number.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
