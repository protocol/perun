The readout contains two critical flaws. First, it admits the test was "ended early... after daily looks" when significance was reached. This is **PEEK**ing; repeated monitoring without a sequential testing correction inflates the false-positive rate, making the p-value invalid. Second, while the pooled total shows a lift, the treatment "trails within each platform" because it accidentally received more high-converting desktop traffic. This is **SIMPSON**'s paradox; the aggregate win is a composition artifact, not a real effect.

Although the readout claims the sample size met power requirements (**POWER** handled) and the denominator matches the randomization unit (**DENOM** handled), the presence of peeking and Simpson's paradox means the reported 3.1% lift is unreliable. The apparent success is likely due to traffic mix imbalance and statistical error rather than the feature's value. You cannot ship based on a result that reverses direction when segmented and relies on an invalid stopping rule.
