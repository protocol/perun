The readout contains critical flaws that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, the test suffered from **PEEKING**. Stopping the test the moment the p-value crossed 0.05 after daily looks inflates the false-positive rate, making the "win" statistically unreliable. A fixed-horizon test must be read only once at the pre-committed end, or a valid sequential design must be used.

Second, **SIMPSON'S PARADOX** is present. While the pooled total shows a lift, the treatment actually trails within *each* platform segment. The aggregate win is an illusion caused by the treatment group receiving a disproportionate amount of high-converting desktop traffic. The underlying data suggests the feature may actually be harmful across all specific user contexts.

Although the sample sizes (25k per arm) match the power calculation and the denominator matches the randomization unit, the combination of early stopping and reversed segment trends makes the current result untrustworthy. The 3.1% lift is likely noise or an artifact of traffic mix, not a true causal effect.
