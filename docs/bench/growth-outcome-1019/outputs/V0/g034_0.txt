The readout explicitly states the test was "ended early, on the day the p-value first crossed 0.05 after daily looks." This is a classic case of **peeking** (PEEK). Repeated significance testing without a pre-committed sample size or a valid sequential testing boundary (like mSPRT) inflates the false-positive rate far above the nominal 5%. The claim that the result is a "win" is statistically invalid because the stopping rule was data-dependent.

While the readout attempts to mitigate other concerns—confirming adequate power (25k users), correct denominators, consistent segment results (no Simpson's paradox), and stable lifts over time (no novelty effect)—the fundamental integrity of the hypothesis test is compromised by the early stopping procedure. A result derived from peeking cannot be treated as verified evidence for a rollout. The team must either extend the test to a pre-determined sample size calculated before looking at the data or re-analyze using a sequential testing framework that accounts for the multiple looks already taken. Until the statistical validity is restored, the rollout decision cannot be made.
