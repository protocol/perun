The readout contains three critical analysis errors that invalidate the recommendation to roll out.

First, **SRM** is present: the planned split was even, yet the arms received 60,400 and 50,100 visitors respectively, indicating broken assignment or logging. Second, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is explicitly described: while the pooled result shows a +9.2% lift, the treatment loses within both mobile and desktop segments, meaning the aggregate win is a statistical artifact. Third, **DENOM** is violated: users were the randomized unit, but conversion was computed per page view, misaligning the metric denominator with the experimental unit.

Although the test avoided early stopping (no PEEK), met sample size requirements (no POWER issue), and showed stability over time (no NOVELTY effect), the combination of sample ratio mismatch, reversed segment trends, and incorrect denominator calculation makes the headline lift unreliable. The contradictory segment data suggests the overall positive result is spurious. You cannot ship a change that performs worse in every user segment based on a flawed aggregate calculation.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
