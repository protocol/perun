The readout contains two critical flaws. First, it admits the test was stopped early based on daily significance checks ("ended early... after daily looks"), which invalidates the p-value due to repeated peeking (PEEK). Second, while the pooled total shows a lift, the treatment actually trails within every platform segment; the aggregate win is an artifact of the treatment group receiving a disproportionate share of high-converting desktop traffic. This is a classic case of Simpson's paradox (SIMPSON), meaning the change likely harms users on every specific platform despite the misleading overall average.

Although the sample size met the pre-committed power requirement and the denominator matches the randomization unit, the presence of Simpson's paradox indicates the change performs worse for every user segment analyzed. Rolling out a change that loses in every subgroup based on a distorted aggregate metric would be a mistake. The peeking further undermines confidence in the statistical significance of even the flawed aggregate number.

ERRORS: PEEK, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
