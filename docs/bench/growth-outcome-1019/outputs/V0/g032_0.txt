The readout contains multiple critical flaws that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, the visitor counts (60,400 vs 50,100) deviate significantly from the planned even split, indicating **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch). This suggests broken assignment or logging, making the groups incomparable.

Second, while the overall result is positive, the treatment loses in both mobile and desktop segments. This is a classic case of **SIMPSON** (Simpson's Paradox), where the aggregate metric masks negative performance across all underlying strata, likely driven by the imbalanced traffic mix rather than a true lift.

Third, the lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades, yet the projection assumes this day-one lift persists for a year. This is an unaddressed **NOVELTY** effect; projecting temporary curiosity as permanent value is analytically unsound.

Although the readout correctly notes that power targets were met (**POWER** is not flagged), the stopping rule was followed (**PEEK** is not flagged), and the denominator matches the randomization unit (**DENOM** is not flagged), the presence of SRM, Simpson's paradox, and unadjusted novelty effects renders the +9.4% headline figure unreliable. You cannot ship a change that performs worse in every segment while relying on a distorted aggregate driven by traffic imbalance and temporary user behavior.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
