The readout contains three critical flaws that invalidate the "win" claim. First, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is present: while the pooled result favors treatment, the effect reverses within both mobile and desktop segments, indicating the aggregate win is a statistical artifact of segment mixing. Second, **DENOM**inator error exists: users were randomized, yet conversion is computed per page view rather than per user, violating the unit of randomization and biasing the metric. Third, **NOVELTY** is a risk: the lift remained flat at 3% from day one through day 28. A genuine habit-forming change usually shows ramping adoption; a static immediate lift that never decays or grows often signals a temporary attention grab rather than sustained value, yet the recommendation projects this as a permanent gain.

Although the sample size (**POWER**) and stopping rule (**PEEK**) were handled correctly, and arm sizes appear balanced (no **SRM**), the structural errors in aggregation and metric definition make the current result unreadable. You cannot ship a change where the overall win disappears when looking at the actual user platforms, and where the metric denominator does not match the experimental unit.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
