The readout explicitly addresses several common pitfalls: the stopping rule was pre-commited and read once (mitigating PEEK), sample sizes match the power calculation (mitigating POWER), the denominator matches the randomization unit (mitigating DENOM), and results are consistent across segments (mitigating SIMPSON). The lift remained stable over 28 days, suggesting the effect is not a short-lived novelty (mitigating NOVELTY).

However, the readout states the arms received 52,300 and 47,700 users against a planned 50/50 split, while also claiming "Each arm got 25,000 users." This is a direct contradiction. If the actual traffic was ~100k total with a ~52/48 split, this indicates a Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM), implying broken assignment or logging. If the "25,000 users" figure refers to a subset used for analysis, the basis for that exclusion is unclear and potentially biased. Because the reported final counts (52,300 vs 47,700) deviate significantly from the planned 50/50 split and contradict the later statement about 25,000 users per arm, the integrity of the randomization is compromised.

ERRORS: SRM
DECISION: HOLD
