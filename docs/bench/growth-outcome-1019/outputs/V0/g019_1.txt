The readout contains two critical flaws. First, **PEEK**: the team "checked the dashboard every day and stopped the test the first day p fell below 0.05." This repeated peeking inflates the false-positive rate, making the reported significance unreliable. Second, **SIMPSON**: while the pooled total shows a lift, the treatment "trails within each platform" (mobile and desktop). The apparent win is an artifact of the treatment group receiving a disproportionate share of high-converting desktop traffic, not a genuine improvement in user experience.

Although the sample sizes match the plan (ruling out POWER and SRM concerns regarding volume) and the denominator matches the randomization unit (ruling out DENOM), the combination of early stopping based on peeking and a reversed trend within segments invalidates the causal claim. The 3% lift is likely noise or a distribution artifact rather than a real effect. You cannot ship based on a result that disappears when controlling for platform and was captured via a statistically invalid stopping rule.
