The readout explicitly describes multiple critical flaws that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, the arm sizes (52,300 vs 47,700) deviate significantly from the planned 50/50 split, indicating **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch), which suggests broken assignment or logging. Second, the test was stopped specifically because the p-value crossed 0.05 during daily looks, a classic **PEEK** error that inflates false positives; the claim that "25,000 users" were analyzed contradicts the actual unequal totals and ignores the invalid stopping rule. Third, while the power calculation was met in theory, the early stop and SRM undermine it, but the most glaring issue is **NOVELTY**: the lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades, yet the readout projects this transient spike as a permanent annual gain.

Although the denominator matches the randomized unit and Simpson's paradox is ruled out (consistent lift across platforms), the presence of SRM, peeking, and unadjusted novelty effects makes the reported +6.3% lift unreliable. The "clean" comparison claim is false given the traffic imbalance and sequential testing without correction. You cannot roll out a change based on a biased, prematurely stopped test with a fading effect.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
