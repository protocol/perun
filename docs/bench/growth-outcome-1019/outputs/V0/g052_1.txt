The readout contains multiple critical flaws that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, **SRM** is present: the planned split was even, yet Control has 60,400 users while Treatment has only 50,100. This significant deviation suggests broken assignment or logging, making the groups incomparable.

Second, **PEEK** is explicitly admitted: the test was stopped "on the day the p-value first crossed 0.05 after daily looks." This peeking inflates the false-positive rate, meaning the reported significance is likely noise rather than a real effect.

Third, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is occurring: while the aggregated data shows a +12.3% lift for Treatment, the treatment actually loses within *both* the mobile and desktop segments individually. Aggregating these losing segments into a winning total indicates a severe weighting imbalance (likely driven by the SRM) that masks the true negative performance across all user types.

Although the readout claims the sample size met power requirements (**POWER** handled) and the denominator matches the randomization unit (**DENOM** handled), the combination of sample ratio mismatch, peeking, and reversed segment trends makes the result unreliable. The claim of stability (**NOVELTY** handled) cannot overcome the fundamental validity errors in the test execution and aggregation.
