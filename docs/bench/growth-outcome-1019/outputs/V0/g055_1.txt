The readout describes a classic case of Simpson's Paradox. While the pooled data shows a +3.4% lift for the treatment, the text explicitly states the treatment "trails within each platform" (mobile and desktop). The apparent win is entirely driven by an imbalance in traffic mix: the treatment group received a disproportionately high volume of high-converting desktop users compared to the control. Aggregating these disparate groups masks the fact that the change actually performs worse for every individual user segment. Rolling this out based on the pooled metric would likely decrease overall conversion rates once the traffic mix normalizes.

Additionally, the reported visitor counts (60,400 vs 50,100) versus the analyzed user counts (25,000 each) suggests a potential Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM) in the raw logs or a strict per-user denominator application that excludes multi-visit users differently across arms. However, the primary fatal flaw preventing a ship decision is the reversed trend upon segmentation.
