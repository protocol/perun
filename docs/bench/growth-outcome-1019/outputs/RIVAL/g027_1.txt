The readout contains critical methodological flaws that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the change.

First, the metric definition violates the unit of randomization. Users were randomized, yet conversion is computed per page view (DENOM). This inflates the sample size artificially if users generate multiple views, biasing the significance testing.

Second, and most critically, the results exhibit Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON). While the aggregated data shows a win for the treatment, the treatment loses within *both* the mobile and desktop segments. An overall win driven by aggregation while losing in all constituent segments indicates a severe data integrity issue or confounding variable (e.g., uneven traffic mix between devices across arms) that makes the top-line number unreliable. You cannot ship a feature that performs worse for every user segment you measured.

Although the readout confirms adequate power, proper stopping rules, stable results over time (no novelty effect), and balanced arm sizes, the denominator error and the paradoxical segment performance render the primary finding untrustworthy.

ERRORS: DENOM, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
