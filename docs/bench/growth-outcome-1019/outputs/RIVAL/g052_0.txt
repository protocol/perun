This readout contains multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, the arm sizes (60,400 vs. 50,100) deviate significantly from the planned 50/50 split. With over 110,000 total users, this imbalance is too large to be random chance, indicating broken assignment or logging logic (**SRM**). When randomization fails, causal inference is impossible.

Second, the results exhibit **SIMPSON**'s paradox. While the aggregated data shows a +12.3% lift for the treatment, the treatment actually loses within *both* the mobile and desktop segments. This suggests the overall "win" is driven entirely by a shift in traffic composition (e.g., more high-converting desktop users accidentally landing in the treatment group due to the SRM) rather than the feature itself.

Third, the test was stopped the moment the p-value crossed 0.05 after daily checks (**PEEK**). This "peeking" behavior inflates the false positive rate, meaning the reported significance is likely illusory.

Although the readout claims the sample size matched a power calculation (**POWER** handled) and the lift was stable over time (**NOVELTY** handled), the combination of sample ratio mismatch, Simpson's paradox, and early stopping makes the results unreliable. The denominator is correct (**DENOM** handled), but the data integrity is compromised.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, PEEK
DECISION: HOLD
