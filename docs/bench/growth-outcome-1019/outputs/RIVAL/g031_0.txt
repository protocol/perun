The readout reveals two critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, there is a clear **DENOM** (denominator) error. The experiment randomized users, yet the primary metric divides conversions by sessions. Since heavy users generate multiple sessions, this violates the independence assumption of the statistical test and inflates the sample size artificially, leading to false confidence in the results. The metric must be calculated per user to match the randomization unit.

Second, the forecast relies on a **NOVELTY** effect. The readout explicitly states the lift was 12% on day one but decayed to 1% by day fourteen. Despite this sharp drop-off indicating users were initially intrigued by the animation but derived little lasting value, the team's forecast uses the inflated day-one figure. Projecting a temporary novelty spike as a permanent gain will severely overestimate the feature's impact.

While the test avoided early stopping (PEEK), achieved the planned sample size (POWER), maintained balanced arms (no SRM), and showed consistent direction across platforms (no SIMPSON), the denominator mismatch and the reliance on decaying lift make the current conclusion unsound. The analysis must be re-run with user-level metrics and a forecast based on the stabilized day-14 performance before any rollout decision.

ERRORS: DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
