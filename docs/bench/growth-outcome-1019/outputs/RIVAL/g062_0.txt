The readout describes a critical instance of **Simpson's paradox (SIMPSON)**. While the aggregated data shows a 3.2% lift for the treatment, the text explicitly states that the treatment *loses* within both the mobile and desktop segments. It is mathematically impossible for a variant to perform worse in every segment yet better overall unless there is a severe imbalance in how traffic was distributed between segments across the two arms (e.g., the treatment arm received a disproportionately high volume of high-converting desktop users compared to the control).

This indicates that the randomization or traffic allocation was flawed, rendering the aggregate "win" invalid. The positive headline number is an artifact of the mix, not the treatment effect. Rolling this out based on the pooled metric would likely result in a global conversion decrease, as both actual user segments (mobile and desktop) performed worse with the change.

The other potential errors appear handled: the arm sizes are balanced (no SRM), the analysis occurred at the planned end date (no PEEK), sample size targets were met (no POWER), the denominator matches the randomization unit (no DENOM), and the lift was stable over time (no NOVELTY). However, the segmentation contradiction is a fatal flaw that must be investigated before any decision is made.

ERRORS: SIMPSON
