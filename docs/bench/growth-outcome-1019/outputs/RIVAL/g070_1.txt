The readout presents a statistically sound case for the pause button, but one critical implementation error prevents immediate rollout.

The test suffered from **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch). The planned split was even (50/50), yet the results show 60,400 users in control versus 50,100 in treatment. This ~10% deviation is far too large to be random chance, indicating a broken assignment mechanism or logging error. When assignment is broken, the fundamental assumption of comparability between groups fails, invalidating the "win."

Other error categories are explicitly ruled out by the text:
*   **POWER**: Both arms exceeded the 25,000 user requirement.
*   **PEEK**: The result was read only once at the planned end date.
*   **DENOM**: The metric correctly uses the randomized user as the denominator.
*   **SIMPSON**: The treatment won in the aggregate and within both mobile and desktop segments.
*   **NOVELTY**: The lift remained stable over 28 days.

Despite the robust stability and segment consistency, the SRM means we cannot trust that the treatment group was actually comparable to the control group. The observed lift might be an artifact of *who* ended up in each group rather than the feature itself.

ERRORS: SRM
DECISION: HOLD
