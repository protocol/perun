The readout contains two critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the change.

First, there is a clear **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch). The test was planned as an even split, yet the results show 60,400 users in control versus only 50,100 in treatment. This roughly 55/45 split deviates significantly from the intended 50/50 allocation, suggesting broken assignment logic or data logging issues that compromise the integrity of the experiment.

Second, the results demonstrate **SIMPSON**'s paradox. While the aggregated data shows a +3.1% lift for the treatment, the readout explicitly states that the treatment *loses* within both the mobile and desktop segments. When a variant performs worse in every meaningful subgroup yet appears to win in the total, the aggregate result is a mathematical artifact, likely driven by the uneven sample sizes noted above. You cannot trust the overall lift when the underlying segments contradict it.

Although the test avoided early stopping (no PEEK), achieved sufficient sample size (no POWER issue), and used the correct denominator (no DENOM error), the presence of SRM and Simpson's paradox makes the current results unreliable. The observed "win" is likely false.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
