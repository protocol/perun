The readout describes a critical methodological flaw in how the primary metric was calculated. While users were the unit of randomization (25,000 per arm), the conversion metric was computed **per page view** rather than per user. This violates the principle that the analysis unit must match the randomization unit. If users in the treatment group viewed the page more or less frequently than those in the control (perhaps due to the countdown timer influencing behavior), the conversion rate per view will be biased, regardless of the actual effect on purchasing users. This is a clear case of the denominator error.

Although the test avoided peeking, had sufficient power, showed no sample ratio mismatch, and demonstrated consistency across segments and time, the fundamental metric definition renders the 7.4% lift unreliable for decision-making. The result could be driven entirely by changes in page-view frequency rather than true conversion improvement.

ERRORS: DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
