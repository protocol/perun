This readout contains multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the change.

First, the traffic split (50,200 vs 50,100) appears balanced, but the result is driven entirely by an uneven distribution of high-converting desktop traffic. The treatment leads in the total but trails within every platform segment, a classic case of **SIMPSON**'s paradox. Aggregating this data hides the fact that the variant actually performs worse for every user type.

Second, the analysis projects a day-one lift over a full year despite the lift fading daily after the first three days. This ignores the **NOVELTY** effect, where users react temporarily to a new interface rather than a genuine improvement in value.

Third, with only 120 users per arm and no power calculation, the test is severely underpowered (**POWER**). The sample size is insufficient to detect meaningful differences or support the claimed 14.2% lift with statistical confidence.

While the readout correctly notes that the denominator matches the randomized unit (avoiding DENOM) and that the stopping rule was followed (avoiding PEEK), the presence of Simpson's paradox, novelty bias, and low power makes the results unreliable.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, NOVELTY, POWER
DECISION: HOLD
