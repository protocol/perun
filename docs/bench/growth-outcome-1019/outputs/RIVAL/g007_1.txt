The readout presents two critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, the test exhibits **SIMPSON**'s paradox. While the aggregate data shows an 8.2% lift for the treatment, the treatment actually loses within *both* the mobile and desktop segments. When a trend appears in different groups of data but disappears or reverses when these groups are combined, the aggregate result is misleading and likely driven by an imbalance in how traffic was distributed between segments across the arms, not by the actual effect of the form change.

Second, the methodology confirms **PEEK**ing. The readout explicitly states the test was "ended early, on the day the p-value first crossed 0.05 after daily looks." Checking significance daily and stopping the moment a threshold is crossed inflates the false positive rate significantly, meaning the reported p-value is invalid.

Although the sample size met the power requirement (**POWER** is not flagged) and the arm sizes are close to the 50/50 plan (**SRM** is not flagged), the presence of Simpson's paradox and early stopping makes the results unreliable. You cannot determine if the change is truly beneficial or harmful until these analytical errors are resolved.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, PEEK
DECISION: HOLD
