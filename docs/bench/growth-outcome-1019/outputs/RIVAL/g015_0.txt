The readout contains multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, the test suffered from **PEEK**. The team performed daily looks and stopped the experiment the moment the p-value crossed 0.05. This "peeking" behavior inflates the false positive rate, meaning the observed significance is likely illusory.

Second, there is a severe **POWER** issue. The readout explicitly states "nobody sized the test" and reveals a sample size of only 120 users per arm. For a homepage metric like add-on rate (typically low single digits), detecting a 7.3% lift requires tens of thousands of users, not hundreds. The current sample is vastly underpowered to support the claimed result.

While the readout notes that the denominator matches the randomization unit (ruling out DENOM), segments align with the top-line result (ruling out SIMPSON), and the lift was stable over time (ruling out NOVELTY), the combination of optional stopping and insufficient sample size makes the result unreliable. The slight difference in visitor counts (50,200 vs 50,100) is negligible and likely due to random variation given the huge total N, so SRM is not the primary concern here compared to the design flaws.

You cannot roll out a change based on a statistically invalid test. The team must rerun the experiment with a pre-determined sample size calculated for the minimum detectable effect and commit to running the full duration without early stopping.

ERRORS: PEEK, POWER
DECISION: HOLD
