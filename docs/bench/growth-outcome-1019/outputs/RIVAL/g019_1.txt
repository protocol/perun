The readout contains multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, the team engaged in **PEEK**ing by checking the dashboard daily and stopping the test the moment p < 0.05, rather than waiting for a pre-determined sample size or using sequential testing methods. This inflates the false positive rate.

Second, there is clear evidence of **SIMPSON**'s paradox. While the pooled total shows a lift, the treatment actually trails within *each* platform segment. The aggregate win is an artifact of the treatment group receiving a disproportionate amount of high-converting desktop traffic, not because the variant is better.

Third, because the result reverses when looking at segments, the aggregate lift likely represents a **DENOM**inator issue where the metric calculation is skewed by the uneven traffic mix across platforms, failing to isolate the true effect of the change on comparable user groups.

Although the sample size met the power calculation (**POWER** is not an issue) and the split is roughly even (**SRM** is not explicitly confirmed as broken, though the traffic mix suggests potential assignment bias), the presence of Simpson's paradox and early stopping makes the result unreliable. The variant is actually worse for every specific user type tested.

ERRORS: PEEK, SIMPSON, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
