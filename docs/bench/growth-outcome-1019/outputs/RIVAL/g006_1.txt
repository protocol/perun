The readout contains two critical analytical errors that invalidate the rollout recommendation.

First, while the test was properly powered and stopped according to plan, the **forecast uses the day-one lift (12%)** rather than the full-period result (1%). The day-one spike likely represents users reacting to the change simply because it is new, not because it is better. Projecting this temporary spike as a permanent gain is a classic **NOVELTY** error. The decision must be based on the stabilized day-14 metric.

Second, the test modified two distinct elements simultaneously: the submit button label AND the email field placeholder. Because both variables changed between the control and treatment, it is impossible to isolate which specific change drove the observed effect. While this is primarily a design flaw, it compounds the risk of acting on the inflated novelty lift. If the placeholder change caused confusion that only manifested after day one, it could explain the drop from 12% to 1%. Rolling out both changes based on the initial spike ignores the possibility that one element is actually harmful.

The recommendation to roll out based on the 12% figure is unsupported by the full data window. The team must re-evaluate using the day-14 conversion rate and consider isolating variables in a future test to determine which specific change is valuable.

ERRORS: NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
