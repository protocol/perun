The readout contains multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, the test suffered from **PEEK**. The team performed daily looks and stopped the experiment the moment the p-value crossed 0.05. This "peeking" behavior drastically inflates the false positive rate, meaning the observed win is likely statistical noise rather than a real effect.

Second, there is a clear **POWER** issue. The readout explicitly states "nobody sized the test for the lift it reports" and notes only 120 users per arm. Detecting a modest 3% lift requires tens of thousands of users (as seen in the actual traffic of ~50k per arm), not hundreds. The sample size is insufficient to support the conclusion.

While the arm sizes (50,080 vs 49,920) are acceptable and segments align with the overall result (ruling out SRM and SIMPSON), the combination of optional stopping and severe underpowering makes the result unreliable. The consistency of the 3% lift over 28 days is irrelevant if the statistical method used to declare significance was flawed from the start.

ERRORS: PEEK, POWER
DECISION: HOLD
