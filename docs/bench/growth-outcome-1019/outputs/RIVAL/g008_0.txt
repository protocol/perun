The readout contains multiple critical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, **PEEK** is present: the test was stopped early based on daily looks until p < 0.05. This "peeking" behavior drastically inflates the false positive rate, meaning the observed win is likely statistical noise rather than a real effect.

Second, **DENOM** is flagged. The readout states users were randomized, but conversion was computed per page view. If users generate different numbers of page views across variants, this denominator mismatch biases the result.

Third, **NOVELTY** is evident. The lift collapsed from 12% on day one to 1% by day fourteen, yet the forecast relies entirely on the initial 12% spike. This indicates a short-lived novelty effect rather than a sustainable improvement. Projecting the day-one lift as permanent is analytically unsound.

While the arm sizes (50,080 vs 49,920) appear acceptable and the test had sufficient power (25k per arm), and Simpson's paradox is explicitly ruled out (treatment wins in both segments), the combination of early stopping, metric definition errors, and novelty decay makes the current result unreliable.

ERRORS: PEEK, DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
