The readout describes a critical instance of **Simpson's paradox (SIMPSON)**. While the aggregate data shows a +3.1% lift for the treatment, the result reverses within every specific segment: the treatment loses against the control on both mobile and desktop. This indicates the overall "win" is a statistical artifact caused by an imbalance in traffic allocation between segments (e.g., if the treatment group had a disproportionately high number of users from the higher-converting segment), rather than a genuine improvement in the user experience.

Rolling out a feature that performs worse for every user segment based on a misleading aggregate number would likely decrease overall renewal rates once the traffic imbalance normalizes. The persistent lift over 28 days argues against a **NOVELTY** effect.

Because the directional result is inverted when controlling for segments, the aggregate finding is invalid for decision-making.
