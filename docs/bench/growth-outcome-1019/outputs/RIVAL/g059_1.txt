The readout describes two critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, there is a **Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM)**. The planned split was 50/50, yet the arms received 52,300 and 47,700 users respectively. This 4,600-user deviation (a 52.3% vs 47.7% split) strongly suggests a bug in the assignment logic or data logging, meaning the groups are not comparable.

Second, the test exhibits **Peeking (PEEK)**. The team monitored results daily and stopped the experiment the moment $p < 0.05$. This "optional stopping" invalidates the p-value because repeated significance testing inflates the false positive rate. Even though the final sample size matched the power calculation, the decision to stop was driven by an interim look rather than a fixed horizon.

While the readout indicates the test was adequately powered (POWER handled), the denominator matches the randomization unit (DENOM handled), results are consistent across segments (SIMPSON handled), and the lift remained stable over time (NOVELTY handled), the presence of SRM and PEEK makes the reported +6.2% lift unreliable. The assignment bias and inflated false positive risk mean the observed difference could be entirely artificial.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK
DECISION: HOLD
