This readout contains multiple critical flaws that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the treatment.

First, stopping the test the moment the p-value crossed 0.05 after daily looks is a classic **PEEK** error. This "peeking" inflates the false positive rate, meaning the reported significance is likely illusory.

Second, while the power calculation was correct for 25,000 users per arm, the actual traffic was roughly 50k/50k. However, the forecast relies entirely on the **NOVELTY** effect: using the 12% lift from day one to predict long-term results, despite the lift decaying to 1% by day fourteen. Projecting the initial spike as sustainable is misleading.

Third, the metric definition triggers a **DENOM** error. Users were randomized, but conversion was computed per page view. If the treatment changed user behavior regarding page reloads or multiple views, the denominator no longer aligns with the unit of randomization, biasing the result.

Although the segment results (mobile/desktop) align with the overall win (ruling out **SIMPSON**) and the arm sizes are acceptably close to 50/50 (ruling out **SRM**), the combination of early stopping, novelty bias, and denominator mismatch makes the data unreliable for a rollout decision.

ERRORS: PEEK, DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
