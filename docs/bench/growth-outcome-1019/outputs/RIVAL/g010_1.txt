The readout demonstrates sound experimental design in several areas: the arm sizes are balanced (ruling out SRM), the sample size exceeds the power calculation (ruling out POWER), the analysis occurred only at the planned end date (ruling out PEEK), and the metric denominator matches the randomized unit (ruling out DENOM). Additionally, the treatment leads consistently across segments, avoiding Simpson's paradox.

However, a critical error exists in how the results are interpreted and projected. The data explicitly shows that the lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades daily thereafter. Despite this clear decay pattern indicating a temporary behavioral response, the readout projects the day-one lift across a full year. This is a classic NOVELTY error: users likely reacted strongly to the new reminder initially, but the effect diminishes as they habituate. Projecting a transient spike as a permanent annual gain will significantly overestimate the feature's long-term value and ROI.

Because the financial justification for the rollout relies on an inflated, unsupported projection rather than the observed decaying trend, the decision cannot be made until the forecast is corrected to reflect the fading effect.

ERRORS: NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
