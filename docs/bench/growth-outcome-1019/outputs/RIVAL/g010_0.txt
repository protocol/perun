The readout demonstrates sound experimental design in several areas: the arm sizes are balanced (ruling out SRM), the analysis occurred only at the planned end date (ruling out PEEK), the sample size exceeded the power calculation requirements (ruling out POWER), and the metric denominator correctly matches the randomized unit (ruling out DENOM). Additionally, the treatment lead is consistent across segments, avoiding Simpson's paradox.

However, a critical error exists in how the results are interpreted and projected. The data explicitly shows that the lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades daily thereafter. Despite this clear decay pattern indicating a temporary behavioral response, the readout projects the day-one lift across a full year. This assumes a permanent change in user behavior based on a transient spike, which is the definition of a novelty effect error. Rolling out the feature based on inflated long-term revenue projections derived from short-term novelty would lead to incorrect business decisions.

ERRORS: NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
