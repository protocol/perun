This readout contains multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the "Shelfie" feature.

First, the traffic allocation is broken. With a planned even split, the observed counts (60,400 vs. 50,100) indicate a **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch), suggesting assignment or logging failures.

Second, the results exhibit **SIMPSON**'s paradox. While the pooled total shows a lift, the treatment actually trails the control within every individual platform segment. The aggregate "win" is an artifact of the treatment group receiving a disproportionate amount of high-converting desktop traffic, not a genuine improvement.

Third, the projection suffers from **NOVELTY** bias. The lift is explicitly described as concentrated in the first three days and fading thereafter, yet the readout incorrectly projects this temporary spike as a sustained annual gain.

Although the test avoided early stopping (**PEEK**) and met the minimum sample size threshold (**POWER**), the fundamental integrity of the assignment, the reversal of effects in segments, and the unrealistic projection render the current conclusion unsafe.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
