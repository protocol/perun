The readout contains two critical analytical errors that invalidate the rollout recommendation.

First, there is a **NOVELTY** error. The data explicitly shows the lift decaying from 12% on day one to 1% by day fourteen. Despite this clear evidence of a fading novelty effect, the forecast incorrectly uses the initial 12% spike to project long-term value. This overstates the benefit and ignores the trend indicating the change has negligible impact once users acclimate.

Second, there is a **SIMPSON** error context. While the readout claims the treatment leads in both the pooled total and within each platform, the aggregation of results across platforms while reporting a single pooled lift creates the conditions for Simpson's Paradox. If the traffic mix between platforms shifts over time or differs in future cohorts, the pooled average may not hold, especially given the conflicting signal between the strong Day 1 result and the weak Day 14 result. The reliance on the pooled 12% figure despite segment-level decay suggests the aggregate is masking the true lack of efficacy in steady-state conditions.

Additionally, the test changed two variables simultaneously (button label and placeholder text), violating the principle of isolating variables, though this is a design flaw rather than a specific analysis error code provided.

Because the forecast relies on a known transient spike rather than the stabilized Day 14 performance, the projected ROI is false.

ERRORS: NOVELTY, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
