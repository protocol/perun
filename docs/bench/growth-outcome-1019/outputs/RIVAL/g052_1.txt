The readout contains multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch) is present. Under a planned even split, Control received 60,400 users while Treatment received only 50,100. This significant deviation suggests broken assignment or logging, making the groups incomparable.

Second, **SIMPSON** (Simpson's Paradox) is explicitly described. While the pooled data shows a +12.3% lift for the treatment, the treatment actually *loses* within both the mobile and desktop segments. Aggregating these losing segments to produce a winning total indicates a severe data integrity issue or confounding variable, rendering the headline metric meaningless.

Third, **PEEK** (Peeking) occurred. The test was stopped "on the day the p-value first crossed 0.05 after daily looks." This optional stopping procedure inflates the false positive rate, meaning the reported significance is unreliable.

While the readout notes the sample size matched a power calculation (**POWER** handled) and the denominator matched the randomization unit (**DENOM** handled), the presence of SRM, Simpson's Paradox, and Peeking makes the results untrustworthy. You cannot roll out a feature based on data where the treatment loses in every segment yet wins overall, especially when the test was stopped early and traffic allocation was broken.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
