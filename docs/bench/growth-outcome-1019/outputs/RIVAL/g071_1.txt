The readout presents a mixed picture. While the traffic split is balanced (ruling out SRM) and the analysis occurred only at the planned end date (ruling out PEEK), and the sample size matches the power calculation (ruling out POWER), there are critical methodological and interpretive flaws.

First, the metric definition creates a DENOM error. The experiment randomized users, but the primary metric divides conversions by sessions. Since heavy users generate more sessions, this violates the principle that the analysis unit must match the randomization unit, biasing the results.

Second, the readout exhibits a NOVELTY error. The lift is explicitly concentrated in the first three days and fades thereafter, yet the recommendation projects this initial spike as a sustained annual gain. This ignores the decay pattern evident in the data.

Finally, while the text claims consistency across platforms, the denominator mismatch and novelty decay make the pooled +4.8% lift unreliable for decision-making. The projection of a temporary effect as permanent is particularly dangerous for a rollout decision.

ERRORS: DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
