This readout contains multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, the test suffered from **PEEK**. The team performed daily looks and stopped the experiment the moment the p-value crossed 0.05. This "peeking" behavior inflates the false positive rate, meaning the reported significance is likely illusory.

Second, there is a clear case of **SIMPSON**'s paradox. While the pooled data shows an 8.7% lift for the treatment, the readout explicitly states the treatment *trails* within every platform segment. The apparent win is entirely driven by an imbalance in traffic composition (treatment received more high-converting desktop users), not the layout change itself. If rolled out to everyone, the lift would likely vanish or reverse.

While the sample size met the initial power calculation (**POWER** is not flagged because $N$ was reached), the validity of the result is destroyed by the stopping rule and the aggregation bias. The stability of the lift over 28 days argues against a **NOVELTY** effect, and the visitor counts suggest **SRM** is not the primary issue compared to the compositional skew.

You cannot ship a feature that loses in every segment but wins only due to uneven traffic allocation.

ERRORS: PEEK, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
