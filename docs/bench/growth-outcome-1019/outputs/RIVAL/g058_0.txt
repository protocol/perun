The readout contains two critical analytical errors that invalidate the rollout recommendation.

First, while the test achieved the required sample size and the overall result is positive, the **POWER** of the specific claim is mismatched. The readout states the lift is "concentrated in the first three days and fades," yet it projects the "day-one lift for a full year." A power calculation based on the initial spike does not support a conclusion about sustained annual performance if the effect decays to zero. The sample is insufficient to prove the long-term value being claimed.

Second, and more severely, this is a clear **NOVELTY** error. The data explicitly describes a short-lived novelty effect ("fades each day after"), yet the recommendation relies on projecting that temporary spike indefinitely. Rolling out a feature based on novelty bias often leads to disappointing long-term results once the "newness" wears off for the broader population. The readout identifies the decay but ignores its implication for the forecast.

The other categories are handled correctly: the split is even (no SRM), the analysis happened at the planned end (no PEEK), segments align with the total (no SIMPSON), and the metric denominator matches the randomization unit (no DENOM). However, the novelty and projection errors are fatal to the current business case.

ERRORS: POWER, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
