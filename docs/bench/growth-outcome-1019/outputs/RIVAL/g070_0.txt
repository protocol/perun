The readout describes a significant Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The planned split was even, yet the control group received 60,400 visitors while the treatment group received only 50,100. This ~17% discrepancy strongly suggests a broken assignment mechanism or logging error, which invalidates the comparison between groups. Even though the treatment wins in overall and segment-level analysis, the fundamental integrity of the randomization is compromised.

Additionally, the readout notes the lift held steady from day one through day twenty-eight. While this consistency might initially seem positive, the massive imbalance in group sizes casts doubt on whether the "steady" lift is real or an artifact of the skewed traffic distribution. The fact that the result was read only once at the planned end date avoids peeking errors, and the sample size exceeds the power calculation requirements, ruling out PEEK and POWER issues. The metric denominator matches the randomized unit, avoiding DENOM errors. There is no evidence of Simpson's paradox since the treatment wins in both segments. No novelty effect is indicated since the lift remained constant over four weeks.

However, the SRM is a critical failure that must be investigated and resolved before any rollout decision can be made. The traffic imbalance indicates a systematic problem with how users were assigned or tracked, making the reported lift unreliable.
