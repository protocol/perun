The readout contains two critical methodological errors. First, the team engaged in **PEEK**ing by checking the dashboard daily and stopping the test the moment p < 0.05. This optional stopping procedure invalidates the p-value and significantly inflates the false positive rate; the result is not statistically reliable.

Second, there is a **DENOM**inator error. The readout explicitly states that "users were the unit randomized," yet conversion was "computed per page view." If users generate multiple page views, analyzing at the page-view level violates the independence assumption of standard statistical tests, artificially narrowing confidence intervals and increasing the risk of false positives. The analysis must be re-run using the user as the unit of analysis.

While the sample size met power requirements (**POWER** handled), arm sizes were balanced (**SRM** handled), and results were consistent across segments (**SIMPSON** handled), the statistical validity is compromised by the stopping rule and the unit-of-analysis mismatch. The sustained lift over 28 days suggests **NOVELTY** is less likely to be the sole driver, but the statistical proof is broken.

ERRORS: PEEK, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
