The readout describes multiple critical flaws that invalidate the reported lift.

First, **SRM** is present: an even split was planned, yet Control received 60,400 visitors while Treatment received only 50,100. This significant deviation suggests broken assignment or logging logic.

Second, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is explicitly admitted. While the pooled total shows a 6.8% lift, the Treatment actually trails within *each* platform segment. The aggregate "win" is an artifact of the Treatment group having a disproportionate amount of high-converting desktop traffic, not because the feature works.

Third, **DENOM** error exists. The experiment randomized users, but the metric divides by sessions. Since heavy users generate many sessions, this violates the requirement that the analysis unit match the randomization unit, biasing the results toward power users.

While the readout confirms the test avoided early stopping (not PEEK) and met sample size requirements (not POWER), the combination of sample ratio mismatch, Simpson's paradox, and denominator mismatch makes the 6.8% lift unreliable. The fact that the feature loses in every specific segment strongly suggests it provides no value or is harmful.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
