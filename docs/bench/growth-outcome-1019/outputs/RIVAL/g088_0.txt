The readout explicitly identifies two critical statistical errors that invalidate the headline result. First, **SRM** is present: the planned even split was not achieved (60,400 control vs. 50,100 treatment), indicating broken assignment or logging. Second, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is confirmed: while the pooled total shows a +6.2% lift, the treatment actually trails within every platform segment. The aggregate lift is an artifact of the treatment group receiving a disproportionate share of high-converting desktop traffic, not a genuine improvement. Additionally, **DENOM** is flagged because the conversion metric is computed per page view, whereas users were the randomized unit; this mismatch can bias variance estimates and significance testing.

The readout confirms that other common errors are absent: the test ran to the planned end date (no **PEEK**), sample sizes exceeded the power calculation (no **POWER**), and the lift remained stable over four weeks (no **NOVELTY**). However, the presence of Simpson's paradox alone is sufficient to reverse the conclusion: the change is likely performing worse than the control across all user types. Rolling this out based on the pooled number would be a mistake.

DECISION: HOLD
