The readout contains multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is explicitly present: the treatment wins overall but loses within both the mobile and desktop segments. This indicates a severe imbalance in traffic composition between arms (e.g., the treatment arm received disproportionately more high-converting traffic), making the aggregate result misleading. The true effect is negative.

Second, **DENOM** error exists because the metric divides conversions by sessions, while randomization occurred at the user level. Since heavy users generate many sessions, this violates the independence assumption required for standard significance tests, inflating false positive rates.

Third, **NOVELTY** error is evident. The lift dropped from 12% on day one to 1% by day fourteen, yet the forecast relies on the initial 12% spike. This suggests a short-term curiosity effect rather than a sustainable improvement. Projecting the day-one lift as permanent is analytically unsound.

While the test avoided **PEEK** (stopped at planned end date), **SRM** (arm sizes are within chance expectations), and **POWER** (sample size exceeded requirements), the presence of Simpson's paradox alone proves the treatment is actually detrimental to both major user segments. Combined with the denominator mismatch and novelty decay, the data supports rejecting the change, not rolling it out.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
