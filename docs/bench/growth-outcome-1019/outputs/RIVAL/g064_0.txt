This readout contains multiple critical analytical errors that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the new layout.

First, while the sample sizes are balanced (ruling out SRM) and the test reached its planned sample size without early stopping (ruling out PEEK and POWER), the metric calculation is fundamentally flawed. The test randomized users, but conversion was computed per page view. If users in one arm visited the page more frequently, this inflates the denominator incorrectly, constituting a DENOM error.

Second, the data exhibits Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON). Although the treatment leads in the pooled total, it actually trails within every individual platform segment. The aggregate lift is an artifact of the treatment group receiving a disproportionate amount of high-converting desktop traffic, not because the layout is superior.

Third, the projection suffers from a NOVELTY error. The lift is explicitly described as concentrated in the first three days and fading thereafter, yet the readout incorrectly projects this transient spike as a sustained annual gain. This suggests users reacted to the change's newness rather than its utility.

Because the aggregate result reverses when looking at segments, the metric denominator mismatches the randomization unit, and the long-term value projection ignores the fading effect, the evidence does not support a rollout.

ERRORS: DENOM, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
