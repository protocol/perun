The readout contains multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, **PEEK** is present: the team checked results daily and stopped immediately upon reaching p < 0.05. This "peeking" behavior inflates the false positive rate, meaning the reported significance is likely illusory.

Second, **DENOM** is a fundamental flaw. The experiment randomized users, but the metric divides conversions by *sessions*. Since heavy users generate multiple sessions, they are over-represented in the denominator, violating the unit of randomization and biasing the result.

Third, **POWER** is misapplied. While the sample size (25k/arm) met the pre-calculated requirement, that calculation is invalid because the analysis method (peeking) and metric definition (session-based) violate the assumptions required for the power calculation to hold.

Although **SRM** is not an issue (49,920 vs 50,080 is within chance variation) and **SIMPSON**'s paradox is avoided (treatment wins in both segments), the combination of peeking and the wrong denominator renders the primary finding unreliable. The consistency of the lift over 28 days suggests the effect might be real, but **NOVELTY** cannot be fully ruled out without a proper holdout analysis, and the statistical proof provided is broken.

You cannot make a valid rollout decision on broken statistics. The test must be re-analyzed using user-level metrics and proper sequential testing methods or re-run with a fixed horizon.
