The readout contains multiple critical flaws that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, **DENOM** is present: assignment occurred at the user level, but the primary metric divides conversions by sessions. Since heavy users generate many sessions, this violates the unit of randomization, biasing the result toward high-frequency users rather than reflecting true user-level impact.

Second, **NOVELTY** is evident: the lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades daily, yet the readout explicitly projects this day-one lift over a full year. This assumes a temporary curiosity effect is a permanent behavioral change, vastly overstating long-term value.

While the readout indicates proper sample sizes were reached (addressing POWER), a single analysis at the end date (addressing PEEK), and consistent results across platforms with balanced traffic (addressing SRM and SIMPSON), the denominator mismatch and unjustified long-term projection are fatal errors. You cannot calculate a valid retention rate by dividing user-level outcomes by session-level denominators, nor can you annualize a decaying novelty spike.

ERRORS: DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
