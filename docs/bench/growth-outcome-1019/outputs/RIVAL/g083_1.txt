The readout contains three critical analytical errors that invalidate the rollout recommendation.

First, **POWER**: The test is severely underpowered. With only 120 users per arm, the sample size is insufficient to reliably detect the reported +8.3% lift, making the result statistically fragile and likely a false positive.

Second, **NOVELTY**: The lift is explicitly described as concentrated in the first three days and fading thereafter, yet the readout incorrectly projects this transient day-one lift over a full year. This ignores the habituation effect where users initially engage with novelty before reverting to baseline behavior.

Third, **PEEK**: While the readout claims the result was read once at the planned end date, the specific mention of observing daily fade patterns ("fades each day after") suggests interim monitoring occurred. If the decision to stop or the interpretation of results was influenced by observing these daily trends before the final analysis, it constitutes peeking, which inflates false positive rates. However, since the text explicitly states "result was read once, at the planned end date," PEEK is technically handled by protocol, even if the analysis describes interim patterns. The primary fatal flaws are the lack of power and the novelty projection.

The combination of an underpowered sample and projecting a fleeting novelty effect as a permanent gain makes the recommendation to roll out dangerous. The team cannot distinguish signal from noise, nor can they justify the long-term value proposition.

ERRORS: POWER, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
