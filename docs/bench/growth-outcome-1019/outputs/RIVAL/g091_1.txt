The readout contains two critical errors that invalidate the rollout recommendation.

First, there is a **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch). The planned split was 50/50, yet the arms received 52,300 and 47,700 users respectively. This significant deviation suggests broken assignment or logging logic, meaning the groups are not comparable.

Second, there is a **NOVELTY** error. The lift dropped precipitously from 12% on day one to 1% by day fourteen, indicating a short-lived novelty effect rather than a sustainable improvement. Despite this decay, the forecast incorrectly uses the initial 12% figure to justify the rollout.

While the readout correctly notes a single analysis at the planned end date (avoiding PEEK), sufficient sample size per arm (addressing POWER), consistent results across segments (avoiding SIMPSON), and the correct metric denominator (avoiding DENOM), the presence of SRM and the misinterpretation of the novelty effect are fatal flaws. You cannot trust the results when the randomization is compromised and the projected lift ignores the rapid decay of the effect.

ERRORS: SRM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
