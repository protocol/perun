First, **DENOM** is present: users were randomized, but the metric (conversion) was computed per page view. This denominator mismatch violates the unit of randomization, biasing the results if users generated different numbers of page views across arms.

Second, **SRM** is evident: the planned split was 50/50, yet the arms received 52,300 and 47,700 users. This 4,600-user deviation suggests a broken assignment or logging mechanism, meaning the groups are likely not comparable.

While the readout confirms the test avoided **PEEK** (stopped only at the planned end date) and **POWER** (sample size exceeded requirements), and explicitly notes consistency across segments (ruling out **SIMPSON**), the presence of SRM and DENOM errors makes the observed 3.1% lift unreliable. The stability of the lift over 28 days suggests **NOVELTY** is not the primary issue here, but the fundamental structural errors in measurement and assignment take precedence.

You cannot trust the result until the unit of analysis matches the unit of randomization and the sample ratio mismatch is explained and fixed.
