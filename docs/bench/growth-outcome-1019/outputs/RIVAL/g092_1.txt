The readout contains multiple critical analysis errors that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the change.

First, **SRM** is present: the arms received 52,300 and 47,700 users against a planned 50/50 split. This significant deviation suggests broken assignment or logging, making the groups incomparable.

Second, **DENOM** is explicitly admitted: "Conversions are divided by sessions while assignment was by user." Since heavy users generate many sessions, this inflates the sample size artificially and violates the unit of randomization, rendering the p-values and confidence intervals invalid.

Third, **NOVELTY** is evident: the lift dropped from 12% on day one to 1% by day fourteen, yet the forecast relies on the initial 12% spike. This projects a short-lived novelty effect as a permanent gain.

While the test avoided **PEEK** (single analysis at end date) and met **POWER** requirements (exceeded 25k per arm), the combination of sample ratio mismatch, incorrect metric denominators, and novelty bias makes the results unreliable. The fact that the treatment wins in both mobile and desktop segments does not rescue the analysis when the fundamental metric calculation is flawed.

ERRORS: SRM, DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
