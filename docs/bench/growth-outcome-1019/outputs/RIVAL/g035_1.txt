The readout describes multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, the test exhibits **PEEK**. The team performed daily looks and stopped the experiment the moment the p-value crossed 0.05. This "peeking" behavior inflates the false positive rate, meaning the observed significance is likely illusory.

Second, the results demonstrate **SIMPSON**'s paradox. While the pooled total shows a treatment lift, the treatment actually trails within every individual platform segment. The aggregate win is driven entirely by an imbalance in traffic composition (the treatment group received disproportionately more high-converting desktop users), not by the filter's effectiveness.

Third, there is a **DENOM** error in the analysis logic. Although the text claims conversion is computed per randomized user, the explanation for the paradox relies on the treatment drawing "far more" high-converting traffic. If the arms truly had equal user counts (25,000 each as stated in the power section, despite the 50k/50k visitor split mentioned earlier), the composition argument suggests the metric attribution or randomization integrity failed. The discrepancy between the visitor split (50,200 vs 50,100) and the power calculation sample (25,000 each) also hints at **SRM** or data inconsistency, but the Simpson's paradox driven by traffic mix is the most explicit fatal flaw described.

Because the aggregate lift is a mathematical artifact of traffic mix rather than a true treatment effect, and the stopping rule was invalid, the results cannot support a rollout.

ERRORS: PEEK, SIMPSON, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
