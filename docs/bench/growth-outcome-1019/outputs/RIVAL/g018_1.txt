The readout presents a statistically sound experiment with one critical methodological flaw. The sample sizes (50,080 vs. 49,920) align closely with the planned 50/50 split, ruling out **SRM**. The test ran to a pre-determined end date without interim peeking, avoiding **PEEK**, and exceeded the required sample size of 25,000 per arm, satisfying **POWER**. Results are consistent across the pooled total and individual platforms, eliminating **SIMPSON**'s paradox. The lift remained stable over 28 days with no decay, suggesting the effect is not a fleeting **NOVELTY** bias.

However, the analysis suffers from a denominator error. Users were the unit of randomization, yet the primary metric (booking conversion) was computed per page view. If users in the treatment arm generated a different number of page views than those in the control (e.g., due to the simplified flow encouraging more exploration), the metric violates the independence assumption of the statistical test. This is a **DENOM** error. Because the metric's denominator does not match the randomized unit, the reported significance and confidence intervals are invalid. The analysis must be re-run using user-level aggregation (e.g., total bookings per user) before a rollout decision can be made.

ERRORS: DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
