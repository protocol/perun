The readout explicitly identifies multiple critical errors that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the change.

First, **SRM** is present: the arms received 52,300 and 47,700 users against a planned 50/50 split. This significant deviation suggests broken assignment or logging, making the groups incomparable.

Second, **PEEK** occurred: the test was stopped early on the day the p-value crossed 0.05 after daily looks. This "peeking" inflates the false positive rate, meaning the reported significance is likely illusory.

Third, **NOVELTY** is admitted: the lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades thereafter, yet the readout incorrectly projects the day-one lift for a full year. This indicates a short-term curiosity effect rather than a lasting usability improvement.

While the readout claims the sample size matches the power calculation (**POWER** handled), the denominator matches the randomization unit (**DENOM** handled), and the result holds across segments (**SIMPSON** handled), the presence of SRM, PEEK, and NOVELTY makes the results unreliable. You cannot ship a feature based on data where the groups are mismatched, the stopping rule was violated, and the projected benefit ignores the observed decay.
