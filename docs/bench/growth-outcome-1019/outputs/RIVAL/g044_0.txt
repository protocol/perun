The readout describes multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the results.

First, **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch) is present: the planned split was 50/50, yet the arms received 52,300 and 47,700 users. This 4,600-user discrepancy suggests broken assignment or logging, making the groups incomparable.

Second, **PEEK** (Peeking) occurred. The team checked the dashboard daily and stopped the test the "first day p fell below 0.05." This optional stopping rule inflates the false positive rate, rendering the p-value meaningless without correction.

Third, **DENOM** (Denominator Error) is explicitly admitted: "Conversions are divided by sessions while assignment was by user." Because heavy users generate more sessions, this biases the metric toward users with higher activity rather than measuring the true conversion rate per randomized unit. While a secondary check was tracked, the primary decision metric is flawed.

Although the readout claims sufficient sample size (**POWER** is handled) and consistent results across segments and time (**SIMPSON** and **NOVELTY** appear handled), the presence of SRM, PEEK, and DENOM errors means the observed lift cannot be trusted as causal. The imbalance in group sizes combined with early stopping and incorrect metric calculation creates a high risk that the "win" is an artifact of methodology rather than a real improvement.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
