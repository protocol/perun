The readout contains critical statistical contradictions that invalidate the recommendation. First, there is a clear case of **SIMPSON**'s paradox: the treatment wins overall yet loses in both the mobile and desktop segments. Since these segments comprise the total population, the aggregate win is mathematically impossible unless there is a data aggregation error or hidden confounding variable.

Second, the **POWER** category is flagged. The readout explicitly states, "nobody sized the test for the lift it reports," and the sample size is absurdly low ("120 users" per arm) compared to the visitor counts mentioned earlier (50,000+). A sample of 120 is insufficient to detect typical conversion lifts with statistical significance, rendering the reported win unreliable.

While the readout claims no early looks (avoiding **PEEK**) and defines the denominator correctly as the randomized user (avoiding **DENOM**), the presence of Simpson's paradox and the admitted lack of power make the results untrustworthy. You cannot ship a feature based on data where the whole contradicts the sum of its parts and the sample size was never validated.
