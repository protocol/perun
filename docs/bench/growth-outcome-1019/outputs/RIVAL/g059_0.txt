The readout describes a Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). The planned split was 50/50, yet the arms received 52,300 and 47,700 users. This 4,600-user difference (approx. 9.2% deviation) exceeds what chance typically allows in a properly randomized experiment, indicating a broken assignment or logging mechanism. When SRM is present, the comparison groups are not equivalent, invalidating the causal claim that the carousel caused the +6.2% lift. The observed difference could be driven by the type of users skewed into the treatment arm rather than the feature itself.

While the readout notes the team stopped the test the moment p < 0.05 (a practice known as peeking), it also states they reached the pre-calculated sample size of 25,000 per arm required for power. Because the stopping point coincided with achieving the required sample size rather than occurring prematurely *before* reaching it, the specific "PEEK" error defined as stopping early due to interim looks inflating false positives is technically mitigated by the sample size achievement, though the methodology remains risky. However, the SRM is a fundamental structural failure that cannot be ignored. The stability over four weeks addresses potential novelty effects, and the denominator matches the randomization unit.

Because the group sizes differ significantly from the planned split, the results are unreliable regardless of the p-value or stability. You cannot trust the lift figure until the randomization logic is fixed and the test is re-run with balanced arms.
