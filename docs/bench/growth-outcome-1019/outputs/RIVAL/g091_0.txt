The readout contains two critical errors that invalidate the rollout recommendation.

First, there is a **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch). The planned split was 50/50, yet the arms received 52,300 and 47,700 users respectively. This roughly 5% deviation suggests a broken assignment or logging mechanism, meaning the groups are not comparable.

Second, there is a **NOVELTY** error. The lift dropped precipitously from 12% on day one to 1% by day fourteen. This pattern indicates a short-lived novelty effect where users reacted to the change initially but disengaged once it became familiar. The forecast incorrectly uses the inflated day-one figure (12%) rather than the stabilized day-fourteen figure (1%) to project long-term value.

While the readout correctly notes that the analysis avoided peeking, had sufficient power based on the *claimed* 25k per arm (ignoring the SRM contradiction), avoided Simpson's paradox, and used the correct denominator, the presence of SRM and the misinterpretation of the novelty effect make the current recommendation unsafe.

ERRORS: SRM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
