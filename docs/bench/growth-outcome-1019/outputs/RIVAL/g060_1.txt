The readout presents multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is explicitly present: the treatment wins overall but loses within both the mobile and desktop segments. This indicates a severe aggregation bias, likely driven by unequal traffic distribution between segments across arms, making the overall "win" misleading.

Second, **DENOM** error exists because the metric divides conversions by sessions while randomization occurred at the user level. Since heavy users generate many sessions, this violates the independence assumption of the statistical test, inflating false positive rates.

Third, **NOVELTY** effect is evident: the lift dropped from 12% on day one to 1% by day fourteen. The forecast incorrectly uses the initial spike rather than the stabilized rate, projecting a temporary curiosity effect as permanent value.

While the test avoided **PEEK** (fixed stopping rule) and **POWER** issues (sample size met), and **SRM** appears acceptable (50,080 vs 49,920 is within chance variation), the three flagged errors are fatal to the current conclusion. The segment-level losses contradict the aggregate win, the metric definition is statistically invalid, and the projection ignores decay.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, DENOM, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
