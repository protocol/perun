The readout contains multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to roll out.

First, **DENOM** is present: users were randomized, but the conversion metric was computed per page view. This mismatch between the randomization unit and the analysis unit can produce biased estimates and incorrect significance levels.

Second, **PEEK** is explicitly described: the team checked daily and stopped the test the first day p < 0.05. This "peeking" behavior without proper sequential testing corrections severely inflates the false positive rate, making the observed significance unreliable.

Third, **SRM** is likely present: the observed split (52,300 vs 47,700) deviates from the planned 50/50 allocation by 4,600 users. A chi-square test on these numbers yields a p-value far below 0.05, indicating the assignment mechanism or logging is broken, which biases all downstream comparisons.

While the readout notes the effect was consistent across platforms (addressing SIMPSON) and sustained over 28 days (mitigating NOVELTY concerns), and the sample size exceeded the power requirement (addressing POWER), the presence of DENOM, PEEK, and SRM errors means the reported +3.1% lift cannot be trusted as a causal effect.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
