The readout contains two critical methodological errors that invalidate the results for a rollout decision.

First, there is a **Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM)**. The planned split was 50/50, yet the arms received 52,300 and 47,700 users respectively. This 4,600-user deviation suggests a broken assignment mechanism or logging error, meaning the groups are not comparable.

Second, there is a **Denominator (DENOM)** error. The unit of randomization was the *user*, but the metric analyzed was conversion per *page view*. Since users can generate multiple page views, this violates the independence assumption of the statistical test. A user-level metric (e.g., did the user convert at least once?) is required.

While the test avoided peeking (stopped at planned end date), had sufficient power, showed consistent segment results (no Simpson's paradox), and demonstrated sustained lift (no novelty effect), the SRM and DENOM errors fundamentally compromise the validity of the "win." You cannot trust the reported 3.1% lift when the groups were unbalanced and the metric unit did not match the randomization unit.

ERRORS: SRM, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
