The readout explicitly addresses several common pitfalls: the stopping rule was followed (no PEEK), sample sizes match power requirements (no POWER), the denominator matches the randomized unit (no DENOM), and results are consistent across segments (no SIMPSON). The lift remained stable over 28 days, suggesting no novelty effect (no NOVELTY).

However, a critical Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM) exists. The planned split was 50/50, yet the actual assignment was 52,300 vs. 47,700 users. This 4,600-user discrepancy (a 52.3%/47.7% split) significantly deviates from the expected distribution. Such a mismatch often indicates broken assignment logic, data logging errors, or systematic exclusion of specific user types from one arm, rendering the comparison invalid regardless of the reported lift. Because the fundamental integrity of the randomization is compromised, the results cannot be trusted for a rollout decision.

ERRORS: SRM
DECISION: HOLD
