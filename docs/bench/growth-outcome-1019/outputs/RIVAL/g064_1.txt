This readout describes multiple critical analytical errors that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the treatment.

First, **DENOM** is present: users were randomized, but the conversion metric is computed per page view. If users generate different numbers of views across variants, this biases the result.

Second, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is explicitly described: the treatment wins in the pooled total only because it received disproportionately more high-converting desktop traffic. Within every platform segment, the treatment actually performs worse than the control. The aggregate lift is an artifact of imbalanced segmentation, not a true treatment effect.

Third, **NOVELTY** is evident: the lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades thereafter, yet the readout incorrectly projects this transient spike as a sustained annual gain. This overstates the long-term value.

While the test avoided **PEEK** (stopped at planned end), **POWER** (sample size met), and **SRM** (split is effectively 50/50), the presence of Simpson's paradox alone indicates the treatment is likely inferior to the control across all user segments. Projecting a fading novelty effect as permanent further distorts the business case.

ERRORS: DENOM, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
