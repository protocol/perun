The readout explicitly states the test "was ended early, on the day the p-value first crossed 0.05 after daily looks." This describes **PEEK** (peeking), where repeated significance testing without correction inflates the false positive rate, invalidating the claimed p-value.

While the readout claims the sample size met power requirements ("Each arm got 25,000 users, the size a power calculation required"), the act of stopping early based on interim looks violates the fixed-horizon assumption underlying that power calculation. The reported significance is therefore unreliable.

Other error categories are explicitly ruled out by the text:
- **SRM**: Arm sizes (50,200 vs 50,100) match the planned even split.
- **SIMPSON**: Treatment wins overall and within both mobile and desktop segments.
- **DENOM**: Conversion is correctly computed per randomized user.
- **NOVELTY**: Lift is described as stable across four weeks with no fade.

However, the presence of peeking alone compromises the statistical validity of the result. You cannot confidently ship based on a p-value derived from optional stopping without proper sequential testing corrections, which are not mentioned.

ERRORS: PEEK
DECISION: HOLD
