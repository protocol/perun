The readout presents a mixed picture. On one hand, the test avoided several common pitfalls: the arm sizes (50,200 vs 50,100) are balanced, ruling out **SRM**; there was a single analysis at the planned end date, avoiding **PEEK**; and the sample size met the power calculation requirements, addressing **POWER**. Additionally, the treatment lead is consistent across platforms, so **SIMPSON**'s paradox is not present.

However, two critical errors undermine the recommendation. First, the forecast relies on the 12% lift observed on day one, ignoring the decay to 1% by day fourteen. This projects a short-lived spike as a permanent gain, which is a clear **NOVELTY** error. Second, the metric definition is flawed: conversions are divided by sessions, but randomization occurred at the user level. Since heavy users generate multiple sessions, this mismatches the denominator to the unit of randomization, constituting a **DENOM** error that biases the variance and significance testing.

Because the projected impact is based on transient behavior and the statistical validity is compromised by the metric definition, the decision to roll out cannot be supported by this data alone.

ERRORS: NOVELTY, DENOM
DECISION: HOLD
