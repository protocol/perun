The readout explicitly identifies a denominator error: users were randomized, but the conversion metric was computed per page view rather than per user. This violates the unit of analysis, making the reported lift statistically invalid (DENOM).

Additionally, the arm sizes (52,300 vs. 47,700) deviate significantly from the planned 50/50 split. With a total sample of 100,000, a difference of 4,600 users is far larger than chance allows, indicating a broken assignment or logging mechanism (SRM).

The readout confirms other potential issues were handled: the sample size exceeded the power calculation requirement (not POWER), the analysis occurred only once at the planned end date (not PEEK), and the lift was consistent across segments and time, ruling out Simpson's paradox (not SIMPSON) or novelty effects (not NOVELTY).

However, the presence of Sample Ratio Mismatch and the Denominator error renders the results unreliable for decision-making. The metric calculation must be corrected to match the randomization unit, and the cause of the traffic imbalance must be investigated before trusting any lift claims.
