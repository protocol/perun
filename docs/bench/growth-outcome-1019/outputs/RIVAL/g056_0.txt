The readout contains multiple critical flaws that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the change.

First, the traffic split (50,200 vs 50,100) appears balanced, but the result is driven entirely by an uneven distribution of high-converting desktop traffic. The treatment leads in the aggregate but trails within every specific platform segment. This is a classic case of **SIMPSON**'s paradox; the pooled result is misleading and the variant is actually performing worse across all user types.

Second, the analysis projects a day-one lift over a full year, despite explicit data showing the effect fades daily after the initial launch. This indicates a **NOVELTY** effect (users reacting to the change itself rather than the value) that is being incorrectly modeled as a permanent gain.

Third, with only 120 users per arm and no power calculation, the test lacks the sample size required to detect meaningful differences reliably, especially given the segmentation issues. This flags **POWER** concerns; the results are likely statistical noise.

Finally, while the denominator matches the randomized unit (users), the severe segmentation imbalance suggests the randomization or logging may have failed to distribute user characteristics evenly, though the primary error here is the interpretation of the skewed aggregate.

Given that the variant loses in every segment and the projected value relies on a transient novelty effect, rolling this out would likely decrease overall conversions.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, NOVELTY, POWER
DECISION: HOLD
