The readout describes multiple critical methodological failures that invalidate the results.

First, **SRM** is present: the arms received 52,300 and 47,700 users against a planned 50/50 split. This significant deviation suggests broken assignment or logging logic, making the groups incomparable.

Second, **PEEK** occurred: the team checked the dashboard daily and stopped the test the moment p < 0.05. This "peeking" behavior inflates the false positive rate, rendering the significance claim unreliable.

Third, **DENOM** is flagged: the primary metric divides conversions by sessions, while randomization happened at the user level. Since heavy users generate more sessions, this biases the result toward high-frequency users rather than measuring the true effect on the randomized unit.

While the readout notes that power requirements were met (**POWER** handled), segments aligned (**SIMPSON** handled), and lift was stable over time (**NOVELTY** handled), the presence of assignment bias, statistical cheating, and denominator mismatch makes the "win" untrustworthy.
