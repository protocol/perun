The readout contains two critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to roll out.

First, **PEEK**: The team "stopped the test the first day p fell below 0.05" after checking daily. This is optional stopping (peeking), which drastically inflates the false positive rate. A p-value of 0.05 is only valid if the sample size was fixed in advance and analyzed once, not if the test was monitored and halted at the first sign of significance.

Second, **POWER**: The readout claims each arm received 25,000 users as required by the power calculation. However, the actual counts provided are 50,080 and 49,920. This massive discrepancy (total ~100k vs. planned 50k) suggests either the power calculation was ignored, the numbers are misreported, or the test ran far longer than intended to reach these numbers after already stopping early. If the test stopped early due to PEEK, it likely did not achieve the 25k per arm required for power, rendering the +4.2% lift unreliable.

While the readout correctly notes that conversion is computed per randomized user (avoiding DENOM), shows consistent results across segments (avoiding SIMPSON), and reports stable lift over four weeks (avoiding NOVELTY), the fundamental validity of the result is compromised by the stopping rule. You cannot trust a p-value from a test that was peeked at and stopped early.

ERRORS: PEEK, POWER
DECISION: HOLD
