The readout contains multiple critical flaws that invalidate the recommendation to roll out immediately.

First, the arm sizes (52,300 vs. 47,700) deviate significantly from the planned 50/50 split, indicating a broken assignment or logging mechanism (**SRM**). Second, the team performed daily looks and stopped the test the moment significance was reached, a classic "peeking" error that drastically inflates false positive rates (**PEEK**). Third, while the sample size matches the power calculation, that calculation is irrelevant because the stopping rule was violated; the effective sample size for a sequential test is undefined without correction. Finally, the readout admits the lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades thereafter, yet it projects this transient spike as a permanent annual gain, ignoring the **NOVELTY** effect.

Although the denominator matches the randomized unit and Simpson's paradox is explicitly ruled out (consistent results across platforms), the combination of assignment bias, statistical invalidity due to early stopping, and unrealistic projection of a novelty effect makes the current results unreliable for a rollout decision. The test must be re-run with a fixed sample size, proper randomization checks, and a measurement window long enough to capture the decay of the novelty effect.

ERRORS: SRM, PEEK, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
