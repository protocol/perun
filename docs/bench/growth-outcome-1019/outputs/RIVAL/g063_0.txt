The readout contains critical methodological flaws that invalidate the recommendation to roll out.

First, the test suffered from **PEEK**. The team performed daily looks and stopped the experiment the moment the p-value crossed 0.05. This "peeking" behavior inflates the false positive rate, meaning the reported significance is likely illusory.

Second, the results exhibit **SIMPSON**'s paradox. While the pooled data shows an 8.7% lift, the treatment actually trails the control within every individual platform segment. The aggregate win is entirely driven by an imbalance in traffic composition (the treatment group received disproportionately more high-converting desktop users), not the layout change itself. When you control for platform, the new layout performs worse.

Although the sample size met the initial power requirement (**POWER** is not flagged) and the split was roughly even (**SRM** is not flagged), the combination of optional stopping and reversed segment trends makes the headline metric unreliable. The claim that the lift was stable at 3% contradicts the final 8.7% figure and does not negate the structural bias introduced by the traffic mix and early stopping.

You cannot ship a change that performs worse across all specific user segments based on a distorted aggregate number.

ERRORS: PEEK, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
