The readout contains multiple critical flaws that invalidate the recommendation to roll out.

First, the traffic allocation is severely imbalanced: 60,400 visitors in control versus 50,100 in treatment under a planned even split. This indicates **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch), suggesting broken assignment or logging that biases the results.

Second, while the treatment wins overall, it loses in both mobile and desktop segments. This is a classic case of **SIMPSON**'s paradox, where the aggregate result contradicts the segmented data, rendering the headline lift unreliable.

Third, the lift is concentrated in the first three days and fades thereafter, yet the readout projects this day-one lift over a full year. This ignores the **NOVELTY** effect, overstating the long-term value of the change.

Although the test avoided peeking (stopped at the planned date) and met power requirements with sufficient sample sizes, the presence of SRM, Simpson's paradox, and unaddressed novelty effects makes the current data unfit for a rollout decision. The traffic imbalance alone requires investigation into the experimentation platform's integrity before any further analysis.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
