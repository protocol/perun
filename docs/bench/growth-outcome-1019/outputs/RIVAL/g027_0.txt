The readout describes multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is explicitly present: the overall treatment wins, yet the treatment loses within both the mobile and desktop segments. This indicates a severe aggregation bias, likely driven by unequal segment sizes between arms, making the top-line result unreliable.

Second, **DENOM**inator error is confirmed. The analysis states users were the unit randomized, yet conversion is computed per page view. This mismatch inflates the sample size artificially and violates the independence assumption required for standard significance tests, rendering the p-values and confidence intervals invalid.

While the readout confirms the test avoided **PEEK**ing (single read at planned end), achieved **POWER** (sample size met), maintained balance (avoiding **SRM**), and showed sustained lift (mitigating **NOVELTY** concerns), the presence of Simpson's paradox and the denominator mismatch are fatal flaws. You cannot trust the direction or magnitude of the effect when the aggregate contradicts the strata and the metric denominator does not match the randomization unit.
