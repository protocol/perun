The readout reveals three critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, **PEEK** is present: the team stopped the test the moment p < 0.05 after daily checking. This "peeking" behavior drastically inflates the false positive rate, making the significance claim unreliable.

Second, **POWER** is compromised. The readout explicitly states "nobody sized the test for the lift it reports." With only 120 users per arm at the stop point, the sample is likely insufficient to detect the reported effect reliably, rendering the result noisy.

Third, **NOVELTY** is ignored. The lift collapsed from 12% on day one to 1% by day fourteen, indicating a strong novelty effect where users initially engaged with the new button out of curiosity. The forecast erroneously uses the Day 1 spike rather than the stabilized Day 14 rate, vastly overprojecting the long-term impact.

While SRM is not an issue (split is ~50/50), SIMPSON is ruled out (treatment wins in all segments), and DENOM is correct (unit matches randomization), the combination of optional stopping, lack of power, and novelty bias makes the current data unsafe for a rollout decision.

ERRORS: PEEK, POWER, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
