The readout contains multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, the team stopped the test the first day the p-value fell below 0.05 after checking daily. This is **PEEK** (peeking), which drastically inflates the false positive rate; fixed-horizon tests require committing to a sample size before analyzing results.

Second, the forecast relies on the 12% lift observed on day one, ignoring the decay to 1% by day fourteen. This indicates a **NOVELTY** effect where users reacted to the new visual elements temporarily, not a sustained improvement in conversion. Projecting the day-one spike as lasting is analytically unsound.

Third, the overall result shows a win for treatment, yet treatment loses within both mobile and desktop segments. This is a textbook case of **SIMPSON** (Simpson's Paradox), suggesting an imbalance in traffic composition between arms that makes the aggregate result misleading. When segments move in the opposite direction of the total, the aggregate finding cannot be trusted.

While the sample size met the power requirement and the denominator matches the randomization unit, the combination of peeking, novelty decay, and paradoxical segment data means the observed "win" is likely an artifact of bad methodology rather than a true lift.

ERRORS: PEEK, NOVELTY, SIMPSON
DECISION: HOLD
