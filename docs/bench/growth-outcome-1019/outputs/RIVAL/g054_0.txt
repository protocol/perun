The readout describes a critical statistical anomaly: the overall treatment group shows an +8.4% win, yet the treatment loses within *both* the mobile and desktop segments. This is a textbook description of Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON), where aggregated data reverses the trend found in every subgroup. This usually indicates a severe imbalance in how traffic was distributed between segments across the two arms (e.g., the treatment arm received a disproportionately high volume of high-converting desktop users, masking the fact that the popup actually performed worse for both device types individually).

Because the aggregated "win" is mathematically contradictory to the segment-level performance, the headline metric is invalid. Rolling out a feature that performs worse for every specific user segment based on a distorted aggregate number would be a significant error. The analysis must be re-run to correct the weighting or identify the allocation imbalance before any business decision can be made. The other potential errors (SRM, PEEK, POWER, DENOM, NOVELTY) are explicitly ruled out by the text, but the presence of Simpson's Paradox alone invalidates the conclusion.
