The readout explicitly addresses several common pitfalls: it confirms the stopping rule was followed (no PEEK), the sample size matched the power calculation (no POWER), the denominator matches the randomization unit (no DENOM), and the lift was consistent across segments and time (no SIMPSON or NOVELTY).

However, the arm sizes are 52,300 and 47,700 against a planned 50/50 split. The total sample is 100,000, so the expected split is 50,000 per arm. The observed deviation is 2,300 users. A chi-squared test reveals this imbalance is statistically significant (p < 0.001), indicating a Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM). This suggests a fundamental flaw in the assignment or logging mechanism, rendering the comparison invalid regardless of the reported lift. Because the randomization integrity is compromised, the results cannot be trusted for a rollout decision.
