The readout contains critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to roll out.

First, **SRM** (Sample Ratio Mismatch) is present. The arms received 52,300 and 47,700 users against a planned 50/50 split. This 4,600-user discrepancy (a 52.3%/47.7% split) strongly suggests a broken assignment or logging mechanism, meaning the groups are not comparable.

Second, **SIMPSON** (Simpson's Paradox) is explicitly described. While the treatment leads in the pooled total, it "trails within each platform." The aggregate lift is an artifact of the treatment group receiving a disproportionate amount of high-converting desktop traffic, not because the variant is actually better. Since the variant performs worse in every segment, rolling it out would likely decrease overall conversion.

Third, **POWER** is an issue. The text states "only 120 users were enrolled per arm" while simultaneously citing totals in the tens of thousands. Even ignoring the contradiction, if the effective sample for the metric was only 120 per arm, the test is severely underpowered to detect the claimed 3.4% lift reliably, especially given the variance introduced by the traffic mix.

The combination of broken randomization (SRM) and reversed segment performance (Simpson) makes the headline result meaningless. The variant is actually losing in all meaningful segments.

ERRORS: SRM, SIMPSON, POWER
DECISION: HOLD
