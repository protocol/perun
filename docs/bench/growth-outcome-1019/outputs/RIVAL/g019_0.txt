The readout contains two critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the change.

First, the team engaged in **PEEK**ing. By checking the dashboard daily and stopping the test the "first day p fell below 0.05," they performed multiple interim looks without statistical correction. This practice significantly inflates the false positive rate, meaning the observed significance is likely illusory.

Second, the results demonstrate **SIMPSON**'s paradox. While the pooled total shows a treatment lift, the treatment actually trails within *each* platform segment. The aggregate win is driven entirely by a sampling imbalance where the treatment group received a disproportionate amount of high-converting desktop traffic. Since the change performs worse for both mobile and desktop users individually, the overall positive result is a mathematical artifact, not a genuine improvement.

Although the sample size met power requirements (**POWER** is not flagged) and the denominator matches the randomization unit (**DENOM** is not flagged), the combination of early stopping and reversed segment trends makes the current data unreliable for decision-making. The test must be re-analyzed using proper sequential methods or re-run with fixed horizons to confirm if any true effect exists.
