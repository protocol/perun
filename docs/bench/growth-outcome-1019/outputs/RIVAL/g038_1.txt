The readout contains two critical errors. First, it exhibits **NOVELTY**: the lift collapsed from 12% on day one to 1% by day fourteen, yet the forecast and rollout recommendation rely on the initial 12% spike. This projects a short-lived novelty effect as a lasting gain, violating the principle of using stable data for decisions.

Second, there is a **POWER** issue. The readout states the sample size (25,000 per arm) was calculated for a "target lift," yet the actual observed effect by the end of the test window is only 1%. A sample sized for a larger effect (likely the 12% initially hoped for) is almost certainly underpowered to statistically validate a mere 1% lift. Claiming validity for a 1% result using a sample calculated for a much larger target indicates a mismatch between the design assumptions and the actual outcome.

While the assignment split (SRM), stopping rule (PEEK), metric denominator (DENOM), and segment consistency (SIMPSON) appear correctly handled, the reliance on the novelty spike and the likely underpowered status of the final result prevent a safe rollout decision.
