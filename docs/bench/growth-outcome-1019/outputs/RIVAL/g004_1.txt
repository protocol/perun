The readout contains multiple critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, **DENOM** is present: users were randomized, but conversion is computed per page view. This mismatch inflates sample size artificially and violates the unit of analysis, making standard errors and p-values incorrect.

Second, **SIMPSON**'s paradox is explicitly described: the overall treatment wins, yet the treatment loses within *both* mobile and desktop segments. This indicates a severe aggregation bias, likely driven by uneven traffic distribution between segments across arms, rendering the overall "win" misleading.

Third, **POWER** is flagged: the readout states "nobody sized the test for the lift it reports" and mentions only 120 users per arm (contradicting the earlier 50k figure, which suggests further data integrity issues). Regardless of which number is real, the explicit admission that no proper sizing occurred for the claimed effect means the result is unreliable.

While the split (50,080 vs 49,920) appears acceptable and the stopping rule was followed (avoiding PEEK), the combination of denominator mismatch, Simpson's paradox, and lack of power makes the result unusable for decision-making. The contradictory user counts (50k vs 120) also suggest potential logging errors, though SRM isn't strictly confirmed without expected ratios for the 120 figure.
