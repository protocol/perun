The readout presents two critical statistical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, the test exhibits **SIMPSON**'s paradox. While the aggregate data shows an 8.2% lift for the treatment, the treatment actually loses within *both* the mobile and desktop segments. When a trend appears in different groups of data but disappears or reverses when these groups are combined, the aggregate result is mathematically misleading and likely driven by an imbalance in traffic composition between arms rather than the feature itself. Rolling out a feature that performs worse in every user segment is ill-advised.

Second, the methodology indicates **PEEK**. The readout explicitly states the test was "ended early, on the day the p-value first crossed 0.05 after daily looks." Checking significance daily and stopping the moment a threshold is crossed (p-hacking) drastically inflates the false positive rate. The stated p-value is invalid because it does not account for the multiple comparisons made during the daily checks.

Although the sample size met the power requirement (**POWER** is not flagged) and the denominator matches the randomized unit (**DENOM** is not flagged), the combination of a reversed segment trend and invalid significance testing makes the result unreliable.

ERRORS: SIMPSON, PEEK
DECISION: HOLD
