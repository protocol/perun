The readout reveals multiple critical errors that invalidate the recommendation to ship.

First, **PEEK** is present: the team explicitly stopped the test the first day the p-value fell below 0.05 after checking daily. This "peeking" behavior inflates the false positive rate, making the statistical significance unreliable.

Second, **POWER** is a major issue. The readout admits "nobody sized the test," yet it reports results based on only 120 users per arm. For a subscription cancellation metric (likely a low base rate), this sample size is almost certainly too small to detect the reported effect reliably, rendering the findings statistically fragile.

Third, **NOVELTY** is evident. The lift dropped precipitously from 12% on day one to 1% by day fourteen. Despite this decay suggesting a short-lived novelty effect, the forecast incorrectly uses the initial 12% spike to project long-term value.

While the arm sizes are balanced (ruling out SRM) and the denominator matches the randomization unit (ruling out DENOM), the combination of optional stopping, lack of power, and novelty decay makes the current results unreliable for a rollout decision. The test must be re-run with a pre-determined sample size and a fixed horizon to validate if the 1% residual lift is real and sustainable.

ERRORS: PEEK, POWER, NOVELTY
DECISION: HOLD
