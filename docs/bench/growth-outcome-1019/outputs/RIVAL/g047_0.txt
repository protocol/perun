The readout reveals three critical analytical errors that invalidate the recommendation to roll out the change based on the current forecast.

First, **DENOM** (Denominator Error) is present: the analysis divides conversions by sessions, but randomization occurred at the user level. Since heavy users generate many sessions, this artificially weights their behavior and biases the metric, violating the unit of randomization.

Second, **NOVELTY** (Novelty Effect) is explicitly ignored. The lift dropped from 12% on day one to 1% by day fourteen, indicating the initial spike was likely curiosity. The team's forecast relies on the Day 1 figure (12%) rather than the stabilized Day 14 result (1%), grossly overstating the long-term value.

Third, **PEEK** (Peeking/Early Stopping) applies to the *forecasting logic*, if not the test execution. While the test ran to completion without early stopping, selecting the Day 1 metric for the final business case because it was the highest observed value constitutes a form of data dredging that inflates false positive expectations for the rollout.

Although the sample sizes are balanced (no SRM) and sufficient (no POWER error), and the directional win is consistent across segments (no SIMPSON), the reliance on a biased metric and a transient novelty spike makes the current projection unreliable.

ERRORS: DENOM, NOVELTY, PEEK
DECISION: HOLD
