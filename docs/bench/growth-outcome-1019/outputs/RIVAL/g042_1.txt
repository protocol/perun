The readout demonstrates sound experimental design in several areas: the sample sizes indicate no Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM), the analysis occurred only at the planned end date avoiding peeking (PEEK), the sample size matches power requirements (POWER), results are consistent across segments avoiding Simpson's Paradox (SIMPSON), and the metric denominator correctly matches the randomized unit (DENOM).

However, the readout explicitly identifies a critical validity issue regarding the longevity of the effect. It states that the observed lift is "concentrated in the first three days and fades each day after," yet the conclusion "projects the day-one lift for a full year." This is a textbook Novely Effect error. Users often engage more deeply with new interfaces initially due to curiosity, not because the change provides lasting value. Projecting transient early gains as permanent annual revenue or retention improvements will lead to significantly inflated ROI expectations and a failed rollout. The decision to ship relies on a false premise about the durability of the improvement.
