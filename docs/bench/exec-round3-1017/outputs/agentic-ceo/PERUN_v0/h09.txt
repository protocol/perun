**Green Crunch Rollout Plan & Recommendation**

**Executive Summary:** Proceed with the product development and packaging redesign immediately to meet the Jan 15, 2027 launch. Defer the full $400k marketing spend until customer churn data from a limited beta is available. Explicit sign-off is required from the CFO on the final unit economics before the marketing pilot begins.

**Recommended Sequence of Activities:**
1.  **Development & Redesign (Start Today):** Allocate the $500k product and $300k packaging budgets immediately. Finalize the pea-protein blend formulation and biodegradable pouch specs.
2.  **Stakeholder Briefing (Week 2):** Present the finalized technical specs and confirmed material costs to the board.
3.  **Limited Beta & Measurement (Months 2–4):** Produce a small batch (5,000 units) to test with existing customers. Measure churn rate and price sensitivity.
4.  **Pricing & Economics Review (Month 5):** Calculate final unit margins using actual churn data and confirmed packaging costs.
5.  **Marketing Pilot (Month 6+):** Unlock the $400k marketing budget only if unit economics remain positive after accounting for churn.

**Three Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Margin Erosion:** The biodegradable pouch costs exceed the $0.08 estimate, or churn reduces effective revenue per user, pushing total unit costs above the $4.25 price point.
2.  **Customer Rejection:** Existing customers dislike the new texture or format, causing churn to exceed 15% in the beta phase, invalidating the 150,000 unit sales forecast.
3.  **Supply Chain Delay:** The new packaging supplier cannot scale production by Q4 2026, forcing a launch delay past Jan 15, 2027.

**Pause/Change Course Triggers:**
*   **Financial:** If the fully loaded cost per unit exceeds $3.40 (approx. 20% margin erosion) or if total Q1 projected revenue falls below $500,000 due to churn.
*   **Operational:** If the packaging redesign fails stability testing by August 2026.
*   **Market:** If beta churn exceeds 20% among existing customers.

**Note:** This plan assumes the $0.45/oz protein cost remains fixed; any increase here directly threatens viability. The $400k marketing fund must remain frozen until the beta validates that the new format retains customers.
