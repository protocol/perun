**Recommendation: Build the AI Match Engine first, defer Dashboards, and parallel-track Legal.**

**Order of Work:**
1.  **Weeks 1–3: AI Match Engine.** This is your revenue anchor. Increasing match rates from 68% to 85% directly unlocks the $22k/month upside and improves retention immediately. It relies on existing data, minimizing external dependency risk.
2.  **Parallel Track (Day 1): Legal Compliance Review.** Do not wait for engineering to finish. Submit the data-sharing proposal for the Dashboards to legal immediately. The "five-day" assumption is a single point of failure; if this slips, it cannot block the Engine.
3.  **Weeks 4–5: Tiered Pricing.** Once the Engine is stable, implement the pricing logic. This is low-risk backend work that prepares you for Q4 enrollment pushes.
4.  **Defer: Live Performance Dashboards.** Do not start development until Legal provides written clearance. The risk of building a feature that cannot be shipped due to state compliance (e.g., COPPA, FERPA, or state-specific student privacy laws) is too high for a $250k runway.

**How This Plan Could Fail:**
*   **The Compliance Trap:** Legal determines that real-time third-party analytics require explicit parental re-consent or are prohibited entirely under new state laws. If this happens after you've built the UI, you waste the sprint and potentially damage school trust by promising data you can't deliver.
*   **Adoption Friction:** The 40% tutor adoption rate assumes tutors will engage with the new AI suggestions. If the UI is confusing or the matches feel "uncanny," adoption may stall at <15%, delaying revenue realization by months.
*   **Data Integrity:** The AI engine is only as good as the historical data. If the 1,800 tutor profiles have inconsistent tagging or outdated availability, the "85% match rate" will fail in production, leading to immediate churn.

**Stop/Pivot Trigger:**
** Halt the rollout if the post-launch match acceptance rate drops below 72% (a >5% absolute drop from baseline) for three consecutive days.**
*   *Why:* A drop this significant indicates the AI is making *worse* pairings than the legacy system, likely due to cold-start errors or bad weighting. Continuing would actively degrade the user experience and threaten the core value proposition. Do not proceed to Pricing or Dashboards until the matching algorithm is retrained and validates above 70% in a staging environment.

**Note:** Secure a "conditional go" from Legal now that defines exactly what data fields are permissible for the Dashboard pilot; do not assume "performance data" is a monolithic green light.
