**Recommendation:** Proceed with the two-drone pilot starting July 1, 2025, but **do not commit** to the full five-drone fleet or the claimed $360,000 first-year savings until the pilot validates flight endurance and maintenance downtime. The current proposal contains a critical arithmetic error and relies on unproven operational assumptions that could turn projected savings into a net loss.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Regulatory Stagnation:** If FAA Section 107 rules tighten regarding beyond-visual-line-of-sight (BVLOS) flights before 2026, the drones cannot cover the required 1,200 miles autonomously, forcing costly manual interventions that erase efficiency gains.
2.  **Maintenance Underestimation:** If drone downtime at 2,000 flight hours exceeds 48 hours per unit due to parts scarcity or repair complexity, fleet availability will drop below the threshold needed to replace the helicopter, causing inspection backlogs.
3.  **Financial Miscalculation:** The proposal claims a $360,000 saving (12% of $2.4M), but purchasing three drones alone costs $2.55M. Even amortized, the first-year cash outflow is vastly higher than the helicopter's $2.4M annual run rate, creating a liquidity shock rather than immediate savings.

**Pre-agreed Kill Criteria:**
Terminate the project immediately if, after the 3-month pilot, the **total cost per mile inspected** (including amortization, pilot labor, and maintenance) exceeds **$2,000/mile**, or if cumulative program spend reaches **$1.2M** without demonstrating a validated path to 1,200 miles/month coverage.

**Hidden Risks & Discrepancies:**
*   **Arithmetic Error:** The proposal cites a $360,000 saving against a $2.4M baseline, implying a new cost of $2.04M. However, buying three drones at $850,000 each is $2.55M—one-time capex that already exceeds the entire annual helicopter budget. The "12% reduction" is mathematically impossible in Year 1 unless the drone costs are excluded from the calculation or amortized over many years, which contradicts the "first year savings" claim.
*   **Scaling Gap:** The pilot uses two drones, but the capacity claim (1,200 miles) relies on three. Scaling to five by January 2026 introduces integration risks and training costs not modeled in the $360k figure.
*   **Obsolescence Risk:** Unlike helicopter contracts which are variable, drone hardware depreciates rapidly; a $850k unit today may be obsolete in 24 months, requiring reinvestment not accounted for in the static cost model.

**Note:** The financial model must be rebuilt to separate CapEx (drone purchase) from OpEx (operations) to present an accurate cash-flow picture to the COO. Do not present the $360k figure as realized Year-1 cash savings.
