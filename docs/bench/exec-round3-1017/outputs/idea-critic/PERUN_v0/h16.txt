**Recommendation: Go, but only as a constrained pilot (Go-If).**
Proceed with the $45k spend only if you cap the initial rollout to 10% of tutors (120 users) rather than the projected 20%, and explicitly exclude your top 15% of performers from the first cohort to mitigate churn risk. The math requires correction: a 15% lift on a $350k run-rate yields $52.5k additional revenue per quarter, not $120k. Your current projection implies a ~34% total lift, not 15%. At the corrected $52.5k/quarter gain, the payback period is roughly 2.5 quarters (assuming the lift is sustained), which is acceptable but far less aggressive than your pitch suggests.

**Three Principal Failure Modes:**
1.  **Algorithmic Churn:** The matching engine inadvertently deprioritizes high-earning tutors in favor of "learning style" compatibility, causing them to leave for competitors. Without a baseline of top-performer retention, you cannot distinguish algorithmic displacement from normal attrition.
2.  **Adoption Bottleneck:** The assumption that 20% of tutors will adopt the tool in month one is unverified. If adoption stalls at <5% due to UX friction or lack of incentive, the fixed $45k development cost cannot be amortized over enough sessions to break even within the year.
3.  **Revenue Dilution:** To fill matches based on "style" rather than availability, the system may push students toward lower-priced tutors or less optimal time slots, reducing the average session price below the $45 baseline and eroding the projected revenue lift.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Condition:**
Stop the project and pivot to manual matching if, by **August 1, 2025** (end of Month 3), the pilot cohort shows either: (a) a churn rate among participating tutors that exceeds the non-participating control group by more than 5 percentage points, OR (b) an adoption rate below 8% of the invited pilot group.
