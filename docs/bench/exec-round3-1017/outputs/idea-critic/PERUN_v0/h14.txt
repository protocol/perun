**Recommendation: Proceed with a "Go-If" Pilot**
Launch the tier as a limited 60-day pilot capped at 500 subscribers rather than a full rollout. This validates the unverified delivery fee impact and churn assumptions before committing to the $30,000 bean cost and full marketing spend.

**Three Most Likely Failure Modes**
1.  **Logistics Margin Erosion:** The third-party delivery fee increases by the feared $0.30/bag (or more due to weight/frequency), reducing the unit margin from $5.29 to below $4.00, making the tier unprofitable at scale.
2.  **Cannibalization & Churn Spike:** Existing subscribers upgrade to the new tier without generating net-new revenue, while the higher frequency causes churn to exceed the historic 15% (e.g., hitting 20%) because customers cannot consume coffee fast enough.
3.  **Operational Bottleneck:** Doubling the roast frequency creates fulfillment delays or quality inconsistency in the fresh-roast promise, damaging brand reputation and increasing support tickets beyond the budgeted capacity.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Condition**
Terminate or pivot the initiative immediately if, after the first 30 days of the pilot: (A) the actual delivery cost per bag exceeds **$0.20** (lowering the safety margin below the projected $0.30 risk), OR (B) the churn rate for the new tier exceeds **18%**, OR (C) fewer than **10%** of the pilot cohort converts to a paid second cycle, indicating low retention viability.

*Note: The current projection assumes 100% retention of the 2,400 converted users for the profit calculation; ensure the financial model explicitly accounts for the 15% monthly churn in the net profit forecast to avoid overstating the $78,000 figure.*
*Ensure the "fresh-roast" promise is operationally defined (e.g., roasted within 48 hours of shipping) to prevent customer dissatisfaction if logistics delay shipments.*
