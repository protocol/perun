**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with the launch only if you secure a binding commitment that telemedicine licensure in all three target states will be active before 15 Oct 2025; otherwise, delay the launch date until licenses are confirmed to avoid burning the $30k campaign and $120k license on a service you cannot legally deliver.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **Regulatory Blockade:** The "unknown" time to obtain licensure becomes a hard stop. If even one target state delays approval past the October launch, you incur the full $150k in fixed costs (salaries pro-rated + license + marketing) with zero revenue from that region, pushing breakeven well beyond Q1 2026.
2.  **Unit Economics Collapse:** The model assumes a 12-minute consult at $30. If complex cases bleed into 20 minutes while the fee remains fixed, your effective hourly revenue drops from $150/hr to $90/hr. With 12 vets costing $90k each ($7.5k/month each), you need roughly 833 billable hours/month just to cover salaries, ignoring overhead. If the average duration increases by 30%, you may never reach breakeven even with the projected 2,500 new users.
3.  **Churn & CAC Mismatch:** Acquiring 2,500 users/month requires a specific Cost Per Acquisition (CPA). If the $30k campaign yields fewer than 2,500 users, your CPA rises. Combined with high churn after month three (a stated unknown), the Lifetime Value (LTV) may fall below the acquisition cost, meaning every new customer accelerates losses rather than contributing to the $1.08M annual salary burden.

**Pre-agreed Kill Condition**
Terminate the rollout immediately if, by 1 September 2025, written regulatory approval is not secured for all three target states, or if the pilot data (if available) shows an average consult duration exceeding 16 minutes.
