**Recommendation:** Proceed with the proposal to CEO Whitfield only if you explicitly re-baseline the revenue forecast downward to reflect the distribution gap and add a "regulatory delay" contingency to the capital budget; do not present the current numbers as achievable without these caveats.

**Three Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Overestimation (Arithmetic/Logic Slip):** You project $5.5M revenue capturing 10% of the premium segment, yet your distribution covers only 3 of 7 regional chains (43% of the market). Mathematically, you cannot access 10% of the total segment volume if you are physically blocked from 57% of the primary sales channels. Unless the 3 chains represent >23% of the *total* regional volume (unlikely for a balanced market), your revenue target is mathematically impossible without assuming instant, unproven direct-to-consumer sales or non-regional exports not mentioned in the plan.
2.  **Margin Compression via Feed Cost Volatility:** The plan locks feed costs at $0.45/kg to achieve an 18% margin. Dairy feed markets are historically volatile; a mere 15% increase in feed cost (to ~$0.52/kg) would erase approximately one-third of your projected profit margin, dropping you closer to your current 12% baseline while carrying significantly higher operational risk and debt service from the $3.2M outlay.
3.  **Regulatory Timeline Mismatch:** The "unknown" timing of state health department approval for the packaging line is treated as a scheduling variable, but it is a binary gate. If approval is delayed by 90 days (common for new pasteurization tech), you incur fixed costs (staff, maintenance, debt interest on the $1.5M line) with zero revenue, burning roughly $375k of your buffer before selling a single unit.

**Kill Criterion:**
Stop or pivot the project immediately if, within the first 6 months of operational readiness, the actual cost-of-goods-sold (COGS) per unit exceeds the projected model by more than 8% **OR** if distribution agreements are not signed with at least one additional major chain (4th of 7) by Month 4. This trigger forces a decision before the full $3.2M is sunk into a low-volume, high-cost operation.
