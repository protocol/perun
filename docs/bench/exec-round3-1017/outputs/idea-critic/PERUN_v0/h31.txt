**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with development only if the vendor provides a written cap on the ongoing licensing fee (e.g., max $25k/month) before June 1, 2025. Without this cap, the "2% of revenue" model creates uncapped downside risk that could erase the projected profit margin if the revenue doubling occurs.

**Primary Failure Modes**
1.  **Financial (Uncapped Variable Cost):** The assumption that licensing is "around 2%" is dangerous. If the feature successfully doubles revenue to $1M/quarter, the fee scales linearly. If the vendor defines "revenue" broadly or raises the rate later, your marginal cost per user could exceed the marginal revenue, making growth unprofitable.
2.  **Adoption (The "Rough Double-Up" Fallacy):** Increasing session time from 7 to 10 minutes does not automatically double revenue. If your monetization is subscription-based, longer sessions increase infrastructure costs without increasing top-line revenue unless they directly drive conversion or retention. If monetization is usage-based, ensure the 2% fee doesn't outpace the gain.
3.  **Technical (Speech-to-Text Latency & Accuracy):** "Live" coaching requires near-zero latency. If the third-party engine introduces >500ms delay or struggles with background noise, user engagement will drop, invalidating the session time projection.

**Kill Criteria & Pivot Triggers**
*   **Kill Project:** If the signed vendor contract lacks a hard ceiling on monthly licensing fees OR if post-launch Beta NPS scores below 45 (indicating the "coach" is annoying rather than helpful).
*   **Pivot:** If session time increases but conversion/retention remains flat after 60 days, pivot from a "Live Coach" to an "Asynchronous Feedback" model to reduce real-time compute and licensing costs.

**Hidden Costs & Flawed Assumptions**
*   **Flawed Revenue Math:** You assume session time $\propto$ revenue. This is only true if longer sessions cause upgrades. If you are on a flat subscription model, a 43% increase in session time (7m $\to$ 10m) increases your server/load costs significantly while revenue stays fixed, potentially *lowering* margins.
*   **Hidden Integration Debt:** The $45k one-time fee likely excludes ongoing maintenance, error handling for failed transcriptions, and data privacy compliance (GDPR/CCPA) for recording user voice data, which may require legal review and additional storage security.
*   **Churn Static Assumption:** Assuming churn stays at 4.8% ignores the risk that a buggy AI feature could *increase* churn among early adopters, offsetting any gains from new users.
