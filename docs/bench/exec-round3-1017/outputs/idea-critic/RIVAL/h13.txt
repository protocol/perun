**Recommendation:** Proceed with the two-drone pilot starting July 1, 2025, but withhold commitment to the full five-drone scale-up until the pilot validates the 12% cost reduction and 20% speed gain. The potential savings are significant, but the arithmetic discrepancy in the proposal ($360k saved vs. $2.55M spend) and the unknown maintenance costs at 2,000 flight hours create unacceptable risk for an immediate full rollout.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Regulatory Halt:** FAA Section 107 rules change to restrict beyond-visual-line-of-sight (BVLOS) flights, grounding the autonomous fleet and forcing a reversion to expensive manned operations.
2.  **Maintenance Cost Spike:** Unexpected battery or motor failures at the 2,000-hour mark drive operational costs up by >15%, erasing the projected 12% savings.
3.  **Coverage Bottleneck:** Three drones fail to reliably cover 1,200 miles/month due to weather downtime or charging logistics, causing report delivery to slip back toward the 48-hour baseline.

**Kill Condition:** Terminate the project immediately if the total all-in cost (hardware amortization + operations + maintenance) exceeds **$2.1 million** in the first 12 months of full operation (equivalent to less than a 12.5% reduction vs. the $2.4M helicopter baseline).

**Hidden Risks & Discrepancies:**
*   **Arithmetic Error:** The proposal claims $360k savings (12% of $2.4M) but implies a $2.55M total spend over two years ($850k x 3 drones = $2.55M capex alone). This suggests the hardware cost is being treated as an expense rather than a capital investment, or the savings calculation ignores the massive upfront outlay. You must clarify if the $360k is *net* of hardware costs or just *operational* savings.
*   **Pilot Scale Mismatch:** The pilot uses two drones, but the savings model relies on three. You cannot validate the core economic premise (1,200 miles/month coverage) with only 66% of the required capacity.
*   **Obsolescence Risk:** Drone technology evolves rapidly; locking into a specific fleet architecture now may prevent adopting superior, cheaper sensors or batteries available by 2026.

**Note:** The 20% speed gain (48h to 38h) is operationally nice but financially negligible compared to the capex risk; do not let it drive the decision. Ensure the pilot contract includes an exit clause for regulatory changes.
