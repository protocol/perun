**Recommendation: Proceed with a Phased Pilot, Not Full Rollout**
Do not commit the full $2.5M or target all 12 hospitals immediately. Instead, approve a **$450,000, 90-day pilot** at two high-volume Northshore sites. This caps initial exposure while validating the critical assumption that your existing ERP can handle the API load and that data cleansing costs do not exceed the $50K/site estimate. Full rollout approval should be contingent on hitting specific performance milestones in the pilot.

**Three Most Likely Failure Modes**
1.  **Technical Integration Bottleneck:** The assumption that the legacy ERP handles the additional API load without upgrades is the highest technical risk. If the ERP throttles connections or crashes during peak inventory updates, the optimizer becomes useless, and you face unexpected infrastructure costs.
2.  **Data Cleansing Cost Explosion:** Your $50K per site guess is unverified. If Northshore’s data hygiene is poor, cleansing could cost $150K+ per site, blowing the implementation budget by over $1M and destroying the projected ROI timeline.
3.  **Adoption Lag & Value Realization:** The model assumes 100% adoption within three months. In healthcare, workflow changes are slow. If staff bypass the tool due to complexity or if CMS reporting changes mid-implementation, the $3.6M annual avoidance will not materialize in Year 2, turning this into a cost center rather than a savings engine.

**Kill/Pivot Triggers**
*   **Kill Trigger:** If the pilot reveals data cleansing costs exceed **$100,000 per site** or if the ERP requires **hardware upgrades costing >$200,000** to sustain the API load. This fundamentally breaks the unit economics.
*   **Pivot Trigger:** If adoption at the pilot sites is **<60% after 45 days** of go-live. This indicates a workflow or training failure; pivot immediately to a "managed service" model where your team runs the optimizer internally rather than expecting hospital staff to adopt the tool directly.

**Note:** The current 140% ROI calculation is fragile because it assumes immediate, full-scale success; the pilot approach protects the board from betting $2.5M on unproven assumptions.
Ensure the pilot contract includes a clause allowing termination if CMS requirements shift significantly before go-live.
