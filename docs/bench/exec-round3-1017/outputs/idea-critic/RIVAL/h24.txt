**Recommendation:** Do not approve the full $120,000 build yet. Instead, authorize a $15,000, four-week "feasibility sprint" to validate the core algorithm on historical data and measure the actual support ticket impact on a 50-church pilot. If the pilot confirms a >10% efficiency lift without spiking support costs beyond 5%, greenlight the remaining budget. Proceeding now bets $120k on an unproven assumption that "optimization" equals "adoption," ignoring that scheduling friction is often political, not mathematical.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **The "Black Box" Rejection:** Church administrators distrust AI-driven schedule changes they cannot explain. If the system moves a key volunteer without a clear "why," leaders may revert to manual overrides immediately, rendering the $75k vendor code useless while you still incur the $45k internal integration cost.
2.  **Support Cost Eclipse:** You estimated a 10% ticket increase, but haven't priced it. If your current support cost is $15/volume-ticket, a 10% spike on a larger base could add $20k–$30k annually in OpEx. This recurring cost could swallow the ROI from the 15% activity lift within 18 months, turning a "growth" feature into a margin reducer.
3.  **Churn Acceleration:** You noted an 8% quarterly churn but didn't model the risk of *increasing* it. If the optimization algorithm inadvertently schedules volunteers at times they can't make (due to data gaps), you risk frustrating the very users you're trying to activate. A 1% increase in churn (to 9%) would wipe out the revenue gain from the 1,200 additional volunteers, making the project net-negative.

**Kill/Pivot Trigger:**
Establish a **"Support-to-Activation Ratio"** checkpoint at the 30-day pilot mark. If the pilot generates more than **1 support ticket for every 15 new volunteer shifts successfully scheduled**, kill the project or pivot to a "human-in-the-loop" suggestion model rather than full automation. This ensures you aren't buying growth that your operations team cannot sustain.
