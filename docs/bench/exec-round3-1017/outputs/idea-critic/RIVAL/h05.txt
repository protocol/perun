**Recommendation: Proceed with a Controlled Pilot, Not a Full Rollout**

Do not launch this tiered model to all 3,200 podcasts immediately. The math relies on a fragile assumption: that the 20% fill-rate lift and higher CPMs from top-tier shows will completely offset the revenue loss and network effects of churning the bottom 15%. If those small podcasts leave, you lose the inventory density that makes your platform attractive to advertisers in the first place.

Run a 6-week pilot with 10% of your inventory (randomly selected). This validates the fill-rate lift and measures actual churn elasticity before you risk the entire $1.5M baseline.

**Main Failure Modes & Hidden Risks**

1.  **The "Ghost Inventory" Trap:** You assumed the ad server handles complexity without extra bandwidth. Tiered pricing fragments your inventory. If advertisers demand specific tiers, your "fill rate" might drop for the lower tiers because demand isn't perfectly elastic. You could end up with unsold inventory in the $20 tier while turning away demand that wanted volume but not at $45.
2.  **Churn Contagion:** You are modeling a 15% churn rate among small podcasters. However, small podcasters talk to each other. If they perceive the new model as punitive ("we get penalized for being small"), churn could spike to 25-30%, or worse, trigger a PR backlash that scares off mid-tier creators.
3.  **Engineering Underestimation:** The $120k estimate covers building the logic, but likely misses the operational cost of support. Explaining variable CPMs to 3,200 creators will flood your support team. If your billing system isn't built for dynamic retro-active tier adjustments, you risk revenue leakage from billing errors.
4.  **Advertiser Confusion:** If your sales team cannot clearly explain why CPMs fluctuate based on aggregate volume rather than show-specific performance, enterprise buyers may pause spending during the transition, causing an immediate Q1 dip that takes quarters to recover.

**Kill Switch Triggers**

Establish these hard gates at the end of the 6-week pilot or the first full quarter post-launch:

*   **Churn Trigger:** If **>8%** of the pilot group cancels or downgrades within the first 60 days. (This suggests your 15% total churn assumption is too conservative and threatens the network base).
*   **Revenue Trigger:** If net revenue (after accounting for lost shows) is **<4%** above the baseline projection after 90 days. (You are taking significant operational risk; anything less than a 4% net gain in one quarter does not justify the $120k engineering spend and potential churn).
*   **Fill-Rate Trigger:** If the fill rate for the bottom tier ($20 CPM) drops by **>5%** compared to historical averages. (This indicates fragmentation is hurting liquidity rather than helping it).
