**Recommendation: Do not green-light the pilot in its current form.**

The proposal contains a fundamental financial error: it is undercapitalized by $15,000. You have budgeted $55,000 ($45k dev + $10k integration) but omitted the mandatory $15,000 data licensing fee, creating an immediate $70,000 total cost against only $30,000 in projected annual savings. Even if the pilot succeeds perfectly, the project operates at a net loss of $40,000 in Year 1. Furthermore, the assumption of 90% accuracy without extra training data is unverified; if accuracy drops to 80%, the projected $30,000 savings likely evaporates, turning the pilot into a pure expense with no ROI.

**Primary Failure Modes & Hidden Risks:**

1.  **The Accuracy Trap:** The $30,000 savings calculation relies entirely on the "12% lift in order accuracy." If the model performs below 90% accuracy due to lack of training data, order errors may increase rather than decrease, causing food waste and customer dissatisfaction that costs more than the manual audits you are trying to replace.
2.  **Adoption Cliff:** You noted adoption is the "only real unknown," but it is the single point of failure. If the three flagship clients (Bistro 42, Harbor Grill, Green Table) do not actively use the tool during the pilot, you generate zero validation data. Without usage data, you cannot prove the $30,000 saving exists, making the Jan 2027 rollout impossible to justify.
3.  **Integration Debt:** The $10,000 integration budget assumes clean APIs. If the restaurants' existing POS systems require custom middleware to accept predictive orders, integration costs could double, further widening the gap between cost and return.
4.  **Licensing Escalation:** The $15,000 data cost is treated as fixed. If the model requires retraining mid-pilot to hit the 90% target, licensing fees could recur quarterly, not annually.

**Pre-agreed Kill Condition:**

Terminate the project immediately if, at the 3-month mark of the pilot (September 1), **actual verified savings are less than $3,750** (25% of the projected annual run rate) OR if **active weekly usage by any of the three pilot clients falls below 40%**.

*Note: Before approaching Maya, revise the budget to include the $15,000 licensing fee and redefine the success metric from "model accuracy" to "verified dollar savings."*
*Without a path to profitability in Year 1, this is an R&D expense, not a business investment.*
