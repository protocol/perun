**Recommendation: Proceed with a constrained pilot, not the full $45k sprint.**
The math is indeed off: a 15% lift on a $350k run-rate yields $52.5k extra revenue per quarter, not $120k. Even if you achieve the target, the ROI is marginal (~15% return over 3 months) given the high risk of tutor churn. Do not build the full engine yet. Instead, spend $5k–$8k on a "Wizard of Oz" manual match test for 4 weeks to validate the lift and churn impact before writing code.

**Three Principal Failure Modes**
1.  **Supply-Side Revolt (Hidden Risk):** High-performing tutors may reject algorithmic assignment, viewing it as a loss of autonomy or a threat to their premium pricing power. If your top 20% of tutors (who likely drive >50% of revenue) churn, the 15% session lift will be wiped out by lost capacity.
2.  **The "Average Price" Fallacy:** The model assumes a static $45 session price. In reality, matching efficiency often creates downward price pressure as students find cheaper fits faster. If the average session price drops to $40, your revenue lift evaporates even if session volume increases.
3.  **Adoption Lag:** The plan assumes 20% tutor adoption in month one. Without a forced mandate or significant incentive, organic adoption of new workflow tools typically hovers near 5–8% initially. Building the engine for a user base that doesn't show up creates immediate sunk cost.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Condition**
Stop or pivot the project if, during the 4-week manual pilot:
*   **Tutor Churn > 3%:** More than 3% of active tutors deactivate their accounts or explicitly cite "platform changes" as a reason for leaving.
*   **Net Revenue Lift < 5%:** The increase in booked sessions, adjusted for any change in average session price, fails to exceed 5% after normalizing for seasonality.
