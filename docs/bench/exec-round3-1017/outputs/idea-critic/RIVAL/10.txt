**Recommendation: Proceed with a constrained pilot launch, not a full rollout.**

The unit economics are attractive on paper ($675k ARR vs. $195k initial outlay), but the assumption that current support staff can absorb a 50% ticket increase without degradation is the single point of failure. Solo practitioners have zero tolerance for downtime during billing or filing windows; if support slows down, churn will spike before you ever get to year two. Launch immediately to capture the Q4 marketing window, but cap enrollment at 50 users (one-third of your projected conversion) until you validate the support load and actual churn behavior.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **Support Collapse:** The 50% ticket increase overwhelms your current team, leading to response times >4 hours. Solo lawyers interpret this as product instability and cancel within 60 days, destroying the LTV model.
2.  **Usage Mismatch:** The "3 hours per month" estimate is wrong. If they use it for 10 hours, your API costs triple, wiping out margins. If they use it for 30 minutes, they perceive low value and refuse to renew after year one.
3.  **Feature-Price Disconnect:** The UI redesign fails to solve the specific "premium" workflow bottlenecks. Users stay on the free tier because the $15k price tag demands flawless automation, not just a prettier interface.

**Pre-agreed Kill Condition:**

Terminate the project if **Net Revenue Retention (NRR) falls below 80% after the first 90 days of paid usage.**

This metric captures both churn and contraction. If you cannot keep 80% of the revenue value from your initial cohort after a quarter, the product-market fit for the premium tier is false, and scaling will only accelerate cash burn. Do not wait for the six-month mark; the Q4 spend must be protected by an early exit clause.
