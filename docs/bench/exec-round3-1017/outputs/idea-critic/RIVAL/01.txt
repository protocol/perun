**Recommendation:** Proceed with the 3-month pilot, but **immediately revise the adoption assumption** from 70% of tutors to a conservative 15% for financial modeling. While the unit economics ($800/week per active tutor) are strong, assuming 280 tutors (70% of 400) will successfully recruit and schedule four full groups weekly within month one is operationally unrealistic. This creates a dangerous gap between your projected $320k monthly lift and a more likely initial reality of ~$68k. Frame the pilot explicitly as a test of **tutor recruitment capability** and **student conversion**, not just platform stability.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Cannibalization of High-Margin Revenue:** Students may switch from existing 1-on-1 sessions (likely priced higher than $25/hr) to the cheaper group option, causing net revenue to drop even if group class volume is high.
2.  **Tutor Operational Churn:** Tutors may sign up but fail to fill seats. If a tutor runs a class with only 1–2 students, the effective hourly rate plummets, leading to tutor frustration and abandonment of the feature before the 3-month mark.
3.  **Quality Dilution:** Managing 8 students simultaneously requires different pedagogical skills than 1-on-1 instruction. Poorly managed classes could damage brand reputation and increase churn among students who feel they aren't getting individual attention.

**Hidden Costs & Risks Missed:**
*   **Scheduling Complexity:** Coordinating 8 students across time zones for a fixed 90-minute block is exponentially harder than matching pairs. You may need to build or buy sophisticated scheduling logic not included in the $120k dev budget.
*   **No-Show Volatility:** In 1-on-1, a no-show is a lost hour. In an 8-person class, one no-show is manageable; four no-shows make the session economically unviable and pedagogically weak. You need a clear refund/reschedule policy that hasn't been costed.
*   **Support Overhead:** Group dynamics introduce conflict, technical issues for multiple users simultaneously, and payment disputes that will spike customer support ticket volume.

**Pre-Agreed Halt Trigger:**
Halt the rollout immediately if, by the end of Month 2 of the pilot, the **Net Revenue Contribution per Active Group Tutor** falls below **$300 per week**. This accounts for partial fill rates while ensuring the feature remains accretive to margins after support and hosting costs. Additionally, halt if **cannibalization exceeds 20%** of existing 1-on-1 bookings from participating tutors.

*Note: Ensure your $120k development budget includes a robust waitlist and auto-fill mechanism to mitigate no-shows.*
*Do not announce the 15% revised adoption number publicly; use it internally to set realistic board expectations.*
