**Recommendation: Proceed with a strict 90-day pilot on 4 boats, not a full 12-boat launch.**

Your current plan assumes 100% utilization of the 50 booked hours and ignores variable costs that will destroy your margins. By launching with only 4 boats, you limit your exposure to the unknown occupancy rate while validating the $250/hour price point. If the pilot succeeds, scale to the remaining 8 boats in Q3 2025. Do not commit to the full fleet or the $20,000 video production until you have verified demand.

**The Three Biggest Failure Modes:**

1.  **The "Empty Seat" Margin Trap:** Your $150,000 revenue figure assumes every booked hour is profitable. You explicitly noted missing fuel and crew overtime. Sunset charters often require premium fuel blends and trigger overtime pay for captains staying past standard shifts. If fuel is 15% of revenue and overtime adds another 10%, your gross margin collapses from ~80% to ~55%. If occupancy dips below 60%, you will likely operate at a net loss per trip after allocating fixed marketing costs.
2.  **Cannibalization of Core Revenue:** You project a 25% increase in *total* bookings, but you haven't accounted for existing customers shifting from standard rates to the premium Sunset package. If 40% of your Sunset bookings are just existing customers upgrading (rather than new volume), your net revenue gain is negligible, yet your operational complexity and fuel costs skyrocket. You are risking high-margin steady income for volatile premium income.
3.  **The Video Production Sunk Cost:** The $20,000 one-off video cost is a vanity expense before product-market fit is proven. If the concept fails, this money is gone. If it succeeds, you can afford to shoot a better video with real customer footage. Spending this upfront signals confidence you don't yet have data to support.

**Kill Trigger:**

Terminate the initiative immediately if **average occupancy per boat falls below 30 hours/month (60% of your projection) for two consecutive months** during the pilot.

At 30 hours, your revenue drops to $90,000/month for the fleet. Once you deduct the newly identified fuel (~$13,500), crew overtime (~$9,000), and the two new captains ($9,000), you are left with ~$58,500 to cover the $30,000 marketing budget, boat maintenance, insurance, and the video amortization. This leaves almost no buffer for unexpected repairs or seasonal dips, making the unit economics unsustainable.
