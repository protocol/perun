**Three Main Failure Modes:**

Unless the three existing chains have disproportionately high volume, your realistic addressable market is likely closer to 4–5%. At 5% share, revenue drops to ~$2.75M. With an 18% margin, that yields only $495k gross profit, which cannot cover the fixed costs of the new line or service the debt on the $3.2M investment, leading to immediate cash flow insolvency.
2.  **Margin Compression from Hidden Costs:** The 18% margin projection relies on feed costs staying flat at $0.45/kg and ignores the "regulatory drag." The unknown timing of the state health department's approval creates a "dead money" period where you incur depreciation and staffing costs on the new pasteurization line ($1.5M asset) with zero revenue. A 3-month delay alone could erase $150k–$200k in projected first-year profits, slashing your actual margin to single digits.
3.  **R&D Underestimation for Premium Segment:** Allocating only $800k for R&D on a *premium* line is risky if "premium" implies complex flavor profiles or specific aging processes not currently in your workflow. If the initial product batch fails taste tests or consistency checks required by the new grocery chains, you face a dual cost: wasted R&D spend and delayed market entry, allowing competitors to solidify their hold on the shelf space you need.
