**Recommendation: Proceed with a Phased Pilot, Not a Full Rollout**
Do not commit to the full $470k+ investment immediately. The integration cost is labeled "unknown," and the revenue model relies entirely on unproven behavioral shifts (25% push open rates and 10% visitation lifts). Instead, authorize a $50k–$75k MVP pilot in **two** locations only. Validate the POS integration feasibility and actual user engagement in a live but contained environment before signing the full build contract. If the pilot fails to hit adoption targets within 90 days, you have lost <15% of the budget rather than 100%.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes**
1.  **POS Integration Quagmire:** The $120k estimate is a guess. If your legacy POS systems lack modern APIs, integration could spiral to $250k+ or require manual workarounds that destroy the "order ahead" convenience, rendering the app useless.
2.  **Notification Fatigue & Churn:** Assuming a 25% open rate is dangerous. If users receive weekly specials without hyper-relevant personalization, open rates will likely crash to <5% within three months, turning the app into an ignored icon that drives zero incremental revenue.
3.  **Operational Friction:** A 10% lift in visits means significantly higher peak-time volume. If the kitchen workflow isn't redesigned to handle mobile orders alongside walk-ins, ticket times will slow, degrading the experience for *all* customers and causing net revenue loss.

**Kill Switch Metric**
Terminate the project if **Monthly Active Users (MAU) fall below 15% of total unique customers** at any pilot location after 60 days of operation. This indicates the value proposition (convenience/rewards) is not strong enough to overcome the friction of downloading and using an app for coffee/pastry purchases.

**Hidden Risks in Your Numbers**
*   **The "Unknown" Integration Cost:** In software projects, "unknown" integration costs almost always resolve to "higher than expected" and "takes longer." You are budgeting $120k but effectively risking $200k+.
*   **Revenue Attribution Error:** Your $2.16M projection assumes *all* increased spend comes from the app. However, some of that 10% lift might be cannibalized from existing loyalists who would have visited anyway, meaning your *net* new revenue is significantly lower than projected.
*   **Maintenance Underestimation:** $75k/year covers bug fixes, but it likely excludes feature updates required to keep up with iOS/Android OS changes or marketing spend required to actually drive downloads. The true Year 2 cost is likely closer to $150k when including customer acquisition costs (CAC).
