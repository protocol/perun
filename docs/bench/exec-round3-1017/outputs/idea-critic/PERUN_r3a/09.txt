**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **capped pilot** targeting 500 subscribers from the existing base starting 1 March 2025, rather than the full $650k upfront spend. The current financial model contains a critical arithmetic error and unverified unit economics that make the full launch a "no-go" as written.

**Figures:** Your revenue projection is incorrect. 8,000 subscribers × $25/box × 12 months equals **$2.4 million**, not $2 million. More critically, your margin assumption is likely fatal: a 20% gross margin on a $25 box allows only $5.00 for food, labor, and overhead. You have not priced the $0.08/mile fuel surcharge for the new refrigerated trucks, nor confirmed if the $0.30 packaging cost is inclusive of the required cold-chain materials. If the fuel surcharge averages $1.50 per delivery and cold-chain packaging adds $0.50, your margin drops to ~12%, potentially rendering the unit economics negative before marketing costs.

**Main Failure Modes:**
1.  **Margin Collapse:** The unpriced fuel surcharge and specialized cold-chain packaging costs erode the already thin 20% margin, turning every additional subscriber into a loss leader.
2.  **Cannibalization without Growth:** The 8,000 new subscribers are largely existing customers upgrading from lower-margin plans, resulting in high "gross" subscriber counts but flat or reduced net revenue.
3.  **Churn Underestimation:** Elite athletes have seasonal training cycles; a flat 5% monthly churn assumption ignores potential spikes during off-seasons, which would drastically increase the cost of replacement acquisition against the $500k influencer spend.

**Kill Criteria:**
Terminate or pivot the initiative immediately if either of the following occurs during the pilot:
*   **Verified Gross Margin** falls below **15%** after accounting for all variable logistics and packaging costs.
*   **Monthly Churn** exceeds **8%** for two consecutive months post-launch.

*Note: The 15-minute delivery reduction must be validated as a primary driver for retention before scaling logistics spend; otherwise, it is an unnecessary cost.*
