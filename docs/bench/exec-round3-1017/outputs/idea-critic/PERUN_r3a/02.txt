**Recommendation: MODIFY (Cap expansion to two pilot branches; defer remaining three until Q3 performance review).**

The current proposal rests on a flawed revenue baseline and unverified capacity assumptions that expose Harbor Trust to significant downside.

**Three Primary Failure Modes:**
1.  **Revenue Miscalculation:** The projected $2M gain assumes 15% growth applies to the full $12M principal immediately. In reality, loan books grow incrementally; a 15% *year-end* increase implies an average outstanding balance growth of roughly 7.5%, halving the projected interest income unless the ramp-up is impossibly fast.
2.  **Compliance Bottleneck:** The assumption that current underwriting capacity can absorb a 30% staff increase and 15% volume growth without additional compliance hires is a critical single point of failure. Regulatory scrutiny on small-business lending is high; a backlog in compliance review will stall the "rapid" expansion, incurring the full $1.2M cost while generating zero revenue.
3.  **Competitive Blindness:** Proceeding without data on the competitor's "instant-approve" adoption rate is dangerous. If their product has captured >20% of the Metro market's new applications, our traditional branch model may be launching into a shrinking pool of eligible borrowers, rendering the $1M technology upgrade insufficient to compete on speed.

**Abort Trigger:**
If net new loan originations in the pilot markets do not exceed **$450,000** (representing 2.5% of the current book) by **September 30, 2025**, immediately halt the rollout of the remaining three branches and freeze hiring for the final eight loan officers.

**Note:** The $1.2M outlay also omits ongoing operational costs (lease, utilities, insurance) for five new locations, which could exceed $250k annually. The "flat interest rate" assumption ignores potential margin compression if the competitor forces a rate war to defend their share.
