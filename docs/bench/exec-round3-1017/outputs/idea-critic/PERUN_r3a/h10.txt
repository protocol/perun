**Recommendation: Proceed with a capped pilot, not a full rollout.**
Approve a $150k budget to build a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) for **two** locations only. Do not commit to the full $470k+ investment until you verify the POS integration cost and achieve a 15% active user rate in the pilot. The current plan relies on unverified integration costs and optimistic industry averages that may not apply to your specific customer demographic.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Integration Cost Overrun:** The "unknown" POS integration could exceed the $120k estimate or prove technically impossible with legacy registers, stalling the entire launch.
2.  **Adoption Delusion:** Achieving a 25% push notification open rate requires an installed base; if only 5% of customers download the app, the "10% lift in visits" math collapses regardless of open rates.
3.  **Operational Friction:** If "ordering ahead" creates bottlenecks at the pickup counter without additional staff, service speed drops for walk-in customers, reducing overall revenue despite higher ticket sizes.

**Kill Criteria:**
Terminate the project if, after 6 months of the two-store pilot:
*   **Adoption:** Fewer than 8% of transacting customers activate the app.
*   **Revenue:** The net increase in average ticket size (after discounts/promos) is less than $0.75.
*   **Cost:** The final POS integration bill exceeds $180k (50% over estimate).

**Hidden Risk in Numbers:**
Your revenue projection double-counts growth drivers. You assume a 10% increase in visit frequency **and** a $1.50 increase in ticket size simultaneously. Historically, loyalty programs often cannibalize margin (via rewards) to drive frequency, rarely lifting both metrics to their maximum potential at once. If the app only delivers half the projected lift on both fronts, the 24-month return drops from $2.16M to roughly $540k, which barely covers the $545k initial + Year 1 maintenance cost, leaving zero profit margin for the risk taken. Additionally, the $75k annual maintenance is a fixed cost that persists even if adoption fails, creating a negative cash flow trap.

*Note: The "industry average" 25% open rate is likely inflated for a first-time launch without an existing email/SMS list to seed downloads. Treat this assumption as a best-case scenario, not a baseline.*
