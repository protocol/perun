**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with a capped, four-week discovery sprint ($15,000 max) to validate the revenue math and tutor adoption model before committing to the full $45,000 development build. The current financial justification contains a critical arithmetic error that invalidates the projected ROI, and the risk of high-performer churn requires empirical testing rather than assumption.

**Three Principal Failure Modes**
1.  **Mathematical Invalidity:** Your projection claims a 15% lift yields $120k extra revenue on a $350k run-rate. A 15% increase on $350,000 is actually $52,500, not $120,000. To achieve $120k growth, you need a 34% lift, not 15%. If the engine only delivers the stated 15%, the project loses money relative to the $45k cost and ongoing maintenance.
2.  **Adoption Bottleneck:** The plan assumes 20% of tutors (240 users) adopt immediately. If the algorithm mismatches high-performers with difficult students or lower-paying slots, these tutors will reject the tool or leave for competitors. Without a "opt-in" safety valve, you risk alienating the top 20% of your supply base who drive the majority of your current revenue.
3.  **Price Elasticity Blindspot:** The model assumes the average session price remains static at $45. If the matching engine pushes students toward cheaper tutors to maximize "matches," or if high-quality tutors raise prices due to increased demand efficiency, the $45 average will shift, breaking the revenue forecast.

**Pre-Agreed Kill Condition**
Stop the project immediately if, after the four-week discovery sprint, a live A/B test with 50 tutors does not demonstrate a statistically significant increase in **net** booked sessions (excluding cannibalized existing bookings) of at least 8% (half the target) without increasing tutor churn by more than 2% compared to the control group.

**Figures Correction:** 15% of $350k = $52.5k (not $120k). To get $120k, you need 34% growth. Current math implies a 228% overstatement of returns.
