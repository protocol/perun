**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **capped 6-week pilot** starting 1 Oct 2024, limited to **20 tutors** (not 400) and a hard development budget of **$60,000**. Do not commit to the full $200k spend or the 70% adoption target until the pilot proves students will cannibalize 1-on-1 sessions at less than a 20% margin loss.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Cannibalization without Volume:** High-value 1-on-1 students downgrade to the $25/hr group rate, dropping average revenue per user (ARPU) while tutor capacity remains maxed out, resulting in a net revenue decline despite high "group" uptake.
2.  **Adoption Friction:** The assumption that 70% of tutors will adopt in month one is unsupported; if tutors prefer the simplicity of 1-on-1 or cannot fill groups, the fixed development cost cannot be amortized, extending break-even beyond 12 months.
3.  **Operational Collapse:** Managing scheduling, no-shows, and mixed-level dynamics for 8 students per session creates support overhead that exceeds the $80k marketing/ops buffer, turning a profitable unit economics model into a logistical loss.

**Halting Trigger:** Halt the rollout immediately if, by Week 4 of the pilot, the **net incremental revenue** (group revenue minus lost 1-on-1 revenue minus support costs) is **negative** for two consecutive weeks, or if tutor adoption remains below **15%**.

**Hidden Risk:** The calculation assumes 100% attendance and perfect scheduling efficiency. In reality, group classes suffer higher no-show rates than 1-on-1, and the "90-minute" block may fragment tutor availability, reducing their total billable hours if groups aren't perfectly filled.
