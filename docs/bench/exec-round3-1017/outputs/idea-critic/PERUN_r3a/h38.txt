**Recommendation: Proceed with a capped 90-day pilot on 4 boats, not a full 12-boat launch.**

Your current plan assumes 100% utilization of the 50 booked hours and ignores variable costs that could erase margins. By limiting the initial rollout to 4 boats, you cap exposure while validating the critical unknown: summer sunset occupancy. If the pilot succeeds, scale to the remaining 8 boats in Q3; if it fails, you lose only 33% of the projected downside.

**Three Biggest Failure Modes:**
1.  **Margin Erosion from Variable Costs:** You calculated revenue ($150k) but omitted fuel and crew overtime. At $250/hour, if fuel consumes 15% and overtime 10%, your gross margin drops significantly. If net margin falls below 20% after these variables, the volume game fails.
2.  **Occupancy Overestimation:** The model relies on 50 hours/boat/month (~1.6 hours/day). If actual average occupancy is below 60% (30 hours/boat), revenue drops to $90k, likely failing to cover the fixed $30k marketing spend plus new captain salaries ($9k total).
3.  **Cannibalization of Existing Bookings:** The "25% increase in total bookings" assumes new demand. If 40% of these sunset charters simply replace higher-margin daytime rentals or standard charters already in the pipeline, the *net* revenue gain may be negligible despite the high top-line number.

**Kill Criteria:**
Terminate the expansion to the full fleet if, after 60 days of the pilot:
*   Average occupancy per boat is **below 35 hours/month** (70% of target).
*   Total variable costs (fuel + overtime) exceed **$45 per operating hour**.
*   Net new revenue (after subtracting cannibalized standard bookings) is **less than $15,000/month** across the pilot group.
