**Recommendation: GO-IF**
Proceed with the pilot only if the revised total budget is capped at **$300,000** ($250k content + $50k manager) and the district cost-share agreement is signed in writing before September 1, 2025. Do not commit to the full three-year rollout based on the current adoption assumptions.

**Strongest Failure Modes**
1.  **Financial Miscalculation & Cash Flow:** Your budget contains a $20,000 error ($320k vs. $300k). More critically, you assume districts cover 60% of implementation costs, yet your budget lists the full $50k manager salary as an internal expense. If districts do not pay upfront, your actual cash outlay remains $300k, not the net amount you may be projecting. If the "30% increase in training hours" does not directly correlate to immediate renewal revenue, you face a $300k loss against a projected $120k gain, requiring a 250% over-performance to break even in year one.
2.  **Adoption Cliff:** The plan hinges on 12 of 15 schools (80%) signing three-year contracts. Public school procurement cycles are notoriously slow and subject to board approvals unrelated to pilot success. If only 8 schools renew (your kill threshold), you recover only ~$64,000 in renewal value (pro-rated), leaving a $236,000 sunk cost. The "unknown" adoption rate is not a minor variable; it is the single point of failure for the entire business case.
3.  **Metric Misalignment:** You are measuring "training hours per student" as a success metric. Schools care about security incidents reduced or compliance met, not hours logged. If the curriculum increases hours but fails to demonstrate tangible safety improvements, districts will not renew regardless of engagement stats.

**Kill Condition**
Terminate or radically pivot the initiative immediately if **fewer than 8 schools** sign a binding three-year contract by **June 30, 2026**. This triggers the pre-agreed stop-loss, preventing further sunk costs into a market that has demonstrated insufficient willingness to pay.
