**Three Biggest Failure Modes**

1.  **Unit Economics Inversion:** The "hidden cost" is the support tail. A 10% increase in tickets on a complex scheduling engine often requires specialized Tier-2 agents, not just general staff. If the margin per church is thin, this operational drag could make the new cohort unprofitable immediately.
2.  **Adoption Fallacy:** Assuming adoption will mirror "smart reminders" is dangerous. Reminders are passive value; optimization requires active trust in an algorithm. If churches perceive the AI suggestions as rigid or error-prone, adoption may stall at <10%, leaving you with fixed dev costs and zero lift.
3.  **Churn Acceleration:** You noted an 8% quarterly churn but didn't model the risk of *increasing* it. If the optimization layer misallocates volunteers (e.g., over-scheduling key leaders), it creates friction that drives churn. A mere 1% increase in churn would wipe out the lifetime value of the projected 1,200 new volunteers.
