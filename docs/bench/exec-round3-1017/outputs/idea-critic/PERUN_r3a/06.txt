**Recommendation: GO-IF (Pilot First)**
Proceed with a capped 6-month pilot targeting 80 installs (not 250) before committing to the full $1.15M upfront spend. The current plan relies on unverified labor costs and optimistic incentive continuity, creating a high risk of negative cash flow. Limit initial hiring to one lead installer and $150k inventory; trigger full expansion only if net margin exceeds 15% after accounting for actual Dallas labor rates.

**Primary Failure Modes**
1.  **Margin Collapse:** Using Arizona's $2.5k/kW cost basis against Texas's higher Dallas labor rates ignores the specific cost differential. If actual installed costs rise to $2.8k/kW, the 40% gross margin evaporates, potentially turning the projected $3.2M gross profit into a loss once fixed overhead is applied.
2.  **Revenue Shortfall & Cash Crunch:** The plan assumes $8M revenue (250 installs) immediately. If permitting delays (the acknowledged unknown) stretch cycles by 4 weeks, you may only complete 150 installs ($4.8M revenue) in year one. This leaves you with $655k in fixed new costs (salaries + licensing) and $500k in idle inventory, burning cash rather than generating profit.
3.  **Incentive Cliff:** Basing the three-year model on the 30% incentive remaining unchanged is a single point of failure. Any reduction to 20% or less directly reduces customer demand and effective contract value, making the $32k average price uncompetitive.

**Kill Criteria**
Stop or rethink the expansion immediately if, after the first 80 installs:
*   **Net Margin drops below 5%** (after adjusting for actual Dallas labor rates and permitting delays).
*   **Sales velocity averages fewer than 10 installs per month** over any 90-day period post-licensing.
*   **The state incentive is formally proposed for reduction** before the pilot completes 50% of its target.
