First call: GO. A premium large phone with a stylus is a two-way door: it can be pulled from the line, and the maker already sells phones at four inches. The do-nothing path cedes the in-between space to rivals. The steelman is that a segment of buyers wants a single device for media, note-taking, and calls, and will pay a premium for it. The strongest attack is that the device is neither a good phone nor a good tablet, so demand is thin and cannibalization is the only real outcome. That attack fails on the stated facts: the maker already has a four-inch line, so any cannibalization is internal revenue preserved, not lost; and the "too big / too small" objection is a reviewer framing, not evidence that no buyer wants the middle. The risks are real but they are risks, not blockers: no stated fact contradicts the core claim that a premium, stylus-equipped large phone can find a paying segment. The kill criterion is simple: set a sales threshold within the first two quarters; if the device misses it, discontinue. The stylus is the differentiator that justifies the premium and the category; without it the device is just a bigger phone. The proposal is sound as a capped, time-boxed bet with a named stop condition.

DECISION: GO
