**First call: GO.** Large-screen flagships are the strongest seller; moving the launch ahead of a known rival's September flagship is a defensible, time-sensitive competitive move. The risks raised are real but are execution risks, not blockers.

**Steelman:** Shipping earlier with meaningful hardware upgrades captures demand before the rival's flagship announcement, reinforcing the large-screen line's market leadership.

**Alternatives:** (1) **Do nothing** — keep the prior schedule, let the rival's September announcement capture the early-upgrade demand. (2) **Ship on original schedule with same upgrades** — safer testing window but cedes the pre-rival-launch window. (3) **Ship in August with only proven upgrades** (water resistance, larger battery in a standard body) — lower defect risk but a thinner body with a larger battery is the differentiator that justifies the early ship. None of these dominate the proposal as stated.

**Blocker test — NONE.** The proposal states the large-screen line sells well (confirmed demand), names a specific competitive trigger (rival's September flagship), and the feature set (larger battery, iris scanner, water resistance) is coherent and marketable. The risks raised — compressed testing, battery-in-thin-body, untested biometric at volume, global defect cost — are all **risks**, not blockers. They are addressable: add a kill criterion on defect rate, cap the initial ship volume, and run a focused pilot on the new sensor and battery thermals before full ramp. The proposal already commits to shipping in volume, so the cap is a phasing decision, not a scope change.

**Kill criteria:** (1) Pilot-line defect rate for the new iris scanner exceeds the prior biometric rollout's rate by more than X% — hold the sensor, ship without it. (2) Battery thermal runaway incidents in pre-production stress testing exceed zero — revert to the proven battery size. (3) Global launch readiness (carrier certification, inventory) not met by week 2 of August — phase the rollout by region rather than delay globally.

**Leading metric:** First-week return rate vs. the prior large-screen flagship launch; review by end of August.

The competitive window is the reason to move now; the execution risks are the reason to cap, pilot, and define kill criteria — not to delay.

DECISION: GO
