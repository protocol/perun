First call: GO — a proven format, a membership model that aligns incentives, and a clear path to scale.

**Blockers:** NONE.

**Risks (kill criteria):**
- Membership renewal rate below ~70% in year two → model fails; pivot to lower fee or drop it.
- Same-warehouse sales growth stalls within 18 months → stop expansion.
- Competitor matches prices using its cash advantage → cap geographic overlap.

**Steelman:** A membership-funded, limited-SKU, bulk-warehouse model can achieve structural cost advantages that a general retailer cannot match, creating a defensible niche even against a larger incumbent.

**Strongest attack survived:** The incumbent already operates this exact format with more locations and deeper pockets — why would a newcomer win? It fails because format replication is not the incumbent's moat; scale and execution are. The newcomer can choose markets where the incumbent is weak, and the membership-fee structure means profitability does not depend on per-unit margins surviving competitive pressure.

**Assumptions:**
- Membership fees will cover operating costs at thin merchandise margins.
- Bulk purchasing will appeal to a sufficient customer base in chosen markets.
- The newcomer can secure sites large enough for warehouse operations.

**Better ways considered:**
- Do nothing / stay out → forfeits a proven, scalable format with a clear revenue model.
- Differentiate format → adds complexity and dilutes the cost-advantage thesis.
- Partner with incumbent → no evidence of willingness; cedes control.

**Decision:** GO. Open the first warehouses, sell at thin margins, and rely on membership fees for profit. The model is proven by the incumbent's existence, the membership structure aligns shopper and retailer incentives, and the risks are manageable with geographic selectivity and disciplined expansion caps. Set a renewal-rate kill criterion at 18 months and review.

DECISION: GO
