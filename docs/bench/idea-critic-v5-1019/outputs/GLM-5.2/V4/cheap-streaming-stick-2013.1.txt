First call: GO — the stated risks are execution problems, not blockers; the steelman is that a cheap, phone-controlled dongle removes the remote, the on-screen menu, and the app-catalog arms race that sank the prior device.

**Blockers:** NONE. No risk raised is a fact already in hand that shows the plan fails. The catalog gap is a launch state, not a verdict; the margin concern is a price claim, not a margin claim.

**Risks (kill criteria):**
- Returns / support load from remote-less confusion exceeds 2× the dongle's unit cost within 90 days → add a minimal on-screen setup or a $9 companion remote.
- Gross margin per unit stays below the threshold where fulfillment + support costs are covered at projected volume by day 60 → raise price or cut BOM.
- Top-3 requested apps are absent at day 30 → subsidize ports or the open kit isn't pulling weight.

**Strongest attack survived:** "No remote + no on-screen menu = buyer can't set it up, so returns spike and the thin margin evaporates." This fails because the *phone is the remote* — that's the design, not a gap. Setup is phone-initiated, the dongle just receives. The confusion risk is real but testable: a small pilot can measure support-call rate before full rollout. The attack describes a UX challenge, not a structural flaw.

**Better-way check:** A more expensive box with a remote (the prior failed approach) reaches the same upside at higher cost and higher buyer friction — not better. "Do nothing" leaves the company absent from the living room while competitors entrench. The dongle is the cheaper, simpler, more reversible path.

**Reversibility:** High. A cheap dongle is a small, two-way-door bet. If it fails, the write-off is minimal. If it succeeds, it establishes a platform foothold at low cost — the open kit means third-party apps compound the value without the company building them all.

The proposal's own risks are its kill criteria, not its blockers. The bet is small, the downside is capped, and the upside is a platform position the company doesn't currently hold.

DECISION: GO
