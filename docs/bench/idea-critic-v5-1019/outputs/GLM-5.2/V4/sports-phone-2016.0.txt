**First call: GO.** The stated upside — beating a rival's expected September flagship to market with a differentiated large-screen device shipped in volume worldwide — is a sound operator bet on a two-way door that rewards speed.

**Steelman:** Moving launch to August captures demand before the rival's flagship lands, and the feature set (larger battery, iris scanner, water resistance) is defensibly competitive.

**Blockers: NONE.** The proposal's own numbers work: a large-screen flagship already sells well, the feature set is achievable, and August still precedes September. No stated fact contradicts the core claim. The risks raised (less testing time, larger battery in thinner body, untested biometric at volume, costly defects at global scale) are all *risks*, not *blockers* — each becomes a kill criterion.

**Risks → kill criteria:**
- Battery density in thinner body → thermal/runaway test must pass before mass production begins; if it fails, hold the thinner chassis and ship the prior thickness.
- Iris scanner untested at volume → pilot line yield threshold; if scanner defect rate exceeds X% at 100K units, revert to fingerprint-only SKU.
- Compressed testing window → gate final firmware on a defined regression suite; if critical defects remain at T-minus-3-weeks, delay 2 weeks rather than ship broken.

**Cheaper alternative considered:** ship on the original schedule with incremental battery improvement only. Rejected — it cedes the pre-rival-launch window, which is the proposal's core strategic value.

**Pre-mortem:** The most likely failure is a battery or iris-scanner defect escaping compressed testing and forcing a global recall. Mitigation: the kill criteria above gate mass production, and a staged regional rollout (home market first, global 2-3 weeks later) contains blast radius without sacrificing the August headline.

**Leading metric:** first-month defect rate vs. prior flagship baseline; review at 30 days post-launch.

The bet is uncertain, as every bet worth making is. But the upside is real, the door is two-way, and the risks are addressable with capped, time-boxed gates.

DECISION: GO
