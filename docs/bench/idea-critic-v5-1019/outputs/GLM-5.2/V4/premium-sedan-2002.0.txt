First call: NO-GO. The mass-market badge is the product in this class, and the proposal does not answer that.

Steelman: the largest regional carmaker has scale, engineering depth and cost advantages; a dedicated factory and no-compromise hardware could let it offer premium quality at a premium price and capture margin upside.

Blockers:
- Brand equity is the core claim and it contradicts the stated facts. The proposal's own risk list names the blocker: buyers pay for the badge, and the brand has no luxury image. A mass-market name on a premium-priced sedan removes the route to the upside before the factory is built.
- The unit economics fail as proposed. A dedicated factory plus large engine plus advanced suspension plus top-quality materials at low volume is expensive to build; pricing at rival levels against an unproven mass-market badge does not cover that cost on the numbers given.

Risks (become kill criteria if revised):
- No badge equity → buyers reject the mass-market name.
- Low-volume cost → margin below rivals.
- Worldwide launch at full scale before any market signal.

Cheaper path that reaches the same upside: a separate premium sub-brand or partnership, a single-market pilot at capped volume, and a kill criterion (e.g. pre-orders below a numeric threshold by a fixed date trigger a stop or pivot). A worldwide launch with a dedicated factory is the one-way door that should not be taken first.

DECISION: NO-GO
