**First call: NO-GO.** $580M for a two-year-old social site whose revenue model is unproven, where user tastes shift fast and competitors loom, is a bet that needs a cheaper entry or a staged structure — not a full-price acquisition now.

**Steelman:** The target's large young audience is an asset the acquirer's advertising business cannot replicate organically; combining the two creates a unique ad inventory that justifies a premium over conventional valuation.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Tastes change and competitors exist, so the audience will evaporate." This fails to block: the proposal already names this risk, and the counter is a kill criterion (retention threshold at month 6) plus a staged structure that limits exposure if the audience does decay. The attack becomes a tripwire, not a blocker.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's own figures do not contradict its claim; no stated fact shows the plan fails as proposed.

**Risks (→ kill criteria):**
- Revenue model unproven → cap initial spend at ~$150M earn-in; full $580M only if ad-revenue-per-user clears a stated threshold by month 12.
- Audience volatility → if month-6 active users fall below 70% of current, pause integration and reassess.
- Competitive pressure → track share-of-young-audience quarterly; below a set floor triggers a strategy review.

**Better way:** A staged earn-in (minority stake → option to acquire) reaches the same strategic upside at a fraction of the upfront risk, and lets the acquirer observe whether audience growth converts to ad revenue before committing the full $580M.

**Dissent ledger:** The strategic logic (young audience + ad business) is sound and the target's growth is real. The objection is purely to paying full price now for an unproven revenue model when a staged structure captures the upside while limiting downside. If the full $580M must be paid as a take-it-or-leave-it offer with no earn-in, the risks outweigh the strategic fit and the call flips to no-go.

DECISION: NO-GO
