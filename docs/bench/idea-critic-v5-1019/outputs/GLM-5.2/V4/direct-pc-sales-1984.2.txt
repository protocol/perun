First call: GO. The numbers work at the stated scale, the model is reversible, and the direct-order discount is the core claim.

**Steelman:** Direct, inventory-light assembly undercuts branded retail on price and capital; growth funds itself.

**Alternatives:** Do nothing (stays a student sideline, no scale). Open retail (kills the price advantage, burns capital — strictly worse on the stated facts). Sell through resellers (adds margin layer, loses customer relationship). The cheapest path to the same upside is the proposal itself.

**Blockers: NONE.** No stated fact contradicts the core claim. The four risks are real but unproven — they are risks, not blockers. The touch-and-try objection is the sharpest: branded buyers expect to see the machine. But the proposal's own logic answers it — the customer who prioritizes price over showrooming is the target segment, and the direct model's margin advantage funds a return policy or on-site service if the "can't see it" objection actually suppresses orders. None of these remove the route to the upside as stated.

**Kill criteria:** Gross margin per unit must cover parts + assembly labor + delivery within 90 days; if not, raise price or halt. Leading metric: repeat/referral rate at 60 days (a direct-only brand lives or dies on word-of-mouth). Review: 90 days.

**Remaining risk:** Supplier credit is the quiet killer — a parts shortage during a growth spike could freeze fulfillment. Cap the first growth stage at what current supplier relationships can guarantee, not at what revenue permits.

The model is sound, the downside is bounded, and the risks have named tripwires. The proposal survives its sharpest attack.

DECISION: GO
