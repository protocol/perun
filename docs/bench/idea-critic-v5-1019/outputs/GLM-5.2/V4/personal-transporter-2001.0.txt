First call: GO. The stated risks are real but none is a blocker: they are the classic adoption curve for any new personal vehicle, not a fatal flaw in the product or the market.

Steelman: a self-balancing electric transporter replaces short urban trips that are too far to walk and too close to justify a car, at a price point that is high but not prohibitive for the target consumer.

The strongest attack is the price. At 5,000 dollars the transporter costs more than a used car, which also carries cargo and a passenger and works in bad weather. That is a serious objection. But it fails to kill the proposal because the transporter is not competing with cars on the same job: it competes for short trips where a car is a liability (parking, traffic, fuel cost). A used car that costs the same and does more is irrelevant if the trip is two miles across a dense city. The scooter and bicycle comparisons cut the other way: they are cheaper but require no skill to balance, which means the transporter is not obviously superior to them for the mass market. That is a risk, not a blocker.

The sidewalk and road rules are the sharpest practical concern. If no jurisdiction allows the transporter on any road or sidewalk, the product has no market. But the proposal does not claim the rules are already hostile; it lists them as risks. That makes them kill criteria, not a reason to hold.

Kill criteria: within the first pilot window, if no city permits use on at least one class of road or sidewalk, stop. Leading metric: units sold per pilot city, reviewed at 90 days.

The cargo and passenger limitations are features of the category, not defects. They bound the market but do not eliminate it.

DECISION: GO
