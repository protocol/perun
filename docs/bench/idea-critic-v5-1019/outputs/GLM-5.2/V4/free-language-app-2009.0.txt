First call: GO. A zero-marginal-cost learning app with free distribution, built by people who can live on academic salaries, has no downside worth blocking the launch.

Steelman: free language learning at internet scale, where the learning activity itself produces a translation marketplace — the users' practice becomes the supply side of a real business.

Do-nothing comparison: building nothing preserves capital but forfeits a market with near-zero marginal cost and strong network effects. The downside of building is small (two people's time, modest hosting) and fully reversible.

Blockers test — NONE:
- "Translations may be poor" is a risk, not a blocker. It is testable on day one: run a batch through the pipeline and measure quality against a known-good benchmark. If below threshold, the revenue model shifts but the free product still stands.
- "Free learners may not become paying customers" misunderstands the model. Revenue comes from selling translation work, not from charging learners. The objection attacks a model the proposal does not rely on.
- "Established courses are more polished" is true of every entrant in every market. Free + accessible + gamified is a defensible wedge against expensive, polished, seated courses. This is a competitive risk, not a fatal flaw.
- "Revenue model is unproven" is correct and irrelevant to the build decision. The proposal explicitly defers revenue and keeps learning free. You do not need a proven model to build a free product that two people can afford to give away.

Pre-mortem: the most likely failure is that learner translations are too noisy to sell at quality publishers demand. That is a one-way door only for the revenue thesis; the free-learning product survives it. The cost of delay is low but non-zero — 2009 is a moment when mobile app stores are about to open a distribution channel that rewards early movers.

Kill criteria: within the first pilot window, measure (a) completion rate of a full short-lesson track and (b) translation quality score against professional output. If completion is below 20% or quality below 60% of professional baseline, pivot the revenue model before scaling sales.

DECISION: GO
