First call: NO-GO. The proposal's own facts defeat it.

**Blockers:**
1. **The stated target users do not want it.** The proposal concedes the phone "must win on the screen and apps buyers use most," then states the app catalog is "far larger" for leading phones at the same price. The plan offers no answer to this gap — it is a negative the proposal itself names as decisive.
2. **The core differentiators are unproven demand dressed as features.** A 3D head-tracking effect and a camera product-identifier serve the retailer's goal (selling more products) more than the buyer's. The proposal raises but does not resolve whether buyers value the 3D effect.
3. **Distribution narrows the already-thin upside.** A single-carrier launch limits the addressable market for a device whose only edge is novelty.

**Steelman:** A retailer with proven hardware execution (tablets, e-readers) can extend brand trust into a new category, and the membership bundle creates a recurring-revenue hook that subsidizes the device.

**Strongest attack survived:** None. The steelman depends on buyers choosing this phone over incumbents at the same price, and the proposal's own risk list shows that gap is unaddressed.

**Kill criteria (if revised):** A capped pilot (single carrier, limited units) measuring 30-day retention against the retailer's own tablets. If retention does not exceed the tablet baseline by a named margin within 60 days, stop.

The proposal asks the retailer to enter the one category where its strengths (content ecosystem, low-price disruption) are inverted, against incumbents whose advantage the proposal itself names. That is a blocker, not a risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
