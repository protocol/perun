**First call: GO.** Two expensive cars prove the core works; the only path from boutique to scale runs through exactly this bet.

**Steelman:** The cheapest viable electric sedan, funded by refundable deposits, converts niche proof into volume demand and forces the supply chain to scale.

**Alternatives vs do nothing:**
- Do nothing: stay a boutique maker of expensive cars; never reach scale, die slowly.
- Build the cheaper car without deposits: same risk, no demand signal, more capital needed upfront.
- Partner with an established automaker: slower, dilutes control, may never ship.

The proposal beats do nothing and the alternatives on the stated facts.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks raised are real but are execution risks, not contradictions in the plan. "Never produced at that volume" is the nature of scaling. "Battery supply must scale" is a supply chain challenge, not a logical impossibility. "Deposits may not become sales" is a risk to monitor, not a fact that defeats the claim. "Little margin" is a pricing concern, not a blocker to starting.

**Risks → kill criteria:**
- Deposit-to-sale conversion below a threshold → pause production ramp.
- Battery supply commitments not secured by a date → the plan cannot scale.
- Production line not validated at target rate by a date → delay customer deliveries.

**Strongest attack survived:** "A company that has built only two expensive models in small numbers cannot, in one year, begin producing a much cheaper car at far higher volumes — the operational gap is too large." This is the sharpest objection, and it is a reason the plan is hard, not a reason it is wrong. The proposal does not claim the transition is easy; it claims the transition is necessary and that deposits will validate demand. The alternative — waiting until experience is organically acquired — is a permanent never, because volume experience only comes from attempting volume.

The margin risk is the weakest objection: a cheaper car can still be profitable if battery costs fall as projected, and the deposit-funded model reduces the capital barrier. The company's existing engineering talent and brand momentum are assets the plan leverages.

DECISION: GO
