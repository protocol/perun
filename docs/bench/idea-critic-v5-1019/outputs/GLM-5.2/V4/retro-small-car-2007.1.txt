First call: GO. Retro-styled small cars (Mini, Beetle, Fiat 500) repeatedly commanded premiums and sold strongly; the pattern is proven.

**Steelman:** A beloved heritage design on an existing platform, priced above the platform-mate with extensive personalization, captures emotional-premium margins that commodity small cars cannot.

**Skeptic — assumptions:**
- The 1957 model's appeal transfers to a 2007 buyer — *assumed*; competitor retro launches succeeding is supporting but not confirming evidence.
- Personalization options will be uptake-positive and margin-accretive — *assumed*; trim proliferation can raise per-unit cost without raising price.
- The platform-mate's cost structure supports a premium price without eroding volume — *assumed*.

**Better-way:** Do nothing. The company is financially weak and recent models have sold poorly. But heritage equity on an existing platform is a low-capex way to break that streak; doing nothing does not address the core problem.

**Kill-criteria:**
- If first-12-month sales fall below platform-mate volume by more than 15%, kill or reprice.
- If personalization-option uptake is below 20% of buyers within six months, trim the matrix.
- If gross margin per unit does not exceed the platform-mate's by the targeted premium within 18 months, discontinue.

**Strongest attack survived:** The company's recent poor sales record means its brand cannot command a premium — buyers will cross-shop the cheaper platform-mate. This fails because the proposal's differentiator is not the brand but the specific 1957 heritage design, which is a proven emotional-premium lever independent of current brand perception. The personalization options further differentiate from the platform-mate.

**Remaining risk:** Thin small-car margins plus trim complexity could erode the premium if uptake is low; this is covered by the kill criteria above.

DECISION: GO
