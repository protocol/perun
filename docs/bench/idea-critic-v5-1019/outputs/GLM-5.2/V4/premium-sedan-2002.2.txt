First call: NO-GO. The badge is the product; the factory is sunk cost before the badge earns its premium.

Blocker: the proposal's own stated risk — luxury buyers pay for the badge — is the core claim inverted. A mass-market name at premium price faces rejection not as a *risk* but as the default outcome, and nothing in the plan addresses how the badge crosses that gap. The dedicated factory compounds it: low volume plus bespoke tooling plus worldwide launch is maximum irreversible spend placed *before* any evidence of badge acceptance.

Better way: build the sedan in an existing line at low volume, sell it in one or two home-region markets first, and treat the badge question as the thing being tested — not the thing being overcome by materials and suspension alone.

Kill criteria: if within the first 18 months the sedan cannot sustain at least 60% of the target premium-segment transaction price, or if fewer than 40% of buyers cite the brand as a reason to purchase, stop the worldwide rollout and reassess the badge strategy entirely.

Remaining risk: even a successful regional pilot may not transfer globally, and the brand-equity rebuild could take longer than the product cycle allows.

DECISION: NO-GO
