**First call: GO.** A sports-car maker with a loyal enthusiast following has brand equity that can extend into a high-margin segment; the bet is whether the brand carries, not whether SUVs sell.

**Steelman:** The brand's engineering credibility and loyal following can command a premium price in the luxury SUV segment, diversifying revenue beyond a small, cyclical sports-car range.

**Alternatives:**
- *Do nothing:* keep the small range. Safe, but revenue stays concentrated in a cyclical, low-volume segment with no hedge.
- *Build an independent SUV platform:* preserves brand distance but multiplies cost and time-to-market, with no guarantee of better outcomes.
- *Co-develop on a shared platform (as proposed):* lower cost, faster launch, leverages partner scale. The brand risk is real but manageable with positioning.

**Fit:** Diversification beyond a small sports-car range aligns with long-term revenue resilience. The loyal following is an asset for a halo vehicle, not just a risk.

**Pre-mortem:** The failure scenario is that loyal customers see the SUV as a betrayal and the vehicle fails to attract enough new buyers to offset the first-year cost. But the proposal already names this risk, and the counter is that the sports-car brand's credibility can extend to a high-priced, powerful SUV — a claim that is plausible but unproven.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks raised are real but they are *risks*, not blockers. The proposal does not contradict itself; its own figures are not shown to fail; no alternative is shown to reach the same upside cheaper. The established luxury SUV makers are strong, but that is a competitive risk, not a disqualifier.

**Risks → Kill criteria:**
- Loyal-customer alienation: monitor pre-orders from existing customers; if below a set threshold by a fixed date, reconsider positioning.
- First-year cost: cap the initial investment; review against sales targets at month six.
- Sports image damage: track brand sentiment metrics post-launch.

**Strongest attack survived:** "A heavy SUV damages the sports image irreversibly." This fails because the proposal positions the SUV as high-priced with a powerful engine under the sports-car brand — extending the brand rather than abandoning it — and brand sentiment is measurable and reversible with repositioning.

DECISION: GO
