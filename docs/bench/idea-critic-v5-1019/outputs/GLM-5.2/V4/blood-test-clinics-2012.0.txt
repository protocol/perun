First call: NO-GO — the single largest risk (unproven technology) is unaddressed, and the deal structure removes the chain's ability to verify before committing $350M.

**Steelman:** If the device works as claimed, the chain captures exclusive access to a disruptive health platform across hundreds of stores — a defensible moat and a new revenue stream at a scale competitors cannot match.

**Strongest attack survived:** "No published studies" is not a minor risk — it is the core claim unverified. But the proposal *states* this gap, so it is a risk to be converted into a kill criterion, not a hidden blocker.

**Blockers: NONE.** The stated facts do not self-contradict. The risks are real but are risks, not blockers: the technology *may* not work (not *does not* work), the company *may* resist sharing methods (not stated as refusal), regulators *may* question the tests (not stated as pending action). No fact in hand shows the plan fails as proposed.

**Risks (→ kill criteria):**
1. Technology unproven → require an independent third-party validation of accuracy against standard lab tests across N sample types before any rollout. Kill if variance exceeds X%.
2. Methods undisclosed → contractual right to audit and to terminate exclusivity if validation fails.
3. No medical expertise → partner with an accredited lab operator for clinical governance; chain operates the footprint, not the medicine.
4. Regulatory uncertainty → require a current regulatory clearance letter as a condition precedent.

**Alternatives:** Do nothing (safe, no moat). Smaller pilot (10–20 stores, $10–20M cap, 6-month window with accuracy kill criterion) reaches the same validation at 5% of the cost and preserves optionality. This is strictly better as a first step.

**Decision:** go-if — the chain should pursue the opportunity, but not as proposed. A capped pilot with independent validation, audit rights, and a regulatory condition precedent is the correct first step. The $350M exclusivity deal without verification is a one-way door on unproven technology.

DECISION: NO-GO
