**First call: NO-GO.** Forcing a touch-first interface onto non-touch PCs, with no rollback, bets the dominant OS on a change that its own risk list admits will confuse and burden the users who pay the bills.

**Steelman:** The dominant desktop OS must unify tablet and PC under one touch-first shell now, or cede the tablet market and the next computing era.

**Strongest attack survived:** none — the strongest attack *succeeds*.

**Blocker (stated facts, not risk):** the proposal removes the old start menu and provides "no easy way to go back to the old desktop" on the very machines — keyboard-and-mouse, non-touch — that are 100% of its current revenue base. That is not a risk that might materialize; it is a design decision the proposal already commits to. A forced, non-reversible UX change on non-touch hardware is a concrete failure scenario: users on machines without touch get a touch-first shell they cannot use comfortably and cannot escape.

**Risks (real, become kill criteria):** corporate training and support costs are high and unquantified; developers have not yet committed to the new platform; the change assumes tablet convergence is what desktop users want, untested.

**Cheaper alternative that reaches the same upside:** ship the touch-first start screen and app store on touch hardware and tablets first; leave the traditional start menu on non-touch PCs; measure adoption, then migrate. This captures the tablet opportunity without betting the installed base.

**Decision:** no-go. The tablet strategy is sound; forcing it onto every non-touch PC with no rollback is the blocker. Pilot on touch devices, keep desktop optional, then decide.

DECISION: NO-GO
