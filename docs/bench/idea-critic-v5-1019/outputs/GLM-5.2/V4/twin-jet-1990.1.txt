First call: NO-GO. One anchor customer for 34 aircraft does not underwrite a very-large-cost clean-sheet program, and the regulatory gate the whole business case depends on has not been cleared.

Blocker: the proposal's own economics. Launching an all-new wide-body on a single 34-unit order means the program's break-even — typically several hundred deliveries — rests on follow-on demand that is assumed, not committed. The stated evidence (one airline, 34 frames) does not support the stated cost. No rival action, no design-with-airlines friction changes that arithmetic.

Risks (kill criteria if revised):
- ETOPS certification for extended overwater twin-engine operations is ungranted. If approval is delayed or denied, the aircraft's core route economics collapse and deliveries slip. Kill criterion: secure regulatory framework before long-lead spending.
- Single-customer dependency. If that airline defers or cancels, the program is exposed with no diversified order book. Kill criterion: commitments covering a meaningful share of break-even before launch authority.
- Development cost magnitude vs. order coverage. The cost-to-revenue gap on 34 frames is the financial blocker itself.

Strongest attack survived: "the rival is also building new models, so waiting cedes the market." Rebuttal: launching prematurely on insufficient demand risks a larger loss than a delayed entry. A rival moving first is a risk, not a reason to skip the demand and certification gates.

This is a one-way door: billions committed, multi-year timeline, not cleanly reversible once tooling and supply chain are locked. The cost of delay is lost market positioning and early delivery slots; the cost of premature launch is existential financial exposure on a program that cannot yet stand on its order book.

Path forward if revised: secure binding commitments from multiple airlines totaling a credible break-even threshold, confirm the regulatory path for overwater twin-engine operations, and re-present with corrected economics. The concept is sound; the launch case as stated is not.

DECISION: NO-GO
