First call: GO. The sports-car maker's small range and loyal following are exactly the assets a shared-platform crossover leverages — the parent absorbs engineering cost, the brand commands a price premium, and the segment is growing. The risks are real but they are risks, not blockers.

Steelman: a high-riding, powerful vehicle wearing the sports-car badge extends the brand's emotional promise (performance, engineering purity) to the fastest-growing and highest-margin segment, while the larger group's platform collapses the cost and risk of doing it alone. The upside is access to buyers who love the brand but need space and practicality — a group the two-seat sports car will never capture.

Attack — "betrayal of the brand": this is the sharpest objection. Enthusiasts said the same thing about four-door sedans and front-engine layouts at other marques; the brands that survived are the ones that expanded. The loyal customer is not lost by adding a model; he is lost by going bankrupt. The off-road angle is the weak spot — it muddies the positioning — but that is a trim-level decision, not a reason to abort.

Better-way: do nothing. The sports-car range is small and the following loyal, but small range = thin revenue base, single-segment exposure, and dependence on a product cycle the maker does not fully control. Doing nothing preserves image and cedes the highest-margin segment in the market to everyone else. The crossover wins.

Kill criteria: first-year retail volume below the platform's break-even threshold (to be set against shared-tooling amortization); brand-health score among existing customers dropping more than a defined margin at the twelve-month mark. Leading metric: deposit-to-conversion rate in the first ninety days of order books open. Review at month six.

Dissent ledger: off-road capability dilutes the sports-car positioning — recommend a street-focused, on-road-performance variant first, with off-road as an option package. The shared platform must not dictate suspension tuning that undermines the badge's dynamic reputation.

DECISION: GO
