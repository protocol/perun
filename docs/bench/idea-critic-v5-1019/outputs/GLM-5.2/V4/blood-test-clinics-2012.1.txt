First call: NO-GO — the proposal's own stated facts contradict its core claim.

**Blockers:**
1. The upside depends entirely on the device working as described, yet the proposal states the technology has not been shown in published studies. The central claim is unverified by the proposer's own admission.
2. The company will not share its methods, which means the chain cannot independently verify the device's accuracy before committing 350 million dollars. The exclusivity terms lock the chain into a black box.

**Risks (kill criteria if revised):**
- No medical expertise in-house means no capacity to evaluate or operate clinical testing — this is a capability gap, not a detail.
- Regulatory risk is stated as a possibility; given no published validation and undisclosed methods, regulatory scrutiny is closer to a certainty.
- Reversibility: the 350-million-dollar commitment plus physical clinic buildout in hundreds of stores is a one-way door at meaningful scale.

**Alternatives:**
- A capped pilot (a single store or region, a small fraction of the capital) with a kill criterion of independent third-party validation of test accuracy within a fixed window. This reaches the same upside at a fraction of the risk.
- Do nothing. The chain has no medical expertise, the technology is unvalidated, and the methods are secret — doing nothing is clearly superior to the proposal as stated.

**Strongest attack survived:** None. The attack that the device may simply not work is not survived — it is reinforced by every fact the proposal itself states.

DECISION: NO-GO
