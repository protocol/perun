First call: NO-GO. A retailer with no phone experience, launching at flagship prices through one carrier, betting on a 3D gimmick and a camera-shopping feature in a market defined by screen quality and app catalogs.

**Blockers:**

1. **The value proposition is self-defeating.** The proposal states the price matches leading phones with far larger app catalogs — and then offers a 3D effect and product recognition as differentiation. These are novelty features, not the screen-and-apps quality the proposal itself says a phone must win on. The plan's own evidence removes its route to the upside.

2. **Distribution contradicts ambition.** A premium device sold through a single carrier limits the addressable market to that carrier's subscribers willing to pay flagship prices. The proposal acknowledges this risk but offers no answer — no multi-carrier path, no unlocked sales, no volume assumption that works within one carrier's base.

3. **No core competency transfer.** The retailer sells tablets and e-readers — devices defined by reading and media consumption, sold direct to consumer. A phone is a daily-driver communication device sold through carriers. Nothing in the stated experience bridges this gap, and the membership-bundle incentive is too thin to offset app-catalog and distribution disadvantages.

**Kill criteria if pursued anyway:** a pilot within one carrier, capped at a fixed unit count, killed if attach rate falls below a stated threshold within 90 days of launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
