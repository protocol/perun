**First call: GO.** A small-range sports-car maker has a loyal enthusiast base but limited revenue diversity; a luxury crossover opens a high-margin segment that pure sports cars cannot reach.

**Steelman:** The brand's engineering credibility and enthusiast loyalty can be extended into a luxury crossover that commands a premium price, using a shared platform to control cost and risk while reaching buyers who would never consider a two-seat sports car.

**Alternatives considered:** Do nothing — safe but leaves the maker exposed to any sports-car market downturn with no cushion. Expand the sports-car range — lower risk but hits a ceiling on volume and revenue. The crossover is the only option that materially changes the maker's revenue base.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks raised are real but are *risks*, not blockers. Brand dilution is a concern the launch itself must manage through positioning and quality. High first-year cost is expected for a new platform launch. Strong incumbents exist in every profitable segment — that is a reason to differentiate, not to stay out.

**Kill criteria:** Brand-health metric (enthusiast sentiment and repeat-buyer intent) measured at launch and at six months; if repeat-buyer intent among existing customers drops below a set threshold, reposition. Sales target at twelve months; if missed by a defined margin, review pricing or volume strategy. The shared-platform agreement must cap the maker's financial exposure for the first production year.

**Risks:** Loyal customers may defect — mitigated by keeping the sports-car range uncompromised. The vehicle's weight and off-road positioning may clash with the brand's image — mitigated by emphasizing engineering quality and performance character rather than utility. Incumbents are strong — mitigated by the brand's distinctiveness and premium positioning.

**Strongest attack survived:** "A heavy luxury SUV fundamentally contradicts a sports-car brand's identity, and loyal enthusiasts will abandon the maker." This fails because brand identity is not static — it is built through execution. A high-quality, performance-oriented crossover can *extend* the brand rather than dilute it, and the sports-car range remains intact. The maker's engineering reputation is an asset in a new segment, not a constraint on one.

DECISION: GO
