First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters the most competitive hardware market with no differentiator that matters to phone buyers.

**Steelman:** A retailer with loyal customers, proven hardware chops, and a commerce flywheel enters mobile with a device that turns the camera into a point-of-sale terminal and bundles a year of membership — locking users into the ecosystem.

**Blockers:**
1. The proposal's own facts show the price matches leading phones with far larger app catalogs. A phone must win on screen and apps. The app catalog deficit is structural and the 3D effect is explicitly listed as something buyers may not value. The proposal does not answer why a buyer pays premium-phone money for fewer apps and an unproven gimmick.
2. The single-carrier launch limits who can buy — the proposal states this and offers no multi-carrier path or timeline to broaden reach.

**Risks (kill criteria):**
- Pre-order volume below X units in first 30 days → kill or restructure before full launch.
- App catalog gap not closing through developer incentives by month 6 → exit.
- The Fire Phone's actual outcome (2014, discounted to 99 cents within 60 days, discontinued within 13 months) confirms every risk raised was real and every bet failed. The membership bundle and product-recognition feature did not move phone buyers.

**Alternatives considered:**
- A mid-range phone at half the price through multiple carriers addresses the price and carrier-limit blockers but still faces the app-catalog gap.
- No phone; extend the tablet/reader line. Avoids the structural deficit entirely.

The proposal fails its own stated test: a phone must win on the screen and apps buyers use most, and the proposal concedes the app catalog is smaller and the 3D effect may not matter. The membership bundle is a commerce incentive, not a phone-buying reason. The single carrier compounds the reach problem. This is a one-way door with no pilot, and the real-world outcome confirmed it.

DECISION: NO-GO
