**First call: GO.** Small minority stake with optionality, run independently, capped downside.

**Steelman:** For a modest sum, buy a real option on a fast-casual format that could matter, without betting the core or forcing integration. The upside is a new growth lane; the downside is the investment amount.

**Alternatives:** *Do nothing* — costs zero but cedes the learning and the position. *Acquire outright* — more control but more money, more integration risk, and a one-way door on a 16-unit chain that may not scale. *Wait and watch* — cheaper still, but the right to invest more later is itself the watch. The minority stake beats all three on cost vs. optionality.

**Pre-mortem:** It fails because the format genuinely doesn't scale beyond a regional novelty, or because the giant's culture smothers it even at arm's length. Either way the loss is the stake, not the brand.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks raised are all *risks*, not *blockers*. Tiny scale is the reason the price is low and the stake is minority — it's the opportunity, not the flaw. Slower service and higher skill needs are the chain's operational problem to solve with its own management running independently. Price above the core menu means it targets a different customer, which is the point of a format experiment. Management attention is a resource allocation question, not a deal-breaker for a minority stake.

**Kill criteria:** Scale test — does unit count grow and do new units match existing unit economics within 24 months? If not, exercise the exit (don't invest more). Leading metric: new-unit count and four-wall EBITDA at new vs. legacy units, reviewed at month 12 and 24.

**Remaining risk:** The option to invest more becomes a pressure to invest more regardless of evidence. The kill criteria above are the guardrail.

DECISION: GO
