First call: GO. The product is a deliberate trade-off — premium price for design and silence, not spec-sheet maximization. That is a coherent position in a market where the maker's brand is its recovered asset.

Steelman: a fanless, silent, design-forward desktop at a premium price differentiates on an axis competitors ignore, leveraging brand recovery.

Blockers: NONE. The risks raised are all "some buyers won't like it" — but the product isn't aimed at upgraders. It's aimed at buyers who value silence, design, and simplicity over slots and speed. The price-above-tower objection assumes the market is unitary; it isn't. The all-in-one comparison is closer but the form factors differ.

Risks: manufacturing yield on the acrylic case is real but testable in pilot. Lack of expansion slots is a feature for the target segment, not a defect. The strongest attack: buyers at this price expect either performance (tower) or integrated display (all-in-one), and a sealed silent box fits neither bucket — so demand is unproven. This survives as a risk, not a blocker: it's a capped, reversible first step with a clear kill criterion.

Kill criteria: pilot run, sell-through below target within 90 days → stop. Leading metric: units sold per week against a break-even threshold, reviewed at day 45.

The proposal's own risks are the requester's listed risks, not facts already in hand showing failure. No blocker found.

DECISION: GO
