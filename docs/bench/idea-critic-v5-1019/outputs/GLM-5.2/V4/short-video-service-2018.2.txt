First call: NO-GO. The numbers, as stated, do not work.

**Blockers:**

1. **Unit economics vs. content spend.** ~$1.75B raised, high-budget commissions, two-country launch. Even at a generous $5/month blended ARPU with 50M paying subs (already optimistic for a new platform), that's $3B/year gross — but content amortization, marketing, and tech against a two-country footprint with no TV distribution leaves breakeven far beyond the spend window. The proposal's own risk ("a lot to recoup") is the blocker: the budget scale assumes a subscriber base the launch scope cannot deliver.

2. **Free substitute already won the use case.** Social platforms dominate short-form mobile video at zero price. The proposal acknowledges this risk but offers no differentiation mechanism, no exclusive talent lock, and no reason a phone-only viewer pays monthly for what they already get free. "Professionally made" is not a moat against free-and-good-enough.

3. **No TV at launch removes the retention surface.** Streaming economics run on churn control. A phone-only product in 2020 competes for attention against TikTok/Instagram, not against Netflix on a couch. Removing the largest-screen retention path at launch compounds the breakeven problem rather than solving it.

**Risks (kill criteria if revised):**

- No pilot or capped first step named; $1.75B is an uncapped bet.
- No kill criterion: subscriber threshold or churn cap by month 6.
- No leading metric defined for the pilot window.
- No stated north star or stage gate from the executive team.

**Strongest attack survived:** None — the proposal does not survive its own stated risks because it provides no mechanism (differentiation, cost reduction, phased spend, TV partnership) to address them.

**Dissent ledger:** The talent and format insight is real; short-form premium could work at 1/10th the spend, with TV distribution, launched in one market as a pilot. As proposed at this scale, the bet is uncapped and the breakeven is not reachable on the stated facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
