First call: GO. The stated risks are manageable execution problems, not blockers, and the economics of mail-order subscription razors work on the numbers given.

**Blockers:** NONE. The dominant brands advertise heavily, but advertising spend does not prevent a low-cost entrant from taking price-sensitive customers. The brands could cut prices or sue, but these are risks, not facts already in hand showing the plan fails. Dependence on one supplier is a standard operational risk for a reseller launch.

**Risks:** (1) Brands cut prices — mitigated by the subscription model's convenience and the low starting price point. (2) Brands sue — a real cost, but not a reason not to start; it is a cost of doing business in this category. (3) Single video brings little traffic — this is the sharpest objection. One video is a thin acquisition channel. But the cost of trying is low, and a second video or alternative channel can follow quickly. (4) Subscription aversion — unproven but testable immediately with the first orders.

**Strongest attack survived:** A single low-budget video cannot generate enough traffic to sustain a subscription business, and the low price means thin margins that cannot absorb high customer acquisition costs. This fails because the proposal asks for orders at once on a simple website — the first video is a test of demand, not the entire go-to-market. If it works, more videos follow. If it does not, the founders learn cheaply.

**Kill criteria:** If the first video generates fewer than [X] orders in its first two weeks, produce a second video with different creative before scaling spend. If customer acquisition cost exceeds the gross profit from the average subscription length, stop and reassess the model. Review after 30 days.

The proposal is a small, cheap, reversible bet on an untested demand hypothesis. The risks are real but none shows the plan cannot work as stated.

DECISION: GO
