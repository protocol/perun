**First call: GO.** Cheap, simple, reversible hardware that opens a new surface for an already-large content library.

**Steelman:** A near-zero-price dongle removes the adoption barrier that killed prior TV attempts; phone-as-remote reuses a device everyone already carries, and an open kit turns a thin client into a platform play.

**Better-way:** Do nothing keeps the prior failed device as the lesson; a higher-priced device with a bundled remote is the strongest alternative — it protects margin and removes buyer confusion, but it reintroduces the cost and complexity barrier that already failed.

**Pre-mortem:** It failed because buyers expected a remote and returned it, or because the open kit never attracted developers and the device stayed a dumb pipe with no app ecosystem.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks (no remote, thin margin, few launch apps, incumbent catalogs) are real but not disqualifying: the phone-as-remote design answers the remote concern directly; near-zero margin is acceptable for a land-grab where platform value (developer adoption, content lock-in) exceeds per-unit profit; few launch apps is expected at launch and the open kit is the mechanism to grow the catalog; incumbent catalog size is a headwind, not a blocker, since the dongle's wedge is price and simplicity, not catalog depth.

**Risks / kill criteria:** Return rate above an industry-benchmark threshold within the first 90 days → add a minimal remote or on-screen setup. Developer kit adoption below a target number of shipped third-party apps within 6 months → reconsider the platform thesis. Leading metric: attach rate and activation completion; review at 90 days.

The sharpest attack: no remote means buyer confusion and returns, and thin margin means those returns are disproportionately costly. It fails because the very design choices that enable the low price also create the support and return burden that erodes the margin that was already near zero. This did not survive: the phone-as-remote design is a deliberate answer to the remote question, and the platform upside justifies a margin sacrifice that a pure hardware play could not.

DECISION: GO
