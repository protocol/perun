First call: GO — a cheap, phone-controlled dongle is a small, reversible bet that reaches the living room without competing head-on on app catalogs.

The strongest attack is the catalog gap: established boxes already have thousands of apps, and this launches with few. But the proposal doesn't compete on catalog — it competes on price and simplicity. A dongle with no remote and no on-screen menu is *easier* to use than a streaming box, not harder, for the specific job of playing phone content on a TV. The open kit means the catalog gap is a timing problem, not a structural one. The thin margin is real but acceptable: the goal is television presence and ecosystem reach, not per-unit profit. Earlier TV attempts failed because they were expensive and complex; this inverts both assumptions.

**Blockers:** NONE. No stated fact contradicts the core claim. The risks raised are risks, not blockers — each is a known trade-off the proposal already answers (open kit for apps, phone-as-remote for the missing hardware, low price for adoption despite sparse content).

**Risks (become kill criteria):**
- Buyer confusion from no remote/menu → kill if return rate exceeds X% in first 90 days.
- Few launch apps → kill if third-party app count doesn't reach Y within 6 months of the open kit shipping.
- Thin margin → watch attach rate of premium content; review at quarter 2.

**Leading metric:** units activated within first week of purchase (measures actual buyer understanding, not just shipments). Review at 90 days.

The bet is small (cheap dongle, no service lock-in), reversible (exit the hardware line, keep the software), and the downside is bounded by the low per-unit cost. The upside — a beachhead in the living room powered by the phone people already carry — is worth the risk.

DECISION: GO
