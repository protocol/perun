First call: GO — a free, web-based language-learning app built by an educator is cheap to start, immediately useful, and the revenue model's uncertainty is a risk, not a blocker.

Steelman: free lessons attract millions of learners; their translation practice produces a corpus sellable to publishers, funding the operation without charging students.

Do-nothing: the professor keeps teaching dozens of students per semester. The proposal reaches more people at low cost, so it beats do-nothing.

Cheaper alternative: launch without any revenue plan and treat it as a research project. This is strictly worse for sustainability — the founders already rejected nonprofit for that reason.

Evidence check: the proposal states learning will be free and revenue will come from selling learner translations. No figures, dates, or market sizes are given. The model is explicitly "unproven." This is a known unknown, not a hidden contradiction.

Pre-mortem: the product launches, learners come, but translations are too noisy to sell. Publishers decline. The professor covers server costs from his salary until he can't. The project folds.

Door class: two-way. Building a web app is reversible; shutting it down returns everyone to the status quo.

Kill criteria: within six months of launch, if fewer than 10,000 learners have joined AND no publisher has expressed interest in the translation output, stop investing development time and reassess. Leading metric: weekly active learners. Review date: six months post-launch.

The sharpest attack: learners produce low-quality translations, so publishers won't buy them, so the revenue model collapses, and free learning becomes unsustainable. This fails to kill the proposal because the founders explicitly defer revenue ("look for revenue later") and commit only to building the product and keeping learning free — the first step is executable and reversible regardless of whether the translation marketplace ever works.

The risks are real but none is a blocker: the product is cheap to build, immediately useful for free, and fully reversible. The unproven revenue model is the right kill criterion, not a reason to not start.

DECISION: GO
