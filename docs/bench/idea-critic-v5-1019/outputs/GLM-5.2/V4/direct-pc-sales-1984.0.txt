**First call: GO.** Direct-to-customer assembly at a lower price is the only one of these facts that is not an assumption. The risks listed are real but none of them is a blocker; each is a known cost of the chosen model and addressable operationally.

**Steelman:** A direct-order assembler can undercut branded retail on price because it carries no store overhead, no reseller margin, and no finished-goods inventory beyond what a confirmed order requires. The margin it saves is the margin it lives on.

**Blockers:** NONE.
- "Buyers want to try before buying" is a friction point, not a failure condition. Mail-order and direct sales already exist in adjacent markets; a return policy or local demonstration arrangement mitigates it.
- "Established makers have large channels and brands" is true and is exactly why a lower-price, direct-only model has room: the incumbents' channel advantage is also their cost structure.
- "No service network" is a solvable gap. Warranty service can be contracted regionally or handled by shipping replacement parts. The company controls the bill of materials, so it knows what fails.
- "Parts hard to get on credit" is a cash-flow constraint. It does not block incorporation or the direct model; it caps initial growth velocity and means early operations must be cash-or-short-cycle.

None of these contradict the core claim that direct assembly can sell below branded retail and fund growth from revenue.

**Risks (kill criteria):**
- If gross margin per unit after parts, assembly labor, shipping, and warranty reserve falls below the cost of acquiring a direct customer, the model does not self-fund. Set a minimum contribution margin per unit and review after the first 90 days of orders.
- If the weighted-average payment terms from parts suppliers exceed the cash conversion cycle the business can sustain from order deposits, growth starves the business of working capital. Track days payable against days inventory outstanding monthly.

**Decision:** GO. Incorporate, maintain direct-only sales, and grow from revenue. Cap the first phase at a defined order volume (e.g., first 100 units), establish the contribution-margin floor as the kill criterion, and review at 90 days. The proposal's own risks are manageable and do not rise to the level of blocking incorporation or the direct model.

DECISION: GO
