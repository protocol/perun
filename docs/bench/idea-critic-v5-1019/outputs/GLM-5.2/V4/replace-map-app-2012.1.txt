First call: NO-GO. Removing a working default app for an unproven replacement, at scale, on a flagship launch, is a one-way door with direct user harm.

**Blockers:**
1. The proposal's own risk admission — "a lot less data" — is a stated negative that removes the route to the upside. A maps app with materially less data than the incumbent is not a worse version of the same product; it is a different, broken product for affected users.
2. Removing the rival app by default is irreversible at launch scale. Once millions of phones ship without the working app, the trust damage cannot be unwound by a later fix. This is a one-way door treated as a routine update.
3. The stated upside (more control) is achievable without the stated harm: ship the new app alongside the incumbent, default-switch later when parity is proven. The proposal does not answer why removal must happen now.

**Risks (become kill criteria):** Place/business accuracy gaps are unverified at scale — set a measurable error-rate threshold per region and gate the default switch on it. User annoyance from removal is predictable — pilot in one market first. Voice assistant integration dependency on map data quality is untested.

**Strongest alternative:** Ship own maps as an option this fall; default-switch region-by-region only after the error rate and data coverage hit named thresholds. Same strategic destination, fraction of the blast radius, fully reversible until proven.

The proposal conflates "more control" with "remove the alternative." Control is gained by having your own app; harm is caused by removing the working one before yours is ready. These are separable, and the proposal chooses the version that maximizes user-facing risk for no additional strategic benefit.

DECISION: NO-GO
