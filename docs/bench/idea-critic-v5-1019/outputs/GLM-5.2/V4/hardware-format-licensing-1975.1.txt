**First call: NO-GO.** The format war is won by title availability and recording length, not picture quality alone; the proposal bets on the dimension where its lead is smallest and ignores the one where its deficit is largest.

**Steelman:** Superior picture quality plus an early lead creates a defensible position that partners and consumers will prefer on merit, making broad licensing unnecessary.

**Blockers:**
1. **One-hour tapes cannot record a full film or sports event.** This is a hard product limit stated as fact, not a risk. A VCR whose tape is too short for the primary use cases (movies, football) fails on utility regardless of picture quality. The proposal does not address it.
2. **Fewer manufacturers → fewer pre-recorded titles → fewer reasons to buy.** The rival is licensing broadly; the proposal deliberately restricts licensing. The stated strategy (keep proprietary, few partners) directly produces the stated risk (fewer titles). The plan engineers its own weakness.

**Risks (kill criteria if revised):**
- Rival achieves critical mass of installed players before proprietary format's quality advantage is perceived. Kill metric: rival reaches 3× our installed base within 18 months → open licensing or exit.
- One-hour limitation confirmed as dealbreaker by consumer testing. Kill metric: fewer than 40% of target households cite recording length as acceptable → redesign tape or abandon.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Broad licensing destroys margin and cedes control to partners who may defect." This fails because a proprietary format with no titles and short tapes has zero margin to protect; control over an empty ecosystem is worthless.

**Cheaper alternative:** Open licensing to many manufacturers sacrifices per-unit margin but maximises installed base, title availability, and the probability of becoming the standard — the actual prize.

**Dissent ledger:** The proposal correctly identifies its own risks but answers none of them; it names the rival's strategy (broad licensing) as a threat while pursuing the opposite.

DECISION: NO-GO
