**First call: NO-GO.** The company's own facts show it cannot make what it promises on the timeline it promises.

**Steelman.** Deposits prove real demand before committing capital; a cheaper car is the only path to scale; early movers capture the market.

**Blockers (from the proposal's own facts):**
1. **Volume vs experience.** The company has built only two models in small numbers yet proposes production *next year* at "far higher volumes." Nothing in the proposal states a contract, plant, or trained workforce that closes this gap in ~12 months. The timeline contradicts the experience.
2. **Battery supply uncommitted.** Supply "must be scaled up sharply," but the proposal names no secured supply agreement. A car program without a locked battery supply is not a plan; it is a hope.
3. **Margin math not shown.** A lower price "will leave little margin," yet the proposal provides no unit-economics breakdown showing the car is profitable at that price. Committing to a low-margin, high-volume product without the margin math is a bet the company cannot afford to lose.

**Risks (become kill criteria):** Refundable deposits may not convert; demand is unproven at scale; production ramp risk is severe.

**Alternatives considered.** (a) Do nothing — safe but cedes the market. (b) Unveil, take deposits, but commit to production only when deposits hit a threshold *and* battery supply is contracted — same upside, less ruin. (b) is cheaper and more reversible, and it does not sacrifice the demand signal.

**Decision: NO-GO.** The blocker is that the proposal's own stated facts — no mass-production experience, no secured battery supply, no margin math — contradict its core claim: production next year at far higher volumes. Approve a capped, staged version: unveil, take deposits, set a deposit threshold and a battery-supply contract as preconditions to production, and name a kill date if the threshold is not met.

DECISION: NO-GO
