**First call: GO.** A £400, licence-free vehicle that extends the home-computer channel into personal mobility is a sound asymmetric bet: the downside is a failed launch event, the upside is a new category.

**Steelman.** The entrepreneur has already proven he can move affordable electronics through retail and mail order. A licence-free, sub-£400 vehicle captures the same impulse-buy demographic; the appliance-maker partnership de-risks manufacturing; and optional extras create an upgrade path. The bet is that the home-computer playbook transfers to light transport.

**Skeptic — assumptions.**
1. Mail order for a tricycle assumes delivery and after-sales support work for a bulky, rideable product, not just a boxed computer. *Check:* what does the appliance maker's logistics network actually handle?
2. "Large numbers" by mail order assumes demand at least matches the computer launch. No pre-orders are cited. *Check:* can a deposit-taking trial ad run before committing to the event?
3. Top speed 15 mph, no weather protection, short range — these are stated facts, not risks. The proposal does not answer how a winter-bound, fair-weather-only vehicle sustains year-round sales.

**Better-way.** Do nothing costs nothing but forfeits a signed assembly deal and first-mover positioning. A cheaper alternative: a regional pilot through one appliance-maker retail counter before a national mail-order rollout — same upside, real demand signal, smaller first spend.

**Kill-criteria.**
- *Pre-launch:* fewer than 500 refundable deposits within 4 weeks of a trial ad → do not commit to the January event.
- *Post-launch:* return rate above 15% or fewer than 2,000 units sold by end of Q1 → stop mail-order expansion and reassess.

**Leading metric:** refundable deposits collected (review: 4 weeks after trial ad runs).

**Strongest attack survived.** The vehicle is too slow, too exposed and too seasonal to sell in large numbers year-round. It failed to break the claim because the proposal is a launch-and-learn bet with a capped first step: the January event and initial mail-order batch cost little relative to the signed assembly deal, and the kill criteria above stop the rollout before it scales. The stated limitations constrain the *market size*, not the *decision to test the market now*.

DECISION: GO
