First call: GO. A dominant phone maker entering a nascent wearable category, leveraging its installed base, ecosystem lock-in, and brand, is a sound asymmetric bet.

Steelman: the maker's existing phone user base is the largest addressable market for a companion watch; modest competitor sales prove demand exists but no incumbent has captured it; the ecosystem (app store, health platform) is a moat competitors lack.

Blockers: NONE. The proposal's own risks are real but not disqualifying. Earlier smartwatches selling poorly reflects execution, not category death. One-day battery life is a known constraint, not a plan-killer. The "second screen" objection is the sharpest — but it assumes the watch's value is screen duplication, when the stated value (health tracking, quick interactions, app ecosystem) is orthogonal to the phone's role.

Risks (kill criteria): weak launch demand (units sold below X in first 90 days → pivot to fitness positioning or stop); app developer indifference (fewer than Y native apps at 6 months → subsidize development or narrow scope); one-day battery eroding satisfaction (returns above Z% → prioritize power efficiency in v2). Leading metric: attach rate to the phone installed base, reviewed at 90 days.

Strongest attack survived: "buyers won't see a need for a second screen on the wrist." This fails because it mischaracterizes the product. The watch is not a second screen — it is a glanceable, always-on-wrist health and interaction device whose value the phone cannot provide (on-wrist sensors, immediate access without retrieval). The objection proves the category needs a different framing, not that the category is dead.

Dissent ledger: battery life may cap adoption below projections; the multi-material/multi-price strategy adds launch complexity that could delay shipment; the app store may face a cold-start problem.

DECISION: GO
