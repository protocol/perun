**First call: GO.** Low price + subscription + low-cost online video is a sound operator read of the 2012 facts: dominant brands overspend on ads and retail markup; mail-order removes both; a humorous video is a cheap test of whether the model lands.

**Steelman:** A low-cost, direct-to-consumer subscription can undercut bloated incumbents and learn from real orders before committing capital.

**Blockers: NONE.** The dominant brands' ability to cut prices or sue is a stated risk, not a fact already in hand that defeats the plan. Single-supplier dependence is a real operational risk but not a blocker at launch scale. Low traffic from one video is the expected outcome of a small test, not a failure of the model. Customer dislike of subscription is precisely what the launch tests.

**Risks:** Supplier concentration (mitigate by qualifying a second supplier before volume scales). Brand retaliation (likely slow given retail-channel conflict and ad-heavy cost structures). Single-video traffic risk (treat as a pilot, not a forecast).

**Kill criteria:** If the first video and site do not yield a minimum viable order count within a defined short window (e.g., 30 days), stop and reassess the channel. If early subscriber churn exceeds a set threshold, the subscription model itself is in question.

**Better ways considered:** Do nothing (reject—testable upside at low cost). Raise venture capital first (unnecessary before proving demand). Sell through retail (defeats the cost advantage).

**Strongest attack survived:** "The dominant brands will crush you." They could, but they face channel conflict with their own retail partners, their ad-heavy cost structure makes aggressive price cuts painful, and legal action against a small reseller is slow and uncertain. The plan's low-cost, fast-learning approach survives this attack.

**Remaining risk:** The model depends on sustained customer acquisition at low cost and on supplier reliability—both testable in the pilot phase.

DECISION: GO
