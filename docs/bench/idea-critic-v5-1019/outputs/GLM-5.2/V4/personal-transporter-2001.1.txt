First call: NO-GO. The proposal's own price figure breaks the core claim on the stated facts.

**Blockers:**
1. **Numbers fail the claim.** The stated purpose is replacing short car trips and walking. At ~$5,000 it costs more than a used car, far more than a bicycle or scooter. A device priced above the car it is meant to replace cannot, on the proposal's own figures, be the mass replacement for short urban trips. The core claim contradicts its own evidence.
2. **No target user shown.** The risks list conveys no demonstrated demand from any segment at that price; the proposal asserts a market without naming who already wants it at $5,000.

**Risks (kill criteria if pursued):**
- Regulatory: sidewalks and roads may ban it — a one-way door if cities legislate before adoption.
- Learning curve: balance-required steering means a failure-to-learn rate; set a threshold.
- Capacity: no cargo, no passenger — narrows use cases to solo, light-carry trips.

**Better way:** a capped, time-boxed pilot in one or two cities at a leased or lower price, measuring actual trip substitution and learning success, before a wide consumer launch at $5,000.

The proposal as written asks for a wide consumer launch at a price its own comparison says is too high, for a market it has not shown exists, against regulatory risk it acknowledges. The numbers do not work.

DECISION: NO-GO
