First call: NO-GO. The brand is the product in luxury; the mass-market name is the blocker, not a risk.

Blockers: the proposal's own stated risk — buyers pay for the badge — removes the route to the upside. A luxury sedan sold under a mass-market brand at premium-rival prices asks buyers to pay for a badge they do not want. No amount of advanced suspension or top-quality materials changes the badge on the hood, and the proposal names no answer to this.

Risks: the dedicated factory commits high fixed cost against low volume; worldwide launch front-loads marketing spend before any signal that buyers accept the name; the brand has no luxury image and no stated path to build one.

Kill criteria: pre-launch purchase-intent among premium-class buyers must clear a numeric threshold; if it does not, do not build the factory or launch worldwide. Failing intent, the cheaper alternative is a sub-brand or partnership that borrows luxury credibility rather than asking the mass-market name to carry it.

The strongest attack actually tried: luxury buyers reject mass-market names, so the sedan cannot command premium prices. This survived because the proposal itself raises it and offers no mechanism to overcome it — the badge is fixed.

DECISION: NO-GO
