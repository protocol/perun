**First call: NO-GO.** The economics are broken on the proposal's own numbers.

**Steelman:** Scale creates a data and distribution moat that studios and theaters must join, and the subsidy buys that position.

**Blockers:**
1. The numbers fail as proposed. One film/day at ~$12/ticket = ~$360/month in theater payments against $10/month revenue. The gap is 36:1. No stated data or deal revenue closes this.
2. The proposal's own evidence contradicts its core claim. It relies on heavy use driving scale leverage, but heavy users are precisely the cost center that makes the unit economics impossible.

**Risks (kill criteria):**
- Heavy-user cost overrun: cap subsidized screenings per month; kill if average cost per member exceeds 2x the fee by month 6.
- Revenue-stream failure: kill if data/deal revenue is below 50% of subsidy cost by month 12.
- Theater refusal: kill if >30% of major chains refuse participation by month 3.

**Better way:** A tiered model (limited films at $10, unlimited at a higher price) captures the data play without the 36:1 cost gap. Or: subsidize a *pilot cohort* of 1,000 users for 90 days, measure actual cost per member and theater willingness, then decide on scale.

**Strongest attack survived:** None. The attack that killed it is concrete: the proposal states the fee is $10/month and one film/day is allowed. At the stated full ticket price, the company loses ~$350/month per active user. The "grow rapidly" thesis amplifies the loss, not the leverage. The unproven revenue streams are not merely risks — they are the *only* path to covering a 36:1 cost ratio, and the proposal provides no evidence they will.

DECISION: NO-GO
