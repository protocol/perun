**First call: GO.** A flagship line with strong demand, shipping ahead of a known rival, with features users want (battery, water resistance) — that is a sound operator's bet.

**Steelman:** Moving launch to August captures demand before the rival's September event; volume shipment converts timing into market share.

**Alternatives:** *Do nothing* (ship on prior schedule, cede the pre-rival window) — rejected, the window is the point. *Ship on time without new features* — safer but loses differentiation. *Pilot in one market* — preserves the global volume play but tests less than the plan already implies.

**Evidence check:** The proposal's own risk list is the issue. Less testing time plus a thinner body holding a larger battery plus an untested-at-volume biometric sensor equals a defect probability that, at global scale, is a one-way door (recall costs, brand damage). The plan states these risks; it does not answer them.

**Blockers:** NONE that are fatal on the stated facts — the risks are real but they are *risks*, not contradictions.

**Kill criteria:**
- Internal drop-test and thermal-runaway thresholds must pass before volume shipment; failure = hold the line.
- Iris-sensor false-reject rate must be under a fixed ceiling from a pre-production batch of N units; failure = revert to fingerprint.
- A 2-week field test in one market before global rollout; defect rate above X% = pause global.

**Leading metric:** Early-return / defect-report rate in the first market, reviewed at day 14.

**Strongest attack survived:** "A thinner body with a larger battery is a thermal risk at global scale." This is the sharpest objection — and it survives because it is testable before shipment: thermal limits can be verified in pre-production, and if they fail, the body thickness or battery spec is revised, not the launch.

The risks are manageable with pre-shipment gates; the timing advantage is real and perishable.

DECISION: GO
