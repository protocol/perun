First call: GO. The model is unproven but the unit economics are sound, and the membership fee is the moat.

Steelman: a membership-funded, high-volume, limited-SKU warehouse can earn reliable recurring profit from fees while selling below competitors, making price the acquisition channel.

The strongest attack is that shoppers will not pay a fee to shop at an unproven brand, which collapses the entire revenue model. It fails because the fee is the mechanism that enables below-market pricing: the value proposition to the member is immediate and tangible, and the limited range reduces the cognitive cost of membership. The competitor's existence proves demand for the format; the entrant's edge is executing it more disciplinedly.

Blockers: NONE. The numbers work: membership fees cover fixed costs, bulk purchasing lowers COGS, limited SKU count reduces inventory complexity. No stated fact contradicts the core claim.

Risks (kill criteria):
- Membership sign-ups below break-even within the first 12 months → stop expansion, reassess pricing or fee structure.
- Single-location dependency: if the first warehouse underperforms, there is no portfolio to absorb it → pilot must demonstrate per-warehouse unit economics before committing to a second site.
- Competitor response on price or membership terms → monitor member retention and renewal rates quarterly.

Decision: go-if the first warehouse hits a membership and renewal threshold within 12 months; leading metric is paying members per warehouse, reviewed at month 6 and month 12.

DECISION: GO
