**First call: NO-GO.** The proposal's own numbers break it: free delivery of small orders costs more than the margin on them, and there is no minimum order. The upside (hourly delivery of everything) cannot cover a unit economics loss multiplied across every order and every city.

**Steelman:** Dense urban demand for convenience is real; direct employment gives quality control; speed of capture matters in a land-grab.

**Attack:** The strongest objection is that the proposal states its own failure as a plan — free delivery, no minimum, small orders — then asks to multiply that losing unit across cities and staff. The "risk" that customers use the service only when subsidized is not a risk; it is the model. Remove the subsidy and there is no proposal left.

**Alternatives:** Do nothing — the bankers keep their jobs. Cheaper path — minimum order threshold or delivery fee, making unit economics positive before expansion. The proposal chose the version that loses money on every transaction.

**Blocker:** The stated facts already show the target unit economics are negative and the plan's response is to scale them. No kill criterion rescues a model that requires permanent subsidy of every order with no revenue mechanism named.

DECISION: NO-GO
