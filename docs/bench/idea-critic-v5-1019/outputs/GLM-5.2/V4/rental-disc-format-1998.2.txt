**First call: NO-GO.** The proposal requires a new format coalition (studios + electronics makers) and special phone-line players priced above standard — yet the stated upside depends on customers accepting pay-per-view discs they cannot play on equipment they already own.

**Blockers:**

1. **Coalition failure removes the route to upside.** The format needs simultaneous studio content and electronics-maker hardware support. The proposal names this as a *risk the partners must agree*, but the economics are worse: each partner faces a chicken-and-egg incentive where defection is individually rational. No exclusive content or hardware subsidy is proposed to break the deadlock. The upside is unreachable as stated.

2. **The business model contradicts the customer behavior it relies on.** Discs cost "a few dollars" for 48 hours, then require a fee for continued viewing. Against standard rental (flat fee, several days) and standard purchase (own forever), this format charges more for the player *and* less value per dollar for the disc. The proposal's own risk list concedes customers may dislike repeated payments — that concession, combined with the higher player cost, removes the adoption the model requires.

**Risks (become kill criteria if pursued):**

- Standard DVD launches ~1999-2000 at lower player cost and with broader studio support; this format's window is narrow.
- Phone-line dependency creates infrastructure and privacy concerns.
- Studio economics: studios earn more from sell-through than from a rental-format they don't control.

**Strongest attack survived:** None — the coalition dependency and the customer-value contradiction are both blockers grounded in the proposal's own stated facts.

**Decision: NO-GO.** The plan requires a multi-party coalition with no proposed mechanism to align incentives, and its revenue model depends on customer acceptance of repeated payments for discs playable only on premium hardware — a contradiction the proposal itself acknowledges.

DECISION: NO-GO
