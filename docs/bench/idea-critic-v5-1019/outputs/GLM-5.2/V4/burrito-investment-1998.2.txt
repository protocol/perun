First call: GO. A minority stake with a later-invest right is a cheap option on a category the giant does not own, with the chain's own management carrying execution.

Steelman: the giant can learn the fast-casual burrito category — pricing, throughput, labor model — for the cost of a minority stake, and only commit real capital if the model proves. The optionality is the upside; the cap is the downside.

Blockers: NONE. The proposal's own structure answers the stated risks: minority stake caps the loss, the right-to-invest-more preserves the upside without forcing scale now, and independent operation means the giant's management attention and core operations are not diverted. "Tiny and may not scale" is a reason the option is cheap, not a reason to pass. "Slower and more skilled" describes a different operating model the giant can study rather than adopt. "Price above core menu" is the point — it is a different customer or occasion, not a cannibalization threat to the giant's own menu.

Risks: demand for large made-to-order burritos at a premium price is unproven at scale (but proven in 16 stores today); the chain's labor model may not translate to the giant's footprint if the option is exercised later; the giant has no prior experience operating this format. None of these are blockers because the structure — minority stake, independent operation, right but not obligation to invest more — means the giant can observe the answer before committing.

Kill criteria: if same-store sales growth across the 16 locations is not positive within 18 months, do not exercise the right to invest more. Leading metric: same-store sales growth, reviewed at 12 and 18 months.

The sharpest attack: a minority stake in a tiny chain diverts scarce management attention for an uncertain option that may never pay. This fails because the proposal explicitly keeps the chain independent — the giant is buying a learning option and a right of first refusal on a category, not a management burden. The downside is capped at the minority stake; the upside is the entire fast-casual category if it scales.

DECISION: GO
