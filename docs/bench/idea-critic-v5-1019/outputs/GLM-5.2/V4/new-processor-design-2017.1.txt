First call: GO. Smaller player, behind on performance, one failed prior design — the case for a bold new architecture at aggressive pricing is strong, and the risks cited are the standard risks of any competitive launch, not blockers.

Steelman: a smaller rival can only gain share by changing the basis of competition — more cores at lower prices does exactly that, and a new architecture is the only path to closing the performance gap after the prior design's failure.

Skeptic: the company's finances "leave no room for a second miss" — but this proposal *is* the second attempt, so the constraint cuts against caution, not against action. A smaller firm with one failed design and no new architecture is on a path to irrelevance; doing nothing is the higher-risk option.

Better-way: incremental improvement on the existing architecture would be cheaper and safer, but would not close the performance gap or change the competitive dynamic. The all-new architecture is the higher-upside path and is appropriate for a challenger.

Kill-criteria: price cuts by the rival are a known competitive risk, not a reason to defer. Software adoption of more cores is a market-timing question; the proposal should name a leading metric (core utilization in target workloads, or market share at the 6-month mark) and a review date.

The risks raised are real but manageable. The company's financial constraints actually argue for a decisive, differentiated bet rather than cautious iteration. The proposal as stated — new architecture, more cores, lower prices — is a sound competitive move for a challenger facing a dominant incumbent.

DECISION: GO
