**Steelman:** Undercut the rival on price, simplify development with PC-like hardware, and preserve consumer-friendly policies (used games, offline play) to regain market share lost from the previous late, expensive generation.

**Skeptic — assumptions tested:**

1. *Lower price reduces margin per unit.* Assumed safe because volume compensates. But if consumers are spending less on dedicated consoles (a stated risk), volume may not materialize, and the maker is left with thin margins on weak sales — the worst combination.

2. *PC-like hardware is easy for developers.* True, but it also lowers the barrier for the rival's developers to port titles, eroding any exclusivity advantage. The proposal does not address whether first-party software differentiation survives on commodity hardware.

3. *Little new software at launch.* This is the sharpest attack. A cheaper, friendlier console with no compelling software gives consumers no reason to buy it at launch. The rival's head start in online services compounds this: by the time the maker's studios deliver, the rival's ecosystem lock-in may be insurmountable. Price and policy concessions are wasted if the launch library is thin.

**Better-way:** The proposal already incorporates the strongest alternative (consumer-friendly policies, lower price). But it does not sequence: launching without software sacrifices the one advantage a later entrant has — learning from the rival's mistakes and arriving with a stronger library.

**Kill criteria:** If first-week sell-through falls below the rival's launch-week figure by more than 20%, or if third-party developers do not commit at least 50 titles within the first six months, the price-cut strategy has failed and margin must be recovered through bundling or subscription.

**Strongest attack survived:** The thin-launch-library objection is serious but not fatal: the maker's installed base from the previous generation, the lower price point, and the offline/used-game policies create a defensible day-one value proposition even with a modest library, provided first-party titles ship within the first year.

**Remaining risk:** The rival's online-services lead is structural and cannot be closed by hardware choices alone; this proposal does not address it.

DECISION: NO-GO
