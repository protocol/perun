The steelman: a low-cost, humorous launch tests real demand for mail-order razors before committing capital, and the subscription model builds recurring revenue that dominant brands' retail focus can't easily match.

**Strongest attack survived:** The dominant brands could cut prices below the startup's cost, or sue on trademark/trade dress, crushing a thin-margin reseller with no legal budget. Why it failed: the dominant brands' entire model is built on premium pricing and heavy ad spend to justify it — cutting prices would cannibalize their retail margins across all channels, and they'd have to do so nationally, not just against one online upstart. The brand damage from racing to the bottom against a nobody is itself a deterrent. Legal action is slower and more visible than a price war, giving the startup time to learn. The one-supplier risk is real but addressable: a second supplier can be lined up once volume justifies it, and the first supplier has incentive to keep a new retail channel. A single video bringing low traffic is not fatal — it's a cheap test, and if it works, more content follows. Subscription reluctance is the deepest risk: if customers churn after one box, the model collapses. But the low price and the hassle of remembering to buy razors both push toward retention, and the launch itself will measure churn within weeks.

**Kill criterion:** If the first video and site do not reach a cost-per-acquisition that leaves positive contribution margin on a 3-month subscription within 60 days, stop and reassess — the recurring-revenue thesis is wrong.

**Decision:** Go. The proposal is a cheap, fast, two-way-door test of a real hypothesis with a clear stop condition. The risks named are the known unknowns a pilot exists to measure; waiting to resolve them in theory before testing in practice is more expensive than running the experiment.

DECISION: GO
