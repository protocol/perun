**Steelman:** A compact, self-balancing electric vehicle could replace short urban car trips and walking, capturing first-mover advantage in a new personal-mobility category.

**Skeptic:**
- $5,000 is 5–10× a bicycle or scooter and rivals a used car; the target market (short trips) has far cheaper options. No unit-economics or demand evidence is given.
- Sidewalk and road rules are untested; a single city ban could collapse the launch.
- Learning curve + no cargo/passenger capacity narrows the usable base further.

**Better-way:**
- *Do nothing:* the transporter is a solution hunting for a problem at this price.
- *Pilot first:* launch in one city, limited units, measure adoption, learning time, and regulatory response before a wide rollout.
- *Lower price / lease:* broaden access without a full consumer launch.

**Kill-criteria:**
- If after 90 days in one city, fewer than X% of buyers use it weekly for short trips, or any city bans sidewalk use, pivot or stop.
- One-way door? Partially: capital and brand exposure are at risk, but a pilot keeps this reversible.

**Strongest attack survived:** "At $5,000, nobody will buy it." This could be true, but the proposal's viability hinges on untested demand at that price — which is exactly why a wide consumer launch now is premature. The attack doesn't kill the *concept*; it kills the *go-wide-now* plan.

**Dissent ledger:** Price-vs-alternatives gap is large and unaddressed; regulatory path is assumed, not secured; no demand evidence at the stated price.

**Remaining risk:** Even a pilot may reveal that the price floor is too high for the target use case, making the product non-viable regardless of execution.

DECISION: NO-GO
