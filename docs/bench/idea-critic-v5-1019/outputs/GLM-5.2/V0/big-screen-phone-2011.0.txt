**Steelman:** A 5+ inch stylus device creates a new premium category between phone and tablet, capturing users who want more screen real estate without carrying two devices.

**Strongest attack survived:** "It's too big for a phone and too small for a tablet — a product in no market." This fails because the category gap is exactly the opportunity: the maker already sells phones and would lose nothing testing a new tier alongside its existing line, and the stylus differentiates against all competitors.

**Assumptions:** Premium pricing will hold despite reviewer skepticism (assumed; the check is whether early reviewers focus on size as flaw or feature). Cannibalization of existing phones is net-negative (assumed; the check is whether large-phone buyers would have bought a competitor's phone instead).

**Better ways considered:** Do nothing — but the maker cedes the gap to competitors. Build a small tablet instead — but that competes in a more crowded space without phone-call capability as a differentiator. The large-phone-with-stylus path is the cheapest way to test an unoccupied category.

**Kill criteria:** Review the device's first full quarter. If sell-through falls below the maker's average smartphone for that period, pivot marketing toward the stylus as the primary differentiator rather than screen size. If the second quarter still underperforms, stop the line.

**Remaining risk:** Reviewer consensus forms early and may anchor buyer perception before the category is understood. This is real but not fatal: the maker can price to clear inventory if the category fails to materialize, and the existing line is unaffected.

The proposal is reversible (the device can be discontinued without disrupting the existing line), occupies an unclaimed category, and has a clear stop condition. The risks raised are the known costs of category creation, not reasons to defer.

DECISION: GO
