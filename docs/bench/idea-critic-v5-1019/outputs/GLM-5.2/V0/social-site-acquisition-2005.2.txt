**Steelman:** A fast-growing social network with a large, hard-to-reach young audience is a rare strategic asset; owning it lets the media company monetize that demographic through its existing advertising infrastructure, creating a durable competitive moat.

**Skeptic — assumptions tested:**
- *“Combine audience with advertising” = revenue.* Unproven. The proposal itself admits turning rapid audience growth into advertising revenue is unproven. Two years of growth is not a moat; it is a trajectory that can flatten or reverse.
- *The young audience is durable.* Social tastes change quickly — also admitted in the proposal. A 580-million-dollar price assumes the audience persists long enough to amortize the cost.
- *The price reflects strategic value.* 580 million dollars for a two-year-old site with unproven monetization is speculative. The downside is a near-total write-off; the upside depends on assumptions the proposal flags as risks without quantifying them.

**Better-way:** A strategic partnership or exclusive advertising agreement captures most of the monetization learning at a fraction of the capital and with a clean exit. It tests the core claim — can advertising revenue scale against this audience? — without committing 580 million dollars to acquire it.

**Kill-criteria:** The proposal names risks but sets no tripwires. A sound bet here would specify: (1) a capped first step (e.g., a 50-million-dollar advertising partnership for 12 months), (2) a leading metric (advertising revenue per active young user, reviewed at month 6), and (3) a kill threshold (if revenue per user is below X at month 6, walk away rather than acquire). Without these, the 580-million-dollar acquisition is an uncapped bet on unproven monetization.

**Strongest attack survived:** “The audience could vanish” — true, but the deeper failure is paying 580 million dollars before proving the one assumption the entire return rests on: that social-network audience growth converts to advertising revenue. That proof does not require acquisition.

**Remaining risk:** A competitor acquires the site during the partnership test. This is a real option cost, but it does not justify an uncapped acquisition of an unproven model.

DECISION: NO-GO
