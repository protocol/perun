**Steelman:** Free one-hour delivery of small orders could lock in urban customers at scale, making the logistics investment worthwhile.

**Skeptic:** The proposal's core assumption—that volume will offset per-delivery losses—is untested. Delivering a $3 coffee within an hour costs more in labor and vehicles than the margin allows. "Free delivery" with "no minimum order" guarantees losses on every small transaction. No evidence suggests customers will pay premium prices once subsidies end.

**Better-way:** Start with one city, one product category, and a minimum order threshold. Test whether customers value speed enough to pay for it. The current proposal bets everything on an unproven model.

**Kill-criteria:** If average order margin cannot cover delivery cost within 6 months, raise the minimum order or shut down.

**Strongest attack survived:** The objection that free delivery of small orders is inherently unprofitable is sharp but not fatal—it is a testable hypothesis, not a proven impossibility. However, the proposal as written does not include any test, threshold, or single-city pilot. It commits to direct employment, rapid multi-city expansion, and free delivery with no minimum, all simultaneously, all untested.

**Remaining risk:** The combination of no minimum order, free delivery, direct employment, and fast expansion creates compounding losses with no stated reversal trigger.

The proposal asks for full commitment to an unproven, loss-generating model across multiple cities simultaneously, with no pilot, no kill criteria, and no evidence that customers will behave as needed once subsidies end.

DECISION: NO-GO
