**Decision method**

1. **Steelman.** Build a free language-learning web app now, defer revenue, and hope the translation work-product becomes a business. The strongest version: free learning attracts a large user base; the translation activity is both pedagogically useful and commercially valuable; the gap between "free" and "polished paid" is bridgeable over time.
2. **Options.** (a) Do nothing — costs nothing, misses a potential market. (b) Build a smaller pilot — test translation quality and buyer interest before full build. (c) Build the full product now, defer revenue. Option (c) is the proposal.
3. **Fit.** The stated goal is free language education for all. Building the product fits. The revenue model is speculative but the proposal does not depend on it.
4. **Pre-mortem.** Assume it failed badly. Why? The translation work is too poor to sell, and the founders run out of money before finding any revenue. The one-way door is the founders' time and any capital invested. The cost of delay is low: the market is not going away in a quarter.
5. **Evidence.** The proposal cites no evidence that learner translations are sellable, no evidence that publishers will buy, and no evidence that the user base will grow. The risks raised are all valid and all unresolved.
6. **Close.** The proposal asks for a full build with no revenue validation and no kill criteria. The translation-quality risk and the publisher-buyer risk are both untested. A smaller pilot would test both cheaply.

**Strongest attack survived:** The translation model is unproven and learner output may be unsellable — but the proposal explicitly defers revenue and does not commit capital beyond the build, so the failure mode is "wasted time," not "irreversible loss."

**Remaining risk:** No kill criteria. If the founders invest years without validating translation quality or buyer interest, they will have no tripwire to stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
