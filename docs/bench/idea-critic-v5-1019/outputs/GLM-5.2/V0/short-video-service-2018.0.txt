**Claim:** Spend ~$1.75B on a phone-only short-form premium streaming service, launching spring 2020 in two countries.

**Steelman:** Hollywood credibility plus tech execution could create a new category — "premium short-form" — where incumbents (YouTube, social platforms) are weak: high production value, curated, ad-supported.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Free short video already wins." This fails if the proposal's bet is *premium* short-form (named producers, high budgets) competing on quality, not on free-volume platforms. But the proposal never states why quality short-form commands a subscription when free short-form already satisfies the use case.

**Assumptions (unverified):**
- Viewers will pay monthly for <10-minute phone-only episodes.
- Named producers' output will justify budgets requiring massive recoupment.
- Spring 2020 launch is achievable at the stated spend.

**Better ways:**
- Smaller pilot (one country, fewer shows) before committing $1.75B.
- Ship TV support at launch; phone-only narrows the addressable base for a premium product.

**Kill criteria — missing:**
- No named breakeven subscriber threshold.
- No churn ceiling that triggers stop.
- No cap on spend beyond the initial raise.

**Fit:** The core mechanism — *why premium short-form beats free short-form for paying subscribers* — is not named. The risks raised are the right risks; none is resolved in the proposal. Spending $1.75B before a pilot exists is a one-way door with no pre-committed tripwires.

DECISION: NO-GO
