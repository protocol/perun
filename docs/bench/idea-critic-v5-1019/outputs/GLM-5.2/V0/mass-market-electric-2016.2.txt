**Steelman:** A cheaper sedan with refundable deposits de-risks demand and funds capex before the factory is built; first-mover advantage in a segment incumbents have ignored.

**Strongest attack survived:** The company has never built at volume and plans to begin production the next year at far higher volumes — a gap so wide that refundable deposits could convert to orders before the factory is ready, forcing either delivery delays that trigger mass refunds, or rushed production that compromises quality. This is a one-way door: the capital spent on retooling and battery contracts cannot be recovered if demand collapses or production stalls. The proposal does not name a kill criterion, a fallback, or a cap on deposit exposure.

**Why this still fails to pass:** The battery supply must scale sharply, yet no supplier commitments or capacity evidence is cited. The lower price leaves little margin, so even successful production yields thin cash to fund the next stage. Refundable deposits are not sales; treating them as committed revenue is the classic pre-order failure mode. No alternative was considered — not a higher-volume but slightly more expensive variant, not a partnership with an experienced manufacturer, not a slower ramp — and "do nothing" (continue low-volume luxury sales) is cheaper and reversible. The proposal asks for approval of a high-blast, low-reversibility bet with no pre-committed kill criteria, no named leading metric, and no cap on the first step.

**Remaining risk:** Even with criteria added, the production-volume gap is the binding constraint and may not be closable in the timeframe stated.

DECISION: NO-GO
