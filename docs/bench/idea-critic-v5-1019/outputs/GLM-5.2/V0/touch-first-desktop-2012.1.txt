# Executive Review

## Steelman
Tablets are eating PC share. Shipping one touch-first OS across tablets and PCs creates a unified platform, app ecosystem, and developer story—exactly the strategic move that could protect the dominant position.

## Skeptic
The proposal conflates *strategic direction* with *execution risk*. The steelman is sound; the mechanism is not.

- **Unsupported claim:** "Full-screen start screen on *all* PCs, including keyboard/mouse." No evidence is given that the touch-first interface is usable or desirable with a mouse. The proposal asserts the conclusion it needs to prove.
- **Inverted incentive:** The dominant OS maker's advantage is its installed base of corporate and desktop users. This proposal taxes the revenue base to subsidize the strategic bet. The people who pay the bills are the ones harmed.
- **No-rollback is a design choice, not a constraint.** "There is no easy way to go back" is stated as fact. It is a decision the team is making—and it converts a reversible bet into an irreversible one.

## Better Way
**Decouple the two bets.** The tablet app store and touch-first interface are the strategic move; forcing them onto 1.5 billion existing keyboard/mouse users is the execution risk. Ship the new interface on tablets and new touch hardware; let corporate and desktop users opt in. This preserves the ecosystem play without alienating the revenue base. Cheaper, reversible, and it still tests the thesis.

## Kill Criteria
- If corporate adoption stalls or support costs exceed projections within 12 months, restore the traditional start menu as the default on non-touch hardware.
- Leading metric: enterprise upgrade rate vs. prior OS cycle, reviewed at 6 months.

## Strongest Attack Survived
"Shipping one OS everywhere is the only way to get developers to write for the new interface." This is plausible but unproven: a larger touch-hardware install base, even without forcing desktop users off the start menu, could achieve the same developer incentive at lower risk. The proposal does not show why *forced* migration is necessary rather than *available* migration.

---

The strategic thesis is defensible. The execution—forcing a touch interface onto all keyboard/mouse PCs with no rollback—is not. The proposal asks the executive team to bet the revenue base on an untested usability assumption, and it removes the escape hatch. That is a one-way door dressed as a product decision.

DECISION: NO-GO
