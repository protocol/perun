**Steelman:** Membership fees let you sell near cost, undercutting the larger competitor on shelf price while locking in recurring revenue.

**Skeptic:** Every risk raised is load-bearing and none is resolved.
- "Shoppers may not pay a fee to shop" — this is the entire thesis. No evidence is offered that enough will.
- "An earlier competitor has more locations and cash" — first-mover advantage in scale and capital is conceded.
- "Thin margins leave no room for errors" — the membership fee must cover fixed costs from day one; a slow ramp is fatal.
- "Large warehouses need big sites and customers who will buy in bulk" — site availability and bulk-buying behavior are unverified assumptions.

**Better-way:** Do nothing. The competitor already operates this format with more locations and more cash. Entering a format race against a better-funded incumbent, on thin margins, with an unproven revenue lever, is the most expensive path to the same shelf.

**Kill criteria:** None provided. The proposal does not name a membership-count floor, a timeline to break-even, or a stop condition. Without these, there is no way to distinguish "early" from "failing."

**Strongest attack survived:** The attack that membership fees are unproven is not survived — it is conceded by listing it as a risk without evidence or a test. The competitor's scale advantage is also conceded.

**Remaining risk:** Capital is committed to warehouses, inventory, and sites before the membership fee is proven. Losses compound while the thesis is tested at full scale rather than in a pilot.

**Questions the parent must resolve:**
1. What is the minimum membership count per warehouse to cover fixed costs, and by what date?
2. What is the stop condition — cumulative loss threshold or membership shortfall by month?
3. Why will shoppers pay the fee here when the competitor already offers the format?

DECISION: NO-GO
