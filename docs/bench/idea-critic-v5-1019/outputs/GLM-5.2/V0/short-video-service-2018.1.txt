**Strongest claim:** Netflix-quality production, mobile-first delivery, and dual revenue (subscription + ads) justify a $1.75B raise to launch a short-form premium streaming service in spring 2020.

**Skeptic — assumptions presented as fact:**

1. *"Viewers will pay for premium short-form on phones."* No evidence. The closest precedent — YouTube Premium and short-form web series — suggests audiences expect short content free or ad-supported. The ad-supported tier cannibalizes the paid tier's core proposition.
2. *"Well-known producers guarantee audience."* Name talent in film does not equal mobile-content audience pull. Quibi later proved this exact assumption false.
3. *"$1.75B is sufficient."* High-budget commissions, technology build, marketing in two countries, and a spring 2020 launch leave a razor-thin margin. One production delay or one weak launch title burns the runway.
4. *"Spring 2020 launch is achievable."* Commissioning, producing, and finishing high-budget short episodes — plus building the tech stack — in under 18 months is aggressive and unverified.

**Better-way:** Launch smaller: one country, fewer titles, a lower raise, and a longer runway to test whether the target audience converts from free social video to paid premium short-form. The proposal's all-in bet does not beat a phased pilot.

**Kill criteria:** If paid-subscriber conversion is below a set threshold within 90 days of launch, stop commissioning new titles and pivot the model. The proposal names no such threshold.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Free short video is already popular" — the proposal's counter is premium production quality. That survives *only if* the audience values production quality over the free alternative's breadth and social features, which is unverified.

**Remaining risk:** The entire model rests on an unproven willingness-to-pay assumption, backed by a budget that leaves no room for the assumption being wrong.

DECISION: NO-GO
