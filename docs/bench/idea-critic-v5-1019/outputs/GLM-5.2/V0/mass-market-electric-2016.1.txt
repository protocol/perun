**Steelman:** A cheaper sedan at scale could unlock the mass market, with refundable deposits funding the ramp.

**Skeptical attacks:**
1. **Volume jump is unvalidated.** Two hand-built cars → mass production next year is not a plan; it is a hope. No evidence the supply chain, body shop, paint, or final assembly can scale 100× in twelve months.
2. **Battery supply is a hard constraint.** "Must be scaled up sharply" with no named supplier commitment or gigafactory contract means the single most critical component is assumed, not secured.
3. **Refundable deposits ≠ revenue.** They are a liability. If production slips (likely, given zero mass-production experience), deposits convert to cancellations, not sales.
4. **Margin math is missing.** A lower price leaves "little margin" — but no unit-cost target, no break-even volume, no capital plan is given. The proposal asserts scale without costing it.

**Better way:** Do not commit to a public unveil with deposits until (a) a battery supply contract is signed, (b) a production engineer confirms the line can hit target volume, and (c) a unit-economics model shows positive contribution margin at the lower price. Until those three checks pass, the cheaper sedan is a concept, not a commitment.

**Pre-mortem:** Production starts late because the battery cells never arrive in volume. Deposits are refunded. The company has spent capital on a factory line that cannot run. Reputation is damaged.

**Kill criteria:** If battery supply is not contracted within six months, or if deposits exceed a number the company can refund from cash on hand, pause new deposits immediately.

**Remaining risk:** Even with contracts and engineers, the company has never done this. The gap between "two cars" and "mass production" is the entire risk, and the proposal treats it as a scheduling detail rather than an existential capability question.

DECISION: NO-GO
