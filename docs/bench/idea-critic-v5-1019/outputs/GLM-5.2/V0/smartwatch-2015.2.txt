**Steelman:** A watch deepens lock-in to the phone ecosystem and opens a health-data beachhead competitors lack.

**Skeptic:** "Buyers may not see a need" is asserted, not tested. "Battery about one day" is a known constraint, not a blocker. The real unsupported claim: that health tracking plus an app store clears the bar the earlier watches missed.

**Better-way:** Do nothing costs ~zero and risks only a rival owning the wrist first. A cheaper path: ship one model in one material to learn demand before committing to a multi-tier line. The proposal's several-materials-and-prices scope front-loads cost before the need is proven.

**Kill-criteria:** Define a stop or pivot before launch. Example: if pre-orders for the flagship tier fall below a set number by a fixed date, cut to one model and one price. Name the metric, the threshold, and the date.

**Pre-mortem:** Assume it failed. Most likely cause: the watch is a second screen with no standalone reason to exist, and one-day battery makes it a nightly chore. The proposal names this risk but gives no answer to it.

**Fit:** Lock-in and health data fit a phone-and-computer maker's strategy. But fit is not demand. The modest sales of existing smartwatches are evidence the category is unproven, not proof it will fail.

**Remaining risk:** Multi-tier launch commits tooling and inventory before the core question—do people want this on their wrist—is answered.

The proposal bets on a category with modest existing sales, front-loads scope across materials and price levels, and leaves the sharpest risk—no standalone need—unanswered. A single-model pilot would test demand cheaply; as written, the scope and the unanswered need exceed the evidence.

DECISION: NO-GO
