**Steelman:** Free language lessons at internet scale create both a massive learner base and a supply of human translations; the translation marketplace could fund the mission where a nonprofit cannot.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Learner translations will be poor and unsellable." This is real but not fatal: even partial translations have value (corpora, training data, draft scaffolding), and quality can rise as learners progress or through review layers. The model can degrade gracefully to "free learning, no revenue" — the mission survives even if the business does not.

**Skeptic (assumptions):**
- Translation revenue is assumed, not validated. No publisher is named as committed.
- "Nonprofit unsustainable" is asserted without showing what was tried.
- Free-to-paid conversion is unproven, but the proposal already defers this — not a blocker.

**Better-way:**
- *Validate before building:* approach 2–3 publishers with sample learner translations; if none bite, the core assumption is dead before code is written.
- *Nonprofit + grants* is the rejected alternative; the proposal should say why grants were ruled out, since that path needs no revenue model at all.

**Kill criteria:**
- **Tripwire:** if no publisher expresses concrete interest within 3 months of a working pilot, stop pursuing translation revenue and treat the project as a free-learning nonprofit.
- **Leading metric:** number of learners completing ≥5 lessons per week. If this stays flat after launch, the translation-supply thesis weakens regardless of publisher interest.

**Fit:** Stage is pre-revenue, 2009, two-person team. North star: free language education for everyone. The proposal serves the north star directly (free learning) and treats revenue as speculative — appropriate for the stage. The risk is not building; it is over-investing before the revenue assumption is tested.

**Remaining risk:** Building a full product before any market signal (publisher or learner) is the classic premature-build trap. A thin slice — minimal lessons, a few real translation tasks, one publisher conversation — would settle more than any plan.

DECISION: NO-GO
