The strongest case for this plan is that mail-order razors are a proven model: the proposal notes dominant brands already sell them, so the only question is whether a low-cost challenger can capture share. The dominant brands' advertising spend is a barrier to entry that protects incumbents, not a reason a challenger should also spend heavily. The cheapest test is to launch with a single humorous video and a simple site, take orders immediately, and learn whether subscription demand exists before committing capital.

The sharpest objection is that a single low-budget video may generate too little traffic to test the hypothesis, and that dependence on one supplier leaves the company exposed if that supplier cuts them off. But the proposal already names these risks, and neither is fatal to a reversible first step. A video that flops costs little; a supplier relationship can be diversified once volume justifies it. The risk that incumbents cut prices or sue is a risk of success, not of the test itself.

The plan beats the alternatives: doing nothing teaches nothing; spending heavily on advertising before validating demand is the costlier, less reversible path; and a more complex launch delays learning. The blast radius is small: minimal capital, no long-term contracts, no irreversible commitments. The kill criterion is straightforward—if the video and site cannot generate enough orders in the first weeks to cover the marginal cost of fulfillment, stop and reconsider. The leading metric to watch is cost of customer acquisition against subscription lifetime value, reviewed within the first month.

No concrete failure scenario survives the stated reversibility, the named risks, and the kill criterion. The plan is a cheap, reversible test of a proven model with a clear stop condition.

DECISION: GO
