**Strongest defensible version:** The dongle is not a box competitor; it is a cheap, phone-as-remote bridge that turns any HDMI TV into a second screen, using an open kit to close the app gap over time.

**Strongest attack tried and why it failed:** "No remote and no on-screen menu will confuse buyers, so it will fail like the earlier device." That attack fails because the failure mode of the earlier, poorly-selling device is not specified as "no remote"; it is asserted, not evidenced. The proposal's design choice — phone as controller, dongle as dumb pipe — is a coherent, lower-support-cost model, not an oversight.

**Skeptic — assumptions:**
- The claim "earlier attempts sold poorly" is used to argue *against* a different form factor. That is a weak inference unless the prior failure cause overlaps. *Next action:* name the prior device's actual failure cause; if it was price or app catalog, the new device addresses both.
- "Few apps at launch" is fatal only if apps cannot arrive post-launch. The open kit is the mitigation; it is reasonable for a 2013 Android-based device.

**Better-way — alternatives:**
- *Do nothing:* leaves the TV surface to competitors who already own the living room elsewhere. Cost of delay: every quarter cedes installed base and developer mindshare to Roku/Apple.
- *Ship a full remote box:* higher margin per unit, but higher BOM, more support, and enters directly against entrenched incumbents with larger catalogs. Worse risk-adjusted bet.
- *Dongle with a cheap remote:* splits the difference but adds SKU complexity and support cost at launch.

**Kill criteria:** Set a 90-day post-launch review. Kill or pivot if fewer than X active users per dongle or fewer than Y third-party apps shipped. (X and Y to be set by the owner; absence of exact numbers here is the main unresolved question.)

**Remaining risk:** Margin is near-zero; this is a land-grab play, not a per-unit profit play. That is a defensible 2013 bet if the company monetizes the platform, not the hardware.

DECISION: GO
