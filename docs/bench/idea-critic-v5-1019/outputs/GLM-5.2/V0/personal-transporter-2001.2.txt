**Steelman:** A clean, compact EV that replaces short urban car trips could cut congestion, emissions, and parking pressure at scale.

**Skeptic:** The "replace short car trips" claim is unsupported. Most short urban trips carry cargo, children, or a passenger; a solo, cargo-less device addresses a narrow slice. Price ($5,000) is above a used car and multiples of a bicycle — the payback versus walking/transit is unproven. Sidewalk and road legality is unresolved in every target city. Learning-to-balance friction means the early buyer experience includes failure risk. Each of these is a *concrete failure scenario*: a buyer spends $5,000, cannot legally ride to work, cannot carry a bag, and the trip-replacement rate stays low.

**Better-way:** (1) Pilot in one tourist or campus setting where speed, range, and legality are controlled and learning time is acceptable; measure trips-replaced and incident rate before a city-wide launch. (2) Sell to postal/police fleets first — defined routes, professional riders, cargo handled differently — to prove durability and unit economics. (3) Do nothing until a city grants legal sidewalk/road access; without that, the consumer launch has no usable market.

**Kill-criteria:** Stop the consumer launch and revert to fleet pilot if, in the first pilot window, either (a) trips-actually-replacing-a-car-trip are below 20% of rides, or (b) one serious injury attributable to learning-time balance failure occurs. Review at 90 days.

**Fit:** The north star is presumably urban-mobility transformation. A $5,000 solo device with no legal access and no cargo capacity does not, as proposed, move that star — it ships a consumer product into unresolved regulatory and practical headwinds.

**Strongest attack survived:** "No city has granted sidewalk or road access, so the consumer launch has no usable market." This did not sink the *idea* — it sank the *go-to-consumers-everywhere-now* framing; a single-city pilot resolves it.

**Dissent ledger:** Price-to-value unproven; regulatory access absent; cargo/passenger gap unaddressed; learning-curve safety unmeasured.

**Remaining risk:** Even a successful campus pilot may not generalize to dense-city commuting.

DECISION: NO-GO
