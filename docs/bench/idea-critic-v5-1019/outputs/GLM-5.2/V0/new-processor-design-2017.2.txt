**Steelman:** A smaller, behind processor maker can leapfrog a dominant rival by shipping a radically higher-core-count architecture at aggressive prices, betting that the parallel-performance shift rewards the disruptor and that the rival's margins and roadmap constrain its response.

**Skeptic.** The premise that software will broadly use many cores is the load-bearing assumption; in 2017, most desktop workloads remained lightly threaded. The claim that the rival "could cut prices or launch faster chips" understates the asymmetry: a dominant rival with higher margins and a deeper roadmap can absorb a price war longer than a financially fragile challenger can survive one. "No room for a second miss" means the downside is not a weak product but insolvency.

**Better way.** The strongest alternative is an incremental, higher-margin refresh on a proven architecture: lower R&D outlay, lower reversal cost, preserves cash, and does not provoke a price war from a stronger rival. The proposal must beat this, not only the status quo.

**Kill criteria.** The proposal lacks a named, observable stop condition. What single metric, measured at what date, would trigger a pivot or stop? Without it, a miss becomes a sunk-cost spiral—the exact failure mode the finances cannot absorb.

**Pre-mortem.** Assume it failed badly. Most likely cause: the new architecture ships with teething issues or modest single-thread gains, software adoption lags, the rival cuts prices to defend share, and the challenger burns remaining cash on a price war it cannot win.

**Door / delay.** This is a one-way door: large, committed spend on an all-new architecture with no room for a second miss. The cost of delay is one product cycle, which is real but survivable; the cost of a wrong-direction commitment is not.

**Strongest attack survived.** "The rival undercuts and the challenger runs out of cash" is the sharpest objection. It fails to kill the proposal only if the challenger's pricing is set to survive a sustained price war and the architecture delivers a credible single-thread improvement alongside core count—neither condition is established in the proposal as given.

DECISION: NO-GO
