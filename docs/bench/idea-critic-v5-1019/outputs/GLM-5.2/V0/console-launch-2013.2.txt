**Steelman:** A cheap, developer-friendly, consumer-rights-respecting console can reverse market share loss by removing the friction that caused the previous generation to fail.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Standard PC hardware erodes the console's differentiation; the rival's head start in online services locks in network effects the maker can't overcome." This fails because the proposal's bet is that *hardware accessibility and consumer-friendly policy* drive adoption more than premium online ecosystems—Xbox 360's early lead didn't prevent PS3 from catching up once pricing corrected.

**Skeptic — unresolved:**
- *Launch software gap.* No first-party title is named. A console launching without exclusive software relies entirely on multiplatform titles also available on the rival's installed base. **Next action:** confirm at least one exclusive launch title exists or is within 6 months of launch.
- *Margin sustainability.* Below-rival pricing with standard hardware compresses per-unit margin. The proposal doesn't state whether the business model absorbs this via volume or software attach. **Next action:** state the break-even attach rate and the volume assumption.

**Better-way:** Status quo (match rival's price, differentiate on services) is rejected by the failure history. No stronger alternative is visible given the constraints.

**Kill criteria (if approved):** If unit sales in the first two holiday seasons fail to exceed the previous generation's comparable window by at least 20%, pivot pricing or bundle strategy.

**Remaining risk:** The online-services gap is real and unaddressed by this proposal; it may cap lifetime attach even if hardware sells.

**Dissent ledger:** Launch software gap and margin model are unverified but do not contradict the stated strategy; they are conditions to confirm, not reasons to block a reversible, well-positioned bet.

DECISION: GO
