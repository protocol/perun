**Steelman:** Urban consumers will pay a premium for instant gratification across multiple categories, and scale economics will eventually cover per-delivery losses.

**Strongest attack survived:** The unit economics are fatally broken. A $3 snack with free 30-minute delivery costs more to fulfill than the entire revenue. This isn't a "scale fixes it" problem — the marginal cost of a dedicated courier delivering a low-value item exceeds any plausible margin at any volume.

**Why the attack fails to kill it:** It doesn't fail. The proposal concedes the core risk ("customers may use the service only when it is subsidized") yet offers no mechanism to transition from subsidized to profitable. Kozmo.com raised $280M, hit 11 cities, employed thousands of couriers directly, and collapsed in 2001. The model was empirically tested and failed precisely on the predicted dimensions.

**Assumptions (unverified):**
- Delivery cost per order < margin per order (no evidence; arithmetic suggests the opposite)
- Customer behavior changes when subsidies end (behavioral assumption, untested)
- Direct employment of couriers is necessary vs. gig/contract models (1998 context: gig economy didn't exist, but the employment model multiplies fixed costs)

**Better ways:**
- **Do nothing:** Avoid burning capital on a model with negative contribution margin per transaction
- **Charge for delivery:** Tests true willingness to pay; likely reveals demand is subsidy-dependent
- **Minimum order threshold:** Improves basket economics; still risky but less catastrophic
- **Single-category focus (coffee only):** Higher frequency, simpler logistics, faster path to unit economics validation

**Kill criteria:** Contribution margin per delivery must reach positive within 18 months, measured at the order level (not blended with subscription revenue). If not, stop expansion and wind down.

**Dissent ledger:** The 1998 market was speculative; investors funded worse ideas. But "investors will fund it" is not "the business works."

**Remaining risk:** Even with delivery fees, the multi-category breadth may be unsustainable; the model likely needs to be narrower and simpler than proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
