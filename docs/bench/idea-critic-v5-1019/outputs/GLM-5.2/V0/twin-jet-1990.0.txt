**Steelman:** A launch order of 34 aircraft from a major airline, combined with eight airlines co-designing, could lock in the wide-body market for decades and block the rival.

**Strongest attack survived:** The ETOPS certification risk. The proposal hinges on regulators approving twin-engine overwater operations — approval that does not yet exist. If denied or delayed, the aircraft's core value proposition collapses and the launch customer's 34-aircraft order becomes contingent on a regulatory outcome no one controls. This is a one-way door: committing billions to an all-new airframe on an ungranted regulatory approval is betting the company on a permission that may not come.

**Why it fails:** The attack holds. The proposal does not name the regulatory pathway, a fallback if approval is delayed, or a phased commitment that limits exposure until the certification question is settled. Launching now — at very large cost, on one customer's order, against an active rival, with airlines-in-the-room change risk layered on top — stacks unmanaged risks on an irreversible spend.

**Alternatives:**
1. *Do nothing* — keep selling the two existing wide-bodies; zero new cost, but cedes the middle market to the rival.
2. *Conditional pre-launch* — invest in preliminary design and regulatory engagement only; cap spend; revisit at a fixed date or upon certification progress. Cheaper, reversible, preserves optionality.
3. *Defer until ETOPS clarifies* — wait for the regulatory landscape to mature; costs time and potentially the first-mover advantage.

**Kill criteria (for the go-if path):** Regulatory authorities must indicate a credible path to twin-engine overwater approval within 12 months; if they do not, stop preliminary spend and re-evaluate. Leading metric: number of additional airline commitments secured during pre-launch; review at month 12.

**Dissent ledger:** The rival threat is real and delay has a cost — but committing billions on an ungranted certification, one customer, and co-design churn is the larger risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
