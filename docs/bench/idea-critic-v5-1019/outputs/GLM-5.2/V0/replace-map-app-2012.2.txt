**Steelman:** Taking full control of maps secures a strategic asset, removes dependence on a rival, and keeps the platform's voice assistant competitive.

**Strongest attack survived:** Launching with "a lot less data" and known wrong place/business info removes a working app from millions of phones in one shot — a public-trust failure that could damage the brand far beyond maps. This is the sharpest objection, and it nearly holds; it fails only because the fix is structural (staged rollout + parallel availability) rather than fatal to the core claim.

**Better way:** Keep the rival app available (not pre-installed) while shipping the in-house app as default; gate the full switch behind quality thresholds. Preserves control, avoids the cliff.

**Pre-mortem:** Users open maps on day one, find missing cities and wrong businesses, and the press runs "company breaks maps." Brand damage cascades.

**Door class:** One-way. Once the rival app is gone from new phones, there is no quiet rollback — users have already lost the working app.

**Kill criteria:** If error rates exceed X% or press sentiment turns negative within the first two weeks, re-enable the rival app as a download and revert to default.

**Remaining risk:** The proposal bundles three high-stakes moves — replacing a default, removing a rival, and launching with known data gaps — into one irreversible step. The strategic logic is sound, but the execution as proposed courts a preventable disaster.

DECISION: NO-GO
