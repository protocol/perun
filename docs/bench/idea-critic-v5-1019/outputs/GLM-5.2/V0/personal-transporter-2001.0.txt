**Steelman:** A clean, compact electric vehicle could capture short urban trips that walking is too slow for and cars are too wasteful for.

**Skeptic — assumptions**
1. "Replace short car trips" assumes a large share of car trips are short urban solo runs with no cargo. No figure given. *Next action:* pull the trip-length distribution for target cities and size the addressable share.
2. $5,000 is 5–10× a bicycle, 2–4× a scooter, comparable to a used car. "Sell widely" at that price is the untested claim. *Next action:* run a price-elasticity pilot against the stated substitutes.
3. "Sell widely in cities" assumes sidewalk/road legality. No jurisdiction check cited. *Next action:* confirm legal status in the top three launch cities.

**Better-way**
- *Do nothing:* the substitutes already solve the trip at lower price and zero learning curve.
- *Cheaper path:* a small, time-boxed pilot in one city at a lower price point, measuring repeat use and trip substitution, before committing to a wide launch.

**Kill criteria**
- Pilot fails if <30% of buyers still use it weekly after 8 weeks, or if >2 of the top 5 cities ban it from sidewalks and roads.
- Leading metric: weekly-active ratio among pilot buyers; review at week 8.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch flops because at $5,000 the only buyers are affluent early adopters, the learning curve keeps casual users off, and cities classify it as neither a legal sidewalk nor road vehicle.

**Strongest attack survived:** "At 5–10× the price of existing substitutes, with a learning curve and uncertain legal status, a wide consumer launch is a bet on three untested assumptions at once." It survived only by narrowing from "wide launch now" to "small pilot first, wide launch later" — which is a different proposal.

**Remaining risk:** even a successful pilot may not generalize if legality and willingness-to-pay vary sharply across cities.

DECISION: NO-GO
