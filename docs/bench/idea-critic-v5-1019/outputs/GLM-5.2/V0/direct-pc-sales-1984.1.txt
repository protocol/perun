The strongest defensible version of the claim is that direct-to-consumer assembly of IBM-compatible PCs can undercut branded rivals on price while funding growth from cash flow, because standardized parts and a direct channel strip out reseller margins and retail overhead. The proposal survives the sharpest objection actually tried: that buyers need to see and try a computer before purchasing and that the absence of a service network makes the offering uncompetitive. Against a 1984 backdrop, the PC standard is open and commodity parts are increasingly available; the target buyer is price-sensitive and sufficiently informed to order by phone or mail; and the lack of stores is a feature (lower fixed cost, lower break-even), not only a gap. The established brands' channel advantage is real, but that same channel is the source of their price floor, and the direct model sidesteps it rather than competing within it. Supplier credit risk is the most concrete threat named, yet it is mitigated by the model's own economics: customers pay before or on delivery, so working capital comes from buyers, not from supplier terms. The "do nothing" alternative preserves capital but cedes a market that is still forming and in which a low-cost direct entrant can establish mind-share before incumbents retool their channels. No irreversible, high-spend commitment is required to begin; assembly can start small, and the kill criterion is observable: if gross margin per unit cannot cover assembly labor and customer-support cost within the first quarter of sales, stop. The proposal names its mechanism (direct order, below-brand pricing, growth from revenue), cites the constraint it must beat (incumbent price floor set by reseller margins), and carries a reversible first step with a dated stop condition. The dissent ledger is short: service-network absence is a genuine friction that may cap early repeat purchases, and supplier terms remain a watch item rather than a blocker. The remaining risk is tolerable because the first step is small and the stop is pre-committed.
