The proposal's core claim is that rapid membership growth at a loss-leading price will be offset by unproven revenue streams (data, studio deals, theater partnerships). The steelman: a subsidized land grab can lock in network effects and negotiate from strength once scale is achieved.

**Strongest attack survived:** The unit economics are structurally broken. At ~$10/month with the company paying full ticket price (~$8-15 per film), a member seeing one film per day generates $240-450/month in costs against $10 in revenue. The gap is not a marginal subsidy problem; it is a 24-45x cost-to-revenue ratio per active user. Data monetization and studio deals are cited as offsets, but no revenue figure, conversion rate, or deal probability is attached. The proposal conflates "we will grow" with "growth solves the economics," when the economics worsen with every heavy user added.

**Why the kill criterion fails:** The proposal names risks but sets no numeric threshold, no date, and no stop or pivot action. Without a capped subsidy, a defined churn ceiling, or a trigger to raise price, the default path is unlimited cash burn chasing revenue streams that remain unproven even today.

**Better way:** A tiered pilot — $10/month capped at 3-4 films, tested in 2-3 markets for 90 days — would measure whether lighter users subsidize heavier ones and whether theater chains cooperate, at a fraction of the blast radius. This is reversible and cheap; the proposal as written is irreversible at scale and expensive.

The claim does not survive: the revenue offsets are unquantified, the cost structure is inverted, and no kill criteria are pre-committed.

DECISION: NO-GO
