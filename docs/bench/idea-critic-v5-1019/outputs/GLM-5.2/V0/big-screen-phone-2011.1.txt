The steelman: a 5"+ stylus phone captures users who find 4" too small for media, note-taking and navigation, and creates a new premium tier rather than cannibalizing the existing line. The market in 2011 is moving toward larger screens; a first mover in the "phablet" space can define the category.

Skeptic: the proposal asserts "premium device" without naming the price point, the target buyer, or the first-year unit target that would justify the tooling and marketing spend. "Reviewers may call it too big" is treated as a risk to manage rather than a signal that the core form factor is wrong for the 2011 market. The stylus is added as a feature without a stated user job it solves—note-taking, drawing, or precision selection—so there is no way to know whether the stylus is the draw or dead weight. No evidence is offered that 4" screens are a constraint users actually feel; the jump from 4" to 5"+ is presented as obvious when it is the central bet.

Better-way: the cheaper path is a limited pilot in one or two markets at a lower price point, measuring attach rate and return rate before a global premium launch. "Do nothing" keeps the existing line intact and avoids the risk that the new device simply splits the same buyer base.

Kill criteria: none given. The proposal lacks a stop condition—an attach-rate floor, a return-rate ceiling, or a review-score threshold below which the line is pulled. Without that, a soft launch can drift into a full rollout on sunk-cost logic.

The strongest attack survived: "no one wants a stylus in 2011" fails because the stylus is optional differentiation, not the core bet; the core bet is screen size, and the pilot can isolate which drives sales.

DECISION: NO-GO
