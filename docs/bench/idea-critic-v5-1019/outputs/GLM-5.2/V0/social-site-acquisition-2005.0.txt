**Steelman:** A fast-growing social network with a large, young, hard-to-reach demographic could be monetized through the acquirer's existing advertising infrastructure and media relationships, creating a unique strategic asset.

**Strongest attack survived:** The core revenue model is unproven — translating rapid audience growth into advertising dollars has no demonstrated precedent at this scale, and social tastes shift quickly. This did not survive because the strategic value lies in owning the audience relationship and growth trajectory, not in immediate ad-revenue conversion; the audience itself is the asset, and advertising monetization is an execution risk the acquirer is uniquely positioned to manage.

**Assumptions:**
- The audience growth trend will continue or stabilize, not reverse.
- The acquirer's advertising infrastructure can be adapted to social-network monetization.
- The purchase price reflects the strategic value of the audience, not just current revenue.

**Better ways considered:**
- *Do nothing:* preserves capital but cedes the social-networking space to competitors.
- *Smaller stake or partnership:* tests monetization assumptions at lower cost, but may not secure control of the asset.

**Kill criteria:**
- Audience growth stalls or reverses within 12 months post-acquisition.
- Advertising revenue per user remains below projected thresholds after 18 months.
- Competitor consolidation threatens the acquired platform's market position.

**Remaining risk:** The price is high relative to the target's age and unproven monetization; if audience growth decelerates, the strategic premium may not be recoverable.

DECISION: GO
