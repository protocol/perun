**Steelman:** A high-volume retailer captures a new health-services revenue stream and store-traffic lift via exclusive rights to a potentially disruptive, low-cost diagnostics platform.

**Skeptic:** The core claim — many blood tests from a few drops, quickly and cheaply — is presented with **zero published studies**, **no shared methods**, and **active regulatory uncertainty**. This is not a risk to mitigate; it is the entire bet resting on an unverified assertion. The chain's own lack of medical expertise means it cannot independently evaluate the device even if data were shared. Putting a brand on unvalidated medical tests invites liability that dwarfs the 350M outlay.

**Better-way:** "Do nothing" costs zero and loses only an unproven option. A far cheaper alternative: a **capped pilot** (e.g., $5–10M) in a handful of stores, gated on the company providing (a) at least one peer-reviewed validation study, (b) regulatory clearance for the specific tests to be offered, and (c) a method-disclosure or third-party-audit clause. If the technology is real, the startup should welcome this — it needs validation anyway. If it refuses, that refusal is the signal.

**Kill criteria:** Walk away if, within a defined diligence window (say 90 days), the company cannot produce independent validation, regulatory clearance, or method transparency. No validation, no deal — regardless of exclusivity allure or founder charisma.

**Strongest attack survived:** "You are rejecting a potentially transformative partnership based on absence of evidence." Counter: absence of evidence in a **medical** claim is not a conservative bias — it is the standard. Retailers have no competence to serve as the validation layer for clinical diagnostics; betting 350M on that competence is a mispriced option.

**Remaining risk:** If the technology is genuinely breakthrough and a competitor grabs exclusivity during a pilot window, the chain loses first-mover advantage. But that downside is bounded; the downside of deploying unvalidated medical tests at scale is not.

DECISION: NO-GO
