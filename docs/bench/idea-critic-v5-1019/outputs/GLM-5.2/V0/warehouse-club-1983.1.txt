**Steelman:** Membership fees convert shoppers into a pre-committed, high-repeat base; bulk sales at cost drive volume that covers warehouse overhead, making the fee the profit engine rather than the markup.

**Skeptic — assumptions tested**

1. *Shoppers will pay an annual fee to shop.* This is the load-bearing assumption and it is **unverified**. Nothing in the proposal shows evidence that enough target customers will pay upfront for the right to buy a limited range in bulk. If this fails, the model has no profit source.
2. *Thin margins are sustainable.* The proposal acknowledges no room for error but offers no buffer mechanism. A single cost shock (rent, freight, spoilage) turns thin margins into losses before the fee revenue scales.
3. *The competitor's advantage is surmountable.* "More locations and cash" is named as a risk but not addressed. The competitor can match prices, undercut the membership fee, or out-spend on site acquisition.

**Better-way test**

The alternative — a conventional small-margin retailer without a membership fee — is rejected implicitly but never compared. The membership model is higher-risk (upfront customer commitment) for potentially higher reward (recurring fee revenue). No evidence is offered that the reward justifies the risk in this market and year.

**Kill-criteria test**

No stop condition is specified. At minimum the first warehouse needs a defined trial window and a threshold for membership conversions and renewal intent that, if missed, halts expansion. Without this, sunk-cost escalation is the likely path.

**Strongest attack survived**

The sharpest objection: *an earlier competitor already operating this format with more locations and cash will crush the entrant.* The proposal survives this only partially — a focused single-site pilot with measured conversion rates could demonstrate whether the model works where the competitor is weak. But the proposal as written asks for expansion funding, not a pilot.

**Remaining risk:** The entire profit thesis rests on an unverified willingness-to-pay. Approving expansion now commits capital against an assumption that has not been tested in this market.

DECISION: NO-GO
