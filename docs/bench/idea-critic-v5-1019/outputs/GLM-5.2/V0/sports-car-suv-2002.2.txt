**Steelman:** A high-margin halo vehicle, built on a shared platform to control cost, opens a new revenue stream and extends the brand to buyers who will age out of sports cars.

**Skeptic.** The core assumption — that sports-car buyers will accept an SUV under the same badge — is unverified and carries the risk that the new vehicle dilutes the brand's performance image. "Loyal enthusiast following" is the asset at stake; the proposal names the risk but offers no test of whether the badge survives it. The off-road claim is implausible: a crossover co-developed with a volume group is unlikely to deliver credible off-road capability, and asserting it invites ridicule from the enthusiast press.

**Better-way.** Two alternatives beat "launch as proposed." (1) Wait one product cycle: the shared-platform cost saving does not disappear, but the brand-damage risk can be tested through clinics and concept reception before committing the high first-year cost. (2) Sub-brand: launch the SUV under a separate name with shared architecture, capturing the revenue while insulating the sports badge. The proposal's "under the sports-car brand" is the riskiest variant, not the only one.

**Kill-criteria.** The proposal gives none. A credible version would name: a pre-launch enthusiast-clinic acceptance threshold below which the launch is restructured or cancelled; a first-year sales floor below which the model is discontinued; and a brand-equity tracking metric with a stop rule.

**Strongest attack survived:** "A heavy SUV under a sports-car badge will permanently damage the brand." This survives only conditionally — it holds if the vehicle is launched as proposed without a sub-brand, a clinic gate, or a kill metric. With those in place the bet becomes reversible; without them it is a one-way door on brand equity with no tripwire.

**Remaining risk:** The shared-platform cost saving is real, but launching under the sports-car brand at high price with an implausible off-road claim, no clinic gate, and no kill metric bets the core asset on an untested assumption.

DECISION: NO-GO
