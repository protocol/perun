**Steelman:** A high-volume retailer captures a new health-services revenue stream and store-traffic lift via exclusive rights, while a nimble partner innovates testing.

**Strongest attack survived:** The technology is unvalidated in published literature, the partner refuses to share its methods, and the chain lacks medical expertise to independently assess the device — meaning the chain would deploy a black-box diagnostic into its stores with no way to verify it works before patients are harmed. This is the sharpest objection because it makes the investment not merely risky but *unverifiable*: no amount of commercial structuring fixes "we cannot confirm the core technology works."

**Why it fails (and why the proposal still cannot pass):** The objection *should* kill the deal as proposed. Exclusivity at $350M with no published validation, no method transparency, and no internal medical expertise to evaluate the device means the chain is paying a premium for something it cannot independently vet. The regulatory risk is not a future footnote — if regulators later question the tests, the chain owns the storefront liability. The "do nothing" alternative costs $0 and loses only an unproven option. A smaller, non-exclusive pilot with milestone-gated funding and a right to audit the technology would address the core defect — but that is a *revision*, not the proposal as stated.

**Remaining risk:** Even a revised pilot carries reputational risk if the device fails in the field; the as-proposed deal carries that risk at full scale with no validation gate.

**Dissent ledger:** Commercial logic (foot traffic, exclusivity, category entry) is sound; the defect is procedural — no validation before a full-scale, exclusive, $350M commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
