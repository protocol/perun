**Steelman:** Price-competitive, PC-like, offline-friendly hardware with permissive used-game policy recovers developer support and price-sensitive consumers lost in the previous cycle.

**Origin:** agent-originated (executive proposal).

**Blast:** high — billions in hardware, studio, and marketing commitment; multi-year lock-in.

**Reversibility:** one-way door once silicon, manufacturing contracts, and launch marketing are committed.

**Assumptions:**
1. Margin loss per unit is recoverable through software attach and subscription over the console's life — *assumed*; no attach-rate or lifetime-value figure is given.
2. "PC-like" architecture is sufficient to close the developer-relationship gap despite the rival's head start — *assumed*; no developer-survey evidence is cited.
3. The maker's studios having "little new software at launch" is treated as a tolerable risk rather than a launch-killing gap — *assumed*; no first-party lineup or release-window plan is given.
4. Consumer spending softness is acknowledged but not priced into the demand model — *assumed*; no price-elasticity or TAM-revision figure is given.

**Better ways:**
1. **Do nothing / extend current generation.** Preserves cash; risks further share loss. Cheapest.
2. **Launch at parity price, invest the margin delta in first-party launch titles and online services.** Addresses the named software gap and the rival's online lead directly rather than betting that hardware architecture alone closes them.
3. **Delay launch one cycle to ship a stronger first-party lineup and differentiated online services.** Higher risk of permanent share loss; lower risk of a weak-launch death spiral.

**Kill criteria:** first-party attach rate below a named threshold at the six-month mark; developer-active-title count below a named floor at the same mark; if either trips, pivot marketing spend to first-party bundles and accelerate studio acquisitions.

**Strongest attack survived:** The proposal bets that hardware accessibility and price undercut a rival whose stated advantage is *online services* — a domain this proposal does not address. That is a real gap, but the proposal's reversibility on pricing and its permissive used-game/offline stance give it a defensible wedge even if online parity is not reached at launch.

**Remaining risk:** No online-services plan against a rival whose head start is precisely there; no first-party launch lineup to drive attach; margin compression unmodeled against lifetime value.

DECISION: NO-GO
