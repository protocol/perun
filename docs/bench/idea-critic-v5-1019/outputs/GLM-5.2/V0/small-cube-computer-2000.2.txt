**Steelman:** A differentiated, design-led product creates margin and brand identity beyond commodity towers.

**Assumptions:**
- The premium price yields sustainable margin after hard-to-manufacture acrylic costs.
- Sufficient buyers value silence/design over speed and expansion.

**Better ways:**
- Keep the acrylic aesthetic but add one expansion slot — closes the upgrade objection.
- Do nothing — eleven profitable quarters mean no rescue is needed.

**Kill criteria:**
- If first-quarter unit sales fall below 50% of the tower's run rate, cut price or halt.
- If acrylic defect rates exceed 15% in pilot, switch material.

**Strongest attack survived:** The product costs more than a faster, expandable tower, so rational buyers should reject it. This fails because the target buyer is not the tower buyer — silence, compact size, and design appeal are the value, not specs. The Cube is a margin and brand play, not a volume play.

**Remaining risk:** If the design premium does not resonate, the product cannibalizes tower margin without adding volume.

DECISION: GO
