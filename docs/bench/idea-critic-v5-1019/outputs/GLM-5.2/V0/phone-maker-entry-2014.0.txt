**Strongest defensible version:** A dominant online retailer can extend its commerce flywheel into the most personal device, using point-camera-to-buy as a native shopping shortcut and a bundled membership to lock in high-value customers.

**Strongest attack survived — barely:** The phone competes head-on on price against incumbents with vastly larger app catalogs, yet it is gated to a single carrier. The attack "buyers won't pay premium-phone prices for a device whose core differentiators are a gimmicky 3D effect and a shopping shortcut, while locked out of their carrier" is *not* survived — it is the exact failure mode the proposal walks into.

**Why this fails:**
- **No north-star alignment.** The retailer's edge is selection, price, and fulfillment speed. A premium hardware bet does not leverage that; it competes where the incumbents are strongest.
- **Single-carrier launch** caps addressable demand at a fraction of the market, yet the device must win the screen buyers use most. That is a self-inflicted wound.
- **3D head-tracking and camera product-ID** are unproven value drivers, not a category-defining feature. Bundled membership is a sweetener, not a reason to switch phones.
- **Cheaper alternative not beaten:** A shopping app preinstalled on existing phones, or a commerce API exposed to all handsets, captures the "point to buy" value at a fraction of the cost and risk.

**Kill criteria (if attempted anyway):** Pre-order threshold of [X] units within 30 days of announcement; failure triggers cancellation before manufacturing commitment. The proposal names none.

**Verdict:** REVISE. The claim that a premium, single-carrier phone with a 3D gimmick advances the retailer's commerce flywheel is unsupported against the cheaper app-first path, and no kill criterion gates the spend.

DECISION: NO-GO
