**Steelman:** A differentiated, design-led product creates margin and brand identity beyond commodity towers, targeting buyers who value form over expandability.

**Skeptic:** The proposal bundles four separate bets—premium pricing, novel manufacturing, feature reduction, and positioning above two established products—without isolating which bet must succeed. No market evidence is cited that buyers in this segment will pay more for fewer features; the assumption rests entirely on aesthetics. The manufacturing risk is acknowledged but not priced: if the acrylic case cannot be produced at scale without flaws, the entire launch stalls. No fallback material or design is named.

**Better-way:** The strongest alternative is to ship the same internals in the existing tower enclosure at a lower price, capturing the design investment without the manufacturing risk. A second path: prototype the acrylic case in a pilot run of a few hundred units, measure defect rates and buyer response, then decide on full launch. Both are cheaper and more reversible than a full launch commitment.

**Kill criteria:** None stated. The proposal does not name what result would reverse the decision—no defect-rate threshold, no sales floor, no review date.

**Fit:** Eleven profitable quarters suggest the company has recovery momentum but not necessarily surplus capital to absorb a manufacturing failure. A design-led halo product can reinforce brand, but only if it ships clean.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Buyers will not accept the lack of slots." This fails as a blocker because the target buyer is defined by not needing expansion; the risk is mis-targeting, not feature absence, and that is testable in a pilot.

**Remaining risk:** The manufacturing process is unproven at volume; a defect-prone launch damages the brand the product is meant to elevate.

The proposal lacks a kill criterion, a fallback for manufacturing failure, and any pilot stage. These are fixable, but not absent.

DECISION: NO-GO
