**Steelman.** A halo crossover expands the addressable market, funds future sports-car development, and lets the brand define the segment before rivals do.

**Skeptic.** The proposal asserts "loyal enthusiast following" and "betrayal" as soft risks, but the loyal base is the *only* asset that justifies putting a sports-car badge on an SUV. If they defect, the badge premium collapses and the project loses its central premise. No evidence is offered that the base will accept this; the proposal treats brand equity as transferable without testing that assumption. The "powerful engine" and "off-road ability" claims are also unsupported—what engine, what off-road standard, at what weight penalty?

**Better-way.** Two alternatives beat the proposal as written:
1. *Do nothing.* Continue building sports cars for a loyal, profitable base. Avoids brand dilution and the high first-year cost.
2. *Pilot under a sub-brand or limited run.* Tests enthusiast acceptance before committing to full-scale production on a shared platform.

The proposal jumps from "consider" to "launch as a high-priced model" without a stage gate between concept validation and market commitment.

**Kill criteria.** None provided. The proposal names risks but specifies no observable threshold, date, or pivot action. What pre-order count or enthusiast-survey score would stop the launch? What weight-to-power ratio would trigger a re-scope? Without these, the team is committing to a high-cost, brand-risky vehicle on intuition.

**Fit.** The north star is implied to be sports-car brand strength. A heavy crossover with off-road positioning is not obviously aligned; the proposal does not state how this moves the brand forward beyond "expand market."

**Strongest attack survived.** None. The central assumption—that the sports-car brand can absorb a luxury SUV without losing the enthusiast base—is untested and un-evidenced. The proposal is not ready for approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
