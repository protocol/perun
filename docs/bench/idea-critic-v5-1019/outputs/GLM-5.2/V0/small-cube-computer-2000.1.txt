**Steelman:** A recovered, profitable company uses a distinctive, premium, fanless desktop to extend brand identity into a new form factor, capturing buyers who value design and quiet operation over raw specs.

**Skeptic:**
- *Price above a faster, expandable tower.* No justification given for the premium. Why would buyers pay more for less?
- *Price above the popular all-in-one.* The all-in-one already serves the "compact, simple" buyer. This cannibalizes a winning product.
- *Manufacturing risk acknowledged but unmitigated.* If the acrylic case is flawed, the flagship launch is dead on arrival.
- *No target market named.* "People who like design" is not a segment with a verified size.

**Better-way:**
- *Do nothing.* Eleven profitable quarters prove the current lineup works. The burden of proof is on the disruption.
- *Price at parity with the tower.* If the form factor is the value, don't penalize buyers for choosing it.
- *Pilot a limited run.* Test demand before committing to a full launch with manufacturing risk.

**Kill-criteria:**
- What observable metric tells us this failed? Units sold in the first 90 days below what threshold?
- What stops the bleeding? A pre-committed pull date if returns exceed a rate.
- Neither is in the proposal.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, buyers compare it to the cheaper, faster tower and the cheaper all-in-one, reviews highlight "paying more for fewer features," manufacturing defects in the acrylic surface, and the product is discounted and discontinued within 12 months, damaging the recovered brand.

**Fit:** The company's north star appears to be sustained profitability after near-collapse. A premium, hard-to-manufacture, low-volume product with no verified demand and no kill criteria does not clearly serve that star. The proposal raises real risks and provides no answers: no target segment size, no manufacturing mitigation, no pricing rationale, and no stop condition. Approving as proposed means committing to manufacturing complexity and brand risk on an untested thesis.

DECISION: NO-GO
