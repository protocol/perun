**Steelman:** Voluntarily withdrawing a best-selling product nationwide, despite suspected local contamination, is the only action that demonstrably places customer safety above profit, preempting fatal outcomes and regulatory seizure.

**Skeptic:** The proposal's core assumption is that nationwide withdrawal is proportionate to a localised event. The stated risks are severe: a large financial cost, loss of hard-won market share, and competitors filling the void. However, the alternative—doing nothing or withdrawing only locally—bets the company's existence on the contamination remaining geographically limited. If a single additional death occurs elsewhere, the brand is irrecoverably destroyed and criminal liability follows. The cost of nationwide withdrawal is high but finite; the cost of under-reacting is existential.

**Better-way:** Consider a localised recall combined with a public warning to inspect packaging. This is cheaper and less disruptive. Yet it fails the pre-mortem: if the poisoner strikes again outside the initial area, the company's measured response becomes complicit negligence. A partial response signals uncertainty; a full withdrawal signals decisive control.

**Kill-criteria:** The pre-mortem failure mode is relaunching into a market that has permanently lost trust, having spent the recall capital for no return. The leading metric to watch is market share recovery at the 6-month mark post-relaunch. The tripwire: if tamper-resistant packaging fails to restore 50% of pre-incident market share within 12 months, pivot strategy to rebranding or product retirement rather than continued spend on a dead brand.

**Dissent ledger:** The financial cost is substantial and the market share loss is a real risk. The proposal does not detail the relaunch marketing budget. However, the asymmetry of the risk—potential deaths vs. financial cost—makes the withdrawal the only defensible action.

**Strongest attack survived:** "The recall will cost a very large sum and the brand may not recover." This attack fails because the alternative to withdrawal is risking further deaths, which would cost more financially and destroy the brand entirely. The proposed action is the minimum required to address the severity of the threat.

DECISION: GO
