**Steelman:** Free language lessons at internet scale could produce a translation-workforce that publishers pay for — Duolingo's actual 2009 thesis.

**Skeptic:** The revenue model is unproven AND the core mechanism (learner translations sold to publishers) is unverified. Three of four named risks attack the same load-bearing assumption: that learner output has commercial value. No evidence is offered that it does. This is the proposal's central claim presented as a future solved problem.

**Better-way:** "Nonprofit is unsustainable" is asserted, not shown. A grant-funded nonprofit with the same free-lesson model eliminates the unproven revenue dependency entirely. The proposal does not beat this alternative — it ignores it.

**Kill-criteria:** None stated. The proposal commits to building a product with an unverified business model and no defined point at which "look for revenue later" becomes "this doesn't work." Without a tripwire, sunk-cost drift is the default outcome.

**Strongest attack survived:** None. The sharpest objection — that learner translations may be too poor to sell, making the entire revenue thesis void — is listed as a risk and then set aside. It is not survived; it is deferred. A deferral is not a resolution.

**Remaining risk:** Building the product is cheap and reversible. Approving the *strategy as stated* — free learning plus a translation-revenue model with no validation, no kill criterion, and an unexamined nonprofit alternative — is not. The proposal conflates "build the product" (reasonable) with "commit to an unverified revenue model" (not yet earned).

The product can be built. The revenue model cannot be approved on these facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
