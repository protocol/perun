**Strongest attack survived:** At £400, licence-free, assembled by a major appliance maker and sold by mail order, the tricycle undercuts every motorised alternative; the 15 mph ceiling and winter weather cap the addressable market, but the low fixed cost means the bet pays back even at modest uptake — so the attack fails because the downside is bounded and the upside is real.

**Assumptions (NONE verified):** the appliance maker's agreement is binding; mail-order logistics handle a 30 kg vehicle; the £399 price covers parts, assembly, distribution and warranty; optional extras carry meaningful margin; no type-approval or insurance barrier blocks licence-free sale.

**Better ways (NONE chosen):** pilot in a single warm-weather region before a national mail-order rollout; or sell via the appliance maker's existing retail channels rather than mail order, which has no test-ride and high return risk on a vehicle.

**Kill criteria (NONE set):** the proposal names no metric, no threshold, no date, no stop condition. It asks the executive team to commit to a January launch and large-volume mail-order sales with no way to know whether to press or pull back.

**Questions the parent must resolve:**
- What is the break-even unit volume at £399, and does the appliance maker's assembly price hold at that volume?
- What licence, type-approval, insurance and road-tax requirements apply to a 15 mph pedal-assisted tricycle in the UK?
- What is the pilot region, the pilot duration, and the single metric that decides whether to scale mail-order nationally?

**Verdict:** The proposal has a defensible product and a credible assembler, but it commits to a national launch with no kill criteria, no verified cost model, and no evidence that mail-order works for a vehicle people cannot test-ride. The cost of a January launch without those answers is higher than the cost of a six-week pilot in one warm-weather region first.

DECISION: NO-GO
