**Steelman:** A time-limited disc lets studios sell cheap, consumable viewing without cannibalizing rental revenue, and early format lock-in captures the market.

**Strongest attack survived:** The need for studio and electronics-maker agreement is the critical dependency. Without it, there is no content and no hardware ecosystem. This is a one-way door with multiple veto points.

**Key concerns:**

1. **Critical-path dependency unaddressed.** The proposal lists studio and electronics-maker agreement as a risk but provides no evidence that such agreements are secured, in negotiation, or even feasible. This is a multi-party coordination problem where any single party can block the entire venture.

2. **Customer value proposition is weak.** Customers must pay more for a special player, cannot use standard players, and then face additional fees for continued viewing. The proposal acknowledges customers dislike paying for repeated viewing. This stacks three customer-facing costs against a benefit that is not clearly articulated.

3. **No kill criteria or alternatives presented.** The proposal does not specify what observable metric would trigger a stop, nor does it compare against doing nothing or a simpler approach (e.g., standard rental without new hardware).

**Questions that must be resolved:**
- Which studios and electronics makers have committed, and on what terms?
- What is the total cost to the customer for the first year of typical use?
- What cheaper or simpler alternatives achieve the same revenue goal?

**Remaining risk:** Launching without secured multi-party agreements commits capital to a format that may never reach market.

DECISION: NO-GO
