**Steelman:** A cheaper sedan with refundable deposits converts pent-up demand into interest-bearing cash that funds the production ramp, while the price point unlocks the mass market the expensive models never could.

**Skeptic:** "Take refundable deposits" is treated as validation, but depositors risk only $1,000 refundable; buyers must spend $35,000. The conversion assumption is unsupported. "Begin production the next year" assumes battery-cell supply scales from thousands to hundreds of thousands in under twelve months—no evidence the supply chain can. The margin claim is asserted, not derived; a cheaper car built at lower volume by an inexperienced team likely has *worse* margins, not better.

**Better-way:** Unveil the cheaper sedan but *not* take mass deposits yet. Run a paid-reservation pilot (e.g., 5,000 units, non-refundable above a threshold) to measure real conversion before committing to a production schedule. Alternatively: build 50,000 units first, prove the line and the battery contracts, then open mass orders. The cheaper path—*don't* take deposits—avoids the refund liability and the reputational cliff if the ramp slips.

**Kill-criteria:** Refund rate above 30% in the first 60 days, or battery-cell supply contracts signed for less than 60% of Year-1 volume by month six → stop, return deposits, reassess.

**Pre-mortem:** The company took 400,000 deposits, could not scale battery supply or its own line, burned cash on refunds and delays, and faced margin collapse as unit costs stayed high while the price was locked.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Refundable deposits prove nothing because the downside to the depositor is zero." This is real and the proposal does not distinguish interest signals from purchase commitments. It fails to fully resolve the concern, but the core mechanism—deposits as cheap, non-dilutive growth capital—is sound *if* conversion is measured before the ramp is locked.

**Remaining risk:** Production timeline assumes battery supply and line-build both scale in parallel within one year; neither is evidenced here.

DECISION: NO-GO
