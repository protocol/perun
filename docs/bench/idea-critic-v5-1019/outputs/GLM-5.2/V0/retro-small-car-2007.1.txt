**Steelman:** Nostalgia styling on a proven, low-cost platform can command a price premium and emotional pull that a conventional small car cannot.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Buyers won't pay above the platform-mate." This is the sharpest objection because it targets the core economic logic. However, the personalization options (a known high-margin lever in small cars) and the emotional differentiation of heritage styling create a plausible path to the premium. The attack fails because it assumes commodity-small-car pricing in a segment where Mini had already proven buyers pay for character.

**Assumptions:**
- The 1957 design language has present-day emotional equity (assumed; no survey cited).
- The platform-mate's cost structure leaves room for a higher trim at acceptable margin (assumed; no costed bill of materials).
- The carmaker can fund tooling for multiple personalization options despite weak finances (assumed; no capex figure given).

**Better ways:**
- *Do nothing:* avoids sinking scarce capital into a thin-margin segment during financial distress. Cheapest; reversible.
- *Heritage trim on the existing car:* one personalization package, not a separate model. Lower tooling risk; tests premium willingness cheaply.

**Kill criteria:**
- Pre-orders below a set threshold by a fixed date → cancel or reduce to a trim package.
- Net margin per unit below the platform-mate after one quarter of sales → stop.

**Pre-mortem:** The car launches, initial curiosity fades within two quarters, and the personalization options — expensive to tool and inventory — sit unbought. The price premium drives buyers to the cheaper platform-mate. The carmaker has spent scarce capital replicating a car whose appeal was assumed, not measured, and is now financially weaker.

**Remaining risk:** Weak finances mean limited ability to absorb a miss; the one-way door is capital commitment before demand is tested.

**Dissent ledger:** No market evidence cited for the nostalgia premium; no cost data to support margin viability; financial fragility raises the stakes of a wrong call.

DECISION: NO-GO
