The proposal seeks strategic control over a critical user experience by replacing a mature, rival mapping application with a first-party solution. While the long-term strategic goal of owning such core infrastructure is compelling, the execution plan presents significant, asymmetric risks that outweigh the immediate benefits.

The primary issue is the readiness of the replacement product. The proposal acknowledges that the new maps possess substantially less data than the established application and that place and business information may be incorrect. In mapping, accuracy and completeness are not mere features; they are the foundation of user trust. A mapping application that provides incorrect directions or fails to locate businesses can cause immediate, real-world disruption for users. This elevates the risk from minor annoyance to a potential breach of the core brand promise of reliability and quality.

Furthermore, the plan to make this swap the default on all new and updated phones amplifies the exposure. By simultaneously removing a known, working application, the company forces an immediate comparison. Users who relied on the previous app will experience a sudden downgrade in functionality. This abrupt transition risks significant user frustration, negative publicity, and damage to the overall platform’s reputation at a mass scale.

A more prudent path would involve a phased integration. The new application could be offered alongside the established rival, allowing users to opt-in and the company to gather critical feedback and improve data accuracy iteratively. Once the first-party solution reaches a demonstrable parity in reliability and coverage, the company could then confidently make it the sole default.

Approving the proposal as presented means accepting a high probability of a degraded user experience and the associated reputational harm. The strategic value of control is negated if the product deployed is not yet fit for purpose. The current plan is too aggressive given the acknowledged data deficits.

DECISION: NO-GO
