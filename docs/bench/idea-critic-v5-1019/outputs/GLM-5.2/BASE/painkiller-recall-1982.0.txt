The proposal is sound because the company faces not a local law-enforcement issue but a national trust crisis. Once consumers learn that bottles were poisoned after leaving the factory, every package becomes suspect unless the company acts decisively.

The financial cost of a nationwide recall is large but finite; the cost of a credibility collapse is potentially terminal for the brand. If the company limits action to the affected area, it gambles that no copycat or undiscovered contamination exists elsewhere. A single additional incident would be interpreted as negligence, and the liability would dwarf the cost of a proactive recall.

Halting production and advertising is consistent with the recall: continuing to promote a product consumers fear would appear callous and would waste spend. Relaunching in tamper-resistant packaging directly addresses the root vulnerability—post-factory access—and converts a disaster into industry leadership on safety.

Competitors may take shelf space, but only if consumers abandon the category. A trusted brand that sacrifices short-term profit to protect lives can recover loyalty; a brand that hesitates risks permanent association with harm.

The risks raised are real but manageable, and they are outweighed by the existential risk of under-responding. Decisive, voluntary action preserves long-term brand equity and consumer trust.

DECISION: GO
