The proposal aims to counter tablet market encroachment by unifying the PC and tablet experience. While the strategic intent is valid, the execution strategy carries significant, unmitigated risks.

First, forcing a touch-first interface onto non-touch hardware creates an immediate usability friction. The majority of the installed base—especially in corporate environments—relies on keyboard and mouse setups. Introducing a full-screen start menu that ignores this reality invites immediate user frustration and productivity loss.

Second, the business segment faces high training and support costs. Corporate clients prioritize stability and incremental improvements over disruptive paradigm shifts. Without a clear, easy rollback option to the traditional desktop, IT departments will likely delay adoption or seek alternative platforms, threatening the core revenue stream.

Third, the success of a new app store hinges on developer participation. The proposal assumes developers will naturally flock to the new ecosystem. However, if the installed user base rejects the new interface due to usability issues, developers will have little incentive to invest in the platform, creating a negative feedback loop.

While the desktop operating system maker holds a dominant position, leveraging that dominance to force a disruptive change risks alienating the core user base. A more measured approach—offering the new interface as an option alongside the traditional desktop—would mitigate these risks while still advancing the strategic goal.

DECISION: NO-GO
