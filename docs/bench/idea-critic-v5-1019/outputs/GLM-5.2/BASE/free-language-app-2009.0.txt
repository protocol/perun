The proposal has a compelling social promise—free language education for all—but the executive case is too weak to approve as presented.

The central revenue model is the biggest concern. Learner-generated translations are inherently low quality and inconsistent. Publishers seeking translation services demand professional accuracy; they are unlikely to pay for amateur output that requires heavy editing. This makes the core business engine unreliable before it even starts.

Second, the team lacks commercial experience. A professor and a student bring academic credibility but no demonstrated ability to build, scale, or monetize a consumer web product. The decision to avoid a nonprofit structure is reasonable only if a viable revenue model exists, and that has not been established here.

Third, the competitive landscape is hostile. Established paid language courses are polished, trusted, and well-funded. A free, learner-driven platform can attract users, but user acquisition without monetization only burns resources. The assumption that revenue can be "found later" is not a strategy; it is a deferral of the hardest question.

The risks raised are not minor edge cases—they strike at the foundation of the model. Poor translation quality undermines the revenue thesis. Free learners not converting undermines sustainability. Polished competitors threaten differentiation. None of these risks are answered with mitigations, milestones, or evidence.

Approving now would mean funding a product with no validated revenue path, an inexperienced commercial team, and serious structural risks. The idea is worth exploring, but not in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
