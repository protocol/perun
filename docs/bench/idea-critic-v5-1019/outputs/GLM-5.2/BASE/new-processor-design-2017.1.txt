The proposal carries severe downside risk. The company's explicit constraint—"no room for a second miss"—makes this an existential bet, not a routine product launch.

Three of the four identified risks compound each other destructively. If software cannot utilize the additional cores, the central value proposition collapses. The rival can then respond with price cuts or faster single-threaded chips, neutralizing the only advantages proposed: more cores and lower prices. A larger rival with superior resources can sustain a price war longer than a smaller competitor operating under financial distress.

The historical evidence directly warns against this approach. The prior all-new architecture underperformed. Repeating that same high-risk strategy—another all-new architecture—while betting on a software ecosystem that may not cooperate, and against a rival with multiple countermove options, creates a probability of failure too high to accept given the stated consequences.

The architecture is unproven, the software support is uncertain, the rival has clear response paths, and the company cannot survive another miss. These factors collectively make approval as proposed untenable.

DECISION: NO-GO
