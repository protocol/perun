The company has proven it can return to profitability, but eleven profitable quarters indicate recovery, not dominance. The proposal asks us to charge more than our popular tower for a machine with no expansion slots and few upgrade options. The tower is faster, cheaper, and more flexible. That is not a premium product. That is a worse product at a higher price.

The manufacturing risk is serious. A clear acrylic case will show every defect. If production yields are poor, we either ship flawed units and damage the brand, or we delay shipments and burn cash. The proposal acknowledges this difficulty but offers no solution.

The target market is unclear. Enthusiasts who upgrade their machines will reject the lack of slots. Mainstream buyers will gravitate toward the cheaper all-in-one. We are being asked to launch a product that costs more than alternatives in our own lineup while offering less. The only differentiator is silence and design, but those benefits do not obviously command a premium at this stage.

The company is still fragile. Eleven profitable quarters is a foundation, not a cushion. Introducing a product with high manufacturing risk, uncertain demand, and direct internal competition at a higher price point is reckless. We should not approve this as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
