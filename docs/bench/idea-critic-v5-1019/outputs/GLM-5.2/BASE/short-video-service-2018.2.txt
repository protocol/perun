The proposal asks us to commit roughly 1.75 billion dollars to an unproven content model with significant structural vulnerabilities. The core hypothesis—that viewers will pay monthly for high-budget, phone-only short episodes—rests on assumptions that the identified risks directly undermine.

Free short-form video already enjoys massive engagement on established social platforms. Our paid offering must compete with free, algorithmically served content that requires zero friction. The under-ten-minute format limits advertising inventory per session, weakening the ad-supported tier's revenue potential before we even consider conversion rates from free to paid.

Excluding television at launch removes a primary discovery and shared-viewing channel. Streaming audiences increasingly expect cross-device access from day one. Launching without it concedes ground to incumbents who already provide seamless multi-screen experiences.

The production budget scale creates a steep breakeven path. High-budget shows from well-known producers do not guarantee audience retention in a short-form, mobile-first context. If viewers sample but do not subscribe at projected rates, the capital burn accelerates without clear milestones for course correction.

The veteran film and technology backgrounds provide relevant leadership, but experience alone does not resolve the fundamental tension between premium production costs and an unproven willingness to pay. The two-country launch limits initial market feedback while still requiring the full infrastructure investment.

The combination of uncertain demand, limited device support at launch, heavy capital requirements, and entrenched free competition makes approval as proposed untenable. The financial exposure is too large relative to the evidence supporting the model's viability.

DECISION: NO-GO
