The proposal hinges on a single 34-aircraft order to justify a very large development cost, creating an unacceptable ratio of committed capital to secured revenue. Launching with one customer provides no margin for cancellation and leaves the program exposed to renegotiation.

The central technical assumption is unproven: regulatory approval for twin-engine overwater routes has not been granted. Without it, the aircraft cannot serve the missions that make it valuable, and the entire business case collapses. Approving now means betting on a regulatory outcome the company cannot control.

Designing with eight airlines sounds collaborative, but it risks scope creep and schedule delays before metal is cut. A rival is simultaneously building new models, meaning any delay hands market share and pricing leverage to a competitor.

The proposal is not dead, but it is not launch-ready. The company should first secure regulatory clarity, broaden the customer base, and lock down design requirements before committing capital.

DECISION: NO-GO
