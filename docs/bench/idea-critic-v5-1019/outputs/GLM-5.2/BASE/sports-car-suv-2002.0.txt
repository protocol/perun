The proposal asks us to stretch our brand beyond its core identity at significant financial cost. 

We must weigh the opportunity against the risks. Our brand equity is built entirely on lightweight, high-performance sports cars. Introducing a heavy, off-road luxury SUV risks alienating our loyal enthusiast base, which is our primary source of revenue and brand advocacy. 

The financial risk is severe. The first-year cost is high, and we would be entering a segment dominated by established luxury SUV makers with decades of experience. As a sports-car maker with a small range, we lack the scale and distribution network to compete effectively in the high-priced luxury SUV market. 

The co-development strategy with a larger group on a shared platform could mitigate development costs, but it introduces a risk of brand dilution. If the shared platform compromises the driving dynamics or performance characteristics our customers expect, the damage to our brand could far exceed any short-term revenue gains. 

Given the high cost, the strong competition, and the potential for brand damage, the risks outweigh the potential benefits. We should not proceed with this proposal as it stands. We must protect our core brand and focus on what we do best: building high-performance sports cars for our loyal enthusiasts. 

DECISION: NO-GO
