The proposal targets a real gap but carries compounding risks that make approval as presented unwise.

The core economics are unfavorable. The retailer is asking customers to buy a more expensive player, buy discs that self-destruct in value after 48 hours, and pay again to keep watching. That is three layers of cost and friction where the existing rental model offers one: a low fee for a few days. The convenience of not returning a disc does not offset paying more upfront for hardware and accepting per-viewing charges.

The partnership risk is severe. Launching a new format requires studios to withhold content from standard channels and electronics makers to build proprietary players. If either side declines, the format dies. Both sides have strong reasons to resist: studios fear piracy and margin loss, and manufacturers see limited volume in a closed system tied to one retailer.

The timing is also problematic. A new high-capacity format is already emerging. Introducing a competing proprietary format divides consumer attention and retailer shelf space. The proposal bets that customers will adopt a restrictive, more expensive system just as the market is moving toward flexibility and ownership.

The model also assumes customers will accept paying repeatedly for the same disc. This conflicts with established expectations: rentals are temporary, purchases are permanent. A disc that costs money to buy but then demands further payment sits in an awkward middle ground that is easy to explain but hard to sell.

The opportunity is genuine. A disc that never needs returning and handles its own payment could capture convenience-driven renters. But the proposal asks the executive team to accept a format that is more expensive for customers, dependent on multiple external partners, and positioned against an emerging standard. The risks are not peripheral; they are structural to the value proposition.

The right path is to revisit the model: reduce player cost, simplify the pricing structure, and secure studio commitment before launch. As proposed, the balance of risk and reward does not support approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
