The economics don't work. Free delivery on a single coffee or snack with no minimum order guarantees per-order losses. The contribution margin on these items is too thin to cover a dedicated courier's wages, vehicle costs, and overhead. Volume doesn't solve this: more orders mean more losses, not scale efficiencies, because each order requires a separate trip.

Direct employment of delivery staff compounds the problem. Fixed labor costs in every city create a cash burn rate that venture funding can sustain only temporarily. Rapid expansion multiplies this burn across cities before any single market proves profitable.

The customer behavior risk is the most damning. If delivery is free, customers have no reason to consolidate orders or increase basket size. The service becomes a convenience utility with no path to positive unit economics. Removing the subsidy likely removes the customer.

The proposal treats logistics as a software problem, but last-mile delivery of low-value goods is a physical constraint problem. No amount of venture capital changes the math that a courier's time costs more than the margin on a bag of snacks.

DECISION: NO-GO
