The core risk is execution against a larger, established rival using the identical format. The proposal depends on a model where merchandise profit is sacrificed to make the membership compelling; if sign-ups lag, the structure fails immediately.

The membership-fee question is pivotal. If shoppers reject paying merely to enter, the low-markdown strategy becomes a liability rather than an advantage. The proposal assumes consumer willingness to buy in bulk and travel to large sites, but it provides no evidence of local demand density or site availability sufficient to support the expansion pace implied.

The competitor's advantages—more locations and greater cash—are serious. With the same format, the rival can replicate improvements, leverage scale, and outspend the newcomer in marketing or site acquisition. Thin margins intensify this vulnerability: any operational inefficiency, pricing misstep, or supply-chain problem could erase the narrow cushion and undermine the value proposition.

While the model has structural merit in principle, the specific 1983 proposal lacks defined mitigations for these competitive and operational risks. It does not demonstrate how the new entrant will overcome the incumbent's scale, secure suitable large-format locations at viable cost, or guarantee sufficient membership conversion to sustain profitability.

Given these unresolved vulnerabilities, approving the proposal as presented would expose the venture to substantial competitive and financial risk without adequate safeguards.

DECISION: NO-GO
