The core concept is commercially weak and the proposed launch plan amplifies its flaws.

At just under £400, the tricycle is cheap, but the target market is unclear. For adults, 15 mph is too slow for roads and too exposed for winter commuting, making it a fair-weather leisure vehicle. For children, it is an expensive, heavy toy. The short range further limits utility. Selling by mail order in large numbers is mismatched to the product: buyers cannot test it, and the low speed and lack of weather protection are likely to disappoint customers who have not tried it first. Returns and complaints could be significant.

The reliance on a large appliance maker to assemble a novel vehicle is a risk. Appliance assembly lines and supply chains are not naturally suited to small-scale vehicle production, and quality or cost problems could emerge.

Safety objections are serious. A low-slung, slow vehicle among traffic is inherently vulnerable, and any sustained campaign by safety groups could damage the brand before it establishes itself.

The January launch timing is poor for a vehicle with no weather protection and limited winter use. A spring or summer launch would align better with the period when the product is most usable.

Overall, the proposal combines a marginal product with a high-volume, mail-order sales model and a launch date that conflicts with the product's seasonal strengths. The risks are not adequately mitigated.

DECISION: NO-GO
