The proposal has a compelling vision: free language education at scale. The core strategic hook is elegant—learners practice by translating real web content, and that translation work becomes the revenue stream. In theory, this creates a virtuous loop where usage generates value.

However, the risks raised are not peripheral—they strike at the foundation of the model. If learner translations are poor, publishers will not buy them, and the entire revenue thesis collapses. There is no evidence presented that learners at early stages can produce translation quality that publishers would pay for. This is not a minor execution risk; it is an existential threat to the business model.

The competitive landscape is equally daunting. Established paid courses are described as much more polished. A free offering from a professor and a student, no matter how well-intentioned, will face enormous difficulty gaining traction against entrenched competitors with superior resources.

The proposal's own admission that a nonprofit is "unsustainable" is telling, because the for-profit model presented here is equally unproven. The team acknowledges they will "look for revenue later," which means they are asking for investment in a product with no validated path to income. The two-sided value proposition—learners producing translations valuable enough to sell—remains entirely hypothetical.

What is missing is any proof: no prototype demonstrating translation quality, no pilot showing publisher interest, no evidence that learners will engage with this format. The team is intelligent and the idea is creative, but creativity without validation is speculation.

A "go" would mean committing resources to an unproven model with known structural weaknesses. The responsible path is to seek evidence first—build a minimal test of the translation-quality assumption and gauge publisher interest—before approving the full proposal.

DECISION: NO-GO
