The proposal asks us to defend a one-hour format with limited licensing against a rival pursuing broad licensing and longer recording times. That defense rests on three assumptions: that picture quality will outweigh duration, that a small partner group can sustain market presence, and that early launch will create an insurmountable lead. Each assumption is weak against the stated risks.

The one-hour tape is a fundamental mismatch with how households actually use recording. Feature films run roughly two hours, and sports events frequently exceed one hour. A device that cannot capture a full film or game in one session fails the primary use case. Picture quality matters, but consumers are unlikely to celebrate superior resolution on incomplete recordings. The rival's longer tapes directly address real viewing habits, giving it a structural advantage regardless of our technical superiority.

Limited licensing compounds the vulnerability. If the rival licenses broadly, it gains shelf space across multiple brands, accelerates installed base growth, and drives down prices through competition. A small partner coalition cannot match that distribution surface. Fewer manufacturers also means fewer incentives for content owners to release pre-recorded titles, creating a negative feedback loop: fewer titles reduces consumer interest, which further discourages publisher support.

The early lead is real but perishable. First-mover advantage only endures if the format meets ongoing consumer needs and achieves sufficient scale before rivals mature. A one-hour proprietary format with constrained licensing does neither. The rival's strategy of openness and longer duration aligns better with network effects and practical usage patterns.

Approving this proposal as written means accepting a narrow technical edge over broader market fit. The risks are not speculative warnings; they are predictable consequences of the format's limitations and the licensing approach. Revision should explore broader licensing, extended recording time, or both.
