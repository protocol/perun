The proposal presents a high-reward opportunity that directly addresses competitive timing, but the risk profile as currently structured is unbalanced.

Releasing in August to preempt a rival's September launch is strategically sound. Early market entry would capture demand before competitors and could secure favorable carrier and retail positioning. The feature set—water resistance, iris scanning, larger battery in a thinner body—is compelling and differentiating.

However, the convergence of four simultaneous risk factors creates an unacceptable probability of a critical defect:

1. **Compressed testing window**: Less validation time across all systems
2. **Battery-body tension**: A larger battery in a thinner chassis is the most dangerous risk. Thermal management, swelling tolerances, and structural integrity all become marginal. This is precisely the type of issue that passes initial checks but fails in the field.
3. **Untested biometric at scale**: Iris scanning is new to the line. Low-yield manufacturing or poor real-world performance across diverse users and lighting conditions could create both supply shortfalls and reputational damage.
4. **Global launch exposure**: A defect discovered post-launch at worldwide volume means massive recall costs, carrier relationship damage, and brand erosion that extends well beyond this single product cycle.

The core problem is that all risks are correlated and compounding. A thinner body with a larger battery already strains engineering tolerances; adding compressed test timelines and an unverified new component means a single failure in any area could cascade. The proposal asks the team to accept maximum technical risk at maximum commercial scale simultaneously.

A revised approach would preserve the August window while reducing risk: lock the battery and body specifications early and run extended thermal and cycle testing in parallel with other validation work; consider a phased rollout beginning with a smaller initial market to surface field issues before committing to full global volume; and establish pre-validated fallback options for the iris scanner if production yields or performance prove insufficient.

As proposed, the risk-reward balance is not yet acceptable.

DECISION: NO-GO
