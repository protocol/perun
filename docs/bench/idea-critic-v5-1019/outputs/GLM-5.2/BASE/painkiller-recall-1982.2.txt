The recall is the only defensible option.

First, consumer safety supersedes short-term financial concerns. With poisoned bottles confirmed, continuing to sell the product anywhere risks further harm and death. The liability, both legal and moral, of leaving the product on shelves after being informed of the tampering is catastrophic.

Second, the risk of signaling danger is unavoidable but must be weighed against the alternative. If the company does nothing and another death occurs, the public will view the brand as callous and negligent. The reputational damage from inaction would be permanent and likely fatal to the enterprise.

Third, while the cost of a nationwide recall is substantial, it is a known, survivable expense. The company's market dominance provides a foundation for recovery. Competitors may temporarily take shelf space, but a decisive, safety-first response builds long-term trust. Consumers will remember which company prioritized their lives.

Fourth, the proposal to relaunch in tamper-resistant packaging is essential. It converts a crisis into an industry-leading safety innovation, giving the brand a reason to return to the shelf and recapture market share. A local recall would be insufficient because the source is unknown and public panic would be national; only a nationwide action can restore confidence across the entire market.

The financial risks of the recall are severe but manageable. The risks of not recalling—loss of life, permanent brand destruction, and immense legal liability—are existential. This proposal protects the public and, ultimately, the company's long-term viability.

DECISION: GO
