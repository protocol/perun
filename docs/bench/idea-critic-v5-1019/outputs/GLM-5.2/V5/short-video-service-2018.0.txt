**First call: NO-GO.** The numbers are the blocker.

The proposal's own figures do not work as stated. Raising $1.75B to commission high-budget short-form content, split across two countries with a paid and ad-supported tier, carries a breakeven path that the proposal itself flags as strained — "a lot to recoup" — while the addressable revenue from phone-only, sub-ten-minute episodes in two markets at launch cannot, on any reasonable subscriber math for spring 2020, service that capital intensity before the stated risks compound.

**Blockers:** The proposal's own risk list contains a *negative stated as already true* that removes its route to upside: free short video on social platforms is *already* popular. Combined with "viewers may not pay" and "cannot be watched on TV at launch," the core claim — that enough subscribers will pay monthly for phone-only short episodes to recoup high production budgets — is contradicted by the proposal's own facts. The $1.75B commitment is too large and too irreversible to stop once demand uncertainty is resolved the hard way.

**Risks (kill criteria if revised):** No pilot named; no subscriber threshold or churn cap set; no cost-per-hour ceiling against free incumbents. No stated path to TV distribution, which narrows the paying audience at launch. No breakeven date tied to a cumulative-subscriber figure.

**Strongest attack survived:** None — the attack succeeded. The central objection (the proposal's own facts show the target users already have a free substitute, and the spend is too large to course-correct once launched) was not refuted by anything in the proposal.

The right move is a capped, one-country pilot at a fraction of the budget, with a kill criterion on subscriber acquisition cost and thirty-day retention, before committing $1.75B.

DECISION: NO-GO
