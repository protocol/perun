**First call: NO-GO** — the proposal's own facts (price, learning curve, regulatory uncertainty, no cargo/passenger) show the core claim fails as proposed.

**Steelman:** A self-balancing electric transporter could replace short car trips and walking in cities, launching at ~$5,000 to consumers.

**Attack:** The claim is that it replaces short car trips and walking. But the proposal itself states it costs well above a bicycle, scooter, or used car; riders need time to learn; sidewalk/road rules may not allow it; and it carries no cargo or second person. These are not risks that *could* materialize — they are negatives the proposal states as already true, and they directly remove the route to the upside. At $5,000 it is not competitive with a used car for someone who needs to carry anything or transport a passenger. The learning curve means adoption friction beyond a bicycle or scooter. The regulatory uncertainty means the primary use case (cities, sidewalks/roads) may be legally blocked. Every stated weakness attacks the core claim that this replaces walking and short car trips.

**Alternatives:** Do nothing — preserves capital. Launch to a niche (e.g., gated communities, industrial campuses) at a lower price — same upside with less risk. The niche path is cheaper and safer, and it reaches a verifiable demand signal before a broad city launch.

**Blockers:** The proposal's own stated facts contradict its core claim. It says the price is well above alternatives, riders need learning time, rules may not allow it, and it cannot carry cargo or passengers. These are not speculative risks — they are presented as already true, and the plan does not answer them. The core claim (replaces short car trips and walking in cities) is removed by the proposal's own negatives.

**Risks:** Unproven consumer demand at $5,000; regulatory uncertainty for sidewalk/road use; adoption friction from learning curve.

**Kill criteria:** Pre-launch consumer purchase-intent testing at the $5,000 price point; regulatory clearance in at least one target city before broad launch; pilot in a controlled environment with a measurable adoption and retention metric.

DECISION: NO-GO
