**First call: GO.** The core promise—free language learning—is deliverable and valuable regardless of whether the translation-revenue model works. The downside is a year of two people's time; the upside is a category-defining product at the dawn of the mobile era.

**Steelman:** If learner translations reach commercial quality at scale, the model is self-funding education—buyers pay for translations, learners get free lessons. That is a genuine insight if it holds.

**Attack:** The model has a circular dependency. Learners produce poor translations because they are still learning. Publishers need polished translations to buy. The very users the product serves (beginners) are the ones least able to produce sellable work. No amount of "build it and see" resolves this: the revenue hypothesis depends on a quality threshold that the product's own learning curve undermines. The proposal defers the model's hardest question ("will learners produce sellable translations?") to "later," but that question is the entire bet.

**Alternatives:** Do nothing—two capable people sit idle. Build and charge for premium features from day one—simpler revenue path, but the free-for-all promise is the differentiator. The translation model is the most ambitious option, and ambition at this stage is cheap.

**Evidence check:** The proposal's own stated risks include "translations by learners may be poor" and "the revenue model is unproven." These are not speculative fears—they are the central mechanism's open questions. The founders acknowledge them but offer no pilot, no quality benchmark, and no fallback revenue path.

**Blockers:** The translation-revenue model's quality threshold is unresolved and the proposal treats it as future work rather than a testable hypothesis now. That is a blocker on the *revenue model as proposed*, not on building the product.

**Kill criteria:** Run a small pilot—have intermediate learners translate sample content, measure whether quality reaches a publishable bar within the lesson structure. If not within one iteration, the translation model is dropped and an alternative revenue path is designed before scale.

DECISION: NO-GO
