**First call: NO-GO.** Forcing a touch-first interface onto the entire installed base—including hundreds of millions of keyboard-and-mouse-only machines—stakes the dominant platform on an unproven assumption: that desktop users will adapt rather than resist.

**Steelman:** Unifying phone, tablet, and PC under one touch-first interface creates a single developer target across all form factors, and first-mover advantage in tablets could protect the PC franchise long-term.

**Attack:** The steelman's logic depends on the unified ecosystem materializing fast enough to offset desktop disruption. But the proposal's own risks concede the core failure mode: corporate users finding the change confusing and not wanting touch on non-touch PCs. That is not a *might*—it is the proposal naming its target users' likely reaction as near-certain, while betting the entire PC base on it. No rollback path means a one-way door on a billion-device installed base.

**Alternatives:**
- *Do nothing:* keep desktop dominance, accept gradual tablet share loss. Reversible.
- *Ship touch-first as an option on touch hardware; keep the start menu on keyboard/mouse PCs.* Reaches the same developer-unification upside over time, without forcibly disrupting the non-touch majority.

**Blockers:**
1. The plan forces a touch interface onto machines that lack touch hardware—a contradiction between the interface paradigm and the physical input the target device has. The proposal states this as given and does not answer it.
2. No easy way back makes this a one-way door applied universally, including to the corporate base whose confusion the proposal itself concedes.

**Risks (kill criteria):**
- Corporate pilot adoption below threshold within 90 days → maintain classic desktop as default.
- Developer submissions to the new app store below target at 6 months → subsidize or reconsider.

DECISION: NO-GO
