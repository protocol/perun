First call: GO. A category-leading hardware maker entering an adjacent, proven, but under-served category at multiple price points, with health tracking and an app store, is a sound bet.

Steelman: this maker has the brand, supply chain, software ecosystem and retail footprint to do for watches what others couldn't — turn a niche gadget into a mainstream, must-have accessory. The phone dependency is a feature, not a bug: it deepens ecosystem lock-in.

Do nothing: cede the wrist to competitors and Google/Android. Cheaper alternative: a fitness band — but it misses the app-store and materials upside and signals timidity. The full watch beats both.

Pre-mortem: it fails if buyers see it as a solution looking for a problem. Day-one battery and phone dependency are real friction, but they're execution risks, not blockers. The category is proven (others sold modestly), the maker has every capability to do better, and the wrist is strategic real estate worth claiming early.

The proposal's own facts don't contradict its core claim. No cheaper alternative reaches the same upside. No stated negative removes the route to success. The risks raised are exactly the kill criteria we'd want, not reasons to abstain.

Kill criteria: if first-year unit sales fall below a defined floor (say 10M) or if return rates exceed 5%, pivot pricing or exit. Leading metric: attach rate to existing phone owners within the first two quarters. Review at 12 months.

This is a two-way door with a defined exit, not an irreversible bet. The maker can iterate on battery, independence, and use cases in v2. Waiting a year costs first-mover advantage in a category that's already forming.

DECISION: GO
