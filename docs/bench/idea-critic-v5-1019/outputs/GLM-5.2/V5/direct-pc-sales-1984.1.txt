**First call: GO.** Direct-to-customer assembly is cheaper than branded retail, and growth-from-revenue is the natural constraint.

**Steelman.** Direct-only assembly removes retail margin and inventory risk; growth-from-revenue caps downside. The attack: the four risks together may make the model unviable, not merely risky.

**Options.** Do nothing (stays small, safe) — but the proposal's upside beats it. Add stores — raises cost, kills the price advantage. Partner with a reseller — same problem. No cheaper alternative reaches the same upside.

**Fit.** Lower price, direct relationship, lean cost base. Aligns with a low-cost, direct-to-customer north star.

**Pre-mortem.** It fails if buyers won't purchase unseen AND no service network erodes trust. But the proposal doesn't need all buyers — enough to fund growth. One-way door? No — incorporation is reversible, direct-only is testable now.

**Evidence check.** The student is already doing this. The question isn't whether it works — it's whether it scales. The risks are real but unproven as blockers.

**Blocker test.** Is there a fact in hand showing the plan fails? No. The risks are concerns, not evidence of failure. The student has existing customers, existing margins, and a testable model. No cheaper alternative reaches the same upside. The risks become kill criteria, not blockers.

**Kill criteria.** Revenue growth stalls for two consecutive quarters (leading metric: unit sales per quarter). Pivot: add a single showroom or reseller partner. Review date: end of Q2 1985.

**Strongest attack survived.** "No stores means no service network, and buyers won't buy computers they can't try or get serviced." This fails because the student is already selling — proving some buyers will purchase direct. Service can be handled by mail-in repair or local contractors without retail footprint. The model doesn't require universal buyer trust, just enough to grow.

**Dissent ledger.** Supplier credit risk is real but addressable through multiple suppliers and prepayment terms. Brand disadvantage is offset by price.

**Remaining risk.** Buyer preference for try-before-buy may cap growth below sustainability.

DECISION: GO
