**First call: NO-GO.** The economics fail on the proposal's own numbers.

**Steelman.** A £400 licence-free tricycle, assembled by a major appliance maker and sold by mail order, could capture a new commuter market at Sinclair-scale volume.

**Blockers.**

1. **The numbers don't work.** Price: just below £400. Battery-powered, pedal-assisted. A traction battery plus motor plus chassis at 1985 prices cannot leave a margin at sub-£400 retail; the appliance maker's assembly cost sits on top. The proposal states the price and the format; the arithmetic closes negative.
2. **The product defeats its own use case.** Top speed 15 mph, short range, no weather protection, sits low in traffic. A commuter vehicle that cannot commute in British winters, cannot travel far, and is hard to see is not a vehicle the target users will keep using. The proposal names these as risks; they are the core claim — that people will buy and use it — failing in the stated facts.

**Risks (non-blocking, become kill criteria):** safety-group opposition; mail-order returns volume on a low-margin product.

**Decision: no-go.** The plan commits to a January unveil and large-volume mail-order sales on a product whose own stated price cannot cover its stated bill of materials and whose own stated limitations remove the route to repeat use. A capped pilot would not fix the unit economics.

DECISION: NO-GO
