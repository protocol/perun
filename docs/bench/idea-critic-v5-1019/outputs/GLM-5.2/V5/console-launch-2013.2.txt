**First call: GO.** The prior launch failed on exactly the dimensions this proposal fixes: price, hardware accessibility, and consumer-unfriendly restrictions. The rival's advantages (online services, head start) are real but secondary to the core value proposition.

**Steelman:** Compete on simplicity and consumer rights rather than matching the rival's online infrastructure. Standard hardware lowers developer friction and production cost; the price gap plus used-game/offline support removes the anti-consumer stigma that hurt the prior generation.

**Alternatives considered:** Match the rival feature-for-feature at parity price — requires online infrastructure the maker lacks today and abandons the cost advantage. Do nothing / extend current generation — cedes another cycle. A premium-priced, more powerful console — repeats the prior failure's economics. Neither beats this proposal on stated facts.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch flops because (a) the price cut erodes margin but software attach rates never recover it, (b) the rival's online lead matters more than hardware simplicity, (c) first-party software gaps make the platform feel empty at launch despite easy third-party development.

**Evidence check:** The proposal's own facts support the core claim. The maker lost ground after a late, expensive launch — the direct inverse of this plan. The rival's head start in online services is a risk, not a blocker: it concerns an adjacent category, the commitment is reversible (price can adjust; online services can be built over the console's life), and the plan does not require winning online to succeed. Margin reduction is real but offset by standard-PC hardware lowering BOM cost. Consumer spending risk is industry-wide and affects the rival equally.

**Blockers:** NONE. No stated fact contradicts the plan's route to upside. The risks are genuine but each is either reversible, shared with the rival, or addressable with a kill criterion.

**Kill criteria:** Pre-order volume below the prior generation's launch trajectory by T-90 days → reconsider price or bundle. First-party software attach rate below target by end of Q1 → accelerate second-wave titles.

**Leading metric:** Week-one sell-through versus the rival's comparable window; review at day 30.

DECISION: GO
