**First call: NO-GO.** The core claim is that viewers will pay a monthly fee for professionally produced short episodes designed for phones. The proposal's own stated risks attack this claim directly, and the financial structure makes the bet too large and too irreversible to survive the uncertainty.

**Blockers:**

1. **The stated risks target the core claim, and the proposal does not answer them.** The central question is whether people will pay for short phone content when free short video already dominates social platforms. The proposal names this risk itself — viewers may not pay for short shows made only for phones — yet commits roughly $1.75 billion before that question is settled. A risk the proposal itself raises, that concerns whether buyers will pay at all, and that is paired with a too-large irreversible commitment, meets the kill test.

2. **The production budgets leave too much to recoup before breakeven, on the proposal's own facts.** High-budget commissions plus a two-country launch plus a spring 2020 deadline means the burn rate is front-loaded and the payback window is long. The plan spends the majority of its capital before learning whether the format works.

**Risks (become kill criteria if revised):** No television support at launch narrows the addressable audience. No stated pilot, cap, or kill metric — the proposal goes straight to full launch. A capped, single-country pilot with a defined engagement and retention threshold would let the format question be answered at a fraction of the cost.

**Strongest attack survived:** None — the attacks that matter (will they pay, can the budgets be recouped) were not survived because the proposal does not answer them.

DECISION: NO-GO
