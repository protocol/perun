First call: NO-GO — the plan's own risks show it ships an under-built product to every user at once, removing the working alternative.

Steelman: own maps as default is the only path to control and parity; staying dependent locks in a strategic vulnerability.

Blockers:
- The proposal's own stated risks — less data, wrong places — are negatives presented as already true that remove the route to the upside (a better maps experience). The plan does not answer them.
- Removing the rival app by default is a one-way door at full population scale: every new and updated phone loses a working alternative simultaneously, with no rollback path once shipped.
- The stated risks concern the core claim (that users get a better experience); the proposal's own facts support that the data gap is real, not merely possible; and the commitment is too large and irreversible to stop once seen.

Risks:
- No pilot or staged rollout to validate data quality before full deployment.
- No kill criterion or rollback trigger if user dissatisfaction spikes.
- Turn-by-turn and 3D features add execution risk on top of already-weak data.

The core failure: shipping a known-inferior product as a default to every user while removing the established alternative is not a risk to manage — it is the plan itself. A capped pilot on a subset of phones, with a measured data-quality threshold and a rollback trigger, would test the claim. As proposed, the blast radius is too large and too irreversible for the acknowledged quality gap.

DECISION: NO-GO
