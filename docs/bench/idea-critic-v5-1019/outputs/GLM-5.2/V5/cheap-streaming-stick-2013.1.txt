First call: GO. A $35 dongle with phone-as-remote is a simpler, cheaper bet than another failed set-top box.

The steelman: a dongle at impulse-buy price, using the device everyone already holds, removes the friction that killed earlier attempts. The open kit compounds developer interest over time.

**Blockers: NONE.** The stated risks are real but not fatal as proposed. No remote is the feature, not the gap — the phone *is* the remote, and buyers already understand casting from their mobile devices. Low margin is acceptable at launch if volume follows; hardware margins can improve with scale, and the services revenue is the real play. Few launch apps is expected for any new platform and is exactly why the open developer kit matters.

**Risks (kill criteria):** If developer adoption stalls within the first year, the open-kit bet fails and the dongle becomes a dead-end accessory — set a review at twelve months with a minimum threshold of active third-party applications. If unit sales do not reach a volume that justifies continued hardware investment within two holiday cycles, exit. Watch the leading metric: weekly active cast sessions per device, which proves both consumer adoption and developer integration value.

The core claim survives its sharpest attack: that established streaming boxes with large app catalogs make a new entrant pointless. Those boxes compete on catalog completeness and charge premium prices; this dongle competes on simplicity and price, serving the buyer who wants their phone's content on the television without another complex device. The earlier failed television attempt actually supports this — it proves the company's brand alone does not move premium hardware, so a cheap, simple dongle is the right de-risking move.

The proposal is small, cheap, reversible if it fails, and attacks a real gap. Approve it.

DECISION: GO
