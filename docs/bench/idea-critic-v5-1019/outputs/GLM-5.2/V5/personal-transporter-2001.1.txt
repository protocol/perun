First call: GO. A novel, well-engineered personal transporter addressing real urban congestion is a strong bet; the raised risks are execution problems, not falsifications of the core claim.

The steelman: cities are choked with short car trips that are slow, polluting and hard to park; a compact, self-balancing electric vehicle replaces those trips at a price a city commuter can justify, creating a new category.

Blockers: NONE. The four risks are real but none is a stated fact that removes the route to upside. Price is high versus a bicycle but low versus a car and comparable to a used car with none of the parking cost. Learning time is a friction, not a wall. Regulatory uncertainty is a risk to manage, not a fact already banning it. No cargo or passenger capacity limits the use case but does not falsify the core claim for solo short trips.

Risks: regulatory ambiguity (sidewalk vs road classification), unproven consumer demand at this price, no prior category to anchor expectations, single-rider limit narrows the addressable market.

Kill criteria: pre-orders or pilot sales below a stated threshold within the first sales window trigger a price cut or channel pivot; a leading metric of units-per-store-per-week reviewed at 90 days.

A capped, time-boxed first step (pilot in one or two cities with a fixed marketing budget) lets demand prove itself before a wide launch. The proposal's own logic supports starting narrow.

DECISION: GO
