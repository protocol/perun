**First call: NO-GO.** The economics are broken on the proposal's own numbers.

**Steelman:** At $10/month with one film/day, the company pays theaters ~$120-300/month per active member against $10 received. Even at 2017 average ticket prices (~$9), one film/week exceeds the fee. The bet is that data and partnership revenue scale faster than the subsidy burn.

**Attack — the numbers don't work:**
- At ~$10/ticket and one film/day, monthly cost per heavy user ≈ $300 against $10 revenue. Break-even requires ~30 films/month per member in *partner revenue alone* — unstated and unverified.
- Even moderate users (4 films/month) cost $40 against $10 received. The subsidy per member is structurally negative at any plausible usage.
- "Grow membership rapidly" *amplifies* the loss: more members = more negative-margin transactions, not less.

**Blocker (stated fact, not risk):** The proposal itself states the fee does not cover cost and the revenue streams are unproven. That is the plan failing as proposed on its own evidence — the core claim (grow now, monetize later) has no demonstrated path to covering a per-unit loss that scales linearly with growth.

**Kill check:** The risk "revenue streams are unproven" concerns the core claim (that data and deals will cover the gap). The proposal's own facts support that this is not merely possible but *certain* — no revenue figure is given. The commitment (price cut + rapid growth) is large and hard to reverse. Three yeses → blocker.

**Kill criteria if revised:** Cap membership growth at X; set a per-member subsidy ceiling of $Y/month; kill if average subsidy exceeds $Z by date D. Name one signed studio or theater deal before cutting price.

**Remaining risk:** Even with caps, the per-unit economics may never close; data monetization in cinema has no proven benchmark.

DECISION: NO-GO
