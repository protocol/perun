**First call: NO-GO.** Removing a working default app for a known-inferior replacement, at scale, with no pilot, bets the brand on data the proposal itself admits is incomplete.

**Steelman.** Control of a core platform surface is strategically vital; dependence on a rival for maps, voice and location weakens the product over time. Owning the stack is worth short-term pain.

**Attack.** The proposal's own risks confirm the core failure mode: less data, wrong places, annoyed users. This is not a *risk* (could go wrong) — it is a *stated fact*: the replacement is worse, now, at launch. Users lose a working app and get a broken one. The blast radius is every new and updated phone. There is no pilot, no cap, no kill criterion, no staged rollout. The pre-mortem writes itself: launch day, users open maps, directions are wrong, businesses are missing, the press ridicules it, the CEO apologizes publicly.

**Alternatives.** Do nothing: keep the rival's app as default, ship the own-maps app as a beta alongside it. Cheaper, reversible, preserves user trust while data matures. The proposal does not beat this.

**Blockers.**
1. The proposal states the new maps have less data and wrong information — the replacement is known-inferior at the scale of every phone.
2. It removes a working default app users rely on, with no staged rollout or fallback.

**Risks (become kill criteria).** Launch only on new phones in one market; keep the rival app available; kill if error rate exceeds X% in 30 days.

**Dissent ledger.** Strategic case for control is sound; execution as proposed ships a known-broken product to every user simultaneously.

**Remaining risk.** Even a staged rollout may damage the brand if the data gap is as large as stated.

DECISION: NO-GO
