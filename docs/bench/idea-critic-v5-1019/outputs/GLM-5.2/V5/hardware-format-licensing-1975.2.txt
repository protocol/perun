**First call: NO-GO.** The format's own facts defeat it: one-hour tapes cannot record a feature film or a sports event, and a proprietary, few-partner strategy yields fewer players, higher prices and fewer pre-recorded titles against a rival licensing broadly.

**Steelman.** Superior picture quality and an early lead, kept proprietary, capture premium margin and control the standard.

**Attack.** The one-hour limit is a hard ceiling on the primary use case (recording films and events). The rival's broad licensing creates the network effect that makes a format standard: more players → lower prices → more pre-recorded titles → more buyers. A proprietary strategy concedes that network effect while the rival claims it.

**Alternatives.**
- *License broadly now*: match the rival's network play; trade some margin for ecosystem scale.
- *Open the format, retain brand*: players proliferate, prices fall, titles grow; standard-setting through adoption.
- *Do nothing*: the rival out-licenses, wins the network, sets the standard.

The proposal does not beat do nothing; it accelerates the rival's win by ceding the network.

**Pre-mortem.** It is 1978. The rival format is the standard. Why? Broad licensing made players cheap and titles abundant; one-hour tapes limited this format to short recordings; few partners meant few titles and high prices. The market chose the ecosystem, not the picture quality.

**Door.** Two-way early (format not yet locked), but the window closes fast: once the rival's network compounds, proprietary quality is stranded.

**Blockers.**
1. One-hour tapes cannot serve the primary use case — stated as fact, unaddressed.
2. Few-partner proprietary strategy yields fewer players, higher prices and fewer titles — the proposal's own risks confirm this.
3. The rival's broad licensing creates the network effect that sets standards; the proposal concedes it.

**Risks.** Early lead may compensate (kill criterion: rival format adoption share at 12 months).

**Kill criteria.** If rival format adoption exceeds 30% of installed base within 12 months, open the format or license broadly.

**Leading metric.** Installed base of each format, reviewed at 6 and 12 months.

DECISION: NO-GO
