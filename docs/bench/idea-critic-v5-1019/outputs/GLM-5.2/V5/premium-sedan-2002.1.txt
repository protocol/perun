**First call: NO-GO.** A mass-market brand cannot charge premium prices for a luxury sedan because buyers are paying for the badge, and the proposal's own risks confirm this.

**Steelman:** The carmaker is the regional volume leader, so it has manufacturing scale, capital, and dealer reach. A dedicated factory and premium engineering could let it cross into a segment where margins are far higher than mass-market cars.

**Attack — the badge problem:** Premium buyers pay for the badge. The proposal keeps the mass-market brand name. No amount of advanced suspension or top-quality materials changes the badge on the hood. Volkswagen tried exactly this with the Phaeton (launched 2002) and it failed for precisely this reason. The proposal's own risk list names this problem and offers no answer to it.

**Alternatives:**
- *Do nothing:* keep mass-market volume and margins. Zero risk.
- *Launch a new premium sub-brand* (as Toyota did with Lexus, Nissan with Infiniti): same engineering investment but a badge buyers can accept. Cheaper path to the same upside.
- *Start in one market, not worldwide:* smaller bet, same test of whether the badge carries.

**Pre-mortem:** The sedan launches worldwide at premium prices under a mass-market name. Buyers compare it to Mercedes, BMW, and Audi — established badges at the same price — and see no reason to choose the unknown. Low volume, high cost, no sales. The factory sits underused.

**Blockers:** (1) The proposal's own facts state that buyers pay for the badge and that luxury buyers may not accept a mass-market name — yet the plan keeps that name and offers no mechanism to overcome the objection. (2) A cheaper alternative (a new sub-brand) reaches the same upside without the badge problem; the proposal does not address why it rejects this path. (3) Worldwide launch maximizes the bet before any market validates whether badge-averse buyers will pay.

**Risks:** Low volume at high cost. No luxury image. Expensive factory.

**Kill criteria:** If pre-orders in the first target market do not exceed a stated threshold within six months of launch, stop and pivot to a sub-brand strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
