**First call: NO-GO.** The numbers do not work: a 15 mph, short-range, weather-exposed tricycle at £400 cannot sustain mail-order volume through a British winter, and the launch plan has no kill criterion.

**Blockers:**

1. **The plan's own facts remove the route to the upside.** Mail-order in large numbers is the core claim. But: 15 mph top speed makes it impractical for most roads; short range limits utility trips; no weather protection plus British winters means ~4-5 months of usable selling. The proposal states these as already true and answers none of them. The appliance maker assembling it is capacity without demand.

2. **No kill criterion on a real-money, time-boxed commitment.** A January launch event, mail-order infrastructure, and assembly line setup all commit real money. The plan names no metric, no threshold, no date at which to stop. Several risks raised (safety groups objecting, low traffic position) concern whether the product can be sold at all, and the commitment is large enough that stopping once it is seen is costly.

**Risks (become kill criteria if added):** safety-group opposition before launch; appliance-maker minimum-order commitments; battery performance in cold; liability for an unlicensed vehicle among traffic.

**Strongest attack survived:** None. The attack that the stated limitations (speed, range, weather, winter) make large-volume mail-order unachievable was not rebutted by the proposal. It stands.

**What would change the call:** A capped pilot (one region, one season) with a named stop metric (e.g., units returned vs. retained at 30 days), a liability and safety-group clearance before the launch event, and a reversible first step instead of a national mail-order rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
