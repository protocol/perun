First call: NO-GO. The plan removes the primary navigation path from the dominant desktop OS for all users, including the enterprise base that did not ask for a touch interface.

The steelman is clear: converge the PC and tablet into one platform, capture tablet growth, and create a new app-store revenue stream. That is a defensible strategic bet.

The blocker is the proposal's own facts. It states there is no easy way to return to the old desktop. It states corporate and desktop users will find the change confusing and will not want a touch interface on a non-touch PC. It states training and support costs for businesses are high. The core claim is that converging the platforms captures tablet growth. The proposal's own evidence removes the route to that upside: the existing desktop base is forced into an interface the proposal itself says they will not want, with no fallback, high switching costs, and no demonstrated demand from the PC side. A cheaper alternative reaches the same upside: ship the touch-first start screen on touch hardware and tablets, leave the traditional desktop as the default on keyboard-and-mouse PCs, and still launch the app store. That captures convergence without breaking the installed base.

The risks the proposal names are real, but they are not the blocker. The blocker is that the plan, as stated, takes a two-way door (ship a new interface) and makes it a one-way door (remove the old one) for the entire installed base, against the proposal's own stated evidence of rejection.

DECISION: NO-GO
