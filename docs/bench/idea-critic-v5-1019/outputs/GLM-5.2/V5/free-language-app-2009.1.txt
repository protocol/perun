**First call: GO.** Two people, near-zero capital, a free web app: the blast is tiny and the door is two-way. The risks named are all "may" and "unproven" — none is a blocker on the stated facts.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal commits to building a free product and deferring revenue. No large spend, no irreversible commitment. The risks (learner translation quality, conversion, incumbents, unproven model) are real but unproven — they are kill criteria, not blockers.

**Risks → kill criteria:** If a revenue experiment is run, the metric is publisher acceptance rate of learner translations, with a threshold and date inside the first window. If it falls below threshold, pivot the revenue model. Leading metric to watch: learner retention past lesson 5. Review date: 90 days after launch.

**Strongest attack survived:** "Free learners will never become paying customers, so the whole model collapses." This fails because the proposal does not depend on learners paying — it depends on their translation work being sold. Those are different claims, and the proposal correctly separates them. The attack also assumes the translation work has no value, which is an unproven assertion, not a stated fact.

**Cheaper alternative considered:** Do nothing. Rejected: building a free web app in 2009 costs almost nothing in infrastructure, the founders' time is their own, and the upside (a free language-learning platform with a novel revenue angle) clearly beats zero. No large capital is committed; the cost of delay is simply a later start in a space with no dominant free player yet.

The proposal is sound on its own terms: keep learning free, build cheaply, look for revenue later. The risks are acknowledged and become tripwires, not reasons to stop. A two-person team with no capital and a two-way door should build and learn.

DECISION: GO
