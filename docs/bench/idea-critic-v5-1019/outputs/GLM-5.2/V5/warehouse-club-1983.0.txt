First call: GO. A negative-margin retail format funded by membership fees is a real business; the question is whether the stated facts show it fails as proposed.

Blockers: NONE. The risks raised are exactly the uncertainties a pilot tests, not facts that already defeat the plan. "Shoppers may not pay" is unproven demand, not a shown refusal. The competitor's advantages are real but do not prove the format is closed.

Risks: membership rejection, thin-margin fragility, site and bulk-buying requirements, incumbent scale.

Kill criteria: membership sign-ups below a numeric threshold within the first pilot window, triggering a stop or pivot. Leading metric: paid memberships per warehouse; review date inside that first window.

The strongest attack: an earlier competitor already operating this exact format with more locations and more cash will out-execute a new entrant, making this a me-too play in a market already taken. It failed because the proposal's own economics do not depend on being first—membership fees fund thin margins regardless of incumbent presence, and a limited-range, bulk, low-markup warehouse is structurally cheaper to run than the competitor's likely broader operation. The competitor's scale is a risk the pilot prices, not a blocker already in hand.

DECISION: GO
