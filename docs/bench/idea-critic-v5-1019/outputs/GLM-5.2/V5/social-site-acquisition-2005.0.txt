**First call: GO.** Large, fast-growing young audience; ad-revenue synergy is the stated upside.

**Steelman:** A two-year-old site with explosive growth and a hard-to-reach demographic is worth a premium because that audience and growth cannot be replicated cheaply.

**Options:** (1) Do nothing — safe, but cedes the audience to competitors. (2) Build internally — slower, no guarantee of matching growth. (3) Buy now at 580M — captures audience immediately. Buying beats do-nothing if the audience persists and monetizes.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal fails because (a) the young audience migrates to the next platform within 18 months, leaving an expensive ghost town, or (b) advertising revenue never materializes at the scale needed to justify 580M. Both are plausible given that social-site loyalty is notoriously fickle and ad-monetization of social audiences is unproven.

**Evidence check:** Growth is stated as rapid; audience is stated as large and young. But the proposal concedes that turning audience into advertising revenue is **unproven** — this is the core claim, and the proposal itself flags it as unresolved. No revenue figures, retention rates, or engagement metrics are offered to offset 580M.

**Blockers vs. risks:**

- *Blocker:* The proposal's own facts state that monetization is unproven, yet the entire rationale rests on advertising-revenue synergy. Paying 580M for an unproven revenue model, with no pilot, cap, or staged commitment, means the downside is uncapped while the upside is speculative. The numbers as stated do not work: no revenue evidence supports the price.
- *Risks (not blockers but kill criteria):* audience churn to rival platforms; competitive saturation; taste shifts among young users.

**Kill criteria:** 90-day exclusivity with a capped earn-in (e.g., 150M upfront, remainder tied to 12-month revenue and retention targets). If 6-month active-user retention falls below 50% or advertising revenue per user stays below a set floor, walk away.

The audience is real and valuable, but 580M upfront for an unproven monetization model, against acknowledged fickle-taste and competition risks, is not a sound bet as structured.

DECISION: NO-GO
