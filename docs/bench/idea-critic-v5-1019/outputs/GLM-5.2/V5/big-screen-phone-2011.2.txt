First call: GO. A 2011 large-screen-plus-stylus phone is a cheap, reversible bet on an unaddressed category.

Steelman: the market has a gap between phones and tablets that a 5+ inch stylus device fills, capturing premium margin and a new segment.

Do nothing: leaves the gap to a competitor. Alternatives: a smaller 4.7-inch phone (safer, smaller upside); a pure tablet (ignores the phone-as-primary-device trend). The proposal beats do nothing.

Pre-mortem: it fails if the device is neither good phone nor good tablet, and if reviewers' "too big" framing kills demand at launch.

Evidence: the proposal states four-inch phones are current, so a five-plus-inch device is a genuine step-change in size, not an incremental bump. No market data is cited for stylus demand or large-screen appetite; these are assumed.

Blockers: NONE. No fact stated in the proposal contradicts the core claim. The risks are real but speculative ("may"), and none is shown as already true.

Risks: reviewer backlash on size; low stylus adoption; cannibalization of existing phones; the device sitting awkwardly between two categories. These are kill criteria, not blockers.

Kill criteria: pre-order volume below a threshold set by the finance team within the first two weeks of availability; reviewer consensus framing the device as "too big to be a phone" across the top five tech publications at launch; cannibalization exceeding the margin gain within one quarter.

Leading metric: first-week pre-order count against the finance team's threshold, reviewed at the two-week mark.

Decision: go. A capped, staged launch (limited initial production run, select markets first) with clear kill criteria is the right first step. The bet is uncertain, but the downside is bounded and the upside is a new premium category.
