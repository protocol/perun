**First call: NO-GO.** The proposal's own stated risks are each a core-claim blocker, and the commitment is large and hard to reverse.

**Blockers:**
1. *Core claim unverified by the proposer's own admission.* The technology "has not been shown in published studies" — the central claim (many tests, few drops, cheaply, accurately) is unsupported by any evidence the proposal offers. Investing 350 million on an unverified core claim fails as proposed.
2. *Methods concealed.* The company "will not share its methods." Due diligence is impossible; the chain cannot independently assess what it is buying. This is a stated fact, not a risk.
3. *No medical expertise, exclusivity locks it in.* The chain "has no medical expertise" yet takes *exclusivity* — it cannot evaluate, operate, or pivot the clinical offering, and exclusivity removes the escape of learning from competitors.

**Risks (kill criteria if revised):** Regulatory challenge is real but downstream; it becomes a tripwire, not a blocker today.

**Strongest attack survived:** None — the attack succeeded. The proposal asks for 350 million and exclusivity while conceding the technology is unproven, the methods are hidden, and the chain lacks the expertise to judge either. That is not a bet with uncertainty; it is a blank check on a claim the proposer has not substantiated.

**What would change this:** A capped, time-boxed pilot (single store, low capital) gated on published, peer-reviewed validation of the device's accuracy across the claimed test panel — before any 350-million commitment or exclusivity grant.

DECISION: NO-GO
