**First call: GO.** In a flagship market, timing beats perfection; an August launch preempts the rival and captures back-to-school demand.

**Steelman.** Moving to August with meaningful hardware upgrades (larger battery, iris scanner, water resistance) lets the line own the premium large-screen window before the rival's September event.

**Options.** Do nothing (keep the prior schedule): cedes the early-August window and the press cycle to the rival. August with staged rollout: same upside, less global-defect blast radius — the strongest alternative. September head-to-head: only wins if the rival stumbles.

**Blockers: NONE.** The risks raised (less testing time, larger battery in thinner body, untested biometric at volume, costly global defect) are all real, but each is a *risk the plan can answer*, not a fact already in hand that breaks the proposal. No stated fact shows the battery physics are impossible, the sensor is proven defective, or testing cannot be compressed.

**Risks:** compressed QA window; thermal/battery envelope in a thinner chassis; iris-scanner yield and accuracy at scale; a latent defect hitting a global simultaneous launch with no staged recall path.

**Kill check.** Does each risk concern the core claim (buyers will pay, the phone works)? Yes for battery-in-thin-body and untested biometric at volume. Do the proposal's own facts support that it will happen? Not yet — no yield data, no thermal test results cited. Is the commitment too irreversible to stop once seen? A global simultaneous launch is; a staged rollout is not. The plan as proposed commits to the irreversible form without a gate.

**Decision: go-if** the August launch ships as a **staged rollout**: first wave in a limited set of markets, with a **kill criterion** of defect rate above X% (the requester's own threshold when given) or iris-scanner false-reject above Y% within the first 14 days, triggering a hold on subsequent waves. Leading metric: field defect rate per shipped unit, reviewed at day 14.

As proposed (global simultaneous volume ship, no staged gate), the irreversibility is unmanaged.

DECISION: NO-GO
