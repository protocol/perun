**First call: GO.** The assets are paid for, the styling is the brand's own heritage, and small cars at a weak carmaker are a volume play.

**Steelman:** A retro-styled city car on an existing platform, priced above the platform-mate and personalized, trades on emotional differentiation the platform-mate cannot match.

**Alternatives:** Do nothing — continues poor sales. Price at platform-mate level — leaves margin on the table. The personalization premium is the cheapest path to better margins.

**Pre-mortem:** It fails if buyers see "premium price, economy car" and walk. But retro styling has worked elsewhere, and personalization options add perceived value at low cost.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks are real but addressable: backward styling dates quickly, but so does any styling; buyers may not pay more, but the personalization options justify the premium; recent poor sales are a brand problem this car directly addresses; thin margins are improved by the premium positioning and personalization options.

**Risks:** Styling dates quickly. Buyers reject the premium. Recent poor sales persist. Thin margins remain thin.

**Kill criteria:** Pre-orders below a threshold within the first month. Cancellation rate on personalization options above a threshold. First-quarter sales below a breakeven volume.

**Decision:** GO. The proposal uses existing assets, targets emotional differentiation, and positions for better margins. The risks are inherent to any car launch and are addressed by the premium positioning and personalization strategy. The kill criteria provide clear early warning.

DECISION: GO
