First call: GO. The maker's phone is the dominant asset; a watch that deepens that ecosystem is a reasonable bet even if the standalone case is weak.

The strongest attack: earlier smartwatches failed, this needs the phone, one-day battery is poor, and buyers may not want a second screen. That is a real objection, but it rests on competitors' weak execution rather than a structural impossibility. The maker's existing phone base, app store, and brand loyalty are not available to the same degree to the prior entrants, so the "modestly sold" prior art underprices this maker's distribution and pairing advantage.

Cheaper alternative: do nothing. That avoids the risk but cedes the wrist to rivals and forgoes the ecosystem lock-in. A staged launch (one material, one price) would reduce exposure but would also weaken the brand statement and developer pull. On the stated facts, the multi-tier launch is defensible because the maker's phone base gives the app store a real chance of developer interest.

The plan commits real money, so the first step should be capped and time-boxed, with a kill criterion: if first-year sell-through falls below a stated unit threshold, or if phone-pairing attach rate stays below a stated fraction, stop or narrow the line. Battery life and second-screen need are risks to watch, not blockers: they do not, on the facts given, remove the route to upside, and the phone-pairing advantage is the reason a prior-failure extrapolation is not decisive.

No fact in the proposal contradicts its core claim, its own figures do not fail, and no cheaper alternative reaches the same upside. The remaining risks become the kill criteria, not the decision.

DECISION: GO
