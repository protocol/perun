First call: NO-GO. The stated risks are not speculative "mays" — the proposal itself concedes the software installs without clear consent, is hard to remove, and treats every customer as a thief. Those are self-admitted facts that remove the route to the upside: customers who feel violated don't buy more discs, they stop buying them.

**Blockers:**
1. The proposal's own facts contradict its core claim. It ships software "without clear consent" that is "hard to remove" — by definition this is unauthorised installation on millions of computers. The upside (reduced copying) is destroyed by the downside (customer revolt, legal exposure, brand damage) that the proposal itself names.
2. The numbers don't work. Millions of discs × hidden software = millions of compromised systems. A single security disclosure (which the proposal admits is possible) turns a copy-protection feature into a botnet-scale liability. The blast radius is catastrophic and irreversible — you cannot uninstall software from computers you don't control.
3. The stated facts already show the target users reject the approach. "Customers may object to being treated as thieves" is the proposal naming its own failure condition. Customers who are alienated don't return to buying CDs; they accelerate to piracy out of spite.

**Risks (kill criteria if revised):**
- Legal: unauthorised software installation violates computer-fraud statutes in multiple jurisdictions.
- Security: hidden rootkit-style software will be found, exploited, and publicly shamed.
- Reputational: the brand becomes "the company that hacked its own customers."

**Better way:** A voluntary, clearly disclosed digital-copy system (the model that later became industry standard) achieves the legitimate goal — letting paying customers use their music — without the self-inflicted damage of covert installation.

The proposal asks the company to secretly compromise millions of its own customers' computers to fight piracy, while admitting the customers will hate it. That is not a plan that survives its own risk list.

DECISION: NO-GO
