First call: GO. This is a two-way door: a single warehouse tests membership willingness and bulk-buying behavior at low cost, and the model's profit engine (the fee) is structurally sound.

**Steelman.** Membership fees decouple profit from unit margin, so selling near cost maximizes volume and fee renewals; the format is proven by the competitor, and the limited range keeps overhead low.

**Attack.** The sharpest objection: shoppers will not pay a fee to shop, and without that fee the thin-margin model loses money. But this is testable in one location before scaling, and the competitor's existence proves the format has demand. A second objection: the competitor has more cash and locations. Yet scale is earned warehouse by warehouse, and a focused range is a cost advantage the larger player may lack.

**Alternatives.** Do nothing: forfeits a proven, defensible model. Higher margins without fees: abandons the volume flywheel that makes the membership worth renewing. The proposal beats both.

**Blockers: NONE.** Every risk raised is a real but unproven concern—demand, execution, site availability—not a fact already in hand that breaks the model.

**Risks → kill criteria.** If the first warehouse's membership sign-ups or renewals fall below a set threshold by month 12, stop expansion and reassess. Watch the leading metric: membership renewal rate; review at the one-year mark.

**Decision: GO.** Open the first warehouse, cap the initial commitment to that location, and gate further expansion on membership sign-up and renewal metrics. The proposal survives the strongest attack: the fee model is uncertain, but that uncertainty is exactly what the first warehouse is sized to resolve.

DECISION: GO
