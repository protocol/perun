**First call: NO-GO.** A retailer with no phone history enters a saturated market at flagship pricing, via one carrier, betting on a gimmick and a shopping feature.

**Blockers.** The proposal's own facts show it fails as proposed. (1) Price matches leaders that have "far larger app catalogs" — the phone loses on the screen and apps buyers use most, which the proposal itself names as the test. (2) "Single-carrier launch limits who can buy" — the distribution channel caps the addressable market before the product's merits matter. Both are negatives the proposal states as already true, and neither is answered.

**Risks (kill criteria).** Buyer demand for 3D head-tracking is unproven; the shopping feature presumes users want a storefront in their pocket. Kill metric: first-quarter sales below 50% of the carrier's comparable flagship; if fired, stop. Leading metric: pre-order volume in week one. Review: 90 days.

**Better way.** Do nothing: the retailer already captures mobile purchases through its app and website, at zero hardware risk. A cheaper alternative — a co-branded device with an established OEM — reaches the same shopping-integration upside without building a phone from scratch. Both beat launching a flagship solo.

**Decision: NO-GO.** The plan loses on its own stated terms: it concedes the screen/apps war it says a phone must win, concedes the single carrier limits buyers, and offers no evidence the 3D effect or product-ID feature overcome those deficits. A premium first phone from a non-phone retailer, at flagship price, through one carrier, is a one-way door with no pilot and no kill criterion. The proposal is not ready; revise with a narrower entry (co-branded or mid-tier), a multi-carrier or unlocked path, and a defined stop metric, or do not pursue it.

DECISION: NO-GO
