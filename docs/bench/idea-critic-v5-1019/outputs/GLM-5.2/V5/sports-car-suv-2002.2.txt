**First call: GO.** A sports-car maker with a loyal following and a willing larger partner has a genuine shot at a new, profitable segment; the risks are real but they are the normal risks of any brand extension, not a case that the plan fails on its own facts.

**Steelman:** A sports-car brand's equity — performance, engineering credibility, desirability — is exactly what a crowded, commoditised luxury-SUV market lacks; co-development caps the cost and the partner's platform de-risks execution, so the brand can enter a high-margin, growing segment with a defensible point of difference.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's own facts do not contradict its core claim. The named risks — brand dilution, high first-year cost, heavy vehicle, strong incumbents — are all *risks* (could go wrong), not *negatives already shown to be true*. No figure in the proposal is recomputeable as failing. No cheaper alternative reaches the same upside on the stated facts.

**Risks (kill criteria):**
- Brand betrayal: pilot the positioning with the enthusiast base first; kill or re-scope if pre-launch sentiment testing shows a majority see it as a betrayal of the brand's core promise.
- Cost overrun: cap the first-year co-development spend at a fixed amount; review at a set date.
- Incumbent strength: set a first-18-month sales threshold at or below the partner's comparable model; if it misses, re-evaluate the powertrain/price mix.

**Strongest attack survived:** "A heavy, off-road SUV under a sports-car brand will dilute the image so badly that the core customer base erodes faster than the new SUV revenue grows." This is the sharpest objection because it attacks the brand equity that makes the whole plan work. It fails as a blocker because the proposal's own facts do not show this erosion is *already* true — it is a plausible risk, and a brand-extension risk is exactly what a capped, time-boxed first step with a sentiment kill criterion is designed to test. The upside (a profitable, defensible entry into a growing segment) is large enough, and the first step is reversible enough, that the risk does not stop the call.

DECISION: GO
