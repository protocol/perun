**First call: GO.** The upside is real and the downside is capped: the platform already exists, the styling is a marketing lever not an engineering one, and a carmaker with weak finances has no cheaper route to showroom traffic than a retro skin on an existing platform.

**Steelman.** Retro styling is proven to pull buyers into a brand's heritage at a premium; Fiat 500 (2007) is the canonical case. The proposal bets that nostalgia plus personalization options justifies a price above the platform-mate.

**Attack — the one that almost broke it.** Buyers won't pay more than the platform-mate for a backward-looking skin. This is the core claim: that styling and trim options create enough perceived value to clear a premium price point. The proposal's own facts don't settle it — they assert the strategy without evidence that the target segment pays for nostalgia. The recent poor-selling models cut both ways: retro could be the differentiation that reverses the trend, or the brand may be too damaged to command a premium. Either way, this is the risk most likely to be a blocker: if the premium doesn't hold, thin small-car margins turn this into a loss-maker on an existing platform.

**Why it survives.** The premium-pricing question is testable before full launch — through pre-orders, pricing clinics, or a capped pilot market — and the platform reuse means the downside of being wrong is bounded. The proposal commits real money to tooling and launch, but the kill criterion (premium uptake below a threshold in early markets) can be set before that spend is irreversible.

**Blockers:** NONE. The premium-demand risk is real but not yet shown to fail; it becomes the kill criterion.
**Risks:** Backward styling dating quickly; premium price resistance; brand damage from prior models; thin margins.

**Decision:** go-if the first market pilot hits a named premium-uptake threshold before full rollout.
**Kill criterion:** if early-market sales at the premium price fall below X units in Y months, stop or reprice.
**Leading metric:** pre-order conversion rate at the announced price; review before tooling commitment.

DECISION: GO
