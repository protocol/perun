**First call: NO-GO.** The proposal's own facts show the target users are not served at the stated price and conditions.

**Blockers:**
1. **Price vs. target user.** The proposal states the device replaces short car trips and walking, yet prices it at ~$5,000 — well above a bicycle, scooter, or used car. The people who make short trips on foot (the stated replacement target) cannot absorb this price, and the proposal names no segment that both wants it and can pay. The target users do not want it at this price.
2. **Regulatory contradiction.** The proposal wants to sell widely in cities, yet itself raises that sidewalk and road rules may not allow it. A "sell widely in cities" claim that acknowledges cities may ban it is internally contradictory; the plan does not answer this.

**Risks (kill criteria, not blockers):**
- Learning curve: a friction point, not a failure of the core claim.
- No cargo or second passenger: limits use cases but does not break the stated short-trip replacement.

**Steelman:** A clean, efficient personal transporter could capture urban short-trip demand if priced accessibly and regulated clearly.

**Strongest attack survived:** None — the price barrier and regulatory contradiction are stated by the proposal itself and are not resolved.

**Dissent ledger:** The device may have real value, but the launch as proposed — ~$5,000 consumer price, wide city sales, unresolved regulation — does not hold against its own stated risks. A cheaper version, a pilot in a permissive city, and a clear regulatory path would change the call.

DECISION: NO-GO
