First call: NO-GO — removing a working default app for an admitted weaker replacement, at scale, with no escape hatch named.

The steelman: control of a core platform surface matters strategically; a staged migration could capture that upside without forcing every user onto an unready product.

The strongest attack I tried: the proposal itself states the new maps "have a lot less data" and that place/business information "may be wrong in places" — yet it removes the established app as default on *all* new and updated phones simultaneously. That is a one-way door executed at maximum blast radius on the proposal's own admitted weakness. The pre-mortem writes itself: users update their phones, their working maps vanish, the replacement is visibly worse, and the brand tied to premium polish ships something half-baked. The kill criterion the plan needs — a quality threshold the replacement must clear *before* it replaces the default — is absent. No pilot, no cap, no fallback is named.

**Blockers:** the proposal's own facts show the replacement is inferior to what it removes ("a lot less data"), and the plan commits to full-scale default replacement with no stated quality gate or rollback. The numbers the proposal states contradict the execution it proposes.

**Risks:** brand damage from shipping visibly incomplete software; user trust erosion; loss of a differentiated feature (turn-by-turn) if the replacement fails. These become kill criteria: ship the default switch only after the new maps clear a measured accuracy/completeness threshold against the established app in a pilot market.

A capped first step: launch the new maps as an *optional* app alongside the established default, pilot in one region, measure data-completeness and user-retention metrics, and only then migrate the default. This captures the strategic upside without forcing every user onto an unready product on day one.

DECISION: NO-GO
