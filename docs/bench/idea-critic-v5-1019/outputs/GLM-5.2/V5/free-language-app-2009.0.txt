First call: GO. The product (free language lessons via web app) is cheap to build, easily reversible, and valuable on its own. Revenue can be found later; that is a risk, not a blocker.

Steelman: A web app delivering short lessons at near-zero marginal cost could reach millions who cannot afford Rosetta Stone. The translation-revenue idea is a bonus option, not the foundation.

Blockers: NONE. The core bet — build free lessons, find revenue later — does not fail on its own facts. The proposal already names every risk (translation quality, conversion, polish gap, unproven model). Naming a risk is not the same as resolving it, but none of these are shown as already-fatal; they are uncertainties on a cheap, reversible first step.

Risks: Translation quality could make the revenue model unworkable. Free learners may never convert. Incumbents are more polished. The revenue model is entirely unproven. All real, all stated, none blocking a build-and-learn first step.

Kill criteria: If, within the first window, learner translations cannot reach a quality threshold publishers will pay for, the translation-revenue hypothesis is dead and a different model must be found. Leading metric to watch: learner retention and engagement, since a product people do not stick with has neither educational nor revenue value.

The strongest attack: free learners will never produce translations good enough to sell, so the only revenue path collapses and the nonprofit was right to seem unsustainable. This fails to block because the proposal does not depend on translation revenue to justify building — the product itself is the bet, revenue is found later, and the build cost is low enough to be worth that uncertainty.

DECISION: GO
