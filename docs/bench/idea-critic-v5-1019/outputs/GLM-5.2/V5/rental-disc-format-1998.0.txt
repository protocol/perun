**First call: NO-GO.** The economics depend on customers buying a more expensive player for discs that self-destruct, while every alternative (rental, standard DVD) is cheaper and more convenient.

**Steelman:** A few-dollar disc watched for 48 hours, then pay-per-view, could capture impulse rentals without late fees or return trips. The phone-connected player enables a metered model studios and retailers would both profit from.

**Attack — the core claim is that customers will adopt this.** The proposal states the players cost more than standard players and the discs cannot be played on standard players. That is a self-stated negative removing the route to the upside: the format requires customers to buy a premium, locked-down player for content that costs more over time than a rental. The proposal also states customers dislike paying for repeated viewing. Each is a fact in the proposal, not a risk.

**Alternatives:** Do nothing — standard disc rental already serves the market at lower cost. Existing pay-per-view or standard DVD purchase both reach the same upside without requiring special hardware.

**Blockers:** (1) The proposal's own facts show the target users will not want it — more expensive player, discs incompatible with existing hardware, repeated fees customers dislike. (2) The format depends on studios and electronics makers agreeing to support it, yet the higher player cost and restricted discs give them less, not more, incentive.

**Risks (kill criteria):** Studio/electronics-maker adoption is not secured — if no partners commit within one quarter, stop. Customer purchase rate of the special player — if it does not reach a named threshold within the first pilot window, stop.

**Decision: no-go.** A capped pilot with named kill criteria would be the path to test the demand assumption, but as proposed — committing to a new format, special players, and partner alignment against self-stated customer resistance — the plan fails on its own facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
