First call: GO. A low-cost, reversible test of subscription razors is a sound operator's bet.

The steelman is clear: razors are a high-margin, frequently-purchased category dominated by two brands whose pricing leaves room for a value player; a humorous, low-budget video is cheap to produce and can be iterated; a simple website taking orders immediately tests the core assumption—will people subscribe?—with minimal capital at risk. The risks raised (price cuts, lawsuits, supplier dependency, low traffic, subscription reluctance) are real but unproven, and none is a blocker on the stated facts.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's own numbers are not contradicted by any fact given. The dominant brands *could* cut prices or sue, but "could" is a risk, not a blocker. The single supplier is a dependency, but the plan is a small launch, not a commitment to scale. One video may bring little traffic, but that is a testable hypothesis at low cost. Customers may not like subscriptions, but that is the core question the launch answers.

**Risks:** (1) Dominant brands respond aggressively—price war or legal action. (2) Supplier dependency—if they cut supply, the business stalls. (3) Single video underperforms—traffic too low to learn. (4) Subscription model rejected—customers prefer one-time purchase.

**Kill criteria:** If after 30 days the video has fewer than 10,000 views and the website has fewer than 100 subscriptions, pivot the traffic strategy or stop. Leading metric: subscription conversion rate from video traffic; review at 30 days.

The cheapest alternative (do nothing) leaves the core question unanswered. A more elaborate launch raises cost without reducing the key uncertainty. The proposal is a small, reversible bet that tests the one assumption that matters: will people buy razors by subscription?

DECISION: GO
