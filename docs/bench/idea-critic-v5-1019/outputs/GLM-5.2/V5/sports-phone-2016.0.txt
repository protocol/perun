First call: GO. A flagship large-screen phone pulled forward to August to beat a rival's September launch is a sound competitive move; the features listed (larger battery, iris scanner, water resistance) are the kind of differentiators that matter at the top of the market.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's own risks (less testing time, larger battery in thinner body, untested biometric at volume, defect cost at global scale) are real execution risks, but none is shown as already-failed or already-true. They are risks to manage, not facts that remove the route to the upside. The competitive timing is the core claim, and nothing in the proposal contradicts it.

**Risks:** The four raised risks are exactly the kill criteria. The sharpest is the battery-in-thinner-body combination: a thermal or swelling defect at global scale is the one-way door here, not the schedule. The iris scanner is a single-sensor bet; if it fails at volume, the phone still ships, but the differentiator is gone and the rework cost is high.

**Kill criteria:** Global defect rate for battery/thermal issues exceeding [industry threshold] within the first 30 days of volume shipment → halt shipment, recall affected units. Iris scanner field-failure rate exceeding [threshold] within 30 days → disable via software update, ship without the feature. Leading metric to watch: early-returns and warranty-claim rate in the first 14 days post-launch, reviewed weekly.

The competitive window is the reason to move now; the risks are the reason to cap the exposure with early telemetry and a pre-committed stop. The plan beats doing nothing (cede the September window) and beats a delay (lose the timing advantage that is the entire point).

DECISION: GO
