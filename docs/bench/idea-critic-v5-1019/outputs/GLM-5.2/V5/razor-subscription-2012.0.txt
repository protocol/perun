First call: GO. Low spend, reversible, tests the real question.

Steelman: a cheap, funny, direct-to-consumer subscription undercuts two bloated incumbents whose ad spend is their moat, not their margins. The irreversibility is near zero.

The sharpest attack: two dominant brands will crush a reseller who owns neither the product nor the customer relationship yet. But the proposal's own facts answer it — the spend is low, the site is simple, and the downside is the cost of one video and a basic site. That is a one-way door priced as a two-way door. A cheaper alternative (no video, just a landing page) reaches less upside; it shapes the first step, not blocks it.

The risks listed are real but none is a blocker on these facts:

- Incumbents cut prices or sue: possible, not yet shown, and a suit against a reseller of a supplier's own product is not obvious.
- One supplier: a dependency risk, not a plan-killer for a launch test.
- One video brings little traffic: the kill criterion, not a reason to stay off the field.
- Customers may not want subscription: the core claim to test — blocking on it would be assuming the answer.

Kill criteria: if the video plus site does not yield a named order threshold by a fixed date (say, 30 days), stop and reassess. Watch the leading metric — conversion from site visits to paid subscriptions — and review at day 30.

The commitment is small, the door is reversible, and the test is exactly the question that matters. Approve as a capped first step with a 30-day kill metric.

DECISION: GO
