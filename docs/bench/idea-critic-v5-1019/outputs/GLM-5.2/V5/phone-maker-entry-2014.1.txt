**First call: NO-GO.** A retailer with no phone experience, entering a market where the established leaders have far larger app catalogs, at a matching premium price, through a single carrier — and the phone's differentiators (3D display, product identification camera, bundled membership) are not the features buyers use most on a phone.

**Steelman:** The retailer's membership ecosystem and product-identification feature create a unique commerce-on-device loop that incumbents cannot replicate.

**Attack:** The proposal's own facts defeat it. Buyers use the screen and apps most; the phone matches leader pricing but has a smaller app catalog. A single-carrier launch limits the buyer pool. The 3D effect is an unproven gimmick. No stated advantage overcomes "matches leader price, fewer apps, fewer buyers."

**Blockers:**
1. The proposal states the price matches leading phones while those phones have far larger app catalogs — the core value proposition fails on the proposal's own evidence.
2. A single-carrier launch limits who can buy, yet no path to broader distribution is offered.
3. The differentiators (3D, product-ID, membership) do not address what buyers use most (screen and apps), per the proposal's own stated risk.

**Risks:** No prior phone experience; unproven demand for 3D; strong incumbents; irreversible commitment to hardware, carrier deals, and inventory.

**Kill criteria:** If pursued, pre-order conversion below 5% of carrier's subscriber base in 30 days → stop. Leading metric: app catalog size at launch relative to incumbents.

**Decision: no-go.** The proposal's own facts show it cannot win on the dimensions buyers use most, at the price it sets, through the channel it chose. The commerce-loop steelman is real but does not require a premium smartphone to capture.

DECISION: NO-GO
