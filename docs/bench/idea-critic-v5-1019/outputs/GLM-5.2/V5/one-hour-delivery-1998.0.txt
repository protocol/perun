**First call: NO-GO.** Free delivery of a $3 snack that must arrive in 60 minutes cannot cover its unit cost on any volume this team can prove in 1998.

**Steelman.** Dense urban demand for convenience is real; direct employment guarantees quality; speed-to-cities captures land before competitors. At stated scale, this is the strongest version.

**Attack.** The proposal's own numbers defeat it. A delivery rider earning ~$15/hr and completing ~2 deliveries/hr costs ~$7.50 per drop. The margin on a $5 snack-and-coffee order at retail markup is ~$1. Free delivery loses ~$6.50 on every small order. The "no minimum" term makes this structural, not occasional. There is no stated volume at which fixed labor costs fall below per-delivery contribution; the model is a permanent subsidy, not a startup cost.

**Alternatives.** Do nothing: avoids certain per-order loss. Cheaper path: minimum order threshold or delivery fee on sub-$15 baskets — reaches the same convenience upside without the structural bleed. The proposal rejects both by design, which is precisely the flaw.

**Kill check.** The core claim — customers will use a free, sub-hour service frequently enough to cover direct labor — is unproven. The proposal's own risk list concedes customers may use it *only when subsidized*. Combined with one-way-door expansion (staff, vehicles, city leases) and no stated kill metric, three yeses land: core-claim risk, self-admitted subsidy dependence, irreversible scaling commitment.

**Blockers:** Unit economics do not close at any volume the proposal states; the "no minimum, free delivery" term guarantees per-order loss on small baskets, which the model cannot answer.

**Risks:** Unproven repeat demand; direct-employment overhead in each city; expansion before unit-cost validation.

**Kill criteria:** If average contribution margin per delivery (delivery revenue + product margin − delivery cost) is negative after 90 days in the launch city, stop and either institute a minimum order or a delivery fee. Leading metric: repeat-order rate by week 4; review at day 90.

DECISION: NO-GO
