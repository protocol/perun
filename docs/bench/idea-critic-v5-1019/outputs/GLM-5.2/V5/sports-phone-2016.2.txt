First call: GO. The core claim is that beating a known September rival to market with a flagship that has headline features (larger battery, iris scanner, water resistance) will capture sales that would otherwise go to the rival. The steelman: first-mover advantage in a two-horse race is worth the compressed timeline, and volume shipment maximizes the window before the rival's device appears.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's own figures do not contradict its core claim. No alternative reaches the same upside cheaper: "ship in September as before" forfeits the first-mover advantage that is the entire point. The risks raised (less testing time, larger battery in thinner body, untested biometric sensor, defect cost at global scale) are all *could-go-wrong* risks, not facts already in hand showing the plan fails. They are real, but they become kill criteria, not a veto.

**Risks → kill criteria:** The battery-in-thinner-body risk and the untested iris scanner are the two that concern the core claim (does the device work at volume). Commit to a pilot run with a numeric threshold for thermal performance and sensor failure rate; if either breaches threshold before August ship date, hold the specific component, not the whole launch. The testing-time risk is the one that is too irreversible to ignore: a global recall on a flagship is a one-way door. Gate the worldwide volume ship on a defined acceptance test pass; stage it so early markets surface defects before full global rollout.

**Leading metric:** field defect rate within first 30 days of pilot-market launch, reviewed before global volume ramp.

The proposal is sound on its stated facts. The risks are manageable with a staged rollout and pre-committed kill criteria. Approving as proposed, now.
