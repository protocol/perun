First call: NO-GO — four independent failure modes, each fatal alone, none mitigated.

Blockers:
1. **No mass-production capability.** The company has built two vehicles in small numbers. The proposal demands far higher volumes next year. The gap between "hand-built two" and "mass-produced tens of thousands" cannot close in twelve months. This is the proposal's own fact.
2. **Battery supply unproven at scale.** Scaling cell production sharply is a prerequisite the plan assumes away. No supplier commitments are stated.
3. **Refundable deposits ≠ revenue.** Deposits signal interest, not purchase commitment. Capital cannot be deployed against refundable orders without solvency risk.
4. **No margin at lower price.** A cheaper car built by a company with no scale economies, no supplier leverage, and no production learning curve will cost more per unit than the price supports. The proposal concedes "little margin" — meaning the business model does not close on its own stated figures.

Risks (kill criteria if revised): production ramp timeline must be tied to demonstrated capacity, not aspiration; battery supply contracts must be signed before accepting deposits; margin must be recomputed at realistic unit costs for a first-year, low-yield line.

The steelman — that refundable deposits fund the ramp and brand momentum carries early demand — fails because it depends on every risk resolving favorably simultaneously, and the plan provides no evidence any will. The cheapest alternative (build a pilot line at 10% target volume, prove unit economics, then scale) reaches the same upside with reversible commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
