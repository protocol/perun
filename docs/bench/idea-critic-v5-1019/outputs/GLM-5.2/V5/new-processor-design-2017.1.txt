**First call: GO.** A smaller player losing ground has one rational move: a differentiated bet, not an incremental one. The proposal is exactly that — more cores, lower prices, new architecture. The risks are real but generic to any comeback attempt.

**Steelman:** A struggling smaller player can't outspend the rival on the same axis, but can shift the fight to core count and price-performance, forcing the rival to defend its margin.

**Alternatives:** (1) Do nothing — concede desktop, guaranteed decline. (2) Incremental refresh on existing architecture — safer, but repeats the failure mode that caused the miss. The all-new architecture is riskier but it's the only path that changes the outcome.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's own facts don't contradict the claim. The risks — software ecosystem, rival response, financial fragility — are the *kill criteria*, not blockers. They describe what could go wrong, not that the plan already fails.

**Kill check:** "Finances leave no room for a second miss" is the sharpest risk. Does it concern the core claim? Yes — survival. Do the facts support it happening? It's stated as already true. Is the commitment too irreversible to stop? The architecture investment is large but staged — a first product line, not an all-in bet. Three yeses are close, but the plan can cap spend and set a kill date before full commitment. This makes it a **capped, time-boxed first step**, not a blocker.

**Kill criteria:**
- Desktop performance parity or lead within one product cycle (measured against rival's shipping parts at launch).
- Revenue from the new line covering its architecture investment within 18 months of launch, or pivot to licensing the architecture.
- Leading metric to watch: pre-order volume and benchmark leaks 60 days pre-launch.

**Strongest attack survived:** "The last all-new design underperformed, so this one will too." This fails because it treats one data point as a pattern while ignoring that the *reason* for the prior miss is precisely what this proposal must solve — and the proposal doesn't claim the architecture is proven, it claims the bet is worth making given the alternative is decline.

DECISION: GO
