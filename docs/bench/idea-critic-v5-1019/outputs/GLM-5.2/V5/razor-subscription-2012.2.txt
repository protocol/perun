First call: GO. Low price + subscription + humorous video against lumbering incumbents is a sound operator's read.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's own facts do not contradict its core claim. Reselling a supplier's product rather than manufacturing is an advantage, not a flaw: near-zero capex, fast launch, and the supplier relationship is a manageable risk, not a fatal one.

**Risks (kill criteria, not blockers):**

- **Supplier dependency.** Mitigation: identify a second supplier within 90 days. Kill criterion: if the sole supplier raises price >30% or threatens cutoff with no alternative sourced, renegotiate or pivot.
- **Incumbent retaliation (price cut or lawsuit).** Pre-mortem: Gillette slashes cartridge prices and Dollar Shave Club's unit economics break. Kill criterion: gross margin per subscriber below a set floor for two consecutive months triggers a price or supplier review. A lawsuit is a two-way door — defend and use the publicity.
- **Single video, low traffic.** This is the cheapest customer-acquisition test possible. Kill criterion: if cost per acquisition exceeds the first-month subscription revenue by >3× across the first 10,000 views, pause paid promotion and iterate on creative before scaling spend.
- **Subscription resistance.** Kill criterion: if month-2 churn exceeds 60%, redesign the plan (frequency, pause option) before expanding.

**Cheaper alternative considered:** a landing page with price test, no fulfillment. Rejected — it tests willingness to click, not willingness to subscribe and pay. The full launch *is* the pilot, and at this cost level the downside is trivial.

The bet is uncertain, as every worthwhile bet is. But the commitment is small, the cost of delay is first-mover advantage in a category the incumbents are ignoring, and every named risk has a concrete tripwire. The dominant brands spend heavily on advertising — which means they are structurally slow to respond to a low-price, direct-to-consumer entrant.

DECISION: GO
