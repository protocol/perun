First call: GO. The previous generation lost because it was late, expensive, and hard to develop for. This proposal directly reverses all three failures.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's own facts show the prior strategy failed (late, expensive, lost ground). The four risks raised are all risks, not blockers: a head start in online services is a disadvantage but not a plan-killer; reduced margin per unit is a known trade-off that lower price exists to make; thin launch software is a timing risk; and consumer spending trends are a market risk. None of these contradict the core claim or show the plan fails as proposed.

**Risks (kill criteria):** The sharpest objection is that lower price + standard hardware commoditizes the console, eroding the reason developers and players choose it over a PC or the rival. That is a real risk but it does not block: the plan can launch, test demand at the lower price point, and adjust. Kill criterion: if attach rate (software units sold per console) falls below the prior generation's level within the first 12 months, the price-undercut strategy is failing and must be revised. Leading metric to watch: weekly sell-through rate relative to the rival's, reviewed at 90 days post-launch.

The proposal addresses a concrete, documented failure (lost the last generation) with a specific, reversible set of choices (price, hardware, used games, offline play). The risks are real but manageable through a capped first step: launch in the primary market, measure sell-through and attach rate, and decide on broader rollout based on those numbers.

DECISION: GO
