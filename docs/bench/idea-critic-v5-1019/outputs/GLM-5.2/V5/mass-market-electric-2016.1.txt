First call: NO-GO. The company's own facts show a volume jump of roughly 100× in one year against zero mass-production experience, a battery supply chain that does not exist at scale, and a price point that removes the margin cushion needed to absorb the inevitable production errors.

The steelman is strong: refundable deposits prove real demand before committing capital, and the cheaper car is the only path to scale. But the attack that survives is this: the proposal treats next-year production as a plan when it is a bet that a company with no mass-production track record can simultaneously stand up a battery supply chain, a high-volume line, and a low-margin cost structure in twelve months. The pre-mortem writes itself — tooling delays, battery cell shortages, and quality rework compound because the low margin leaves no room to absorb them. The proposal names every one of these risks and then answers none of them.

A cheaper, staged alternative blocks here: take deposits, build a pilot line at 10–20× current volume (not 100×), prove the battery supply and the margin at that scale, then raise volume. That reaches the same upside with a reversible first step.

**Blockers:** the proposal's own figures (2 vehicles → thousands, next year) do not work against its own stated facts (no volume experience, no battery supply at scale, refundable deposits, near-zero margin). It commits to an irreversible volume jump without a pilot, a cap, or a kill criterion.

**Risks:** demand may not convert from deposits; battery scaling may fail; margin may prove insufficient. All become kill criteria under a staged plan.

**Kill criteria:** deposit-to-non-refundable conversion below 60% within 90 days; pilot line throughput below 50% of nameplate after 60 days; unit cost above the price that leaves the stated margin.

DECISION: NO-GO
