**First call: NO-GO.** The retailer's core advantage (shopping integration, membership) is bundled into a device whose primary function (phone/apps) it cannot win, at a price that invites direct comparison with entrenched leaders.

**Steelman:** A retailer with millions of existing customers, a proven membership hook, and unique shopping-flow integration enters the most consequential device category at a time when hardware is commoditizing and ecosystem lock-in is still forming.

**Attack — the numbers don't work as proposed:**

1. **Price parity with category leaders who have 100x the apps.** The proposal states the price matches leading phones. Those phones have the app catalogs buyers use most — a stated risk. At price parity, the buyer compares app catalogs and picks the leader. The shopping and membership features are secondary on a phone; they do not justify matching the leader's price with a fraction of its apps. This is a blocker: the proposal's own facts show the target buyers will compare on screen and apps, and the device loses that comparison at the asked price.

2. **Single carrier compounds the app-catalog gap.** The proposal limits launch to one carrier. Fewer buyers can access it, and the app catalog is already weak — developers won't build for a single-carrier phone with small install base. The two stated risks reinforce each other rather than being mitigated.

3. **The 3D effect is a feature, not a reason to switch phones.** The proposal raises that buyers may not value it. Combined with price parity and a weak app catalog, it is not a compensating advantage.

**Blockers:** Price parity with app-rich leaders while having a fraction of their apps — the proposal's own facts show buyers compare on apps and screen, and this device loses that comparison at the asked price. Single-carrier launch reinforces the weak-app-catalog problem rather than compensating.

**Risks:** Unproven hardware capability; brand stretch from tablets/e-readers into phones; carrier dependency.

**Kill criteria:** App catalog within 50% of leaders by month 6; cost-to-acquire below membership lifetime value by month 12.

DECISION: NO-GO
