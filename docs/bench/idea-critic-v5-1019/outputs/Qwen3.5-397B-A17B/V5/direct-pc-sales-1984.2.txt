First call: GO. The direct-to-consumer, low-overhead model logically undercuts established competitors on price, which is a decisive advantage in a nascent, price-sensitive market for commodity hardware.

**Steelman:** A lean entity can dominate by offering near-equivalent technology at significantly lower prices through a direct-order channel, reinvesting margins to scale without the burden of physical retail or heavy inventory.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Touch-and-Feel Risk:** While buyers prefer testing electronics, the price differential (estimated 30-50% below brands) creates a compelling incentive to bypass this friction. Early adopters and cost-conscious businesses will trade certainty for savings.
2.  **Channel/Brand Moat:** Incumbents' large sales channels are cost centers, not just assets. Their pricing must support retailer margins; the proposed model eliminates this layer, turning the incumbents' strength into a pricing weakness.
3.  **Service Network:** Lack of physical service is a valid concern but solvable via mail-in repair or third-party local technicians, costs far lower than maintaining proprietary stores.
4.  **Supply/Credit:** New entities face credit hurdles, but the "assemble to order" model minimizes inventory capital requirements. Cash flow from direct sales can fund parts procurement before assembly, reducing the need for extensive supplier credit lines initially.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if component supply chains tighten, preventing just-in-time assembly, or if defect rates are so high that mail-in repair costs erase the price advantage. However, standard IBM-compatible parts suggest a robust, multi-vendor supply market, mitigating single-source risk.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** None. The risks are operational hurdles, not fundamental flaws. The math works: lower overhead + direct sales = lower price = market entry.
*   **Risks:** Brand trust and service logistics. These are managed by setting clear warranty terms and focusing on the price-sensitive segment first.
*   **Alternatives:** Adding stores or resellers immediately destroys the cost advantage. "Do nothing" cedes the low-end market.

The proposal leverages a structural inefficiency in the current market (retail markup) to gain foothold. The risks are manageable growing pains, not showstoppers.

DECISION: GO
