First call: NO-GO. The price point excludes the mass market required to replace car trips, and the regulatory environment is undefined.

**Steelman:** A revolutionary urban transporter that eliminates short car trips through superior maneuverability and zero emissions, justifying a premium price via transformative utility.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** At $5,000, the unit costs more than a reliable used car, a motorcycle, or hundreds of bicycles. It fails to beat "do nothing" or existing cheap alternatives on cost. The value proposition collapses for the average commuter when a $500 scooter or $2,000 used car offers weather protection, cargo capacity, and passenger ability.
2.  **Regulatory Void:** Launching "widely" ignores that cities have no framework for sidewalk vs. road use. A mass launch invites immediate bans, creating stranded inventory and liability nightmares before product-market fit is proven.
3.  **Utility Gap:** The inability to carry cargo or a passenger disqualifies it from many "car replacement" scenarios (groceries, childcare). The learning curve further restricts the addressable market to early adopters, not the masses needed to justify a wide launch.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 12 months. Cause: Regulatory bans in key cities due to sidewalk accidents, combined with sales stagnation because the price forces buyers back to used cars. The "wide" launch strategy maximizes exposure to these blockers before they are resolved.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price vs. Alternatives:** The core claim (replace car trips) is contradicted by the existence of superior, cheaper alternatives (used cars) that solve the cargo/passenger/weather constraints.
*   **Regulatory Ambiguity:** A wide consumer launch is impossible without defined legal operating spaces; the proposal admits this uncertainty but proceeds anyway.

**Risks:** High learning curve limiting adoption; lack of utility for multi-purpose trips.

**Kill Criteria:** None proposed. A viable plan requires a capped pilot in a permissive jurisdiction to test regulatory response and learning curves before a wide launch. The current proposal skips validation for a full-scale rollout.

**Decision:** The plan commits real money to a wide launch despite fatal flaws in pricing strategy and regulatory clearance. It does not beat the "used car" alternative on utility or cost.

DECISION: NO-GO
