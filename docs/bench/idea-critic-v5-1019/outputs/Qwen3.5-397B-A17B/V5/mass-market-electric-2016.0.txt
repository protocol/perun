First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on executing a complex, capital-intensive manufacturing scale-up that the company has never demonstrated, funded by a fragile deposit mechanism with no proven conversion rate.

**Steelman:** The company can leverage early adopter demand for expensive cars to fund a mass-market sedan, using refundable deposits as proof of interest to secure battery supply and finance production tooling for a next-year launch.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capability Gap:** Moving from "small numbers" of expensive vehicles to "far higher volumes" of a cheap sedan is not a linear step; it requires entirely different supply chains, automation, and quality control systems. The timeline (unveil 2016, production 2017) allows zero time to debug mass-production line failures, which historically plague new automakers.
2.  **Supply Chain Reality:** Battery scaling is a long-lead item. Securing enough cells for "far higher volumes" usually takes years of contracting and plant construction. There is no evidence the supply chain exists to support this jump in one year.
3.  **Financial Fragility:** Refundable deposits are not revenue; they are liabilities that can vanish instantly if confidence wavers. With "little margin" on a lower price point, any delay in production or increase in unit cost (likely given the inexperience) burns cash immediately. The company lacks the buffer to absorb the inevitable delays of a new production ramp.
4.  **Alternative:** A cheaper path is to validate the battery supply chain and manufacturing processes with a limited, non-refundable pre-order run or a partnership with an established manufacturer before committing to a full independent scale-up.

**Blockers:**
*   **Production Inexperience:** The stated fact that the company has "little experience of mass production" directly contradicts the feasibility of hitting "far higher volumes" within one year. This is a factual mismatch between capability and commitment.
*   **Supply Chain Lead Times:** The requirement to "scale up sharply" battery supply in under a year contradicts standard industry lead times for cell manufacturing capacity, creating a physical impossibility without existing unused capacity (not stated).

**Risks:** Deposit refund cascade causing liquidity crisis; margin erosion from production inefficiencies.

The plan assumes operational maturity and supply chain availability that the proposal explicitly admits the company lacks. The timeline is incompatible with the physical realities of automotive manufacturing.

DECISION: NO-GO
