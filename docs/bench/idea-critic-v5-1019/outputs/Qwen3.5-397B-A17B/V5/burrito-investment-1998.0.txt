First call: GO. The option to invest later with minimal upfront capital creates an asymmetric bet: limited downside if the concept fails to scale, but significant strategic upside if "fast-casual" becomes a dominant category.

Steelman: A low-cost minority stake secures a foothold in an emerging higher-price segment without diverting management attention or forcing immediate operational integration, preserving the right to double down only if unit economics prove scalable.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Zero cost, but cedes the emerging fast-casual category to competitors and loses learning opportunities.
2. Full acquisition: High cost and integration risk; premature given the unproven scalability of the 16-unit chain.
3. Internal development: Slow, expensive, and lacks the specialized culinary knowledge already present in the target chain.

Fit: Aligns with long-term growth by exploring adjacent price points and service models without compromising the core fast-food value proposition.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the investor forces premature scaling or operational standardization before the model is proven, destroying the very agility that makes the chain attractive. This is a two-way door; the initial stake is small enough to reverse or let expire.

Evidence: The proposal explicitly limits exposure to a minority stake with independent operations, directly mitigating the raised risks of management distraction and operational mismatch. The "tiny" size is a feature for a pilot, not a bug, allowing validation of the higher price point and skilled labor model before committing significant capital.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (scale, labor, price) are hypotheses to be tested by the independent chain, not facts preventing the initial investment. The structure protects the investor from these specific execution risks.

Risks: The concept may never scale beyond 16 units; the higher price point may remain niche. These are addressed by making the initial check small and contingent on future performance.

Kill Criteria: If the chain cannot open 5 new profitable locations within 24 months under its own management, the option to invest more should not be exercised.

DECISION: GO
