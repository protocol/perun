First call: NO-GO. The price is extreme for an unproven revenue model in a volatile market.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform now secures a dominant position with young demographics before competitors do, allowing immediate monetization via existing ad infrastructure.

**Options:**
1. **Buy:** Costs $580M. High risk of taste shifts and revenue failure. Irreversible capital outlay.
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero cost. Risks losing the audience to a rival who buys it. Fully reversible.
3. **Partner/License:** Lower cost. Tests revenue sharing without full acquisition risk. Less control but safer.

**Fit:** Assumes the north star is "youth audience ownership." However, the strategy relies on the unverified assumption that this specific audience converts to traditional advertising models.

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition fails because user migration to a newer trend occurs within 18 months, leaving the buyer with a $580M obsolete asset and no revenue lift. This is a one-way door; the capital is gone. The cost of delay is losing exclusivity, but not necessarily the revenue opportunity if a partnership is possible.

**Evidence:** All figures are assumed from the proposal. The core claim—that rapid growth equals sustainable ad revenue—is explicitly marked "unproven" in the proposal itself. No data exists to support the $580M valuation multiple.

**Blockers:**
1. **Unproven Revenue Model:** The proposal admits turning growth into revenue is unproven. Committing $580M without proof of unit economics violates basic capital allocation logic.
2. **Valuation vs. Volatility:** The price assumes permanence in a sector defined by fleeting tastes. The risk of rapid obsolescence is a known fact, not a speculation.

**Risks:** Competitor action, taste shifts, integration failure. These are secondary to the fundamental lack of financial proof.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot ad integration cannot prove positive ROI within 6 months post-acquisition, the asset must be divested. However, the initial commitment is too large given the current unknowns.

The proposal asks for a massive, irreversible bet on a hypothesis explicitly stated as unproven. A cheaper, staged approach (partnership) reaches the same learning upside without the catastrophic downside.

DECISION: NO-GO
