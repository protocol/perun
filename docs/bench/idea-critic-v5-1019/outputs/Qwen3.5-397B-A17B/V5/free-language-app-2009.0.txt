First call: NO-GO. The proposal explicitly defers revenue validation while relying on an unproven, high-risk dual-sided marketplace (learners as workers) to eventually sustain free access, creating a fatal gap between cost and cash flow.

**Steelman:** A free language platform leveraging user-generated translations for revenue can scale education globally if quality controls and market demand align.

**Attack:**
1.  **Revenue Fantasy:** The core economic engine—selling novice translations to professional publishers—is contradicted by the stated risk of "poor" quality. Professional publishers require accuracy novices cannot guarantee without expensive expert oversight, destroying margins.
2.  **Deferred Validation:** "Look for revenue later" is not a strategy; it is a hope. With established, polished competitors charging fees, the assumption that users will tolerate a beta product long enough for a monetization model to emerge is unsupported.
3.  **Missing Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks specific metrics to validate the translation market or user retention before building the full product. Without a pilot proving publishers will buy novice work, the entire sustainability thesis collapses.
4.  **Alternative:** A cheaper, safer path exists: validate the translation marketplace with a manual concierge service (no app build) or secure publisher commitments before writing code. The current plan commits to high fixed costs (development) before verifying the variable value proposition.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Contradiction:** The proposal admits translations may be "poor" yet relies on selling them as the primary revenue source. This is a direct factual conflict within the plan's own risk assessment.
*   **No Viable Path to Profit:** Deferring revenue discovery while incurring development costs violates basic operational hygiene for a project judging nonprofits "unsustainable."

**Risks:**
*   User quality insufficient for commercial sale.
*   Free users never convert or generate value.
*   Competitors outpace feature development.

**Decision Method:**
The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test. Building a full product without validating the unique revenue mechanism (novice translations) is a sunk-cost trap. The admitted risk of poor quality directly blocks the proposed revenue stream. A "go-if" is impossible here because the fundamental unit economics are unproven and likely negative. The correct move is to validate the B2B translation demand manually before any software construction.

DECISION: NO-GO
