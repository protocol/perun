First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits irreversible capital to unproven technology with no independent verification.

Steelman: If the device works as claimed, exclusive access would create a dominant, low-cost health screening network. However, the claim rests entirely on the founder's assertion.

Attack:
1.  **Evidence Void:** Zero published studies exist. In medicine, "trust us" is insufficient for a $350M bet. The core claim (accuracy/speed) is unverified.
2.  **Capability Mismatch:** The chain lacks medical expertise to validate results or manage liability if tests fail.
3.  **Regulatory Trap:** Secretive methods guarantee regulatory scrutiny. If regulators later reject the device, the $350M infrastructure becomes stranded assets.
4.  **Alternative:** The "do nothing" or "wait for peer review" options cost $0 and avoid catastrophic downside. A cheaper alternative is a small pilot *after* independent validation, not before.

Pre-mortem: Two years post-launch, regulators ban the device due to inaccurate readings. The chain faces massive lawsuits from misdiagnosed customers and writes off the entire clinic investment. This is a one-way door; once built, these clinics cannot be easily repurposed.

Blocker Test:
*   **Blocker:** The proposal's own facts state the technology is unproven and methods are hidden. This directly contradicts the requirement for medical reliability. The cost of delay (waiting for proof) is negligible compared to the cost of being wrong ($350M loss).
*   **Risk:** Even if the tech works, execution risk is extreme. But the lack of proof is a factual blocker, not just a risk.

This fails the "evidence" check fundamentally. You cannot build a medical empire on a black box with no data. The commitment is too large and irreversible to proceed without verified facts. The correct move is to demand independent, published validation before discussing investment, let alone exclusivity.

DECISION: NO-GO
