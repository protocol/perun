First call: GO. The market lacks a high-end device bridging the phone-tablet gap, and screen real estate is a defensible premium differentiator despite skepticism.

Steelman: A large-screen stylus device creates a new "phablet" category, capturing users who need more productivity than a 4-inch phone offers but less bulk than a tablet, driving higher average selling prices.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Cede the emerging large-screen segment to competitors; risk irrelevance as media consumption shifts to larger displays.
2. Incremental increase (4.5"): Minimal differentiation; fails to solve the productivity gap; easily ignored.
3. Full tablet entry: High cannibalization of existing tablet lines; loses the "pocketable" utility.
The proposal beats "do nothing" by defining a new market rather than fighting for share in a saturated small-screen segment.

Fit: Assumes the north star is expanding the total addressable market through form-factor innovation. This aligns with moving upmarket to premium pricing.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if reviewers successfully frame the device as "comically large" and unusable one-handed, stigmatizing early adopters. However, this is a two-way door; pricing and marketing can adjust, or the line can be discontinued if adoption lags. The cost of delay is high, as competitors are likely exploring similar form factors.

Evidence: No internal data is available, but the trend toward larger screens in media consumption is observable. The risk of cannibalization is real but preferable to losing customers to other brands offering larger screens.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (reviewer bias, stylus adoption, awkward sizing) are uncertainties, not proven facts that invalidate the core claim. They represent execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The fear of cannibalization is a standard trade-off for innovation.

Risks: Market rejection of the form factor; stylus perceived as niche; brand dilution. These become kill criteria: if Q1 sales do not meet 60% of small-phone premium models or if return rates exceed 15% due to size complaints, the line must be capped or pivoted.

DECISION: GO
