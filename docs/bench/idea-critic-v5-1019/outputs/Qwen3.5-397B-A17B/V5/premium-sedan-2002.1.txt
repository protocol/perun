First call: NO-GO. A mass-market badge cannot command premium pricing without a distinct identity, making the core revenue assumption false.

Steelman: Leveraging existing scale to fund a high-spec sedan could undercut rivals on price while delivering equal quality. However, this steelman collapses because the proposal explicitly targets "pricing at the level of established premium rivals" under the "own, mass-market brand name."

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids massive CAPEX risk and brand dilution.
2. Create a new luxury sub-brand (e.g., Lexus, Acura model): Proven path to separate perception from mass-market roots.
3. Acquire an existing luxury marque: Instantly grants badge equity and distribution.
The proposed plan beats none of these; it combines the highest cost (dedicated factory) with the lowest probability of sales success (wrong badge).

Fit: Entering the premium class requires moving the "north star" of brand perception. Using a mass-market name actively opposes this goal.

Pre-mortem: The factory opens, but showrooms see no traffic. Luxury buyers refuse to pay $60k for a car associated with economy models, viewing it as status-negative. The company is stuck with high fixed costs and unsold inventory. This is a one-way door; a dedicated factory cannot be easily repurposed for low-volume niche production if the launch fails.

Evidence & Blockers:
The proposal admits the primary blocker: "buyers pay for the badge" and "brand has no luxury image." Yet, the plan ignores this by insisting on the mass-market name. In the automotive industry, badge equity is the primary driver of margin in the luxury segment. Building a superior product (large engine, advanced suspension) does not overcome the psychological barrier of the logo when pricing matches incumbents. The financial model fails because volume projections will not materialize at the proposed price point without the badge.

Blockers:
1. Brand mismatch: Mass-market name prevents premium pricing (stated in proposal risks).
2. Economic failure: High fixed costs of a dedicated factory cannot be covered by the low volumes expected given the brand handicap.

Risks:
Brand dilution of the core business; competitor retaliation; inability to recruit luxury-tier design talent under a mass-market banner.

DECISION: NO-GO
