First call: NO-GO. The price point creates an immediate market mismatch where the cost exceeds superior alternatives (used cars) while offering significantly less utility (no cargo, single passenger, weather exposure).

Steelman: A revolutionary urban micro-mobility device that eliminates short car trips through superior maneuverability and zero emissions, justifying a premium price via technological novelty and time savings.

Attack:
1. **Economic Dissonance**: At $5,000, the unit costs more than a reliable used car, which offers safety, cargo capacity, passenger seating, and weather protection. The value proposition collapses when a strictly superior substitute is cheaper.
2. **Adoption Friction**: Requiring users to "learn to balance" introduces a high barrier to entry compared to bicycles or scooters which have near-zero learning curves. This limits the addressable market to early adopters rather than the mass market required to sustain wide city distribution.
3. **Regulatory Void**: Launching "widely" without established legal frameworks for sidewalks or roads invites immediate bans, confiscations, or liability lawsuits. Selling a product that may be illegal to operate in its primary use case is a critical failure mode.
4. **Utility Gap**: The inability to carry cargo or a second person disqualifies it from many practical short trips (groceries, child transport), further narrowing its use cases relative to the high price.

Blockers:
- **Price/Utility Mismatch**: The cost exceeds a used car while providing fractionally less utility. This contradicts basic consumer rationality for mass adoption.
- **Regulatory Uncertainty**: No defined legal path for operation creates an existential risk to the business model immediately upon launch.

Risks:
- Learning curve limits market size.
- Safety liabilities from falls or collisions.

The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" test (a used car) and lacks a regulatory strategy. The risks are not merely executional; they are foundational to the product's viability. A pivot to a niche, lower-cost, or regulated pilot is required before a wide consumer launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
