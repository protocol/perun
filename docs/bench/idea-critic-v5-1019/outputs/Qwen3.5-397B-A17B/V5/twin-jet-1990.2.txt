First call: NO-GO. Launching a multi-billion dollar aircraft program on a single order of 34 units is financially unsustainable and exposes the manufacturer to catastrophic risk if that sole customer defaults or reduces the order.

Steelman: Securing eight major airlines during design ensures market fit, and a single anchor order provides enough momentum to start.
Attack: One order does not constitute a viable market for an all-new aircraft family. The development cost is "very large," implying billions in R&D. Thirty-four units cannot amortize these costs at a profitable price point. Furthermore, the proposal admits critical regulatory approval for twin-engine overwater flights (ETOPS) has not been granted. Without this, the aircraft's primary advantage over existing tri-jets or four-engine jets is nullified for long-haul routes, which is the target market between wide-bodies.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital, wait for regulatory clarity on twin-engine operations, and let the rival test the market.
2. Secure more orders: Delay launch until a critical mass of orders (e.g., 200+) is secured to share development risk.
3. Upgrade existing models: Offer improved versions of current wide-bodies to fill the capacity gap immediately.

Fit: The strategy assumes immediate regulatory approval and sufficient demand from one partner to sustain a decade-long program. This contradicts the stated risk that approval is pending.

Pre-mortem: The program launches, regulatory approval is delayed by three years, the single airline cancels due to financial distress, and the company faces insolvency due to unrecoverable development costs.

Blockers:
1. Insufficient order book: 34 units cannot fund a "very large" development program; the math fails.
2. Regulatory uncertainty: The core value proposition (twin-engine efficiency on long routes) relies on ungranted approval.

Risks: Design delays from committee input; rival capturing market share.

The proposal fails the basic financial and regulatory viability test. It requires a larger order book and regulatory clarity before launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
