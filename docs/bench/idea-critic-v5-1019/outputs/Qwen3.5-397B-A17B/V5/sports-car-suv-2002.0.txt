First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets the brand's core equity—sports performance and enthusiast loyalty—on a product that directly contradicts those values without a proven bridge to mitigate the betrayal risk.

Steelman: Leveraging the brand's prestige to enter the lucrative luxury SUV market via a high-performance, exclusive model will generate necessary margins while retaining brand allure through power and price.

However, the attack reveals a critical blocker. The proposal explicitly admits that loyal customers may view an SUV as a "betrayal." For a niche sports-car maker, the existing enthusiast base is the primary asset and revenue engine. Alienating this core group to chase a new, unproven segment dominated by established luxury giants is a fatal strategic error. The "heavy vehicle" risk directly undermines the "sports image" required to justify the high price point.

Alternatives analysis:
1. Do nothing: Preserves brand purity and current loyalty; misses market trend but survives.
2. License the badge only: Lower risk, but dilutes control.
3. The proposed co-developed SUV: High cost, high risk of core customer churn, and direct competition with entrenched rivals who already own the "luxury utility" space.

The pre-mortem is clear: The launch occurs, core enthusiasts boycott or resell their sports cars in protest, the brand loses its "sports" credibility, and the SUV fails to gain traction against established competitors because the badge no longer signifies performance purity. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of brand destruction.

The evidence provided in the risks section (betrayal, high cost, image damage, strong incumbents) constitutes a blocker. The plan relies on the assumption that "high price and power" override "brand betrayal," but the proposal offers no data to support this inversion for this specific demographic. Without a concrete mechanism to prevent the core base from feeling betrayed, the plan fails its own logic.

Blockers: Explicit risk of alienating the only guaranteed customer base; lack of evidence that price/power offsets brand dissonance.
Risks: Execution failure against incumbents; margin erosion from high development costs.

DECISION: NO-GO
