First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks for massive capital to solve a problem (short-form video) that the market already satisfies for free, while removing the primary consumption method (TV casting).

**Steelman:** Premium, phone-native storytelling can command a subscription if the quality gap versus social media is insurmountable and exclusive.

**Attack:**
1.  **Value Mismatch:** Consumers overwhelmingly prefer free, algorithmic short video (TikTok, YouTube) over paid, linear short video. Paying $15/month for content available for $0 elsewhere requires a moat this proposal does not define.
2.  **Distribution Handicap:** Launching in 2020 without TV app support ignores that "short" does not mean "mobile-only." Users expect to cast phone content to living room screens. Excluding TVs artificially caps engagement and perceived value.
3.  **Unit Economics:** High-budget production for <10 minute episodes creates a terrible cost-per-minute ratio compared to feature films or hour-long series. Recouping $1.75B requires unrealistic subscriber density given the low content volume per dollar spent.
4.  **Timing:** By Spring 2020, TikTok will be globally dominant. Entering as a paid walled garden against a free, addictive, infinite-scroll competitor is a strategic error.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Avoid burning $1.75B on a likely failed category pivot.
*   *Cheaper Path:* License existing short-form content or produce low-budget pilots to test willingness-to-pay before committing billions.
*   *Pivot:* Build a free, ad-supported network (FAST) to compete on volume, not a premium subscription service.

**Pre-mortem:** The service launches, gains initial curiosity, but churns immediately as users realize the library is small, expensive, and incompatible with their TVs. The high burn rate forces a fire sale within 18 months.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic:** High production costs for short formats prevent breakeven at realistic subscription prices.
*   **Product:** Lack of TV support at launch contradicts user behavior for premium video.
*   **Market:** Inability to differentiate value proposition against entrenched free competitors.

**Risks:** Brand damage from high-profile failure; opportunity cost of capital.

The proposal fails the basic test of product-market fit and unit economics. It solves no user pain point that isn't already solved better and cheaper.

DECISION: NO-GO
