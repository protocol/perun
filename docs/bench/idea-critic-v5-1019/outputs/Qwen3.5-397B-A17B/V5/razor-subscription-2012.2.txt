First call: GO. The low-cost, direct-to-consumer model attacks a clear inefficiency: dominant brands over-spend on advertising, inflating prices for a commodity product.

Steelman: By bypassing retail markups and ad spend, the venture can offer superior unit economics at a lower price point, using humor to generate low-cost viral awareness where traditional ads fail.

Options analysis:
1. Do nothing: Maintain status quo; consumers overpay, founders miss the shift to e-commerce.
2. Traditional retail launch: Prohibitively expensive due to slotting fees and required ad spend; high failure risk.
3. Proposed subscription model: Low capital requirement, immediate cash flow, direct customer relationship. It beats "do nothing" by capturing margin and beats traditional launch by reducing risk.

Fit: Aligns with a "challenger brand" strategy, leveraging digital native distribution to disrupt incumbents burdened by legacy cost structures.

Pre-mortem & Door Type:
Failure scenario: Supplier quality slips or incumbents launch a predatory pricing war.
Door: Two-way. If the video fails or supply breaks, the website shuts down with minimal loss. If it succeeds, scaling is iterative.
Cost of delay: High. First-mover advantage in "subscription for commodities" is critical before incumbents react or similar startups emerge.

Evidence & Assumptions:
Assumed: A reliable supplier exists at a cost allowing profitability at the proposed price.
Assumed: Humor translates to conversion for personal care items.
The proposal's own facts admit risks (supplier dependence, legal threat), but these are manageable risks, not blockers. Incumbents are slow to cut prices on core revenue drivers; lawsuits are unlikely against a small reseller initially. The "single video" risk is mitigated by the low cost of production; if it fails, the loss is contained.

Blockers: NONE. No fact presented proves the model cannot work. The risks cited are standard startup execution risks, not fundamental flaws.
Risks: Supplier concentration, incumbent retaliation, demand uncertainty. These become kill criteria: if customer acquisition cost exceeds lifetime value after the first video, or if supplier quality fails QC, the venture pivots or stops.

The plan is a classic low-fidelity test of a high-margin disruption. It requires minimal capital to validate the core hypothesis: will customers switch for price and convenience? The asymmetry of upside (massive market share) vs. downside (cost of one video and basic site) favors immediate action.

DECISION: GO
