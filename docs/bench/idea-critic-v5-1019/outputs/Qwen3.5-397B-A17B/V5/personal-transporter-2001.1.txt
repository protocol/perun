First call: NO-GO. The price point excludes the mass market required for urban replacement, and the regulatory environment is undefined.

Steelman: A revolutionary personal transporter that eliminates short car trips through superior maneuverability and zero emissions, commanding a premium for breakthrough technology.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids massive capital loss on an unproven category with hostile regulation.
2. Fleet-only B2B launch: Rent to tourists or security in controlled zones to bypass consumer price sensitivity and clarify laws.
3. Licensed tech sale: Sell the stabilization IP to established vehicle manufacturers to share development risk.

Fit: Assumes "urban mobility" is the north star, but a $5,000 device accessible only to early adopters fails to move the needle on mass car replacement.

Pre-mortem: The product launches widely, sits unsold due to cost, and is subsequently banned by city councils citing sidewalk safety and lack of classification, rendering inventory illegal. This is a one-way door with high sunk costs. A delay allows regulatory frameworks to mature.

Evidence: The proposal admits the price exceeds cars and bicycles, the learning curve is steep, and rules are non-existent. These are not risks; they are current facts contradicting the "sell widely" strategy.

Blockers:
1. Economic mismatch: The core claim is replacing car trips/walking, but the price ($5,000) exceeds a used car, making the primary value proposition impossible for the target volume.
2. Regulatory void: Selling widely without defined legal status invites immediate bans, destroying the business before it starts.

Risks: Learning curve adoption friction; lack of utility (cargo/passenger).

Kill criteria check: The economic blocker is fatal. No metric can save a product priced out of its intended use case while facing potential illegality. The plan must pivot to a controlled pilot or B2B model to validate demand and regulation before a consumer launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
