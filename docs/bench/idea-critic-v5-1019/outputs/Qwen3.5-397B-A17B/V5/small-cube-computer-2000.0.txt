First call: GO. The proposal targets a distinct, high-margin segment willing to pay for silence and aesthetics over raw expandability, leveraging the company's renewed brand equity.

**Steelman:** A premium, fanless desktop in a clear case serves professionals who prioritize acoustic silence and industrial design, creating a new category where the company commands a price premium despite lower raw specs.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Retain current tower/all-in-one mix; miss the emerging "lifestyle computing" trend.
2.  **Cheaper alternative:** Fan-cooled mini-tower; fails the silence requirement, commoditizes the product.
3.  **Proposal:** High-risk, high-reward category creation; beats "do nothing" by capturing non-upgrader revenue.

**Fit:** Aligns with a "recovered from brink" narrative requiring bold innovation to sustain momentum, not just incremental spec bumps.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if manufacturing defects in the acrylic case become visible showstoppers or if the core base refuses to pay a premium for a non-upgradeable machine. This is a two-way door; the line can be discontinued if sales miss targets, though tooling costs are sunk. Delay costs first-mover advantage in the nascent premium-small-form-factor market.

**Evidence:** The eleven profitable quarters provide the financial runway to absorb niche manufacturing complexity. The risks cited (cost, manufacturing difficulty, lack of slots) are inherent to the value proposition (silence, design), not contradictions of it.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks are trade-offs accepted by the target demographic, not factual errors in the plan. The higher price is justified by the unique form factor and silence, not raw performance.

**Risks:** Manufacturing yield on acrylic; market confusion over price vs. specs. These become kill criteria: if defect rates exceed 5% or initial sell-through is below 50% of the popular tower in Q1, pivot or cancel.

**Kill Criteria:** Stop production if first-quarter return rates exceed 8% due to case flaws or if unit sales fall below 40% of the entry-level tower volume. Leading metric: Gross margin per unit vs. return rate.

DECISION: GO
