First call: GO. The proposal converts a historical failure (standalone TV device) into a low-friction accessory by leveraging existing user hardware (phones/PCs) for control, directly addressing the "confusing buyer" risk without adding cost.

Steelman: A disposable, sub-$50 HDMI dongle that eliminates the cost and complexity of remotes and local UIs by offloading control to devices users already possess, seeding an ecosystem via an open developer kit to overcome initial app scarcity.

Options analysis:
1. **Do nothing:** Cede the living room to established boxes; lose relevance as TV viewing grows.
2. **Build a full box:** Repeat past failures; high upfront cost, inventory risk, and direct combat with incumbents on their strongest turf (app catalogs).
3. **The Dongle:** Lowest barrier to entry, minimal hardware margin required (subsidized by services), turns the "no apps" risk into a developer challenge rather than a pre-launch blocker.

Fit: Aligns with a search company's core asset (content discovery) rather than hardware manufacturing. It bypasses the need for a polished TV UI, which is not a core competency.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the casting protocol is laggy or if major content providers refuse to integrate with the open kit. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The "no remote" feature is a cost-saving constraint, not a defect, provided the mobile experience is seamless. A one-way door? No; unsold dongles are low-cost inventory, and the software layer can be updated or abandoned with minimal loss compared to a full console.

Evidence: The proposal explicitly cites poor sales of previous dedicated devices as the rationale for this pivot. The low price point is a strategic wedge, not a margin error. The "confusion" risk is mitigated by the ubiquity of smartphones in the target demographic.

Blockers: NONE. The objections raised (no remote, low margin, few apps) are inherent features of the strategy designed to bypass the true blocker: the high cost and complexity of previous attempts.
Risks: Slow developer adoption; casting latency. These become kill criteria: if developer kits do not yield 10+ major apps within 6 months, or if latency exceeds 2 seconds in beta, the project pivots.

DECISION: GO
