First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the brand's core identity without a mechanism to reconcile the conflict, risking alienation of the only existing revenue base.

**Steelman:** Leveraging the loyal enthusiast following to fund a high-margin expansion into luxury SUVs via partnership, using shared platforms to mitigate development risk while maintaining performance standards.

**Attack:**
1.  **Brand Betrayal (Blocker):** The proposal admits loyal customers may view an SUV as a betrayal. For a niche sports-car maker, the brand *is* the product. Alienating the core base to chase a new segment where established luxury makers dominate is suicidal. The proposal offers no narrative or product feature to convert enthusiasts or insulate the core brand.
2.  **Market Positioning:** Entering a crowded luxury SUV market in 2002 against entrenched competitors with a "high-priced" entry from a non-luxury-SUV brand is strategically weak. The "sports image" risk is not just reputational; it dilutes the unique selling proposition that drives current sales.
3.  **Financial Exposure:** High first-year costs combined with the risk of cannibalizing or destroying the core brand's equity creates a negative expected value. The shared platform reduces engineering cost but increases brand contamination risk.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserve brand equity and cash.
*   *Licensing:* Allow the larger partner to build the SUV under *their* badge, collecting royalties without brand risk.
*   *Delayed Entry:* Wait until the sports brand has sufficient capital and market dominance to dictate terms in the SUV segment.

**Pre-mortem:** The SUV launches, enthusiasts boycott the brand feeling sold out, sales of the core sports cars plummet due to image dilution, and the SUV fails to gain traction against established rivals. The company faces insolvency.

**Blockers:**
*   No strategy to prevent core customer alienation (stated risk).
*   No clear path to win against established luxury SUV makers with a diluted brand image.
*   High cost structure threatens the small manufacturer's survival if the bet fails.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, financial loss, partner dependency.

The proposal relies on hope that the brand stretches without breaking, despite admitting the fracture risk. Without a concrete plan to secure the core base or differentiate uniquely beyond "powerful engine," this is a speculative gamble with the company's existence.

DECISION: NO-GO
