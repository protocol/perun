First call: NO-GO. The unit economics fail immediately: a $10/month fee cannot cover even one $15 ticket, let alone "one film a day."

**Steelman:** Aggressively lower prices to dominate market share instantly, monetizing later via data and studio deals funded by deep-pocketed owners.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unit Economics:** At $10/month, a single member seeing two films costs the company ~$30+ in ticket fees (assuming average ticket >$15), creating an immediate $20+ loss per user. "Rapid growth" linearly scales this bleeding. The subsidy required is not operational support; it is paying strangers to watch movies at a massive loss per transaction.
2.  **Unproven Revenue:** The plan relies entirely on future data monetization and studio deals to offset current, guaranteed cash burn. These are speculative upside caps against uncapped downside liability. No evidence suggests data revenue can exceed the hundreds of dollars in monthly losses per heavy user.
3.  **Incentive Misalignment:** Theater chains receive full price but lose control over pricing and customer relationships. They have little incentive to cooperate if the platform dictates terms while relying on their inventory to sustain a loss-leading model.
4.  **Adverse Selection:** A $10/day-unlimited plan attracts only the heaviest users (the most expensive cohort), accelerating the burn rate. Light users subsidize nothing because the fee ($10) is below the cost of a single ticket.

**Blockers:**
*   **Mathematical Insolvency:** The core pricing mechanic ($10 fee vs. full ticket cost) guarantees a loss on every active user who watches more than zero films. The proposal admits the fee "does not cover the cost."
*   **Dependency on Speculation:** The business model functions only if unproven future revenue streams materialize quickly enough to outpace guaranteed, scalable cash burn.

**Risks:** Theater refusal, regulatory scrutiny on predatory pricing, inability to raise follow-on capital when burn rates become public.

**Kill Criteria:** N/A (Decision is No-Go).

The proposal asks to approve a mechanism where every unit sold increases the loss, betting on undefined future miracles to close the gap. This is not a strategy; it is a liquidation plan disguised as growth.

DECISION: NO-GO
