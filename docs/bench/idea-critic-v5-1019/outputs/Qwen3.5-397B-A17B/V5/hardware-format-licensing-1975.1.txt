First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores basic market dynamics where compatibility and content availability trump marginal technical superiority in consumer electronics.

Steelman: Proprietary control preserves margins and brand prestige while leveraging a temporary picture-quality lead to capture early adopters before the rival scales.

Attack: The "better picture" advantage is negligible to mainstream consumers compared to the inability to record a full movie (one-hour limit) or access a wide library of pre-recorded films. By restricting licenses, the proposal intentionally limits the install base. In format wars, the network effect is the primary driver of value; fewer players mean fewer titles, which means fewer buyers, creating a death spiral. The rival's strategy of broad licensing directly attacks the weakest link: content availability and hardware price. Relying on an "early lead" is futile if that lead is built on a format consumers cannot use for their primary use case (recording movies).

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Maintain status quo (no product launch).
2. Broad licensing: Sacrifice some per-unit margin to rapidly expand the install base and encourage title availability, matching the rival's distribution speed.
3. Hybrid: License broadly but charge higher royalties for the proprietary tech, prioritizing market share over immediate control.

Fit: The strategy contradicts the goal of becoming the standard. A proprietary format with limited content and hardware availability cannot win a format war against a ubiquitous, cheaper alternative.

Pre-mortem: The format fails because video rental stores refuse to stock tapes for a niche player, and consumers buy the rival machine to watch movies at home. The company is left with superior technology but zero market relevance.

Blockers:
1. The one-hour tape capacity fails the core user requirement of recording full films or sports events.
2. Restricting licenses ensures the rival will achieve lower prices and wider distribution, making adoption of this format irrational for consumers.

Risks: High price point, limited content library, consumer confusion. These are not just risks; given the constraints, they are certainties that block success.

DECISION: NO-GO
