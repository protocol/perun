First call: GO. The market leader has grown complacent on performance, creating a clear opening for a challenger to win back enthusiasts and professionals with superior core counts at aggressive price points, provided the execution is flawless.

**Steelman:** A desperate underdog must bypass incremental updates and leapfrog the incumbent with a radically efficient architecture, betting that multi-threaded workloads have matured enough to expose the rival's core-count weakness.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary fear is repeating past architectural failures. However, "do nothing" guarantees slow death as the performance gap widens. The alternative—incremental updates—fails to differentiate. The only viable path is the proposed leap, but it must be constrained. The risk of software not utilizing extra cores is mitigated by the rising tide of content creation and streaming tasks in 2017 which scale well. The financial risk is real but manageable if the initial launch is targeted strictly at the high-margin enthusiast segment to generate cash flow before mass-market expansion.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if the new architecture hits power/thermal walls similar to the previous generation, or if the rival slashes prices on their existing inventory to starve the new entrant of margin.

**Evidence & Constraints:** The proposal admits financial fragility. This dictates a "go-if" structure: the launch must be capped to a specific high-end segment first to verify yields and performance claims before full volume production.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws in the strategy. The market dynamic (incumbent stagnation) strongly favors a bold architectural shift.

**Risks:** Thermal inefficiency repeating past errors; aggressive price war from the rival; slower-than-expected software adoption. These become kill criteria: if early benchmarks show per-core performance lagging significantly behind the rival, or if power consumption exceeds thermal design limits by >15%, the mass-market rollout must be halted.

**Decision:** The strategic necessity of differentiating via core count outweighs the financial risk, provided the rollout is staged to limit exposure.

DECISION: GO
