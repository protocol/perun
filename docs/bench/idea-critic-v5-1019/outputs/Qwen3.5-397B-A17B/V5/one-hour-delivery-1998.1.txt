First call: NO-GO. The unit economics described guarantee that every small order loses money, and scaling this model multiplies losses rather than creating value.

**Steelman:** Rapid urban delivery of diverse goods creates a dominant consumer habit that justifies burning capital now to secure market share before competitors, assuming subsidies can be withdrawn later.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unit Economics:** Free delivery on small orders with no minimum means the cost of labor and vehicles exceeds revenue per transaction. The proposal admits this ("costs more than the margin"). Scaling simply increases the burn rate linearly with volume; there is no path to profitability without fundamentally changing the terms (minimums or fees), which contradicts the core value proposition.
2.  **Operational Leverage:** Employing staff directly in multiple cities creates massive fixed costs and management complexity immediately. Unlike asset-light models, this requires heavy capital expenditure for vehicles and payroll before proving demand.
3.  **Behavioral Risk:** If customers only use the service when subsidized, the "habit" being formed is dependency on free delivery, not loyalty to the brand. Removing subsidies likely collapses demand.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "do nothing" or "pilot in one city with fees/minimums" approach tests demand without guaranteeing insolvency. The current plan beats "do nothing" only in speed of failure.

**Pre-mortem:** The company runs out of cash within 18 months. Investors refuse further funding because the metric that matters (profit per order) is structurally negative. The attempt to raise fees triggers mass churn because the service has no intrinsic value beyond the subsidy.

**Blockers:**
*   **Structural Loss Leader:** The core mechanic (free delivery, no minimum) ensures negative margin on every unit sold. No volume solves a negative margin problem without infinite capital.
*   **Capital Intensity:** Direct employment and vehicle ownership in "major cities" (plural) requires unsustainable upfront capex for an unproven model.

**Risks:** Customer churn upon subsidy removal; regulatory hurdles for direct employment in multiple jurisdictions; logistics complexity of diverse inventory (games, coffee, books) requiring different storage/handling.

**Kill Criteria:** If a single city pilot cannot achieve positive contribution margin per order within 6 months by introducing minimum order values or delivery fees, the model is invalid.

The proposal asks to scale a known loss-maker. This is not a bet; it is a guaranteed liquidation event.

DECISION: NO-GO
