First call: GO. The proposal converts a historical failure (expensive, standalone TV devices) into a low-cost, phone-dependent accessory, directly addressing the primary cause of past poor sales while leveraging existing user habits.

Steelman: A sub-$35 HDMI dongle without a remote or on-screen UI forces reliance on the user's smartphone as the controller, drastically reducing hardware costs and complexity while enabling immediate content casting from familiar devices.

Options analysis:
1. **Do nothing:** Cede the living room to established competitors; risk becoming irrelevant as TV viewing shifts.
2. **Full TV Box:** High cost, high risk of repeating past failures, requires remote/UI development.
3. **Proposed Dongle:** Lowest cost, highest reversibility, leverages existing phone ecosystem. It beats "do nothing" by establishing a foothold with minimal capex.

Fit: Aligns with a search company's strength in organizing information on screens users already possess (phones/PCs) rather than building new interaction models.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if users cannot discover how to cast content, leading to "bricked" feeling devices. However, the open kit mitigates long-term app scarcity by allowing third-party innovation without direct vendor overhead. The lack of margin is a feature, not a bug; it creates a barrier to entry for competitors while monetizing via service usage, not hardware.

Evidence: The proposal explicitly cites the failure of previous "television devices" due to cost/complexity. This plan inverts those variables. The risk of "confusing buyers" is real but manageable via packaging and phone-side UX, not hardware changes.

Blockers: NONE. The stated risks (no remote, low margin, few apps) are inherent to the strategy's cost advantage and are not factual contradictions. Low margin is required to hit the "very cheap" price point that differentiates it from failed predecessors. "Few apps" is a launch state, solvable via the open kit.

Risks: User confusion during setup; slow developer adoption. These are execution risks, not blockers. Kill criteria: If activation rate < 60% within 90 days, pivot to including a basic IR blaster or revising onboarding.

The proposal correctly identifies that the barrier to TV adoption was the device model itself, not the content. By removing the remote and UI, it removes the cost and complexity that killed previous attempts.

DECISION: GO
