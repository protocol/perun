First call: NO-GO. The proposal requires customers to adopt expensive, single-purpose hardware for a rental model that offers inferior convenience and ownership compared to emerging or existing alternatives.

**Steelman:** A secure, high-capacity physical distribution network enabling flexible, time-based viewing rights without requiring constant internet connectivity, leveraging existing telephone infrastructure for authorization.

**Attack & Options:**
The claim fails on cost, friction, and market fit.
1.  **Cost & Friction:** Requiring users to buy specialized players *and* discs that phone home creates double friction. Standard players are cheaper; standard rentals (VHS/DVD) require no phone calls.
2.  **Consumer Psychology:** The "pay-per-view after 48 hours" model on a physical disc contradicts the primary value proposition of physical media: permanent ownership or simple flat-fee rental. Customers dislike recurring micro-transactions on hardware they bought.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" and wait for DVD adoption (cheaper players, no phone lines) or cable VOD (no new hardware) offers better upside with less risk. A "cheaper alternative" is standard DVD rental with late fees, which the market already accepts.
4.  **Fit:** In 1998, the north star is convenience and capacity (DVD). This proposal moves backward toward restriction and complexity.
5.  **Pre-mortem:** The format fails because studios refuse to fragment the market, electronics makers won't subsidize niche players, and consumers reject the "tethered" experience. The cost of delay is low; the cost of execution is massive.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Misalignment:** The proposal states players cost *more* than standard ones while offering *less* utility (no playback on existing devices, mandatory phone calls). This violates basic adoption curves for consumer electronics.
*   **Behavioral Mismatch:** The stated risk that "customers dislike paying for repeated viewing" is confirmed by the proposal's own mechanics, which mandate exactly that. There is no evidence provided that this specific pain point has been solved; the mechanism exacerbates it.

**Risks:**
*   Studio fragmentation.
*   High hardware barrier.

**Decision:**
The plan commits to a high-cost hardware rollout based on a usage model (recurring fees on physical media) that the proposal itself identifies as a customer dislike. No kill criteria can save a fundamental value proposition error.

DECISION: NO-GO
