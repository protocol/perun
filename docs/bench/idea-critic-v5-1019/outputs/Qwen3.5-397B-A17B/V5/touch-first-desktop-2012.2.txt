First call: NO-GO. Forcing a touch-first interface on keyboard/mouse users ignores the primary workflow of the dominant OS's existing base.

**Steelman:** Unifying the interface accelerates the tablet transition and forces app ecosystem modernization.
**Attack:** The proposal mandates a "full-screen" start screen for *all* PCs, removing the traditional menu. This directly contradicts the stated risk that desktop users will find it confusing and costly to retrain. Unlike a dual-mode option, this removes the known efficient workflow for the core revenue-generating segment (enterprise/desktop) to chase a nascent tablet market.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Retain desktop dominance; lose some tablet share.
2.  **Proposed:** Force full-screen on all. High risk of enterprise churn; massive support costs.
3.  **Better way:** Offer the touch interface as an optional mode or distinct "tablet mode" triggered by hardware, preserving the start menu for mouse/keyboard users. This captures tablet upside without alienating the installed base.

**Fit:** Assumes the goal is total UI unification rather than market retention. If the north star is "tablet share at any cost," it fits; if it is "overall profitability," it likely fails due to support/training backlash.

**Pre-mortem:** Enterprise IT departments reject the upgrade due to productivity loss and training costs, stalling adoption and fragmenting the user base between those who can revert and those forced into the new UI. Developers hesitate because the "forced" user base is hostile, not engaged.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits "no easy way to go back." This creates a one-way door with high reversal costs. The risks listed (confusion, support cost, developer buy-in) are not mitigated; they are amplified by removing the fallback.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Self-contradiction:** The plan ignores its own cited risk of high corporate training costs by removing the familiar interface entirely.
2.  **Missing alternative:** It fails to consider a hybrid approach that serves both form factors, which would achieve the app store goal without the UX penalty.

**Risks:** Developer fragmentation, enterprise rejection, brand damage.

**Kill Criteria:** If enterprise upgrade rates drop below 50% in the first quarter, or support ticket volume regarding navigation triples, the rollout must halt and revert to a dual-mode interface.

DECISION: NO-GO
