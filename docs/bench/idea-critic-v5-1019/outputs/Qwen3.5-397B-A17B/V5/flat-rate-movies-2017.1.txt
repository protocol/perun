First call: NO-GO. The unit economics fail immediately: a $10/month fee cannot cover even one full-price ticket, let alone "one film a day."

**Steelman:** Aggressively subsidize user growth with a $10/month unlimited plan to achieve market dominance, betting that future data monetization and studio deals will eventually outweigh massive per-user losses.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unit Economics:** The proposal admits the company pays "full ticket price" while charging ~$10. If a single ticket costs >$10 (standard in 2017), every single usage generates an immediate cash loss. A user seeing one film a day creates a liability of ~$300/month against $10 revenue. No amount of growth fixes a model where marginal cost vastly exceeds marginal revenue.
2.  **Unproven Revenue:** The plan relies entirely on "data and deals" to rescue the business. These are speculative future hopes, not current assets. Building a massive loss-making operation on the hope of future monetization is a failure pattern, not a strategy.
3.  **Supply Side Risk:** Theater chains are explicitly noted as potentially refusing cooperation. If they reject the model because it devalues their tickets or creates accounting nightmares, the service has no product to sell, regardless of demand.
4.  **Adverse Selection:** At $10 for unlimited films, the only rational customers are heavy users who will maximize the service, guaranteeing maximum loss per member. Light users get no value; heavy users bankrupt the firm.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fatal Math:** Revenue ($10) < Cost of Goods Sold (1+ tickets at full price). This is not a risk; it is a mathematical certainty of failure under the stated terms.
*   **Dependency:** The core value proposition requires theater cooperation, which is flagged as unlikely.

**Risks:**
*   Data monetization may never materialize.
*   Cash subsidy runs out before "rapid growth" yields leverage.

The "cheaper alternative" is to price above the break-even point or limit usage strictly, but the proposal explicitly rejects this by setting the price at $10 for daily use. As proposed, this burns cash with no viable path to profitability.

DECISION: NO-GO
