First call: GO. The proposal directly attacks the previous generation's failures (late, expensive, hard to develop for) by flipping each constraint: lower price, PC-like ease, and consumer-friendly usage rights.

Steelman: By pricing below the rival and removing friction for developers and users (used games, offline play), the console can regain market share despite lower per-unit margins, relying on volume and software ecosystem growth to restore profitability.

Options analysis:
1. **Do nothing:** Continue losing ground to the rival; likely fatal given the current trajectory.
2. **Premium strategy:** Match rival features/price. Risks repeating the "expensive/late" failure without a differentiator.
3. **Proposed path:** Sacrifice margin for adoption. Best reversibility; price can be raised later, but market share lost now is gone forever.

Fit: Aligns with the urgent need to stop bleeding users. The "north star" here is survival and developer mindshare, which this approach prioritizes over immediate hardware profit.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the lower price signals "cheap/weak" rather than "value," or if the lack of first-party launch titles cannot be offset by third-party ease-of-use. However, the rival's strong online lead is a high barrier; forcing a price war and removing used-game restrictions are the only viable wedges.

Evidence & Math: The proposal admits margin reduction but implies volume compensation. Without specific numbers, we must assume the maker has the cash reserves to sustain lower margins until scale is reached. The risk of "consumers spending less" actually supports a lower-price entry point.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (margin, online services, software lineup) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The lack of first-party software is mitigated by the "easy for developers" claim, aiming to flood the gap with third-party content.

Risks: Lower margin sustainability; slow online service catch-up. These become kill criteria: if unit sales do not exceed the rival's by 20% within 12 months, or if developer adoption lags, the strategy must pivot.

The plan addresses the root causes of the previous failure. Waiting for perfect conditions (strong software, online parity) guarantees continued loss.
