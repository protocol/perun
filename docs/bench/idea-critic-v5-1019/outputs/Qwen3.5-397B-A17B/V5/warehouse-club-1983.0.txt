First call: NO-GO. Entering a mature market against a dominant incumbent with a capital-intensive model and unproven customer willingness to pay fees is financially reckless without evidence of differentiation.

**Steelman:** The model succeeds only if membership fees create a psychological lock-in that drives volume sufficient to offset razor-thin product margins, despite the competitor's head start.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** High capex expansion; relies entirely on fee adoption. Risk: Catastobic cash burn if fees fail.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid direct confrontation; preserve capital. Risk: Missed opportunity if the niche is underserved.
3.  **Pilot Single Site:** Open one location to validate fee conversion before scaling. Risk: Slower growth, but prevents systemic failure.

**Fit:** Assumes a large segment values bulk pricing enough to pre-pay for access. No evidence provided that the incumbent has alienated this specific demographic.

**Pre-mortem:** The venture fails because shoppers refuse the upfront fee, viewing it as a barrier rather than a benefit. With thin margins, operating costs exceed revenue before membership volume breaks even. The competitor undercuts prices temporarily to starve the new entrant. This is a **one-way door**; once capital is sunk into large warehouses, exit is costly.

**Evidence:** All figures are `assumed`. The claim that shoppers will pay a fee contradicts the risk that they won't; no data resolves this. The assertion that "more warehouses" is the solution ignores the prerequisite: proof of unit economics at a single site.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Unproven Core Premise:** The entire profit structure rests on membership fees, yet the proposal admits shoppers may not pay them. Scaling before validating this is a fatal flaw.
2.  **Capital Disadvantage:** Competing on price against a cash-rich incumbent with thin margins leaves no buffer for error or price wars.

**Risks:** Execution complexity, site availability, bulk-buying behavior shifts. These are secondary to the blocker.

**Kill Criteria:** If a single pilot location does not achieve 80% membership renewal within 12 months, the model is invalid.

**Decision:** The proposal demands immediate scaling ("open more warehouses") without validating the single most critical variable: fee acceptance. This violates basic capital preservation principles.

DECISION: NO-GO
