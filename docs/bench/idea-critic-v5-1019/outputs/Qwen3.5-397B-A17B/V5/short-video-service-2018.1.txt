First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and distribution constraints cited in the proposal's own risk assessment contradict the path to breakeven.

Steelman: A premium, mobile-first short-form service can capture high-value attention if exclusive talent and vertical formatting create a defensible niche distinct from free social clips.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test on three fronts derived strictly from its stated facts:
1.  **Value Mismatch:** The core claim relies on users paying for content where a free, popular substitute already exists (social short video). The proposal admits viewers "may not pay," yet commits $1.75 billion before validating willingness to pay. This is a fundamental demand risk treated as an assumption rather than a validated fact.
2.  **Distribution Handicap:** Launching without TV support in 2020 ignores the primary consumption mode for "high-budget" narrative content. Restricting a premium product to phones while competing against free, ubiquitous mobile video removes the only potential advantage (quality) from its most natural display environment.
3.  **Financial Impossibility:** High production budgets require massive scale to recoup. The combination of a mobile-only constraint (limiting total addressable market) and unproven willingness to pay makes the $1.75 billion burn rate mathematically unsustainable before breakeven. The stated risk that "budgets leave a lot to recoup" is not a risk; given the other constraints, it is a near-certain outcome.

Alternatives: A "do nothing" approach avoids the $1.75 billion loss. A "cheaper alternative" would be a low-budget pilot in one market to test the "mobile-only payment" hypothesis before scaling. The current plan beats neither; it assumes success where the stated facts suggest failure.

Pre-mortem: The service launches, fails to convert free social users to paid subscribers due to the lack of TV utility, burns through capital on expensive content that cannot find an audience large enough to cover costs, and shuts down before the second season.

Blockers:
- Unproven demand for paid mobile-only video against free competitors.
- Distribution strategy (no TV) contradicts high-budget content norms.
- Capital commitment ($1.75B) exceeds validated learning.

Risks: Execution difficulty, talent availability.

DECISION: NO-GO
