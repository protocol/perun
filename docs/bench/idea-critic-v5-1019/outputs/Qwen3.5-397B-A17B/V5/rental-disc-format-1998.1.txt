First call: NO-GO. The proposal requires customers to adopt expensive, single-purpose hardware for a rental model that contradicts established consumer preferences for ownership or flat-fee unlimited access.

Steelman: A secure, time-limited rental ecosystem could theoretically reduce late fees and piracy while offering studios recurring revenue.

Attack:
1. **Hardware Friction:** Requiring a specialized player that costs *more* than standard players creates an insurmountable barrier to entry. Consumers will not buy a $200+ device just to rent movies when VHS/DVD players are cheap and ubiquitous.
2. **Consumer Psychology:** The model charges per view after 48 hours. In 1998, the market is shifting toward "buy-to-own" (DVD) or flat-fee rentals. Charging repeated fees for the same disc creates "bill shock" and intense resentment, unlike the predictable cost of a rental store visit.
3. **Coordination Risk:** Success depends on simultaneous adoption by studios (content) and electronics makers (hardware). Without a dominant player forcing the standard, this chicken-and-egg problem usually kills new formats.
4. **Connectivity:** Requiring a phone line for every playback verification is technically fragile and inconvenient for users, increasing support costs and friction.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Continue selling/renting existing formats; margins are stable.
*   **Cheaper Path:** Partner with existing rental chains to digitize inventory management rather than replacing physical media and players.
*   **Better Model:** Subscription-based streaming (though nascent) or flat-fee disc rentals without hardware locks.

Pre-mortem: The format launches, studios hesitate to license content due to low install base, consumers refuse to buy the expensive player, and the system is abandoned within 18 months as a costly failure. The "security" feature becomes the primary reason for rejection.

Blockers:
*   **Hardware Cost:** Players cost more than standard units, guaranteeing low adoption.
*   **Value Mismatch:** Repeated viewing fees contradict the consumer desire for predictable pricing or ownership.
*   **Ecosystem Dependency:** Requires impossible coordination of competitors before any value exists.

Risks: High execution complexity, potential for rapid obsolescence if internet speeds improve.

The proposal fails the basic economic test: it asks the customer to pay more (hardware + recurring fees) for less convenience (phone line, no standard playback) compared to current options.

DECISION: NO-GO
