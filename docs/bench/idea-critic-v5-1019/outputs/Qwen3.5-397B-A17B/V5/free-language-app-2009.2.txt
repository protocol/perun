First call: NO-GO. The plan explicitly defers revenue validation while incurring full build costs, relying on an unproven mechanism (learner translations) to eventually fund free access.

**Steelman:** A free, crowd-sourced language platform can achieve scale and quality sufficient to monetize via B2B translation services, sustaining free education without upfront fees.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits the revenue model is "unproven" and the core product (learner translations) faces a quality deficit against "polished" incumbents. In 2009, machine translation was nascent, and crowdsourcing quality control required significant engineering overhead not accounted for. Building first and "looking for revenue later" violates basic unit economics: if the asset (translation) is defective, no market exists to subsidize the user.
2.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "pilot first" alternative is superior. Before building a full web app, validate if publishers will buy *unverified* learner translations. A manual concierge test (professor/student manually translating a small batch and attempting sale) costs near zero and proves demand. The current plan skips this, risking total capital loss on a product with no confirmed buyer.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The project fails because the dual-sided market collapses: learners leave due to inferior tools compared to paid competitors, and publishers reject low-quality translations. Without revenue, the nonprofit structure deemed "unsustainable" becomes the only option, leading to shutdown.
4.  **Blocker:** The proposal contains a internal contradiction: it rejects a nonprofit as unsustainable yet proposes a model with no immediate revenue path. This is a factual blocker; the math does not work as stated. The risk of poor quality is not merely a "risk" but a likely outcome that destroys the value proposition entirely.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal lacks a specific metric to validate translation quality or publisher interest before build. It commits to high fixed costs (building the app) before verifying the variable value (sale of work).

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. The assumption that revenue can be found later is contradicted by the admitted unproven nature of the model and the quality gap.

Blockers: Revenue model admitted unproven; core asset quality likely insufficient for sale; "build first" approach guarantees sunk costs without validated demand.
Risks: User churn to polished competitors; inability to monetize low-quality translations.

DECISION: NO-GO
