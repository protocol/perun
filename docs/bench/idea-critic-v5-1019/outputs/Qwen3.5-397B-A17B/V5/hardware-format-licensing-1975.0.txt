First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores the fundamental driver of this market: content availability and recording duration, not just picture quality.

**Steelman:** Proprietary control preserves margins and brand prestige while leveraging a technical lead in picture quality to secure an early niche before competitors mature.

**Attack:**
1.  **Duration Mismatch:** A one-hour tape cannot record a full movie or major sports event. Consumers will reject a format that requires swapping tapes mid-feature, regardless of picture clarity. The rival's longer tapes solve the primary use case.
2.  **Content Starvation:** Limiting licenses to a few partners restricts hardware proliferation. Without widespread players, studios will not release pre-recorded titles for this format. Without titles, consumers will not buy players. This is a classic chicken-and-egg failure.
3.  **Standardization Risk:** The rival's strategy of broad licensing creates a network effect. If they become the de facto standard due to lower prices and higher volume, the superior technical quality of the proprietary format becomes irrelevant (the "better mousetrap" fallacy). History shows the market often chooses "good enough" and ubiquitous over "superior" and rare.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Maintain status quo (no VCR).
*   **Broad Licensing:** License the format widely to flood the market, sacrificing per-unit margin for ecosystem dominance and content support.
*   **Hybrid:** License broadly but retain a premium tier for high-end components.

**Pre-mortem:** In two years, the rival format is in 80% of homes. Video rental stores stock only their tapes. Our superior picture quality is a footnote in a museum of failed formats because no one can buy a movie to watch on our machine.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** One-hour capacity < Typical movie length (90+ mins). This fails the core user requirement.
*   **Fact:** Restricted licensing reduces hardware install base, directly preventing content adoption.

**Risks:** Price wars, margin erosion (mitigated by volume).

The proposal relies on technical superiority overcoming ecosystem deficits. The evidence suggests the opposite: ecosystem size dictates survival. The one-hour limit is a fatal functional flaw for the primary use case.

DECISION: NO-GO
