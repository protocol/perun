First call: NO-GO. The core claim—that a mass-market brand can command premium pricing for a luxury sedan without a distinct luxury image—contradicts the stated risk that buyers pay for the badge, not just the hardware.

Steelman: Leveraging existing manufacturing scale to undercut premium rivals on price while matching their feature set could capture value-conscious luxury buyers.

Attack: The proposal ignores the fundamental driver of the luxury segment: status signaling via brand heritage. A "very large luxury sedan" under a mass-market name creates cognitive dissonance; it signals "expensive mass-market car," not "entry luxury." The stated risk that "luxury buyers may not accept a mass-market name" is not a hypothetical risk but a verified market constraint in the premium sector. Building a dedicated factory for low-volume production destroys the cost advantage of the mass-market parent, making the unit economics untenable without the premium margin that the brand cannot command.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital and brand equity; avoid diluting the mass-market position.
2. Create a sub-brand: Isolate the luxury identity (e.g., Lexus, Acura) to allow premium pricing without badge contamination.
3. Joint venture/Acquisition: Buy an existing luxury marque to inherit the badge value immediately.

Fit: This contradicts the implicit north star of brand equity preservation. Entering premium requires a premium perception, which the stated facts confirm the current brand lacks.

Pre-mortem: The factory opens, but sales are 20% of forecast because fleet buyers dominate the mix, destroying residual values and scaring off private luxury buyers. The brand becomes associated with "discount luxury," hurting core sales.

Blockers:
1. Brand Perception: The proposal admits the brand has no luxury image, yet relies on it to sustain premium pricing. This is a direct contradiction.
2. Economics: Dedicated factory + low volume + mass-market badge = negative margin potential.

Risks:
1. Cannibalization of core brand value.
2. Failure to achieve scale in the new factory.

The absence of a sub-brand strategy or a plan to manufacture badge equity makes this venture structurally flawed. The hardware (engine, suspension) is necessary but insufficient; the software (brand) is missing.

DECISION: NO-GO
