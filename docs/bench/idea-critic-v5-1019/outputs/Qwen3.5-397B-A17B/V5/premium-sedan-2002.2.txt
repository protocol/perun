First call: NO-GO. The core claim—that a mass-market brand can command premium pricing for a luxury sedan without a distinct luxury identity—contradicts the stated risk that buyers pay for the badge, which is the primary driver of luxury value.

**Steelman:** A superior product (large engine, advanced suspension, top materials) alone can overcome brand stigma and justify premium pricing globally.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits the brand lacks a luxury image and buyers prioritize the "badge." In the luxury segment, the brand *is* the product. Without a separate marque or decades of brand equity building, the "mass-market" name actively devalues the "top-quality materials," creating a cognitive dissonance that premium buyers will reject.
2.  **Options:** The "do nothing" or "create a sub-brand" alternatives are superior. Building a dedicated factory for low-volume production under a weak brand locks in high fixed costs with low probability of sales. Established rivals beat this on both brand prestige and economies of scale.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: The car launches, critics praise the hardware, but sales flatline because corporate fleet buyers and status-conscious individuals refuse to be seen in a "mass-market" vehicle at a luxury price. The dedicated factory becomes a stranded asset.
4.  **Blocker:** The proposal's own facts state that "luxury buyers may not accept a mass-market name" and "buyers pay for the badge." This is a confirmed negative that removes the route to the upside (premium pricing). No amount of hardware quality fixes a brand perception blocker in the luxury category. This is not a risk; it is a stated market reality that the plan ignores.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The core claim (buyers will pay) is contradicted by the proposal's own evidence (buyers pay for the badge, not just hardware). The commitment (dedicated factory) is large and irreversible.

**Decision:**
Blockers: The brand name prevents premium pricing; dedicated factory creates unsustainable unit economics at low volume.
Risks: Global launch complexity; competitor retaliation.

The plan fails because it attempts to solve a branding problem with engineering. You cannot build a luxury reputation solely on specifications while wearing a mass-market label.

DECISION: NO-GO
