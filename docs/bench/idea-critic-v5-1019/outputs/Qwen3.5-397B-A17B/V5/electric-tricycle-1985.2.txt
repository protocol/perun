First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and safety profile presented make mass mail-order sales in January commercially suicidal.

**Steelman:** A sub-£400, license-free trike leverages a major assembler to dominate the low-end commuter market via direct sales.

**Attack:**
1.  **Seasonal Mismatch:** Launching a vehicle with "no weather protection" and "short range" via mail order in January ignores British winter realities. Rain, ice, and cold will immediately suppress demand and generate returns from buyers who cannot use the product as intended.
2.  **Safety & Liability:** A low-sitting vehicle at 15mph mixed with traffic invites severe safety objections. Without a pilot program to validate safety data, a mass launch exposes the entrepreneur to immediate regulatory bans or lawsuits if accidents occur.
3.  **Channel Risk:** Mail order requires customer confidence. Selling a complex mechanical device with known limitations (speed, range, exposure) without a dealer network for test rides or service creates a high probability of mass dissatisfaction and brand failure upon first contact.
4.  **Economics:** At £400 with "optional extras," the margin is likely too thin to absorb the return logistics of a seasonal product failure.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Preserve capital until a weather-protected model or spring launch.
*   *Pilot:* Limited regional sale in spring to gather safety/usage data before national mail order.
*   *Dealer Network:* Partner with bike shops for assembly and test rides, increasing price but reducing returns.

**Pre-mortem:** By March, the company is insolvent due to a flood of returns from users unable to ride in rain/snow, compounded by negative press from safety groups citing winter accidents. The assembler halts production due to unpaid invoices.

**Blockers:**
*   **Seasonality:** Launching an exposed vehicle in January guarantees poor initial user experiences and returns.
*   **Liability:** Mass distribution without verified safety data for a novel vehicle class is an uninsurable risk.

**Risks:** Low speed limiting utility; battery range anxiety.

**Kill Criteria:** If a 50-unit spring pilot yields >15% return rate or any serious injury reports, terminate the project.

DECISION: NO-GO
