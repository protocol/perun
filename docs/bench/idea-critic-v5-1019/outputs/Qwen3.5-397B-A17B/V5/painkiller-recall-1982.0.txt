First call: GO. The stated facts describe an unknown actor poisoning products post-factory in one metro area, creating an immediate, unquantifiable risk of death for consumers nationwide. As an operator, the only defensible move today is to prioritize human life over short-term financial metrics or market share.

Steelman: A nationwide withdrawal and production halt are the only actions that guarantee no further deaths occur while the scope of contamination is unknown, preserving the brand's long-term viability through demonstrated responsibility.

Alternatives considered:
1. **Do nothing/Local recall only:** Risks additional deaths if poisoned bottles traveled or if the actor expands. Cost: Potential loss of all future trust and massive liability.
2. **Wait for evidence:** Delays protection; unacceptable when the failure mode is fatality.
3. **Proposed plan:** High immediate financial cost and market share risk, but reversible upon relaunch with safer packaging. It beats "do nothing" because the upside of survival outweighs the cost of the recall.

Fit: Aligns with the fundamental north star of any consumer health company: do no harm. A brand cannot exist if its users are dead.

Pre-mortem: If we proceed and the poisoning was truly isolated to one city, we lose millions and competitors gain shelf space. However, if we do not proceed and another person dies, the brand is permanently toxic. The cost of delay is measured in potential fatalities.

Evidence: The proposal states the poisoning occurred after leaving the factory and the perpetrator is unknown. This confirms the company cannot vouch for the safety of any bottle currently in the wild. The risks raised (cost, market share, signaling danger) are valid financial concerns but are secondary to the immediate threat to life.

Blockers: NONE. No fact provided suggests the recall would cause more harm than the poisoning itself. The financial risks are severe but do not block the moral and operational imperative to stop sales.

Risks: Significant financial loss, permanent market share erosion, competitor encroachment. These are managed by the planned relaunch in tamper-resistant packaging, which turns the crisis into a industry-leading safety standard.

Kill criteria: If evidence emerges proving the contamination is physically impossible outside the specific metro area and all bottles there are accounted for, the scope could be reduced. Until then, the nationwide halt stands.

DECISION: GO
