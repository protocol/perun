First call: NO-GO. The proposal requires customers to adopt expensive, single-purpose hardware for a rental model that contradicts established consumer preferences for ownership or simple flat-fee rentals, while relying on fragile coordination between studios and hardware makers.

**Steelman:** A secure, time-limited rental ecosystem enabled by phone-verified players could unlock new revenue from back-catalog titles without cannibalizing sales, provided the hardware subsidy is managed.

**Attack:**
1.  **Friction vs. Value:** Customers already reject paying repeatedly for viewing (as noted in risks). Adding a mandatory phone call to a specialized, expensive player creates massive friction compared to the emerging convenience of DVD ownership or impending digital solutions. The "few dollars" disc cost is irrelevant if the player barrier is high.
2.  **Coordination Failure:** Success requires simultaneous alignment of studios (content), electronics makers (hardware), and retailers (distribution). History shows such consortia often fracture over licensing fees and hardware standards. If one major studio or player manufacturer opts out, the format dies.
3.  **Dead-End Technology:** A player that *only* plays these restricted discs and cannot play standard discs is a non-starter for consumers who already own or plan to buy standard players. It forces a duplicate hardware purchase for a subset of content.
4.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "wait" option is stronger. The market is shifting toward DVD ownership (no expiration, plays on standard players) and early digital experimentation. A cheaper, simpler path is licensing existing DVD players with a software-based rental window (if possible) or waiting for broadband distribution, rather than building a proprietary phone-linked hardware moat.

**Blockers:**
*   **Hardware Incompatibility:** The requirement for a special player that cannot play standard discs is a fatal adoption barrier. Consumers will not buy a single-use device when a multi-use device exists at a similar or lower price point.
*   **Negative Unit Economics:** High player cost + repeated customer fees + phone line dependency creates a value proposition inferior to buying the disc outright or waiting for next-gen distribution.

**Risks:** Studio fragmentation, consumer rejection of "rental fatigue," rapid obsolescence by broadband.

The proposal fails because it solves a distribution problem with a hardware constraint that lowers consumer utility. The friction of the phone call and the cost of the specialized player outweigh the benefit of time-limited access.

DECISION: NO-GO
