First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits massive capital to a localized event without verified scope, risking permanent brand erosion and ceding market share to competitors based on fear rather than confirmed systemic failure.

Steelman: Immediate nationwide withdrawal and production halt are the only actions sufficient to guarantee public safety and preserve long-term trust, accepting short-term financial devastation as the cost of survival.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing locally: Risks immediate deaths and total brand collapse if poisoning spreads; cheapest but existential risk.
2. Regional recall only: Targets the affected metro area; balances cost and safety but risks perception of negligence if cases appear elsewhere.
3. Full nationwide recall (proposed): Maximum safety signal, maximum financial loss, highest chance of competitors permanently displacing the product.

Fit: Assumes the brand's north star is "absolute safety" over profitability. If the star is "market leadership," this approach fails by surrendering shelf space.

Pre-mortem: The company halts production, spends millions on a recall, and relaunches months later only to find the poisoning was an isolated criminal act in one city. Competitors have locked in exclusive contracts during the gap. The brand returns as a "dangerous" product in consumers' minds due to the scale of the recall, never recovering its #1 position. The cost of delay (relaunch time) allows rivals to own the category.

Evidence: The proposal states plants are unaffected and the event is in one metropolitan area. No evidence suggests a manufacturing defect or widespread contamination. The "unknown person" implies criminal tampering, not product failure.

Blockers:
1. The proposal treats a localized criminal act as a systemic manufacturing failure without evidence. The facts state plants are unaffected; therefore, a nationwide production halt addresses no root cause and incurs unnecessary ruinous costs.
2. The plan guarantees competitors take shelf space. With no proof of widespread danger, surrendering the entire national market is a disproportionate response that ensures the very market share loss it fears.

Risks: Brand perception of danger (mitigated by targeted communication), recurrence in other cities (mitigated by rapid monitoring).

The proposal fails the proportionality test. A regional recall with aggressive public warning in the affected metro, coupled with immediate tamper-resistant packaging development for a fast relaunch, addresses the safety threat without the suicide pill of a total national shutdown.

DECISION: NO-GO
