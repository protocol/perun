First call: GO. Beating a rival's September launch to capture the holiday quarter and mindshare justifies the aggressive August target; delay cedes the narrative.

Steelman: Releasing a differentiated, high-spec device in August pre-empts the competitor, maximizing sales volume before their announcement dilutes market attention.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing (wait for normal cycle): Safe, but yields the "first mover" advantage to the rival.
2. Delay to September: Matches rival but loses the preemptive strike; marketing noise doubles.
3. Proceed as proposed: Highest risk, but only path to dominant Q4 positioning.

Fit: Aligns with the goal of maintaining flagship leadership and volume growth.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the battery swells or the iris scanner fails in humidity, triggering a global recall that destroys brand trust and costs more than the lost sales. This is a two-way door for features, but a one-way door for reputation. Cost of delay: Losing the "first large-screen with iris" headline and ~15% of projected Q4 volume.

Evidence: The proposal admits the risks (less testing, new tech in thin body) but offers no mitigation data. The claim that it can ship "in volume worldwide" contradicts the admission of "less time for testing" for untested components.

Blockers:
1. Contradictory premises: The plan demands global volume shipment while explicitly stating there is insufficient time to test the new, complex integration (larger battery in thinner chassis + new sensor). You cannot verify reliability for a global launch without the testing time you just removed.
2. Catastrophic downside: A defect in a new battery chemistry or biometric sensor at global scale is an existential brand event, not a mere cost. The proposal lacks a "kill criterion" or a staged rollout (e.g., limited markets first) to validate the hardware before worldwide exposure.

Risks: Supply chain yield issues, sensor false rejects, battery thermal events.

The plan fails the blocker test: it commits to an irreversible global scale before validating the very risks that could destroy the product line. A staged launch or proven reliability data is required before a "GO."

DECISION: NO-GO
