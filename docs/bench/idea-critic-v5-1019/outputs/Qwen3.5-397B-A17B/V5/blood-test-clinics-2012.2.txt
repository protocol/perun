First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits irreversible capital to unproven technology with no independent validation.

Steelman: Partnering offers a first-mover advantage in accessible, low-cost diagnostics if the device functions as claimed. However, the claim rests entirely on the founder's assertion without published studies or shared methods.

Alternatives: Doing nothing preserves capital and reputation. A small-scale pilot with strict performance milestones costs less and validates the tech before full rollout. Licensing the tech without exclusivity reduces risk. The proposed $350M upfront investment beats none of these on risk-adjusted returns.

Fit: This diverges from a supermarket's core competency (retail logistics) into high-liability medical diagnostics without internal expertise. It assumes regulatory approval is guaranteed, which contradicts the stated risk that regulators may question the tests.

Pre-mortem: The device fails accuracy standards in real-world conditions. Regulators ban the tests due to lack of transparency. The chain faces massive lawsuits from false positives/negatives, reputational destruction, and stranded assets in unused clinics. This is a one-way door; once built, clinics cannot be easily repurposed, and the brand damage is permanent.

Evidence: The proposal admits zero published studies, no method sharing, and acknowledged regulatory uncertainty. The $350M figure is a massive commitment based on "trust us."

Blockers:
1. **No Validation:** The core claim (device works) is unsupported by any external evidence or data.
2. **Irreversibility:** $350M is a sunk cost before the first test is verified.
3. **Capability Gap:** The chain lacks medical expertise to manage the operational or legal fallout of errors.

Risks: Regulatory rejection, technical failure, brand erosion. These are not mere risks here; they are probable outcomes given the lack of evidence.

The "kill check" confirms this is a blocker: The core claim (buyers/patients will get accurate results) has no supporting facts, the commitment is too large to stop once started, and the downside (harm to patients, lawsuits) is catastrophic.

DECISION: NO-GO
