First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the brand's core identity without a mechanism to preserve it.

**Steelman:** A high-margin SUV leverages the brand's performance heritage to fund future sports cars while retaining enthusiasts through exclusive engineering.

**Attack:**
1.  **Identity Betrayal:** The primary asset is the "loyal enthusiast following." For a niche sports-car maker, an SUV is not an extension; it is a dilution. Unlike mass-market brands, niche players cannot absorb reputation damage. If the core base perceives this as a "betrayal," revenue from the existing small range collapses, negating SUV profits. The proposal admits this risk but offers no mitigation (e.g., limited edition, distinct sub-brand, or explicit "halo" narrative).
2.  **Competitive Moat:** Entering the "large luxury crossover" segment against "established luxury SUV makers" with a "first-year cost [that] is high" is financially suicidal for a small manufacturer. They lack the volume to amortize R&D and the marketing budget to displace incumbents.
3.  **Image Contamination:** A "heavy vehicle" directly undermines the "sports image." Without a proven strategy to decouple the SUV's mass from the brand's agility ethos, the entire product line suffers.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve brand equity and cash; risky if the sports market shrinks, but safe from immediate reputation collapse.
*   **License the Platform:** Allow the larger group to build the SUV under *their* badge, collecting royalties without brand risk.
*   **Small Runabout:** Develop a smaller, agile urban vehicle that aligns with sports dynamics rather than fighting them.

**Pre-mortem:** The SUV launches, loyalists boycott the brand, sales of the core sports cars drop 40%, and the high development costs of the SUV bankrupt the firm within three years.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Strategic Contradiction:** The plan targets a segment (large luxury SUV) that inherently conflicts with the stated brand promise (sports car) without a defined bridge to prevent alienating the only existing customer base.
2.  **Resource Mismatch:** High entry costs against established incumbents with a small balance sheet is a factual path to insolvency.

**Risks:** Execution failure, brand dilution, capital exhaustion. These are not merely risks; given the lack of mitigation for the identity clash, they are probable outcomes.

**Kill Criteria:** If brand sentiment tracking among current owners drops below 80% approval pre-launch, or if pre-orders from existing customers are <15% of volume targets, the project must stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
