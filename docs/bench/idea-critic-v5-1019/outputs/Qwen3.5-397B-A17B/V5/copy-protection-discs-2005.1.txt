First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades long-term brand trust and system security for a fragile, easily bypassed restriction that treats paying customers as adversaries.

**Steelman:** Automatically installing restriction software is the only way to immediately stem revenue loss from casual copying in 2005 before legal streaming exists.

**Attack:**
1.  **Security & Liability:** Software that installs silently and resists removal is functionally indistinguishable from malware (rootkits). Security researchers will inevitably expose these mechanisms, turning a "protection" feature into a massive security vulnerability that compromises user systems. The reputational damage from being labeled a malware distributor exceeds lost sales from copying.
2.  **Customer Alienation:** Explicitly treating paying customers as thieves by restricting their ability to rip CDs for personal use (e.g., to iPods, which were rapidly gaining market share in 2005) creates immediate hostility. This drives customers toward pirated MP3s which offer a superior, restriction-free experience.
3.  **Ineffectiveness:** Determined pirates will crack the protection within days, distributing cracked versions widely. Only legitimate customers suffer the friction of broken software and reduced functionality.
4.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option preserves brand goodwill. A better alternative is embracing digital sales with fair DRM or no DRM, rather than sabotaging physical media.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, a major security flaw in the auto-installer is exposed by researchers, leading to class-action lawsuits, mandatory recalls, and a permanent brand stain as "the company that infected our computers." Sales dip slightly due to copying, but the lawsuit costs and lost future revenue dwarf this.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Silent installation without clear consent violates emerging norms of user autonomy and will be categorized as malicious behavior by the security community.
*   **Fact:** The technical measure punishes legitimate users while failing to stop determined pirates, inverting the value proposition.

**Risks:** Backlash, legal action, technical incompatibility with future OS updates.

**Kill Criteria:** If any security researcher publishes a method to exploit the installer for privilege escalation, the program must be immediately recalled and patched.

DECISION: NO-GO
