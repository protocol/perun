First call: NO-GO. The unit economics fail immediately: paying theaters full ticket price (approx. $10–$15) while charging members $10/month for daily access guarantees a massive loss on every active user, regardless of growth speed.

Steelman: Aggressive pricing creates a monopoly-scale user base quickly, forcing studios and theaters to accept data/revenue deals out of necessity rather than choice.

Attack:
1.  **Unit Economics:** If a member sees 3 films/month at $12/ticket, cost is $36 against $10 revenue. Subsidizing $26/user to chase "data" value assumes data monetization exceeds $26/user/month immediately, which is unproven and historically false for subscription models relying on downstream ad/data revenue.
2.  **Incentive Misalignment:** Theaters receive full price but lose control over pricing and customer relationship. Chains have no incentive to cooperate if the service cannibalizes their own premium pricing or membership programs without offering upside beyond the base ticket (which they already get).
3.  **Adverse Selection:** At $10 for unlimited daily films, the service attracts only the heaviest users (costing the most), while light users subsidize nothing because the price is too low to generate surplus. This is a classic "death spiral."
4.  **Unproven Revenue:** Banking on data/studio deals as the primary profit engine before validating the core transaction is speculative. Studios have historically resisted data-sharing models that undermine windowing strategies.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid burning cash on a structurally negative margin model.
*   **Tiered/Capped Model:** Charge a price covering ~2 films/month, charging overage fees. This aligns revenue closer to cost.
*   **Revenue Share:** Negotiate a discounted bulk rate with theaters rather than paying full price, sharing risk.

Pre-mortem: The company runs out of cash within 18 months as heavy usage spikes costs faster than membership growth, theaters refuse renewal due to channel conflict, and data revenue fails to materialize at the required scale.

Blockers:
*   **Negative Unit Margin:** Cost per user > Revenue per user for any user watching >1 film/month.
*   **Lack of Theater Incentive:** No value prop for chains beyond base ticket sales they already capture.

Risks: Data monetization failure, capital exhaustion, studio pushback.

DECISION: NO-GO
