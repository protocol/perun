First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on mail-order sales of an unproven, safety-sensitive vehicle in winter without addressing critical usability flaws.

**Steelman:** A sub-£400, license-free trike assembled by a major partner can capture the low-end commuter market via direct mail if launched immediately in January.

**Attack:**
1.  **Seasonal Mismatch:** Launching a battery-powered, open-air vehicle with short range and no weather protection via mail order in January ignores British winter realities. Cold drastically reduces battery performance; rain and ice make low-speed, low-visibility trikes dangerous. Demand will be near zero at launch, killing momentum.
2.  **Safety & Liability:** Selling a low-profile vehicle without safety group buy-in invites immediate backlash and potential regulatory bans. Mail order prevents customers from testing stability or comfort, increasing return rates and reputational damage if accidents occur.
3.  **Product Fragmentation:** Selling essential parts as "optional extras" on a base model already compromised by speed and range risks customer dissatisfaction. Buyers expecting a complete solution may feel deceived.
4.  **Distribution Flaw:** Mail order is ill-suited for vehicles requiring assembly or maintenance understanding, especially for a demographic likely unfamiliar with mechanical tinkering.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Wait for spring launch; avoid winter failure.
*   *Delay & Refine:* Postpone to March, add basic weather shielding, secure safety endorsement, and bundle essential parts.
*   *Pilot:* Limited regional release post-winter to gather safety data before national mail-order push.

**Pre-mortem:** By June, the company faces mass returns, negative press from winter accidents, and cash flow collapse due to unsold inventory. The "large numbers" sales target fails because the product is unusable in the launch season.

**Blockers:**
*   Launch timing (January) directly contradicts product limitations (weather exposure, battery range in cold).
*   Lack of safety validation creates unacceptable liability for a mail-order vehicle.

**Risks:** Low speed limits utility; optional extras confuse value proposition.

**Kill Criteria:** If a spring pilot cannot achieve 80% customer retention without weather upgrades, the model is invalid.

DECISION: NO-GO
