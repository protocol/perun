First call: NO-GO. The unit economics of high-budget professional content cannot be sustained by sub-ten-minute mobile viewing habits, especially when dominant free alternatives exist.

**Steelman:** A premium, mobile-first short-form service can carve a niche by offering cinematic quality that social platforms lack, justifying a subscription through exclusivity and prestige.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Fit & Behavior:** In 2018, short-form video consumption is dominated by free, algorithmic feeds (YouTube, emerging TikTok). Users accustomed to free, infinite scroll are unlikely to pay a monthly subscription for short episodes, particularly when those episodes are locked to phones. The "premium" label does not automatically override the zero-price anchor of social media.
2.  **Distribution Handicap:** Launching without TV support in 2020 ignores the primary use case for "premium video." High-budget productions demand high-fidelity screens; restricting them to phones artificially caps the value proposition while competitors offer cross-device flexibility.
3.  **Unit Economics:** Raising $1.75 billion for high-budget shows creates a massive recoupment hurdle. Short episodes generate less engagement time per dollar of content cost compared to hour-long dramas. The revenue per user (ARPU) from ads or subs on <10 minute clips is unlikely to cover the amortization of "well-known producer" budgets before cash runs out.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "do nothing" or "wait and see" approach is superior. Let social platforms validate the willingness to pay for short-form premium content first. A cheaper alternative is licensing existing short content or producing lower-budget pilots to test price elasticity before committing billions.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Mismatch:** The proposed cost structure (high budget) conflicts with the consumption format (short, mobile-only) and competitive landscape (free). The math does not work as stated.
*   **Feature Deficit:** Excluding TV access at launch for "high-budget" content contradicts consumer expectations for premium streaming, severely limiting market penetration.

**Risks:**
*   User acquisition costs will exceed lifetime value due to low retention on mobile-only short content.
*   Content creators may demand TV rights, forcing a costly pivot post-launch.

The plan fails the basic test of fitting the cost base to the likely revenue ceiling of the specific format and distribution constraints.

DECISION: NO-GO
