First call: NO-GO. The unit economics described guarantee that every small order loses money, and scaling directly employed staff across cities multiplies fixed costs before product-market fit is proven.

**Steelman:** Rapid urban delivery of mixed goods creates unmatched convenience, justifying heavy upfront subsidy to capture market share before competitors.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unit Economics:** Free delivery on small orders with no minimum means revenue per transaction is likely lower than the variable cost of labor and transport. The proposal admits this risk but offers no mitigation (e.g., delivery fees, minimums, or dynamic pricing). Scaling losses does not create a business; it creates a larger hole.
2.  **Operational Complexity:** Directly employing delivery staff in multiple cities simultaneously creates immense legal, managerial, and capital overhead. This "blitzscale" approach assumes capital availability and execution perfection, ignoring the friction of hiring, training, and managing a distributed workforce without a proven model in even one neighborhood.
3.  **Demand Fragility:** If usage relies on subsidies, churn will be catastrophic once prices rise to cover costs. The proposal treats customer acquisition as a function of spending rather than value creation.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid burning capital on a logically flawed model.
*   **Pilot:** Test in one dense zip code with delivery fees and minimum orders to validate true demand and unit economics before hiring staff or expanding.
*   **Asset-Light:** Use independent contractors or existing logistics partners to reduce fixed costs and liability.

**Pre-mortem:** The company runs out of cash within 18 months because the cost to deliver a $10 item is $12, and investors refuse to fund infinite losses for a service customers abandon when subsidies end.

**Blockers:**
*   **Negative Unit Economics:** The core mechanism (free delivery, no minimum) ensures a loss on every transaction, contradicting the goal of a sustainable business.
*   **Premature Scaling:** Expanding to "more cities quickly" with direct employment commits massive capital to an unproven, loss-making model.

**Risks:** Customer churn post-subsidy, regulatory hurdles for employment, capital scarcity.

**Kill Criteria:** If a single-zone pilot cannot achieve positive contribution margin per order within 6 months without subsidies, terminate the project.

The proposal fails the basic test of selling a dollar for less than a dollar while committing to infinite scale.

DECISION: NO-GO
