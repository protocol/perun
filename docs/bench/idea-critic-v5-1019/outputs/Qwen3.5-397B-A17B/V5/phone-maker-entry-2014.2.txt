First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to enter a saturated, app-centric market with a differentiated hardware feature (3D) that does not address the primary buyer constraint (app ecosystem size) while accepting a severe distribution handicap (single carrier).

**Steelman:** A premium device leveraging unique computer-vision shopping integration and 3D media can carve a niche among loyal customers, offsetting the smaller app catalog with exclusive retail utility and membership value.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Strategy:** The core value proposition of a smartphone in 2014 is the app ecosystem. A "premium" price demands parity with leaders. Limiting distribution to one carrier contradicts the need for maximum reach to sustain a new platform. The "3D effect" is a solution searching for a problem; historical data suggests novelty hardware features fail without strong software support.
2.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "partner" option is superior. Building a phone requires massive R&D and carrier subsidies. A cheaper alternative is integrating the proposed "visual search" and "membership" features into an app for existing dominant phones, capturing the retail upside without the hardware risk.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The device launches to poor reviews citing the lack of popular apps. The 3D feature is viewed as a gimmick. Sales are cannibalized by the carrier's promotion of established brands. The inventory becomes obsolete within 12 months, damaging the retailer's brand perception as a tech leader.
4.  **Evidence & Blocker:** The proposal admits the app catalog is "far larger" on competing devices. In the smartphone market, this is a fatal blocker for a premium-priced entry. No amount of "visual search" compensates for the inability to run standard banking, social, or utility apps that users expect at a premium price point. The single-carrier restriction further reduces the total addressable market below the threshold required to sustain a new hardware line.

**Blockers:**
*   **Ecosystem Deficit:** Premium pricing is unjustifiable with a significantly smaller app catalog; this contradicts the core claim of being a viable premium competitor.
*   **Distribution Cap:** Single-carrier launch prevents the volume necessary to attract developer support or achieve economies of scale.

**Risks:** High inventory obsolescence, brand damage, opportunity cost of diverted capital.

**Kill Criteria:** If a prototype cannot run the top 50 essential apps natively or via robust emulation by launch, the project must stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
