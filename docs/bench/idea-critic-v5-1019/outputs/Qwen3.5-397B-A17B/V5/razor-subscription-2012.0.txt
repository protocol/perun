First call: GO. The low-cost, direct-to-consumer model exploits a clear inefficiency in the incumbent's high-overhead, ad-heavy pricing structure, offering immediate value to price-sensitive customers.

**Steelman:** A lean reseller can undercut dominant brands by stripping away retail markups and massive ad spend, using viral humor to acquire customers cheaply while relying on established manufacturing quality.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" leaves money on the table for consumers. Building a factory is capital-prohibitive and slow. The proposed "video + simple site" is the cheapest, fastest way to validate demand.
*   **Fit:** This moves the north star of "affordable quality" directly, bypassing traditional gatekeepers.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the supplier blocks sales or if the video fails to convert. However, the cost of the video is negligible compared to traditional ad spend. The "one video" risk is mitigated by the low absolute cost; if it fails, the loss is minimal.
*   **Evidence:** The proposal admits dependence on one supplier and legal risk. However, in 2012, patent cliffs and generic manufacturing capacity make supply accessible. Legal threats are costly for incumbents to pursue against a small, compliant reseller.
*   **Blockers:** None found. The risks cited (price wars, lawsuits, low traffic, subscription fatigue) are *risks*, not *blockers*. They are uncertainties inherent to any new venture, not facts proving failure. The incumbents' high fixed costs make a price war damaging to them, not just the startup. A single video is sufficient for a "test," not a final scale strategy.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If the supplier refuses to sell or the video generates zero orders despite distribution, the thesis fails.

**Decision:**
The plan is a low-cost experiment with high upside. It beats "do nothing" and requires no heavy upfront investment. The risks are manageable and typical for a startup; they do not invalidate the core economic arbitrage. The lack of a pilot or metric is not a blocker; the launch *is* the pilot.

Blockers: NONE
Risks: Supplier dependency, incumbent retaliation, marketing flop (all standard startup risks, not fatal flaws).

DECISION: GO
