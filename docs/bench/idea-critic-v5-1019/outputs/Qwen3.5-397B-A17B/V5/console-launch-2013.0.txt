First call: GO. The proposal directly addresses the previous failure modes (latency, cost, developer friction) and attacks the rival's vulnerabilities with a clear value proposition.

**Steelman:** By pricing below the rival, simplifying the architecture for easier development, and preserving consumer rights (used games, offline play), the maker can regain market share despite lower per-unit margins, betting on volume and a stronger software ecosystem over time.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** Low price, PC-like hardware, consumer-friendly policies. High volume potential, lower margin, high developer appeal. Reversible via price hikes later, but hardware specs are fixed.
2.  **Do Nothing/Status Quo:** Continue losing ground with expensive, complex hardware. Certain decline.
3.  **Premium Strategy:** Match rival's price and online focus. High risk given the maker's weak online services and current brand damage.

**Fit:** Aligns with the immediate need to stop market share bleeding. The "north star" is assumed to be market relevance and developer adoption.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the lower price triggers a price war the rival wins due to deeper pockets, or if the "PC-like" architecture leads to poor optimization despite ease of use. However, the rival's head start in online services is mitigated by focusing on offline/used strengths where they are neutral.

**Evidence & Math:** The proposal relies on the economic principle that lower prices drive volume in elastic markets. While margin per unit drops, the total profit depends on install base growth driving software sales. The risk of "little new software" is addressed by making hardware "easy for developers," lowering the barrier for third-party support to fill the gap.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (margin reduction, rival's online lead, soft consumer spending) are valid but do not invalidate the core logic; they are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The strategy explicitly trades margin for volume, which is a calculated choice, not a contradiction.

**Risks:** Lower margin sustainability; reliance on third-party software uptake; consumer shift to mobile. These become kill criteria: monitor attach rates and developer sign-ups within the first two quarters.

**Kill Criteria:** If developer adoption does not exceed the rival's rate within 12 months, or if hardware sales fail to reach 50% of the rival's volume despite the price advantage, the strategy must pivot to a premium service model or cost restructuring.

DECISION: GO
