First call: GO. The market lacks a high-end device bridging the phone-tablet gap, and screen real estate is a defensible premium differentiator despite skepticism.

Steelman: A large-screen device with a stylus creates a new "phablet" category, capturing users needing more productivity than a phone offers but more portability than a tablet, justifying a premium price.

Options analysis: Doing nothing cedes the emerging large-screen segment to competitors. A standard large phone without a stylus misses the productivity angle. A tablet fails the portability test. This proposal beats "do nothing" by capturing early adopters and defining a new form factor.

Fit: Assumes the strategy is market expansion and premiumization. A device that doesn't fit existing categories aligns with a vision to lead rather than follow.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if reviewers successfully frame the size as "awkward" rather than "generous," or if the stylus is perceived as a gimmick rather than a tool. However, these are risks, not proven blockers. The cost of delay is high; waiting for market validation allows competitors to establish the category standard.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the observed gap between 4-inch phones and tablets. No internal data contradicts the existence of this gap. The risks cited (reviewer bias, stylus adoption) are speculative hurdles common to category creation, not factual impossibilities.

Blocker test:
Blockers: NONE. The risks listed are uncertainties inherent to innovation, not facts proving the plan fails. No evidence suggests the target users explicitly reject large screens or styluses; the fear is merely that they *might*.
Risks: Reviewer backlash, niche appeal, cannibalization. These become kill criteria: if initial pre-orders or first-month sales do not meet a specific threshold (e.g., 10% of flagship volume), the line is discontinued.

The "too big/too small" argument is a perception risk, not a functional blocker. The potential to define a new market segment outweighs the risk of cannibalizing existing 4-inch sales, which can be managed via pricing tiers. The proposal includes a clear path to revenue and brand elevation.

DECISION: GO
