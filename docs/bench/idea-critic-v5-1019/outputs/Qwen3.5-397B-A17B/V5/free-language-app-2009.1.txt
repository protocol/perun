First call: NO-GO. The plan explicitly defers revenue validation while incurring full build costs, relying on an unproven mechanism (learner translations) to eventually fund a free service.

**Steelman:** A free, crowd-translated language platform can achieve scale and quality sufficient to monetize later, bypassing the need for upfront nonprofit funding.

**Attack:**
1.  **Quality vs. Value:** The core revenue engine assumes learners produce sellable translations. Early learners produce low-quality work; publishers require high accuracy. This creates a "cold start" paradox where the product cannot generate revenue until users are proficient, but users need the revenue model to sustain the platform.
2.  **Market Reality:** Established competitors offer polished, guaranteed quality. Selling uncertain, crowd-sourced translations from beginners is a hard sell against professional agencies or established software.
3.  **Financial Suicide:** Building a full web app before validating if anyone will pay for the output violates basic lean startup principles. The "build now, monetize later" approach ignores the specific risk that the revenue model itself is flawed. If translations aren't sellable, the entire project collapses after costly development.
4.  **Alternatives:** A cheaper, safer path exists: manually validate the translation market first. Have the professor and student translate a small batch of content with early students and attempt to sell it *before* writing code. If they cannot sell manual translations, an app will not fix the demand problem.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Revenue Core:** The proposal admits the revenue model is unproven yet commits to building the product first. The stated facts (poor quality risk, unproven model) directly contradict the feasibility of the "revenue later" strategy.
*   **Misaligned Incentives:** Free learners have no financial incentive to produce high-quality work for sale, creating a structural defect in the supply chain.

**Risks:**
*   Competition from polished paid courses.
*   Low conversion of free users to value-generating contributors.

**Conclusion:** The plan fails the "cheaper alternative" test. Validating the sale of learner translations manually is a prerequisite to building software. Committing to development without this proof is irresponsible.

DECISION: NO-GO
