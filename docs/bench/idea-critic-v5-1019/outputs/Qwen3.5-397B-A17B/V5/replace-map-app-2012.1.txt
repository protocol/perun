First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits critical data deficiencies and known user friction without a mitigation plan, making a forced default swap a strategic liability.

Steelman: Replacing the rival's map with a proprietary solution built from aggregated providers will secure long-term platform control and enable deep OS integration (turn-by-turn, 3D, voice) that licensing cannot match.

Options:
1. **Do Nothing:** Retain rival app; maintain user satisfaction but cede control and roadmap influence.
2. **Dual Default/Opt-in:** Offer the new map as an optional download or secondary choice to gather usage data without forcing a degraded experience.
3. **Proposed Plan:** Force replacement immediately. Highest risk of user backlash due to admitted data gaps; lowest reversibility once devices ship.

Fit: While aligning with a "control" north star, the execution contradicts the implicit goal of customer satisfaction by deploying an inferior product as the default.

Pre-mortem: Users encounter missing businesses and incorrect navigation immediately upon update. Trust erodes; the brand becomes synonymous with "broken maps." Competitors leverage this failure in marketing. The cost of delay (waiting for data parity) is lower than the cost of reputational damage.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits "a lot less data," "wrong information," and anticipated user annoyance. These are not hypothetical risks but stated facts of the current build state.

Blockers:
1. **Functional Deficit:** The core utility (navigation/data accuracy) is explicitly stated as inferior to the incumbent. Deploying a known inferior default violates the basic standard of product quality.
2. **User Harm:** The plan knowingly degrades the experience for existing users ("users may be annoyed") to solve a strategic problem, treating the user base as a testing ground.

Risks: Data gaps persisting longer than expected; competitor capitalizing on the switch; inability to rollback on updated devices.

The proposal fails the kill check: the risk concerns the core claim (that the map works), the facts support it will happen (admitted data gaps), and the commitment (global default swap) is irreversible for shipped units. A capped pilot or opt-in rollout is required before a global mandate.

DECISION: NO-GO
