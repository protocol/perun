First call: GO. The direct-to-consumer, low-overhead model directly addresses the price sensitivity of the market while bypassing the margin-heavy retail channels of incumbents.

**Steelman:** A lean, incorporated entity can undercut established brands by eliminating retail markups and focusing on assembly efficiency, proving that brand prestige is secondary to price/performance for early adopters.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Remain a small, unincorporated hobbyist; limited growth, high personal liability.
2.  **Retail expansion:** Open showrooms; high capital cost, destroys the price advantage, slow reversibility.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Incorporate and scale via direct mail; low capital requirement, high reversibility, maximizes price competitiveness.

**Fit:** Aligns with the "north star" of affordable personal computing; assumes customers prioritize specs/price over tactile evaluation.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if suppliers cut credit lines simultaneously with a spike in returns due to lack of local service. However, this is a two-way door; service contracts can be outsourced later, and credit terms negotiated upon incorporation. The cost of delay is losing the first-mover advantage in the emerging "clone" market.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on the verified fact that IBM-compatible parts are commoditized and available. The risk of buyers wanting to "try" before buying is mitigated by the growing trust in mail-order for technical goods and money-back guarantees.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (service, credit, brand) are operational hurdles, not factual contradictions. They do not prevent the core claim (price leadership via direct sales) from working.
**Risks:** Supplier credit denial, high return rates due to no local support. These become kill criteria: if supplier credit is denied within 60 days or return rates exceed 15%, pivot to local assembly partnerships or halt expansion.

**Kill Criteria:** Stop expansion if cash flow turns negative for two consecutive months due to parts inventory buildup or if a major supplier demands 100% upfront payment. Leading metric: Monthly unit sales growth vs. return rate.

DECISION: GO
