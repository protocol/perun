First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits core functionality (data completeness, business accuracy) is inferior to the incumbent at launch, directly threatening user trust and device value for a strategic goal that does not require immediate total replacement.

**Steelman:** Replacing the rival's map is essential for long-term strategic control and ecosystem independence, necessitating a bold, all-in launch to force adoption and accelerate data improvement through usage.

**Attack & Options:**
The "all-in" approach fails the blocker test. The proposal explicitly states the new maps have "a lot less data" and "wrong" business information. Launching this as the *default* for *all* users creates a guaranteed negative experience for millions, likely driving churn or mass re-installation of the rival app, undermining the very control sought.
*   **Do Nothing:** Maintain status quo; retain user satisfaction but cede strategic control.
*   **Staged Rollout (Better Way):** Introduce the new map as an *option* or default only for new users in specific regions with better data, keeping the rival app available. This allows real-world testing and data patching without alienating the entire existing base.
*   **All-In (Proposed):** High risk of immediate user backlash, brand damage, and potential failure if the data gaps are too severe to fix quickly.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
While strategic fit (control) is clear, the execution ignores the "north star" of user satisfaction. Pre-mortem: Users encounter broken navigation and missing businesses immediately upon update, leading to negative reviews, support spikes, and a rapid loss of confidence in the phone maker's software quality. The cost of delay (waiting to improve data) is lower than the cost of a botched launch.

**Blocker:**
The proposal's own facts confirm the product is not ready for prime-time default status. The admitted data deficiencies and inaccuracies constitute a functional failure for a core utility. A default app must work reliably for everyone; this one will not. The lack of a fallback or staged approach turns a known risk into a certain failure mode.

**Decision:**
The strategic goal is valid, but the tactic is fatal. You cannot force adoption of an inferior product on your entire user base without severe consequences.

DECISION: NO-GO
