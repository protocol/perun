First call: GO. The model converts fixed membership revenue into a moat against thin operating margins, aligning retailer and shopper incentives for volume.

**Steelman:** A membership warehouse club leverages upfront fees to fund low prices, driving high inventory turnover that offsets razor-thin product margins and neutralizes a competitor's size advantage through superior unit economics.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposal:** Scale warehouses, rely on fees for profit. (High capex, high recurring revenue, defensible).
2.  **Do Nothing:** Cede the bulk market to the incumbent. (Zero risk, zero upside).
3.  **Traditional Retail:** Higher markups, no fee. (Lower barrier to entry, vulnerable to price wars, lower loyalty).
The proposal beats "do nothing" by capturing a specific value-conscious segment the incumbent may be over-serving.

**Fit:** Assumes a 1983 market with inflation-weary consumers seeking bulk value. The "no-frills" approach directly targets the north star of lowest possible shelf price.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if membership renewal rates drop below breakeven volume, turning fixed cost sites into liabilities. Thin margins offer no buffer for supply chain errors. This is a two-way door initially (close underperforming units), but rapid over-expansion creates a one-way debt trap. Delay costs first-mover advantage in site acquisition and member lock-in.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the competitor is larger and richer, implying the only path to victory is a different cost structure, not a head-to-head price war on identical terms. The risk of shoppers refusing fees is real but is the core hypothesis to test, not a blocker.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (fee resistance, competitor size, thin margins, site requirements) are inherent to the business model, not contradictions of it. They are execution risks, not logical flaws. The model specifically solves the "thin margin" issue by shifting profit center to fees.

**Risks:** Membership churn, capital intensity, supply chain rigidity. These become kill criteria: if renewal rates fall below X% or same-store sales decline for two quarters, expansion halts.

**Decision:** The logic holds. The fee structure is the differentiator required to survive against a larger incumbent.

DECISION: GO
