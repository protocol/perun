First call: GO. The stated fact that bottles were poisoned after leaving the factory creates an immediate, unquantifiable threat to human life that cannot be contained by a local response when the distribution chain is national and the perpetrator is unknown.

Steelman: A nationwide withdrawal and production halt are the only actions that guarantee no further deaths while restoring the absolute trust required for a mass-market ingestible product, despite massive short-term financial loss.

Alternatives considered:
1. **Do nothing/Local recall only:** Lowest cost, but risks additional deaths if poisoned units exist elsewhere. If another death occurs, the brand faces existential litigation and total reputational collapse.
2. **Partial halt with warnings:** Reduces cost slightly but fails to remove the physical danger from shelves immediately. Consumers often ignore warnings; the liability remains.
3. **Proposed Plan (Nationwide Halt):** Highest immediate cost, highest reversibility of trust. It sacrifices quarterly revenue to save the franchise.

Fit: The north star for a consumer health brand is "safety above all." Any strategy that tolerates a non-zero probability of further poisoning violates this core constraint. The proposal aligns perfectly with survival logic.

Pre-mortem: Assume the relaunch fails. Why? Because competitors entrenched themselves during the gap, or consumers permanently associated the brand with death. However, without the halt, there is no brand left to relaunch. The cost of delay (waiting for more data) is measured in potential fatalities, which is an unacceptable metric for optimization.

Evidence: The premise states poisoning occurred post-factory in one area, implying the supply chain is compromised unpredictably. No evidence suggests the threat is contained.

Blockers: NONE. The financial risks (cost, market share loss) are severe but are consequences of the event, not flaws in the response plan. They do not prevent the plan from achieving its primary goal: stopping deaths and saving the brand's long-term viability.

Risks: Competitors gaining shelf space and high recall costs. These are managed by the speed of the tamper-resistant relaunch and the moral authority gained by the decisive action.

Kill criteria: If independent testing confirms the poison source was strictly limited to a single batch never distributed beyond the initial metro area *before* further deaths occur, the scope could be reduced. However, given the "unknown person" and current uncertainty, the broad stroke is necessary.

DECISION: GO
