First call: GO. The proposal converts previous hardware failures into a low-cost distribution play by leveraging existing user devices as controllers, directly addressing the "confusing buyer" and "margin" risks through simplicity and volume rather than feature completeness.

**Steelman:** A sub-$35 dongle removes the price barrier for trial, using smartphones as the UI to bypass remote/menu complexity, while an open kit accelerates app adoption despite a thin launch catalog.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Cedes the living room to established boxes with higher prices and complex remotes, maintaining low TV penetration.
*   **Full Device (Remote + OS):** Repeats past failures; high BOM cost kills margin, and building a menu system delays launch.
*   **The Claim:** Users prefer casting from phones over navigating TV menus.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if Wi-Fi setup is too difficult for non-technical users or if latency makes casting unusable. However, the "no remote" constraint forces a two-way door experiment: if casting fails, users revert to phones; if it works, we own the screen at near-zero marginal cost.
*   **Evidence Check:** The proposal admits low margins but implies volume compensates. Previous device failures support the pivot to a "dumb" dongle. The lack of apps is a chicken-and-egg problem solved by the open kit and low hardware price attracting developers.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (confusion, margin, app count) are inherent to the strategy but not fatal facts. Confusion is mitigated by the phone UI; margin is accepted for market share; apps follow users.
*   **Risks:** Wi-Fi friction, latency, developer apathy. These become kill criteria: if setup completion <80% or latency >200ms in pilot, pivot.

**Decision:** The plan correctly identifies that the barrier to TV adoption is not hardware capability but price and complexity. By stripping the device to its core function (streaming) and outsourcing the UI to the phone, it creates a viable path to market where previous attempts failed. The low margin is a feature, not a bug, driving rapid distribution.

DECISION: GO
