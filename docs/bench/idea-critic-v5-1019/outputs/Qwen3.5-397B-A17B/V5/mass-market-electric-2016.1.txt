First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a company with negligible mass-production experience to instantly scale to "far higher volumes" while simultaneously scaling a complex battery supply chain, all on a thinner margin, funded by refundable deposits that may not convert. This ignores the fundamental constraint: production capability.

**Steelman:** The company can leverage early adopter demand from expensive models to fund a cheaper sedan, using deposits to validate market size before committing to full tooling.

**Attack:** The steelman collapses under the specific facts provided. The company has built only two vehicles in small numbers. Jumping to "far higher volumes" is not a scaling step; it is a phase change in manufacturing physics and logistics they have never demonstrated. Battery supply scaling is a separate, capital-intensive bottleneck that cannot be solved by deposit money alone, especially with "little experience." Refundable deposits create a liability, not guaranteed revenue; if production slips (highly likely given the inexperience), deposits flee, starving the very production ramp they were meant to fund. The lower price eliminates the buffer needed to absorb the inevitable inefficiencies of a first-time mass production run.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve cash, avoid reputational suicide from a failed mass launch.
2.  **Pilot scale:** Build a limited run (e.g., 10x current volume) to prove the supply chain and assembly line before promising mass market delivery.
3.  **Partner:** License the design to an established manufacturer to handle the volume risk.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch is announced, deposits flood in, but the factory cannot hit yield targets. Battery shortages halt lines. Customers demand refunds en masse. The company burns through cash trying to fix production bugs at scale, goes bankrupt, and the brand is destroyed.

**Blocker:** The stated fact that the company has "little experience of mass production" directly contradicts the requirement to begin production "at far higher volumes" the next year. No amount of deposit money bridges the gap between building two cars and building thousands without a proven intermediate step. The plan fails as proposed because the core operational capability is absent.

**Risks:** Supply chain failure, margin erosion, deposit flight, brand damage.

DECISION: NO-GO
