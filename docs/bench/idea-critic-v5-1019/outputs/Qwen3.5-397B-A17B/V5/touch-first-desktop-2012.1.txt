First call: NO-GO. Forcing a touch-first interface on keyboard/mouse users ignores the primary workflow of the dominant OS's existing base.

**Steelman:** Unifying the interface accelerates the tablet transition and forces app ecosystem modernization.
**Attack:** The proposal mandates a full-screen start screen for *all* PCs, removing the traditional menu. This directly contradicts the stated risk that desktop users will find it confusing and costly to retrain. Unlike a dual-mode option, this removes the familiar workflow entirely.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Retain market share but risk slow tablet adoption.
2.  **Proposed:** Force unification. High risk of alienating the core revenue base (enterprise/desktop) for uncertain tablet gains.
3.  **Better way:** Offer the touch interface as an optional mode or distinct device tier, keeping the desktop menu as default for non-touch hardware. This captures tablet upside without forcing friction on the installed base.

**Fit:** Assumes the north star is "tablet dominance" over "desktop utility." If the strategy is transition, the cost of delay must be weighed against immediate churn.

**Pre-mortem:** Enterprise IT departments reject the update due to productivity loss and training costs; they delay upgrades or switch platforms. Developers ignore the new store because the active user base remains on the desktop interface. The "no easy way to go back" constraint turns early adopters into detractors.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits high training costs, user confusion, and developer reluctance. It offers no mitigation for these confirmed negatives other than force.

**Blockers:**
*   **Self-contradiction:** The plan proceeds despite admitted facts that the target users (corporate/desktop) do not want the interface and it increases their costs.
*   **Alternative superiority:** A phased or optional rollout achieves ecosystem growth without the guaranteed backlash of a forced removal of the primary navigation tool.

**Risks:** Developer apathy, enterprise churn, brand damage. These are not merely possible; the proposal states the conditions for them are already true.

**Kill Criteria:** If enterprise adoption drops >5% in Q1 post-launch, revert the default interface.

The proposal fails because it treats a segment-specific opportunity (tablets) as a mandate to break the core product for its largest existing customers.

DECISION: NO-GO
