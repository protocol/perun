The proposal presents a compelling social mission but suffers from critical structural flaws that threaten immediate viability. The core revenue model relies on selling translations generated by learners who are, by definition, not yet proficient. This creates an inherent quality contradiction; publishers require accuracy and reliability, which novice learners cannot guarantee without extensive, costly oversight. If the output is poor, no revenue will be generated. If the output requires heavy professional editing to be sellable, the cost structure collapses, rendering the "free for all" promise unsustainable.

Furthermore, the strategy to "build the product first and look for revenue later" is dangerously speculative. The market already contains polished, established competitors. Entering this space with an unproven monetization hook and a product dependent on unverified user quality offers no defensive moat. The risk that free users will never convert or generate value is high, especially when the proposed conversion mechanism (selling their work) depends on them reaching a proficiency level that might cause them to leave the platform for paid opportunities elsewhere.

The team has correctly identified that a nonprofit structure may be unsustainable, yet their proposed alternative lacks a credible path to profitability. Launching without a validated method to cover server costs, development, and potential quality control measures invites rapid failure. The risks regarding quality, customer conversion, and competition are not merely hurdles; they are fundamental blockers to the business logic as currently stated. Proceeding now would expend resources on a hypothesis that has not been stress-tested against the basic requirement of delivering sellable value. The gap between "learning to translate" and "producing commercial-grade translation" is too wide to bridge with the current plan.

DECISION: NO-GO
