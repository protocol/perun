The proposal presents a fundamentally flawed business model that misaligns with consumer expectations and market realities. The core value proposition requires customers to purchase a disc for a few dollars yet restricts viewing to a 48-hour window, after which additional fees are mandatory. This structure effectively charges users repeatedly for content they physically own, creating a perception of unfairness that will likely drive immediate rejection. Consumers accustomed to owning media indefinitely will not accept a rental-like constraint on a purchased product.

Furthermore, the hardware requirements create a significant barrier to entry. The necessity of special players that are more expensive than standard units, while also requiring a phone line connection, adds friction and cost. Since these discs are incompatible with existing standard players, the entire installed base of consumer electronics is rendered useless for this format. This forces a complete ecosystem overhaul rather than a seamless upgrade.

The reliance on widespread agreement from both studios and electronics manufacturers introduces massive execution risk. Securing simultaneous buy-in from disparate industry giants for an unproven, consumer-hostile format is highly improbable. Even if partnerships were formed, the combined friction of high hardware costs, mandatory connectivity, and recurring fees for owned media creates a value equation that fails to compete with existing rental models or emerging alternatives.

The risks identified in the proposal are not merely hurdles; they are fatal flaws. The customer dislike for paying for repeated viewing is a definitive market signal that cannot be engineered away. Launching a product that alienates users through restrictive licensing and expensive, proprietary hardware will result in significant financial loss and brand damage. The market is not ready for a format that penalizes ownership, and the technical dependencies make adoption unlikely. Proceeding now would be a strategic error.

DECISION: NO-GO
