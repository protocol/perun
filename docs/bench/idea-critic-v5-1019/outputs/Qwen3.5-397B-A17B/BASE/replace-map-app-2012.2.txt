The proposal seeks to replace a rival's established mapping application with a proprietary solution to gain strategic control. While the ambition to own the user experience and integrate features like turn-by-turn navigation, 3D views, and voice assistance is sound, the execution plan presents immediate, critical threats to user satisfaction and brand reputation.

The facts indicate the new system relies on aggregated data from several providers yet still possesses significantly less data than the incumbent rival. This deficit directly correlates with the identified risk of incorrect place and business information. Releasing a product known to have accuracy issues in the fall, a peak sales season, invites widespread user frustration. Furthermore, removing a working, trusted default application in favor of an unproven alternative is a high-stakes gamble. Users who rely on accurate maps for daily navigation are likely to view this change as a degradation of service rather than an improvement.

The risks of user annoyance and the loss of a reliable rival product by default are not merely theoretical; they are direct consequences of the current data gaps. Launching with inferior data undermines the very value proposition of the phone. Control is meaningless if the controlled experience fails to meet basic functional expectations. The proposal acknowledges these severe shortcomings but proceeds with a full replacement strategy anyway. A phased approach, beta testing, or a dual-default period would be necessary to mitigate these specific, admitted failures before a full swap. As it stands, the plan guarantees a negative user experience upon launch.

Approving this now would prioritize internal strategic desires over immediate customer utility, likely resulting in public backlash and loss of trust that could take years to repair. The gaps in data and accuracy must be resolved before forcing this transition on the entire user base.

DECISION: NO-GO
