The proposal presents a critical strategic pivot for a niche sports-car manufacturer in 2002. While the opportunity to enter the lucrative luxury SUV segment via co-development offers scale and shared costs, the specific execution plan carries unacceptable risks. Launching a heavy, high-priced off-roader directly under the core sports-car brand threatens to alienate the loyal enthusiast base that sustains the company. These customers view the brand as a symbol of performance and purity; introducing an SUV could be perceived as a betrayal, eroding brand equity that took decades to build.

Furthermore, the market landscape is hostile. Established luxury competitors already dominate this space with strong reputations. Entering with a first-year cost burden, while simultaneously risking the dilution of the brand's sports image, creates a fragile financial and reputational position. The heavy nature of the vehicle directly contradicts the agile, high-performance identity of the existing range. Without a distinct sub-brand or a clearer strategy to segregate this new lineup from the core sports heritage, the potential damage to the company's soul outweighs the immediate revenue potential. The risks of customer churn and brand confusion are too high to proceed with the current structure. The proposal fails to adequately mitigate the cultural clash between off-road utility and track-focused heritage. Approval in its current form invites catastrophic brand degradation.

DECISION: NO-GO
