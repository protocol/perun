The proposal to limit chip replacements only to users who can prove they are affected is fundamentally flawed and dangerous for the company's long-term viability. The core issue is not the statistical rarity of the error, but the impossibility for a typical user to detect it. Since users cannot easily demonstrate that their specific calculations have been compromised, the condition for receiving a replacement effectively denies relief to almost everyone, regardless of their actual risk exposure.

This approach ignores the critical factor of public perception. The problem is already being widely discussed, indicating that awareness is high and trust is fragile. By refusing a blanket replacement, the company signals that it prioritizes short-term cost savings over product integrity and customer confidence. The risk of reputational damage far outweighs the immediate financial cost of replacing every chip. If buyers believe the company is hiding a defect or dismissing valid concerns, the resulting loss of brand equity could be catastrophic, potentially exceeding the cost of the recall many times over.

Furthermore, stating that the flaw is "not serious" while simultaneously acknowledging it exists creates a contradictory narrative that fuels skepticism. In the face of widespread discussion, transparency and decisive action are required to maintain market leadership. A partial solution that places the burden of proof on the customer will be viewed as an admission of guilt without accountability. The company must choose between paying a large sum now to secure its future or saving money today at the expense of its reputation tomorrow. Given the inability of users to verify the error and the heightened public scrutiny, the proposed strategy invites a backlash that the organization cannot afford. The current plan fails to address the reality of the situation and exposes the firm to unacceptable strategic risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
