The proposal presents a fundamentally flawed business model that misaligns with consumer expectations and market realities. The core value proposition relies on charging customers repeatedly for the same content after an initial 48-hour window, a mechanism explicitly identified as disliked by the target audience. In the home entertainment sector, consumers prioritize ownership or unlimited access within a rental period; introducing a "pay-per-view-after-48-hours" structure on a physical medium creates friction and perceived unfairness that will likely drive customers toward existing rental models or emerging alternatives.

Furthermore, the hardware requirements create an insurmountable barrier to entry. Requiring customers to purchase specialized players that are more expensive than standard units, while offering no backward compatibility with existing libraries, drastically reduces the potential install base. The necessity for these players to connect to a phone line adds further complexity and potential cost for the user, diminishing the user experience.

The success of this venture is contingent on simultaneous agreement from both major studios and electronics manufacturers. Given the high costs for manufacturers to produce niche players and the risk of alienating customers with a restrictive viewing model, securing this dual alignment is highly improbable. Studios are unlikely to back a format that consumers reject, and manufacturers will not invest in producing expensive, non-compatible hardware without a guaranteed software library and consumer demand.

The combination of a hated payment structure, expensive proprietary hardware, lack of compatibility, and the massive coordination risk required to launch makes this initiative untenable. The market conditions described suggest that the friction points outweigh any potential revenue from repeated viewing fees. Proceeding would likely result in significant capital loss and reputational damage.

DECISION: NO-GO
