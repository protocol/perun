The proposal presents a fundamentally flawed economic model that threatens immediate capital depletion. The core offering promises free delivery with no minimum order on low-margin items like snacks and books. In any physical logistics operation, the cost of labor, vehicles, and fuel for a single trip exceeds the profit generated from small individual transactions. Without a minimum order threshold to bundle costs or a delivery fee to offset expenses, every transaction incurs a net loss.

The plan to employ delivery staff directly rather than using contractors or third-party networks significantly increases fixed overhead. This approach demands substantial upfront capital for recruitment, training, salaries, benefits, and fleet management before a single customer is served. Expanding quickly to multiple major cities multiplies these fixed costs exponentially, creating a massive cash burn rate that venture funding may not sustain if unit economics remain negative.

Furthermore, the risk that customers will only utilize the service while it is subsidized indicates a lack of genuine product-market fit. If demand vanishes once fees are introduced or subsidies removed, the business has no path to profitability. The 1998 technological landscape also limits the ability to optimize routes dynamically or manage real-time inventory efficiently compared to modern standards, making the one-hour promise even more costly to fulfill.

Raising venture money to scale a model where every unit sold loses money is not a strategy for growth; it is a recipe for insolvency. No amount of expansion can fix a broken unit economy. The company would simply be losing money faster as it grows. The risks identified regarding cost structures and customer behavior are not merely potential hurdles; they are fatal flaws in the current design. Approval in the proposed form would result in the rapid waste of investor capital with no viable exit or sustainability strategy. The model requires a complete restructuring of pricing, delivery logistics, and staffing before any funds should be committed.

DECISION: NO-GO
