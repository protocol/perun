The proposal presents a compelling social mission but suffers from critical structural flaws that threaten immediate viability. The core revenue mechanism relies on selling translations generated by learners who are, by definition, not yet proficient. This creates an inherent quality conflict; publishers require accuracy that novices cannot guarantee without expensive, unmentioned oversight layers. If the output is poor, no market exists. If the output requires heavy editing, the cost structure collapses, negating the "free" model's sustainability.

Furthermore, the strategy to "build the product and look for revenue later" is dangerously speculative. The risks identified are not merely potential hurdles but fundamental contradictions to the business case. Established competitors offer polished, reliable services. A free alternative with unproven monetization and questionable output quality struggles to capture value. The assumption that a massive user base will naturally convert to a revenue stream via an untested translation marketplace lacks evidence. Without a validated path to income, the project risks burning resources while delivering a substandard product to both learners and potential commercial clients.

The rejection of the nonprofit status due to perceived unsustainability suggests a misunderstanding of funding landscapes for educational initiatives, yet the proposed for-profit alternative offers no clearer path to survival. Launching now, without a proven method to ensure translation quality or a confirmed buyer for that work, invites failure. The gap between "learning to translate" and "selling professional translation" is too wide to bridge with the current plan. The team must first validate the quality control process and secure letters of intent from publishers before writing a single line of code. Proceeding immediately ignores the severe disconnect between the value proposition and the operational reality.

DECISION: NO-GO
