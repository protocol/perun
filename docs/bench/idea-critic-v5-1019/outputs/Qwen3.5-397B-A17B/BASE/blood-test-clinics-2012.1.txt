The proposal demands an immediate $350 million investment in unproven technology within a sector where the chain possesses zero expertise. The core device lacks validation through published studies, meaning its accuracy and reliability are entirely unknown. Investing hundreds of millions based solely on a founder's claims without independent verification violates basic due diligence principles.

Furthermore, the health-tech company's refusal to share its methods creates an unacceptable black box. Without transparency, neither the supermarket chain nor external regulators can verify how results are generated. This opacity directly invites the regulatory risks already identified. If regulators later question the tests, the chain faces potential lawsuits, massive fines, and severe reputational damage that could destroy consumer trust in its core grocery business.

The chain currently has no medical infrastructure or expertise to manage clinical operations, interpret results, or handle liability issues arising from incorrect diagnoses. Attempting to build this capability from scratch while relying on a secretive, unvalidated vendor amplifies the probability of catastrophic failure. The promise of exclusivity offers no value if the underlying product is flawed or banned.

The combination of unverified technology, lack of transparency, absent internal expertise, and high regulatory exposure makes this investment financially reckless. The potential downsides far outweigh the theoretical benefits of entering the health market. Proceeding now would expose the company to existential risk without any factual basis for confidence in the technology's performance. The proposal must be rejected in its current form to protect the organization's assets and reputation.

DECISION: NO-GO
