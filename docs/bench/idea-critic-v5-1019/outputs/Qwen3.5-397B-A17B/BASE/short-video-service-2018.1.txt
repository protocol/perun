The proposal seeks $1.75 billion to launch a mobile-only, short-form paid streaming service in 2020. The core value proposition relies on convincing users to pay for content that is currently abundant for free on social platforms. This presents an immediate, fundamental market friction. Viewers are accustomed to accessing short videos without cost, making the conversion to a subscription or ad-supported model highly uncertain.

Furthermore, the restriction preventing television viewing at launch severely limits utility. In 2020, consumers expect seamless cross-device integration. Confining high-budget productions exclusively to small phone screens ignores established viewing habits and reduces the perceived value of the service. The combination of high production costs and a restricted distribution channel creates a precarious financial model. The budgets require significant recoupment, yet the addressable market is artificially constrained by the device limitation and the competitive pressure of free alternatives.

The timeline allows for development, but the strategic flaws are structural, not temporal. Launching with such high fixed costs against entrenched free competitors, while deliberately excluding the living room experience, exposes the venture to unacceptable risk. The capital requirement is massive, and the path to breakeven is obscured by these strategic choices. Without a clear mechanism to overcome the "free" expectation or the device restriction, the likelihood of failure is high. The risks outweigh the potential rewards given the current parameters.

DECISION: NO-GO
