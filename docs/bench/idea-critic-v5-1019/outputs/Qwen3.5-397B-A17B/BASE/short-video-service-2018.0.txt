The proposal seeks $1.75 billion to launch a mobile-first, short-form paid streaming service in spring 2020. The core strategy relies on high-budget content from知名 producers, targeting a niche of professionally made episodes under ten minutes. However, the identified risks present critical structural flaws that threaten viability before operations begin.

First, the market landscape in 2018 already features dominant, free short-form video on social platforms. Convincing users to pay a monthly subscription for content similar to what they access for free elsewhere is an immense behavioral hurdle. Second, the restriction preventing television viewing at launch severely limits utility. In 2020, consumers expect seamless cross-device integration; excluding the living room reduces the value proposition significantly, especially for a premium service.

Third, the financial model appears precarious. High production budgets combined with a narrow distribution window (mobile only) and a two-country rollout create a difficult path to breakeven. The capital requirement of $1.75 billion is substantial, and the risk of failing to recoup these costs is amplified by the unproven willingness of audiences to pay for short-form mobile content. The combination of high fixed costs, intense free competition, and limited platform accessibility suggests a high probability of financial failure.

While the leadership team brings relevant experience, the strategic constraints outlined in the proposal undermine the potential for success. The inability to watch on televisions and the reliance on a paid model for short-form content in a saturated free market are fundamental errors in product-market fit. Proceeding as proposed exposes the organization to unacceptable financial loss without a clear mechanism to overcome these adoption barriers. The risks outweigh the potential rewards in the current configuration.

DECISION: NO-GO
