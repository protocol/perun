The proposal outlines a high-risk entry into a market with an established, larger competitor possessing superior locations and capital. The core strategy relies on shifting profit generation from product sales to membership fees, operating on thin margins for goods. This model faces immediate skepticism regarding consumer willingness to pay upfront fees when a dominant alternative already exists.

The identified risks are severe and currently unmitigated. If shoppers reject the membership fee, the revenue model collapses, as product margins are too small to sustain operations independently. The lack of financial cushion means any operational error could be fatal. Furthermore, the requirement for large sites and a customer base capable of bulk purchasing limits potential locations and demographic reach. Entering this space in 1983 against a entrenched rival without a clear differentiator beyond being a "no-frills" copy creates a precarious position.

While the concept of bulk buying has merit, executing it as a direct challenger to a well-funded incumbent with no unique advantage described is dangerous. The proposal admits that thin margins leave no room for errors, yet offers no specific strategy to ensure error-free execution or to convince customers to switch from the existing competitor. The dependence on a behavioral shift—paying to shop—without proof of concept in this specific competitive landscape makes the immediate expansion plan untenable.

Approving this now would expose the organization to significant financial loss with a high probability of failure. The risks outweigh the potential rewards in the current configuration. The plan requires substantial revision to address customer acquisition, competitive differentiation, and risk mitigation before any capital is committed. Therefore, the proposal cannot be approved in its present form.

DECISION: NO-GO
