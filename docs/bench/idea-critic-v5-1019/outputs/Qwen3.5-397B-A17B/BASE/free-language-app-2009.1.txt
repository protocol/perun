The proposal presents a noble mission but suffers from critical structural flaws that threaten immediate viability. The core revenue mechanism relies on selling translations produced by learners who are, by definition, not yet proficient. This creates an inherent quality contradiction; publishers require accuracy and reliability, which novice learners cannot guarantee without extensive, costly oversight. If the output is poor, no market exists. If the output requires heavy editing by experts, the cost structure destroys the margin, making the "sell later" approach dangerous.

Furthermore, the decision to build the product first and validate the revenue model later is financially reckless. The risks identified—poor translation quality, low conversion from free to paid, and competition from polished incumbents—are not minor hurdles; they are existential threats. Entering a market with established, high-quality competitors using an unproven monetization strategy based on unverified user output invites rapid failure. The team has explicitly acknowledged that a nonprofit structure is unsustainable for their goals, yet they propose a timeline that delays revenue indefinitely.

Without a pilot program to prove that learner translations can meet commercial standards, or without a pre-committed buyer for such services, proceeding to full build is unjustifiable. The assumption that a large user base will naturally solve the quality and monetization issues is speculative. Resources should not be committed to building a platform on the hope that a flawed economic model will correct itself post-launch. The gaps in the business case are too wide to ignore. The project must be halted until the revenue model is validated with real data, not theory. A prototype testing the quality of learner translations against publisher requirements is necessary before any further development. As it stands, the proposal asks to burn resources on a hypothesis that has already shown significant signs of failure in its own risk assessment.

DECISION: NO-GO
