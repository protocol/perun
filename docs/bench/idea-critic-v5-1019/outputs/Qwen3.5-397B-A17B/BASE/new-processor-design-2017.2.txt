The proposal outlines a high-stakes pivot for a smaller processor manufacturer aiming to recover from a previous architectural failure. The core strategy involves launching an all-new desktop line in 2017 featuring significantly higher core counts at lower price points than the dominant rival. While this aggressive pricing and core-density approach could disrupt the market, the identified risks are severe and currently unmitigated.

The company's financial position is described as having "no room for a second miss." Given that the previous all-new design underperformed, proceeding immediately with another entirely new architecture represents an existential gamble. The risks of the rival retaliating with price cuts or superior speed, combined with the uncertainty of software optimization for extra cores, create a volatile environment. If the new architecture fails to deliver immediate performance gains or if software ecosystems do not adapt quickly, the financial fallout could be catastrophic.

Approving this proposal "as proposed, now" ignores the critical need for validation. There is no evidence presented that the architectural flaws of the previous generation have been definitively resolved through prototyping or rigorous testing. Without proof that the new design avoids past pitfalls, or that software partners are ready to leverage the increased core count, the probability of failure remains unacceptably high relative to the company's fragile finances. The potential reward does not justify the immediate risk of total collapse without further verification steps. A "hold" or "revise" stance is necessary to demand concrete data on architectural stability and software readiness before committing scarce capital. Therefore, immediate approval is irresponsible.

DECISION: NO-GO
