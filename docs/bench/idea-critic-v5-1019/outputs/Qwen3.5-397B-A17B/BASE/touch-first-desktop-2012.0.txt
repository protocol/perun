The proposal mandates a radical shift from a proven desktop paradigm to a touch-first interface across all devices, ignoring the distinct input methods of keyboard and mouse users. The identified risks are severe and immediate. Corporate clients, the primary revenue base for this operating system, face high training and support costs due to forced workflow disruptions. Confusion among non-touch users is not a minor hurdle but a fundamental usability failure that threatens customer retention.

Furthermore, the ecosystem relies on developer adoption. Convincing developers to build for an unproven interface without a guaranteed user base creates a chicken-and-egg problem that could stall application availability. Most critically, the inability to revert to the traditional desktop removes user choice and eliminates a safety net for migration. This "all-or-nothing" approach alienates the existing install base before the new value proposition is validated.

Market share erosion by tablets is a real threat, but solving it by dismantling the core desktop experience for all users is disproportionate. The strategy assumes touch is the universal future, disregarding the productivity needs of professional users who rely on precision pointing devices. Implementing this now, without a hybrid transition path or an opt-out mechanism, invites widespread backlash and potential loss of enterprise contracts. The costs of support and the friction of adoption outweigh the speculative gains in tablet relevance. The risks regarding user confusion, developer hesitation, and irreversible deployment make the current plan untenable. Approval requires a revised strategy that respects diverse hardware configurations and offers a gradual transition rather than a forced replacement.

DECISION: NO-GO
