The proposal presents an unacceptable level of risk relative to the potential reward. The core technology lacks validation through published studies, meaning its efficacy and accuracy remain unproven claims rather than established facts. Investing $350 million based solely on a founder's assertion without independent verification violates basic due diligence principles.

Furthermore, the operating model is fundamentally flawed. The supermarket chain possesses no medical expertise, yet the plan requires it to operate clinical testing facilities. This creates a severe competency gap that cannot be bridged quickly or cheaply. The situation is exacerbated by the health-technology company's refusal to share its methods. This lack of transparency prevents any technical assessment and exposes the retailer to significant liability if the devices fail or produce erroneous results.

Regulatory uncertainty adds another layer of danger. If regulators later question the tests, the supermarket chain faces potential fines, legal action, and irreparable reputational damage. The promise of exclusivity offers little protection against these existential threats. Without proven technology, internal expertise, methodological transparency, or regulatory clearance, proceeding with this investment would be reckless. The sheer scale of the financial commitment amplifies every identified weakness. The proposal fails to address how these critical gaps will be closed before deployment. Approval in the current state is impossible.

DECISION: NO-GO
