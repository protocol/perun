The proposal outlines a high-risk entry into a market with an established, larger competitor possessing superior locations and capital. The core strategy relies on shifting profitability from product sales to membership fees, a model that demands immediate, massive consumer adoption to succeed. The identified risks are severe and currently unmitigated. Shoppers have no proven incentive to pay an upfront fee when a dominant alternative exists without one. Thin operating margins provide zero buffer for execution errors, supply chain disruptions, or slower-than-expected membership growth. Furthermore, the requirement for large sites and bulk-buying customers restricts potential locations and limits the total addressable market significantly.

Expanding the number of warehouses before validating the unit economics of the first location amplifies these dangers. Without a network, proprietary data, or financial reserves to absorb early losses, scaling now invites catastrophic failure. The competitor's head start creates a formidable barrier to entry that cannot be overcome simply by copying their format. The assumption that customers will switch loyalty based on a fee-based model is unproven in this specific context. Proceeding with an expansion plan under these conditions ignores the critical need to first prove that the membership value proposition can overcome consumer resistance and competitive pressure. The lack of room for error combined with the capital intensity of large warehouses makes the current trajectory unsustainable. Approval requires evidence that the membership model works in isolation before multiplying the exposure. As presented, the plan lacks the necessary safeguards and validation steps to justify immediate expansion.

DECISION: NO-GO
