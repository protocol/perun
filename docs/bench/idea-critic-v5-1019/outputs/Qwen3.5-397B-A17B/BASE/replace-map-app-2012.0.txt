The proposal seeks to replace a rival's established mapping application with a proprietary solution to gain strategic control. While the ambition to own the user experience and integrate features like turn-by-turn navigation, 3D views, and voice assistance is sound, the execution plan presents critical flaws based on the facts provided.

The primary concern is the significant disparity in data quality. The new application explicitly contains "a lot less data" than the incumbent rival product. In the context of mapping services, data completeness is not merely a feature but the core utility. Users rely on these tools for accurate directions and local business information. The admission that place and business information "may be wrong in places" indicates a high probability of functional failure upon launch.

Furthermore, the strategy ignores the immediate impact on user sentiment. Removing a working, trusted application and replacing it with an inferior alternative will likely cause significant user annoyance. This friction is compounded by the fact that the rival product will no longer be available by default, forcing users to seek workarounds for a service they previously relied upon.

Launching a product with known data deficiencies and a high risk of user dissatisfaction threatens the phone maker's brand reputation. Releasing an incomplete product to achieve a strategic goal of control is premature. The risks of alienating the user base and providing a substandard core utility outweigh the benefits of immediate independence. The proposal lacks a mitigation strategy for the data gap or a phased rollout to manage user transition. Proceeding as proposed in the fall guarantees a negative customer experience and potential reputational damage. The initiative requires a fundamental revision to address data accuracy and user migration before any approval can be considered.

DECISION: NO-GO
