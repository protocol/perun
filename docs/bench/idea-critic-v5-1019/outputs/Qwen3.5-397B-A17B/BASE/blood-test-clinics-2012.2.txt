The proposal presents an unacceptable level of risk relative to the potential reward. The core technology lacks validation through published studies, meaning its efficacy and accuracy remain unproven claims rather than established facts. Investing $350 million based solely on a founder's assertion violates basic due diligence principles, especially when the company refuses to share its methods. This secrecy prevents independent verification and creates a significant liability exposure.

Furthermore, the supermarket chain possesses no medical expertise required to operate clinical testing facilities safely and effectively. Entering a highly regulated medical domain without internal competency or a clear path to acquire it invites operational failure. The explicit warning that regulators may later question the tests suggests a high probability of future legal challenges, fines, or forced shutdowns. Given the secrecy of the methods, addressing regulatory concerns post-investment would be nearly impossible.

The demand for exclusivity locks the chain into a single, unproven vendor, eliminating flexibility to pivot if the technology fails or if superior alternatives emerge. The combination of unverified science, lack of internal expertise, regulatory uncertainty, and vendor opacity creates a perfect storm for financial loss and reputational damage. The potential harm to customers from inaccurate blood tests could destroy the brand's trust overnight. No amount of potential speed or cost savings justifies bypassing fundamental verification and regulatory compliance. The proposal relies on hope rather than evidence. Proceeding now would be negligent. The project must be rejected in its current form until independent, peer-reviewed data confirms the technology works, regulatory pathways are clearly defined, and the vendor agrees to transparent methodology reviews.

DECISION: NO-GO
