The situation presents an immediate, life-threatening crisis where product safety has been compromised in at least one location. Although the contamination occurred post-production and appears localized to a single metropolitan area, the nature of an unknown actor poisoning over-the-counter medication creates an unacceptable risk of escalation. Waiting for confirmation of further incidents or limiting the response to the affected city assumes the perpetrator will not strike elsewhere, a gamble that could result in additional fatalities and irreversible reputational destruction.

The financial risks cited, including the high cost of a nationwide recall and potential loss of market share to competitors, are significant but secondary to the preservation of human life and the long-term viability of the brand. If the company hesitates and more people die, the brand will likely never recover regardless of cost-saving measures taken today. Conversely, a decisive, transparent, and sweeping withdrawal demonstrates an unwavering commitment to consumer safety. This action, while costly in the short term, builds the trust necessary for a future relaunch.

The concern that withdrawing the product signals danger is valid, but the signal sent by *not* withdrawing it would be far worse: that the company prioritizes profits over lives. Competitors taking shelf space is a temporary setback; dead customers are a permanent catastrophe. The proposal to halt production, stop advertising, and execute a nationwide withdrawal is the only ethical and strategically sound path to ensure the brand survives this event. The subsequent plan to relaunch with tamper-resistant packaging addresses the root vulnerability exposed by this attack, turning a crisis into an industry-wide safety standard. Immediate approval is required to mobilize resources before the situation worsens.

DECISION: GO
