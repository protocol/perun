The proposal presents a high-risk venture with significant structural vulnerabilities. The core assumption that consumers will pay a monthly subscription or tolerate ads for content exclusively designed for mobile phones contradicts existing market behavior. In 2018, free short-form video on social platforms is already dominant, creating a formidable barrier to entry for a paid competitor offering a similar format. The restriction preventing television viewing at launch severely limits utility, as users often prefer casting mobile content to larger screens, and this limitation ignores a primary consumption habit.

Financially, the plan is precarious. Raising $1.75 billion to fund high-budget productions from well-known producers creates an immense breakeven threshold. Short episodes under ten minutes generate less engagement time per unit compared to traditional hour-long dramas, making it difficult to justify high production costs or sustain subscriber retention at the proposed price point. The combination of high fixed costs, a restricted viewing ecosystem, and a product format that the market currently consumes for free suggests a low probability of success.

The risks identified in the proposal are not merely hurdles; they are fundamental flaws in the business model. Launching in only two countries does not mitigate the core issue that the value proposition is weak against free alternatives. Without the ability to view on televisions, the service ignores a major segment of streaming usage. Furthermore, the timeline to spring 2020 allows competitors to further entrench their free offerings. Proceeding with this plan as written exposes the company to massive capital loss with little strategic upside. The mismatch between the high cost of goods sold and the perceived value of mobile-only short content makes immediate approval untenable.

DECISION: NO-GO
