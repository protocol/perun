The situation presents an immediate, life-threatening crisis where consumer safety is compromised by external tampering in a specific metropolitan area. Although the contamination originated post-factory and the company's plants remain unaffected, the public cannot distinguish between affected and unaffected bottles. The proposal to withdraw the product nationwide, halt production and advertising, and subsequently relaunch with tamper-resistant packaging addresses the core imperative: preserving human life above all financial considerations.

The risks cited are substantial but secondary to the moral and legal obligation to prevent further deaths. A localized recall is insufficient because the poison could spread beyond the initial metro area through consumer travel or secondary distribution, and the public will likely lose trust in the entire brand if any bottle remains on shelves. The cost of a nationwide recall, while large, is a finite expense compared to the infinite liability of additional fatalities and the total collapse of brand equity. If consumers believe the product is unsafe, market share will vanish regardless of competitor actions; conversely, demonstrating absolute commitment to safety offers the only viable path to eventual recovery.

Withdrawing the product does signal danger, but it signals that the company is responsible and prioritizes customers over profits. Failing to act decisively would signal negligence. Competitors may temporarily gain shelf space, but a destroyed reputation is far harder to reclaim than physical retail positioning. The introduction of tamper-resistant packaging upon relaunch directly mitigates the specific vulnerability exploited here, turning a crisis into an industry-leading safety standard. Hesitation or partial measures in the face of poisoned consumer goods invite catastrophe. The only acceptable course of action is immediate, total withdrawal to secure public trust and ensure no further harm occurs.

DECISION: GO
