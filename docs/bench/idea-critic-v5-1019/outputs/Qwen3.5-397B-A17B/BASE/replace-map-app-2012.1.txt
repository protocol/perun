The proposal seeks to replace a rival's established mapping application with a proprietary solution to gain strategic control. While the ambition to own the user experience and integrate features like turn-by-turn navigation, 3D views, and voice assistance is sound, the execution plan presents unacceptable risks based on the facts provided.

The primary concern is the significant disparity in data quality. The proposal admits the new maps contain far less data than the incumbent, leading to likely errors in place and business information. In a utility as critical as navigation, accuracy is not a luxury but a requirement. Releasing a product known to be inferior in core functionality invites immediate user frustration and erodes trust in the brand.

Furthermore, the strategy involves removing a working, preferred application from the default setting without a seamless transition or guarantee of equal performance. Users who rely on the rival's app for daily commuting or travel will face disruption. The annoyance of losing a reliable tool, combined with the experience of encountering incorrect data in the replacement, creates a high probability of negative public reception and customer churn.

The timing of a fall release for all new and updated phones leaves no room for a phased rollout or beta testing to identify and fix data gaps. Aggregating data from several providers is a complex task that rarely yields perfect results immediately. Launching with acknowledged deficiencies suggests the product is not ready for prime time.

Strategic independence is valuable, but not at the cost of immediate product degradation. The current plan prioritizes speed of deployment over product viability. Without a period dedicated to data verification, user acceptance testing, and a gradual migration strategy, this move threatens the phone maker's reputation more than it secures its independence. The risks of user alienation and functional failure outweigh the benefits of control in this specific configuration.

DECISION: NO-GO
