The proposal addresses critical failures of the previous generation: lateness, high cost, and developer friction. By launching in 2013 with a price point below the rival, the company directly targets the identified consumer sensitivity to cost and the competitor's expensive positioning. Adopting standard PC-like hardware removes a significant barrier for third-party developers, potentially accelerating software availability to compensate for the weak internal studio pipeline. This approach leverages the broader PC development ecosystem rather than relying solely on first-party titles, which are currently scarce.

Allowing used games and offline play serves as a powerful differentiator against a rival heavily focused on restrictive online services. While the rival holds a head start in online infrastructure, consumer sentiment in 2013 is increasingly wary of always-online requirements and restrictions on ownership. Positioning this console as the consumer-friendly alternative could capture significant market share from users alienated by the competitor's policies.

The risks are manageable. Lower margins per unit are an acceptable trade-off for gaining market penetration, especially given the previous generation's loss of ground. Volume growth can offset reduced per-unit profitability. The lack of new first-party software at launch is mitigated by the ease of development for third parties; if the hardware is familiar and the audience is price-sensitive, external studios will prioritize this platform. The concern regarding reduced consumer spending on dedicated consoles is best countered by a lower entry price, making the device more accessible during tighter economic times.

Delaying to build more first-party titles or improve online services would repeat the mistake of being late. The market opportunity exists now because the rival has created openings with high prices and restrictive policies. Executing this strategy immediately capitalizes on competitor weaknesses and aligns with demonstrated consumer preferences for value and flexibility.
