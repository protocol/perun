The proposal presents a fundamentally flawed economic model that ignores basic unit economics. The core offering promises free delivery on small orders with no minimum spend. Since the cost to employ staff directly and maintain vehicles in major cities exceeds the margin on typical snack or book purchases, every single transaction generates a loss. Scaling this model by expanding to more cities quickly will not fix the underlying deficit; it will only accelerate the rate at which capital is consumed.

The reliance on direct employment rather than a flexible contractor model creates a rigid, high-fixed-cost structure. This approach demands significant upfront capital for hiring, training, and vehicle acquisition in each new market before revenue can be validated. Furthermore, the risk that customers will only utilize the service while it is heavily subsidized suggests there is no genuine product-market fit at a sustainable price point. If demand vanishes once subsidies are removed, the business has no path to profitability.

Raising venture money to fund a strategy where the marginal cost of fulfillment is guaranteed to be higher than the marginal revenue is financially irresponsible. The plan lacks a mechanism to ever achieve positive contribution margins. Without a minimum order value to cover logistics or a delivery fee to offset labor costs, the company is essentially paying customers to take products, a strategy that leads inevitably to insolvency regardless of how rapidly it expands. The identified risks are not merely potential hurdles; they are certain outcomes based on the proposed operational structure. Approving this plan would result in the immediate and total loss of invested capital with no realistic prospect of return. The fundamental assumptions regarding cost structure and customer behavior render the venture unviable in its current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
