The proposal introduces a device with a screen exceeding five inches and a stylus, positioning it between a smartphone and a tablet. In 2011, the standard screen size is approximately four inches. The plan is to launch this as a premium device alongside the existing lineup.

Significant risks threaten this venture. Reviewers are likely to criticize the screen size as excessive for a phone, potentially damaging the brand's reputation for practicality. Consumer demand for a stylus appears uncertain, suggesting a feature that may go unused by the majority. The device faces an identity crisis: it risks being too small to effectively replace a tablet while simultaneously being too large to serve as a convenient primary phone. Furthermore, launching this product threatens internal cannibalization, drawing sales away from the company's established phone models rather than capturing new market share.

Without data indicating a shift in consumer preference toward larger screens or proven demand for stylus input on mobile devices, proceeding carries excessive risk. The "uncanny valley" positioning between two distinct form factors often leads to market failure, as the product satisfies neither use case completely. Launching a premium device with such polarizing characteristics could alienate the core customer base while failing to attract tablet users. The potential for negative reviews to define the product narrative before it gains traction is high. Given the lack of tools, network, or files to validate a hidden market opportunity, the logical course is to rely on the explicit risks presented. The uncertainties regarding size perception, utility of the stylus, and channel conflict outweigh the potential benefits of being first to market with this specific configuration. Approval should be withheld until these fundamental viability questions are resolved through further study or prototype testing.

DECISION: NO-GO
