The proposal outlines a direct-to-consumer model for assembling and selling IBM-compatible computers at prices below established brands. The core strategy relies on incorporating the entity, maintaining a strict direct-order channel, and funding growth exclusively through generated revenue. This approach eliminates the capital expenditure associated with physical retail locations and third-party reseller margins, directly enabling the promised lower price point.

The risks identified are significant but not insurmountable given the specific market context of 1984. While buyers traditionally prefer seeing products before purchase, the substantial cost savings offered here can overcome hesitation, particularly among technically literate early adopters who understand the components. The lack of a physical service network is a valid concern; however, this can be mitigated by designing systems with standardized, easily replaceable parts and offering mail-in repair services or clear documentation for user-level troubleshooting. The dominance of established brands with large sales channels is precisely why this disruptive pricing model is necessary; competing on brand recognition is impossible, but competing on price and specification flexibility is viable.

Regarding supplier credit, starting with revenue-funded growth means the company does not initially rely on large lines of credit. As volume increases and payment history is established with parts suppliers, credit terms can be negotiated progressively. The "no stores" constraint is actually the primary competitive advantage, allowing overhead to remain low enough to undercut major competitors. The direct model also fosters a closer relationship with the customer base, allowing for rapid iteration based on feedback. The risks are operational challenges to be managed, not fundamental flaws that prevent the business model from functioning. The market opportunity for affordable, compatible computing power is vast, and the proposed structure is the most efficient way to capture it without heavy upfront debt.

DECISION: GO
