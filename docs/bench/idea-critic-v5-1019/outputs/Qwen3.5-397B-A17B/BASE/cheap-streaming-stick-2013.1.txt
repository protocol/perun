The proposal addresses previous failures by shifting from a standalone device to a low-cost, phone-dependent dongle. This eliminates the need for a remote and on-screen menu, directly mitigating the risk of user confusion regarding navigation, as the familiar smartphone interface becomes the controller. The strategy leverages existing user behavior rather than forcing new habits.

While the low price point suggests minimal hardware margin, the primary objective appears to be ecosystem expansion rather than immediate unit profitability. By placing video and music services on televisions, the company increases engagement and subscription retention, which typically yields higher lifetime value than hardware sales. The open developer kit is a critical differentiator; it allows the application catalog to grow organically without the company bearing the full cost of content acquisition upfront. This approach directly counters the risk of a limited launch library and the dominance of established competitors with large catalogs.

The risks are manageable. The "confusion" risk is nullified by the phone-centric design. The margin risk is acceptable if the strategic goal is market penetration and service adoption. The app gap is temporary and solvable through the open kit, which incentivizes third-party innovation faster than internal development could. Waiting for a perfect catalog or higher margins would likely result in missed market opportunities, especially given the historical difficulty of selling dedicated television devices. The low barrier to entry for consumers creates a volume opportunity that premium boxes cannot match.

The proposal represents a pragmatic pivot that turns previous weaknesses into strengths by relying on the user's existing hardware. It minimizes upfront investment while maximizing potential reach. The timing is appropriate to capture the growing trend of second-screen interaction. Approval should be granted to proceed immediately with this lean, ecosystem-focused approach.
