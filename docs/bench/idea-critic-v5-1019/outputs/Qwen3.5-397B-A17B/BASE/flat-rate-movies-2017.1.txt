The proposal suggests slashing the monthly fee to approximately ten dollars while allowing members to watch one film daily. Under the current model, the company pays theaters the full ticket price for every visit. With average ticket prices often exceeding ten dollars individually, a single monthly visit by a member would likely exhaust the entire subscription revenue. Allowing up to 30 visits per month creates a scenario where heavy users could generate costs thirty times higher than their contribution.

The plan relies on future monetization through data sales and studio deals to offset these immediate, guaranteed losses. However, the proposal explicitly admits these revenue streams are unproven. Banking on speculative future income to cover definite, massive operational deficits is financially unsound. The math indicates that rapid membership growth under these terms would accelerate cash burn rather than build a sustainable base.

Furthermore, the model faces significant external resistance. Theater chains are identified as likely to refuse cooperation, which would render the service useless to subscribers. If theaters reject the terms because the reimbursement model threatens their own margins or operational stability, the core value proposition collapses immediately. Even with a new owner possessing cash reserves, subsidizing a business model where every additional active user deepens the loss per unit, without a confirmed path to profitability or partner buy-in, is reckless.

The risks outlined are not minor hurdles but fundamental flaws in the unit economics and supply chain viability. The cost structure guarantees that high engagement leads to insolvency, and the proposed solutions depend on variables that currently do not exist. Approving this as proposed would commit capital to a strategy with a high probability of total failure before the hypothetical future revenues can materialize. The lack of proven alternative income and the high likelihood of theater boycotts make immediate execution impossible.

DECISION: NO-GO
