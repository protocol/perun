Steelman: A minority stake in a 16-unit burrito chain offers a low-capital option to learn about the "fast-casual" segment without operational distraction, preserving the right to scale later if the model proves viable.

**Skeptic Attack**: The primary failure mode is not operational drag, but strategic irrelevance. In 1998, the gap between "fast food" and "sit-down" was widening. If the chain cannot prove unit economics that support expansion beyond 16 locations, the investment becomes a dormant asset. The higher price point and skilled labor requirement are features, not bugs, of the target demographic, but they prevent immediate integration into the giant's core supply chain, isolating the venture.

**Better Way**: The alternative is "do nothing" or internal R&D. Internal development would be slower and brand-diluting. Acquiring a larger competitor is too costly and risky. This specific proposal—minority stake with independence—is the cheapest, safest way to buy a "real options" call on the sector. It avoids the "not invented here" syndrome and management diversion risks cited, as the chain runs independently.

**Fit & Pre-mortem**: This fits a growth vision seeking new revenue pillars. If it fails, the loss is capped at the minority stake (two-way door). If it succeeds, the "right to invest more" captures the upside. The cost of delay is ceding the fast-casual definition to competitors.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria**: The facts provided (16 units, made-to-order, higher price) confirm a distinct market position. The risks (scale, labor, price) are inherent to the segment, not defects in this specific deal structure. The deal structure itself (minority, independent) mitigates the execution risks.

**Decision**: The proposal is a classic "real options" play: limited downside, uncapped upside, and minimal operational friction. The risks raised are characteristics of the asset class, not reasons to reject this specific entry vehicle. Proceed with the minority stake, treating the investment as a paid learning experiment with a predefined follow-on trigger.
