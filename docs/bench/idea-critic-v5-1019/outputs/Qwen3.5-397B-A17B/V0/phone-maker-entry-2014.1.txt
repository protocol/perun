A pre-mortem reveals catastrophic failure: the 3D feature is a gimmick that drains battery and confuses users, while the single carrier deal caps adoption below critical mass. The "visual search" feature depends on perfect lighting and angles, creating friction compared to simple text search.

The cost of delay is low; the market for premium phones is growing, and waiting allows the retailer to negotiate better carrier terms or improve app support. Conversely, launching now risks brand damage if the hardware is perceived as inferior to established competitors.

The strongest attack is that the proposed differentiators (3D, visual search) solve non-existent problems for the mass market, while the limitations (app catalog, carrier lock-in) are deal-breakers for premium buyers. The evidence suggests premium buyers prioritize ecosystem and network flexibility over novel display effects. The proposal fails to demonstrate why a user would switch from a market leader with thousands more apps to a device with a gimmick and restricted availability.
