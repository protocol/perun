The claim is that a niche luxury EV maker can instantly pivot to mass-market volume production with a cheaper sedan, funded by refundable deposits, despite zero track record in high-volume manufacturing or supply chain scaling.

The steelman: Leveraging brand hype from low-volume successes to secure upfront cash flow via deposits, forcing the supply chain and production lines to scale rapidly to meet anticipated demand for a lower-margin vehicle.

However, the failure hypothesis is overwhelming. The company has never produced at scale; transitioning from hand-built luxury units to hundreds of thousands of mass-market cars involves fundamentally different engineering, quality control, and logistics. History shows "production hell" is the graveyard of such ambitions. Scaling battery supply sharply requires capital and partnerships the company likely lacks, creating a bottleneck that deposits cannot solve.

Refundable deposits are a liability, not revenue. If production delays occur—which is highly probable given the inexperience—depositors will demand refunds, causing a cash crunch precisely when capital is most needed. Furthermore, lowering the price while lacking economies of scale destroys margins. Without a proven path to profitability per unit, every car sold increases losses. The proposal assumes execution capability that does not exist and treats customer intent (deposits) as guaranteed sales, ignoring the high churn risk if delivery slips.

The "do nothing" alternative (continuing low-volume luxury sales) preserves cash and brand exclusivity. A "better way" would be securing non-refundable pre-orders or firm financing based on a proven prototype before committing to mass production tooling. The cost of delay here is low compared to the cost of failure, which includes bankruptcy and total brand collapse.

There is no evidence provided that the battery supply chain can expand fast enough or that the manufacturing team can handle the complexity jump. The risks are existential, the door is one-way (once capital is burned on failed tooling, it is gone), and the kill criteria are absent. The proposal relies on hope rather than verified operational capacity.

DECISION: NO-GO
