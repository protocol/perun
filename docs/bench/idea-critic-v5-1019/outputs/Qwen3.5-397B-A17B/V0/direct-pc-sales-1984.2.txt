The claim is that a direct-to-consumer, low-cost IBM-compatible computer assembler can succeed in 1984 by incorporating and growing strictly through revenue, bypassing retail and service networks.

Steelman: By eliminating retail margins and overhead, the company can offer significantly lower prices, appealing to price-sensitive early adopters who prioritize value over physical inspection or brand prestige, allowing organic growth without debt.

However, critical failure modes exist. First, the "try before you buy" expectation is dominant in 1984; high-ticket electronics are rarely purchased sight-unseen by the mass market. Second, the lack of a service network is a fatal flaw for business customers who require uptime; a broken machine means no support, destroying reputation. Third, suppliers may deny credit to an unproven entity without physical assets or retail partnerships, choking cash flow despite the "revenue-only" growth plan. Established competitors can easily engage in a price war, leveraging their scale to squeeze margins until this lean operation collapses.

Better ways include establishing a single flagship showroom for demonstrations while maintaining direct sales, or partnering with existing mail-order catalogues to gain trust without building a full retail chain. Ignoring the service gap is not an option; a third-party repair network must be secured before scaling.

The strongest attack is the service vacuum: if a unit fails, the customer has no recourse, leading to immediate brand toxicity in a niche where word-of-mouth is vital. The proposal's strict "no stores" rule prevents even a minimal trust-building presence. The assumption that price alone overcomes the lack of tactile verification and support is unproven and risky given the era's consumer behavior.

Without a concrete plan to address service logistics or supplier credit hurdles, and relying solely on price in a market demanding trust and support, the risk of immediate cash flow strangulation and reputational collapse is too high.

DECISION: NO-GO
