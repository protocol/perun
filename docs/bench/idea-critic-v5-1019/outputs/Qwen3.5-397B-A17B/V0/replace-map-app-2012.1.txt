Steelman: Replacing a rival's mature mapping application with a proprietary solution grants strategic control, eliminates dependency on a competitor, and enables deep OS integration (turn-by-turn, 3D, voice) that third-party APIs restrict.

However, the proposal fails the "fit" and "pre-mortem" checks. The admission that data is "a lot less" and business information "may be wrong" constitutes a fundamental quality deficit. In mapping, utility is binary: if users cannot find a location or are routed incorrectly, the feature is broken, not "beta." Swapping a working default for a degraded one violates the core value proposition of the device.

The pre-mortem is clear: Users updated in the fall will immediately encounter missing businesses and navigation errors. Trust in the brand erodes instantly. Unlike a social network or media player where content gaps are tolerable, navigation errors cause real-world harm (missed flights, lost time). The "annoyance" of removing a working app is an understatement; it is an active degradation of the product experience.

The "do nothing" alternative (keeping the rival's app while building the own version in parallel) carries near-zero risk compared to the proposed "big bang" switch. The proposal lacks a "go-if" condition, such as "only switch if independent testing shows <1% deviation in top 100 cities." Without a quality parity gate, launching this in the fall is reckless.

The cost of delay (waiting another year to mature data) is negligible compared to the cost of shipping a broken core utility. The strategic desire for control does not justify releasing a demonstrably inferior product to all users immediately. The proposal must be revised to a phased rollout or a dual-default strategy until data parity is proven. As written, it trades immediate user satisfaction for long-term strategic positioning, a trade-off the market will punish before the strategy pays off.

DECISION: NO-GO
