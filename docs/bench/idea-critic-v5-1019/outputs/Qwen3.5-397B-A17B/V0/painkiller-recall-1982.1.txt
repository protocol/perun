The claim is that a nationwide withdrawal, production halt, and advertising stop are necessary despite the poisoning being localized and post-factory. The steelman: immediate total recall is the only way to preserve long-term brand trust and prevent copycat crimes, outweighing massive short-term financial loss and competitor gains.

However, critical assumptions remain unverified. First, the scope of "unknown person" and "one metropolitan area" relies on current data which may be incomplete; without forensic confirmation that the tampering mechanism cannot be replicated elsewhere, a local fix is insufficient. Second, the assumption that competitors will permanently capture shelf space ignores the likelihood that a dominant brand can reclaim position once safety is proven, especially if the alternative is being associated with deaths. Third, the risk of signaling danger is real, but the signal of *not* acting while people die is catastrophic to the brand's existence.

The "do nothing" or "local recall only" alternatives fail the pre-mortem: if one more person dies outside the initial zone due to inaction, the company faces existential litigation and permanent reputational destruction. The cost of delay is measured in human lives, which renders financial calculations of recall costs secondary in the immediate term. The "very large sum" is a known cost; the cost of inaction is unbounded liability and potential criminal negligence charges.

While the proposal risks market share, the tamper-resistant packaging relaunch addresses the root cause of vulnerability. The strongest attack—that this is an overreaction to a local crime—fails because the product category (ingestible OTC medicine) creates a unique psychological contract with consumers that, once broken by death, requires absolute demonstration of safety to repair. A partial recall suggests the company is weighing profits against lives; a total recall signals that lives are the only metric that matters.

The kill criterion is clear: if any additional poisoning occurs after a limited recall, the company is finished. Therefore, the only viable path to survival is the proposed total withdrawal.

DECISION: GO
