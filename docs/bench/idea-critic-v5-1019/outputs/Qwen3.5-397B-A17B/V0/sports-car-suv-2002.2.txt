Steelman: Leveraging brand equity to enter the high-margin luxury SUV segment via partnership mitigates development risk while funding core sports-car operations.

**Skeptic**: The claim assumes loyal enthusiasts will tolerate brand dilution. In 2002, "sports car" implied purity; a heavy SUV contradicts this DNA. If the core base revolts, the brand loses its only defensible moat. The proposal admits this risk but offers no mitigation other than high pricing, which may not offset reputation loss.

**Better-way**: Alternatives include: (1) Do nothing and focus on niche dominance; (2) Create a sub-brand to isolate the SUV from the main logo; (3) License the platform without badging it as a sports car. The current plan ignores the sub-brand option, which preserves the parent brand's integrity while capturing the market.

**Kill-criteria**: This is a one-way door; once the brand is associated with "soft" luxury, returning to hardcore exclusivity is nearly impossible. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of brand erosion. A pre-mortem suggests failure occurs if initial sales rely entirely on non-enthusiasts, leaving the core base alienated. The proposal lacks a specific metric to measure brand sentiment shift before full commitment.

**Fit**: The strategy conflicts with the "north star" of being a pure sports-car maker. Entering the SUV market in 2002 is a trend chase, not a vision alignment. The shared platform reduces financial risk but increases identity risk.

**Evidence**: No data provided on customer willingness to buy an SUV from this specific brand. The assumption that "high price" equals "acceptance" is unverified. Established luxury makers have decades of comfort credibility; this brand has none.

**Decision**: The proposal fails to address the existential threat to brand identity. Without a sub-brand strategy or verified customer demand, the risk of alienating the loyal base outweighs the potential revenue. The plan must be revised to include brand isolation mechanisms or concrete validation of enthusiast acceptance.

DECISION: NO-GO
