The claim is that a sub-£400, license-free electric tricycle can succeed via mail order despite severe performance and weather limitations. The steelman posits that low cost and regulatory exemption create a massive volume market for short-distance utility travel, outweighing comfort deficits.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the 1985 context. First, the "large numbers" mail-order model assumes consumer trust in an unproven, low-speed vehicle without a test ride, which is historically unlikely for big-ticket hardware. Second, the British winter constraint is not a minor seasonal dip but a structural barrier; a vehicle with no weather protection and short range becomes unusable for half the year, destroying its value proposition as a primary transport alternative. Third, safety objections regarding low visibility among traffic present a liability risk that could halt sales post-launch, a risk amplified by the lack of a licensing framework to enforce safety standards on users.

The "better way" analysis suggests that selling critical extras (weather protection, lights) separately fragments the value proposition and invites criticism that the base unit is unsafe or incomplete out of the box. A bundled, weather-ready package, even at a higher price, would be more viable. Furthermore, relying on a large appliance maker for assembly may introduce quality control mismatches for automotive-grade safety requirements.

The pre-mortem indicates a scenario where winter arrives, usage plummets, safety groups highlight accidents involving invisible trikes, and the mail-order return rate spikes due to unmet expectations of utility. The cost of delay is low; waiting to validate demand with a smaller pilot or improving the base spec (weather protection) before a massive January launch reduces the risk of brand destruction.

The proposal fails to address the core contradiction: selling a weather-exposed, low-range vehicle as a mass-market solution in a rainy, dark climate. The kill criterion is immediate: if winter usability cannot be guaranteed or demonstrated, the mass launch will fail.

DECISION: NO-GO
