**Steelman:** Rapid, subsidized urban delivery creates a defensible habit and network effect that justifies initial losses, assuming venture capital remains abundant to fund expansion before unit economics must balance.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "what must be true" test on three fronts. First, unit economics: free delivery on small orders guarantees a negative margin per transaction. Without a minimum order, volume amplifies losses rather than covering fixed costs. Second, operational leverage: employing staff directly in multiple cities creates massive fixed costs and management complexity before demand is proven, unlike an asset-light or gig-model approach. Third, behavioral risk: if usage relies on subsidies, churn will be catastrophic the moment prices rise or promotions end. The "expand quickly" mandate burns cash on unproven markets before fixing the broken core model in a single city.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "wait" option preserves capital. A superior alternative involves: (1) setting a minimum order value to protect margin; (2) using independent contractors to vary costs with demand; (3) proving retention without subsidies in one dense neighborhood before raising venture funds or expanding.

Cause: The company ran out of cash because the cost to acquire and serve a customer permanently exceeded their lifetime value, driven by the refusal to charge for delivery or enforce order minimums. The direct employment model made it impossible to downsize quickly when growth slowed.

**Verdict:** The proposal as written is a capital destruction machine. It explicitly rejects the only mechanisms (minimums, fees, variable labor) that could make the unit economics work. Expanding this broken model quickly only accelerates insolvency. The risks raised in the prompt are not merely possible; they are mathematical certainties under the proposed constraints.

**Kill Criteria:** Do not proceed without a path to positive contribution margin per order. Do not expand to a second city until the first demonstrates retention without subsidies.
