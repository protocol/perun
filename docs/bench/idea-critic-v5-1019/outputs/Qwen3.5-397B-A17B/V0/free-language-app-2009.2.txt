The claim is that a free language-learning platform, powered by user-generated translations sold to publishers, can succeed without immediate revenue or a nonprofit structure. The steelman: leveraging real-world translation tasks creates a sustainable flywheel where learning produces value, funding the platform without charging learners.

However, three fatal flaws exist in the 2009 context provided. First, the quality risk is existential; publishers require professional-grade accuracy, while beginners produce errors. Without a costly expert review layer (unfunded), the product is unsellable. Second, the "revenue later" strategy ignores the cash burn of hosting and development; with no initial capital and an unproven model, the project will likely collapse before monetization is viable. Third, the market mismatch is severe: established competitors offer polished, structured curricula, whereas this model offers fragmented, task-based learning that may not appeal to serious students or generate consistent translation volume.

The "do nothing" or "nonprofit" alternatives were dismissed too quickly. A nonprofit structure could unlock grants and volunteer expert reviewers to solve the quality gap, which the current for-profit-but-unfunded model cannot. The "cheaper path" is to validate the translation market first with a manual service before building a full web app.

The pre-mortem reveals the most likely failure: the platform launches, learners produce low-quality translations, publishers reject the data, revenue never materializes, and the founders run out of personal funds within six months. The cost of delay is low; waiting to prove the translation quality manually saves significant wasted engineering effort.

There is no evidence provided that publishers want amateur translations or that learners will complete tasks accurately enough to be valuable. The assumption that "learning by doing" aligns perfectly with "commercial translation needs" is unverified and risky. Without a verified buyer for the output or a mechanism to guarantee quality, the business model is speculative.

DECISION: NO-GO
