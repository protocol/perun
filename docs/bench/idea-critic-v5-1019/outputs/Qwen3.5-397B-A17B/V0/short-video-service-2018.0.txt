The claim asserts that users will pay a monthly subscription and tolerate ads for high-budget, phone-only short episodes, despite abundant free alternatives on social platforms. The steelman is that professional quality and exclusive talent create a differentiated premium tier that free user-generated content cannot match, justifying a dual revenue model.

However, the "better-way" analysis reveals a critical flaw: the proposal ignores the dominant market behavior of 2018, where short-form video was successfully monetized via ads alone (YouTube, emerging TikTok), not subscriptions. Competing directly with free content by gating it behind a paywall, while simultaneously including an ad-tier, creates a confused value proposition. Why would a user pay for short content they are accustomed to getting free, especially when the content is restricted to phones and unavailable on televisions? This artificially limits the viewing window and reduces perceived value compared to full-length streaming services available on all screens.

The pre-mortem indicates a high probability of failure: the service launches in 2020 with massive debt ($1.75B) and high burn rates from producer fees. With no TV presence, the service misses the primary living-room engagement driver for premium video. The "kill criterion" here is the assumption that "short" equals "mobile-only." This is a false constraint; short content thrives on TVs via ambient viewing. Furthermore, the financial model requires recouping huge budgets before breakeven, yet the pricing power for phone-only shorts is unproven and likely negligible compared to full-series libraries.

The strongest attack is the mismatch between the cost structure (film-grade budgets) and the consumption mode (casual, mobile, short-form). Free platforms already satisfy this need with lower-cost, algorithmically driven content. Introducing a payment barrier for a format defined by frictionless, free consumption is a strategic error. The proposal fails to demonstrate why users would switch from free to paid for the same duration of content, especially with reduced device compatibility.

DECISION: NO-GO
