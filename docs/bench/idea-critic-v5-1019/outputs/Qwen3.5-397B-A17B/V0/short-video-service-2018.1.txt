The claim assumes viewers will pay a premium for short-form, mobile-only content despite the dominance of free, algorithm-driven alternatives on social platforms. The steelman is that professional curation and high production values create a differentiated brand worthy of subscription, targeting an underserved affluent demographic.

However, the "better-way" analysis reveals fatal flaws. The proposal ignores Chesterton's Fence: free short video exists because the unit economics of attention favor ad-supported, infinite-scroll models over paid gates for sub-10-minute content. Competing directly with entrenched giants (TikTok, YouTube Shorts) on their home turf (mobile) without a unique distribution hook is strategically unsound. Furthermore, excluding television access at launch artificially caps the addressable market and ignores consumer behavior where "short" content is often consumed on larger screens during downtime.

The pre-mortem is clear: the service launches to modest initial sign-ups driven by marketing spend, but churn skyrockets as users realize the library is too small to justify a monthly fee compared to free alternatives. The $1.75 billion raise creates immense pressure to recoup costs quickly, leading to panic spending on content that fails to find an audience. The "high-budget" approach for short episodes inflates cost-per-hour drastically compared to traditional series or user-generated content, making breakeven nearly impossible without massive scale that the mobile-only restriction prevents.

The kill criteria are missing. There is no defined metric for early cancellation if subscriber acquisition costs exceed lifetime value in the first quarter. The risk of building a specialized infrastructure for a behavior (paid mobile shorts) that may not exist is too high. The "do nothing" or "partner with existing platforms" options offer significantly lower capital risk. The proposal demands irreversible capital commitment based on unverified assumptions about willingness to pay for a format that is currently monetized via attention, not subscription.

DECISION: NO-GO
