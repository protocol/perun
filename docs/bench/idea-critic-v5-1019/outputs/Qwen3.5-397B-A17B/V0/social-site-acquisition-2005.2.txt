**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform secures a dominant, hard-to-replicate youth audience for $580M, creating a unique moat for future ad monetization that organic growth cannot match.

**Attack:** The core failure mode is not competition, but behavioral volatility. In 2005, social networking lacks a proven revenue model; "large audience" does not equal "profitable inventory." The $580M price assumes current growth rates persist indefinitely, ignoring the high historical churn of youth-centric platforms where tastes shift rapidly. If the platform's cultural relevance dips even slightly post-acquisition, the valuation collapses because the asset has no hard infrastructure or content library to fall back on—only active users. The risk of turning growth into revenue is not just "unproven"; it is the primary value driver, yet the proposal offers no concrete mechanism to achieve this without alienating the very user base being purchased.

**Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "wait-and-see" alternative is superior. Let the platform prove its monetization viability independently over the next 12–18 months. If they succeed, the acquisition cost may rise, but the risk of buying a "fad" at peak valuation disappears. If they fail to monetize, the company saves $580M. A smaller strategic investment or partnership could secure exclusivity options without the full balance sheet risk.

**Kill Criteria:** The proposal fails the reversibility test. Once $580M is spent and the culture integrated, exiting is impossible without massive write-downs and brand damage. The cost of delay (losing the deal) is speculative; the cost of error (overpaying for a dying trend) is catastrophic and immediate. Without a guaranteed path to ad revenue conversion verified by current data—not projections—the deal relies entirely on the "greater fool" theory of valuation.

The strongest attack is the lack of a proven revenue engine. The proposal admits monetization is unproven yet demands a premium price based solely on user count. This is a classic bubble dynamic. The evidence provided confirms high risk and zero proof of return.

DECISION: NO-GO
