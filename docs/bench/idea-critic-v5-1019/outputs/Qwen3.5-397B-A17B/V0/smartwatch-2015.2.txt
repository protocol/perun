The claim is that a phone manufacturer should enter the smartwatch market in 2015 despite historical poor sales and dependency constraints. The steelman posits that unlike prior standalone failures, a watch tightly integrated with a dominant phone ecosystem creates unique utility (notifications, health tracking) that justifies a secondary screen, turning the "dependency" into a feature rather than a bug.

However, the skeptic identifies a fatal flaw in the value proposition: if the device requires the phone to function and offers only one day of battery life, it adds friction (daily charging, carrying two devices) without solving a problem the phone doesn't already address in a pocket. The "modest" sales of competitors suggest the market has not yet found a compelling reason to wear a computer on the wrist. The proposal relies on the assumption that brand loyalty alone can overcome the lack of a standalone use case, which is unproven.

The better-way analysis suggests that "doing nothing" or waiting for battery technology to improve (allowing multi-day life) or for sensors to become accurate enough to stand alone (cellular/GPS) is superior. Releasing a dependent, daily-charge device now risks branding the category as a "nice-to-have" accessory rather than an essential tool, potentially poisoning the well for future iterations. The cost of delay is low compared to the reputational risk of launching a product perceived as incomplete.

The pre-mortem reveals that the app store will remain empty because developers won't build for a niche, dependent device, creating a vicious cycle of low utility. The kill criteria are missing: there is no defined metric for adoption success or a pivot point if the "second screen" narrative fails to resonate. Without a clear path to standalone utility or a breakthrough in battery life, this proposal attempts to force a market that isn't ready, betting on ecosystem lock-in before the product value is self-evident. The risk of cannibalizing phone sales with a distracting, high-maintenance accessory outweighs the potential incremental revenue.

DECISION: NO-GO
