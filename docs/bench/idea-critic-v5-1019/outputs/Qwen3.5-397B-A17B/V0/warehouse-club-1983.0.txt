**Steelman:** A membership-based bulk retailer can succeed against an entrenched incumbent by strictly limiting SKUs to drive volume, negotiating superior supplier terms, and treating membership fees as the primary profit center rather than a side revenue stream.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is consumer rejection of the fee model when a free alternative exists. However, if the per-unit savings on essential goods exceed the membership cost within 2–3 visits, the fee becomes a sunk-cost incentive for loyalty rather than a barrier. The "do nothing" option cedes the market to the incumbent. A "high-margin, low-volume" alternative fails the core value proposition of bulk buying. A "broad assortment" model destroys the operational efficiency required for thin margins.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a strategy of disruptive pricing for value-conscious families. If this fails, it will likely be due to over-expansion before unit economics are proven or diluting the limited-SKU focus. This is a two-way door for the first few locations; if a specific warehouse fails, it can be closed with limited systemic damage. The cost of delay is high: waiting allows the incumbent to solidify supplier exclusivity and real estate dominance.

**Evidence & Constraints:**
The proposal acknowledges the incumbent's cash advantage but correctly identifies that thin margins force operational discipline the competitor may lack due to complexity. The risk of "shoppers not paying" is mitigated if the math (savings > fee) is visibly obvious at the shelf. The requirement for large sites is a constraint, not a killer, provided locations are in lower-rent industrial zones accessible by car.

**Decision:**
The model relies on a verified behavioral loop: fee payment drives loyalty, loyalty drives volume, volume drives supplier leverage, leverage drives lower prices. The risks are manageable through disciplined, phased expansion rather than immediate saturation. The kill criterion for future expansion should be: if any new location does not achieve positive operating cash flow within 12 months, halt further openings in that region.

DECISION: GO
