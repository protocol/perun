**Steelman:** A lean, direct-to-consumer model can undercut legacy pricing by eliminating retail markups and inventory overhead, targeting cost-sensitive buyers who prioritize value over physical inspection.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is trust: customers in 1984 rarely buy high-ticket electronics without physical interaction. Established brands offer immediate service and credit terms this startup lacks. "Do nothing" preserves capital but forfeits the price advantage. A hybrid model (limited showrooms or reseller partnerships) mitigates trust issues but erodes the cost edge.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a "low-cost leader" strategy but assumes the market values price over tactile verification. If suppliers deny credit due to lack of collateral, assembly halts immediately. If returns spike because units differ from expectations, cash flow collapses. The "one-way door" is incorporating and committing to a pure direct model before validating demand; a failure here creates legal and debt baggage with no assets to liquidate.

**Evidence & Constraints:**
No data exists on 1984 consumer tolerance for mail-order computers. The proposal assumes parts are available on credit, which is unverified for a new entity. The risk of supplier refusal is critical; without parts, the business cannot start.

**Decision:**
The proposal ignores the fatal dependency on supplier credit and the unproven consumer behavior of buying computers sight-unseen in this era. While the cost structure is sound, the operational premises are unsupported assumptions. Incorporating now locks the company into a rigid model before validating these two pillars. The correct move is a capped, time-boxed pilot: secure a single supplier credit line and sell a small batch via direct order *before* incorporation and full-scale growth.

However, the prompt demands a decision on the proposal *as stated* (incorporate now, grow from revenue). Given the unverified credit risk and the high probability of cash flow failure without established supplier trust, approving the full plan now is reckless.

DECISION: NO-GO
