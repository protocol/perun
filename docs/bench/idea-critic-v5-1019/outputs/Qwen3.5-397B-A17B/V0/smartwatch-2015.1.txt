The claim is that a phone manufacturer should enter the smartwatch market in 2015 despite historical poor sales and hardware limitations. The steelman: leveraging an existing phone user base and ecosystem integration can overcome the "modest" historical performance of standalone smartwatches, turning a wrist accessory into a necessary ecosystem lock-in rather than a standalone utility.

However, the skepticism is severe. The proposal admits the watch requires the phone to be useful, creating a hard ceiling on the total addressable market limited strictly to current phone owners. With battery life capped at one day and prior market data showing weak demand, the risk of inventory write-downs across "several materials and price levels" is high. The "small app store" suggests a chicken-and-egg problem: developers won't build for a niche device, and users won't buy a device with few apps.

The "better way" analysis suggests a cheaper alternative: instead of a full hardware launch across multiple materials, release a single, low-cost tracker band to validate demand for health metrics without the complexity of an app store or multiple SKUs. This reduces exposure if the "second screen" value proposition fails.

The pre-mortem reveals a likely failure mode: the device becomes a costly niche accessory that cannibalizes phone upgrade cycles without generating sufficient recurring revenue from apps to justify the R&D and inventory costs. The "one-day battery" is a critical friction point that competitors may exploit.

While ecosystem integration is a valid strategic moat, launching a multi-SKU hardware line with known battery constraints and unproven demand violates the principle of starting small. The proposal lacks a specific kill criterion for the inventory rollout or a phased approach to test the app store viability. The risk of over-extending into a category with "modest" historical success, based on the assumption that brand loyalty alone drives adoption, is too high for an immediate, full-scale approval. The company should validate the health tracking value proposition with a simpler device first.

DECISION: NO-GO
