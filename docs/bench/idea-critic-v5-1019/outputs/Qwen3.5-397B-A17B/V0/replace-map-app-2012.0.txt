Steelman: Replacing the rival's mature mapping app with a proprietary solution grants strategic independence and control over the user experience, despite initial data gaps.

However, the proposal fails the pre-mortem and fit checks. The admission that "place and business information may be wrong" and data is "a lot less" constitutes a functional regression at launch. In mobile ecosystems, core utility failures (wrong addresses, missing businesses) generate immediate, high-volume user resentment that outweighs abstract strategic gains. The plan to remove a "working app" as the default on "all new and updated phones" simultaneously creates a single point of failure with no fallback for the entire installed base.

The "better-way" analysis reveals a fatal omission: the proposal lacks a phased rollout or an opt-out mechanism. A "do nothing" stance maintains user satisfaction while the proprietary app matures in the background. A "go-if" approach would limit the switch to a beta cohort or specific regions until data parity is verified. The current plan forces a binary, irreversible switch (one-way door) based on unverified assumptions about user tolerance for broken core features.

The strongest attack survived is the fear of rival dependence, but this does not justify shipping a demonstrably inferior product to everyone immediately. The risk of brand damage from widespread navigation failures exceeds the benefit of early control. Without a kill criterion tied to data accuracy thresholds or a staged deployment plan, this is a reckless gamble with the user base. The proposal treats strategic desire as a substitute for operational readiness.

Remaining risk: Mass user churn, brand reputation collapse, and regulatory scrutiny over removing a preferred default without a viable alternative. The dissent ledger notes that no evidence suggests users value "control" over "accuracy" in navigation.

DECISION: NO-GO
