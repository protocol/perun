The proposal relies on a fatal unit-economic mismatch: charging ~$10/month while paying theaters full ticket price per visit. If a member sees one film daily at an average ticket cost of $12, the monthly liability is ~$360 against $10 revenue, creating an immediate loss of $350 per user. The plan assumes rapid growth will unlock unproven revenue streams (data, studio deals) to cover this gap, but these are speculative and cannot subsidize immediate, guaranteed cash burn.

The "heavy user" risk is not a risk; it is the core business model. Under this pricing, every active user accelerates insolvency. Theater chains refusing cooperation is a secondary concern; the primary failure mode is running out of the owner's cash subsidizing tickets faster than hypothetical data revenue can materialize. Cutting the price to ten dollars removes the only mechanism (high monthly fees) that previously kept the small subscriber base viable.

No amount of growth solves a model where marginal cost vastly exceeds marginal revenue. The assumption that data monetization will eventually exceed hundreds of dollars in monthly ticket subsidies per user is unsupported by the facts provided. This is not a scaling challenge; it is a mathematical impossibility under the proposed terms. The owner's cash would be burned entirely on ticket purchases with no clear path to profitability or leverage over studios without an existing massive user base, which cannot be built without sustaining losses that likely exceed the owner's capacity before data revenue kicks in.

DECISION: NO-GO
