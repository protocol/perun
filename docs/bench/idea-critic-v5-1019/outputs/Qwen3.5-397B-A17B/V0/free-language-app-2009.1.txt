The claim is that a free language-learning platform, funded by selling novice translations, can succeed without immediate revenue validation. The steelman: leveraging real-world translation tasks creates a sustainable flywheel where learning subsidizes operations, bypassing nonprofit limitations.

However, the "better-way" analysis reveals fatal flaws. The primary alternative—delaying build until a pilot validates the translation market—is ignored. The proposal assumes learners produce sellable work immediately, contradicting the stated risk of "poor" quality. Publishers rarely buy unvetted, novice-level translations at scale; the cost of professional editing would likely exceed revenue, destroying the margin needed to sustain free access. Furthermore, established competitors offer polished experiences; a free but frustrating product risks high churn before value is realized.

The pre-mortem is clear: the platform launches, attracts users, but fails to monetize because the translation output is unsellable without expensive correction. The "do nothing" option (waiting for a proven micro-task model) is safer. The cost of delay is low compared to the sunk cost of building a product with an unproven, high-risk revenue engine.

Critical assumptions remain unverified: that publishers want novice translations, and that editing costs won't eclipse income. The proposal offers no kill criteria or staged validation, betting everything on a "build it and they will pay" hope. The strongest attack—that the core economic loop (learn -> translate -> sell) breaks at the "sell" stage due to quality constraints—survives because the proposal defers revenue validation entirely. Without a capped, time-boxed experiment to prove publishers will buy this specific output, the full build is premature.

DECISION: NO-GO
