The claim is that a new entrant can profit in 1983 by replicating an existing bulk-warehouse model against a dominant, cash-rich incumbent with more locations, relying primarily on membership fees rather than product margins.

**Skeptic:** The premise assumes shoppers will pay a fee to access a store with higher prices or fewer choices than the incumbent, or that the incumbent cannot instantly lower fees to zero to starve the newcomer. With thin margins, any operational error or failure to hit volume targets immediately burns cash. The "no-frills" approach offers no defensive moat if the competitor matches prices.

**Better Way:** The safest path is "do nothing" or a single pilot to prove unit economics before expanding. Alternatively, differentiate by targeting a specific geographic gap the incumbent ignores or offering exclusive private-label goods not available elsewhere, rather than competing head-on on identical terms. Expanding rapidly ("open more warehouses") before validating the membership conversion rate against a aggressive competitor is reckless.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a one-way door; once capital is spent on large sites and inventory, reversal means total loss. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of premature expansion. A pre-mortem suggests failure occurs if membership sign-ups fall below 50% of projections in the first quarter, leaving fixed costs uncovered.

**Decision:** The proposal to "open more warehouses" immediately violates the constraint of unproven viability against a stronger rival. The reliance on membership fees without a proven differentiator is a fatal flaw in a head-to-head battle. The plan lacks a kill criterion for the pilot phase and ignores the risk of incumbent retaliation. It must be revised to focus on a single location test with clear membership traction metrics before any expansion.

DECISION: NO-GO
