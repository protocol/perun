**Steelman:** A $16M minority stake in a 16-unit burrito chain offers a low-cost real option to enter the "fast-casual" segment, validating a higher price point and made-to-order model without operational distraction or brand dilution.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary failure mode is **strategic drift**: the giant's core competency is speed and low cost, while this target relies on skilled labor and slower throughput. Scaling this model could cannibalize the core brand or confuse the market. However, the proposal explicitly mandates **independent operation** and a **minority stake**, which isolates these risks. The "tiny scale" is a feature, not a bug, for a pilot; it limits downside exposure while providing critical data on consumer willingness to pay premium prices for fresh food.

Alternatives considered:
1.  **Do Nothing:** Cedes the emerging fast-casual territory to competitors, risking long-term relevance as consumer tastes shift away from traditional fast food.
2.  **Internal Build:** Attempting to replicate this model internally ignores the specific cultural and operational nuances required for made-to-order success, likely resulting in a costly, slow failure.
3.  **Majority Acquisition:** Increases capital risk and forces immediate integration, violating the "learn first" objective.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a "explore adjacent markets" strategy. If we assume the goal is market intelligence rather than immediate profit, the investment succeeds even if the chain never scales, provided it yields data on pricing and operations. A pre-mortem reveals failure only if the giant interferes with operations or demands rapid scaling before unit economics are proven. The proposal's structure prevents this.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:**
The facts provided (16 units, independent run, minority stake) support a low-risk experiment. The risks raised (scale, labor, price) are inherent to the sector but mitigated by the investment structure.
*   **Kill Criterion:** If the chain cannot maintain food quality or speed standards over the next 12 months, or if same-store sales decline by >5%, the option to invest more is voided.
*   **Leading Metric:** Same-store sales growth and customer return rate compared to the giant's core menu.

**Decision:**
The proposal is a classic "two-way door" decision with capped downside and significant strategic upside. It buys a window into a new market segment without jeopardizing the core business.

DECISION: GO
