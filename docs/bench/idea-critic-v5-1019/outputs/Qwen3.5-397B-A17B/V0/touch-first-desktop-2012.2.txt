The claim assumes touch-first interaction improves productivity on non-touch devices and that users will adapt quickly enough to offset disruption. This fails the steelman test: the strongest version admits keyboard/mouse users lose efficiency due to context switching between full-screen tiles and legacy desktop apps, yet argues ecosystem unity justifies the friction.

Alternatives were not sufficiently weighed. "Do nothing" preserves enterprise stability. A "hybrid mode" (optional toggle) reduces training costs while allowing early adoption. The proposal forces a one-way door: removing the start menu eliminates the fallback, creating immediate support burdens for IT departments managing thousands of non-touch units.

Pre-mortem analysis suggests high failure probability in enterprise segments. Corporate users prioritize workflow continuity over interface modernization. Forcing a tablet paradigm on desktops risks driving customers to competitors who maintain traditional workflows. The cost of delay is low; waiting for touch hardware penetration to mature before forcing software changes would reduce friction.

Evidence is absent regarding developer buy-in. Without a critical mass of touch-optimized apps, the new store adds little value but fragments the development ecosystem. The metric for success (adoption rate) is vulnerable to forced upgrades rather than genuine preference.

Strongest attack survived: The argument that "unity across devices" outweighs "productivity loss" holds only if touch becomes the primary input method soon. In 2012, mouse/keyboard dominance remains absolute in enterprise. The proposal ignores this reality, betting on a future state that does not yet exist.

Kill criteria: If enterprise churn exceeds 5% within six months or support ticket volume related to navigation doubles, the strategy must pivot. Leading metric: Percentage of active hours spent in legacy desktop mode vs. new interface.

The risk of alienating the core desktop user base while chasing a nascent tablet market is too high without an escape hatch.

DECISION: NO-GO
