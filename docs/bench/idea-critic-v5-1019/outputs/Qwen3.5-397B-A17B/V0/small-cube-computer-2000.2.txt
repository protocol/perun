The claim is that a premium, non-upgradeable, fanless desktop in a clear case will succeed despite higher costs and manufacturing risks compared to existing profitable towers and all-in-ones.

**Skeptic:** The proposal assumes buyers will pay a premium for *less* functionality (no slots, few upgrades) and accept a fragile, hard-to-manufacture form factor. Historically, "pro" users demand expansion; "consumer" users demand value. This product alienates both: too limited for pros, too expensive for consumers. The clear acrylic magnifies manufacturing defects, threatening the brand's fragile recovery.

**Better Way:** If the goal is high margins, improve the existing tower or all-in-one lines where supply chains are proven. If the goal is design leadership, launch a lower-risk accessory first. "Do nothing" preserves the eleven-quarter winning streak without introducing unproven manufacturing complexity.

**Kill Criteria:** This is a one-way door; once tooling is cut for acrylic, sunk costs are massive. A pre-mortem reveals failure via yield rates (scrapped cases) and channel rejection (retusers refusing high-price/low-spec units). The cost of delay is low; the market isn't vanishing next week.

**Verdict:** The proposal fails the "fit" test. It contradicts the company's recovery narrative of reliable, profitable hardware by introducing a niche, high-risk SKU that cannibalizes higher-spec sales. The risks (manufacturing flaws, price resistance, upgrade frustration) outweigh the unproven benefit of a "silent, clear" aesthetic. The strongest attack—that this alienates the core base while failing to attract a new one at the required price point—stands unrefuted by the provided facts. No evidence suggests a market segment exists that prioritizes "clear acrylic" over "expansion/value" at a premium price.

DECISION: NO-GO
