The proposal attempts to monetize scarcity in a market rapidly shifting toward ownership and unlimited access. The core mechanism—discs that expire and require phone-line verification for extended viewing—introduces severe friction. Customers explicitly dislike paying repeatedly for the same content; this model institutionalizes that hatred.

The "better way" analysis reveals fatal flaws against existing and emerging alternatives. Standard VHS and DVD offer permanent ownership without recurring fees or hardware locks. Emerging digital distribution (though nascent in 1998) points toward convenience, not physical tethering to phone lines. The "do nothing" option allows the market to settle on standard DVDs, which are dropping in price and rising in capacity.

The steelman argument—that this creates a low-cost entry point for casual viewers—collapses under the hardware requirement. Forcing customers to buy expensive, specialized players that cannot play standard discs creates a walled garden with no content library at launch. Without immediate, universal studio support (a massive coordination failure risk), the players are useless. Conversely, without installed players, studios will not support the format. This is a classic cold-start problem exacerbated by consumer hostility to the usage model.

The pre-mortem is clear: The format launches with high player costs and limited titles. Customers buy a disc, watch it once, and refuse to pay again or deal with phone-line hassles. The discs become coasters; the players become e-waste. The "kill criterion" here is immediate: if the value proposition relies on customers accepting less functionality for a complex payment scheme in a market moving toward simplicity and ownership, the venture fails.

The risks are not merely "raised"; they are definitive blockers. The requirement for new hardware, the incompatibility with existing libraries, and the antagonistic payment model ensure adoption will be near zero. There is no path where this beats the simplicity of buying or renting a standard disc that plays forever on cheap, ubiquitous hardware.

DECISION: NO-GO
