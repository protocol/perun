**Steelman:** Unifying the interface across tablets and PCs creates a single ecosystem, forcing developer alignment and future-proofing the OS against the mobile shift.

**Attack:** The proposal mandates a touch-first interface on non-touch hardware, directly degrading the primary workflow of the dominant user base (keyboard/mouse corporate users). The "no easy way to go back" constraint removes the safety valve for enterprise adoption, guaranteeing resistance. High training costs and developer friction are acknowledged risks without mitigation. The assumption that tablet growth justifies alienating the existing desktop monopoly is unproven; it bets the core revenue stream on an unproven hybrid behavior.

**Better Way:** Offer the touch interface as an optional mode or a distinct SKU for convertible hardware, preserving the traditional workflow for standard PCs while still providing a path for app store growth. This captures the upside without forcing a destructive migration on resistant users.

**Fit:** This violates the immediate needs of the current north star (enterprise stability and productivity) for a speculative future state.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, enterprise CIOs ban the update due to productivity loss and support costs. Developers ignore the new store because the active user base is hostile to the UI. The company loses desktop dominance without capturing the tablet market, stranded between two worlds.

**Kill Criteria:** If enterprise churn exceeds 5% or helpdesk tickets related to navigation triple within Q1, the rollout must halt and a legacy mode restored.

**Verdict:** The proposal forces a high-risk, irreversible change on the wrong hardware segment with no fallback. It prioritizes a hypothetical future over the verified present value of the desktop. The lack of a rollback path is fatal for enterprise trust.

DECISION: NO-GO
