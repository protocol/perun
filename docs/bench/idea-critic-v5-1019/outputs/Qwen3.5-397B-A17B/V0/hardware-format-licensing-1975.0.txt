**Verdict: REVISE**

**Steelman:** Proprietary control ensures premium quality and brand exclusivity, leveraging an early market lead to establish a high-end niche before competitors mature.

**Critical Failures:**
1.  **Capacity Mismatch:** The one-hour limit is a fatal functional defect. Consumers cannot record full movies or major sporting events, which are primary use cases for home recording. This alone disqualifies the product for mass adoption regardless of picture quality.
2.  **Distribution Disadvantage:** Restricting licenses while a rival aggressively courts multiple manufacturers guarantees the competitor will achieve lower prices and wider hardware availability faster. In format wars, installation base and content availability (pre-recorded titles) historically outweigh marginal technical superiority.
3.  **Content Ecosystem Risk:** Fewer licensed partners directly correlates to fewer studios committing to pre-recorded tapes. Without a robust library, the hardware loses value.

**Better Way:**
The proposal must be revised to either:
*   Extend tape capacity to at least two hours immediately to match consumer needs.
*   Abandon the strict proprietary model in favor of broader licensing to build market share and prevent the rival from becoming the de facto standard.

**Kill Criteria:**
Do not proceed if the tape capacity remains under two hours or if the licensing strategy does not expand to include at least three major manufacturing partners within six months. The current plan relies on "quality" overcoming "utility" and "availability," a bet that historically fails in consumer electronics format wars.

**Decision:**
The current proposal ignores fundamental market requirements (recording duration) and competitive dynamics (licensing breadth). Approving this as-is invites immediate obsolescence.

DECISION: NO-GO
