First call: GO. The asymmetric upside of a small, independent test with an option to scale later outweighs the contained downside of a minority stake in 16 units.

**Steelman:** A low-capital, non-dilutive probe into a higher-price, made-to-order segment that preserves the core brand while securing a foothold in a growing category, with the right to double down only if unit economics prove scalable.

**Options:**
1.  **Invest (Proposed):** Low cost, high learning, optionality retained. Beats doing nothing by capturing early market data and potential growth without operational drag.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Zero risk, zero learning. Cedes the "fast-casual" niche to competitors; misses the shift toward higher-quality quick service.
3.  **Acquire Majority/All:** High cost, high integration risk. Premature given the unproven scalability of the labor model; violates the "test first" principle.

**Fit:** Aligns with growth via exploration rather than immediate transformation. Assumes the north star includes diversifying beyond traditional fast food.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the parent company ignores the "independent" clause, forcing standardized processes that break the made-to-order model, or if they divert excessive management attention to a 16-unit distraction. This is a two-way door; the investment can be written off or increased. The cost of delay is allowing a competitor to define the fast-casual burrito category.

**Evidence:** The proposal explicitly limits exposure (minority stake, 16 units) and preserves operational separation. Risks regarding labor skill and speed are inherent to the concept but contained within the target chain, not the giant's core operations.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks (scale, labor, price, attention) are valid concerns but are mitigated by the structure of the deal (minority, independent, option to invest later). They do not falsify the core claim that this is a valuable strategic option.

**Risks:** Scalability of the labor model; consumer acceptance of higher prices long-term. These become kill criteria for the *next* tranche of investment, not blockers for this entry.

**Kill Criteria:** If same-store sales decline for two consecutive quarters post-investment, or if the parent company is forced to intervene operationally to maintain margins, halt further investment.

**Decision:** The structure isolates risk while purchasing a strategic option.

DECISION: GO
