First call: NO-GO. The price point excludes the mass market required to replace car trips, and the regulatory environment is undefined.

**Steelman:** A revolutionary urban transporter that eliminates short car trips through superior efficiency and novelty, justifying a premium price despite a learning curve.

**Options:**
1.  **Launch now ($5k, wide release):** High cost, high regulatory risk, low immediate adoption due to price and skill barrier. Irreversible brand damage if safety incidents occur before laws adapt.
2.  **Do nothing:** Zero cost, zero risk, but forfeits first-mover advantage.
3.  **Targeted Pilot (Go-if):** Limit sales to specific zones or corporate campuses at a lower entry price to validate demand, refine training, and shape regulation. Cheaper, reversible, de-risks the core claim.

**Fit:** Assumes "replace short car trips" is the north star. At $5,000 (exceeding used cars), the product fails to move this needle for the vast majority of users. The stated facts contradict the mass-adoption requirement.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches widely; early adopters crash due to the learning curve, causing injuries. Cities immediately ban the device on sidewalks and roads due to lack of classification. Inventory piles up as the price remains uncompetitive against bicycles and scooters. The company burns capital on a regulatory fight it cannot win without proven utility.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the price is "well above" substitutes and that rules "may not allow it." These are not risks; they are current facts blocking the core value proposition. No evidence suggests consumers will pay a car-like price for a device with less utility (no cargo, one person) and higher friction (learning balance).

**Blockers:**
1.  **Price/Value Mismatch:** The cost exceeds superior alternatives (used cars) while offering less utility.
2.  **Regulatory Void:** Selling widely where rules "may not allow" operation creates an immediate legal blockade.

**Risks:** Learning curve adoption friction; lack of cargo capacity limiting use cases.

**Decision:** The plan fails as proposed because the economic and legal foundations are absent. A capped, time-boxed pilot to establish regulations and prove value at a lower price point is required before wide release.

DECISION: NO-GO
