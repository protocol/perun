First call: NO-GO. The unit economics described guarantee losses on every small order, and the capital intensity of direct employment in multiple cities creates an uncapped burn rate with no path to profitability stated.

Steelman: Rapid urban delivery can dominate market share if subsidized logistics create an unbreakable habit, allowing future price increases to cover costs.

Attack: The proposal explicitly admits free delivery on small orders costs more than the margin. Without a minimum order or delivery fee, every transaction increases total losses. Scaling this model ("expand to more cities quickly") multiplies the burn rate linearly with volume. Direct employment adds fixed overhead before revenue stability is proven. The risk that customers only use the service while subsidized is not a possibility but a certainty given the pricing structure; there is no mechanism described to transition them to profitable pricing without losing demand.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids guaranteed capital destruction.
2. Charge for delivery/set minimums: Aligns unit economics immediately, though may slow initial adoption.
3. Partner with existing couriers: Reduces fixed labor costs and city-by-city infrastructure build-out.

Pre-mortem: The company raises significant venture capital, expands to ten cities, and burns through cash within 18 months. As subsidies end or funding dries up, order volume collapses because the service was never economically viable for the customer at true cost. The company cannot pivot due to heavy fixed labor contracts and leased vehicles.

Evidence: The proposal's own facts state costs exceed margins on small orders and that heavy staffing is required. No data suggests customers will pay higher prices later.

Blockers:
1. Negative unit economics per transaction (cost > margin) with no volume-based cost reduction described.
2. Unlimited liability via direct employment and rapid expansion without a pilot.
3. Dependence on perpetual subsidies with no exit strategy for pricing.

Risks: Customer churn upon price correction; capital markets tightening before profitability.

Kill criteria: None can be set because the core model fails on day one. A capped pilot in one city with strict loss limits and a mandatory fee structure after 3 months would be required to proceed, but the current proposal demands immediate multi-city expansion with free delivery.

DECISION: NO-GO
