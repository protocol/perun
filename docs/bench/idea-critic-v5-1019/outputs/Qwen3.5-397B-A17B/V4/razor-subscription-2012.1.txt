Steelman: A lean, direct-to-consumer razor subscription can capture price-sensitive customers ignored by bloated incumbents, using viral humor to bypass traditional ad costs, provided supply chain risk is managed.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Miss the shift to e-commerce; incumbents retain monopoly pricing.
2. Retail launch: Prohibitive slotting fees and ad spend required; likely failure.
3. Proposed DTC: Low capex, immediate cash flow, high risk on supply/video performance.
The proposal beats "do nothing" by capturing early adopters; it beats retail by avoiding fatal upfront costs.

Fit: Assumes the market values price/convenience over brand loyalty. In 2012, e-commerce trust was rising; this fits the "unbundle" trend.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the single supplier raises prices or cuts quality, or if the video fails to convert. It is a two-way door: if the video flops, iterate creative; if supply breaks, pivot suppliers. Cost of delay is high; incumbents may wake up and lower prices or copy the model.

Evidence: Facts provided are internal assumptions. No external data on supplier reliability or video virality exists yet. However, the low barrier to entry validates testing immediately.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (lawsuits, price wars, supplier dependence) are standard startup hazards, not factual impossibilities. No evidence suggests incumbents *will* sue or cut prices immediately; these are threats to manage, not walls. The "single video" risk is mitigated by the low cost of producing more.

Risks: Supplier monopoly power, customer churn, low traffic. These become kill criteria: if CAC exceeds LTV by month 3, or if supplier terms worsen, pivot or stop.

Decision: The plan is a low-cost probe into a high-margin market. It requires no heavy infrastructure before validation. The potential upside dwarfs the minimal initial investment. Approve the launch to test the hypothesis immediately.
