First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on membership fees for profit while entering a market dominated by an established competitor with superior scale and cash, creating an immediate cash-flow vulnerability before a loyal base is proven.

Steelman: A focused, low-overhead warehouse can undercut a larger rival on price for a specific basket, using membership fees to fund operations and lock in loyalty, provided volume compensates for thin margins.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** Rapid expansion. High capital burn, high risk of insolvency if membership uptake lags. Irreversible lease commitments.
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoid direct confrontation; preserve capital. Zero upside, zero risk.
3. **Single-Store Pilot:** Open one location to validate the membership model and bulk-buying behavior before scaling. Lower cost, high reversibility, same learning upside.

Fit: Assumes the market supports a second player. Unverified.

Pre-mortem: The venture fails because early membership sales do not cover operating losses, and the competitor lowers prices temporarily to starve the new entrant of volume. The large warehouses become empty, costly liabilities. This is a one-way door; lease signings are hard to reverse. Delay costs first-mover advantage in specific local sites but prevents total capital loss.

Evidence: All figures are assumed. The claim that shoppers will pay a fee despite an existing alternative is unproven. The assertion that thin margins can sustain rapid expansion is mathematically fragile without proven volume.

Blockers:
1. **Unproven Revenue Model:** The core premise (profit from fees) depends on immediate, massive membership adoption in the face of a dominant incumbent. No evidence supports this feasibility in 1983.
2. **Capital Mismatch:** Rapid expansion requires significant upfront capital for sites and inventory, contradicting the "no-frills" low-cash reality of a startup facing a cash-rich rival.

Risks: Competitor price wars, site selection errors, bulk-buying resistance.

The proposal demands rapid expansion before validating the fundamental unit economics or membership appeal against a stronger rival. The lack of a pilot phase creates an unacceptable risk of total capital depletion. A single-store proof of concept is a mandatory prerequisite to de-risk the membership assumption.

DECISION: NO-GO
