First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the core brand identity of a "sports-car maker" with a "loyal enthusiast following" by introducing a product category (large luxury SUV) that directly threatens the brand's defining attribute (sports image) without a proven mechanism to retain the existing base.

Steelman: Leveraging shared platforms to fund expansion into high-margin segments while preserving the core sports line through distinct engineering and marketing.

Attack:
1.  **Brand Betrayal:** The primary asset is the "loyal enthusiast following." For this demographic, a "large luxury crossover" is often the antithesis of a "sports car." Launching it under the same badge risks alienating the core base before securing new buyers. The proposal acknowledges this risk but offers no mitigation strategy other than "high price," which does not resolve the identity conflict.
2.  **Image Dilution:** A "heavy vehicle" fundamentally undermines the "sports image." Unlike a sedan extension, an SUV changes the physical perception of the brand. Established luxury makers have the heritage to absorb this; a niche sports maker may not.
3.  **Financial Exposure:** High first-year costs combined with the risk of brand erosion create a asymmetric downside. If the SUV fails, it drains capital; if it succeeds but alienates fans, it kills the core business.
4.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option preserves brand equity. A sub-brand or distinct badging strategy (not proposed) would be a safer alternative to test the market without contaminating the main badge.

The proposal fails the "fit" test against the stated north star of serving enthusiasts. It assumes price alone insulates the brand from category contradiction. The risk of destroying the unique selling proposition (the sports image) to chase a crowded luxury SUV market is a structural blocker, not merely a risk to be managed. The plan lacks a specific kill criterion for brand sentiment or a pilot to test enthusiast reaction before full commitment.

Blockers: The stated fact that the brand relies on a "loyal enthusiast following" contradicts the action of launching a product category ("large luxury crossover") that historically alienates such groups when badged identically, with no mitigation plan provided.
Risks: High cost, strong incumbents, execution difficulty.

DECISION: NO-GO
