First call: GO. The market lacks a large-screen, stylus-equipped device, creating an uncontested space despite perceived risks.

**Steelman:** Launching a premium 5"+ stylus phone creates a new "phablet" category, capturing users who need more screen real estate than a phone offers but greater portability than a tablet, while leveraging existing manufacturing and carrier relationships.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the emerging large-screen segment to competitors; risk irrelevance as media consumption shifts to mobile.
2.  **Launch a tablet:** Enter a saturated market dominated by established players; higher price point reduces impulse adoption; lacks always-on connectivity convenience.
3.  **Proposed hybrid:** High risk of initial reviewer skepticism, but first-mover advantage in a blue ocean. Cheaper than developing two separate lines; reversible via limited initial production run.

**Fit:** Aligns with the vision of expanding mobile utility beyond voice/text to productivity and media. Assumes users value screen size over one-handed ergonomics.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the device is priced too high for a "phone" yet lacks tablet apps, or if the stylus is perceived as a gimmick rather than a productivity tool. This is a two-way door; if sales lag, the form factor can be discontinued or adjusted without brand collapse. Cost of delay is high: competitors could define the category standard first.

**Evidence:** No internal data exists (2011 context), but the trend toward larger screens is observable in early market shifts. The risk of cannibalization is outweighed by the risk of losing customers to other manufacturers who solve the screen-size pain point.

**Blockers:** NONE. The objections raised (reviewer bias, stylus utility, awkward sizing) are unproven risks, not factual blockers. They represent market education challenges, not structural impossibilities. The fear of cannibalization is a standard growth pain; better to cannibalize oneself than be cannibalized by a rival.

**Risks:** Initial market confusion; niche adoption. Mitigation: Position strictly as premium; bundle high-value software to justify stylus.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders do not match 60% of the flagship model's typical launch volume within 30 days, halt mass production and re-evaluate marketing positioning. Leading metric: Daily active usage of stylus-specific features.

DECISION: GO
