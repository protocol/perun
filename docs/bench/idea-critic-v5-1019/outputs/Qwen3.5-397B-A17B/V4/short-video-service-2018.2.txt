First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks for massive capital to enter a market where the dominant competitor (free, mobile-first video) already satisfies the core user behavior, while artificially restricting the product (no TV) and inflating costs (high-budget shows) without a proven willingness to pay.

Steelman: A premium, mobile-exclusive short-form service can succeed by offering cinematic quality that social platforms cannot match, justifying a subscription before expanding to TV.

Attack:
1.  **Fit & Demand:** In 2018, "short video" is synonymous with free, algorithmic social feeds (YouTube, emerging TikTok). Users do not associate "under 10 minutes" with "paid premium content." The proposal admits viewers may not pay, yet proceeds with a $1.75B commitment.
2.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "wait" option is superior. Entering later allows validation of short-form monetization without burning billions. A cheaper alternative is licensing existing content or producing lower-budget pilots to test price elasticity before greenlighting a full slate.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The service launches in 2020 to poor adoption because users refuse to pay for mobile-only content they get for free elsewhere. The inability to cast to TV frustrates users who want to watch on larger screens, a standard expectation by 2020. High fixed costs from producer deals create an immediate cash crunch before subscriber growth can offset it.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal itself lists the core risks as facts: "viewers may not pay" and "free short video... is already popular." These are not uncertainties to be gambled on; they are known market conditions that invalidate the business model as stated.

Blockers:
*   **Contradictory Value Prop:** Charging for a format (mobile short-form) that is established as free, while removing a standard feature (TV viewing), creates a product with negative perceived value relative to the status quo.
*   **Capital Efficiency:** Committing $1.75B before validating willingness to pay for the specific constraint (mobile-only) violates basic unit economics logic.

Risks:
*   High burn rate before breakeven.
*   Competitor response (free tiers improving quality).

The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test. It spends billions to solve a problem users don't have (lack of free short video) while creating new friction (paywall, no TV).

DECISION: NO-GO
