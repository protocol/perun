First call: NO-GO. A mass-market badge cannot command premium prices without a distinct identity, making the unit economics of a low-volume, high-cost factory untenable.

Steelman: Leveraging existing supply chains to build a feature-rich sedan could undercut rivals on price while delivering comparable quality. However, this assumes luxury buyers prioritize specifications over brand equity, which contradicts the core risk identified: the "badge" drives the purchase.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids capital destruction but cedes the segment.
2. Create a sub-brand: Proven path (e.g., Lexus, Acura) to isolate luxury perception from mass-market roots.
3. Proceed as proposed: High risk of brand dilution and financial failure due to inability to charge premium margins on a mass-market name.

Fit: Entering the premium class requires a premium image. The current strategy ignores the psychological barrier of the existing brand association.

Pre-mortem: The factory opens, but sales remain flat because affluent buyers reject the logo. The company is stuck with high fixed costs, low volume, and a damaged mainstream reputation. This is a one-way door with massive sunk costs.

Evidence: The proposal admits buyers pay for the "badge" and the brand lacks a "luxury image." Yet, it insists on using the mass-market name. This is an internal contradiction. No evidence suggests a mass-market brand has successfully launched a true luxury competitor at full premium pricing under the same name without a separate marque or acquisition.

Blockers:
1. Brand Architecture: The plan explicitly rejects the industry-standard solution (sub-brand) required to overcome the stated "no luxury image" deficit.
2. Unit Economics: Building a dedicated factory for a low-volume product under a brand that cannot command the necessary margin guarantees financial loss.

Risks: Market rejection, brand dilution, stranded assets.

The proposal asks to ignore the primary driver of luxury sales (brand equity) while incurring the highest possible cost structure. It fails to address the fundamental mismatch between the asset (mass-market brand) and the goal (premium pricing).

DECISION: NO-GO
