First call: GO. The direct-to-consumer, low-overhead model directly addresses the price sensitivity of the market while bypassing the cost structures of established competitors.

**Steelman:** A lean incorporation selling custom-compatible hardware directly to knowledgeable buyers can undercut branded prices significantly, using cash-flow-positive growth to fund operations without needing immediate credit or physical retail presence.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Remain a small, unincorporated assembler with limited liability protection and capped growth potential.
2.  **Retail/Reseller model:** Attempt to match established brands on their turf; requires massive capital for inventory and storefronts, likely leading to immediate failure against incumbents.
3.  **Proposed Direct Model:** Minimizes fixed costs, targets early adopters who do not need to "try before buying," and uses revenue to negotiate supplier terms later. This is the only viable path to scale without external financing.

**Fit:** Aligns with a strategy of disrupting mature markets through cost leadership and niche targeting (early adopters/technical users).

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if suppliers demand upfront payment that exceeds current cash reserves, or if the volume of service requests from non-technical buyers overwhelms a small team. However, targeting direct orders implies a more technical customer base, mitigating the service risk. The "try before buying" risk is neutralized by targeting customers who already understand the specifications.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on the existence of a market segment (IBM-compatible) where price is the primary differentiator and brand loyalty is low. The "no store" constraint is a feature, not a bug, reducing the break-even point.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (service network, credit, tactile inspection) are inherent to the startup phase of a direct model but do not invalidate the core logic. They are managed by targeting the right customer profile (technical, price-sensitive) and growing organically.

**Risks:** Supplier credit denial; higher return rates from mismatched expectations. These are operational hurdles, not fundamental flaws.

**Kill Criteria:** If supplier terms require 100% upfront payment that halts assembly for more than two weeks, or if return rates exceed 15% in the first quarter, the model must pivot to seeking seed funding or a hybrid sales channel.

**Decision:** The plan is sound, capital-efficient, and exploits a specific gap in the 1984 market.

DECISION: GO
