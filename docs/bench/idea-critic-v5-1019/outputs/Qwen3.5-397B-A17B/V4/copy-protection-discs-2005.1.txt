First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades long-term brand trust and computer security for a fragile, easily circumvented restriction that treats paying customers as adversaries.

Steelman: Embedding copy-protection software on audio discs is the only viable method available in 2005 to immediately reduce casual copying and preserve revenue streams against rising peer-to-peer sharing, assuming no better distribution model exists yet.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Self-Inflicted Security Liability:** Software that installs silently and resists removal functions identically to rootkits. This creates a massive attack surface; once security researchers inevitably expose vulnerabilities (a certainty, not a risk), the record company becomes liable for compromising users' systems.
2.  **Hostile User Experience:** Explicitly treating buyers as thieves by restricting fair use (ripping for personal players) and installing hidden software will trigger immediate consumer backlash and boycotts, accelerating the shift to unauthorized, DRM-free sources.
3.  **False Economy:** The cost of developing, maintaining, and defending this software, plus the reputational damage from inevitable failures, exceeds the marginal revenue saved from casual copiers who will bypass it anyway.

**Risks:**
*   Compatibility issues causing system crashes on diverse consumer PCs.
*   Legal action from consumer protection groups regarding undisclosed installation.
*   Permanent brand damage associating the label with malware.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "do no harm" baseline of software distribution. The mechanism (silent installation) is fundamentally deceptive. The "risk" of customers objecting is actually a guaranteed outcome; the value proposition is inverted, punishing the paying user while failing to stop determined pirates. The alternative of "do nothing" preserves trust and avoids turning the company into a vector for security exploits. There is no kill criterion that can mitigate the inherent hostility of the design; the design itself is the blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
