First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to sell a slower, non-upgradable machine at a premium price to a brand just recovering from near-collapse, directly contradicting the value proposition that likely drove the recovery.

**Steelman:** A niche, design-first appliance for status-conscious buyers who prioritize silence and aesthetics over performance or longevity, creating a high-margin halo product.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Protect the recovery streak; avoid manufacturing complexity.
2.  **Cheaper alternative:** Sell the existing faster tower with a silent cooling kit; lower risk, lower margin.
3.  **Proposal:** High risk, high margin, potential brand redefinition.

**Fit:** Assumes the market values "appliance" computing over "tool" computing. This contradicts the historical strength of computer makers selling upgradeable towers. The "clear acrylic" suggests a shift to fashion, which is unproven for this specific recovering brand.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, and early units suffer visible manufacturing flaws in the acrylic (scratches, warping), which are impossible to hide in a clear case. Tech reviewers hammer the "high price for low specs" narrative. Upgraders feel betrayed. Sales stall, inventory piles up, and the company burns cash on a complex supply chain for a niche item, threatening the eleven-quarter streak.

**Evidence & Math:** The proposal admits the unit costs more than a *faster* tower with *more* features. The value equation is negative on pure utility. The only justification is emotional/design value, which is a "one-way door" bet on a trend not yet validated by the company's recent history. The manufacturing risk ("hard to manufacture without flaws") is a stated fact that directly threatens quality perception.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Negative Value Proposition:** Selling less performance and flexibility for more money contradicts the core utility of a "computer maker" without established proof of a luxury brand pivot.
2.  **Manufacturing Reality:** The stated difficulty in making the case flaw-free creates a high probability of visible defects, which is fatal for a transparent product.

**Risks:** Market rejection of the price point; brand confusion; supply chain drag.

**Kill Criteria:** If the first production run yields >5% cosmetic rejection rate, or if pre-orders do not cover the tooling costs within 30 days, the project halts.

DECISION: NO-GO
