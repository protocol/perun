First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to manage a mathematical defect with a bureaucratic filter in a high-trust market, ignoring the asymmetry between technical rarity and reputational catastrophe.

Steelman: Limiting replacements to proven cases preserves capital for a flaw with negligible real-world impact, avoiding a costly blanket recall.

Attack: The core assumption—that users can verify affected status—is false. Floating-point errors are non-deterministic and opaque to typical users; requiring proof creates an impossible barrier, signaling evasion rather than precision. The "rare occurrence" metric is irrelevant to buyer psychology; the mere existence of a calculated error in a processor destroys the "correctness" guarantee essential to the brand. The cost of a full replacement is finite and calculable; the cost of reputation loss when the market perceives a cover-up is unbounded and potentially existential. The proposal bets financial certainty against reputational infinity.

Options:
1. Do nothing/Status Quo: Saves immediate cash, guarantees long-term brand erosion and loss of enterprise trust.
2. Proposed (Proof-based): Angers users who cannot prove the bug, fuels narrative of hiding defects, high reputational risk.
3. Full Replacement (Alternative): High immediate cash cost, definitively closes the issue, reinforces brand reliability, converts a crisis into a service demonstration.

Fit: A hardware vendor's north star is absolute reliability. This proposal contradicts that by admitting faults but refusing to fully stand behind the product.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-announcement, major enterprise clients ban the chip due to liability fears, citing the company's refusal to guarantee correct math. Stock collapses not because of the recall cost, but because the market loses faith in the silicon's integrity.

Blockers:
1. The requirement for users to "show work is affected" is operationally impossible for the target demographic, rendering the remedy void.
2. The reputational risk of perceived concealment outweighs the known financial cost of replacement.

Risks: Immediate cash flow impact; supply chain strain.

Kill Criteria: If customer trust drops by 5% in next quarter surveys, pivot to immediate full recall. Leading metric: Enterprise contract renewals.

DECISION: NO-GO
