First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on mail-order sales for a novel, low-speed vehicle with significant safety and usability concerns in the target market (UK), without addressing critical barriers to consumer adoption or regulatory acceptance.

**Steelman:** A sub-£400, license-free tricycle offers affordable mobility, and mail order reduces overhead, allowing rapid scale if the product meets a latent demand for short-distance travel.

**Attack:**
1.  **Safety & Regulation:** A low-speed vehicle (15 mph) mixing with traffic invites severe safety objections. UK safety groups and regulators may block sales or demand costly modifications post-launch, especially given the "low among traffic" profile. The proposal assumes "no licence" equals "no regulation," which is a fatal flaw.
2.  **Product-Market Fit:** British winters, short range, no weather protection, and low speed make the vehicle impractical for year-round use. Mail order prevents customers from testing these limitations before purchase, likely leading to high return rates and reputational damage.
3.  **Business Model:** Selling critical parts as "optional extras" while marketing a complete vehicle creates friction and perceived deception. If safety gear or weather shields are extra, the effective price rises, and liability increases.
4.  **Distribution:** Mail order for a large, assembled vehicle in 1985 is logistically risky and unproven for this category. Customers need to see and try such a niche product.

**Blockers:**
*   **Regulatory/Safety:** High probability of intervention by safety groups or regulators due to the vehicle's design and speed relative to traffic, potentially halting sales immediately after launch.
*   **Usability:** The combination of no weather protection, short range, and UK climate renders the product unusable for a significant portion of the year, undermining the "large numbers" sales target.

**Risks:** High return rates, brand damage, assembly partner disputes over returns, and inventory obsolescence.

**Conclusion:** The proposal ignores fundamental product viability and regulatory realities. Launching as planned invites immediate failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
