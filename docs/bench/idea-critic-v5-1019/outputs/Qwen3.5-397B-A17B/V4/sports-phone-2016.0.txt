First call: GO. Beating a rival's September launch to capture the holiday quarter and media cycle is a sound offensive move for a flagship line that already sells well.

Steelman: Releasing in August with superior specs (battery, biometrics, durability) pre-empts the competitor, securing market share before their narrative forms.

Alternatives: Waiting for the traditional schedule cedes the "first mover" advantage to the rival. Reducing features to meet the date dilutes the flagship value proposition. Doing nothing guarantees a reactive posture.

Fit: This aligns with a growth strategy focused on volume and market leadership. The timing directly targets the highest revenue window.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the battery overheats or the iris scanner fails in mass production, leading to a global recall that destroys brand trust. This is a one-way door; a botched global launch cannot be quietly rolled back. The cost of delay is losing the Q4 sales peak to the competitor.

Evidence: The proposal admits the schedule compresses testing and combines conflicting physical constraints (larger battery in a thinner body) with unproven technology at volume. These are not hypothetical risks; they are inherent contradictions in the current plan. The "risk" of a defect is a certainty if the physical and temporal constraints are not resolved.

Blockers:
1. Physical contradiction: A larger battery in a thinner body without confirmed thermal/safety validation is a hardware impossibility or a fire hazard.
2. Process failure: Compressing testing for unproven biometric sensors guarantees undiscovered bugs at global scale.
3. Financial exposure: A global defect on a flagship device creates unrecoverable brand damage and liability.

Risks: Supply chain inability to source new components in time; competitor accelerating their own launch.

The proposal asks to approve a plan that explicitly acknowledges it has less time to test critical, novel, and physically conflicting components. Approving this as-is authorizes a likely disaster. The plan must be revised to prove the battery safety and sensor reliability *before* committing to a global volume ship date.

DECISION: NO-GO
