First call: GO. The market gap (more cores, lower price) addresses a clear competitor vulnerability, and the only path to recover share after a miss is a differentiated product, not incrementalism.

**Steelman:** A desperate underdog must gamble on a discontinuous architectural leap to offer superior multi-core value at a discount, accepting high financial risk because status quo guarantees irrelevance.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Safe financially short-term, but cedes the desktop market permanently as the rival solidifies dominance.
2.  **Incremental Refresh:** Fixes the immediate underperformance but fails to close the gap or excite the market; likely a wasted spend.
3.  **The Proposal:** High risk of another miss, but the only option with a non-zero probability of regaining leadership.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** Fits the "challenger" necessity. If this fails, the company runs out of capital. However, the rival cannot instantly match a new architecture without compromising their own margins or roadmap. Software support for cores is already trending upward; betting against it is betting against the industry trajectory.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal admits the financial tightrope but offers no alternative route to growth. The "disappointing earlier design" is the specific blocker to overcome, which requires an *all-new* approach, not a patch. The risk of the rival cutting prices is real, but they cannot easily cut prices on a mature architecture while maintaining margins if forced to compete with a newer, more efficient core count.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** None. The financial risk is a condition of the business state, not a flaw in this specific plan's logic. The plan acknowledges the risk; avoiding it yields certain failure.
*   **Risks:** Execution error, software lag, aggressive rival response. These are managed by the "lower price" wedge and the necessity of the move.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If the new architecture does not deliver >20% performance-per-dollar advantage within 12 months of launch, pivot to embedded markets immediately.

The proposal is the only viable strategic move. Hesitation is defeat.

DECISION: GO
