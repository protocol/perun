First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $350M to unproven technology with no independent verification, creating massive downside with no confirmed upside.

**Steelman:** Partnering early secures exclusive access to a revolutionary, low-cost diagnostic platform, transforming stores into health hubs before competitors can react.

**Attack:** The claim rests entirely on the founder's assertion. In 2012, "many blood tests from a few drops" contradicts established physics and biology without peer-reviewed data. The refusal to share methods prevents due diligence, making the $350M investment a blind bet. The lack of medical expertise in the chain amplifies execution risk, while regulatory scrutiny is guaranteed for unvalidated medical devices.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do nothing:* Avoids catastrophic capital loss; wait for FDA clearance and published studies.
2. *Pilot:* Fund a single-store trial with strict milestones instead of hundreds of clinics.
3. *Equity only:* Invest in the startup without operational integration until validation.

**Fit:** This diverges from a supermarket's core competency (retail logistics) into high-liability healthcare without the necessary infrastructure or knowledge base.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-launch, regulators shut down the clinics due to inaccurate results, leading to lawsuits, reputational ruin, and a total write-off of the $350M. The "exclusivity" becomes a liability anchor.

**Evidence:** Zero published studies cited; methods withheld; regulatory pathway undefined. The requester's own risk list confirms the technology is unproven.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Unverified Core Claim:** The technology's efficacy is an assertion, not a fact. Investing $350M without independent validation violates basic capital allocation principles.
2. **Information Asymmetry:** The startup's refusal to share methods makes due diligence impossible, guaranteeing the investor cannot assess the true risk.
3. **Capability Gap:** The chain lacks medical expertise required to operate clinics and manage regulatory compliance.

**Risks:** Regulatory rejection, false positives/negatives harming customers, brand destruction, opportunity cost of $350M.

**Decision:** The absence of evidence where evidence is mandatory (medical efficacy) is a fatal blocker. No amount of potential upside justifies a $350M commitment to a black box.

DECISION: NO-GO
