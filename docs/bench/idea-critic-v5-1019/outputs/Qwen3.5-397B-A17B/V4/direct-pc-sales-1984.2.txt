First call: GO. The direct-to-consumer, low-overhead model directly addresses the price sensitivity of the nascent personal computer market, offering a clear path to profitability without the capital burden of retail locations.

**Steelman:** A lean manufacturer can undercut established brands by eliminating retailer margins and real estate costs, relying on direct marketing to reach informed early adopters who prioritize value over physical inspection.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Remain a small assembler; limited growth, vulnerable to supply shocks.
2.  **Retail expansion:** Open stores/showrooms. High capital risk, destroys the price advantage, slow scalability.
3.  **Proposed Direct Model:** Low fixed costs, immediate cash flow from orders, scalable marketing. Beats "do nothing" on growth potential and "retail" on capital efficiency.

**Fit:** Aligns with a strategy of rapid market penetration through price leadership. Assumes customers value cost savings over tactile experience.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if supply chains断裂 (parts unavailable) or if the "no-touch" barrier proves insurmountable for the mass market. However, this is a two-way door; service networks and credit lines can be added post-revenue. The cost of delay is losing the first-mover advantage in the budget segment to larger, slower competitors.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on the existence of IBM-compatible parts (commodity) and a customer base willing to buy unseen (catalog/phone order precedents). No internal contradiction exists.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (desire to try before buying, lack of service, credit) are execution challenges, not fatal flaws. They are mitigated by the low-price entry point and the ability to build service capacity from generated revenue. The "no stores" constraint is a feature, not a bug, enabling the price strategy.

**Risks:** Supply chain disruption; brand trust deficits. These become kill criteria: if parts cannot be sourced at 20% below branded units within 6 months, or if return rates exceed 15%, the model fails.

**Decision:** The plan is sound, capital-efficient, and addresses a specific market gap. The risks are manageable and do not preclude immediate execution.

DECISION: GO
