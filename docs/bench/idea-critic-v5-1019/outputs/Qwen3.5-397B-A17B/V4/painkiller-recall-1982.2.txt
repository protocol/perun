First call: GO. The stated fact that bottles were poisoned after leaving the factory creates an immediate, unquantifiable threat to human life that cannot be contained by a local response when the distribution chain is national and the perpetrator is unknown.

Steelman: A nationwide withdrawal and production halt are the only actions that guarantee no further deaths while restoring the absolute trust required for a mass-market ingestible product, despite massive short-term financial loss.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing locally: Risks additional deaths if the poisoning spreads or if the scope is wider than currently known; destroys brand viability upon the next fatality.
2. Regional recall only: Fails to address the panic of a national consumer base who cannot verify if their bottle is from the affected metro area; signals the company values cost over safety.
3. Wait for more data: Unacceptable delay given the lethal nature of the threat; every hour of delay risks a life and the company's existence.

Fit: Aligns with the fundamental north star of consumer safety; without trust, the product has no market.

Pre-mortem: If we do not withdraw nationwide, another death occurs in a different city tomorrow. The resulting media frenzy, legal liability, and total collapse of consumer confidence would bankrupt the company permanently. Withdrawing now is a two-way door financially (costs are recoverable); a second death is a one-way door for the brand's reputation.

Evidence: The proposal states poisoning occurred post-factory in a metro area. The risk is not the cost of the recall, but the certainty of brand extinction if further harm occurs. The "signal of danger" is mitigated by the immediate pivot to tamper-resistant packaging, turning the crisis into a category-leading safety standard.

Blockers: NONE. The financial cost and competitive loss are severe risks, not blockers, as they are survivable compared to the alternative of multiple fatalities.
Risks: Permanent market share loss, high restructuring costs, competitor entrenchment. These are managed by the speed of the relaunch and the novelty of the new packaging.

Kill criteria: If the relaunch with tamper-resistant packaging does not regain majority market share within 12 months, reconsider the product line's viability. Leading metric: Weekly sales volume vs. pre-crisis baseline.

DECISION: GO
