First call: NO-GO. The proposal requires customers to adopt expensive, single-purpose hardware for a rental model that offers inferior convenience compared to existing options.

**Steelman:** A secure, high-capacity physical distribution channel enabling flexible rental windows without returns, monetized via phone-line authorization.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economic Friction:** The model demands high upfront consumer CAPEX (specialized player) for a recurring OPEX model (rental fees). In 1998, VHS rentals are ubiquitous, cheap, and require no new hardware. DVD players are gaining traction for ownership. Asking users to buy a "phone-calling" player just to rent movies creates a prohibitive barrier to entry.
2.  **Coordination Risk:** Success hinges on a fragile three-way handshake: studios (content), electronics makers (hardware), and retailers (distribution). History shows format wars (e.g., Beta vs. VHS) stall markets when consumer value isn't immediate and obvious.
3.  **User Hostility:** The "pay-per-view after 48 hours" mechanism via phone line introduces friction and anxiety. Customers dislike "ticking clocks" and surprise fees. The inability to play discs on standard players renders the inventory worthless if the user upgrades or moves.
4.  **Technological Obsolescence:** Relying on landline phone connections for authentication in 1998 ignores the rapid shift toward digital connectivity and the impending rise of broadband. The hardware is a dead end before launch.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Continue renting VHS/DVDs with physical returns. Proven, low friction, no new hardware needed.
*   *Mail-order Subscription:* (Emerging model) Flat fee, no late fees, no special player. Superior UX.
*   *Digital Download:* (Future) No physical media, no player cost.

**Pre-mortem:** The format launches, studios hesitate due to low install base, consumers refuse to buy $200+ players for rentals, and the inventory becomes e-waste. The "phone line" feature is seen as intrusive and unreliable.

**Blockers:**
*   **Hardware Barrier:** Requires consumers to buy specialized, expensive hardware for a service (renting) that is currently cheaper and easier without it.
*   **Value Proposition:** The core benefit (no returns) does not outweigh the cost (new player + per-view fees + phone dependency) compared to the status quo.

**Risks:** Studio adoption failure, consumer rejection of "phone-home" DRM, rapid irrelevance against emerging digital solutions.

DECISION: NO-GO
