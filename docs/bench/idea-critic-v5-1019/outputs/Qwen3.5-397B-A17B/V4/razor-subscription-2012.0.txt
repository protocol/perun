First call: GO. The asymmetry of low fixed costs against incumbent inertia creates a viable wedge, provided the creative resonates.

Steelman: A direct-to-consumer razor subscription can succeed by bypassing traditional retail markups and using viral, low-cost humor to acquire customers faster than incumbents can react, validating demand before scaling supply.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited are valid but not fatal facts.
**Risks:** Supplier dependency, incumbent retaliation, traffic failure, subscription fatigue. These are execution risks, not structural blockers.

**Analysis:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" yields zero upside. Building a factory requires prohibitive capital. Retail distribution demands slotting fees the founders lack. The proposed online-only, reseller model is the cheapest path to market validation.
2.  **Fit:** Assumes the goal is rapid market entry with minimal capital. The "humorous video" is the only affordable customer acquisition channel that scales non-linearly.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** If the video flops, the loss is limited to production costs (low). If incumbents cut prices, they sacrifice margin on their entire retail base to chase a tiny online segment; the startup can absorb a price war better due to lower overhead. If the supplier fails, the brand (customer list) is the real asset and can switch manufacturers.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal relies on the assumption that "low price + humor" disrupts "high price + heavy ad spend." In 2012, digital video CPMs were low, and direct shipping was proven in other categories. The math works if Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) stays below Lifetime Value (LTV).
5.  **Decision Logic:** This is a two-way door. If the video fails, they stop. If it succeeds, they scale. Waiting for perfect supply chains or legal immunity guarantees failure against entrenched players. The single greatest risk is not trying.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   **Metric:** CAC must be <30% of the first year's subscription revenue.
*   **Threshold:** If the video generates <1,000 orders in week one, pause paid promotion and pivot creative before spending more.
*   **Review:** 30 days post-launch.

The plan survives the attack because the downside is capped and the upside disrupts the incumbent's pricing power.

DECISION: GO
