First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks for massive capital to enter a market where the dominant competitor (free, social, short-form video) already satisfies the core user behavior, while artificially restricting the product to phones only.

Steelman: A premium, mobile-first short-form service could succeed if it offers narrative quality and curation that social algorithms cannot match, creating a distinct "high-end" category.

However, the attack on this steelman reveals fatal flaws in the 2018 context.
1.  **Value Proposition Mismatch:** Users consume short videos on social platforms for free. Asking them to pay a monthly subscription for the same format (short episodes), even with higher production value, fights entrenched behavior. The "mobile-only" restriction exacerbates this by removing the living room, a primary revenue driver for streaming.
2.  **Unit Economics:** Raising $1.75 billion to fund high-budget shows creates an enormous recoupment hurdle. With a capped audience (mobile only) and a difficult conversion funnel (free to paid for short content), the path to breakeven is mathematically improbable compared to long-form SVOD or ad-supported social models.
3.  **Timing:** In 2018, TikTok was exploding precisely because it monetized short attention spans via ads, not subscriptions. Launching a paid walled garden for short content in 2020 ignores the market's clear signal that short-form is an ad-revenue game, not a subscription game.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Strategy:** High production costs require high ARPU or massive scale. Restricting playback to phones limits scale; asking for subscriptions limits scale in a free-dominated category. The math does not work.
*   **Market Fit:** The core premise (users will pay for mobile-only shorts) contradicts the 2018-2020 trajectory where short-form consumption migrated entirely to free, ad-supported feeds.

**Risks:**
*   Inability to secure TV rights later if the platform fails early.
*   Talent churn if shows don't find an audience quickly.

The proposal demands capital for a model that fights the gravity of the market (free short video) while voluntarily handicapping distribution (no TV). A cheaper, safer alternative is an ad-supported model or integrating short-form into an existing long-form library, not a standalone paid mobile service.

DECISION: NO-GO
