First call: NO-GO. Launching a multi-billion dollar aircraft program on a single order of 34 units is financially insolvent; the development cost cannot be amortized over such a small initial base, creating immediate negative unit economics.

Steelman: Securing eight major airlines as design partners de-risks product-market fit and locks in early demand, making the single 34-unit order a credible anchor for a viable program.

Options:
1. **Proposed:** Launch now. Cost: Extreme. Risk: Catastrophic if regulatory approval fails or the single buyer defaults. Reversibility: Near zero once tooling starts.
2. **Do Nothing:** Wait for regulatory clarity and more orders. Cost: Opportunity loss to rivals. Risk: Losing market share. Reversibility: High.
3. **Staged Launch:** Secure Letters of Intent (LOIs) from the other seven partners before freezing the design. Cost: Moderate delay. Risk: Partners defect. Reversibility: High.

Fit: Assuming the goal is market leadership, this move gambles the company's liquidity on unproven regulatory outcomes.

Pre-mortem: The program fails because aviation authorities deny twin-engine overwater certification (ETOPS) for the required duration. The single customer cancels due to delivery delays caused by redesigns requested by the design partners. The company faces billions in stranded costs with no other buyers. This is a one-way door; once launched, retreat means bankruptcy. The cost of delay (waiting for regulation) is negligible compared to the cost of failure.

Evidence: The proposal admits regulatory approval "has not been granted." It admits only one firm order exists despite eight airlines being "in the room." This implies the other seven have not committed financially. Relying on potential interest rather than signed contracts for a project of this magnitude is a fatal financial flaw.

Blockers:
1. **Financial Insolvency:** One order (34 units) is insufficient to cover development costs for an all-new aircraft family. The math does not work without confirmed orders from the other seven partners.
2. **Regulatory Uncertainty:** Launching before confirming the regulatory path for overwater twin-engine flight introduces an existential technical risk that cannot be engineered away post-launch.

Risks: Design creep from too many partners; rival capturing the market during delay.

DECISION: NO-GO
