First call: NO-GO. The price point excludes the mass market required to replace walking or standard cars, and the regulatory environment is undefined.

**Steelman:** A revolutionary urban mobility device that eliminates short car trips through superior efficiency and novelty, justifying a premium price for early adopters while regulations catch up.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** $5,000 consumer launch. High cost, high regulatory risk, steep learning curve. Fails to beat "do nothing" for the average commuter due to price and legal ambiguity.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Wait for regulations to clarify and manufacturing costs to drop. Zero risk, zero upside now.
3.  **Alternative (B2B/Pilot):** Lease exclusively to security forces, tourists, or large campuses at a lower entry point. Validates utility, builds regulatory precedent, and subsidizes R&D without exposing the brand to mass-market rejection.

**Fit:** Assuming the goal is mass adoption ("replace short car trips"), a luxury price point contradicts the vision. This fits a niche novelty product, not a transportation utility.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sells only to wealthy enthusiasts, and is subsequently banned from sidewalks and roads due to safety incidents involving untrained riders. Inventory piles up as "expensive toys," and the company burns cash waiting for legislation that never arrives. This is a one-way door for brand reputation; a mass failure makes future regulatory approval harder.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the price is "well above" substitutes (bicycles, used cars) and that rules "may not allow it." These are not risks; they are current facts blocking the core value proposition. No evidence suggests consumers will pay 10x the cost of a bicycle for a device with a learning curve and legal uncertainty.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Unit Economics vs. Value:** The $5,000 price creates a barrier insurmountable for the stated goal of replacing walking/biking for the general public.
2.  **Regulatory Void:** Selling widely before rules exist invites immediate bans, destroying the asset's utility.

**Risks:** Learning curve accidents, cargo limitations, battery range (assumed).

**Decision:** The plan fails the basic test of product-market fit at the proposed price and legal status. A capped B2B pilot is the only logical first step, but the current proposal is a mass-market launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
