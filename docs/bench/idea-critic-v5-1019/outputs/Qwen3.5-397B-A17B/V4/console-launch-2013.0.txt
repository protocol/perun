First call: GO. The proposal directly attacks the previous generation's failures (late, expensive, hard to develop for) by prioritizing price, developer ease, and consumer-friendly policies (used games/offline play).

**Steelman:** Undercut the rival on price and friction to regain developer mindshare and consumer trust, accepting lower hardware margins to win the platform war.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposal:** Low price, PC architecture, used games allowed. High reversibility (price cuts easy), high risk (margin compression), beats "do nothing" which cedes the market.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain status quo. Guarantees continued loss of ground due to high cost and dev friction.
3.  **Premium Strategy:** Match rival's online focus. Fails because the rival has an insurmountable head start in services; competing on their strongest turf is suicidal.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of regaining market share. The "PC-like" hardware reduces development costs, offsetting some margin loss.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the lower price point makes the hardware bill-of-materials impossible without severe performance compromises, or if the lack of launch software renders the price irrelevant. However, the proposal explicitly accepts low margins to solve the price barrier.

**Evidence:** The facts state the previous gen failed due to being "late and expensive." The rival has a "strong head start" in online services, making a direct online competition a loser. The proposal avoids this trap by competing on price and openness.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (margin reduction, weak launch software, online competition) are known trade-offs, not factual contradictions. Lower margins are a strategic choice, not a blocker. Weak software is a temporary state solvable by the "easy to build for" architecture attracting third parties.

**Risks:** Margin pressure; reliance on third-party software adoption; perception of being "cheap" vs. "premium." These are managed by the strategic pivot to volume and developer goodwill.

**Decision:** The proposal correctly identifies that winning requires removing the barriers that caused the last failure. It avoids the rival's strength (online) and attacks their weakness (likely higher price/restrictions).

DECISION: GO
