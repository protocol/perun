First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market with a differentiated feature (3D) that does not address the primary purchase drivers (apps, screen quality) while accepting severe distribution constraints (single carrier).

Steelman: A premium device leveraging unique hardware (3D) and integrated commerce (firefly) can carve a niche among loyal customers, offsetting app catalog deficits with membership value.

Attack:
1.  **Fit:** The strategy relies on a "walled garden" approach (single carrier) for a product category where ubiquity and app ecosystem are the north stars. In 2014, app catalog size is a binary gatekeeper for premium adoption; a smaller catalog is a fatal flaw, not a differentiator.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A "cheaper path" would be a mid-range device to test the 3D feature without demanding premium pricing against superior incumbents. The current plan beats neither.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The device launches to poor reviews citing the lack of popular apps. The 3D feature is viewed as a gimmick with no developer support. The single-carrier deal caps sales volume below the break-even point for hardware subsidies and marketing. The membership bundle fails to justify the price premium against competitors offering better hardware.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the price matches leaders with "far larger app catalogs." History shows premium buyers prioritize ecosystem over novelty hardware features. The single-carrier constraint contradicts the need for mass adoption to sustain a new platform.

Blockers:
*   **Ecosystem Deficit:** Selling a premium phone with a known inferior app catalog at a parity price is a factual route to failure; the proposal offers no mechanism to overcome this binary barrier.
*   **Distribution Cap:** Single-carrier launch artificially limits the total addressable market for a new entrant, making the unit economics of a premium hardware launch unviable compared to multi-carrier competitors.

Risks:
*   Consumer rejection of 3D interface.
*   High return rates due to unmet expectations.

The plan fails the basic test of offering a superior or distinct value proposition where it counts (apps, availability). It asks customers to pay more for less.

DECISION: NO-GO
