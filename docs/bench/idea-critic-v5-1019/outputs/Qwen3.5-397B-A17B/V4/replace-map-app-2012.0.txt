First call: NO-GO. Replacing a mature, data-rich rival product with an unproven internal alternative on day one invites immediate user backlash and functional failure.

**Steelman:** Building proprietary mapping is strategically essential for long-term control and differentiation, but launching it as the exclusive default before reaching parity is a gamble that sacrifices current user trust for future potential.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Retain rival app; maintain quality but cede strategic control.
2.  **Proposed (Big Bang):** Switch default immediately; high risk of broken experience and user anger due to data gaps.
3.  **Better Way (Staged):** Launch the new app as an optional update or side-by-side choice while aggressively patching data holes. Switch default only after independent verification proves parity in key markets.

**Fit:** Aligns with the vision of control but violates the north star of user satisfaction if the product is demonstrably worse at launch.

**Pre-mortem:** Users attempt to navigate to critical locations (hospitals, new businesses) and fail due to missing data. Trust evaporates instantly; tech press highlights errors; the brand becomes synonymous with "broken maps." This is a one-way door; once users lose confidence, regaining it is exponentially harder than gaining it.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "a lot less data" and "wrong" information. These are not hypothetical risks but stated facts of the current build state.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Functional Deficit:** The core utility (navigation/data accuracy) is explicitly acknowledged as inferior to the incumbent. Releasing a worse product as the mandatory default contradicts basic product hygiene.
2.  **User Harm:** Forcing a downgrade on the entire installed base guarantees annoyance and utility loss for tasks users rely on daily.

**Risks:** Brand reputation damage, increased support costs, rapid churn to competitors.

**Decision:** The strategic goal is sound, but the execution plan is flawed. You cannot force a downgrade on users. The lack of a pilot, a fallback mechanism, or a parity threshold makes this specific rollout plan unacceptable.

DECISION: NO-GO
