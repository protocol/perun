First call: GO. A small, high-price, operator-heavy concept offers optionality without forcing the giant to abandon its core low-cost, high-speed model.

**Steelman:** A minority stake in 16 units acts as a low-cost real-world laboratory for a "better-fast" segment, preserving capital while capturing upside if the model scales, without distracting management from core operations.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Zero risk, zero learning; cedes the emerging premium-quick segment to competitors.
2.  **Acquire majority/control:** High capital risk, forces integration of incompatible labor models, distracts management.
3.  **Proposed minority stake:** Caps financial exposure, isolates operational complexity within the target chain, retains right to scale later.
The proposal beats "do nothing" by purchasing market intelligence and optionality at a fraction of full acquisition cost.

**Fit:** Assumes the giant's north star includes long-term portfolio diversification beyond strict fast-food metrics. The independent structure prevents cultural collision between slow-craft and fast-assembly models.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the giant attempts to impose its supply chain or labor standards on the burrito chain, destroying the quality premium that justifies the higher price. This is a two-way door; the stake can be sold or diluted if the concept fails to scale. Delay costs the opportunity to learn about consumer willingness to pay for quality in a quick-service setting during a critical market shift.

**Evidence:** The proposal explicitly limits exposure (minority stake, 16 units) and operational interference (run independently). The risks cited (scale, labor, price) are inherent to the experiment, not flaws in the investment structure; they are the variables the stake is meant to test.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented shows the plan fails as proposed; the risks are manageable via the minority/independent structure.
**Risks:** Concept may remain a niche; management might ignore the learning opportunity. These are monitored via the "right to invest more later" clause.

**Kill Criteria:** If same-store sales decline for two consecutive quarters post-investment, or if the chain requires capital injections beyond the initial stake to survive, halt further investment. Leading metric: Same-store sales growth vs. traffic.

DECISION: GO
