First call: GO. The stated fact that bottles were poisoned after leaving the factory creates an immediate, unquantifiable threat to human life that cannot be contained by a local response when the distribution chain is national and the perpetrator is unknown.

Steelman: A nationwide withdrawal and production halt is the only defensible action to prevent further deaths and preserve the brand's long-term viability, despite massive short-term costs and competitive displacement.

Alternatives analysis:
1. Do nothing/Local recall only: Risks additional deaths if poisoned units spread; guarantees brand destruction if more victims emerge.
2. Partial halt: Fails to stop the bleeding of public trust; signals hesitation.
3. Nationwide withdrawal (Proposed): Highest immediate cost, but the only path to retaining the "safe" brand equity required to ever sell again.

Fit: Aligns with the primary north star of any consumer health company: do no harm. Without trust, market share is irrelevant.

Pre-mortem: If we limit the recall and another person dies in a different city, the company faces criminal negligence charges and total annihilation. The cost of delay (waiting for more data) is measured in human lives and irreversible reputational collapse.

Evidence: The proposal states the poisoning occurred post-factory in a metro area, but the mechanism of tampering is unknown and the product is nationally distributed. The risk is not theoretical; the harm has already occurred.

Blockers: NONE. No fact presented suggests the poison is contained or that the brand can survive further casualties. The financial cost, while large, is not a blocker to saving lives; it is the price of admission for future existence.

Risks: Permanent loss of shelf space and market share are real, but they are secondary to the existential risk of being associated with multiple deaths. These risks are mitigated by the planned relaunch in tamper-resistant packaging, which turns the crisis into a category-leading safety standard.

The strongest attack—that this is an overreaction to a local crime—fails because the inability to verify the scope of tampering makes any narrower response a gamble with human life. The "signal of danger" is already present; hiding it compounds the guilt.

DECISION: GO
