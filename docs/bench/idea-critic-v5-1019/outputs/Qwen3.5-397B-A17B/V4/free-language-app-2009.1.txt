First call: NO-GO. The plan explicitly defers revenue validation while incurring full build costs, betting on an unproven behavioral shift (learning via translation) without a mechanism to ensure quality or conversion.

**Steelman:** A free, crowd-sourced translation platform can simultaneously educate users and generate revenue by selling verified translations, provided quality controls and user retention are solved post-launch.

**Attack:**
1.  **Quality vs. Value:** The core revenue engine relies on learners producing sellable work. Early-stage learners produce low-quality output. Publishers will not pay for unverified, novice translations, creating a gap between "learning value" and "market value." Without immediate revenue, the project burns cash with no bridge.
2.  **Incentive Misalignment:** Learners seek proficiency; publishers seek accuracy. If the system prioritizes learning (allowing errors), the product is unsellable. If it prioritizes accuracy (correcting errors heavily), the learning loop breaks or requires expensive expert oversight, destroying the margin.
3.  **Deferred Validation:** "Look for revenue later" is a fatal flaw for a sustainability claim. It assumes product-market fit for the *revenue* side exists before testing it. Established competitors charge because the unit economics of language instruction are hard; giving it away requires a proven alternative monetization path, which is currently hypothetical.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "freemium" model with expert-verified translation tiers or a B2B pilot with a single publisher to test quality thresholds would validate the revenue engine before building the full free platform. Doing nothing preserves capital until the unit economics of "learner-as-translator" are proven on a smaller scale.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Contradiction:** The proposal states the nonprofit model is "unsustainable" yet adopts a build-first, revenue-later strategy that guarantees initial zero revenue while incurring high development and hosting costs. This contradicts the sustainability goal immediately.
*   **Quality Gap:** No mechanism is proposed to bridge the gap between novice output and professional standards required for sale.

**Risks:** High churn of free users; reputational damage from poor translations; inability to pivot to paid models after conditioning users to free access.

The plan fails the sustainability test on its own terms by delaying the only proposed solution to that problem.

DECISION: NO-GO
