First call: GO. The proposal converts a historical failure (expensive, standalone TV device) into a low-friction accessory by leveraging existing user hardware (phones/PCs) and minimizing upfront cost, directly addressing the primary barrier to entry.

Steelman: A sub-$35 HDMI dongle eliminates the need for a new remote or complex UI by using the user's smartphone as the controller, sacrificing marginal hardware profit to rapidly install a streaming platform in living rooms and stimulate ecosystem growth via an open developer kit.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing: Cedes the living room to competitors; stagnant growth in core search/video business.
2. Full standalone box: Repeats previous failures due to high cost, remote dependency, and consumer confusion.
3. Software-only (smart TV apps): Fragmented integration, slow adoption cycles dependent on TV manufacturers.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of ubiquitous content access. It bypasses the "remote confusion" risk by removing the remote entirely, forcing a mobile-first interaction model that matches 2013 user behavior.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if major content providers refuse to build the initial apps, leaving the dongle useless. However, the open kit lowers the barrier for indie developers to fill gaps. The "no margin" on hardware is a feature, not a bug; the value is in the subsequent subscription and ad revenue, not the plastic sale.

Door/Delay: Two-way door; inventory risk is minimal due to low unit cost. Delay allows competitors to lock in living room real estate, making future entry exponentially harder.

Blockers: NONE. The cited risks (confusion, margin, app count) are inherent to the strategy of disruption via commoditization and are mitigated by the design choices (phone-as-remote, loss-leader pricing).
Risks: Slow developer adoption; potential connectivity friction between phone and dongle. These are execution risks, not fundamental flaws.

Kill criteria: If app count remains below 10 critical services within 6 months, or if connection failure rates exceed 15% in beta, pivot to a hybrid remote model or abandon hardware. Leading metric: Weekly active devices connected per 1,000 units sold.

DECISION: GO
