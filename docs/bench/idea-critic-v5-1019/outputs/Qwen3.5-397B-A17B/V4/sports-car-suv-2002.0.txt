First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the brand's core identity without a proven bridge to retain loyalists or justify the dilution.

**Steelman:** Leveraging the brand's performance heritage to create a high-margin SUV that funds future sports cars while satisfying enthusiast demand for versatility.

**Attack:**
1.  **Brand Betrayal:** The core risk is not just perception but active alienation. Enthusiasts buy sports cars for purity; an SUV, regardless of power, signals a shift to mass-market luxury. Without evidence that the "loyal following" wants an SUV, this assumes demand where there may only be disdain.
2.  **Competitive Moat:** Entering a segment dominated by established luxury SUV makers with a "first-year cost [that] is high" creates a fragile financial position. If the brand image fails to transfer, the vehicle becomes an expensive orphan with no distinct value proposition other than a badge that may now be tainted.
3.  **Image Dilution:** A heavy vehicle fundamentally conflicts with the "sports-car" narrative. Even with a powerful engine, the physics of weight undermine the performance credibility required to maintain the brand's premium status. The proposal offers no mechanism to decouple the SUV's bulk from the brand's agility-focused identity.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve brand purity and margin stability; risk stagnation if the sports car market shrinks.
*   **License the Badge:** Allow the larger group to build the SUV under a sub-brand or different name, collecting royalties without risking the core brand equity.
*   **Wait for Proof:** Delay until a smaller, limited-run concept validates enthusiast interest before committing to a full shared-platform launch.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Premise:** The proposal admits loyal customers may see this as a "betrayal" yet proceeds as if the brand equity will automatically transfer to justify the high price. This is a fatal flaw in the value proposition.
*   **Financial Exposure:** High first-year costs combined with strong incumbents and unproven demand create a negative expected value scenario based on the stated facts.

**Risks:** Permanent damage to the sports-car brand's exclusivity; financial loss from low uptake; erosion of the enthusiast base.

The plan fails because it gambles the existing brand's soul on an unproven assumption that enthusiasts will accept a betrayal, while entering a crowded market with high costs and no clear differentiator beyond a potentially compromised badge.

DECISION: NO-GO
