First call: NO-GO. The unit economics fail immediately: a $10 monthly fee cannot cover even one full-price ticket, let alone "one film a day."

**Steelman:** Aggressively subsidize user growth with a $10/month unlimited plan to achieve monopoly scale, then monetize via data and studio deals before cash runs out.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** High burn, negative unit margin per active user. Requires infinite capital or impossible conversion rates to backend revenue.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid guaranteed cash incineration.
3.  **High-Price Niche:** Maintain the original high-fee model where ticket costs are covered by membership dues.

**Fit:** This contradicts basic arithmetic. No strategy overrides the fact that revenue ($10) is structurally lower than the variable cost (full ticket price × visits).

**Pre-mortem:** The company runs out of cash within months. Heavy users (just 2 movies/month) generate losses that data revenue cannot plausibly offset given current market rates for anonymized viewing data. Theater chains refuse participation, fearing price erosion, cutting off the supply needed for the value proposition.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the company pays "full ticket price" while charging "$10." Average ticket prices in 2017 exceeded $9; a single visit wipes out the margin, and the plan encourages daily visits. The "data and deals" revenue is marked as "unproven" in the text itself.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Negative Unit Economics:** The cost of goods sold (ticket price) exceeds revenue ($10) for any user watching more than ~1 film per month. The plan explicitly encourages daily viewing.
2.  **Unproven Monetization:** The pivot to profitability relies entirely on speculative revenue streams ("data," "deals") that are not contracted and historically insufficient to cover massive operational subsidies.

**Risks:** Theater chain boycott; regulatory scrutiny on data usage; inability to raise follow-on capital once burn rate is revealed.

**Decision:** The math does not work. Subsidizing a product where every additional unit sold increases the loss is not a strategy; it is liquidation.

DECISION: NO-GO
