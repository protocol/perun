First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a category with proven weak demand ("sold modestly," "poorly") using a dependency that limits utility ("needs the phone") and a known technical deficit ("one day" battery) without a unique value proposition to overcome these headwinds.

Steelman: A premium, ecosystem-locked wearable leveraging existing brand loyalty to capture high-margin accessory sales despite category immaturity.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Zero cost, zero risk, preserves focus on core phone/PC business while the market validates itself.
2. License OS to existing watchmakers: Lower capex, faster entry, tests demand without hardware risk.
3. Proposed plan: High capex, high execution risk, relies on changing consumer behavior in a stagnant category.

Fit: Assumes "ecosystem expansion" is a north star, but the stated facts suggest the market does not yet value this extension enough to sustain a new product line.

Pre-mortem: The product launches to lukewarm reception; daily charging frustrates users accustomed to week-long battery analog watches; the "second screen" feels redundant without standalone capability; inventory piles up as the category remains niche. Cost of delay is low given the lack of urgent competitive threat described.

Evidence: The proposal admits prior smartwatches "sold poorly" and "modestly." The "one day" battery life is a regression compared to traditional watches. The dependency on the phone reduces the device to a notification peripheral, a use case already shown to have limited mass appeal.

Blockers:
1. Market Validation: Stated facts confirm the category has "sold poorly." Entering a failing category with a constrained product (phone-dependent, short battery) contradicts basic product-market fit logic.
2. Value Deficit: No unique mechanism is proposed to solve the "why" for buyers who already reject existing options.

Risks: Brand dilution if the watch is perceived as a gimmick; inventory write-downs; distraction from core phone/PC innovation.

The proposal fails to demonstrate how this specific iteration succeeds where others failed. It requires a fundamental rethink of the value proposition or a wait-and-see approach.

DECISION: NO-GO
