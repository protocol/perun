First call: GO. The direct-to-consumer, low-overhead model logically undercuts incumbent pricing, which is the primary lever for a new entrant in a commodity market.

**Steelman:** By eliminating retail margins and physical storefronts, the company can offer superior price-performance ratios that compel early adopters to bypass the need for hands-on testing, fueling organic growth through word-of-mouth and technical publications.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Guarantees failure against established brands with deeper pockets.
2.  **Retail Expansion:** Immediate capital exhaustion; destroys the price advantage.
3.  **Reseller Network:** Cedes margin and control; slows feedback loops.

The "try before buy" objection is mitigated by targeting technically literate buyers who prioritize specs over showrooms. The lack of a service network is a valid risk but can be addressed via mail-in repairs and clear warranties, a trade-off acceptable at the proposed price point. Supplier credit is a hurdle, but initial assembly can be funded through direct customer prepayments or small batch cycles, turning inventory risk into cash flow.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if component shortages halt assembly or if a defect wave bankrupts the firm before reputation solidifies. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental flaws in the business model. The "one-way door" of incorporation is reversible via dissolution; the cost of delay is losing the first-mover advantage in the emerging clone market.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's facts (lower prices, direct sales) support the core claim. The risks cited (no stores, no brand) are inherent to the strategy, not contradictions of it.
**Risks:** Supply chain disruption, high return rates due to inability to test. These become kill criteria: if return rates exceed 15% or parts cannot be sourced within 30 days, pivot to a hybrid model or cease operations.

The plan beats "do nothing" and avoids the capital trap of retail. It requires strict cash management but offers a viable path to scale.

DECISION: GO
