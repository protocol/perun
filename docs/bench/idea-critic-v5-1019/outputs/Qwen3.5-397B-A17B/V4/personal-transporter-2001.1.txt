First call: NO-GO. The price point excludes the mass market required to replace car trips, and the regulatory environment is undefined.

**Steelman:** A revolutionary urban transporter that eliminates short car trips through superior maneuverability and zero emissions, commanding a premium for early adoption.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** At $5,000, the unit costs more than a reliable used car or a high-end motorcycle, yet offers less utility (no cargo, single rider, weather exposure). The value proposition fails against established alternatives.
2.  **Regulation:** Launching "widely" assumes legal permission to operate on sidewalks or roads. Current codes likely classify it ambiguously, inviting immediate bans or confiscation before market fit is proven.
3.  **Adoption Friction:** Requiring users to "learn to balance" creates a steep adoption curve compared to bicycles or scooters. Combined with the high price, this limits the addressable market to wealthy enthusiasts, not the general commuting public.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or a limited B2B pilot (security/tourism) is superior. A restricted pilot validates the learning curve and regulatory response without the reputational and financial risk of a failed consumer launch.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price vs. Utility:** The cost exceeds superior alternatives (cars) while offering inferior utility (cargo/capacity).
*   **Regulatory Void:** No stated path to legal operation; a wide launch invites immediate regulatory crackdown.

**Risks:**
*   Safety liability from untrained riders in traffic.
*   Stigma as a "toy" rather than transport due to price/performance ratio.

**Conclusion:**
The proposal attempts a mass-market consumer launch with a product that is legally ambiguous, economically uncompetitive for its stated purpose, and operationally unproven. The risks of regulatory ban and market rejection are immediate and fatal to the business case as presented. A capped, controlled pilot is necessary to resolve legal status and usability data before any wide release. As proposed, the plan fails the basic viability test.

DECISION: NO-GO
