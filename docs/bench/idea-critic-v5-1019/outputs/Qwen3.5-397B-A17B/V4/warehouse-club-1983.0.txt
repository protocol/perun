First call: GO. The model converts fixed costs into prepaid revenue, creating a cash float before inventory turns, which is vital for a capital-intensive startup facing a deep-pocketed incumbent.

Steelman: By strictly limiting SKUs and selling in bulk, the retailer minimizes handling costs and maximizes inventory velocity. The membership fee acts as a psychological lock-in and a direct profit center, allowing goods to be sold at near-cost to undercut the competitor on price while remaining profitable through dues.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Cedes the market to the incumbent; guaranteed failure to enter.
2. Traditional retail markup: Requires higher prices to cover overhead, failing to differentiate against the established low-price leader.
3. High-end niche: Avoids direct competition but limits scale and ignores the identified demand for bulk value.
The proposed model beats "do nothing" by offering a unique value proposition (price + exclusivity) that the incumbent cannot easily match without cannibalizing their existing higher-margin stores.

Fit: Assumes a market segment of small business owners and bulk-buying families sensitive to price over ambiance. This aligns with the economic pressure of the early 1980s.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if membership sales do not reach a critical mass to cover fixed warehouse costs before cash reserves deplete. This is a two-way door; underperforming locations can be closed, and the model tweaked. The cost of delay is high, as the incumbent will further entrench its supply chain dominance.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the assumption that shoppers will pay upfront for the *right* to save. Historical precedents of buying clubs suggest this is viable if the perceived savings exceed the fee significantly.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (fee resistance, competitor size, thin margins) are inherent to the strategy but not factual blockers; they are the very constraints the model is designed to navigate. The competitor's size makes them slower to pivot to a no-frills, membership-only format.

Risks: Execution error in site selection; inability to negotiate bulk pricing low enough to justify the fee. These are managed by the "limited range" constraint.

Kill Criteria: If the first location fails to achieve 80% of projected membership sign-ups within six months, halt expansion and reassess the value proposition. Leading metric: Membership renewal rate.

DECISION: GO
