First call: NO-GO. The proposal adds friction and cost while removing ownership, contradicting the core value proposition of home video at this time.

**Steelman:** A secure, recurring-revenue model for movies using a phone-verified, expiring disc format to prevent piracy and enable micro-transactions.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Value:** In 1998, the market is transitioning from rental (temporary access) to sell-through (permanent ownership, driven by DVD). This proposal regresses to a rental model but with higher hardware costs and "nickel-and-dime" fees for continued viewing. Customers dislike losing access to purchased media; paying extra to finish a movie creates hostility, not loyalty.
2.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option (standard DVD) offers lower player costs, universal compatibility, and a clear choice between renting (Blockbuster) or owning. A cheaper alternative is a standard DVD with a simple license key, avoiding expensive phone-calling hardware.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The format fails because studios refuse to fragment the market with a proprietary standard when DVD adoption is accelerating. Electronics makers reject building niche, expensive players. Customers return the players after realizing the "purchase" is actually a high-cost rental with connectivity hurdles.
4.  **Evidence & Logic:** The proposal admits customers dislike paying for repeated viewing yet makes this the core mechanism. It requires a phone line, excluding users without landlines or creating privacy concerns. The "few dollars" disc cost is offset by the premium player price, raising the barrier to entry significantly above standard DVD.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Incentives:** The core revenue mechanism (fees for extended viewing) is explicitly identified as a customer dislike in the risk assessment, yet no mitigation is offered.
*   **Market Timing:** Launching a proprietary, hardware-dependent format in 1998 fights the industry-wide standardization on DVD, which promises lower costs and broader compatibility.
*   **Economic Failure:** The hardware cost premium cannot be justified by a rental model that consumers already access more cheaply via existing rental stores without buying special players.

**Risks:** Studio fragmentation, consumer backlash against "expiring" media, high hardware adoption friction.

**Kill Criteria:** If pilot data shows >20% return rate on players within 30 days or <5% of users pay the extension fee, terminate immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
