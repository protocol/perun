First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on mail-order sales for a novel, low-speed vehicle with significant safety and usability concerns in the target market (UK), without addressing critical barriers.

**Steelman:** A sub-£400, license-free tricycle offers affordable mobility, leveraging a major manufacturer's assembly capacity to reach mass markets via mail order by January 1985.

**Attack:**
1.  **Safety & Regulation:** A low-speed (15 mph), low-profile vehicle mixed with traffic invites severe safety objections. In the UK, safety groups and regulators may block or restrict a vehicle perceived as a hazard, especially without weather protection. The proposal assumes "no licence" equals "no regulatory scrutiny," which is false for a new vehicle class.
2.  **Market Fit:** British winters and lack of weather protection severely limit utility, contradicting the "large numbers" sales goal. Mail order prevents users from testing stability or comfort, increasing return risks for a niche product.
3.  **Economics:** Selling parts as extras complicates the value proposition. If the base unit is too stripped down (no weather gear), the effective price rises, eroding the sub-£400 advantage.
4.  **Timing:** A January launch ignores seasonal demand cycles for open-air vehicles; winter is the worst possible time to unveil a weather-exposed tricycle in Britain.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Avoids reputational risk from potential safety scandals or mass returns.
*   *Pilot first:* Test sales in warmer months or specific regions before a national mail-order push.
*   *Bundle essentials:* Include weather protection in the base price to ensure usability.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches in January, receives negative press for safety and weather exposure, faces regulatory inquiries, and fails to sell due to seasonal irrelevance. The brand suffers.

**Blockers:**
*   **Safety/Regulatory Risk:** High probability of intervention by safety groups or regulators due to low visibility/speed in traffic, unaddressed in the plan.
*   **Seasonal Mismatch:** January launch for an unprotected vehicle in the UK guarantees poor initial uptake.

**Risks:** Low speed limiting appeal; short range; mail-order trust deficit.

**Decision:** The safety and seasonal blockers are fatal to the proposed "launch and sell large numbers" strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
