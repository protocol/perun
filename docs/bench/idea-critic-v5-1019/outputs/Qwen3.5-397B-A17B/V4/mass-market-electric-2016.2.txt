First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a company with negligible mass-production experience to execute its most complex manufacturing leap immediately, backed only by refundable deposits that may not convert to sales.

Steelman: Unveiling a cheap sedan now creates demand signaling and secures early capital via deposits to fund the tooling required for a 2017 volume ramp, betting that brand momentum will overcome production inexperience.

Options:
1. **Proposal**: High risk of production failure and cash burn if deposits refund; margin is too thin to absorb errors.
2. **Do Nothing**: Preserve capital, but cede the mass-market narrative to competitors and lose the "affordable EV" first-mover advantage.
3. **Limited Pilot**: Produce a small batch first to validate supply chains before full unveiling; slower market entry but reduces catastrophic failure risk.

Fit: Aligns with a vision of mass adoption but ignores the current stage constraint: the company lacks the operational foundation to deliver at the proposed volume/price point.

Pre-mortem: Production misses targets by 18 months; depositors demand refunds en masse due to delays; the company runs out of cash before delivering a single unit because the low margin provided no buffer for supply chain scaling costs. This is a one-way door; once unveiled, the brand promise is public. Delay cost: Competitors may announce similar plans, but execution speed matters more than announcement timing.

Evidence: The proposal admits zero experience at this volume and admits battery supply must be "scaled up sharply" without detailing how. Refundable deposits are not revenue; they are liabilities that vanish if execution slips.

Blockers:
- **Production Capability**: The company has never built at this volume; jumping straight to high-volume production of a complex product is a factual mismatch between capability and requirement.
- **Financial Buffer**: Low margins combined with refundable deposits mean any delay triggers a liquidity crisis with no revenue cushion.

Risks: Battery supply constraints, conversion rate of deposits, brand damage from delays.

The plan fails the basic operator test: you cannot promise high-volume delivery next year when you have never done it, especially with a financial structure (refundable deposits, low margin) that collapses under the slightest delay.

DECISION: NO-GO
