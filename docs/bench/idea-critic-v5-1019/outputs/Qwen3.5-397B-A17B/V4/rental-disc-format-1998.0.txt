First call: NO-GO. The proposal requires customers to buy expensive proprietary hardware for a rental model they already dislike, creating immediate friction against established, cheaper alternatives.

Steelman: A secure, time-limited physical media format could theoretically prevent late fees and enable new revenue streams if the hardware ecosystem aligns.

Attack: The value proposition collapses under basic economic and behavioral scrutiny.
1. **Hardware Friction:** Requiring a player that costs *more* than standard units creates a massive adoption barrier. Customers will not buy a $200+ dedicated device just to rent movies when VHS players are cheap and ubiquitous.
2. **Consumer Hostility:** The core risk identified—customers disliking repeated viewing fees—is fatal. In 1998, the market trend is toward ownership (buying the disc) or flat-fee convenience (emerging rental subscriptions), not micro-transactions per viewing session. This model feels punitive compared to the "all-you-can-watch" psychology of a rental store visit.
3. **Ecosystem Dependence:** Success hinges on simultaneous alignment of studios and electronics makers for a format that offers them no clear advantage over existing VHS/DVD rental economics, while alienating consumers.
4. **Technological Obsolescence:** Relying on phone-line callbacks for verification is brittle and expensive to maintain compared to offline media standards.

Alternatives:
- **Do Nothing:** Continue renting existing formats; margins are proven.
- **Cheaper Path:** Partner with existing DVD manufacturers to create a standard rental disc without proprietary hardware, accepting standard piracy risks but gaining instant compatibility.
- **Better Way:** Focus on the emerging subscription model (flat fee, no late fees) which directly addresses the "dislike paying repeated fees" pain point without new hardware.

Blockers:
- **Fact:** Customers explicitly dislike paying for repeated viewing; this model mandates it.
- **Fact:** Proprietary players cost more than standard ones, guaranteeing low initial install base.
- **Fact:** No studio or maker commitment exists; the proposal admits this is a risk, but without it, the format is dead on arrival.

Risks: High execution complexity, potential for total market rejection, rapid tech shift toward digital.

The proposal fails because it solves a problem (late fees/rental duration) by creating two larger ones (expensive hardware, annoying payment structure). It bets against consumer behavior and economic gravity.

DECISION: NO-GO
