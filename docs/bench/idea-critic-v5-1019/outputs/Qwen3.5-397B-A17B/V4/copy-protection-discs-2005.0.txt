First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades long-term brand trust and system security for a fragile, easily bypassed technical control, creating liabilities that exceed the value of prevented copies.

**Steelman:** Automatically installing restrictive software is the only viable method to stop mass copying in 2005, as user consent mechanisms are too easily ignored and manual installation rates are too low to matter.

**Attack:**
1.  **Security & Liability:** Software that installs silently and resists removal functions identically to rootkits. If security researchers (who will dissect the discs) find vulnerabilities, the record company becomes a vector for malware, inviting lawsuits and regulatory scrutiny far exceeding lost sales.
2.  **Customer Relations:** Treating paying customers as criminals by imposing intrusive software guarantees backlash. Competitors selling unprotected CDs will capture the market share of users who value system stability over the specific album.
3.  **Effectiveness:** History shows copy protection on open platforms (PCs) is broken within days. The company incurs all the reputational cost for zero long-term security gain.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves brand goodwill. Offering added value (digital downloads, better artwork) is a safer retention strategy than coercion.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, a major antivirus vendor flags the software as malware. News outlets report computers crashing after installation. The company is forced to issue a public apology and recall discs, having alienated the core customer base while pirates continue distributing cracked versions effortlessly.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The mechanism (silent install, hard to remove) matches the definition of malicious software, creating immediate legal and security exposure.
*   **Fact:** The proposal admits customers may object; proceeding anyway confirms a strategic misalignment with user expectations of ownership and safety.

**Risks:** High probability of technical failure (cracking), high probability of reputational damage, certain escalation of conflict with the security research community.

**Kill Criteria:** If any security vendor flags the software as malicious, or if customer complaint volume regarding installation issues exceeds 0.1% of units sold, the program must terminate immediately. However, the inherent design flaws suggest these thresholds will be breached instantly.

The risk profile is asymmetric: limited upside (temporary delay of copying) versus catastrophic downside (brand destruction, legal liability).

DECISION: NO-GO
