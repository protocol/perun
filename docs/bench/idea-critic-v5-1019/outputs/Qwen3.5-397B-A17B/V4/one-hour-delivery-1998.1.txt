First call: NO-GO. The unit economics described guarantee that every small order loses money, and scaling directly employed staff in multiple cities before proving demand multiplies fixed costs without a path to profitability.

**Steelman:** Rapid urban delivery of diverse goods creates unmatched convenience, securing dominant market share early to monetize later via scale or subscription.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unit Economics:** Free delivery on small orders with no minimum means the cost of labor and transport exceeds the gross margin on every transaction. Volume amplifies losses, not profits.
2.  **Operational Leverage:** Employing staff directly (vs. contractors) creates massive fixed costs and legal liability before revenue stability.
3.  **Behavioral Risk:** If usage relies on subsidies, removing them (to fix margins) likely collapses demand.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "do nothing" approach avoids burning capital. A better way is a single-city pilot with high minimum orders or delivery fees to validate willingness to pay before hiring staff or expanding.

**Pre-mortem:** The company runs out of cash within 18 months because the cost to acquire and serve a customer permanently exceeds their lifetime value. The "expand quickly" mandate locks in inefficient operations across too many markets before the model works in one.

**Blockers:**
*   **Negative Unit Economics:** The proposal explicitly states free delivery on small orders costs more than the margin. This is a mathematical guarantee of loss per transaction that volume cannot fix without changing the pricing model.
*   **Premature Scale:** Expanding to "more cities quickly" while employing staff directly violates the principle of validating the model in a controlled environment first.

**Risks:** Customer churn once subsidies end; regulatory hurdles for direct employment; capital inefficiency.

**Conclusion:** The proposal asks to scale a fundamentally broken economic model. The core mechanism (free delivery, no minimum, direct employment) ensures that growth accelerates bankruptcy. It must be revised to include delivery fees, minimum order values, or a contractor model, and proven in one city before expansion.

DECISION: NO-GO
