First call: NO-GO. Replacing a mature, data-rich mapping solution with an unproven internal alternative on all devices simultaneously invites massive user backlash and functional degradation.

**Steelman:** Owning the default map stack is strategically vital for long-term ecosystem control, data sovereignty, and feature integration, justifying the short-term pain of a flawed launch.

**Attack:** The proposal admits the new product has "a lot less data" and "wrong" business information. In navigation, accuracy is not a feature; it is the product. Launching a broken core utility as the *only* default on *all* phones removes the user's safety net. The risk of users being unable to find addresses or businesses is not theoretical; it is guaranteed by the admission of inferior data. The "annoyance" of removal understates the likelihood of active hostility and brand damage. A rival product being displaced is irrelevant if the replacement fails to perform basic functions.

**Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option preserves user trust. A "staged rollout" (opt-in beta or specific regions) would allow data correction without holding the entire customer base hostage. The proposal rejects these for a "big bang" launch, prioritizing speed over quality.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, headlines focus on tourists stranded by wrong turns and local businesses losing customers due to missing listings. The company is forced to quietly re-allow the rival app, admitting defeat and cementing the rival's dominance as the "reliable" choice.

**Blockers:** The stated fact that the new maps have significantly less data and contain errors constitutes a functional failure for a default navigation tool. Launching a known-defective core feature as a mandatory replacement violates the basic obligation of product reliability. This is not a risk; it is a confirmed deficit in the proposed solution.

**Risks:** Brand erosion, user churn, and legal liability from navigation errors.

**Decision:** The plan fails the basic utility test. You cannot mandate a worse experience as the default. Fix the data first, then stage the rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
