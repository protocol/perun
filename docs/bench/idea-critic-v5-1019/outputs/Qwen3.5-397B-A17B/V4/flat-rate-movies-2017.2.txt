First call: NO-GO. The unit economics fail immediately: $10/month revenue cannot cover even one $15 ticket, let alone "one film a day."

**Steelman:** Aggressively subsidize membership to achieve dominant market share, monetizing later via data and studio deals while leveraging new owner capital to absorb losses.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unit Economics:** At $10/month, a single user watching two films (average ticket ~$12) generates $10 revenue against ~$24 cost. The contribution margin is deeply negative (-140% per extra film). "Rapid growth" linearly scales losses; there is no path to profitability via volume alone.
2.  **Unproven Revenue:** Data and studio deals are speculative future hopes, not current cash flows. They cannot service the immediate cash burn of millions of daily tickets.
3.  **Incentive Misalignment:** Theater chains receive full ticket price but lose concession upside if subscribers binge-watch without buying food. Chains may block access or demand premiums, breaking the "full price" assumption.
4.  **Adverse Selection:** The model attracts only the heaviest users (daily watchers), maximizing the loss per member. Light users subsidize nothing because the price is too low to generate surplus.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; avoid guaranteed insolvency.
*   **High-Price Niche:** Maintain the existing high-fee/low-volume model where unit economics might balance.
*   **Hybrid:** Charge a base fee plus a reduced per-ticket fee to align marginal cost with marginal revenue.

**Pre-mortem:** The company runs out of cash within 12 months. The owner refuses further subsidies as "data monetization" fails to materialize at the required scale. Theater chains sue or delist the service.

**Blockers:**
*   **Mathematical Impossibility:** Revenue ($10) < Cost of Goods Sold (1+ tickets @ ~$12). No volume solves a negative margin per unit.
*   **Dependency:** Reliance on unproven, non-contractual future revenue streams to fund current operations.

**Risks:** Theater chain retaliation; regulatory scrutiny on predatory pricing; inability to pivot before insolvency.

**Conclusion:** This is not a business model; it is a liquidation plan disguised as growth. The core claim that rapid growth leads to earnings contradicts the arithmetic of the proposed price point.

DECISION: NO-GO
