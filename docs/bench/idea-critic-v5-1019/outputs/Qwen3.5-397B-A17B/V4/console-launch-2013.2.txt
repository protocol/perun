First call: GO. The proposal directly attacks the previous generation's failures (late, expensive, difficult) by prioritizing developer ease, consumer value, and ownership rights, which are the precise levers needed to regain lost ground.

**Steelman:** Launching a developer-friendly, lower-priced console with used-game support will rebuild ecosystem momentum and consumer trust faster than matching the rival's premium online-focused strategy.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" guarantees continued decline. Matching the rival's high price and restricted used-game policy ignores the specific reasons for the previous failure. The proposed path is the only one addressing the root causes.
2.  **Fit:** The strategy assumes the market values hardware ownership and lower entry costs over superior online services. Given consumer spending concerns, this is a defensible bet.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure would occur if the "strong head start" in online services proves to be the sole driver of modern console sales, rendering offline capabilities irrelevant. However, the risk of alienating developers and consumers with high prices is more immediate and verified by the previous generation's struggle.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal cites verified facts: the rival's head start, margin reduction, and weak launch lineup. These are known risks, not blockers. The margin hit is a strategic choice to gain volume, not a mathematical impossibility.
5.  **Blockers vs. Risks:** No factual blocker exists. The listed risks (margin, software lineup, online services) are execution challenges inherent to any turnaround, not proof the plan fails. The lack of launch software is a constraint to be managed, not a reason to abort the hardware strategy that enables future software.

**Decision:** The plan correctly identifies that winning back developers and consumers requires removing friction (price, used games, dev tools). The risks are manageable trade-offs for the potential upside of market re-entry.

DECISION: GO
