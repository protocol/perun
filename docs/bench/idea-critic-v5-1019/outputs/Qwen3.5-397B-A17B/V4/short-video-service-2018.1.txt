First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to charge a premium for a format (short-form mobile video) that the market already supplies abundantly for free, while artificially restricting utility by excluding televisions at launch.

Steelman: A curated, high-budget short-form service can differentiate itself from user-generated social content through professional storytelling, justifying a subscription where free alternatives fail to satisfy narrative hunger.

However, the attack on this claim reveals fatal flaws in the current plan. First, the "better way" analysis shows a massive disconnect: competitors like YouTube and emerging TikTok offer infinite short-form content at zero cost. To win, the paid service must offer unmatched value, yet the proposal removes a primary viewing mode (TV) at launch. This shrinks the addressable market and utility precisely when convincing users to pay is hardest. Second, the financial math is brittle. Raising $1.75 billion to fund high-budget shows creates an enormous recoupment hill. With a capped episode length (<10 mins), the inventory volume is lower than standard series, requiring either hyper-high pricing or impossible penetration rates to break even against free alternatives.

The pre-mortem is clear: The service launches in 2020, users try it on phones, find the library thin compared to free apps, complain they cannot cast to TV, and churn immediately. The high burn rate from producer advances drains the capital before a sustainable subscriber base forms.

Blockers:
1. **Value Proposition Contradiction:** Charging for short-form content while excluding TV viewing (a standard expectation for premium video in 2020) creates a product inferior to free competitors.
2. **Unit Economics:** High production budgets combined with short runtimes and a limited two-country launch make breakeven mathematically improbable without massive, unproven uptake.

Risks:
- Consumer resistance to paying for mobile-only shorts.
- Inability to secure enough hit shows to justify the subscription fee.
- Capital exhaustion before scaling beyond two countries.

The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test because the current trajectory leads to high cash burn with a product disadvantaged against free incumbents. The exclusion of TV support is a self-inflicted wound that cannot be justified by a "mobile-first" strategy when asking for premium dollars.

DECISION: NO-GO
