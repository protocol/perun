First call: GO. The low-price subscription model directly attacks incumbent inefficiencies (ad spend) with a superior unit economics structure, and the low-budget video is a high-upside, low-cost customer acquisition experiment that fits the 2012 digital landscape.

Steelman: A direct-to-consumer razor brand can undercut incumbents by eliminating retail markup and ad waste, using viral humor to acquire customers cheaply via a simple subscription funnel, validating demand before scaling supply.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** Low cost, high reversibility (video/website can be pulled), high risk of supplier dependence but immediate market validation.
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, zero risk, but guarantees missing the shifting consumer sentiment toward subscription convenience.
3. **Pre-sell/Waitlist:** Safer on inventory, but delays revenue, kills viral momentum, and adds friction to the "impulse buy" triggered by humor.
The proposed plan beats "do nothing" by capturing first-mover advantage in a nascent channel; it beats "waitlist" by prioritizing speed and cash flow over hypothetical inventory risk.

Fit: Aligns with a "disruptor" stage and a north star of "maximizing customer lifetime value through direct relationships."

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the single supplier raises prices or cuts quality, or if the video fails to convert. However, these are manageable risks: supplier contracts can include exit clauses, and video performance is measurable within days, allowing rapid pivots. This is a two-way door; if the video flops, the cost is negligible. If it succeeds, the supply chain can be diversified later.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the stated fact of high incumbent ad spend (creating price vulnerability) and the 2012 context where viral video CPMs were low. No internal data contradicts this.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (lawsuits, supplier dependence, traffic volume) are valid concerns but not factual blockers. Lawsuits are a cost of doing business in disruption, not a prohibition; supplier dependence is a standard startup phase risk; traffic uncertainty is the very hypothesis the low-cost video tests.

Risks: Supplier concentration, brand retaliation, conversion rates. These become kill criteria: if customer acquisition cost exceeds lifetime value after the first video, or if the supplier breaches contract terms, the venture pauses.

Kill Criteria: Stop if the cost to acquire a subscriber exceeds $5 within the first 30 days, or if the supplier cannot fulfill 95% of orders on time. Leading metric: Subscriber growth rate week-over-week. Review date: 30 days post-launch.

DECISION: GO
