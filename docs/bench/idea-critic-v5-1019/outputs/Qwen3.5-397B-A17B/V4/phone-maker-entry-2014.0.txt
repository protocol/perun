First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market with a differentiated feature (3D) that is unproven in value, while conceding on the primary drivers of smartphone adoption (app ecosystem and carrier availability).

Steelman: The device leverages unique computer vision for shopping and head-tracking to create a differentiated premium experience that justifies carrier exclusivity through subsidized membership growth.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High risk. Competes on a niche feature against incumbents with massive network effects. Single carrier limits total addressable market immediately.
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoids massive R&D and inventory risk. Focuses on core tablet/e-reader dominance where the brand is strong.
3. **Software-First Alternative:** Build the "point-to-buy" and 3D shopping features as an app for existing iOS/Android devices. Validates the shopping utility without hardware risk.

Fit: Assumes the retailer's north star is hardware market share rather than merchandise sales. If the goal is selling goods, forcing users into a hardware silo with fewer apps contradicts customer convenience.

Pre-mortem: The phone launches, but developers do not optimize for the 3D display or unique camera features due to low install base. Users find the app catalog too limited for a primary daily device. The single carrier deal fails to deliver projected volume. Inventory piles up, requiring deep discounts that destroy brand equity.

Evidence: The proposal admits the price matches leaders with "far larger app catalogs." In 2014, the smartphone market is a two-horse race where app availability is the primary switcher cost. A single-carrier launch further restricts access. There is no evidence provided that "3D-style display" is a desired feature; historically, 3D screens have been gimmicks with low engagement.

Blockers:
1. **Ecosystem Deficit:** The core value proposition (apps) is objectively weaker than competitors at the same price point.
2. **Unproven Differentiator:** The 3D feature is assumed to be valuable without pilot data; if users reject it, the phone has no remaining premium justification.
3. **Distribution Cap:** Single-carrier exclusivity artificially caps adoption in a market where network compatibility is key.

Risks: High inventory write-downs, brand dilution, distraction from core retail business.

Kill Criteria: If a software-only version of the shopping features does not drive significant incremental merchandise sales within 6 months, hardware development should not proceed.

DECISION: NO-GO
