First call: NO-GO. The unit economics described guarantee loss on every transaction, and the operational model creates unmanageable fixed costs before product-market fit is proven.

**Steelman:** Rapid urban delivery of diverse goods can capture high-value convenience seekers if subsidized initially to build habit, scaling volume to eventually offset delivery costs.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unit Economics:** Free delivery on small orders with no minimum ensures the cost of labor and transport exceeds revenue per order. Without a subsidy cap or minimum order value, volume amplifies losses rather than contributing to margin.
2.  **Operational Leverage:** Employing staff directly in multiple cities simultaneously creates massive fixed costs (hiring, management, vehicles) before validating demand in a single location. This violates the principle of staged capital deployment.
3.  **Behavioral Risk:** If usage relies on subsidies, removing them (to reach profitability) likely collapses demand, revealing no organic product-market fit.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Avoids burning venture capital on a structurally loss-making model.
*   *Staged Pilot:* Test in one neighborhood with a minimum order fee or delivery charge to validate willingness to pay before hiring staff or expanding.
*   *Asset-Light:* Use independent contractors or existing logistics networks to variable-ize costs before committing to direct employment.

**Pre-mortem:** The company runs out of cash within 18 months because the cost to acquire and serve a customer permanently exceeds their lifetime value, and the heavy operational footprint prevents pivoting quickly.

**Blockers:**
*   **Negative Unit Economics:** The proposal explicitly states free delivery on small orders costs more than the margin, with no mechanism (like a minimum order threshold) to correct this.
*   **Premature Scaling:** Expanding to "more cities quickly" while employing staff directly locks in high burn rate before validating the model in one location.

**Risks:** Customer churn upon subsidy removal; inability to manage large distributed workforce; capital inefficiency.

**Conclusion:** The proposal fails the basic test of selling a unit for more than it costs to deliver. It requires fundamental restructuring (minimum orders, delivery fees, single-city pilot, asset-light model) before capital deployment.

DECISION: NO-GO
