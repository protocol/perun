First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on mail-order sales for a novel, low-speed vehicle with known safety and usability deficits in the target market's climate, without a mechanism to overcome consumer hesitation or regulatory scrutiny.

**Steelman:** A sub-£400, license-free tricycle offers affordable mobility, leveraging a major manufacturer's assembly capacity to capture a mass market via direct mail order.

**Attack:**
1.  **Safety & Regulation:** A low-sitting vehicle (15 mph) mixed with traffic invites immediate opposition from safety groups. In the UK, such objections can trigger regulatory reviews that halt sales or mandate costly redesigns (lighting, visibility, structural standards) not accounted for in the "optional extras" model. The proposal assumes acceptance where conflict is probable.
2.  **Product-Market Fit:** British winters are wet and cold. A vehicle with no weather protection and short range is functionally unusable for a significant portion of the year. Selling this via mail order removes the ability for customers to test ride or assess comfort, increasing return rates and reputational damage.
3.  **Distribution Risk:** Mail order for a large, assembled mechanical device is logistically fragile. Without a dealer network for assembly support or repairs, early failures will overwhelm the entrepreneur. The "optional extras" strategy complicates the user experience and inventory management at launch.
4.  **Alternatives:** A phased rollout through selected retailers allows for real-world feedback on safety and weather usage before a national mail-order push. Doing nothing avoids the capital risk until weather-proofing or safety certifications are proven.

**Blockers:**
*   **Safety/Regulatory:** High probability of safety group intervention against a low-visibility, low-speed vehicle in traffic, potentially stopping the project post-launch.
*   **Usability:** No weather protection renders the product unsuitable for the UK climate for several months, undermining the value proposition for a general consumer launch.

**Risks:** High return rates due to comfort/speed mismatch; logistical failure of mail-order assembly/support; negative press from safety incidents.

**Conclusion:** The combination of unaddressed safety liabilities, seasonal unusability, and a high-risk distribution channel for a hardware product creates a fatal flaw. The plan does not survive the winter or the safety review.

DECISION: NO-GO
