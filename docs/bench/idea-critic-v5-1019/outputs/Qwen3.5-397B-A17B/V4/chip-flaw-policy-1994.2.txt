First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to ration a universal hardware defect based on an unverifiable burden of proof placed on the customer, ignoring the immediate reputational contagion described in the risks.

Steelman: Limiting replacements minimizes immediate financial bleed for a statistically rare error, preserving capital for future R&D while technically honoring warranty obligations for proven failures.

Attack: The core failure is the condition "only to users who can show that their work is affected." In floating-point arithmetic, errors are non-deterministic to the end-user and often silent; requiring proof of impact is functionally equivalent to denying all claims. This creates an adversarial relationship with the entire install base. While the error frequency is low per user, the *perception* of a hidden defect spreads instantly across the industry ("widely discussed"). The cost of a reputation collapse (loss of future sales, brand trust) vastly exceeds the one-time cost of a blanket replacement. The "do nothing" or "limited replacement" strategy bets that users won't notice, but the risk analysis explicitly states buyers believe the company is hiding the defect. That belief is already forming; the proposed response confirms it.

Alternatives: A blanket replacement program costs a "very large sum" upfront but converts a scandal into a demonstration of integrity, potentially increasing market share long-term. A "do nothing" approach risks total market rejection if independent researchers reproduce the flaw at scale.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-launch, a major academic institution publishes a paper demonstrating the flaw affects common spreadsheet calculations. The company's refusal to replace chips without "proof" becomes a case study in corporate arrogance. Sales of the next generation collapse.

Blockers: The requirement for users to prove impact is a factual blocker because it is technically infeasible for the stated user base, rendering the warranty void in practice while the defect remains universal. The stated risk of reputation suffering is not a possibility but an active trajectory that this plan accelerates.

Risks: High immediate cash outflow; supply chain strain for replacements.

DECISION: NO-GO
