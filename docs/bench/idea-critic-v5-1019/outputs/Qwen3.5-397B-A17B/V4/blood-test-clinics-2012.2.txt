First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $350M to unproven technology with no independent verification, violating basic capital allocation discipline.

Steelman: Partnering could secure a monopoly on cheap, rapid diagnostics if the device works as claimed.
Attack: The claim rests entirely on the founder's assertion. No published studies exist, methods are secret, and regulatory pathways are undefined.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Zero cost, zero risk, misses potential upside.
2. Small pilot: Test one device in one location for $1M to validate accuracy before scaling.
3. Proposed plan: $350M immediate rollout.
The proposed plan fails to beat "do nothing" on risk-adjusted returns because the core value proposition (accuracy) is unverified. A small pilot reaches the same validation upside for 0.3% of the cost.

Fit: This diverges from a supermarket's core competency (retail logistics) into high-stakes healthcare without internal expertise or external validation.

Pre-mortem: The device fails regulatory scrutiny two years later due to inaccurate readings. The chain faces massive lawsuits, reputational destruction, and $350M in sunk costs for useless clinic infrastructure. This is a one-way door; once built, clinics cannot be easily repurposed, and trust, once lost, is hard to regain. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of being wrong.

Evidence: The proposal explicitly states "technology has not been shown in published studies" and "company will not share its methods." These are facts, not assumptions. Investing $350M based on secret, unverified claims contradicts the evidence provided within the proposal itself.

Blockers:
1. No evidence of efficacy: The core claim (accurate tests) is unsupported by any data, while the proposal admits this gap.
2. Irreversible commitment: The scale ($350M, hundreds of stores) precludes learning before burning capital.
3. Regulatory uncertainty: Unverified methods guarantee future regulatory conflict.

Risks: Lack of medical expertise, potential for false positives/negatives harming customers.

This is not a risk to be managed; it is a fundamental failure of the investment thesis. The absence of a pilot is a blocker here because the technology is scientifically unproven. You cannot scale what you have not validated.

DECISION: NO-GO
