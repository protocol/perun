First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on membership fees for profit while entering a market dominated by an established competitor with superior scale and cash, despite the explicit risk that shoppers may refuse to pay fees.

Steelman: The model succeeds only if high volume compensates for thin margins and membership conversion remains high enough to cover fixed costs despite competitive pressure.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** Rapid expansion. High capital risk; low reversibility; depends on unproven customer willingness to pay fees against a dominant incumbent.
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoids capital destruction; preserves cash; misses potential market shift.
3. **Single-Location Pilot:** Opens one warehouse to validate fee conversion and bulk demand before scaling. Lower cost; high reversibility; delays upside but confirms viability.

Fit: Assuming the goal is sustainable profitability, rapid expansion contradicts the stated "thin margins leave no room for errors" constraint when facing a cash-rich rival.

Pre-mortem: The venture fails because the incumbent lowers prices temporarily to starve the new entrant of volume, causing membership renewals to collapse before the second location breaks even. This is a two-way door if limited to one site, but a one-way door if capital is committed to multiple warehouses upfront.

Evidence: The proposal admits shoppers "may not pay a fee" and margins are "thin." No evidence is provided that the specific demographic will pay a fee when a free alternative exists nearby. The financial math relies on an assumption (high fee adoption) that is explicitly listed as a risk.

Blockers:
1. **Unproven Core Revenue Driver:** The plan relies on membership fees for profit, yet the primary stated risk is that shoppers may not pay them. Scaling before validating this contradicts the "no room for errors" constraint.
2. **Capital Disadvantage:** Expanding against a competitor with "more locations and cash" using a "thin margin" model creates a fatal cash-flow vulnerability if volume targets are missed even slightly.

Risks: Execution difficulty, site availability, bulk buying behavior.

The absence of a pilot to verify fee willingness is a blocker given the explicit risk and thin margins. A cheaper, safer alternative (single-location pilot) reaches the same learning upside without the existential risk of premature scaling.

DECISION: NO-GO
