First call: GO. The company has survived collapse and secured eleven profitable quarters; the market is primed for a brand-reaffirming halo product rather than a spec-sheet competitor.

**Steelman:** Launch a premium, fanless, transparent desktop that prioritizes industrial design and silence over expandability, targeting creative professionals and brand loyalists willing to pay for aesthetics and reliability.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
Critics argue the price exceeds faster, expandable towers and the all-in-one model. However, comparing this to a tower is a category error; the value proposition is silence and form factor, not raw upgradability. The "do nothing" option risks stagnation, letting competitors own the high-design niche. A cheaper, plastic-cased alternative would dilute the premium recovery narrative. The manufacturing risk (acrylic flaws) is a production hurdle, not a strategic blocker, solvable by tight quality control and pricing in the defect margin.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a "brand rehabilitation" north star, moving from commodity to lifestyle. If it fails, it will likely be due to pricing out the core base or excessive manufacturing defects. Yet, the cost of delay is losing the "cool" factor to emerging competitors. The lack of slots is a feature, not a bug, forcing a clean break from legacy upgrade cycles.

**Blockers & Risks:**
Blockers: NONE. The proposal's stated risks (price, manufacturing, lack of slots) are inherent to the premium segment strategy, not factual contradictions. The higher price is justified by the target demographic's willingness to pay for design.
Risks: Manufacturing yield on acrylic; alienating price-sensitive upgraders. These are managed via kill criteria: if initial production yield falls below 85% or pre-orders miss targets by 20%, pivot to a limited "collector's edition" run rather than mass market.

The proposal correctly identifies a gap for a non-expandable, high-design machine. The risks are executional, not strategic. The company's financial stability allows absorbing the R&D hit for a potential market-defining product.

DECISION: GO
