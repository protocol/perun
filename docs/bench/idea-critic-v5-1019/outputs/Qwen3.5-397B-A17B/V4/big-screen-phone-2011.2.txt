First call: GO. The market lacks a high-end device bridging the phone-tablet gap, and screen real estate is a defensible premium differentiator despite skepticism.

**Steelman:** A 5+ inch device with a stylus creates a new "phablet" category, capturing users needing more productivity than a 4-inch phone offers without carrying a separate tablet, justifying a premium price.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the emerging large-screen segment to competitors; risk existing phones feeling obsolete as media consumption grows.
2.  **Launch a tablet only:** Misses the "always-with-you" utility; fails to capture users wanting one primary device.
3.  **Proposed large phone:** High risk of niche rejection, but highest upside if it defines a new standard. It beats "do nothing" by owning the category early.

**Fit:** Aligns with a vision of mobile productivity and premium innovation. Assumes users value screen size over one-handed ergonomics.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if reviewers successfully frame the size as "unwieldy" rather than "immersive," or if the stylus is perceived as a gimmick rather than a tool. This is a two-way door; if sales lag, the line can be discontinued or shrunk without destroying the core brand. The cost of delay is losing the "first mover" narrative in the large-screen space.

**Evidence:** Based on the stated gap between 4-inch phones and tablets. No internal sales data contradicts the potential for a premium tier.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (reviewer bias, stylus adoption, cannibalization) are uncertainties, not proven facts that make the plan impossible. Cannibalization is acceptable if the net revenue increases via higher premium unit sales.

**Risks:** Market rejection of form factor; stylus remains a niche accessory. These become kill criteria: if Q1 sales do not exceed X units or attach rate for stylus accessories is below Y%, pivot marketing or discontinue.

**Decision:** The potential to define a new category outweighs the risk of temporary reviewer skepticism.

DECISION: GO
