First call: NO-GO. The price is excessive for an unproven revenue model in a volatile market.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform now secures a dominant position with young demographics before competitors do, allowing immediate monetization via established ad infrastructure.

**Attack:**
1.  **Valuation Mismatch:** $580 million for a two-year-old site with "unproven" revenue is speculative. The proposal admits turning growth into cash is untested; paying a premium for hypothetical synergy violates basic capital discipline.
2.  **Volatility Risk:** The core asset is "taste," which the proposal explicitly states can change quickly. Unlike media libraries or distribution networks, social graphs of this era are fragile. If the audience migrates (a stated risk), the acquisition becomes a total loss with zero salvageable assets.
3.  **Competitive Moat:** With "other social sites competing," there is no defensibility. High churn combined with high entry barriers for competitors is not present here.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves $580M. A partnership or minority stake tests the revenue synergy without assuming total downside risk. A cheaper, staged investment reaches the same learning upside without the catastrophic failure mode.

**Pre-mortem:** One year post-acquisition, the platform's cultural relevance collapses as users migrate to a new competitor. The media company is left with an expensive, empty shell, unable to integrate non-existent revenue streams, resulting in a massive write-down and reputational damage for chasing a fad.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial:** The cost ($580M) relies entirely on future revenue assumptions that the proposal admits are "unproven." No current cash flow justifies the multiple.
*   **Strategic:** The asset's value is contingent on a volatile variable (user taste) with no stated mechanism to lock it in.

**Risks:** Total capital loss, brand dilution, distraction from core business.

**Kill Criteria:** If user growth flattens for two consecutive quarters post-acquisition, or if ad revenue conversion remains below 5% of industry standard within 12 months, divest immediately. However, the initial risk is too high to enter. The lack of proven monetization at this price point is a fatal flaw.

DECISION: NO-GO
