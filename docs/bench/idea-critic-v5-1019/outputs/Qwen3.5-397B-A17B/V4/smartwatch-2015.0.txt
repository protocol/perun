First call: GO. A phone maker extending its ecosystem to the wrist with a differentiated app store and material tiers is a logical defensive and offensive move, despite historical category weakness.

**Steelman:** This is not merely a watch; it is a mandatory peripheral for the existing phone base that increases switching costs and creates a new high-margin revenue stream via apps and materials, leveraging brand trust where generic competitors cannot.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the wrist to competitors, risking future ecosystem fragmentation and loss of health data ownership.
2.  **License OS only:** Lower margin, less control over user experience, weaker brand integration.
3.  **Proposed Path:** High effort, but captures full value, controls the narrative, and deepens hardware lock-in.

**Fit:** Assumes the strategy is ecosystem dominance and recurring services revenue. If the north star is standalone hardware units, this fails; if it is lifetime user value, this is essential.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if battery life frustrates users daily or if the "second screen" offers no unique utility beyond notifications, making it a costly novelty. The one-day battery is a known constraint, not a surprise.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits modest historical sales but ignores that no *major phone maker* has attempted this with a closed, integrated ecosystem. The risk of "needing the phone" is actually a feature: it drives phone loyalty.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (poor history, dependency, battery, necessity) are category characteristics, not fatal flaws specific to this execution. They are mitigated by the app store (utility), material tiers (luxury appeal), and brand integration (necessity).

**Risks:** Low adoption rate; battery complaints; developer apathy for the app store. These are managed via kill criteria: if attachment rate to phone sales is <15% in Year 1, or if app store growth stalls <5% QoQ after launch, pivot to a lower-cost fitness tracker model.

**Decision:** The potential to lock users deeper into the primary hardware ecosystem outweighs the risk of a modestly performing peripheral. The "need" is created by integration, not pre-existing demand.

DECISION: GO
