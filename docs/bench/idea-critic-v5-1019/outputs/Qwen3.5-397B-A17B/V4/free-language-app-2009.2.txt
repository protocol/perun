First call: NO-GO. The plan explicitly defers revenue validation while incurring full build costs, relying on an unproven market behavior (learners producing sellable translations) that directly conflicts with the core value proposition (free education for novices).

Steelman: A dual-sided platform where language learners generate value through translation tasks, subsidizing free education while gaining practical experience, eventually monetizing via B2B data sales.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Zero cost, zero risk, but no mission progress.
2. Manual pilot: Recruit 50 students to translate specific documents manually before writing code. Cost: low; Risk: validates quality demand immediately; Reversibility: high.
3. Paid beta: Charge early users to validate willingness to pay before building free tiers. Cost: medium; Risk: validates revenue model first; Reversibility: medium.

Fit: Assumes "free for all" is the north star, but without a verified revenue engine, this leads to resource exhaustion, not scale.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, learners produce low-quality translations, publishers reject the data, and the team runs out of funding within 18 months because no revenue stream was tested prior to development. This is a one-way door on capital; delay costs opportunity to test cheaper models now.

Evidence: The proposal admits the revenue model is "unproven" and translations "may be poor." No data exists to support the assumption that novices can produce commercial-grade work.

Blockers:
1. The plan commits to building the product before validating the core economic hypothesis (that learner translations are sellable). This violates basic startup hygiene: build only after evidence suggests a market exists.
2. The "free for all" constraint removes the ability to test price sensitivity or user commitment before incurring full engineering debt.

Risks: Quality control failure, publisher rejection, inability to monetize later.

The proposal fails because it treats an unverified hypothesis as a foundation for heavy investment. The correct path is a manual, low-cost pilot to prove publishers will buy learner translations and that learners can produce them. Building the web app now is premature optimization of an unvalidated business model.

DECISION: NO-GO
