First call: NO-GO. The plan explicitly defers revenue validation while incurring full build costs, relying on an unproven dual-sided market where low-quality supply (learner translations) must satisfy paying demand (publishers).

Steelman: A free, crowd-sourced translation model can simultaneously educate users and generate high-quality data if the workflow creates immediate value for publishers, funding the mission without upfront capital.

Attack:
1. **Quality Mismatch:** Publishers require professional-grade accuracy. Learners, by definition, lack proficiency. Without a rigorous, costly verification layer (which contradicts the "free/lean" premise), the product is unsellable. The proposal admits this risk but offers no mitigation.
2. **Revenue Timing:** "Build now, monetize later" is fatal for a self-funded academic project. Without a nonprofit structure or seed capital, the team cannot sustain server costs or development long enough to find product-market fit.
3. **Competition:** Established players offer polished, verified content. A platform offering "learning via work" competes with dedicated LMS tools and professional translation agencies, satisfying neither group fully.

Better Way:
*   **Do Nothing/Wait:** Validate the translation quality hypothesis manually before writing code. Have the student translate a sample set; attempt to sell it immediately.
*   **Pivot:** Focus solely on the learning tool first, monetizing via premium features or donations, removing the complex B2B translation marketplace dependency.
*   **Nonprofit Route:** Re-evaluate the nonprofit stance; grants or donations align better with "free education" than an unproven commercial arbitrage model.

Pre-mortem: The app launches, attracts students seeking free lessons, but produces garbage translations. Publishers reject the output. Revenue remains zero. Servers shut down due to unpaid bills within six months.

Blockers:
*   **Economic Contradiction:** The core value proposition (selling learner work) contradicts the user state (novice learners). No evidence suggests learners can produce sellable goods immediately.
*   **Funding Gap:** Explicitly rejecting sustainable structures (nonprofit) while delaying revenue creates an inevitable cash-flow failure before product maturity.

Risks: High churn of free users; inability to recruit paying publishers; legal liability for poor translations.

The plan fails the basic test of economic viability as stated. It requires a fundamental pivot to validate the supply quality or secure funding before construction.

DECISION: NO-GO
