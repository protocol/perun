First call: GO. The proposal converts a historical failure (expensive, standalone TV devices) into a low-friction accessory by leveraging existing user hardware (phones/PCs) and prioritizing distribution over margin.

Steelman: A sub-$50 HDMI dongle without a remote or on-screen UI acts as a pure receiver for mobile-cast content, minimizing hardware costs and user setup friction while betting on an open developer kit to eventually expand the catalog.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing: Cedes the living room to established streaming boxes with superior app catalogs.
2. Full standalone box: Repeats past failures with high manufacturing costs and poor sales history.
3. Premium dongle with remote: Increases price point, reducing the "impulse buy" potential and margin safety.

Fit: Aligns with a search company's strength in software and ecosystem integration rather than hardware retail. It moves the north star of "universal access" by placing the service in the living room via the device users already hold.

Pre-mortem: The product fails if major content providers refuse to support the casting protocol, leaving the dongle useless at launch. However, the open kit mitigates this by allowing community-driven solutions. This is a two-way door; if it flops, the financial loss is capped by the low unit cost and lack of dedicated support infrastructure.

Evidence: The proposal explicitly acknowledges the risks (no remote, low margin, thin app support) but counters them with the specific constraint of "very cheap" and the mechanism of "phone/computer" control. The historical data on poor sales of previous TV devices supports the pivot to a dongle form factor.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited are execution challenges inherent to a low-margin strategy, not factual contradictions. The lack of a remote is a feature to reduce cost, not a bug, given the casting model.

Risks: Slow developer adoption; consumer confusion regarding setup. These are managed by the low entry price and the clear "cast from phone" value proposition.

Kill criteria: If developer kit adoption yields fewer than 10 functional apps within six months, or if return rates due to setup confusion exceed 15%, the project pivots to a bundled remote or software-only focus.

DECISION: GO
