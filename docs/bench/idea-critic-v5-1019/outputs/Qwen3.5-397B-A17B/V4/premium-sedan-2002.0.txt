First call: NO-GO. The core claim—that a mass-market brand can command premium prices for a luxury sedan without a distinct luxury identity—contradicts the fundamental driver of the luxury segment: status signaling via brand heritage.

**Steelman:** The carmaker leverages its scale to offer superior build quality and features at a competitive price, hoping to win buyers on value rather than badge prestige.

**Attack:**
1.  **Brand Inelasticity:** Luxury purchases are often Veblen goods; high price and exclusivity drive demand. A mass-market badge signals "affordable" and "common," actively repelling the target demographic who buy luxury specifically to differentiate themselves from the mass market.
2.  **Economic Mismatch:** Building a dedicated factory for low-volume production destroys the economies of scale that make the parent company profitable. The unit economics will fail unless sales volume is high, but the brand positioning prevents high volume in the luxury tier.
3.  **Channel Conflict:** Selling a $80,000 sedan alongside $20,000 compacts in the same showrooms dilutes the perceived value of the flagship and confuses the sales narrative.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Maintain profitability in the mass segment; avoid capital waste.
*   **Sub-Brand:** Launch a separate division (e.g., Lexus, Acura model) with distinct dealerships and branding to create the necessary psychological distance. This is the industry-standard solution for this exact problem.
*   **Top-Trim Only:** Offer a high-spec version of an existing large sedan, but price it below true luxury rivals, accepting a "near-luxury" position.

**Pre-mortem:** The factory opens, but sales are 20% of forecast. Fleet buyers purchase the cars for discounts, further tarnishing the image. The brand is stuck with massive fixed costs and inventory that cannot be sold without destroying residual values.

**Blockers:**
*   **Brand Perception:** The proposal admits buyers pay for the "badge of premium makers" and the brand has "no luxury image." There is no mechanism in the plan to change this perception before launch.
*   **Unit Economics:** Dedicated factory + low volume + mass-market pricing power = negative margin.

**Risks:** Brand dilution of the core business; massive capital loss on the dedicated facility.

**Kill Criteria:** If a sub-brand strategy is not adopted, the project fails. If first-year sales do not exceed 50,000 units globally (unlikely for a new luxury entrant), halt production.

DECISION: NO-GO
