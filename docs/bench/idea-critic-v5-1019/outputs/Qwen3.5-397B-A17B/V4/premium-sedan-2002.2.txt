First call: NO-GO. The core claim—that a mass-market brand can command premium pricing for a luxury sedan without a distinct luxury identity—contradicts the fundamental driver of the luxury segment: status signaling via the badge.

Steelman: Leveraging existing manufacturing scale and known reliability to undercut premium rivals on price while matching their feature set could attract value-conscious luxury buyers.

Attack: The proposal ignores the "Veblen good" nature of luxury cars, where high price and exclusive branding *are* the product. Buyers in this segment purchase social signaling, not just materials or engine size. A mass-market badge signals "economy," actively repelling the target demographic who seek differentiation from the mainstream. The risk that "buyers pay for the badge" is not a risk; it is the market reality.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Preserve brand equity and margins in the core segment.
2. Create a sub-brand (e.g., Lexus, Acura model): Isolate the luxury image from the mass-market parent, allowing premium pricing without brand dilution.
3. Proceed as proposed: High probability of brand dilution, channel conflict, and financial loss due to inability to sustain premium pricing without the requisite brand equity.

Fit: Entering the premium class requires a premium image. The current strategy attempts to skip the image-building phase, relying solely on hardware specs, which history shows is insufficient for luxury positioning.

Pre-mortem: The factory opens, but sales are 10% of forecast. The cars sit on lots, discounted heavily to move inventory. The heavy discounting destroys the residual value of the entire brand, causing core mass-market customers to lose confidence in the brand's value proposition, while luxury buyers still reject the badge. The factory becomes a stranded asset.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the brand has "no luxury image" and that "luxury buyers may not accept a mass-market name." These are stated facts, not hypothetical risks. Building a dedicated factory for a product with a known acceptance barrier creates massive fixed costs against uncertain demand.

Blockers: The stated fact that the brand lacks a luxury image directly contradicts the requirement to sell at premium prices. The hardware (engine, suspension) does not overcome the branding deficit.
Risks: Brand dilution, low volume inefficiency, channel rejection.

DECISION: NO-GO
