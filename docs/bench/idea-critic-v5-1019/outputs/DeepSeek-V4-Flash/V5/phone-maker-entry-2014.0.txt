**Steelman:** A trusted retailer with a huge customer base, deep product catalog, and membership ecosystem can enter phones with a differentiated device that ties shopping directly to the camera, justifying a premium price despite being late to the market.

**Attack:**
- **Core contradiction:** The proposal’s own evidence says phones win on screen and apps. This device offers neither advantage; it offers a head-tracking 3D effect (novelty, not utility) and a camera shopping feature that competes with the retailer’s own app, which already exists on every phone.
- **No distribution leverage:** Single-carrier, premium price, no app catalog advantage. The stated facts show target buyers (mainstream phone users) already have superior alternatives.
- **What would have to be true:** That users will switch ecosystems for a shopping shortcut and a gimmick. Nothing in the proposal supports that; the risks explicitly contradict it.
- **Cheaper alternative:** Improve the existing app’s camera-shopping feature. Reaches the same upside (driving purchases) at a fraction of cost and risk, with zero hardware, carrier, or inventory exposure.
- **Do nothing:** Retain margins and focus on core tablet/e-reader strength.

**Verdict:** The proposal’s own stated evidence—that buyers prioritize screen and apps—removes the route to the upside. The alternative (app enhancement) reaches the same goal cheaper. No pilot or cap can fix a fundamental mismatch between the product and the market’s stated criteria. This is a one-way door with no credible kill criterion that would save it.

**Dissent ledger:** Strong brand and membership could drive initial interest, but not sustained premium-phone adoption.

**Remaining risk of rejection:** Low; the downside of saying no is missing a speculative new category.

DECISION: NO-GO
