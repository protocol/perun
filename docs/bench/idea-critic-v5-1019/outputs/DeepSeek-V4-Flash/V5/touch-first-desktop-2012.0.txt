First call: GO — the strategic threat is real, but the forced rollout on non-touch PCs is the fatal flaw.

Steelman: Desktop dominance is eroding; a bold, unified touch-first experience accelerates ecosystem shift and app-store revenue before tablets fully cannibalize PCs.

Attack: The change is irreversible for the core user base. Corporate and desktop users — the OS’s cash cow — are forced into a confusing, touch-first UI on hardware without touch. Training and support costs are high, and there is no easy rollback. Developers face a new paradigm with uncertain payoff. The stated risks are not hypothetical; they are near-certain outcomes for the majority of existing users.

Better way: Ship the start screen as an opt-in mode on non-touch PCs, defaulting to the classic desktop, while making touch-first the default on tablets. Add the app store to both. This captures the tablet upside without alienating the desktop base, is reversible, and lowers developer risk by keeping a large installed base on the familiar UI.

Kill criteria: If within 12 months enterprise adoption drops, support tickets spike, or developer submissions lag targets, the forced rollout has no pivot — you cannot undo it without massive reputational damage.

The proposal’s own evidence — dominant desktop share, high business training cost, no easy return — shows the forced change fails for the core users. The alternative reaches the same tablet upside at far lower risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
