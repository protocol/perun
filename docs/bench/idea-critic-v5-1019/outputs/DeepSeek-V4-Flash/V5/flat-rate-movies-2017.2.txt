First call: NO-GO. The core economics fail on the stated facts.

The fee is ~$10/month for up to 30 films. Even at modest usage (3–5 films), the company pays full ticket price each time—say $8–12. A member seeing 4 films costs $32–48, far above the $10 fee. The subsidy covers only the gap until cash runs out; it does not fix the unit loss. Heavy users amplify this: the more successful the membership, the larger the loss.

The proposed new revenue streams—data and studio/theater deals—are unproven and speculative. There is no evidence they will materialize at scale, and no plan for when they don't. Theater chains refusing to cooperate is a stated risk that could cut off supply entirely, making the product unusable.

The alternative of staying at a higher price with fewer members is not attractive, but the proposal does not show a path to profitability—only to faster cash burn. Growth without a viable margin is not a strategy; it's a subsidy race with no finish line.

The decision is not about whether subscriptions can work, but whether this model, as proposed, is sound. It is not: the fee cannot cover the cost structure, the revenue fixes are unproven, and the downside risks are existential.

DECISION: NO-GO
