FIRST CALL: NO-GO — the subscription fee cannot cover full ticket costs, and the proposed revenue streams are speculative.

The core economics fail: at $10/month for one film a day, a member seeing just two films costs the company ~$20–30 in ticket payments while paying only $10. Heavy users accelerate losses. The stated risks confirm this: the fee does not cover cost, and new revenue streams are unproven. The plan relies on subsidized growth to reach scale, but there is no demonstrated path to profitability — only hopes about data and studio deals, which incumbents control and have no reason to grant.

Doing nothing is superior: the existing small base at a higher price at least limits losses per member. Cheaper alternatives — partnerships negotiated before price cuts, or a capped pilot — are not proposed. The one-way door of mass adoption at a loss-making price with no exit strategy makes this a bad bet, even with cash to burn.

Blockers: the business model as stated loses money on every active member; revenue diversification is unverified; no cap, pilot, or kill criterion is offered.

Risks: theater refusal, heavy-user concentration, and unproven data monetization are acknowledged but not mitigated.

The proposal should not proceed as written.

DECISION: NO-GO
