First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores the core stated risk—brand perception—which the facts already confirm as fatal.

The claim is that a dedicated factory and worldwide launch can overcome the mass-market badge. But the proposal itself states buyers pay for the premium badge, the brand has no luxury image, and luxury buyers may reject a mass-market name. These are not hypotheticals; they are the market's known reality. The plan offers no strategy to change perception, only to build hardware.

Doing nothing costs less and risks nothing. A cheaper alternative—launching a limited, high-spec model under a sub-brand or acquiring an existing premium name—reaches the same upside without the sunk cost of a dedicated factory. The proposed path is a one-way door: massive capital, low volume, and no evidence the target buyer will switch.

Pre-mortem: the sedan sits unsold, discounting destroys the premium price, and the factory becomes a stranded asset. The delay cost is minimal; the failure cost is enormous.

The evidence is all internal and self-defeating. The proposal's own risk list removes its route to success, and it answers none of them.

Blockers: brand perception contradicts the core claim; dedicated factory commits irreversible capital against stated buyer behavior.
Risks: execution, volume, competition—all secondary to the brand.

Kill criteria cannot save this; no pilot or metric fixes a badge buyers won't accept.

DECISION: NO-GO
