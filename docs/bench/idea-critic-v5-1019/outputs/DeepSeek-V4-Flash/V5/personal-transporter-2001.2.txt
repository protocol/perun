First call: NO-GO — the stated price and category dynamics do not support a wide consumer launch as proposed.

Steelman: A novel, fun, efficient personal transporter could carve a new niche if early adopters accept the price and cities adapt.

Attack: The price is 10–20x a bicycle or scooter, and above most used cars. The target use—replacing short trips and walking—competes with free or near-free options. The learning curve and legal uncertainty add friction, not delight. No evidence of consumer demand at $5,000, no pilot, no revenue model beyond hardware. The risks named are not speculative; they are structural. A wide launch now would burn capital on a product with unproven category acceptance.

Better ways: Sell to a niche first (campus, delivery, security) at a premium, gather usage data, then expand. Or license the technology to an established mobility player. Do nothing until legal and demand signals are clearer.

Kill criteria: None stated. No metric for adoption, no cap on marketing spend, no exit if sidewalk bans spread.

Strongest attack survived: None—the proposal offers no counter to the price-demand gap or the regulatory risk.

Dissent ledger: Price, legality, learning curve, and lack of cargo/passenger capacity all weigh against wide consumer launch.

Remaining risk: Even a niche launch could fail if the category never gains traction.

DECISION: NO-GO
