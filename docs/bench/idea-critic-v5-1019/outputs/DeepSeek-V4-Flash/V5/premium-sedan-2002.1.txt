First call: NO-GO. The stated facts—premium buyers pay for the badge, no luxury image, low-volume cost—already undermine the core claim that this model will succeed as proposed.

Steelman: A superior product at parity price can overcome brand weakness and win share.

Attack: The proposal's own evidence contradicts its route to success. It names the badge as the purchase driver, admits the brand lacks luxury cachet, and concedes low-volume cost pressure. A large sedan under a mass-market name at premium prices offers no advantage over incumbents and a clear disadvantage on brand equity. The risks are stated as current facts, not hypotheticals. The plan does not answer how it overcomes them—no pilot, no brand sub-label, no phased entry. Building a dedicated factory for this is a huge, one-way commitment with no kill criterion.

Better ways: Enter premium via a separate brand or acquisition; start with a smaller, higher-volume model; or lease capacity to test demand before building a plant.

Kill criteria: None proposed. If approved, it needs a cap on investment, a sales threshold (e.g., 50k units/year in year two), and a stop/pivot date.

The decision is clear: approve as proposed now is a high-risk, irreversible bet that the proposal's own facts contradict.

DECISION: NO-GO
