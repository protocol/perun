The proposal is a 2011 bet on an unproven category, with real risks: cannibalization, unclear positioning, and lukewarm early reviews. But the upside is a new premium tier that could define a segment, and the downside is contained—it’s one device alongside an existing line, not a full pivot. The stated risks are speculative, not fatal: stylus demand can be tested, and cannibalization is a manageable trade-off if the device captures new buyers or higher margins. The "too big/too small" critique is a review risk, not a demand killer; early skepticism often precedes category creation. The company already has the supply chain and brand to execute, and the cost of entry is moderate and reversible if the product flops. Do-nothing means ceding the category to a competitor. The strongest attack—that it’s a niche with no proven pull—fails because the same was true of every new form factor before it, and the premium price protects margins even at modest volume. The plan is coherent, bounded, and worth the bet.
