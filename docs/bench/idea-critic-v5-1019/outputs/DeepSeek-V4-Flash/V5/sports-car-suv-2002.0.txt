First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a small, enthusiast-focused brand to enter a capital-intensive, crowded segment where its core identity works against it.

Steelman: A high-margin luxury SUV could double the brand’s customer base, fund future sports cars, and leverage the parent group’s platform to cut development cost.

Better ways: Do nothing and stay profitable on the loyal core; or license the brand to the group for a royalty with no capital risk; or launch a limited, low-volume “performance SUV” as a halo test before committing.

Fit: The stated brand is enthusiast purity. A heavy, high-priced SUV contradicts that. The upside is volume, not the stated north star.

Pre-mortem: The SUV sells to luxury buyers who don’t care about the sports heritage, while loyalists feel betrayed. The brand’s small dealer network and service costs strain. The shared platform makes it a rebadged group car, eroding exclusivity. The first-year cost is high with no proof of demand from the brand’s actual followers.

Evidence: The proposal gives no market size, no projected margin, no cost number, no payback. The only facts are: small range, loyal following, strong incumbents, high first-year cost. On those facts, the numbers don’t support the risk.

Blockers: The stated evidence shows target users (loyal enthusiasts) may reject the product, and the segment is already dominated by stronger players. No evidence of a route to the upside.

Risks: Brand damage, capital loss, execution difficulty. These are not kill criteria; they are the reasons not to start.

Decision: Do not approve. The plan fails on its own stated facts: no demand evidence, high cost, strong incumbents, brand conflict.

DECISION: NO-GO
