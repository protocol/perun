First call: GO — a low-cost, licence-free vehicle addresses a real gap in the 1985 market, and the appliance maker’s assembly capacity is a strong asset.

Steelman: A sub-£400, pedal-assisted tricycle with no licence requirement offers affordable, practical urban mobility for a mass market, leveraging a major manufacturer’s scale.

Attacks tried:  
- **Market demand**: Does anyone want a 15 mph, weather-exposed vehicle? Likely yes for short trips, but niche.  
- **Safety**: Low profile among traffic is a genuine risk, but not fatal to launch.  
- **Seasonality**: British winters limit use, but mail order allows year-round sales to warmer regions.

Better ways: Start with a regional pilot rather than a national launch; sell via dealerships for test rides; emphasize safety accessories as standard. These reduce risk but don’t block the core idea.

Kill criteria: If pre-orders don’t reach 1,000 units within 60 days of launch, pivot to fleet or commercial buyers. Review after one quarter.

Strongest attack survived: “The product is too slow and unsafe for real roads, so nobody will buy it.” This fails because the target user is not the commuter but the leisure/short-trip buyer, and the price point undercuts cars and motorcycles.

Remaining risk: Safety-group backlash could dampen sales, but no evidence it will stop them.

DECISION: GO
