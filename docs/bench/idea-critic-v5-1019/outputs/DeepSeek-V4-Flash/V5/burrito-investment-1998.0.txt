First call: GO. The proposal is a low-cost option on a differentiated concept, and the stated risks are mitigable.

Steelman: A small, premium burrito chain, run independently, offers the giant a cheap real option to learn and potentially scale a higher-margin format without operational distraction.

Attack: The chain may not scale, skilled labor and slower service hurt unit economics, and the price point may not attract the giant’s core customers. Management distraction is a real cost.

But none of these are blockers. The investment is minority and the chain runs independently—so the giant’s brand, menu, and operations are untouched. The risk of “not scaling” is exactly what the option structure prices: invest a little now, learn, and invest more only if the concept proves out. Skilled labor and slower service are operational challenges the independent team owns, not the giant. The higher price is a feature, not a flaw—it targets a different customer than fast food, and the giant is not cannibalizing its own core. Management attention is limited by the minority stake and independent management.

Alternative: do nothing. That forgoes a cheap learning opportunity with no downside beyond the initial stake. A cheaper alternative does not exist at this stage.

Kill criteria: if the chain cannot maintain quality at scale, or if the option to invest more is not exercised, the initial stake is a bounded loss. The plan already caps commitment and preserves reversibility.

The proposal is a sound, two-way-door bet with a capped downside and real upside. No stated fact contradicts its core claim.

DECISION: GO
