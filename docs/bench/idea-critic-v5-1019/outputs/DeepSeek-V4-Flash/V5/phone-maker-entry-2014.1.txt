The proposal asks the retailer to enter a mature, app-driven phone market dominated by two entrenched platforms. Its differentiators—head-tracking 3D display, camera-based product identification, and a bundled membership—do not address what buyers actually prioritize: app ecosystem, screen quality, and carrier choice. The single-carrier launch and premium price further constrain adoption. The stated risks are not speculative; they reflect known market dynamics. The plan offers no evidence that these features would overcome the network effects of existing platforms, and the retailer lacks phone-specific expertise in hardware, supply chain, and carrier relations. A one-year membership is a weak retention hook against the cost of switching ecosystems. The investment required is large and irreversible, with no clear path to scale beyond a niche. The proposal fails to demonstrate a compelling reason for mainstream buyers to switch, and the risks raised are precisely the ones that would sink it. Approving as proposed now would commit substantial resources to a product with a low probability of meaningful market share.

DECISION: NO-GO
