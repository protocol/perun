First call: GO. The proposal is cheap, reversible, and testable—the risks are real but none are fatal on the given facts.

The video is a two-way door: low budget, immediate orders, quick signal. If traffic is low, they lose little. If it works, they learn demand without big ad spend. The dominant brands cutting prices or suing is a risk, not a certainty—and a price war would validate their model. Supplier dependence is real but manageable at launch scale; they can diversify later. Subscription aversion is unproven, not disproven—the video answers it.

Do nothing loses the chance to test a differentiated offer at near-zero cost. Cheaper alternatives (e.g., just a landing page) reach the same upside but slower; the video is the proposed vehicle and is fine. The plan’s numbers work: low fixed cost, immediate revenue signal, no large irreversible commitment.

Blockers: none. The stated facts show no contradiction with the core claim—that a humorous low-cost video can drive initial orders. Risks (price war, lawsuit, supplier, low traffic, subscription aversion) are all possibilities, not demonstrated failures; they become kill criteria, not stops.

Kill criteria: if after the first month orders are near zero and the video got negligible views, stop or pivot. Leading metric: orders per video view, reviewed at 30 days.

Strongest attack survived: “A single video won’t get traction.” But the cost of trying is trivial, the downside is bounded, and the upside is a real market entry. That survives.

DECISION: GO
