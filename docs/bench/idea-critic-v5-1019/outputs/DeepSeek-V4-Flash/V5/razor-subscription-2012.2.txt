**First call:** GO — the proposal is cheap, reversible, and tests demand directly.

**Steelman:** A humorous, low-budget video can generate enough initial attention to validate whether a price-led subscription model attracts customers despite entrenched competitors.

**Attack:** The video is a one-shot bet. If it flops, you learn little about whether the core model (price + subscription) works — only that this particular video failed. The stated risks (price cuts, lawsuits, supplier dependency) are real but mostly tail risks at launch scale. The bigger flaw: no mechanism to iterate or measure beyond raw orders.

**Better ways:** (1) Run two or three small video variants cheaply, see which pulls, then scale. (2) Start with a landing page and waitlist to gauge interest before any inventory commitment. (3) Partner with a second supplier upfront to reduce dependency.

**Kill criteria:** Set a numeric threshold — e.g., at least 1,000 orders within 30 days of launch. If not met, stop or pivot messaging. Watch cost per order and repeat-purchase rate as leading metrics.

**Strongest attack survived:** “A single video may bring little traffic” — true, but the cost of testing is trivial and the downside is a small sunk spend, not a strategic failure. The model itself is unproven but cheap to test.

**Remaining risk:** Supplier dependency and brand retaliation are real but not blockers at this stage; they become more relevant only if traction proves the model.

**Dissent ledger:** Video is a weak validation instrument; consider a multi-variant test. Supplier concentration is a known risk to mitigate later.

DECISION: GO
