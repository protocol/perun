Steelman: A premium, silent, minimal desktop offers a unique aesthetic and quiet experience that a faster, expandable tower cannot match, justifying a higher price to a design-conscious segment.

Strongest attack: The price sits above the popular all-in-one and a faster tower, which could kill demand entirely. However, the proposal targets buyers who prioritize silence and aesthetics over raw specs and upgradeability — a distinct segment willing to pay a premium for the acrylic design. The manufacturing risk is a known challenge, not a proven failure. The lack of slots is a feature for the target user, not a bug. The company’s eleven profitable quarters show it can execute and absorb the risk of a niche launch.

The plan is a two-way door: a small, limited production run can be stopped if it fails. No stated fact proves the target users won’t buy it; the risks are all speculative. Therefore, the proposal survives the attack.
