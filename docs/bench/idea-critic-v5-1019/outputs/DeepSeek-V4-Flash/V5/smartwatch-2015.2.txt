First call: GO, because the maker's existing phone ecosystem and brand strength give it a real shot where others saw modest sales.

The steelman: leverage a massive installed phone base, strong brand loyalty, and deep integration to make a watch that is genuinely more useful than generic competitors, justifying multiple price tiers.

Against do nothing: the maker cedes the wearable category entirely, risking long-term ecosystem erosion. Against the cheapest alternative (a fitness band): a band captures health tracking but misses the app store and premium positioning, leaving the higher-margin opportunity untapped. The watch is a two-way door; it can be iterated or discontinued.

Fit: it directly extends the core phone business, deepening lock-in and data collection, which aligns with likely strategic goals.

Pre-mortem: the watch is a me-too product with poor battery life, weak third-party developer interest, and consumers fail to see why they need it, leading to inventory write-downs and a tarnished "innovation" halo.

Evidence: the proposal itself concedes modest prior sales and a one-day battery. These are risks, not proven blockers. The maker's unique assets—phone integration and brand—are not yet disproven by the market.

Blockers: NONE. Risks: weak category demand, one-day battery, phone dependency, unclear consumer need. These become kill criteria: set a sales target and a date; if the watch misses it, stop or pivot.

The call is a go-if: launch as a capped, time-boxed first step with a pre-committed sales threshold and a defined pivot. This preserves upside while limiting downside.

DECISION: GO
