First call: GO. The maker has the phone ecosystem, brand, and distribution to differentiate where others have only modestly succeeded.

Steelman: A tightly integrated watch leveraging the maker’s existing phone, brand trust, and retail muscle can overcome the category’s tepid start and create a new accessory revenue stream.

Options: Do nothing (miss a growing category, cede to competitors). Cheaper alternative (license another watch, no control, weak integration). This proposal leverages core assets and is a two-way door if it fails.

Fit: Aligns with a hardware ecosystem strategy and premium brand; expands the “halo” around the phone.

Pre-mortem: The watch is seen as a gimmick, battery life frustrates, and developers don’t build apps, leaving a dull device. Delay costs a year of category momentum and lets rivals cement the wrist.

Evidence: All cited facts are internal to the proposal. Smartwatch sales “modest” is a given, not a blocker. The one-day battery is a stated tradeoff, not a hidden failure. The phone dependency is the integration point, not a flaw.

Attack survived: “Buyers don’t need a second screen.” This is a risk, not a proven fact. The proposal’s own evidence doesn’t show this is already true; it’s an unproven demand question, which is a risk to manage, not a blocker.

Kill criteria: If first-year sales and app adoption miss internal targets, scale back. Cap initial production to limit downside.

Risks: Category skepticism, battery life, app ecosystem. These are kill criteria, not reasons to stop now.

DECISION: GO
