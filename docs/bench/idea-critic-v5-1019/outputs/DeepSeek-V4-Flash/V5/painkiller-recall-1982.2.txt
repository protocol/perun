The proposal asks for a nationwide withdrawal, production halt, and advertising stop in response to a localized poisoning incident. The core claim is that a full recall protects consumers and preserves long-term brand trust despite immediate costs.

The strongest alternative is a targeted regional recall, which costs less and avoids signaling nationwide danger. However, that leaves consumers elsewhere uncertain and risks more victims if contamination spreads or copycats emerge. A nationwide action is the only option that fully removes the risk, and the cost of even one additional death—or the perception of corporate negligence—far outweighs the recall expense.

The pre-mortem: if the company does nothing or acts regionally, more poisoned bottles surface, public panic grows, and the brand is blamed for inaction. A full recall, while expensive, positions the company as prioritizing safety, which is the strongest foundation for recovery. Competitors may gain temporary shelf space, but the brand can relaunch with tamper-resistant packaging, converting a crisis into a trust-building narrative.

The stated risks—cost, market share loss, competitive gains, and signaling danger—are real but manageable. They are risks, not blockers. The evidence supports that the threat is real and potentially widespread, and the company’s own plants being unaffected does not reduce consumer exposure. The upside of protecting lives and brand integrity outweighs the financial downside.

DECISION: GO
