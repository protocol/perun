First call: GO — the core demand for convenience is real, but the economics as proposed are unsound.

The steelman: convenience at zero marginal cost to the customer creates habit-forming usage and scale that eventually justifies the delivery cost. Attack: free delivery with no minimum on low-margin items (books, coffee) guarantees negative unit economics per order. The proposal’s own numbers show this—no volume of small orders fixes a structural loss. Direct employment of staff and vehicles in each city multiplies fixed costs faster than revenue. The stated risks are not hypothetical; they are arithmetic certainties on the facts given.

Better ways: charge a small delivery fee or set a minimum order; start with one city, high-margin items (games, videos) and a pilot to test real willingness to pay; use third-party couriers to avoid fixed staff costs. Do nothing is superior to scaling a proven loss.

Kill criterion is absent: no metric or cap that would stop expansion when losses exceed projections. The plan commits to rapid multi-city scaling before validating unit economics in one market.

The strongest attack survived: that convenience alone could overcome negative margins—it fails because free delivery on every small order is a guaranteed loss, not a risk. The proposed path fails as stated.

DECISION: NO-GO
