First call: GO — the brand can stretch if the product is excellent and the economics are managed.

Steelman: A high-margin luxury SUV under a storied performance brand can attract new, affluent buyers, fund future sports cars, and expand the brand without diluting its core, provided the SUV is genuinely capable and priced as a halo product.

Attack: The key risk is not the SUV itself but the first-year cost and the strength of incumbents. Loyalists may grumble, but history shows enthusiasts tolerate diversification when the core identity stays sharp. The heavy-vehicle concern is manageable with modern engineering. The real threat is a mediocre product that fails against established rivals, wasting capital and tarnishing the brand.

Better ways: Do nothing (lose growth), or badge-engineer a cheaper, lower-risk SUV (fails to justify the price). The proposed co-development shares cost and platform risk, which is the strongest option.

Kill criteria: If the first-year cost cannot be capped or the platform cannot deliver a credible off-road and performance SUV, stop. Otherwise, proceed.

Assumptions: The shared platform can deliver the required performance; the group partner is reliable; the price point is achievable.

Strongest attack survived: Brand betrayal by loyalists. This fails because the core sports cars remain unchanged, and the SUV expands the base without replacing the halo.

Dissent: None material.

Remaining risk: Execution quality against incumbents; mitigated by co-development and a strong brand.

DECISION: GO
