DECISION: NO-GO

The proposal asks for $1.75B to enter a market where the core premise—that viewers will pay for short-form, phone-only content—is unproven and directly contradicted by the stated facts. Free short video on social platforms is already popular, meaning the product must overcome a zero-price incumbent with massive scale. The plan’s own risk list admits viewers may not pay, yet offers no evidence of willingness to pay or a path to test it cheaply.

The launch constraints compound the problem: no TV availability at launch severely limits the addressable market and value proposition, and the high production budgets create a massive recoupment hurdle before any profit. With a spring 2020 launch, there is no time to iterate on a pilot, validate demand, or adjust pricing. The capital required is disproportionate to the evidence, and the downside—losing nearly $2B—is catastrophic with no stated kill criteria or phased commitment.

A cheaper, staged alternative exists: launch a small slate in one country, test subscription and ad-tier uptake, and scale only if metrics prove demand. The proposal as written skips that validation and bets everything on an unverified assumption. The stated risks are not mitigations; they are fundamental unknowns that the plan does not resolve.

The executives’ experience is real, but the business case does not support the scale or timing. Approving this now would commit irreversible capital to a hypothesis the proposal itself admits is doubtful.
