First call: GO — the core claim is that free, real-content translation can bootstrap a language product, and the stated risks are execution risks, not demonstrated failures.

Steelman: A free, genuinely useful language app with integrated real translation work can attract a large user base, and that base itself becomes a monetizable asset even if direct learner-to-publisher sales are unproven.

Options:
- Do nothing: zero cost, zero upside, opportunity lost.
- Paid courses: competes head-on with polished incumbents, high cost, slow.
- Free + translation revenue: low cost to start, unique differentiator, two-sided upside.

Fit: Aligns with “free for anyone” mission; revenue later is a reasonable sequence for a 2009 web product.

Pre-mortem: Translation quality is poor, users churn, publishers refuse, and no revenue ever comes. But the plan can pivot to other monetization (ads, premium, data) with the same user base.

Evidence: All risks are speculative (“may be poor,” “may not become,” “more polished,” “unproven”) — none are stated as already true. The proposal’s own facts support a low-cost, reversible first step.

Blockers: NONE — no stated fact contradicts the core claim or removes the route to upside.

Risks: Translation quality, monetization, competition — all are kill criteria, not blockers. Add a cap: build a minimal version, measure learner engagement and translation output within 6 months, and set a numeric threshold for continuing.

Strongest attack survived: “Free users will never pay.” This fails because the plan doesn’t depend on them paying; it depends on their translated content or their attention having value, which is plausible and testable.

DECISION: GO
