First call: NO-GO. The proposal’s own facts—a late, expensive prior gen, weak launch software, and a rival with a strong online head start—undermine its core claim that price and hardware alone will reverse lost ground.

Steelman: A cheaper, developer-friendly console undercuts the rival on cost and ease, wins back price-sensitive buyers, and used-game/offline support addresses consumer spending concerns.

Attack: The stated risks are not minor. A lower price with thin margins plus scarce first-party launch titles means the console launches without a must-have reason to buy. The rival’s head start in online services is a real moat; used games and offline play are defensive, not offensive. Consumers spending less on dedicated consoles is a structural headwind the proposal does not answer.

Alternatives: Do nothing (preserve cash, wait), or launch at a modest price with a strong exclusive title and a clearer online value proposition. The cheaper alternative—delay and bundle a compelling game—reaches the same upside with less margin damage.

The proposal’s own numbers (if any) are absent; the risks are stated as already true (rival’s lead, weak software), not as future possibilities. That removes the route to the claimed upside.

Blockers: Weak launch software and a rival’s entrenched online lead are stated facts that contradict the core claim that price alone will win. Risks: margin erosion, consumer spending decline.

Kill criteria: none proposed; no pilot, cap, or pivot metric.

DECISION: NO-GO
