First call: GO — the economics are compelling, but the execution risks are severe.

Steelman: A low-cost, time-limited disc format with proprietary players creates recurring revenue and differentiates the retailer from competitors, while partnerships mitigate adoption hurdles.

Better ways: Do nothing (status quo), or launch a standard-format rental service. The proposal’s upside—recurring fees, low disc cost—outweighs do-nothing. A standard rental lacks the recurring revenue and exclusivity, so the proposal beats it on stated facts.

Fit: Aligns with retailer’s goal to capture new market; assumes north star is revenue growth.

Pre-mortem: Studios and electronics makers refuse to support the format; customers reject the 48-hour fee model and pricier players; phone-line requirement is a barrier. This is a two-way door—can be withdrawn if partners or customers don’t adopt. Delay costs: first-mover advantage lost to competitors.

Evidence: All claims are assumptions—no market data, partner commitments, or cost figures provided. Recompute: “few dollars” disc vs. “more than standard” player; 48-hour window plus fee—no demand validation.

Blockers: NONE—no stated fact shows the plan fails; risks are unproven, not certain.
Risks: Partner refusal, customer price sensitivity, proprietary-player friction, phone-line dependency.

Kill criteria: If, within 12 months, fewer than two major studios commit to the format and fewer than 100,000 players are sold, pivot to standard-format rental. Leading metric: monthly player sales and studio agreements.

Strongest attack survived: “Customers will hate paying again”—but no evidence shows they won’t; the low disc price and convenience may offset. Not a blocker.

DECISION: GO
