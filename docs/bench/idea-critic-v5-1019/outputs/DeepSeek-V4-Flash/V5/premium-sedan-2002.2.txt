First call: NO-GO. The core claim—that a mass-market brand can command premium prices on a luxury sedan—is unsupported by the proposal's own facts.

Steelman: The carmaker's scale and engineering excellence could deliver a technically superior sedan that undercuts premium rivals on price while offering comparable quality, eventually building a luxury reputation.

Attack: The proposal states "buyers pay for the badge" and "the brand has no luxury image" as known risks. These are not hypotheticals; they are the market's established behavior. The plan offers no mechanism to overcome badge aversion. A dedicated factory and worldwide launch commit massive capital to a product whose stated target users—luxury buyers—are explicitly identified as unlikely to accept the brand. The low-volume, high-cost build guarantees poor margins even if sales meet expectations. No pilot, no brand-building phase, no path to change perception is proposed. The alternative—acquiring or partnering with an existing premium brand—is cheaper, faster, and directly addresses the badge problem. Do-nothing preserves capital and brand equity.

The proposal's own evidence contradicts its core claim: it names the exact reason buyers reject the product and offers no counter. This is a blocker, not a risk.

Kill criteria absent: no sales threshold, no brand-perception metric, no exit date. Commitment is large and one-way.

DECISION: NO-GO
