First call: NO-GO. The numbers do not work on the stated facts.

Steelman: Premium, professionally-made short-form content on mobile can attract a paying audience that free, user-generated clips cannot, justifying a large upfront investment.

Blocker: The proposal’s own economics fail. Raising $1.75B to produce high-budget shows, with a launch in two countries in 2020, requires massive subscriber growth to recoup. Free short video is already popular, directly competing for the same attention and wallet share. The stated risk that viewers may not pay for phone-only short shows is not speculative—it is a known market condition. The plan offers no evidence of willingness to pay, no path to differentiate sufficiently, and no television availability, limiting its addressable audience. The production budgets are so high that breakeven is distant, and the plan does not show how it reaches it.

Better ways: A cheaper, staged pilot in one country with modest budgets, or licensing existing content, would test demand without betting $1.75B. Do nothing is also superior to a likely large loss.

Kill criteria: No metric or date is given to stop the bleeding if subscribers underperform. The risks are not answered, only listed.

The decision is clear: the plan fails as proposed because its own evidence (free competition, no TV, high budgets) already shows the core claim—that people will pay—is unproven and the economics do not close.

DECISION: NO-GO
