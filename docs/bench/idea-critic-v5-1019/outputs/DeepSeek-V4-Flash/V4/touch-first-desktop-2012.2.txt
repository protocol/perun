First call: GO. The threat from tablets is real, and the proposal forces a decisive response to protect the core franchise.

Steelman: The desktop OS maker must blunt tablet encroachment by unifying the experience across form factors, making its ecosystem the default on both PCs and tablets, and creating a new revenue channel via an app store.

Attack: The proposal ignores the core user base that generates the current revenue and support economics. It bets the franchise on converting the installed base of keyboard-and-mouse users into a touch-first paradigm they did not ask for, with no fallback. The stated risks are not speculative; they are near-certain outcomes: enterprise confusion, high retraining costs, and developer reluctance are guaranteed frictions, not possibilities. The "no easy way back" removes the safety valve that would let users adapt at their own pace. The tablet threat is real, but the response is a forced migration of the most profitable, least mobile user segment. A cheaper, less destructive alternative exists: keep the familiar desktop for mouse-and-keyboard users, offer the touch-first start screen as an option on touch hardware, and build the app store as a parallel channel. This reaches the same upside—tablet relevance and a new store—without alienating the enterprise base that pays the bills. The proposal's own facts show it fails as proposed.

Blockers: The plan's own stated risks—enterprise confusion, high support costs, no rollback—are sufficient to break the core revenue base. A less costly alternative reaches the same strategic upside.

Risks: Do nothing cedes tablets; a gentler path may be slower.

DECISION: NO-GO
