**First call: NO-GO** — a 15 mph, no-weather-protection tricycle sold by mail order in British winter is a niche product, not a mass-market one.

**Steelman:** A cheap, licence-free, pedal-assisted vehicle could appeal to urban commuters and older drivers losing their car licences, and a big appliance maker’s assembly line keeps costs low.

**Alternatives:**
- Do nothing: no capital risk, no reputational damage.
- Sell through existing bicycle/scooter shops: lower volume but credible channel.
- Regional launch in a milder area: cheaper test.

**Fit:** No stated strategy; assumed novelty-driven. Does not obviously move a north star of reliable, high-volume transport.

**Pre-mortem:** Launch generates press buzz, orders arrive, then returns spike in January rain. Safety groups call it dangerous in traffic. Sales die by March. One-way door: you cannot un-sell a bad reputation.

**Evidence:** All figures are assumed — no market research, no cost model, no price elasticity. The £400 price is below car cost but above a decent bicycle, with no clear buyer.

**Blocker:** The proposal’s own facts contradict its core claim. Top speed 15 mph, short range, no weather protection, low in traffic, and British winters — the stated conditions already show the target user (mass-market commuter) will not want it in the primary season. The plan offers no answer to this.

**Risks:** Safety objections, returns, channel mismatch, seasonal demand.

**Kill criteria if pursued:** If a pilot in a mild region (e.g., Cornwall) sells fewer than 500 units in the first 90 days with a return rate above 10%, stop and pivot to a niche enthusiast channel.

**Dissent ledger:** Cheaper, credible alternatives exist; the launch event magnifies failure.

**Remaining risk:** If the entrepreneur has private data showing a specific underserved segment (e.g., elderly cyclists), this could be viable — but that evidence is not presented.

DECISION: NO-GO
