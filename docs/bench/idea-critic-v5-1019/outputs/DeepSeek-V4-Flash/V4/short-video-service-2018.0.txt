First call: NO-GO. The stated economics and market realities do not support a $1.75B raise for a phone-only, short-form paid service in 2020.

Steelman: Premium, professionally made short-form content could capture a niche willing to pay for quality above free social video, with a strong brand and top-tier talent.

Options: Do nothing—free, low-cost; adapt—start smaller with a pilot in one market; proceed as proposed—$1.75B, two countries, premium budgets.

Fit: The proposal does not move a stated north star; it's a new, unproven category.

Pre-mortem: The service launches, but viewers used to free short video on social platforms see no reason to pay. The phone-only limitation and lack of TV access at launch cap the addressable audience. High production budgets mean enormous content costs before any meaningful subscriber revenue, and the ad tier cannibalizes the paid tier. The company burns through capital, fails to hit subscriber targets, and is forced to shut down or sell at a loss.

Evidence: The proposal itself cites the key risks: free short video is already popular; viewers may not pay for phone-only short shows; no TV access at launch; high budgets require massive recoupment. These are not hypotheticals—they are the stated facts of the market and the plan. The numbers do not work: $1.75B against a subscription base that must be built from zero in a category where the dominant behavior is free.

Blockers: The proposal's own stated facts—popular free alternatives and unproven willingness to pay—already show the core value proposition is contradicted. The stated high budgets with no path to breakeven on the given facts make the plan fail as proposed.

Risks: Execution, market adoption, competition.

Kill criteria: Subscriber acquisition cost and churn metrics in the first pilot; if not met, stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
