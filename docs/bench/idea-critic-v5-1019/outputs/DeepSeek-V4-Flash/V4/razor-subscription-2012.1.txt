**Alternatives:** Do nothing (cost: zero, risk: none, reversibility: n/a). Paid search/test ads (cost: higher, risk: modest, reversibility: high). The video is the cheapest, most reversible option, but traffic is speculative.

**Fit:** No stated north star; assumed goal is low-cost customer acquisition. Video fits if it converts, but that is unproven.

**Pre-mortem:** The video goes viral for laughs, not shaving; orders are trivial; supplier margins squeeze the low price; incumbents match price and crush the niche. One-way door: brand damage and wasted cash, though small.

**Evidence:** All claims are `assumed` — no traffic numbers, no supplier contract terms, no conversion data. The proposal's own facts show: two dominant, ad-heavy competitors; one supplier dependency; no distribution channel besides a single video. Nothing in the stated facts proves demand for subscription razors exists.

**Blockers:** None — no internal contradiction, no arithmetic failure, no cheaper path to the same upside. **Risks:** unproven demand, incumbent response, supplier dependency, low traffic, subscription aversion. These are kill criteria, not blockers.

**Decision:** Go-if — cap the launch at a fixed budget, run the video for two weeks, and set a numeric order threshold (e.g., 500 subscribers) as the kill criterion. If not met, stop. This is a two-way door; the downside is small and reversible.

The proposal as written lacks a cap, a metric, and a stop date. That does not block it — I add them. The idea is cheap, reversible, and worth testing.
