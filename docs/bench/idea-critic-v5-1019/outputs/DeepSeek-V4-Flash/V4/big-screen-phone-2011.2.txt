DECISION: NO-GO

The proposal fails as stated because it lacks any evidence of demand. A larger phone with a stylus is an unproven category, and the internal risks—cannibalizing existing sales, unclear positioning, and lukewarm reviewer reception—are not addressed by any market test, pricing cap, or pilot. The plan commits to a full launch with no kill criteria or leading metric, meaning a failure would be discovered only after significant sunk cost.

The strongest version of the claim—that a premium large-phone-plus-stylus captures a new segment without hurting the core line—is plausible but unsupported by any data in the proposal. No alternative (e.g., a limited regional launch, a survey, or a developer prototype) is considered. The pre-mortem writes itself: reviewers call it awkward, buyers see it as neither phone nor tablet, and sales cannibalize the four-inch line. The one-way door of a full, simultaneous global announcement and sell-in is expensive to reverse.

There is no stated north star or strategic fit, and the cost of delay is low—the category is unproven, so waiting a quarter to test demand costs little. The proposal offers no way to measure whether the stylus is wanted, whether the size is acceptable, or whether cannibalization is real before committing fully. A sound operator would not approve a full launch on this evidence.

The decision is to not approve as proposed.
