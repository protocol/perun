DECISION: NO-GO

The proposal's core appeal—nostalgia for a 1957 model—is a weak foundation for a premium-priced car in 2007. The target buyers of a new small car are unlikely to have strong emotional ties to a car from five decades prior. The platform-mate already exists at a lower price; asking customers to pay more for styling alone, with no functional or efficiency advantage, is a hard sell, especially given the company's recent poor sales performance, which suggests weak brand pull. Thin margins on small cars amplify the financial risk for a company already in a weak position. The plan lacks a concrete, differentiated value proposition beyond aesthetics, and the stated risks are not mitigated. A backward-looking design is a bet on a fickle trend, not a sound strategy for financial recovery. The safer, more rational move is to reject this as proposed.
