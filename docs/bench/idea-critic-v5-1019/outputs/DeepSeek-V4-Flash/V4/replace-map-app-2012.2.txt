The proposal bets on control and differentiation, but the stated risks are not hypothetical—they are near-certainties. The new maps have "a lot less data" and "may be wrong in places," which means the core value proposition of a mapping app—accuracy and reliability—is compromised at launch. Users experience maps daily; a visibly worse default will generate immediate, widespread backlash, not just annoyance. Removing a working rival app forces users into a downgrade, a classic self-inflicted wound. The upside (control, integration) is real but long-term and speculative; the downside is immediate, visible, and affects every phone sold. There is no pilot, no fallback, no evidence that the data gaps can be closed before fall, and no metric to reverse course if adoption tanks. The cost of waiting a year to improve data is far lower than the cost of a botched launch that erodes trust in the device itself. Control is not worth a worse user experience.

DECISION: NO-GO
