First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks customers to pay repeatedly for content on hardware that is more expensive and incompatible, while relying on industry-wide adoption that is far from assured. The stated risks are not hypothetical; they are the core of the business model.

Steelman: A new format that gives customers low upfront cost and a 48-hour rental window could create a new revenue stream, with partners sharing the burden of adoption.

Attack: The economics are backwards. The customer pays more for the player, gets less flexibility (no standard playback), and then pays again to keep watching. This inverts the value proposition of the disc market, which is ownership and convenience. The "few dollars" purchase is a trap, not a deal. The phone-line requirement adds friction and cost, and the industry adoption hurdle is existential: without studios and other hardware makers, the format is a brick. The plan offers no path to critical mass, no pilot, and no kill criterion for partner failure.

Better way: A standard-format disc with a software-based rental lock, or a traditional rental model, reaches the same market at lower cost and risk.

Decision: The proposal fails on its own stated facts: higher hardware cost, incompatibility, and a payment model customers already dislike. The upside is speculative; the downside is structural.

DECISION: NO-GO
