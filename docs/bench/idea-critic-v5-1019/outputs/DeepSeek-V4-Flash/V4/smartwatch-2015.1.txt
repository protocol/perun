First call: GO. The company has a captive phone base, brand strength, and distribution that earlier watch makers lacked. The stated risks are real but addressable, and none are fatal on the facts given.

Steelman: A premium phone maker can leverage its loyal installed base, design reputation, and ecosystem to create a watch that is genuinely useful as a companion device, not a standalone gadget. Multiple price points and materials broaden appeal, while health tracking taps a growing trend.

Options: Do nothing leaves the company out of a nascent category and cedes it to competitors. The watch is a two-way door—inventory and marketing spend are recoverable; the brand is not damaged by a modest product miss. Cheaper alternatives, like licensing another watch, fail to capture the ecosystem value.

Pre-mortem: The watch fails because it is seen as a toy, battery life annoys, and developers ignore the app store. But these are risks, not certainties. The phone dependency is actually the moat—it deepens lock-in. Poor sales of others prove nothing about this company's execution.

Evidence: The proposal states smartwatches "have sold modestly," which is a fact, not a forecast of failure. The risks are listed as possibilities, not already-true negatives. No blocker exists: the numbers work, the route to value is intact, and the downside is capped by a modest entry.

Blockers: NONE. Risks: modest category demand, one-day battery, phone dependency, unclear buyer need. These become kill criteria: if first-year sales fall below a set threshold, stop or pivot.

DECISION: GO
