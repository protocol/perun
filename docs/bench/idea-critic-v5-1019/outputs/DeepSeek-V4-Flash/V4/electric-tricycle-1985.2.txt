First call: GO. The product is novel, the price is accessible, and the appliance maker’s involvement de-risks production.

Steelman: A low-cost, licence-free personal vehicle fills a real gap for short urban trips, leveraging a trusted manufacturer’s scale and a splashy launch to drive mail-order volume.

Attack: The stated risks are real but not fatal. Top speed and range limit use to niche commutes, yet that niche is large enough for a first wave. Mail order avoids dealer costs and matches the enthusiast buyer. Safety objections are speculative; no evidence of regulatory action is given. Winter weather is a seasonal dip, not a structural failure.

Alternatives: Do nothing—loses first-mover advantage. Sell through shops—slower and costlier. A smaller pilot—delays scale and lets imitators catch up. None beats the proposed plan on speed or cost.

Blocker: None. The facts given do not contradict the core claim. The risks are unproven concerns, not demonstrated failures. The appliance maker’s commitment is a strong positive signal.

Kill criterion: If mail-order orders in the first quarter after launch do not exceed 10,000 units, stop further production commitments and pivot to a revised offering.

Decision: Approve as proposed. Launch in January, sell by mail order, and monitor early sales closely. The upside justifies the risk.

DECISION: GO
