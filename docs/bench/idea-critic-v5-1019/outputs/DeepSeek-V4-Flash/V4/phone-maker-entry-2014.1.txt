First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer to enter a high-stakes, low-reversibility hardware market where its stated advantages don't address the core purchase drivers.

Steelman: A premium device with a novel 3D display and seamless product-shopping integration could differentiate the retailer, deepen ecosystem lock-in, and justify a premium price through a unique shopping experience.

Attack: The phone must win on screen quality and app catalog—both dominated by incumbents. The 3D effect is unproven in driving purchase intent; the camera-shopping feature only benefits the retailer's own catalog, not the user's daily needs. Single-carrier distribution caps addressable market at launch, while the premium price matches leaders with far larger ecosystems. The year of membership is a retention perk, not a reason to switch phones.

Pre-mortem: The device launches to lukewarm reviews; buyers compare it to the leading phone and see fewer apps, a gimmicky display, and a shopping tool they rarely use. Carrier partners deprioritize it. Inventory sits, discounts erode the premium position, and the retailer's brand takes a hit in a category it doesn't own.

Alternatives: Do nothing and let incumbents own phones; or a lower-risk pilot—a camera-shopping app on existing phones to test demand for the feature before committing to hardware.

Blockers: The proposal's own evidence shows buyers prioritize apps and screens, where the plan is weakest; no stated evidence that the 3D effect or shopping feature would overcome this. A single-carrier premium launch against entrenched rivals fails as proposed.

Risks: Unproven demand, execution difficulty, one-way door.

Kill criteria: If added, a pilot with clear metrics would be needed; none proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
