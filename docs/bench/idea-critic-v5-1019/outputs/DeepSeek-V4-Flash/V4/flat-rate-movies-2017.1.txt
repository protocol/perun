DECISION: NO-GO

The core claim — rapid membership growth at $10/month will outpace losses and yield profitable data and studio deals — fails on the arithmetic stated. At full ticket price per film, a member seeing just two films monthly costs the company more than the fee. Growth multiplies losses linearly; there is no cap, tier, or negotiated discount in the proposal to break this. The stated upside (data, deals) is unproven and unspecified: no revenue model, no contract terms, no timeline. The proposal does not show how heavy users — who get unlimited value — are priced for. Theaters refusing to cooperate is a stated risk, but the plan offers no mitigation or alternative distribution. The new owner’s cash subsidizes, not solves, the unit economics. A cheaper, simpler alternative — partner with theaters on discounted bulk tickets or revenue share, or cap usage — reaches the same growth with lower bleed, but the proposal rejects staging in favor of an all-in price cut. Pre-mortem: within a year, losses exceed subsidy, data revenue is negligible, and theaters renegotiate or exit. There is no kill criterion, no pilot, no metric threshold, and no exit trigger. The proposal is a one-way door dressed as a growth play. It does not beat “do nothing” on stated facts and does not justify the irreversible spend. Approving as proposed now would commit real money to a model whose own figures show it cannot work.
