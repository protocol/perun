This proposal asks the dominant desktop OS maker to bet its core user experience on a touch-first model, despite most PCs lacking touchscreens. The stated risks are severe: enterprise users face steep retraining costs, the removal of the familiar start menu disrupts daily workflows, and there is no rollback path. The upside—capturing tablet share—is speculative, as tablets were a separate market, and the proposal offers no evidence that desktop users want this change or that developers will follow. Forcing a touch interface on mouse-and-keyboard users risks alienating the very base that made the OS dominant, while the tablet app store faces a chicken-and-egg problem.

The plan is a one-way door with high blast radius and no pilot or kill criterion. A safer path would have been to offer the start screen as an optional mode, test enterprise adoption, and keep the classic desktop as default. The proposal’s own risks are not mitigations; they are admissions of likely failure. The cost of delay is low relative to the risk of a disruptive, irreversible misstep.

DECISION: NO-GO
