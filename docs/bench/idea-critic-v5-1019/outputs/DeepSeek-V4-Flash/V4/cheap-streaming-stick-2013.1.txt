First call: GO. The proposal is a low-cost, two-way-door bet that leverages existing assets and a clear differentiator.

Steelman: A $35 HDMI dongle, controlled entirely by the phone, bypasses the cost and complexity of a full set-top box, creates a new category, and seeds an open ecosystem.

Better ways: Do nothing (lose TV presence); build a full box (high cost, proven poor sales); or a dongle with a remote (adds cost, but solves the confusion risk). The phone-as-remote is the cheapest path and the core innovation.

Pre-mortem: It fails because buyers don't understand a TV accessory with no UI, or because the open kit attracts no developers, leaving it a one-trick pony with no apps.

Blockers: NONE. The stated risks (no remote, thin margin, few apps, incumbent catalogs) are risks, not facts showing failure. The low price and phone control are the answer to the "confusing" risk. The open kit is the answer to the "few apps" risk. The established boxes are a different price tier.

Risks: Buyer confusion at retail; developer apathy; margin pressure; being dismissed as a toy. All are plausible but unproven, and the low cost of the device makes the downside small and reversible.

Kill criteria: If after one year, sell-through is below 100k units or fewer than 50 third-party apps are published, stop and pivot to a software-only strategy.

Decision: GO. The proposal is cheap, reversible, and attacks the market from an angle incumbents ignore. The risks are real but manageable, and the upside of creating a new category outweighs the small, capped loss.

DECISION: GO
