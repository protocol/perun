First call: NO-GO. The proposal explicitly defers revenue indefinitely while relying on an unproven model, and the stated risks are not addressed by the plan.

Steelman: A free, accessible language-learning tool can build a massive user base, and the translation-by-learners model could generate revenue once scale is reached.

Better ways: (1) Pilot the translation revenue model immediately with a small group of willing learners and one publisher to test quality and demand. (2) Partner with an existing nonprofit or university to share costs. (3) Charge for premium features (e.g., certificates, advanced lessons) from day one, keeping core learning free.

Assumptions: That learners will produce sellable translations at scale; that publishers will buy them; that free users will tolerate ads or future monetization without churn.

Blocker: The proposal states the revenue model is unproven and offers no path to test it before launch. Deferring revenue does not resolve the core risk—it merely postpones discovering whether the model works. The stated facts show the plan fails its own goal of sustainability.

Kill criteria: If after 6 months, fewer than 100 learners opt into translation work, or no publisher agrees to a paid pilot, stop the translation effort and pivot to a proven revenue model.

Risks: Poor translation quality; polished competitors; free users never convert; nonprofit alternative rejected prematurely.

Decision: The plan as proposed—build now, monetize later—is a one-way door to sunk cost. Approve only with a revenue pilot built into the first version.

DECISION: NO-GO
