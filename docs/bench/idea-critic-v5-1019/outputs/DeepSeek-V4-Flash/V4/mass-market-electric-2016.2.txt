First call: NO-GO. The plan asks to leap from niche production to mass manufacturing within a year, with no evidence the company can scale supply, margins, or demand.

Steelman: A low-cost sedan could expand the market, secure a first-mover advantage, and deposits prove demand before full commitment.

Better ways: Do nothing until capacity is proven; or start with a modest production run to test supply chains and processes, then scale.

Fit: The proposal moves toward a stated goal of mass-market adoption, but it skips the necessary capability-building stage.

Pre-mortem: It fails because production delays compound, battery suppliers cannot deliver, quality issues emerge at scale, and refundable deposits evaporate, leaving cash-burning operations. This is a two-way door—delaying loses time but not the market—so waiting a quarter costs little.

Evidence: All figures are assumed. The plan states no proven volume, no secured supply, no margin cushion, and no demand beyond refundable deposits. The company’s history contradicts the core claim that it can produce at this scale next year.

Blockers: The stated facts—no mass-production experience, unproven battery scale-up, thin margins—already show the plan fails as proposed. Risks: deposits may not convert; execution difficulty; supplier bottlenecks. None are blockers because they could be managed, but the lack of any demonstrated capability is a blocker.

Kill criteria: None given, so I would add: if deposits do not cover initial production costs within six months, or if supplier contracts are not signed by a set date, stop or scale down.

The proposal’s own evidence removes its route to success: it cannot build what it has never built, at volume, in one year, on thin margins. Approving it now would commit to an unproven leap.

DECISION: NO-GO
