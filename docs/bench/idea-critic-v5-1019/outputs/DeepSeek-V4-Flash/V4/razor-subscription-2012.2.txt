First call: GO. The proposal is cheap, testable, and reversible, with a clear path to validate demand before scaling.

Steelman: A low-budget, humorous video can generate outsized organic attention, and a subscription model creates recurring revenue and customer lock-in, bypassing the incumbents' advertising strength through a different channel.

Attack: The video may flop, the supplier could squeeze margins, and incumbents could retaliate with price cuts. But the cost of failure is tiny—a simple website and one video—while the upside is a new distribution model. The subscription avoids competing on price or ads directly. Supplier dependence is a risk, not a blocker, since volume is initially negligible. Incumbent retaliation is unlikely to be triggered by a small entrant and would hurt their own margins more than yours. The stated risks are all survivable at this scale.

Better way: Do nothing or sell via retail, but both ignore the core insight—subscription shifts the purchase from a considered buy to a habit, which is a genuine advantage. A cheaper alternative (e.g., only a landing page) would not generate the attention needed to test the concept.

Kill criteria: If the video gets under 10,000 views or the site converts under 1% of visitors in the first 30 days, stop and reassess. This is a two-way door; the cost of delay is losing first-mover novelty in a category that incumbents may eventually enter.

Verdict: The plan is sound, cheap, and reversible. The risks are real but not fatal. Proceed now.

DECISION: GO
