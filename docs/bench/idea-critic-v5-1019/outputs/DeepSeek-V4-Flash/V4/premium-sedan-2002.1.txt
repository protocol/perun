First call: NO-GO. The stated facts—no luxury image, badge-driven buyers, high cost at low volume—already undermine the core claim.

Steelman: A well-engineered, expensive sedan can overcome badge perception through sheer product excellence and global availability.

Better ways: Do nothing (preserve capital and brand focus); acquire or partner with an existing premium brand (buys badge and image instantly, at lower risk); launch a smaller, lower-volume sedan as a test of brand acceptance before committing to a dedicated factory.

Fit: Entering premium under a mass-market name contradicts the region’s established brand positioning; it risks diluting the core mass-market identity without a stated path to premium credibility.

Pre-mortem: The sedan launches to weak demand; luxury buyers reject the badge; low volume fails to cover factory costs; the brand’s mass-market image is damaged; the project is a one-way door with billions sunk.

Evidence: All stated facts point to failure: buyers pay for the badge (a known barrier), the brand has no luxury image (a stated negative already true), and high build cost at low volume (stated numbers don’t work). No stated evidence contradicts these.

Blocker: The proposal’s own facts—no luxury image and badge-driven buyers—already show the target users are unlikely to accept the product, and the dedicated factory is irreversible.

Risks: Execution quality, market timing, brand damage.

Kill criteria: None proposed; no pilot, cap, or exit.

DECISION: NO-GO
