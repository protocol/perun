First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a mature market with no stated app ecosystem, distribution, or evidence of demand for the 3D gimmick.

Steelman: A premium device with unique hardware differentiators, deep retail integration, and bundled membership could carve a niche among loyal customers seeking novelty and convenience.

Attack: The stated facts undermine the core claim. Leading phones win on app catalogs and screens. This phone offers neither. The 3D effect is unproven value; product identification duplicates a camera and app already available. Single-carrier launch caps reach. The retailer has zero phone experience, a one-way door on a large, irreversible spend.

Alternatives: Do nothing (low cost, no risk). Partner with an existing phone maker for a co-branded device (cheaper, faster, lower risk). Launch a phone app, not hardware (minimal cost, test demand).

Fit: No stated north star supports hardware. It dilutes focus from the core retail business.

Pre-mortem: The phone launches to lukewarm reviews, app gaps dominate coverage, the 3D effect is dismissed as a toy, carrier sales underperform, and the program is killed after a year with sunk costs and brand damage.

Evidence: All figures are assumed; the proposal provides no market data, no cost estimates, no sales projections, no carrier terms, no app strategy.

Blockers: The proposal's own evidence shows incumbents win on apps and screens—this phone offers neither, and no plan addresses that gap. The stated facts already show target users value apps over the proposed differentiators.

Risks: High price, single carrier, unproven 3D demand, execution in a new category, one-way door.

Kill criteria: None proposed.

No blocker-free path to success on the stated facts. A cheaper, reversible app-based test could validate demand first.

DECISION: NO-GO
