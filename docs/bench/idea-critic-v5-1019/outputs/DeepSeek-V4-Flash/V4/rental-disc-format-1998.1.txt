First call: GO. The economics and control points are strong, but the stated risks are real and must be priced.

Steelman: A $2–$3 rental disc with a 48-hour window, enforced by hardware and a phone check, creates a new revenue stream with near-zero marginal cost, while the special player becomes a lock-in device. The retailer owns the customer relationship and the rental transaction.

Attack: The core failure mode is ecosystem adoption. A new format requires studios to master and release titles, and electronics makers to manufacture players — both have no reason to commit early. The player costs more and plays only this format, so early adopters pay a premium for a catalog that may be thin. The phone-line check adds friction and a failure point (no line, busy signal, privacy concern). The “pay again to watch” model is a behavioral hurdle: customers perceive it as a penalty, not a feature.

Better way: Do nothing. DVD is launching, and rental via existing VHS/DVD channels already works. A proprietary, higher-cost player with a phone check is a worse value proposition than a standard player plus a rental store visit. The upside is not proven; the downside is a stranded investment in a format the market rejects.

Kill criteria: If after 12 months fewer than 3 major studios commit to releasing titles, or fewer than 2 electronics makers produce players, stop. If early customer trials show more than 30% refusal to pay for a second viewing, pivot to a flat rental model.

Blocker: The proposal’s own facts show the target users (mass-market renters) are price- and convenience-sensitive. A higher-cost player, a phone check, and a per-view fee all violate that. The stated risks are not hypothetical; they are the product’s core design. No evidence in the proposal shows studios or makers will sign on.

DECISION: NO-GO
