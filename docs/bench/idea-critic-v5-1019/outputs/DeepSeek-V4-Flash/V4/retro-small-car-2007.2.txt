Steelman: The 1957 model has lasting emotional equity; a modern, well-executed retro design can differentiate in a crowded small-car market and command a premium despite sharing the platform. Personalization drives higher transaction prices and loyalty.

Attack: The company’s recent models sold poorly—this indicates brand weakness, not that nostalgia alone reverses it. Buyers in the small-car segment are price-sensitive; a premium over the platform-mate with no mechanical or efficiency advantage is a hard sell. Thin margins mean any miss is costly. The risks are not hypothetical; they are the stated facts. The plan has no pilot, no volume target, no margin floor, no kill criterion.

Blockers: Recent models sold poorly (stated). Small cars carry thin margins (stated). Premium pricing over an identical platform with only styling and options is unsupported by any evidence of demand. The proposal’s own facts show the upside is speculative while the downside is concrete.
