DECISION: NO-GO

The proposal fails on multiple fronts. First, it assumes the primary sales loss is due to casual copying by buyers, but the largest threat was large-scale commercial piracy, which this software does not address. Second, the installed software creates a significant security and trust liability: if a vulnerability is found, the company faces a PR and legal crisis, and the burden of uninstall issues falls on legitimate customers. Third, the risk of customer backlash is high and direct—treating paying customers as potential criminals often drives them to alternative, legal services or to simply stop buying physical media. The cost of shipping millions of discs with this software is enormous, and the likely outcome is a damaged brand, regulatory scrutiny, and no measurable recovery of lost sales. A better approach would have been to invest in legal digital distribution, which was already emerging. The proposal’s core premise—that invasive software will deter copying—is unproven and likely counterproductive. The potential downside far outweighs any speculative benefit.
