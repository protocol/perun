The proposal underestimates market fit and timing. In 1985, British consumers expect cars for year-round, all-weather utility. A 15 mph, short-range, unprotected tricycle is a niche novelty, not a mass-market product. Mail order cannot overcome fundamental discomfort and safety perception. The appliance maker’s assembly capability does not solve demand. Winter limits use, killing repeat purchases. Launching now risks heavy losses and brand damage.

The risks are not minor—they are core. There is no evidence of target buyer willingness to replace a car for commuting. Safety groups’ objections could trigger regulatory or insurance hurdles. The low price does not offset poor value for most drivers. A launch event cannot mask these flaws.

Better to delay, redesign for weather protection, higher speed, or target a specific leisure segment with a smaller, test-market rollout. Large-scale mail order amplifies any failure.

The proposal lacks a credible plan to overcome the stated risks, and no market research is provided to justify volume.

DECISION: NO-GO
