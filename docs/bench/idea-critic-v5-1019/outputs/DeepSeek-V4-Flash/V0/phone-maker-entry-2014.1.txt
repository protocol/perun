This proposal asks to enter a mature, brutal market against entrenched competitors with far larger app ecosystems and carrier relationships. The single-carrier launch artificially narrows the addressable market at the exact moment scale matters most. The 3D display effect and product-identification camera are differentiated but unproven in driving purchase intent; consumers buy phones for core utility, not novelty gimmicks. Bundling a year of membership service adds cost without addressing the fundamental question: why would a user choose this phone over a proven alternative with more apps and better support? The risks raised are not hypothetical—they are structural. The price point invites direct comparison with superior options, and the retailer has zero supply chain, logistics, or after-sales experience in handsets. This is a high-cost, high-risk entry with no clear path to meaningful share. The idea may be bold, but it fails the test of moving the north star profitably. The evidence on hand does not support a go.

DECISION: NO-GO
