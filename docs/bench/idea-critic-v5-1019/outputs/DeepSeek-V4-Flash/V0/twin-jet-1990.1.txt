This proposal asks to commit enormous capital to a new aircraft program based on a single airline's order. The critical risk is regulatory: the twin-engine design requires overwater approval that has not been granted. If denied, the entire platform is grounded, and the order becomes worthless. The rival's competing models add market pressure, but the regulatory gate is the hard blocker.

The "eight airlines in the room" input could improve fit, but it also invites scope creep and delays, inflating an already massive cost. The single 34-aircraft order is a thin foundation for a program of this scale—it anchors the business case but does not de-risk the launch. A delay to secure regulatory clearance first, or to firm up additional orders, would materially lower the downside without sacrificing the design's core value.

The cost of waiting is real: rivals are advancing, and airline interest may cool. But the cost of a failed launch is catastrophic and largely irreversible. The upside does not justify betting the company on an unproven regulatory path and one customer.

DECISION: NO-GO
