DECISION: NO-GO

The proposal underestimates the reputational and legal risk of a selective replacement policy. Requiring users to “prove” they are affected places an unreasonable burden on them, especially when the flaw is rare and hard to reproduce in typical use. This will read as the company hiding a defect, not managing a minor issue. The widely discussed nature of the problem means the story will be framed by critics, not by the company. A “very large sum” for full replacement is a one-time cost; the long-term cost of lost trust, litigation, and brand damage is likely far greater and ongoing. The correct move is to offer replacement to all who request it, transparently acknowledge the flaw, and frame the cost as the price of integrity.
