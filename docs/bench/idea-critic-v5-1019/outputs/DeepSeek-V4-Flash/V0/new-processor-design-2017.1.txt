DECISION: NO-GO

The proposal fails as presented because it repeats a known failure pattern without addressing its root cause. The company’s last all-new architecture underperformed, yet this plan offers no evidence that the new design solves the specific technical or market failure of the previous one. “Many more cores at lower prices” is a pricing and marketing tactic, not a proven performance advantage—software may not scale to those cores, and the rival can respond with price cuts or faster chips, erasing the margin advantage.

Finances are the decisive constraint. With no room for a second miss, launching a high-risk, all-new architecture is a bet the company cannot afford to lose. The proposal offers no kill criteria, no staged rollout, no fallback if early benchmarks or software support lag. It is an all-or-nothing gamble dressed as a comeback.

A better path would be a limited, time-boxed pilot: ship a small desktop SKU to a narrow enthusiast segment, measure real-world performance and software utilization, and only scale if those metrics beat the rival’s equivalents. That preserves capital and learning without betting the company. As written, the proposal asks for a full launch with no circuit breaker—that is not a strategy, it is a hope.
