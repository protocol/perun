DECISION: NO-GO

The proposal asks for $1.75B to enter a market where free, established alternatives already own the core behavior. The core premise—that audiences will pay for premium short-form, phone-only content—is unproven, and the plan’s own constraints (no TV, launch in only two countries) limit the path to scale.

The production budgets are high, and recoupment requires a subscriber base that is speculative. With free short video dominant on social platforms, the paid value proposition is weak. The launch window in spring 2020 offers no clear competitive advantage.

The plan lacks a low-cost, reversible test. A $1.75B commitment with high-budget commissions is a one-way door. The better path is a smaller, time-boxed pilot in one market with modest budgets, measuring willingness to pay and retention before scaling.

The risks are not mitigated: the content is not accessible on the largest screen in the home, and the ad-supported tier competes directly with free platforms that have far more inventory and reach. The cost of delay is low; the cost of a failed $1.75B bet is very high.

The proposal should not be approved as presented. The evidence does not support the size of the investment or the launch strategy. A revised, smaller-scale approach with clear kill criteria would be a stronger recommendation.
