DECISION: NO-GO

The proposal fails its own test: it is a 1985 launch of a 15 mph, short-range, weather-exposed tricycle in a market defined by wet winters and fast traffic. The safety objection is not a nuisance—it is the core product flaw. A low, slow vehicle among cars is a hazard, and safety-group opposition will likely stall or kill it before mail-order volume materializes. The appliance maker’s assembly agreement does not solve demand; it only solves production.

The optional-extras model hides that the base price is a loss leader, and the January unveiling maximizes bad-weather press. The “large numbers” sales target has no evidence of buyer intent, only a price point. The risks are not residual—they are the business plan. No verification, no pilot, no winter testing, no insurance or regulatory check is mentioned. This is a one-way door with no kill criterion.

The better way is to test in a warmer, more controlled market or with a licensed, higher-speed variant, and to delay the launch until safety and weather issues are addressed. As proposed, it should not proceed.
