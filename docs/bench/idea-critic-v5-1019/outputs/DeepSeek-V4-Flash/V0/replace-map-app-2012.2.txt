DECISION: NO-GO

The proposal fails on its own stated risk list, which is already a strong signal. The core claim—that users will accept a demonstrably worse default for the sake of the maker’s control—is unsupported. The evidence on hand shows the new maps have far less data and likely wrong place information. That is not a minor gap; it is the product’s entire function. Removing a working, trusted app and replacing it with a visibly inferior one will generate immediate, widespread user frustration, negative press, and a perception of brokenness that is hard to reverse. The benefit (control, data, ecosystem) is real but long-term and indirect; the cost is immediate, visible, and borne by every user.

The better way is not to replace the default outright, but to ship the new maps as an optional download and default for those who opt in, while keeping the rival app preinstalled. That preserves control, gathers real usage data, and lets the product earn its place. The two-way door is clearly available: start small, learn, then decide. The proposal as written is a one-way door with no kill criterion and no measured threshold for failure. The cost of delay is low; the cost of a botched launch is high and permanent in user trust. Pre-mortem: the failure is not that the maps are imperfect, but that the maker forced them on users before they were ready. That is entirely predictable from the facts given.
