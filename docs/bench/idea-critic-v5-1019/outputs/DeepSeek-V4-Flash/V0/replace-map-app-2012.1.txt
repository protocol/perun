DECISION: NO-GO

The proposal’s core claim—that replacing a mature, widely trusted mapping app with an internally built, data-poor alternative will improve user control—fails its own risk test. The steelman (“ownership of a critical utility justifies short-term degradation for long-term leverage”) does not survive the evidence given: the new app has materially less data and known inaccuracies in places and business info. That is not a minor edge case; it is the primary function of a map.

The better way is a phased pilot: ship the new app as an optional download, measure user adoption, error reports, and navigation failures against the incumbent, and only then consider a default switch. This preserves the goal (control) while making the change reversible and evidence-based. The proposal as written is a one-way door with no rollback and no kill criterion—if the fall launch fails, the phone maker has degraded its core product for millions of users with no exit.

Pre-mortem: the new maps fail on a high-traffic route in a major city; press amplifies the error; users perceive the brand as broken, not just the app. The cost of delay is low—one quarter—while the cost of a botched launch is reputational and permanent. The proposal names no metric to watch, no threshold to trigger a pivot, and no date to review.

The correct call is to not approve as proposed. Revise to a limited, reversible pilot with clear success/failure metrics before any default replacement.
