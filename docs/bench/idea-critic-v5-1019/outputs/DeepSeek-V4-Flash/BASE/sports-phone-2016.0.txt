The proposal leverages a proven sales base and preempts a rival’s launch, a strong strategic move. The August release captures early demand and disrupts the competitor’s September window. The features—larger battery, iris scanner, water resistance—are meaningful differentiators that justify a faster cycle.

However, the risks are not hypothetical. Less testing time on a thinner body with a larger battery increases the probability of thermal or structural failure. A new biometric sensor at global volume, untested, could have a high defect rate, and a global recall would erase any first-mover advantage. The cost of a failed global launch—reputation, logistics, replacement units—far exceeds the benefit of beating a rival by a few weeks. The rival’s phone is expected; your phone is not yet proven. Shipping a known-risk product worldwide, with no fallback plan in the proposal, is reckless.

The executive team should not approve this as is. The schedule must be extended for at least one full testing cycle, and the iris scanner should be piloted in a single market before global rollout. The battery and thinness trade-off needs a safety validation report. These are not minor tweaks; they are fundamental to the proposal’s viability.

Approve only after these conditions are met, and then launch with a clear risk-mitigation plan. As proposed now, the upside is speculative, the downside is certain and severe.

DECISION: NO-GO
