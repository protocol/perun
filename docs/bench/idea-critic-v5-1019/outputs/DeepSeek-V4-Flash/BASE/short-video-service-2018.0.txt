The proposal targets a real behavioral shift: mobile-first, short-form viewing. The founding team pairs industry access with platform expertise, which is credible. The capital is substantial, but the market for premium, scripted short-form is unproven at scale.

The core risks are not operational but existential. Free, high-quality short-form already saturates social feeds. Asking users to pay monthly for a format they consume for free elsewhere requires a dramatic differentiation in content quality or exclusivity. The plan’s high budgets from known producers could deliver that, but the launch constraint—phone-only, no TV—limits the perceived value. A phone-only subscription for short episodes is a tough sell when the same phone offers endless free options.

The two-country launch mitigates risk, but the spend before breakeven is enormous. Even with an ad tier, the revenue per user is likely low, and churn could be high once the novelty fades. The team’s experience helps secure content, but it does not solve the fundamental question of whether a paid, phone-only short-form library can generate enough habitual viewing to justify the cost. The timing (2020) also faces upcoming competition from major studios entering streaming with full-length libraries.

The proposal is bold but premature. The business model lacks a proven willingness to pay for this specific format, and the launch limitations reduce the chance of building a habit. A smaller, test-market approach with lower budgets and TV compatibility would be wiser. As proposed, the capital and go-to-market strategy carry too much downside.

DECISION: NO-GO
