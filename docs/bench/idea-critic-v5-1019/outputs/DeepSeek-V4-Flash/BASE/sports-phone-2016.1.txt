The proposal leverages a proven product line’s momentum to preempt a rival’s September launch. The August release is earlier than usual, but the phone’s core architecture is already established from prior flagships, reducing design risk. The larger battery in a thinner body is a known engineering tension, yet the company has shipped thin, high-capacity devices before; the incremental change is plausible. The iris scanner is new, but biometric sensors are not novel to the market—failure modes are understood, and volume production can be managed with existing supplier relationships. Water resistance is a mature feature. The main risk is reduced testing time, but this is offset by shipping in volume worldwide, which forces early real-world feedback and allows rapid software patches. A defect at global scale is costly, but the company’s previous large-screen line has a track record of reliability, and the financial upside of capturing the back-to-school and pre-rival window likely outweighs the low-probability, high-impact failure scenario. The rival’s September reveal will dominate media; launching in August secures a full month of uncontested sales and reviews. The battery and sensor risks are mitigable through conservative firmware limits and supplier quality checks. The proposal is aggressive but not reckless—it uses existing strengths to seize a timing advantage. Delaying or revising would cede the initiative.  
DECISION: GO
