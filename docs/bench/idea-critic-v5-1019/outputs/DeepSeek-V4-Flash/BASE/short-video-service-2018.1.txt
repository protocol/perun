The proposal asks for a massive capital raise to enter a crowded market with unproven demand for paid, phone-only short-form content. The founders’ experience is relevant, but the core assumptions are fragile. Free short video already dominates mobile attention, making a paid tier a hard sell. The technical limitation of no TV viewing at launch significantly narrows the addressable audience and value perception. High production budgets from known names do not guarantee subscription conversion, especially when the content is exclusively for a small screen. The path to recouping $1.75 billion before breakeven is speculative and long, with no clear retention strategy or differentiation beyond format. The 2020 launch timeline also ignores the need for extensive user testing and marketing to shift habits. While the ad-supported tier offers a lower barrier, the premium paid tier is the financial engine, and its viability is unproven. The risk of burning through capital without achieving scale is high. The proposal lacks a phased approach, a clear content library strategy, or evidence of consumer willingness to pay for this specific format. It bets on creating a new behavior at a huge cost, with weak fallback options. The executive team should not commit to this scale of investment based on the current assumptions. A smaller pilot or partnership with existing platforms would be wiser, but that is not what is proposed. As presented, the financial and market risks outweigh the potential reward.  

DECISION: NO-GO
