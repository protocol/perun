The proposal leverages a strong existing product line and preempts a rival’s September launch by moving to August. The core trade-off is speed and feature differentiation against compressed testing and novel hardware risk.  

The larger battery in a thinner body is a known engineering challenge, but not unprecedented; thermal and structural risks can be mitigated with existing design margins. The iris scanner is the highest uncertainty—untested at volume, and a global recall on a biometric component would be severe. However, water resistance is mature, and the battery increase is incremental.  

The August release captures a clear window: it hits back-to-school and business cycles, and beats the rival’s momentum. Shipping in volume worldwide means supply chain pressure, but the phone maker has done this before with prior flagships. The defect risk is real but manageable if the company holds back non-critical markets for a week or two post-launch—yet the proposal says ship in volume immediately. That is the sticking point.  

A global immediate launch with an unproven sensor and shortened test cycle could turn a small flaw into a multi-market recall, damaging brand trust more than a one-month delay. The rival’s September phone is a threat, but not an existential one; the existing flagship line already sells well.  

Better to launch in late August in core markets, then expand after 10 days of field data. But the proposal as written demands full global volume on day one. That is not prudent.  

DECISION: NO-GO
