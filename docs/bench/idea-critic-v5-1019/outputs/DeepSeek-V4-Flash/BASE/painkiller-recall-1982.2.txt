The core issue is consumer safety versus commercial preservation. The poisoning is post-factory, but public perception will not distinguish that. If even one more person is harmed, the company faces existential liability and moral condemnation. The proposed nationwide withdrawal is the only action that eliminates further risk immediately. Halting production and advertising prevents any new stock from entering the channel while the threat is active. The later relaunch with tamper-resistant packaging directly addresses the fear and rebuilds trust through visible action. The risks cited—cost, market share loss, competitor gains—are real but secondary. A local recall would leak, appear as a half-measure, and invite speculation that the company is hiding broader contamination. A partial response would be seen as prioritizing profit over lives, destroying brand equity far more permanently than a temporary absence. The brand’s dominance gives it the resources to survive the financial hit and the recognition to reclaim shelf space, especially with a demonstrably safer package. The proposal’s sequence—withdraw, halt, relaunch—is decisive and coherent. Hesitation or revision would prolong the danger window and invite regulatory or legal intervention. The executive team must accept the short-term loss to protect the long-term franchise and, more importantly, public health. The plan is proportionate to the threat and the only responsible course.  

DECISION: GO
