The core question is whether to commit $350M to an unproven, secretive technology in a domain far outside the chain’s core competency. The founder’s claims are compelling, but the evidence base is absent. No published studies mean no independent verification. The refusal to share methods prevents any due diligence on accuracy, reliability, or regulatory viability. The chain’s lack of medical expertise means it cannot even assess the risks it is assuming, nor manage clinical operations safely. The regulatory uncertainty is not a minor concern—it is existential. If regulators later deem the tests invalid, the chain would face massive liability, reputational damage, and stranded investment in hundreds of store clinics. The exclusivity offer is a trap: it locks the chain into a single, unvalidated partner. The upside is speculative; the downside is concrete and enormous. The proposal asks for immediate, irreversible commitment based on faith, not facts. The prudent path is to decline now, or require rigorous, published, peer-reviewed validation and full method disclosure before any further discussion. There is no urgency that justifies bypassing basic diligence. The investment is too large, the risk too asymmetric, and the chain’s position too vulnerable. Approving this as proposed would be a failure of fiduciary responsibility. The executive team should reject this version.  
DECISION: NO-GO
