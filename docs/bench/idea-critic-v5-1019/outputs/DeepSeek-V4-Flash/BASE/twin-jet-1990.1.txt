The proposal asks for a massive investment based on a single airline’s order of 34 aircraft, which is insufficient market validation for a program of this scale. The eight-airline collaboration, while beneficial for design fit, introduces high risk of scope creep and schedule slippage, directly threatening the business case. The unresolved regulatory approval for twin-engine long overwater operations is a critical, non-negotiable gate—without it, the aircraft cannot serve its intended primary routes, making the launch premature. Development cost is stated as “very large,” and no mitigation for cost overruns or break-even analysis is provided. The rival’s new models add competitive pressure, but the proposal does not show a clear market share advantage or pricing power. The single order of 34 is a strong start but not enough to justify committing billions of dollars and years of production capacity. The prudent path is to secure at least one or two more major airline commitments, gain provisional regulatory clarity, and finalize a realistic cost and schedule baseline before committing to launch. The design-with-airlines process should continue, but it must be time-boxed. Approving now would lock in enormous financial exposure with unproven demand and unresolved certification. The executive team should direct the team to return with a revised plan that addresses these gaps.  

DECISION: NO-GO
