The proposal asks for a massive capital commitment based on unverified claims. The core risk is not just regulatory uncertainty but a fundamental lack of evidence. The founder refuses to share methods, and no published studies exist. This means the chain cannot independently validate accuracy, safety, or clinical utility. Investing $350 million in hundreds of store clinics on this basis is not a calculated bet; it is a leap into the dark.

The chain’s lack of medical expertise compounds the issue. Even if the technology worked, the chain would be responsible for patient care, handling adverse events, and interpreting results—all outside its core competency. Exclusivity is worthless if the product fails or is later banned. The regulatory risk is severe: if regulators question the tests, the chain faces not just financial loss but reputational damage and potential liability for misdiagnoses.

A prudent approach would require at least a pilot, third-party validation, and transparent data. None of that is offered. The founder’s refusal to share methods suggests either a trade secret so fragile it cannot withstand scrutiny, or a lack of substance. Either way, the chain is being asked to underwrite an unproven medical device with no recourse. The potential upside does not justify the downside. This is a case where the absence of evidence is the evidence of absence—of readiness, of safety, and of a viable business case.

The proposal should be rejected outright. It is not a matter of negotiating better terms; the fundamental premise is unsound. Approving now would be reckless. Holding for revision would still leave the same fatal flaws. The only responsible answer is no.

DECISION: NO-GO
