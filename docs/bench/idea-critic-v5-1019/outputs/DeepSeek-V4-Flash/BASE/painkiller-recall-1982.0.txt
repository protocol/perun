The proposal targets a known contamination incident, but the evidence points to tampering after distribution, not a factory defect. The risk of nationwide withdrawal is that it converts a localized, external threat into a perceived product-wide failure, which could permanently damage consumer trust and hand market share to competitors. The recall cost is substantial, and the brand’s recovery is uncertain. However, the core product is otherwise safe, and the contamination is criminal, not systemic. The company’s own plants are clean. Withdrawing nationally overreacts to a single metro area, and halting production and advertising amplifies fear without addressing the actual source. A better path is to pull only the affected lot or region, increase security and messaging, and accelerate tamper-resistant packaging for future batches—without a full halt. The proposal’s all-or-nothing approach risks a self-inflicted wound. The brand can recover if it responds proportionately, not by conceding the entire market. The public safety concern is real, but a targeted recall plus transparent communication about the external nature of the threat would protect consumers while preserving the brand’s viability. A nationwide withdrawal also sets a precedent that any external criminal act can shut down a national product, which is impractical and overbroad. Thus, the proposal as written is too drastic and costly for the evidence at hand. It should be revised to a limited, regional response with enhanced packaging and monitoring, not a full halt.  
DECISION: NO-GO
