The proposal targets a real gap: users who want a larger screen but not a full tablet. In 2011, four-inch screens dominate, so a five-plus-inch device with a stylus is a distinct, premium differentiator. The risks are valid but manageable. Reviewers’ “too big” criticism is a perception issue, not a functional one—early adopters of large screens often shift norms. Stylus demand is uncertain, but the stylus can be positioned as an accessory for precision tasks, not a core requirement. The “neither fish nor fowl” concern is mitigated by marketing it as a new category—a “phablet”—not a replacement for either. Cannibalization is possible, but the premium price and distinct use case (media, note-taking, productivity) will attract new buyers or upgrade-loyalists, not just existing phone users. The company already has supply chain and software expertise; adding one SKU is low incremental risk. The timing is early, but being first in a visible niche builds brand leadership, and if it fails, the loss is contained to one model. The alternative—waiting—risks ceding the space to competitors. Given the existing line remains untouched, and the device is sold as premium (not mass-market), the downside is limited. The proposal is concrete, actionable, and reversible. Approve now.
