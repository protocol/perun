The proposal asks for a massive financial commitment based on a single airline’s order of 34 aircraft. The market demand is unproven beyond that one customer. The program’s entire viability hinges on a regulatory approval for twin-engine overwater flights that does not yet exist—if denied or delayed, the aircraft’s core selling proposition collapses, and the launch order becomes worthless.  

Development cost is explicitly “very large,” and the airline-in-the-room design process, while beneficial for fit, introduces a high risk of scope creep and schedule slippage, inflating costs further. A rival is simultaneously developing new models, meaning even if approval arrives, the competitive window may be tight, and the rival could undercut on price or timing.  

The single anchor order of 34 units is insufficient to de-risk a program of this scale. It provides no evidence of broader market pull, no committed second customer, and no contingency for the regulatory or competitive risks. The executive team would be approving a bet on an unproven regulatory outcome, a single customer’s loyalty, and an untested design process—all at extreme cost.  

The prudent path is to secure additional launch customers, obtain preliminary regulatory clarity, and firm up the design scope before committing billions. The proposal as written is too fragile for immediate approval.  

DECISION: NO-GO
