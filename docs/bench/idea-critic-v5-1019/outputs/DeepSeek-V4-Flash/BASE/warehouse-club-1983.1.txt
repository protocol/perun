The proposal bets on a membership model and bulk sales, but the facts given highlight a hostile environment. A larger competitor already runs this exact format, with more locations and cash. That means they can underprice, outspend on marketing, or absorb losses longer. The proposed thin margins leave no buffer for the inevitable early mistakes—site missteps, inventory errors, or slower member uptake. The risk that shoppers refuse to pay a fee is real, and the competitor’s existing footprint makes that hurdle even higher. Without any stated advantage—lower costs, better locations, superior logistics, or a differentiated product range—this is a follower entering a fight where the leader already has scale and capital. The plan offers no timeline, no break-even analysis, no contingency if membership lags. It is a pure hope that the format works, but the format is already proven by someone else, and that someone else is stronger. Approving this now would commit resources to a high-risk, low-margin venture with no clear edge. The prudent move is to hold, study the competitor’s weaknesses, or find a niche they cannot serve. As proposed, it is not ready.

DECISION: NO-GO
