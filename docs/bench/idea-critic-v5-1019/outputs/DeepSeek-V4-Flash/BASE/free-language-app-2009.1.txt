The proposal hinges on an unproven revenue model while prioritizing free user growth. In 2009, the translation marketplace for user-generated content was nascent, and quality control for learner output is a critical flaw—publishers will not pay for poor translations, and the cost of curation could exceed revenue. The plan to “look for revenue later” is a classic trap: without a clear path to monetization, the team risks building a large, non-paying user base with no conversion strategy. The existing competition (polished, paid courses) suggests users expect quality, which free, learner-driven content may not provide. The nonprofit concern is valid, but the proposed pivot does not solve sustainability—it merely postpones the problem. The team has academic credibility but no demonstrated business or operational experience in publishing or SaaS. The risk that free learners never become customers is high, as the value proposition (free learning) is decoupled from the revenue source (translation sales). Even if translations improve, the market for such content in 2009 is speculative. The proposal lacks a pilot, a pricing test, or a minimum viable revenue experiment. It is an idea with enthusiasm, not a viable business plan. Approving now would commit resources to a product with no validated demand for its core economic engine. The better path is to first test whether publishers will pay for any learner-generated translations, even with a small sample, before building the full app. Without that evidence, the risk is unacceptable.  

DECISION: NO-GO
