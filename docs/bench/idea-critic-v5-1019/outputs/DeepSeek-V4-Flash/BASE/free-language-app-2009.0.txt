The proposal hinges on launching a free product with an unproven, potentially low-quality revenue stream. The core value—free language learning—is strong, but the monetization relies on learner-generated translations, which are likely to be inconsistent and require heavy curation, adding cost and complexity. The target market for such translations is narrow and price-sensitive, and publishers already have professional options.  

The plan to "look for revenue later" is a critical flaw. Without a validated payment mechanism from day one, the team risks building a large user base that expects everything free, making future conversion to any paid model—even a different one—extremely difficult. The risks are not just operational but existential: poor translation quality could damage the app’s reputation for learning, and the polished, established competitors will easily out-market a bare-bones tool.  

The team’s passion is clear, but the business model is a hope, not a strategy. The proposed path burns time and resources on a product with no clear path to sustainability, and the “later” revenue pivot would likely require a fundamental redesign. The proposal should be revised to first test a small, paid pilot for translation services or a freemium tier, even at low volume, to prove willingness to pay. Without that evidence, the project is a costly gamble.  

DECISION: NO-GO
