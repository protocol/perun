The proposal leverages a strategic desire for control, but the timing and execution are flawed. The core asset—the rival’s mapping app—is currently a trusted, functional default. Replacing it with a demonstrably inferior product (less data, incorrect place info) risks immediate user backlash and a perception of quality decline. The fall launch window offers no time to close the data gap. While 3D views and voice support are attractive, they do not compensate for basic reliability failures. Users will not accept a regression in a daily utility, especially when the working alternative is still accessible (though no longer default). The risk of annoyance is high, and the reputational damage to the phone’s core experience could outweigh the long-term strategic gain. The proposal should be held until the mapping data reaches parity or a clear, differentiated advantage exists. Proceeding now would likely force a public retreat or a costly fix under duress. The executive team should reject the current timeline and scope, not the ambition. A revised plan with a later launch and a phased rollout—perhaps keeping the rival app as a fallback—would be more prudent. As presented, the downside is immediate and measurable, while the upside is speculative. The data deficiency alone is disqualifying for a default replacement.  
DECISION: NO-GO
