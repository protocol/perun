The proposal targets a real market gap: users who want a larger screen for media and productivity but find tablets too bulky. The stylus addresses precision needs (notes, drawing) not served by capacitive touch alone. The premium positioning protects margins and brand cachet, while keeping the existing line intact limits cannibalization risk—any internal sales shift is offset by higher unit revenue.

Reviewer skepticism about size is a temporary perception issue, not a durable demand problem. The device does not need to replace either category fully; it creates a new one, as evidenced by the later success of similar form factors. Stylus adoption risk is mitigated by making it a signature feature, not an afterthought, and by marketing it as a tool, not a gimmick.

The main threat—cannibalization—is acceptable because the premium price yields better profit per unit, and the new device expands the total addressable market by attracting tablet-bound customers. The risks raised are speculative and based on current conventions, which the proposal is explicitly challenging. First-mover advantage in this niche is valuable; delay invites copycats.

Execution is straightforward: existing manufacturing, distribution, and brand recognition apply. The 2011 timing is early but not premature—the ecosystem (apps, video) already supports larger screens. The downside is limited to a modest inventory write-off if demand disappoints, which is far outweighed by the upside of defining a new category.

DECISION: GO
