The proposal bets on a new category, but every stated risk points to a fundamental mismatch with the market’s current expectations. Reviewers and buyers have anchored on four-inch screens; a five-plus-inch device with a stylus asks them to re-learn what a phone is. The risk that it is “too small for a tablet, too large for a phone” is not a minor concern—it is the core definition of the product. Cannibalization of existing phone sales is likely, since the new device would compete for the same buyer’s upgrade budget, but with higher cost and unclear benefits. The stylus adds friction, not value, for most users who have never asked for one. There is no evidence of unmet demand, no proof that the large screen solves a real pain point, and no signal that the premium price will be accepted. The proposal offers no mitigation for the review backlash or the cannibalization beyond “alongside existing line,” which is weak. Approving now would force a product into a market that is not ready, risking brand confusion and wasted R&D. The safer path is to wait, test with a small pilot, or gather consumer data on larger screens before committing a full launch. The idea may become viable later, but in 2011, the facts given show the downside outweighs the upside. The executive team should not approve this as proposed.  
DECISION: NO-GO
