The proposal hinges on a 2009 bet: build a free learning tool first, monetize later via learner-generated translations. The core product—short, free lessons—has clear appeal, but the revenue path is speculative. Learner translations are likely low-quality, requiring heavy vetting or curation, which adds cost and complexity. Free users rarely convert to paying customers, and the proposed model (selling translations) is unproven and untested in this context. Established competitors offer polished, paid courses, so differentiation rests solely on price (free) and novelty, not quality or reliability. The risks are not minor; they are existential to the business model. The team has no evidence that publishers will buy amateur translations, no plan for quality control, and no fallback if the revenue idea fails. The proposal explicitly defers revenue, meaning the project would burn resources with no clear path to sustainability. The nonprofit alternative was dismissed as unsustainable, yet the for-profit model is equally unproven. The timing (2009) also suggests limited mobile adoption and less mature web-based translation tools, further weakening the feasibility. Without a pilot, a partnership with a publisher, or a validated revenue mechanism, this is an idea in search of a business case. The product may be engaging, but the plan is not investment-ready. It requires a revised approach: secure a revenue commitment, test translation quality with a real publisher, or pivot to a freemium model with clear paid tiers. As proposed, it is premature and high-risk with no concrete upside.  
DECISION: NO-GO
