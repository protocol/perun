The core issue is public safety versus commercial risk. The poisoning is confirmed, but its scope is unknown. A local withdrawal would leave other regions exposed if the contamination spreads, creating catastrophic liability and eroding trust far beyond one city. The brand’s strength is its ubiquity and reliability; a partial response would appear evasive and risk being seen as prioritizing profit over lives.

The proposed nationwide withdrawal, while costly, directly addresses the worst-case scenario. Halting production and advertising removes any ambiguity about the company’s commitment to consumer welfare, and the later relaunch with tamper-resistant packaging turns a defensive move into a market-leading safety standard. Competitors may gain temporary shelf space, but their products lack the established trust that can be rebuilt with a visible, decisive action. The risk of signaling danger is outweighed by the reality that the danger already exists—and the public will judge the response.

A recall’s cost is a sunk investment in brand survival. Market share can be regained with a clear narrative of responsibility and innovation. The alternative—minimizing the threat to save money—would likely trigger regulatory scrutiny, lawsuits, and a permanent association with negligence. The proposal’s speed and totality create a clear moral and strategic position. It does not overreact; it matches the unknown severity with maximum precaution. Any delay or revision would leave the company in a reactive, defensive posture. The only defensible path is to act as if the threat is everywhere, then rebuild on a foundation of demonstrated care.
