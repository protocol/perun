The proposal targets a real threat: tablets eroding PC share. The response—forcing a touch-first start screen on all PCs—is a high-risk bet on a future that is not yet proven for desktop users. The risks are severe: enterprise inertia, high retraining costs, and developer reluctance. Crucially, removing the escape hatch to the classic desktop alienates the core user base without offering a clear, immediate benefit for keyboard-and-mouse work. The app store is a plus, but it does not justify disrupting the primary workflow of millions. The timing is premature; the market has not demanded this change from desktop OS users. A phased, optional approach could test the waters, but the proposal as written mandates a radical shift with no fallback. For a dominant product, preserving reliability and user trust is paramount. The potential upside (catching a tablet wave) does not outweigh the certain short-term disruption and possible long-term brand damage. The executive team should reject this as proposed.  

DECISION: NO-GO
