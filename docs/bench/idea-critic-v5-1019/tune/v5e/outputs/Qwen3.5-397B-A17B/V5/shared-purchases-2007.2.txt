First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "earning more" is a goal, not a competitive advantage, and the mechanism relies on exposing sensitive user data without proven demand.

**Steelman:** Users will tolerate sharing purchase history if given a one-time notification and opt-out, driving ad revenue through social proof.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Mismatch:** The "notification + opt-out" pattern places the burden of privacy protection on the user while assuming default consent for sensitive data (purchases). In 2007, social norms treated financial transactions as private; violating this defaults to hostility, not engagement.
2.  **Partner Risk:** Partner sites face immediate reputational damage if user purchases (e.g., medical supplies, gifts, controversial media) are broadcast to friends. They will likely block the integration or demand liability clauses that erase the revenue upside.
3.  **Irreversibility:** The proposal admits the feature is "hard to withdraw." Once trust is broken by broadcasting sensitive data, it cannot be restored by simply turning the feature off. The "one-way door" risk outweighs the unproven revenue gain.
4.  **No Edge:** Competitors can achieve ad targeting without broadcasting specific purchases to friends. This approach trades long-term platform trust for short-term intrusive metrics.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Purchases are inherently sensitive; broadcasting them defaults to user harm.
*   **Fact:** Partners face embarrassment from public reactions to private buys, creating a conflict of interest that blocks adoption.
*   **Fact:** The proposal admits high irreversibility; a failed launch here damages the core product (trust) permanently.

**Risks:**
*   User backlash leading to mass exodus (high probability given the sensitivity).
*   Regulatory scrutiny emerging from non-consensual data sharing.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal offers no pilot, no cap on data types, and no metric proving users *want* this. It assumes consent where silence is the norm. The "opt-out" mechanism is insufficient for high-sensitivity data.

**Conclusion:** The plan fails the blocker test. The stated risks (privacy invasion, partner embarrassment, irreversibility) are not hypothetical "maybes"; they are direct consequences of the proposed mechanism applied to sensitive data. The lack of a stated edge beyond "earn more" means the massive reputational risk is taken for an unproven gain.

DECISION: NO-GO
