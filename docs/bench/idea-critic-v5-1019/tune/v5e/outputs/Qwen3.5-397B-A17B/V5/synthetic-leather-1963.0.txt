First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it admits higher cost, potential comfort deficits, and adoption friction without offering a compensating advantage in durability, supply stability, or performance that beats existing leather.

Steelman: Launching now secures early market share in a revolutionary material category before competitors react, leveraging heavy marketing to force manufacturer adoption despite initial resistance.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High spend, high friction. Risk: Rejection due to cost/comfort. Irreversible spend on marketing.
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoid sunk costs. Risk: Missing a future trend. Cost: Zero.
3. **Targeted Pilot:** Sell to a single innovative brand for a limited run. Risk: Slower scale. Cost: Low. Reversibility: High. This reaches the same validation upside cheaper.

Fit: Assumes the goal is immediate mass adoption. If the north star is long-term category creation, a "force it now" strategy contradicts the stated risks of manufacturer unwillingness.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because shoe manufacturers refuse to retool factories for a material that costs more and feels worse to wearers. Marketing spend burns out without volume. This is a one-way door for the marketing budget; delay costs only opportunity time, not capital.

Evidence: The proposal's own facts are the evidence against it. It explicitly states the synthetic costs *more* than leather, may be *less* comfortable, and faces *unwilling* manufacturers. There is no cited data showing buyers want this specific trade-off.

Blockers:
1. **Negative Unit Economics:** The material costs more than the incumbent (leather) while offering inferior comfort. Without a proven performance advantage (e.g., waterproofing, weight), there is no economic rationale for a manufacturer to switch.
2. **Channel Resistance:** The proposal admits manufacturers are unwilling to change factories. Heavy marketing to end-users does not solve the supply-side bottleneck if producers refuse to make the shoes.

Risks: Fashion trends shifting (mitigated by pilot); comfort perception (requires blind testing).

Kill Check: The proposal states higher cost and lower comfort as facts. These are not risks; they are current negatives that remove the route to upside unless a specific, unstated benefit exists. Since no such benefit is claimed, the plan fails on its own stated terms.

DECISION: NO-GO
