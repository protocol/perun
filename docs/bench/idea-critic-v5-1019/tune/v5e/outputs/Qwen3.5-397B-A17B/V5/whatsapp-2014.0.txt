First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it presents a desperate defensive reaction to a fear of displacement rather than a plan where the specific facts (no revenue, anti-ad stance, tiny team) create a value advantage over existing alternatives.

Steelman: Acquiring the dominant mobile messaging utility is the only way to prevent total irrelevance as the platform shifts from web to mobile, buying a user base that cannot be organically replicated.

Options:
1. **Acquire:** Costs $19B immediately; high risk of regulatory breakup and culture clash; irreversible capital deployment.
2. **Do Nothing:** Retains $19B cash; risks slow erosion of core business as users migrate; fully reversible until dominance is lost.
3. **Clone/Compete:** Lower cost; high execution risk given the target's network effects; slower time-to-market.

Fit: Aligns with survival (north star) but violates fiscal discipline given the 12x valuation jump in one year with no revenue inflection.

Pre-mortem: The integration fails because the founders leave due to cultural mismatch, the user base flees due to privacy changes or ads, and regulators force a divestiture. This is a one-way door; the cash is gone forever. Delay cost is potential user churn, but paying $19B for a $0-revenue company accelerates financial damage more than slow churn.

Evidence: The proposal admits the target has minimal revenue, refuses ads, and was valued at $1.5B recently. The $19B price relies entirely on the assumption that "hundreds of millions of users" equals inevitable future monopoly profits, which contradicts the target's explicit anti-monetization stance.

Blockers:
1. **Valuation Contradiction:** The price assumes massive future monetization that the target's stated strategy (no ads, $1 fee) explicitly rejects. The math does not work as proposed.
2. **Strategic Mismatch:** Buying a company that refuses your core business model (ads) without a verified plan to change it is a failure of logic, not just risk.

Risks: Founder retention, regulatory scrutiny, culture clash.

Kill Check: The proposal states the target "refused to show advertising." This fact points directly toward the failure of any standard social-network monetization strategy. Since the plan offers no alternative mechanism to justify the $19B multiple against a $1 fee model, this is a blocker, not a risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
