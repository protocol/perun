First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it admits inferior content, poor quality, limited distribution, high costs, and powerful incumbents while threatening the core profitable revenue stream. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Launching a free, limited streaming service now secures essential licensing relationships and user behavior data before competitors lock the market, treating the loss as an R&D cost to preserve long-term relevance.

Attack: The "R&D cost" argument fails because the proposal offers no cap on licensing spend or mechanism to prevent cannibalization of the high-margin DVD business. Entering a market with a worse product at a high cost against better-funded rivals is not a strategic foothold; it is capital destruction. The "free" model removes the only filter for genuine demand, inflating usage metrics with low-value traffic while incurring high variable costs.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Protects $7M subscriber profit; risks eventual obsolescence.
2. Paid pilot: Tests willingness to pay, limits cannibalization, offsets licensing costs.
3. Proposed (Free/Unlimited): Maximizes cannibalization risk with zero revenue offset and undefined cost controls.

Fit: Assumes the north star is "video delivery" rather than "profitable DVD rental." Without a stated pivot to a loss-leader strategy approved by the board, this conflicts with the current profitable model.

Pre-mortem: Streaming costs spiral due to uncapped licensing; DVD subscribers cancel physical plans to use the free tier; the company burns cash competing with tech giants on their terms; the board forces a shutdown in 18 months due to profitability collapse.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: The proposal admits the product is worse (quality/catalog) and more expensive to produce relative to value than the incumbent DVD model.
2. Missing kill criteria: No cap on licensing spend or metric to halt the program if cannibalization exceeds a specific threshold.
3. Economic contradiction: High licensing costs + free price + inferior product = negative unit economics with no path to profit stated.

Risks: Competitor entry, broadband adoption rates, technology scalability. These are secondary to the fundamental lack of a viable business case as proposed.

The proposal asks to burn profitable capital on an inferior product with no guardrails. It must be revised to include a paid tier, strict cost caps, and specific anti-cannibalization metrics before consideration.

DECISION: NO-GO
