Steelman: Doubling production might be an attempt to lower unit costs via economies of scale or to flood the market before a total collapse. However, this logic fails because the cost of holding unsold inventory (financing, storage, depreciation) under high interest rates outweighs any marginal production efficiency.

Alternatives:
1. **Do Nothing/Reduce Production:** Align output with sales to preserve cash. This is the only viable path to survival.
2. **Cut Prices/Incentives:** Attempt to move existing stock, though recession limits effectiveness.
3. **Double Production (Proposed):** Accelerates cash burn.

Fit: A start-up's north star is survival and cash preservation. This plan directly contradicts that by tying up limited cash in depreciating assets that cannot be sold.

Pre-mortem: The company runs out of cash within months. Dealers refuse further inventory due to financing costs. The bank calls the loan due to deteriorating balance sheets. The company enters bankruptcy with 15,000+ unsold cars.

Evidence: The facts state 7,500 produced vs. 3,000 sold. Inventory is growing. Recession and high interest rates suppress demand and increase financing costs. Cash is limited. The math is undeniable: doubling production doubles the cash burn rate on unsellable goods.

Blockers:
1. **Cash Flow Mismatch:** Limited cash cannot support doubled production costs while revenue remains stagnant due to low sales.
2. **Inventory Insolvency:** High interest rates make holding 4,500+ additional units impossible; financing costs will exceed any potential margin.
3. **Dealer Capacity:** Dealers cannot finance more stock, creating a distribution blockade.

Risks: Total company collapse, creditor lawsuits, brand destruction.

The proposal's own facts prove the route to upside is blocked by lack of demand and capital. There is no edge here, only accelerated failure.
