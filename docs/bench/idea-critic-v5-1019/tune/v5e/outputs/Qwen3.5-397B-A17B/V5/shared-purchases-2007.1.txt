First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it assumes exposure equals revenue without proving users will tolerate the trade-off or that advertisers will pay a premium for this specific mechanism over existing targeting.

Steelman: Transparently sharing user actions drives viral growth and ad revenue, with opt-outs mitigating privacy concerns.

Attack: The "notification step" and "option to decline" are insufficient safeguards for sensitive data (purchases, health, finance). Once a user opts out, the viral loop breaks, rendering the feature useless for that segment. If users broadly perceive this as an invasion, the backlash could damage the core platform's trust, not just the feature. Partner embarrassment is a critical failure mode: if a user buys a sensitive item and it appears in a feed before they can react, the partner brand suffers reputational harm, likely causing them to exit the program. The risk of being "hard to withdraw" confirms this is a one-way door; launching creates a permanent liability.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve trust, rely on existing ad models. Cost: $0. Risk: Low. Reversibility: N/A.
2. Aggregated, anonymized insights for advertisers only: Increases revenue without exposing individual user actions. Cost: Low dev. Risk: Medium (less viral). Reversibility: High.
3. Explicit, granular sharing prompts per action (user actively chooses to share): Higher friction but consensual. Cost: Medium. Risk: Low. Reversibility: High.

Pre-mortem: The feature launches, users are shocked by automatic sharing of sensitive purchases, media outlets run stories on "privacy violations," major advertisers flee to avoid association with the scandal, and regulators investigate. The company is forced to kill the feature, but the trust damage is permanent.

Blockers:
- The proposal admits the feature is "hard to withdraw," creating an unacceptable irreversible risk for a core trust function.
- No evidence is provided that users want this or that the revenue uplift outweighs the churn risk.
- The "sensitive purchase" risk is acknowledged but not solved by a post-hoc opt-out.

Risks: User backlash, partner churn, regulatory scrutiny.

DECISION: NO-GO
