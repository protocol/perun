First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state an edge; "free web access" is a feature, not a defensible advantage against incumbents with established users and data licenses.

**Steelman:** A browser-based map with satellite imagery and routing will displace desktop tools and compete with web rivals by offering superior accessibility and unique visual data, despite browser limitations.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. Licensing costs are high, and browsers in 2004 lack the performance for smooth interactive graphics, creating a poor user experience compared to the acquired desktop program or native rivals.
2.  **Fit:** Moving from a paid desktop model to a free web model destroys existing revenue without a stated replacement mechanism. The "free" aspect attracts users but incurs high server and licensing costs per user with no monetization path.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The project launches slowly due to browser rendering bottlenecks. Users reject the sluggish interface. Licensing fees bleed cash. Without a revenue model, the product becomes a costly vanity feature shut down within a year.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no direct revenue model" and "costly licensing." These are not risks; they are stated facts that contradict financial viability. The technical constraint (slow browsers) is a known hardware/software limit of the era, not a solvable variable in this scope.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Contradiction:** The plan combines high fixed costs (licensing) with zero revenue, guaranteeing loss.
*   **Technical Deficit:** Browser technology in 2004 cannot deliver the interactive performance required to beat established competitors or the existing desktop tool.
*   **No Edge:** The proposal offers no reason why users would switch from faster, established sites to a slower, free alternative that costs the company money to serve.

**Risks:** Execution difficulty, market adoption (converted to blockers by the lack of revenue/tech fit).

**Kill Criteria:** N/A (Decision is No-Go).

The proposal fails the fundamental operator test: it spends significant capital on a technically inferior product with no path to return. The absence of a revenue model is a fatal flaw, not a "risk" to be managed later.

DECISION: NO-GO
