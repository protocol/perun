First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; matching the leader's price with a fraction of the apps and no OS share offers no reason for buyers to switch.

Steelman: Leveraging vertical integration (hardware + OS) to deliver a superior user experience that justifies the price despite the app gap.

Attack: The plan explicitly admits a "small app catalog" and "almost no market share" while pricing at the market leader. In a two-sided market, apps drive users and users drive apps. Without a dominant app advantage or a significantly lower price to offset the deficiency, the heavy investment in advertising will likely burn cash acquiring users who churn due to lack of software. The "leadership transition" compounds execution risk during a critical launch window.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital until the app ecosystem matures or leadership stabilizes.
2. Price disruption: Launch significantly below the leader to force trial despite the app gap (sacrificing margin for share).
3. Niche focus: Target a specific vertical where the small catalog is sufficient, rather than a general market assault.

Pre-mortem: The tablet launches, ads run, but reviewers pan the "app gap." Sales flatten, developers see no new users and refuse to port apps, creating a death spiral. The hardware becomes inventory scrap.

Evidence: The proposal's own facts contradict the strategy. A "small fraction" of apps cannot support a "market-leading" price point without a differentiating feature not listed here. The risk of being "unproven" is fatal when the primary value driver (apps) is missing.

Blockers:
1. Price parity with inferior utility: The core value proposition (apps) is weak, yet the cost to the consumer is maximum. This violates basic adoption mechanics for platform businesses.
2. No stated edge: The proposal admits all weaknesses and offers only "heavy investment" as a counter, which is an input, not a strategic advantage.

Risks: Leadership distraction, developer apathy, brand damage from a failed flagship.

The plan attempts to buy relevance without solving the chicken-and-egg problem of platform adoption. It must either undercut price drastically or delay until the catalog grows. As proposed, it fails the basic logic of platform economics.

DECISION: NO-GO
