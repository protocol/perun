First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price and locked supply are disadvantages, not advantages, against existing cheap juicers and fresh produce.

**Steelman:** A premium, hassle-free juicing ecosystem where freshness guarantees and automation justify a 10x cost over manual methods for busy affluent consumers.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** At $699 plus a subscription premium, the cost-per-ounce likely exceeds high-end cold-press delivery services, which require no hardware capex. The "razor-and-blade" model fails if the blade (machine) is too expensive to acquire.
2.  **Friction:** Mandatory Wi-Fi for a countertop appliance introduces failure points (connectivity loss, server shutdowns) without adding clear value to the core mechanical function of pressing fruit.
3.  **Market:** The niche for "smart juicing" is unproven. Consumers generally prefer flexibility (buying any fruit) over proprietary lock-in unless the convenience delta is massive, which is doubtful for a task performed once daily.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" (buy a $100 juicer) or "subscribe to delivered juice" (no machine) offer lower risk and lower entry costs.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unit Economics:** The combined cost of device amortization and subscription bags likely exceeds the customer's willingness to pay compared to substitutes. No data proves otherwise.
*   **Connectivity Requirement:** Mandating internet for a basic mechanical function creates a negative user experience and a potential brick risk, with no stated security or feature benefit that requires it.

**Risks:**
*   Hardware margin erosion.
*   Subscription churn due to price sensitivity.
*   Limited total addressable market for luxury single-use appliances.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   If customer acquisition cost exceeds $300.
*   If subscription retention drops below 40% in month 3.

The proposal relies on unproven assumptions that consumers will accept high lock-in and connectivity friction for a marginal convenience gain. The absence of a clear cost or experience advantage over existing solutions constitutes a fundamental flaw in the business case as presented.

DECISION: NO-GO
