First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a need but fails to state an edge; "low cost" alone is insufficient when buyers must source peripherals and the charity lacks manufacturing or support experience.

**Steelman:** A $35 board creates a unique educational niche by forcing hardware engagement, distinct from closed tablets, if distribution and support gaps are bridged.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Edge:** The claim relies on price, yet the total cost of ownership (board + display + keyboard + power) likely exceeds used laptops or cheap tablets, which are ready-to-use. The proposal admits ordinary devices are "already cheap." Without a stated advantage in durability, curriculum integration, or specific hardware access, the "need" does not translate to a winning product.
2.  **Capability Gap:** The charity explicitly has "no manufacturing or support experience." Relying on licensed distributors does not solve the lack of internal capacity to manage supply chain failures or user support, creating a high risk of reputational damage if the first batch sells out or fails.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows schools to continue using existing cheap laptops. A "better way" would be partnering with an existing hardware manufacturer rather than attempting to sell directly without experience.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because schools reject the hidden costs of peripherals and the charity cannot support frustrated teachers, leading to unsold inventory and wasted funds.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits competitors (laptops/tablets) are cheap and complete. It offers no fact showing why a fragmented $35 solution beats a complete used laptop.
*   **Capability Mismatch:** The core claim (selling hardware) contradicts the stated fact (no manufacturing/support experience).

**Risks:** Supply chain fragility, peripheral friction, lack of support infrastructure. These are not merely risks; the lack of experience combined with the complete nature of competitors acts as a blocker to the plan's viability as proposed.

The plan requires a pivot to partnership or a proven edge before approval. As written, it relies on hope rather than a defensible market position.

DECISION: NO-GO
