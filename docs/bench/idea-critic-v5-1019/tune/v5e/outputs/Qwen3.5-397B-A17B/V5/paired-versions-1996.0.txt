First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a shrinking hardware base and introduces friction (mandatory second player, cable, double purchase) without a stated edge proving this specific friction creates more value than a single complete version.

Steelman: Releasing two complementary versions forces social interaction via trading, creating a viral loop that compensates for the aging console's decline by making the software a social necessity rather than just a game.

Attack:
1. **Fit & Evidence**: The console is "aging" and "declining." A strategy requiring high network density (finding trading partners) fails as the user base fragments. No evidence suggests the remaining users have high connectivity; the risk of orphaned collections is high.
2. **Better Way**: A single version with time-limited or location-based exclusive creatures achieves scarcity and trading incentives without the "price grab" perception of selling two full SKUs. This lowers the barrier to entry while retaining the social mechanic.
3. **Pre-mortem**: The publisher's uncertainty is validated if early adopters cannot find trading partners, leading to negative word-of-mouth ("incomplete game") rather than excitement. The requirement for a link cable (an extra cost) further reduces the addressable market for a declining platform.
4. **Blocker**: The proposal states the publisher is unsure the game will sell. Adding a controversial monetization tactic (two versions) and high friction (hardware dependency) on a dying platform increases the risk of total failure. There is no stated fact showing that "double SKU" performs better than "single SKU" in a declining market; historically, fragmentation accelerates abandonment in shrinking ecosystems.

The risks (declining popularity, price grab perception, hardware friction) are not merely possibilities; they are stated conditions that directly undermine the core mechanic's viability. The proposal offers no mitigation for the "orphaned player" problem other than hoping others buy the other version.

Blockers:
- Declining console popularity contradicts the need for a dense trading network.
- Publisher uncertainty combined with increased consumer friction (cost/cable) creates a high probability of commercial failure.
- No stated edge proves two versions beat one version in this specific context.

Risks:
- Perception of greed alienating the remaining loyal fanbase.
- Technical failure if link cables are scarce.

DECISION: NO-GO
