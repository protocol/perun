First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (hands-free computing) but fails to state an edge: it does not explain why this specific form factor beats existing smartphones or cameras for the stated price, especially given the admitted lack of clear everyday use.

**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to refine the product with tech enthusiasts before a mass-market release, mitigating initial social friction.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits "no clear everyday use for most people." A high price ($1,500) without a definitive utility advantage over cheaper, socially accepted alternatives (smartphones) is not a viable market entry strategy. Need is not an edge; superior utility is.
2.  **Blocker (Social/Privacy):** The risks listed (privacy objections, social acceptance) are not hypothetical future risks; they are immediate barriers to the core function (recording in public). If the device is socially unacceptable, its utility drops to zero regardless of technical specs. The proposal offers no mechanism to overcome this other than "invitation-only," which limits scale without solving the fundamental social rejection.
3.  **Alternative:** "Do nothing" or delaying until a clear "killer app" emerges is superior. Releasing a product known to lack clear everyday use invites brand damage and regulatory scrutiny without guaranteed adoption. The cheaper alternative is to license the tech to enterprise verticals (medicine, manufacturing) where utility is clear and privacy norms differ, rather than forcing a consumer use case that doesn't exist yet.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, early adopters buy it for novelty, but fail to find daily utility. Media backlash over privacy intensifies due to visible usage in public spaces. Sales stall, inventory piles up, and the brand becomes synonymous with intrusive surveillance rather than innovation.

**Conclusion:** The proposal's own facts confirm the target users (general public) have no clear use for it, and the stated risks (social acceptance, privacy) directly negate the value proposition. There is no evidence provided that the $1,500 price point delivers value exceeding the social cost. This is a solution looking for a problem.

Blockers: No stated edge over existing tools; admitted lack of everyday use; immediate social/privacy rejection risks.
Risks: Battery life, high price sensitivity.

DECISION: NO-GO
