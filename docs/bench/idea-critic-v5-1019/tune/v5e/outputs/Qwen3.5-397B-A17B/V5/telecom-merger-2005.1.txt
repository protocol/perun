First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the core assets (networks) are incompatible and the target is limited for data, meaning the buyer acquires a legacy burden without a stated edge in cost, speed, or capability to overcome the $36 billion integration hurdle.

**Steelman:** The merger secures a sticky, high-margin business niche (push-to-talk) that the buyer cannot quickly replicate, justifying the integration pain as a defensive moat against competitors stealing enterprise clients.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or a commercial partnership to resell the target's service avoids the $36 billion cash outlay and the technical nightmare of merging incompatible radio frequencies. A "cheaper path" exists: license the technology or build a bridging application rather than acquiring the entire legacy infrastructure.
2.  **Fit & Evidence:** In 2005, the industry north star is migrating to data (3G/4G). Acquiring a network explicitly "limited for data" contradicts the strategic vision. The "large goodwill" indicates paying a massive premium for intangible value, which becomes an immediate write-down risk if integration stalls.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 24 months. Cause: Integration costs spiral as engineers struggle to force incompatible technologies to interoperate, draining capital from next-gen data rollout. The legacy network becomes a money-losing anchor, and the push-to-talk customers churn due to service degradation during the migration.
4.  **Blocker:** The proposal states the technologies are "incompatible" and the target is "limited for data." There is no stated plan, cost estimate, or timeline to resolve this incompatibility, nor a stated edge showing why this specific merger is superior to a partnership. The core claim (value creation) is contradicted by the stated fact (technical incompatibility + data limitation).

**Kill Criteria:** If a partnership model can serve 80% of the target's business customers for less than 5% of the acquisition cost within 6 months, the merger thesis collapses.

**Decision:** The presence of a fatal technical mismatch and a superior, lower-cost alternative (partnership/licensing) that achieves the same customer access without the integration risk constitutes a blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
