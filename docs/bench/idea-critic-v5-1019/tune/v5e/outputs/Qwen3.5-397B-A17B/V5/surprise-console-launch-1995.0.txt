First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; launching early with no games, limited distribution, and a higher price does not beat the competitor's value proposition or satisfy consumer demand for software.

Steelman: An early May launch secures shelf space and mindshare before the September rival, betting that hardware availability outweighs software scarcity.

Attack:
1. **Options:** "Do nothing" until September allows full game readiness and price parity. The proposed "early launch" offers a bare console at a premium ($399 vs. rival's ~$299). Without a killer app, the hardware is a costly paperweight. The cheaper alternative (wait) delivers a superior product at a better price.
2. **Fit:** A gaming console's north star is playability. Launching with "few games ready" directly contradicts the core value. Developers, unprepared for the date, cannot fill the gap.
3. **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure by Christmas 1995. Cause: Retailers excluded from the limited launch ally with the competitor, blocking future distribution. Gamers buy the cheaper rival console with a robust library. The brand is tainted as "expensive and empty."
4. **Evidence:** The proposal admits the price is $100 higher and games are missing. These are not risks; they are stated facts that invalidate the value proposition. A console without games has zero utility regardless of launch date.
5. **Blockers:** The plan fails on its own stated facts. The target users (gamers) need software; the plan provides none. The channel (retailers) needs fairness; the plan excludes many. The cost ($399) exceeds the rival's without added value. These are not uncertainties; they are guaranteed failures built into the strategy.

Blockers:
- No software library at launch (stated fact: "few games ready").
- Price disadvantage ($100 higher than rival, stated fact).
- Channel alienation (stated risk: "retailers left out may retaliate").

Risks:
- Developer relations (mitigated by waiting).
- 3D programming difficulty (irrelevant if no one buys the unit).

Kill criteria are unnecessary as the plan cannot start successfully. The "early" advantage is negated by the lack of product-market fit at the moment of launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
