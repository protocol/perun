First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a core contradiction: the target asset's users dislike ads and its content is hard to sell, yet the sole monetization strategy is growing advertising on that same base. Need (young audience) without a stated edge (a viable path to revenue given these specific constraints) is a failure as proposed.

Steelman: Acquiring the platform provides immediate demographic access; independence preserves culture while the parent slowly introduces non-intrusive monetization or leverages the data elsewhere.

Attack: The plan explicitly relies on "growing advertising" on a platform where users "dislike ads" and content is "adult/hard to sell." This is not a risk; it is a stated fact that invalidates the revenue model. Past integration failures and the founder's potential exit compound the execution risk, but the revenue mismatch is the primary blocker. A cheaper alternative is "do nothing" or a smaller partnership, as the $1.1B cash outlay for an asset with "little revenue" and a broken monetization fit destroys value immediately.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition closes, ad loads increase, young users flee to the next niche platform, revenue flatlines, and the company writes down the $1.1B. This is a one-way door; cash spent cannot be recovered. The cost of delay is low compared to the certainty of overpaying for a mismatched asset.

Blockers:
1. Revenue Model Contradiction: The proposal states users dislike ads and content is unsellable, yet the plan is to grow ads. The math does not work.
2. Price vs. Reality: $1.1B cash for "little revenue" with no clear path to ROI given the stated constraints.

Risks: Founder departure, integration failure, brand toxicity. These are secondary to the fundamental economic flaw.

Kill Criteria: If the platform cannot support ad growth without user churn (as stated), the upside is zero. No pilot or metric can fix a strategy that fights the asset's core nature.

DECISION: NO-GO
