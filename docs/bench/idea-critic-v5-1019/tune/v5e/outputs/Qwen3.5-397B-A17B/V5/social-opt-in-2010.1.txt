First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it relies on existing distribution but offers no fact showing why users want social features inside an inbox or why this beats standalone networks.

Steelman: Leveraging the massive existing user base via automatic enrollment creates instant network effects, overcoming the cold-start problem that kills most social launches, while default visibility jumpstarts engagement.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** Auto-enroll all users with visible connections. Cost: High regulatory/reputation risk. Risk: Massive backlash, low retention. Reversibility: Low (exposed data cannot be un-seen).
2. **Do Nothing:** Maintain focus on search/mail core. Cost: Zero. Risk: Missing a trend. Reversibility: N/A.
3. **Opt-in Pilot:** Invite a small segment to join manually. Cost: Slower growth. Risk: Lower initial density. Reversibility: High.
The proposal fails to beat "Do Nothing" because the potential downside (regulatory action, user exodus) outweighs the unproven upside of inbox-based socializing.

Fit: Assuming the north star is user trust and utility, forcing social interaction into a private communication channel contradicts the core value proposition of email.

Pre-mortem: The launch triggers a privacy scandal; regulators investigate automatic data sharing; users flee to competitors citing trust violations; the feature becomes a ghost town because inbox usage is task-oriented, not social. This is a one-way door; once connections are exposed, trust is broken permanently. Cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a botched launch.

Evidence: No evidence is provided that users desire this integration. The risks listed (privacy, regulation, reversibility) are confirmed blockers based on the proposal's own admission. The plan assumes automatic enrollment is acceptable without proof.

Blockers:
1. **Privacy/Regulatory:** Automatic enrollment with visible connections violates reasonable user expectations for email privacy, inviting immediate regulatory scrutiny.
2. **Reversibility:** The proposal admits the launch is hard to reverse; exposing user graphs without consent causes irreversible reputational damage.
3. **No Stated Edge:** Need for social features does not equal a viable product; no fact shows this specific implementation wins against incumbents.

Risks: Low ongoing use, brand erosion.

Kill criteria are irrelevant as the plan cannot proceed. The risks identified are not merely possible negatives; they are structural flaws in the approach that guarantee failure or severe harm.

DECISION: NO-GO
