First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high cost and no apps make it inferior to existing rivals for the target demographic.

Steelman: Leveraging acquired messaging popularity to lock teens into a closed ecosystem via exclusive carrier distribution, betting that social graph density outweighs the lack of third-party apps and high data costs.

Attack:
1. **Value Mismatch:** Teenagers on social networks require diverse applications (games, media, emerging platforms). A device with *no* app store at launch directly contradicts the core behavior of the target user, regardless of pre-installed messaging.
2. **Price Barrier:** A mandatory data plan costing as much as a full smartphone plan removes the primary advantage for a price-sensitive demographic (teenagers). There is no cost incentive to switch.
3. **Strategic Fragmentation:** Building a new in-house OS instead of using the company's existing mobile platform doubles execution risk and fractures the ecosystem, wasting the opportunity to unify the user base.
4. **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** Two years of sunk development is not a reason to launch a flawed product; it is a reason to cut losses before incurring manufacturing and marketing expenses.

Alternatives:
- **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; let rivals fight over the teen segment while the company integrates the acquired messaging tech into its existing, proven mobile platform.
- **Existing Platform:** Launch the messaging phones on the company's current OS with an immediate app store, lowering development risk and time-to-market.
- **Cheaper Entry:** Negotiate a lower-cost data tier or Wi-Fi-first strategy to match teen budgets.

Blockers:
- **No App Ecosystem:** The proposal explicitly states "no application store at launch," which is fatal for "teenagers who live on social networks."
- **Prohibitive Cost:** The data plan pricing eliminates the economic case for the target demographic.
- **Platform Redundancy:** Ignoring the company's own mobile platform for a new OS creates unnecessary strategic drag.

Risks:
- Carrier exclusivity limiting reach.
- Delayed app adoption even if a store launches later.

The proposal fails the basic test of product-market fit: it offers less functionality at a higher relative cost to a demographic defined by app consumption. The sunk costs are irrelevant to future profitability.

DECISION: NO-GO
