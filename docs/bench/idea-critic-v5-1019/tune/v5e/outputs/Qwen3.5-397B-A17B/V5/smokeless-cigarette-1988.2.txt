First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "cleaner" is a claim, not a proof of superior user value or regulatory survival against incumbents.

**Steelman:** A heated tobacco product offers a genuinely cleaner experience that retains enough smokers to justify the massive sunk cost, surviving regulatory scrutiny without explicit health claims.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits smokers may dislike the taste and lighting mechanism. If the core user base rejects the sensory experience, the "cleaner" attribute is irrelevant. No evidence is provided that the target market prioritizes cleanliness over taste or simplicity.
2.  **Better Way:** "Do nothing" avoids the regulatory lightning rod of promoting a "cleaner" cigarette, which invites immediate health-claim challenges. A cheaper alternative is a limited regional pilot to validate taste acceptance before a wide launch, rather than committing to a full promotion campaign based on unverified preferences.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Regulators ban "cleaner" marketing as a deceptive health claim, while smokers reject the complex lighting and altered taste. The company loses the additional launch investment on top of the development sunk cost.
4.  **Blockers:** The proposal explicitly states users "may dislike the taste" and the product is "complicated to use." These are not risks; they are stated negatives that directly contradict the requirement for mass adoption. Without data proving these specific frictions are overcome, the plan fails on its own facts. The "very large sum" already spent is a sunk cost, not a reason to launch a product users might hate.
5.  **Kill Criteria:** The proposal offers no metric for taste acceptance or regulatory pre-clearance. Launching widely without these is reckless.

**Conclusion:** The plan relies on hope that users will accept a worse-tasting, harder-to-use product because it is "cleaner," while inviting regulatory war. The stated user objections are blockers that must be resolved via pilot data before any wide launch. As proposed, it ignores the primary drivers of consumer behavior (taste, ease) for a secondary attribute (cleanliness) that carries high legal risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
