First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state a specific edge (cost, access, timing, or evidence) that allows this console to beat established competitors and phone games. Need alone is insufficient.

**Steelman:** A crowdfunded, open-software console can reach a niche audience ignored by major players, leveraging community development to bypass traditional publisher gatekeepers.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" costs zero. Building for existing PCs or phones reaches the same open-software audience with no hardware risk, lower cost, and immediate reversibility. The proposal does not explain why dedicated hardware is required to achieve the stated goal.
2.  **Fit:** The risks listed (no marketing budget, developer hesitation, hardware delays) are not mitigated; they are merely acknowledged. In hardware, "no marketing budget" is a fatal blocker for consumer electronics competing against giants.
3.  **Evidence & Math:** The timeline (ship within a year) contradicts the admitted risk ("hardware delays are common"). No evidence suggests the team has unique supply chain access to guarantee this speed. Crowdfunding validates demand but does not solve the "chicken-and-egg" developer problem cited; backers buy hardware, but without games, the platform dies.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The project fails because production delays exhaust the crowdfunding funds before shipping, leaving backers angry and no capital for a second run. The "open platform" attracts hobbyists, not the hit games needed to retain mainstream users.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits competitors exist and offers no fact showing why this specific hardware wins against them.
*   **Resource Contradiction:** Entering a hardware market with "no marketing budget" against established console makers is factually non-viable.
*   **Timeline vs. Reality:** A one-year ship date is incompatible with the stated high probability of hardware delays for a new team.

**Risks:** Developer adoption, supply chain failure, market saturation. These are severe, but the blockers above make the plan impossible as proposed, regardless of risk mitigation.

The plan relies on hope rather than a defensible advantage. It requires a fundamental pivot to software-only or a proven hardware partner before funding is conceivable.

DECISION: NO-GO
