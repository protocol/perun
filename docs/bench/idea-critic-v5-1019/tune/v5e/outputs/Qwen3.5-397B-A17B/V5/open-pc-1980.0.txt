First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "small-computer makers growing fast" describes market demand, not a specific advantage this mainframe maker possesses to beat existing buyers' options.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, isolated team can bypass corporate inertia to rapidly deploy an open, retail-sold PC using commodity parts, capturing the emerging small-computer market before internal processes kill the opportunity.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits rivals can copy the design (standard parts), margins will be lower, and the company lacks retail experience. It offers no fact showing why *this* company, entering late with lower margins and no distribution, beats current incumbents. Need is not an edge.
2.  **Strategic Misfit:** The core business relies on high margins, vertical integration, and direct sales. This plan contradicts every proven competency, depending entirely on outside suppliers and channels where the firm has zero track record.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The project fails because the parent organization, seeing lower margins and "toy" specs, starves the separate team of capital or re-absorbs it to enforce "quality" (integration), destroying the speed advantage. Alternatively, commodity suppliers prioritize higher-volume existing partners, leaving this newcomer with component shortages.
4.  **Evidence Gap:** The claim that a "small separate team" can succeed contrary to "usual practice" is assumed. There is no evidence provided that the culture will tolerate this anomaly long enough to ship, nor that independent retailers will stock an unproven brand over established micro-computer makers.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Edge:** The proposal explicitly states disadvantages (copyable design, lower margins, no retail experience) without a countervailing fact (e.g., exclusive access to a specific processor, existing enterprise customer demand for ports) that guarantees victory. As proposed, it loses on cost and channel.

**Risks:** Cultural re-absorption, supplier deprioritization, brand dilution. These are secondary to the lack of a winning premise.

**Kill Criteria:** If the team cannot secure signed letters of intent from three major retail chains within 90 days proving channel access is viable despite no prior experience, the project stops.

The proposal relies on the hope that "moving fast" overrides structural disadvantages. Without a specific, verified advantage, this is a capital burn against entrenched competitors.

DECISION: NO-GO
