First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real friction (shipping fees) but fails to state an edge: there is no evidence that $79 covers the marginal cost of unlimited two-day shipping for heavy buyers, nor that the volume increase will offset the guaranteed margin hit.

**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee locks in loyalty and increases order frequency, smoothing cash flow and creating a switching cost that defeats competitors.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** The "heavy buyer" risk is fatal without data. If a member spends $200 on shipping but paid only $79, the retailer loses $121 plus product margins. The proposal admits economics are "unproven."
2.  **Reversibility:** Selling an annual contract creates a one-way door. If costs spiral, the retailer cannot cancel existing memberships without reputational destruction, locking in losses for up to a year.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves current margins. A "per-order cap" or "threshold-based free shipping" achieves frequency goals without uncapped liability. These cheaper alternatives reach the same upside (frequency) without the asymmetric downside.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Unit Economics:** The core claim (fee revenue > shipping costs) relies on an assumption that order frequency increases *just enough* to break even, but not enough to bankrupt the program. No data supports this balance.
*   **Asymmetric Risk:** The downside (unlimited liability per user) is fixed and known; the upside (incremental profit) is speculative.

**Risks:**
*   Customer abuse (ordering small items frequently).
*   Competitor undercutting.
*   Operational strain from volume spikes.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal lists risks but offers no kill criteria, caps, or pilot metrics. It asks for a full launch based on unverified math. A sound operator does not bet the margin on an unproven model with unlimited downside liability. The absence of a capped pilot or a verified break-even model is a blocker for a full launch.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the blocker test. The economic model is explicitly admitted as unproven, and the structural design (unlimited usage for a fixed fee) invites immediate exploitation before data can be gathered.

DECISION: NO-GO
