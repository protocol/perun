First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (adult growth) but offers no stated edge; it attempts to overcome deep brand equity ("child-centered," "value") solely through price increases and spending, which contradicts the core customer value proposition.

**Steelman:** A massive ad blitz can temporarily override brand perception, forcing adults to trial a premium burger before competitors react.
**Attack:** This relies on the unproven assumption that advertising alone can sever the link between "kids/value" and the brand. History shows that premium extensions at value chains often confuse the core base without converting the target if the underlying brand trust is rooted in affordability and family fun. The "largest budget" is a cost, not a strategic advantage; it amplifies the message but cannot fix a mismatched product-market fit.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve margin and brand clarity; risk stagnation in the adult segment.
2.  **Sub-brand or Daypart:** Test premium items during evening hours or under a distinct sub-brand to isolate adult perception without diluting the core value image.
3.  **Value-Add Premium:** Introduce higher-quality ingredients at a marginal price increase, leveraging existing trust rather than fighting it with a luxury price point.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because adults view the high price as unjustified for a "kids' place," while value-conscious regulars feel alienated, causing a net revenue drop despite high ad spend. This is a one-way door for brand reputation; once the "expensive kids' food" label sticks, it is hard to remove.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Evidence:** The proposal admits customers come for "value" and "kids' meals," yet the solution is "premium price" and "adult focus." The stated facts show the target users (adults) currently associate the brand with the opposite of the proposed value (premium).
*   **No Edge:** Spending more than the industry does not create product-market fit; it only accelerates the failure of a mismatched offer.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, core customer churn, wasted capital on unproven demand.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot in 5 markets does not achieve 15% adult mix within 90 days without decreasing total store traffic by >5%, terminate immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
