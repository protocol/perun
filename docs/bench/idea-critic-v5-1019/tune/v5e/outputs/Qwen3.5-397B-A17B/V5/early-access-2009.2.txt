First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a rough state, zero marketing budget, no publisher, and a solo developer unable to support a large audience. Need alone is not an edge; without a stated advantage over existing finished games, this fails as proposed.

**Steelman:** Releasing early creates a funded feedback loop where players co-create the final product, validating demand before full investment.

**Attack:**
1.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves reputation and allows finishing the product privately. A "cheap alternative" is releasing a free technical demo to gather feedback without the reputational risk of selling an unfinished good. The proposal beats neither; selling a broken product to a solo-dev-limited support channel invites negative reviews that permanently kill sales.
2.  **Fit:** The strategy assumes players prefer paying for bugs over playing free alternatives. In 2009, "Early Access" as a trusted model did not yet exist; the market norm was "finished or scam."
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The game launches, early buyers encounter game-breaking bugs, the solo dev cannot reply fast enough, Steam/forum reviews turn negative, and the game dies before feature completion. This is a one-way door for reputation.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the developer "cannot support a large audience" yet plans a public sale. These facts contradict. Selling implies support obligations the proposer explicitly lacks.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Capacity:** The plan requires handling public sales and feedback, but the stated fact is the developer "cannot support a large audience."
*   **Missing Edge:** No stated reason why buyers would choose an unfinished, unsupported game over finished competitors, especially with zero marketing to explain the vision.
*   **Market Timing:** In 2009, selling unfinished games lacked the cultural framework to succeed; buyers viewed it as defective, not collaborative.

**Risks:** Negative review spiral, burnout from support demands, revenue failure. These are not mere risks; given the admitted lack of support capacity, they are probable outcomes based on the proposal's own facts.

**Kill Criteria:** If a free demo does not generate organic interest without sales friction, do not sell. If support tickets exceed 5 per week, halt sales.

The proposal asks to incur maximum reputational risk with minimum capacity to mitigate it.

DECISION: NO-GO
