First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it admits no large paying market exists and relies on niche academic experimentation while raising costs on every unit in a fierce core rivalry.

Steelman: Embedding general-purpose compute capabilities now creates an insurmountable moat by the time scientific demand matures, turning every consumer card into a supercomputer node.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Protect margins and win the core gaming race; let others fund early R&D.
2. Wait for signal: Add features only after enterprise customers commit capital.
3. Proposed: Subsidize unproven demand immediately, bleeding margin on mass-market volume.

Fit: This contradicts the immediate survival need to win the core graphics war. Without a current revenue stream to offset the added unit cost, this dilutes resources against the main rival.

Pre-mortem: The program burns cash for years; the core gaming business loses share due to distracted engineering and higher prices; the scientific market remains too small to matter; shareholders revolt over missed earnings.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits "no large paying market" and "niche demand." Costs are immediate and universal; benefits are hypothetical and distant.

Blockers:
1. Economics: Raising unit cost on a commodity product without committed customers destroys margin in a price-sensitive market.
2. Strategy: Engineering distraction during a "core race" with a rival risks losing the primary revenue engine that funds all future R&D.
3. Demand: Building a full toolchain for users who currently "hack APIs" assumes adoption without a killer app or paying anchor tenant.

Risks: Execution complexity, compiler adoption failure, opportunity cost of lost gaming features.

Kill criteria check: The proposal offers no pilot, no cap, and no metric to validate demand before full commitment. It bets the company on a future that may not arrive, while jeopardizing the present. A sound operator protects the core franchise first; this plan mortgages the core for a speculative side-bet with no near-term payoff. The absence of a paying market is not a risk to manage; it is a blocker to proceeding at this scale.

DECISION: NO-GO
