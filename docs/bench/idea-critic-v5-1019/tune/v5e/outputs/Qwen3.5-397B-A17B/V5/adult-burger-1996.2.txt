First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it attempts to overcome deep-seated brand perception and price sensitivity solely through spending, without a product or positioning advantage that beats existing premium competitors.

Steelman: A massive ad blitz can temporarily override brand associations, forcing adults to trial a higher-priced burger before they realize the mismatch.

Attack: The plan ignores the "value" core of the current customer base while failing to offer a credible reason for adults to accept a premium price from a "kids' brand." The risks cited are not mere possibilities but structural realities of the brand's current equity. Heavy spending before demand validation violates basic unit economics; if the product fails to resonate due to brand mismatch, the largest budget in history only accelerates cash burn.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve margins and brand clarity; zero risk of alienating core value shoppers.
2. Sub-branding: Launch a distinct concept or separate storefront to test premium offerings without diluting the main brand's value proposition.
3. Incremental test: Introduce a limited-time adult offer in select markets with minimal spend to validate demand before scaling.

Fit: This contradicts the "value" and "child-centered" north star. It assumes advertising can fix a product-market fit gap, which is an unproven assumption.

Pre-mortem: The campaign launches, awareness spikes, but trial is low because adults distrust the quality/price ratio. Core customers feel abandoned by the price hike or brand shift. The company burns its largest-ever marketing budget with negative ROI, damaging the stock price and brand equity simultaneously. This is a one-way door for reputation; once the brand tries and fails at "premium," it becomes a joke.

Evidence: The proposal admits customers come for "kids' meals and value." There is no evidence provided that the target demographic wants premium food from this specific chain.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: The plan relies entirely on spend, not a superior product or unique positioning.
2. Contradictory facts: The proposal states adults see the chain as "fast, not premium," yet offers no mechanism to change this perception other than "spend more."
3. Financial suicide: Committing the "largest budget" before validating demand is a blocker for any sound operator.

Risks: Brand dilution, core customer churn, massive financial loss.

DECISION: NO-GO
