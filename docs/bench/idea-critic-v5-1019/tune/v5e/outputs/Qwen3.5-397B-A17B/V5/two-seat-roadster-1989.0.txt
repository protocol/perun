First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a market gap (disappeared roadsters) but fails to state an edge explaining why this specific plan beats the status quo or existing alternatives; need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: A lightweight, affordable RWD convertible can revive a dormant niche by prioritizing driving purity over luxury, appealing to enthusiasts ignored by mass-market safety bloat.

Attack:
1. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" costs zero and avoids inventory risk. A cheaper alternative is a limited-run special edition on an existing chassis, reducing tooling costs and proving demand before a dedicated launch. The proposal does not explain why a full new launch beats these safer options.
2. **Fit:** The company lacks sports-car heritage. Entering a shrinking segment without brand equity or a stated distribution advantage (e.g., exclusive clubs, track days) relies on hope, not strategy.
3. **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in Year 2. Cause: Early enthusiasts bought the first batch, but the "two-seat limit" prevented fleet or secondary market adoption, and rising safety compliance costs erased margins on low volume. The "shrinking segment" trend was structural, not cyclical.
4. **Evidence:** The proposal admits the segment has shrunk for years and sales may fade after early adopters. These are stated facts acting as blockers: a declining total addressable market combined with a product that alienates the broader buyer base (two seats) creates a negative unit economics trajectory.
5. **Blockers:** The stated fact that "sales may fade once early enthusiasts have bought" indicates a lack of sustainable demand. Without a plan to expand the buyer pool beyond this finite group or a verified cost advantage to survive low volume, the core claim of viability fails. The safety cost risk is acknowledged but not quantified or mitigated.

Risks (non-blocking but severe): Regulatory changes banning convertibles; supply chain issues for low-volume parts; brand dilution if quality slips.

Kill criteria check: The proposal offers no metric to validate demand before full launch, no cap on investment, and no pivot trigger. It asks for approval based on a nostalgic gap in a dying market without a defensible economic engine.

DECISION: NO-GO
