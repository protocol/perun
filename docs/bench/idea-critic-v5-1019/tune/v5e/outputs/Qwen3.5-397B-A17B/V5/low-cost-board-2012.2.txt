First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a need but fails to state an edge; "low cost" is insufficient when buyers must procure additional peripherals (display, keyboard, power), likely exceeding the total cost of existing cheap laptops or tablets.

**Steelman:** A $35 bare-board computer creates an accessible, hackable entry point for education that integrated consumer devices cannot match, relying on scavenged peripherals to keep total cost minimal.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** Ordinary laptops and tablets are cited as "already cheap." A complete used laptop often costs less than $35 + peripherals + assembly effort. The "do nothing" option (using existing cheap hardware) offers a superior user experience (integrated screen, battery, keyboard) for a comparable total price. The proposal does not explain why a fragmented setup beats a complete one.
2.  **Capability Gap:** The charity lacks manufacturing and support experience. Distributing hardware requires supply chain management, quality control, and warranty handling. Without this expertise, the risk of project failure or reputational damage is high.
3.  **Supply Risk:** A "small first batch" that could "sell out" indicates an inability to meet demand, frustrating the core mission of widespread adoption. If it sells out, schools cannot get units; if it doesn't, the project stalls.
4.  **Hidden Costs:** The requirement for users to source their own power and display creates a significant barrier to entry for schools, which prefer turnkey solutions. The "low cost" claim ignores the total cost of ownership and setup time.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits competitors (laptops/tablets) are cheap but offers no fact showing why a $35 board + extra costs beats them for the target user. Need is not an edge.
*   **Capability Mismatch:** The organization explicitly lacks the experience required to execute the plan (manufacturing/support).

**Risks:** Supply shortages, user friction due to missing peripherals, lack of support infrastructure. These are secondary to the lack of a competitive edge and operational capacity.

The proposal relies on the hope that "cheap" alone drives adoption, ignoring that "complete and easy" usually wins in education. Without a demonstrated advantage over existing cheap laptops or a plan to address the lack of manufacturing experience, the project is likely to fail or underdeliver.

DECISION: NO-GO
