First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits high licensing costs, poor quality, limited broadband, a weak catalog, and imminent giant competitors while threatening the core profitable DVD revenue with no stated edge to offset these deficits.

**Steelman:** Launch streaming immediately as a free retention shield to gather data and habituate users before giants lock the market, accepting short-term margin compression.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Edge:** The proposal explicitly states the service is inferior (poor quality, old titles) and costlier (high licensing) than what future entrants will offer. Need is not an edge; being worse than the coming standard is a liability.
2.  **Cannibalization without Upside:** Free access to an inferior product invites users to cancel the high-margin DVD service for a low-value digital experience, accelerating revenue collapse without securing loyalty against better future options.
3.  **Capital Misallocation:** Paying premium prices for "mostly older titles" burns cash on assets with no defensive moat against deep-pocketed tech rivals.
4.  **Timing Mismatch:** With broadband not universal, the addressable market is too small to justify the fixed costs of licensing and infrastructure in 2007.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Protect the $7M subscriber profit pool until quality/cost parity is achievable.
*   **Paid Add-on:** Test demand and offset licensing costs without encouraging DVD cancellation.
*   **Delay:** Wait for broadband penetration and licensing costs to mature.

**Pre-mortem:** The service launches, subscribers downgrade from DVD to the free stream, churn increases as users sample the poor quality then leave for competitors, and the company bleeds cash on licenses it cannot monetize.

**Blocker:** The proposal's own facts confirm the unit economics are negative (high cost, low value) and the strategic position is defensive without a weapon. A plan that admits it is inferior, expensive, and cannibalistic, with no mechanism to prevent revenue loss, fails the basic test of capital preservation. The lack of a "stated edge" in the proposal is fatal.

**Risks:** All listed risks (licensing, cannibalization, tech cost, competition) are confirmed by the proposal's own admissions, elevating them from risks to blockers.

DECISION: NO-GO
