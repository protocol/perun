First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "leading a new category" is an ambition, not a competitive advantage over existing solutions or the status quo, especially given the admitted unreliability of the core mechanism.

**Steelman:** A $700 pen-based device with heavy CEO publicity can define a new market despite current technical flaws and size issues, betting that first-mover branding outweighs functional deficits.

**Attack:**
1.  **Core Mechanism Failure:** The primary input method (handwriting recognition) is explicitly "unreliable in testing." Shipping a computer where the main interface fails is a fundamental product defect, not a risk to be managed post-launch.
2.  **Value Mismatch:** At $700 (a premium price point in 1993), users expect reliability. The combination of high cost, large form factor ("too big for a pocket"), and broken core functionality offers no compelling reason for adoption over cheaper, proven alternatives (paper, laptops).
3.  **Ecosystem Void:** "Little software" and an "unproven category" mean there is no immediate utility to justify the hardware investment. Heavy publicity cannot sustain a product that does not work as advertised; it may only accelerate reputational damage.
4.  **No Alternative Path:** The proposal ignores the "cheaper, smaller, staged" alternative: fix the recognition engine and reduce the form factor before a full category launch.

**Blockers:**
*   **Functional Blocker:** The core value proposition (handwriting input) is admitted to be unreliable. A computer that cannot reliably accept input fails its primary function.
*   **Economic Blocker:** The price ($700) is high relative to the delivered value (unreliable software, few apps, poor portability).

**Risks:** Market rejection, brand damage from hyping a broken feature, inventory write-downs. These are secondary to the functional blocker.

**Conclusion:** The proposal asks to launch a broken product at a premium price based on hype rather than utility. The unreliability of the main input is a definitive failure of the device's purpose.

DECISION: NO-GO
