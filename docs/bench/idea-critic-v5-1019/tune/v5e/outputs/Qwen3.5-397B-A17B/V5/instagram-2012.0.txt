First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: paying $1B for zero revenue and no business model does not beat building or waiting, as "threat" alone is not an economic advantage.

**Steelman:** Buying the only credible mobile photo threat at scale prevents a competitor from acquiring it and buying time to fix our weak mobile product.

**Attack:**
1.  **Options:** "Do nothing" costs less than $1B. Building a competitor internally leverages existing infrastructure for a fraction of the price. The "cheaper alternative" (internal build or smaller acqui-hire) reaches the same upside (mobile presence) without the massive premium for unproven monetization.
2.  **Fit:** While it addresses the mobile gap, the cost diverts capital from core product fixes. The strategy relies on the assumption that this specific app is the *only* path to mobile relevance, which is unproven.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure mode: Integration destroys the very culture driving growth, users flee due to privacy changes, and regulators block the deal as anti-competitive, leaving us with wasted legal fees and a demoralized team.
4.  **Evidence:** The valuation is purely speculative based on user count, not unit economics. There is no evidence these users generate value or can be monetized at a rate justifying a $1B exit. The "threat" is qualitative, not quantitative.
5.  **Blocker Test:** The core claim is that this purchase is necessary to secure mobile dominance. However, the absence of a business model and the existence of cheaper alternatives (internal development) means the plan fails as proposed. The price assumes future success that is not guaranteed, while the cost is immediate and massive. The risk of regulatory blocking is a tangible fact given the buyer's dominance and the target's market position.

**Blockers:**
*   No business model or revenue to justify $1B valuation; price relies entirely on speculation.
*   Cheaper alternatives (internal build) exist to solve the "weak mobile product" problem.
*   High probability of regulatory scrutiny blocking the deal entirely.

**Risks:** Culture clash, user churn, integration failure.

The proposal asks to spend $1B on hope rather than evidence, ignoring cheaper paths and regulatory reality.

DECISION: NO-GO
