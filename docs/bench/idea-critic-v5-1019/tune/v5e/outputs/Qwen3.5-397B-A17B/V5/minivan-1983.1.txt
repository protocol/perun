First call: GO. The stated edge is a converted factory and existing compact-car platform, which drastically reduces capital expenditure and time-to-market compared to rivals building from scratch, addressing the "little cash" constraint while delivering a unique "garage-fit" vehicle families cannot currently buy.

Steelman: Leveraging distressed assets (factory) and proven technology (compact platform) to create a new "minivan" category that offers car-like handling for families, solving the cash constraint through reuse rather than new investment.

Options analysis:
1. **Do nothing:** Company remains cash-poor with no growth vector; eventual collapse likely despite loan.
2. **Build new platform:** Impossible due to cash constraints; high risk of failure.
3. **Proposed plan:** Low capital cost, high differentiation, creates a new market. It beats "do nothing" by offering a path to profitability without massive upfront spend.

Fit: Aligns with survival (stage) and innovation (vision). Reusing assets fits the "saved from collapse" context.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if buyers strictly view it as a commercial van. However, the "station wagon" buying habit suggests families want utility without truck-like driving. The risk of rivals copying is mitigated by the first-mover advantage in a new category and the specific use of a converted factory which competitors may not have access to immediately. The space limitation is a trade-off for garage compatibility, which is the core value proposition.

Evidence: The proposal states the company has an existing compact platform and a convertible factory. These are hard assets. The risk of "no category exists" is actually an opportunity for definition, not a blocker, provided the product drives like a car (stated feature).

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (no category, cash, perception, copying, space) are either mitigated by the proposal's specific mechanics (reuse solves cash; garage fit solves space trade-off) or are standard market risks that do not prevent execution. The lack of a category is the very reason the product will succeed if executed well.

Risks: Buyer perception (commercial vs. family), competitor response. These become kill criteria: monitor family vs. commercial sales mix in Q1; if >50% commercial, pivot marketing immediately.

DECISION: GO
