First call: NO-GO. The stated price ($7.4B) against a one-film-per-year output creates an untenable valuation multiple without a stated mechanism to drastically increase throughput or monetization.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio is the only way to prevent a rival from securing its talent and to instantly revitalize the conglomerate's failing hand-drawn unit by installing proven creative leadership.

**Attack:**
1.  **Valuation Blocker:** At ~$7.4B for one film annually, the deal implies a valuation per film far exceeding historical box-office norms. The proposal offers no evidence of a pipeline expansion to justify this multiple. A single production bottleneck makes the asset incapable of servicing the debt-equity risk assumed.
2.  **Integration Risk as Fact:** The "culture clash" and "dependence on few leaders" are not mere risks; they are structural facts confirmed by the breakdown in partnership talks over economics. Placing the studio's leaders in charge of the conglomerate's unit does not solve the dependence; it concentrates it. If those leaders depart, the $7.4B investment yields zero creative output.
3.  **Shareholder Conflict:** The founder becoming a major shareholder of the buyer creates a governance conflict where one individual can block strategic shifts or demand outsized influence, effectively holding the conglomerate hostage.
4.  **Alternative:** The "do nothing" or "re-negotiate distribution only" options preserve capital. The conglomerate's current underperformance suggests a systemic issue that cannot be fixed solely by importing a studio with a limited release schedule.

**Blockers:**
*   **Math Failure:** The revenue potential of one film per year cannot mathematically support a $7.4B purchase price based on standard industry multiples.
*   **Concentration:** The entire thesis rests on a few individuals who have already shown friction with the buyer.

**Risks:** Culture integration, retention of key talent, dilution of existing shareholders.

**Kill Criteria:** If the studio cannot commit to a minimum of two films per year within 24 months, or if the creative leaders do not sign long-term retention agreements, the deal destroys value.

The proposal fails the basic economic test of capacity vs. price.

DECISION: NO-GO
