First call: NO-GO. The proposal offers no stated edge over the current market position; it relies on aesthetic modernization while explicitly removing the brand's primary shelf cue (the orange with a straw) for a loyal, older demographic.

Steelman: A minimalist redesign with a functional cap shape will signal premium quality and attract new buyers without alienating the core base due to the strength of the advertising campaign.

Attack: The plan fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests against its own stated facts. The proposal admits the old image is the "main shelf cue" and the buyers are "older." Removing the primary recognition trigger for a demographic that relies on established visual habits is a direct contradiction of the goal to maintain sales. A "large advertising campaign" is a cost, not a guarantee of re-education; it does not overcome the immediate failure of shelf recognition in the critical split-second decision window. The "vertically rotated brand name" further reduces readability for older eyes.

Alternatives: "Do nothing" preserves the recognized cue. A "hybrid" approach (keeping the orange/straw but updating the cap) captures the functional benefit without the recognition risk. The proposed "all stores at once" launch maximizes the blast radius of a potential failure, making it a one-way door with high irreversibility once printed.

Pre-mortem: Sales plummet because loyal shoppers walk past the unrecognizable cartons, assuming the brand is out of stock or has been discontinued. The advertising spend fails to retrain behavior fast enough to offset the immediate loss of impulse buys.

Blockers: The proposal's own evidence states the old image is the main shelf cue and the base is older. Removing the cue for this specific demographic creates a fundamental barrier to purchase that the plan does not solve, only hopes to overwhelm with ad spend. This is a factual contradiction, not a mere risk.

Risks: High cost of reprinting, potential alienation of loyalists, competitor exploitation of confusion.

Kill Criteria: If a limited market test shows a >5% drop in repeat purchase rate within 4 weeks, halt the rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
