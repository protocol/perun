First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a stated edge (mass distribution and advertising) that directly attacks the asset's core value (cult image and independent distributor loyalty), creating a self-defeating strategy without a proven mechanism to preserve the brand's premium status during the transition.

Steelman: The acquisition captures high-growth tea demand and leverages existing scale to dominate shelf space, assuming the brand's loyalty is robust enough to survive mass-market dilution.

Attack: The plan explicitly intends to replace the "small, quirky" distributor network with a supermarket-focused model and apply mass-market advertising. In 1994, premium tea loyalty is often tied to exclusivity and niche availability. Moving to mass channels risks alienating the core consumer base that drove the "strong growth" and "strong loyalty." Furthermore, independent distributors may defect or reduce effort if displaced, causing immediate revenue leakage before mass channels ramp up. The proposal admits these risks but offers no specific mitigation strategy other than the assumption that the sports drink playbook applies universally.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids $1.7B cash outflow and brand destruction risk; forfeits growth.
2. Hybrid distribution: Maintain independent networks for core SKUs while testing supermarket expansion; preserves cult status while gathering data.
3. Competitor organic launch: Build a tea brand internally using existing distribution; slower but avoids integration risk.

Fit: While growth aligns with a beverage giant's vision, the method contradicts the "premium" nature of the target.

Pre-mortem: One year post-acquisition, sales flatten as core fans abandon the brand due to over-exposure and perceived "sell-out" marketing, while new mass consumers reject the price point. The unique distributor relationships dissolve, leaving a gap in coverage.

Evidence: The proposal itself states the risks of distributor loyalty and image survival but proceeds with a plan that triggers exactly those failures. No evidence suggests the sports drink's mass-market success translates to a premium tea context where perception drives value.

Blockers: The plan's core mechanism (mass distribution/ads) contradicts the source of the target's value (cult image/niche distribution), and no bridge strategy is offered. This is a fundamental strategic mismatch, not just an execution risk.

Risks: Competition from soft-drink giants, price sensitivity, integration friction.

DECISION: NO-GO
