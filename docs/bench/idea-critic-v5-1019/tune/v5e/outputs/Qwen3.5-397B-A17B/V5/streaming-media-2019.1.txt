First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (direct distribution) but fails to state an edge: being cheaper and later than a incumbent with 100M+ subscribers is not a defensible advantage, it is a disadvantage.

**Steelman:** Withdrawing content to launch a lower-priced service will capture enough market share quickly to offset lost licensing revenue and justify multi-year losses.

**Attack:**
1.  **Options:** "Do nothing" (collect licensing fees, zero capex) beats this plan unless the new service reaches massive scale immediately. A cheaper, staged alternative (licensing windows + niche app) reaches similar upside with less burn but is ignored.
2.  **Fit:** Assuming the goal is maximizing long-term value, destroying current profitable revenue streams for uncertain future gains contradicts fiduciary duty without a proven acquisition engine.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The platform fails technically (no prior experience), subscriber growth lags due to late entry, and the cash burn triggers a credit downgrade before break-even.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the company has "never run" such a platform and relies on "targets" rather than verified unit economics. The leading competitor's 100M+ headstart creates a network effect and content moat that a "low price" alone rarely breaches.
5.  **Blocker Test:** The core claim relies on hitting "tens of millions" of subscribers to justify the loss of licensing revenue. The proposal provides no evidence that a late, cheaper entrant can achieve this velocity against a dominant incumbent. The stated fact (no tech experience) combined with the stated risk (execution failure) creates a high probability of burning cash without reaching the required scale. The loss of licensing revenue is immediate and certain; the subscriber growth is hypothetical. This asymmetry blocks the plan as proposed.

**Verdict:** The plan substitutes hope for an edge. Without a verified mechanism to acquire users faster than the cash burn destroys value, and lacking the technical foundation to deliver the product, the proposal fails the operator test.

DECISION: NO-GO
