First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; renaming and gifting do not address the verified lack of demand shown in the four-city test.

Steelman: Free sampling to a targeted office audience will validate latent utility that general retail testing missed, using verbal intent as a proxy for purchase.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** Rename + free samples + survey. Cost: medium. Risk: high (false positives from "free" bias). Reversibility: low (cost sunk).
2. **Do Nothing:** Halt rollout. Cost: zero. Risk: missed opportunity. Reversibility: high.
3. **Paid Pilot:** Sell units in the same offices at a discount. Cost: low. Risk: true demand signal. Reversibility: high.

Fit: Assumes the goal is profitable national sales. A strategy based on "most recipients say they would buy" conflicts with the north star of actual revenue, as verbal intent from free recipients is a vacuous metric that historically overpredicts sales.

Pre-mortem: The rollout fails because the "intent" data was inflated by the reciprocity of receiving a free gift. The company spends a large marketing budget on a product users politely accepted but do not need. This is a two-way door for the sample phase, but the national rollout is a one-way door with high capital loss. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a failed national launch.

Evidence: The only hard evidence is the disappointing sales in four cities. The proposal offers no corrected figures or new data, only a change in name and distribution method. The claim that verbal intent predicts national sales is unsupported.

Blockers:
1. **Vacuous Metric:** The success criterion ("most recipients say they would buy") is not a leading metric for sales. Free samples generate polite affirmation, not purchase commitment. This removes the route to valid upside.
2. **No Edge:** Renaming does not solve the core mechanism failure (lack of perceived need) identified in the first test.

Risks: Office workers still see no need; marketing budget wasted.

Kill Criteria: None exist in the proposal to stop the national rollout if the sample survey is positive but actual conversion is zero.

DECISION: NO-GO
