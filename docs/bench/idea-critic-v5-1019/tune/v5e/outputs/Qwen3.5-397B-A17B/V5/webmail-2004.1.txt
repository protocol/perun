First call: GO. The stated edge is a 200x storage advantage (1GB vs. few MBs) combined with fast search, directly solving the user pain point of routine deletion. This creates a defensible wedge against incumbents who rely on scarcity.

**Steelman:** A search company leverages its core competency (indexing) to offer unlimited utility (storage + search) funded by high-intent contextual ads, disrupting a complacent market where users are conditioned to delete data.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Cost vs. Value:** While 1GB/user seems prohibitive in 2004, storage costs are falling exponentially. The value of indexed user data for ad targeting likely outweighs the marginal hardware cost, provided the ad matching engine is efficient.
2.  **Privacy Objection:** Scanning mail for ads is a genuine risk. However, users already trade privacy for free services. The counter-argument is utility: the search capability requires indexing anyway; monetizing that index subsidizes the storage.
3.  **Market Maturity:** Entrenched incumbents are vulnerable because they are optimizing for cost-cutting (limiting storage) rather than user experience. Their business models rely on users accepting limitations. Removing the limitation changes the competitive landscape from "feature parity" to "category redefinition."
4.  **Revenue Delay:** Invitation-only growth controls server load while validating the ad model. Direct revenue isn't needed at launch if the user acquisition cost is near zero (viral invites) and the lifetime value via ads is high.

**Blockers:** None. The proposal's own facts (falling storage costs, user deletion behavior, search competency) support the thesis. The risks (privacy, cost) are manageable trade-offs for the upside, not factual contradictions. The "no direct revenue" is a strategic choice for growth, not a flaw in the unit economics.

**Kill Criteria:** If the cost per user exceeds the projected ad revenue per user within 18 months, or if privacy backlash causes a churn rate >5% monthly post-launch, the model fails.

**Verdict:** The proposal identifies a clear inefficiency in the market (artificial scarcity) and applies a unique asset (search/ads) to solve it. The risks are standard for disruptive plays and do not invalidate the core logic.

DECISION: GO
