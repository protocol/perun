First call: NO-GO. The stated price ($700) and the requirement for licensee hardware profit create an immediate structural failure against competitors selling at a loss.

**Steelman:** Licensing shifts manufacturing risk to partners while securing royalty streams, leveraging their scale to penetrate the market faster than a single builder could.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $700, the console is 3–4x the cost of established rivals. Early adopters pay premiums for exclusivity, not just "high-end graphics" which are unproven. The proposal admits the price is "far above" existing units but offers no evidence that the performance gap justifies a 300% premium.
2.  **Incentive Misalignment:** Competitors subsidize hardware to maximize game sales (the real profit pool). This model forces licensees to earn profit *on the box*. To hit $700 *and* make a margin, the Bill of Materials must be incredibly low, likely compromising the "high-end" claim, or the retail price becomes prohibitive. Licensees will hesitate to build an expensive unit that undercuts their own potential software margins if they don't control the platform fees.
3.  **Market Friction:** Entering a crowded market with a premium-priced, licensed hardware strategy adds friction. Manufacturers prefer controlling their own destiny; asking them to build a loss-leading commodity is standard, but asking them to build a high-cost niche product for a startup's royalty scheme is a hard sell without a proven hit game lineup.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Contradiction:** The plan requires high retail price ($700) + licensee hardware profit in a market where winners sell at a loss. This math does not work unless the "high-end graphics" create a monopoly, which is unproven.
*   **Adoption Ceiling:** The price point restricts the install base so severely that game developers may not port titles, killing the royalty stream from software.

**Risks:**
*   Licensees abandon the project if initial sales lag.
*   Competitors drop prices further, widening the gap.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   If pre-orders from licensees do not cover tooling costs within 6 months, stop.
*   If attach rate (games per console) is <3 at launch, the royalty model fails.

The proposal fails the basic unit economics test against the stated competitive landscape.

DECISION: NO-GO
