First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: while data exists, the plan does not explain how this specific data proves users will pay for *original* prestige drama versus licensed content, nor why a straight-to-series order beats the industry-standard pilot model for an unproven format.

Steelman: The service's granular viewing data uniquely identifies a hungry audience for this specific genre/talent combination, rendering a pilot redundant and allowing a first-mover advantage in binge-watching to lock in subscribers before competitors react.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Continue licensing; costs rise, margin shrinks, no differentiation.
2. **Pilot first:** Commission one episode to test audience reception and production quality; lower upfront cost, preserves capital, validates the "no pilot" hypothesis on a smaller scale.
3. **Proposed plan:** 26 episodes, no pilot, huge budget. Highest risk, highest potential reward, but unproven execution model.

Fit: Assuming the north star is subscriber retention and differentiation, this fits strategically but fails tactically due to the absence of a validation step for a completely new operational model (original production + binge release).

Pre-mortem: The show is expensive but misses the cultural mark because the data measured *consumption of existing tropes*, not *demand for new originals*. Without a pilot, production flaws go unnoticed until 26 episodes are banked, wasting the majority of the content budget. The "all-at-once" release confuses users accustomed to weekly anticipation, leading to short-term spikes but poor long-term retention.

Evidence: The proposal cites "viewing data" but offers no recomputation linking specific metrics to the viability of a 26-episode order. The claim that "no pilot" is safer is an assumption contradicted by industry norms where pilots catch fatal flaws.

Blockers:
- **Missing Edge:** No factual link provided between current viewing data and the specific risk of a straight-to-series, 26-episode order. Data showing people watch movies does not prove they will subscribe for a specific original series.
- **Alternative Superiority:** A pilot achieves the same strategic goal (testing the original content hypothesis) at a fraction of the cost and risk. The proposed scale (2 seasons) is unjustified without the pilot's validation.

Risks: Audience rejection, production quality issues, budget overrun, confusion over release model. These are compounded by the lack of a pilot.

Kill Criteria: None proposed. A go requires a pilot or a capped single-season order with clear metrics.

DECISION: NO-GO
