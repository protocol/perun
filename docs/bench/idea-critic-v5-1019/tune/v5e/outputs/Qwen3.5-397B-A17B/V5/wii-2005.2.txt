First call: GO. The stated edge is a distinct blue-ocean strategy: targeting non-gamers with a low-cost, motion-controlled device while competitors fight a saturated high-definition arms race. This avoids direct competition on specs where the proposer is third place.

**Steelman:** A dedicated motion-control console can unlock a massive, underserved casual market by lowering price and complexity, creating a new revenue stream independent of the hardcore graphics race.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Continue fighting for hardcore market share against superior hardware; likely status quo or decline.
*   **High-Definition Route:** Attempt to match rivals; high cost, high risk, low probability of overtaking leaders.
*   **The Proposal:** High risk of "toy" perception and third-party abandonment. However, the "casual" demographic is untested but potentially vast. The thin margin is offset by volume potential and first-mover advantage in motion control.

**Fit:** Aligns with the need to differentiate. A sound idea moving the north star of market expansion, even if it alienates the core base temporarily.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the "casual" audience proves unwilling to buy dedicated hardware for simple games, or if the motion controller is deemed a gimmick. The one-way door is the hardware manufacturing commitment; the two-way door is software support, which can pivot.

**Evidence:** No external data available; relying on the proposal's internal logic that the "graphics race" has left a gap for affordability and novelty. The skepticism of hardcore gamers is a feature, not a bug, as it signals a departure from the status quo.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks (perception, third-party support) are valid but constitute *risks*, not *blockers*. They are inherent to entering a new market segment. The proposal acknowledges them. No fact presented proves the casual market does not exist or that the price point is unsustainable.

**Risks:** Limited third-party support, "toy" perception, untested audience. These become kill criteria: monitor attach rates and software sales in the first quarter. If casual adoption fails to materialize by Q2 2007, pivot or halt production expansion.

The plan beats "do nothing" by attempting to create a new market rather than losing the existing one. The lack of HD is a cost-saving feature for the target demographic, not a deficit.

DECISION: GO
