First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; being "larger" or "later" with a broken ecosystem promise is not an advantage against a dominant incumbent with loyal users.

**Steelman:** A late entrant can win by offering superior hardware and a seamless wireless ecosystem, betting that consumers value convenience over the existing copy-protection standard.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Incentives:** The plan explicitly drops the copy-protection standard previously promoted to partners. This alienates the very label partners required to stock the store, creating a supply chokehold before launch.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A "cheaper alternative" would be licensing the existing dominant player's software rather than building a conflicting hardware/software stack from scratch.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. Cause: Labels restrict sharing due to lack of DRM, leaving the store empty. The "large marketing budget" cannot fix a content vacuum.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the leader has an "enormous share," "loyal ecosystem," and the entrant is "late." No fact suggests users will switch for a screen size increase alone.
5.  **Blocker:** The core mechanism (wireless sharing) conflicts with the stated risk that "labels may restrict sharing." Without a signed agreement overcoming this, the feature set is hypothetical. The proposal offers no evidence such agreements exist.
6.  **Kill Criteria:** The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test. It incurs high build costs and partner wrath without a verified route to content or a price/feature edge strong enough to break network effects.

**Verdict:**
*   **Blockers:** No content licensing strategy to replace the dropped DRM; no stated user advantage sufficient to overcome network effects.
*   **Risks:** Label retaliation, empty store, wasted marketing spend.

This is a high-cost gamble with a broken supply chain premise. The absence of a pilot or metric does not save it; the fundamental contradiction between dropping DRM and securing labels is fatal as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
