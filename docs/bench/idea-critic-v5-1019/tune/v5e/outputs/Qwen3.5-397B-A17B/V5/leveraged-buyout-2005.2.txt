First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on stable cash flows to service heavy debt, but the stated facts explicitly highlight seasonality, growing competition, and "little room for error," creating a fundamental mismatch between the financing structure and the business reality.

**Steelman:** The premium is so substantial that it outweighs the risks of leverage, allowing the consortium to restructure operations away from public scrutiny before a potential downturn hits.

**Attack:** The steelman collapses under the specific risks cited. Financing a purchase with "heavy debt" requires consistent, predictable cash flow to meet interest obligations. The proposal admits sales are "seasonal," meaning cash flow is lumpy, increasing the risk of missing payments during off-peak times. Furthermore, "growing competition" from discount and online sellers threatens the very revenue base needed to service that debt. If a "weak holiday season" occurs—as explicitly feared—the company could default immediately. The statement "little room for error" is a disqualifier for a highly leveraged buyout; LBOs require a safety margin to absorb market shocks. Accepting this offer as proposed ignores the mechanical incompatibility between the debt load and the operational volatility described.

**Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves the company's ability to invest in online sales and store updates, which the debt-heavy plan would restrict. A counter-proposal seeking a lower leverage ratio or a higher equity contribution from the buyers would mitigate the default risk, but the current plan offers no such buffer.

**Pre-mortem:** The company defaults within 18 months after two consecutive weak holiday seasons force asset sales to meet interest payments, leaving stores underinvested and unable to compete with online rivals.

**Blockers:** The explicit admission of "little room for error" combined with "heavy debt" and "seasonal sales" creates a factual contradiction: the business cannot safely support the proposed financing structure. The risks are not hypothetical; they are stated as current conditions.

**Risks:** Competition intensifying, interest rates rising, holiday sales missing targets.

**Kill Criteria:** If projected free cash flow in a downside holiday scenario does not cover 1.5x interest payments, the deal must be rejected or restructured.

DECISION: NO-GO
