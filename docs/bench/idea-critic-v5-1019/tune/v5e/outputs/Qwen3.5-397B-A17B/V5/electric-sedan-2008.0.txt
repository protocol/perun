First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "need" for electric cars is not an advantage over existing buyers' options, and the plan admits fatal capital and experience gaps.

Steelman: Committing exclusively to EVs in a purchased large factory forces scale to overcome inexperience.
Attack: This ignores the 2008 reality where capital markets froze, battery costs were prohibitive, and charging infrastructure was nonexistent. Buying a large factory increases fixed costs before product-market fit is proven, directly contradicting the constraint of limited capital.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Preserve cash, wait for infrastructure maturity.
2. Retrofit existing capacity: Lease smaller lines to reduce upfront capital risk.
3. Proposed plan: Maximum capital exposure with unproven supply chains.
The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" or smaller pilots on cost or reversibility.

Fit: Assuming a vision of mass EV adoption, this execution method (large factory, exclusive commitment) misaligns with the stage (startup, 2008). It assumes a mature market that does not exist.

Pre-mortem: The company runs out of cash during factory retooling because battery yields are low and charging scarcity limits sales volume. This is a one-way door; once the factory is bought and the line committed, reversal means total loss. Delay cost is low; waiting allows battery tech to improve and costs to drop.

Evidence: The proposal itself states the company lacks the capital for the factory and batteries. This is an internal contradiction: the plan requires resources the proponent admits they do not have.

Blockers:
1. Capital mismatch: The plan requires "far more capital than the company has" for factory and batteries. This is a factual impossibility as stated.
2. Experience gap: Mass production inexperience combined with a "large factory" setup guarantees execution failure without a phased approach.

Risks: Charging scarcity, competitor response, battery costs. These are secondary to the immediate funding blocker.

Kill criteria: The proposal offers no pilot, cap, or metric. It demands total commitment despite admitting inability to fund the core requirements. The stated facts show the target users (buyers) face scarce charging, reducing demand certainty.

DECISION: NO-GO
