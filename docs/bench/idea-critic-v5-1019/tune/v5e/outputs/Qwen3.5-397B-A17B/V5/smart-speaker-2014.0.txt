First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; poor phone sales do not create an advantage for a new, unproven hardware category, and "need" alone is insufficient.

**Steelman:** A cylindrical speaker with always-on voice at $199 ($99 for members) creates a new home category, leveraging membership pricing to overcome hardware skepticism despite recent phone failures.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits the company's last hardware launch failed and phone assistants already exist. It offers no fact showing why this specific form factor beats existing solutions or why customers would trust this brand again.
2.  **Unverified Premises:** The risks list "customers may not accept an always-on microphone" and "voice recognition often fails" as known issues. The proposal treats these as hurdles to clear rather than blockers to address with evidence. Without proof that privacy concerns can be overcome or accuracy is sufficient, the core mechanism is broken.
3.  **Cheaper Alternative:** "Do nothing" or improving the existing phone assistant is cheaper, safer, and reversible. Launching new hardware after a failure increases sunk costs without guaranteed upside.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The product fails because privacy fears stall adoption, voice errors frustrate users, and the "invitation only" model prevents the volume needed to train the AI, leaving the company with unsold inventory and reputational damage.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Edge:** The proposal states no fact demonstrating a competitive advantage over existing phone assistants or a reason for customers to trust this brand post-failure.
*   **Contradictory Evidence:** The proposal admits voice recognition "often fails" and customers "may not accept" always-on mics. These are stated negatives that remove the route to upside without a mitigation plan.

**Risks:** High price point, unproven category, execution difficulty. These become kill criteria if the blockers were resolved, but currently, the lack of a stated edge and admitted functional failures are immediate blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot cannot demonstrate >80% user acceptance of always-on mic and <5% command failure rate within 3 months, stop.

The proposal relies on hope, not a stated advantage. It asks to bet on a new category with a broken track record and admitted technical/social hurdles.

DECISION: NO-GO
