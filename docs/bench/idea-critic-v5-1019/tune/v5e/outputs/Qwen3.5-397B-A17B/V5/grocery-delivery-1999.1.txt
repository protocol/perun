First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits irreversible capital before validating the core hypothesis that unit economics can work in a single location.

**Steelman:** Rapidly building a nationwide moat of automated warehouses prevents competitors from securing real estate and dominates the market before margins are understood.

**Attack:**
1.  **Evidence Gap:** The proposal admits "no operating track record" and unknown "unit economics." Committing $1B based on unproven assumptions in a low-margin industry is not strategy; it is gambling.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" regarding expansion preserves capital. A "cheaper alternative" involves optimizing the first metropolitan area to prove profitability before replicating the model. This reaches the same upside (proof of concept) with negligible risk compared to the proposed $1B burn.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 2001. Cause: The company scaled a broken model. High fixed costs from 12+ warehouses drained cash while customer density in new cities remained too low to cover delivery costs per order. The inability to pivot due to sunk costs in specialized automation sealed the fate.
4.  **Fit:** This violates basic operational hygiene. You cannot scale what you have not fixed. The "north star" should be sustainable unit economics, not geographic footprint.
5.  **Blocker:** The proposal explicitly states the intent to expand "before learning the unit economics." This is a factual admission that the core financial engine is unverified. In a low-margin business, scaling unverified economics guarantees magnified losses.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal offers no metric for success in the first city, no date to pause, and no pivot condition. It demands immediate, total commitment.

**Conclusion:** The absence of a pilot, cap, or metric is a blocker here because the proposal explicitly rejects learning before spending. The risk is not just "execution difficulty"; it is the certainty of burning $1B on an unproven formula. A sound operator validates the model in one city, proves the margin, and *then* scales.

Blockers: Committing $1B before validating unit economics in the first market; explicit plan to ignore learning in favor of speed.
Risks: Capital intensity, thin margins, customer density failures.

DECISION: NO-GO
