First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high license cost and extreme time compression do not constitute a competitive advantage over existing market offerings, but rather a financial vulnerability.

**Steelman:** A single developer can rapidly distill the film's core appeal into a functional product within five weeks, leveraging the brand's momentum to dominate the Christmas market before competitors react.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capacity Mismatch:** Normal development cycles exceed five weeks by magnitudes. Compressing this into a single programmer's timeline guarantees severe technical debt, bugs, or incomplete features, directly validating the "poor quality" risk.
2.  **Inventory Trap:** Ordering a "very large" production run based on an unproven, rushed product creates massive downside exposure. If quality fails (likely), the company is left with high fixed costs (license + inventory) and zero revenue.
3.  **Missing Edge:** The proposal relies entirely on the film's past success, not the game's inherent value. A bad game based on a hit film damages the brand and wastes the license fee; it does not beat existing alternatives.
4.  **Alternatives:** A smaller, pilot production run or a delayed launch to ensure quality would mitigate inventory risk. Doing nothing preserves capital better than gambling on a defective product.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Five weeks is insufficient for standard quality assurance and feature completion for a "hit" expectation.
*   **Fact:** Large inventory commitments without a verified, high-quality product contradict basic risk management.
*   **Kill Check:** The proposal admits the risks (time, quality, inventory) but offers no mitigation, pilot, or metric to validate the approach before full commitment. The stated facts (rushed timeline + huge bet) point directly toward the failure mode (unsold inventory of a poor game).

**Risks:** Market rejection, brand damage, total loss of license fee and manufacturing costs. These are not merely possible; they are the probable outcome of the current plan.

**Conclusion:** The plan fails the blocker test. It commits maximum capital to a minimum-viability timeline with no safety valve. The absence of a pilot run or quality gate is a fatal flaw in the strategy, not a minor omission.

DECISION: NO-GO
