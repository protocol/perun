First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; higher local prices and an entrenched competitor mean the retailer's domestic loyalty and discount model do not automatically translate to a winning position abroad. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Rapid saturation prevents competitor response and achieves supply chain scale immediately.
Attack: Speed amplifies failure modes. Unproven logistics and new inventory systems cannot support 130+ simultaneous openings. If the system fails, the entire capital commitment is stranded instantly.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Zero risk, zero exposure to foreign regulatory failure.
2. Pilot (3-5 stores): Tests cross-border logistics, labeling, and price sensitivity with minimal capex. Reversible.
3. Proposed (130+ stores): Maximum cost, maximum risk, low reversibility (long-term leases).

Fit: Expansion fits a growth vision, but the *method* contradicts operational prudence for a first international foray. The proposal assumes success rather than validating the core constraint: can the supply chain actually work across borders?

Pre-mortem: Six months in, the inventory system glitches cause stockouts in 80% of stores. Leases are signed, staff hired, but shelves are empty. The brand reputation is destroyed before it launches. Cost of delay: Minimal; the competitor is entrenched and will not vanish in two years.

Evidence: The proposal admits the supply chain and inventory systems are "not yet proven." Scaling unproven infrastructure to 150 sites is a factual blocker. The "higher prices" in the target market suggest the retailer's low-price advantage may be eroded by cross-border logistics costs, contradicting the core value proposition without data proving otherwise.

Blockers:
1. Unproven infrastructure at scale: The plan relies on new systems working perfectly immediately across 130+ sites. Failure is catastrophic, not incremental.
2. No validated price advantage: Entrenched competitor + higher local goods costs + new logistics costs = undefined margin. The edge is assumed, not demonstrated.

Risks: Regulatory hurdles, bilingual labeling errors, cultural mismatch. These become kill criteria for a pilot, but they are secondary to the structural flaw of scaling unproven ops.

Kill criteria check: The proposal offers no pilot, no cap, and no metric to validate the supply chain before full commitment. It demands a binary success/failure on the first try.

DECISION: NO-GO
