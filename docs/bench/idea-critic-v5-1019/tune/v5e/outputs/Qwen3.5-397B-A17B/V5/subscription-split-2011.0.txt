First call: NO-GO. The proposal addresses a self-inflicted wound (the 60% price hike) with structural fragmentation rather than price correction or unified value justification.

**Steelman:** Separating the brands allows streaming to pivot rapidly without the drag of a declining DVD logistics model, preventing the legacy business from stifling future growth.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing & Causality:** Executing a complex brand split immediately after a massive price increase compounds customer anger. It signals an intent to abandon DVD users rather than manage a transition, turning price friction into existential betrayal.
2.  **Friction vs. Value:** The plan introduces significant user friction (two logins, two bills, lost data portability) while delivering zero new value. In a competitive market, increasing hassle while raising prices is a churn accelerator.
3.  **Data Silos:** Severing recommendation engines destroys the primary mechanism for guiding DVD users to streaming content, artificially accelerating the decline of the very asset being spun off.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "do nothing" approach regarding structure, coupled with a rollback or moderation of the price hike, addresses the revenue need without the operational chaos and brand damage of a split. A bundled discount could retain users without separate billing.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-split, the DVD brand collapses faster than projected due to user abandonment, while the streaming brand faces a PR backlash for "abandoning" its roots, resulting in net subscriber loss greater than if the services had remained integrated. The cost of migrating users to separate queues outweighs any management efficiency gains.

**Blockers:**
*   **Compound Friction:** The combination of a 60% price hike and forced account fragmentation creates a "quit moment" for the entire user base, not just DVD users.
*   **Value Destruction:** Explicitly removing cross-service recommendations and shared history degrades the product experience for no immediate compensatory benefit.

**Risks:** Customer confusion is guaranteed; brand dilution is certain. These are not mere risks but guaranteed outcomes of the proposed architecture.

**Kill Criteria:** If the split proceeds, immediate churn exceeding 5% in the first billing cycle post-separation should trigger an emergency reunification of accounts. However, the current proposal lacks a pilot or cap.

The proposal solves an internal management desire at the expense of the customer experience during a period of maximum sensitivity.

DECISION: NO-GO
