First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; being the "cheapest" is a price position, not a product advantage, and the text explicitly notes cheaper alternatives (scooters, used cars) exist. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: A dedicated high-volume factory enables a price point low enough to convert scooter families despite safety concerns and land protests.

Attack:
1. **Alternatives:** The proposal admits scooters and used cars are cheaper. Without a stated feature or status advantage over a used car, the "cheapest new car" label risks becoming a stigma ("unsafe," "low status") rather than a value win. The plan does not explain why a family would choose a risky new cheap car over a proven used one.
2. **Fit & Risk:** The factory site faces active farmer protests. This is a concrete blocker to the "dedicated factory" dependency. If the land is not secured, high-volume production cannot start, making the cost targets impossible. The proposal treats this as a "risk" rather than a precondition.
3. **Evidence:** The claim relies on high-volume targets to sustain the $2,500 price. However, the land conflict threatens the very existence of the factory required for that volume. The math fails if the factory is delayed or cancelled.
4. **Pre-mortem:** The project fails because the "cheapest" branding alienates aspirational buyers who fear safety issues, while the land protests delay the factory, burning cash before a single unit is sold.

Blockers:
- **Land Conflict:** The proposal states the site faces protests. Without resolved land acquisition, the dedicated factory cannot be built, invalidating the volume/cost model.
- **No Stated Edge:** The proposal offers no reason to buy this over cheaper used cars or scooters other than price, which is already undercut by those alternatives.

Risks: Safety perception, brand stigma, execution complexity.

The plan relies on a factory that may not be built and a value proposition that loses to existing alternatives. It cannot proceed as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
