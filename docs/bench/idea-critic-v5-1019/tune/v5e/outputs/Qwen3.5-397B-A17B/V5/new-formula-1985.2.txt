First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; beating a rival in blind taste tests is not an edge when the plan destroys the unique asset (heritage) that defines the brand's market position.

**Steelman:** Replacing the formula with a sweeter variant that wins blind taste tests will recover lost share by aligning the product with demonstrated consumer preference for sweetness.

**Attack:** The evidence cited (blind tests, surveys) explicitly isolates taste while removing the context of brand identity. The proposal admits testing did not inform participants of the original's removal. This creates a fatal disconnect: the data validates a liquid, not a strategy. The "edge" exists only in a vacuum; in the market, the edge is the heritage itself, which this plan incinerates.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Retain share erosion but preserve brand equity and bottler stability.
2.  **Line Extension:** Launch the sweeter formula as a new product ("New Cola") alongside the original. This captures the sweetness-preference segment without alienating heritage loyalists or removing the core asset.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** High cost (reformulation, marketing), extreme risk (total brand alienation), zero reversibility (once poured down the drain, the original is gone).

**Fit:** The strategy contradicts the north star of a heritage brand: trust and continuity. Swapping the core product based on isolated sensory data ignores the emotional contract with the consumer.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch triggers a public backlash not about taste, but about loss and corporate arrogance. Loyalists feel betrayed because the option to choose was removed. Competitors capitalize on the void by positioning themselves as the "original" choice. Sales plummet not because the new drink tastes bad, but because the old one is missing.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fatal Evidence Gap:** The primary evidence (taste tests) is invalid for the specific decision (total replacement) because it withheld the key variable (discontinuation). The data proves the new soda tastes good, not that consumers want the old one gone.
*   **Superior Alternative:** A line extension achieves the upside (selling sweeter soda) without the downside (destroying heritage), rendering total replacement unnecessary and reckless.

**Risks:** Emotional attachment, bottler revolt, no fallback. These are not mere risks; given the evidence gap, they are probable outcomes.

DECISION: NO-GO
