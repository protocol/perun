First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it admits no large paying market exists and relies on raising unit costs for a product sold on gaming performance, without a mechanism to capture value from scientific users who currently pay nothing.

Steelman: Embedding general-purpose compute capabilities now creates an irreversible ecosystem moat, transforming every consumer GPU into a potential supercomputer node before rivals realize the shift, securing long-term dominance beyond graphics.

Attack: The "moat" is speculative while the cost is immediate and universal. Raising unit costs on mass-market chips without committed customers directly threatens the core gaming revenue stream that funds the company. Engineering distraction during a fierce rivalry race could cause loss of the primary market share, killing the company before the secondary market matures. The "cheaper" alternative is maintaining the status quo: let academics hack APIs until demand proves willingness to pay, then add features. This proposal forces the entire customer base to subsidize a niche with no proven ROI.

Fit: This contradicts the immediate survival need of winning the graphics race. It assumes a future market that may never materialize in a form profitable enough to offset the added silicon cost on millions of units.

Pre-mortem: The program consumes three years of engineering bandwidth and margin. The rival releases faster, cheaper gaming cards, capturing the core market. The scientific toolchain remains unused by professionals due to lack of support, and the added chip area makes the product uncompetitive on price/performance for gamers. The company collapses from core market loss.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: The proposal admits no paying market exists, yet raises costs for all units.
2. Core business threat: Engineering distraction and cost inflation risk losing the primary revenue source (gaming) to a rival.
3. Subsidy mismatch: Forces mass-market consumers to pay for features only a non-paying niche uses.

Risks: Niche demand remains niche; toolchain fails to gain traction; rival exploits the distraction.

DECISION: NO-GO
