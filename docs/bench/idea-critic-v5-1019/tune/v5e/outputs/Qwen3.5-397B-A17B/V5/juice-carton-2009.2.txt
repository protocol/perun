First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the old image is the primary shelf cue for a loyal, older demographic yet removes it entirely without a recognition bridge, betting the entire brand on unproven aesthetic assumptions.

Steelman: A modernized design will attract new buyers and signal quality without alienating existing customers if supported by massive advertising.

Attack: The plan fails the "do nothing" and "cheaper alternative" tests. Removing the single strongest visual anchor (the orange with a straw) for an older, loyal base is a one-way door with catastrophic downside if recognition fails. The proposal offers no pilot, no phased rollout, and no evidence that a "cap shaped like an orange" compensates for the loss of the explicit fruit image. The risk is not theoretical; it is a direct contradiction of the stated fact that the old image is the main cue. A nationwide "big bang" launch maximizes exposure to this failure mode.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retains shelf presence, zero cost, zero recognition risk.
2. Phased pilot: Test the new design in a limited region to measure recognition drop-off before national commitment.
3. Hybrid design: Evolve the current package gradually rather than stripping it to minimalism.

The proposal's own facts identify a blocker: the buyer base is older and relies on the current image. Removing that image while keeping the recipe and price unchanged offers no functional upside to offset the recognition risk. The "large advertising campaign" is a cost, not a guarantee of re-education. Without a stated edge proving the new design converts better than the old one preserves, and given the high reversibility cost of a nationwide print run, this is a speculative gamble, not a sound operation.

Blockers: The plan removes the stated primary shelf cue for the core demographic without a verified replacement mechanism or pilot data.
Risks: Total brand alienation, wasted print/ad spend, inability to revert quickly.

DECISION: NO-GO
