First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve an app-ecosystem deficit (a software/network problem) by acquiring a failing hardware division, despite the target's sales already falling and the rival holding dominant developer mindshare. Need alone is not an edge; the stated facts show no mechanism by which this purchase reverses the app gap.

Steelman: Owning the handset maker guarantees a dedicated outlet for the OS and prevents its defection to the rival, creating a unified vertical stack.

Attack: The core blocker is the mismatch between the problem and the solution. The proposal admits the market leader wins via apps and developers. Buying a shrinking hardware manufacturer does not attract developers; it adds integration drag and alienates other handset partners who may flee the rival platform if they perceive favoritism or instability. The €5.4 billion cost drains resources that could directly incentivize app development. The target's falling sales indicate the hardware itself is not the asset; the OS is the value, but the OS is weak precisely because of the lack of apps this deal fails to address.

Alternatives: "Do nothing" preserves cash but risks the manufacturer defecting. A better way is licensing the OS widely to all manufacturers (including the target) while subsidizing app ports, decoupling hardware fate from software reach. Acquiring the hardware unit creates a "walled garden" with no plants.

Pre-mortem: Two years post-acquisition, the company is burdened with a legacy workforce and declining device sales, while developers still prioritize the rival platform due to its larger installed base (now fragmented by partner exodus). The integration distracts from software innovation.

Evidence: The proposal states smartphone sales are falling and the rival has most apps. These facts contradict the claim that hardware ownership fixes the ecosystem.

Blockers: The stated fact that the target's sales are falling and the core problem is an app deficit (which hardware ownership does not solve) means the plan fails as proposed. The cost (€5.4B) diverts capital from the actual bottleneck (developer adoption).

Risks: Partner backlash, integration failure, continued app drought.

Kill criteria: None can save this as proposed; the strategic fit is broken.

DECISION: NO-GO
