First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the core deficits that determine tablet adoption: a tiny app catalog, negligible OS market share, and an unproven brand perception, yet plans to match the dominant leader's price. In this category, software ecosystem is the primary value driver; hardware parity at a premium price with a fraction of the content is a proven failure mode, not a differentiator.

Steelman: Leverage the computer maker's brand trust and supply chain to force a premium ecosystem play, using heavy marketing to bridge the app gap until organic growth kicks in.

Attack: The "small app catalog" is a structural blocker, not a temporary gap. Users buy tablets for applications, not potential. Matching the market leader's price removes the only viable alternative strategy (cost leadership). Heavy advertising cannot compensate for a missing core utility; it only accelerates the realization of the product's emptiness, increasing churn and brand damage. The "leadership transition" compounds execution risk during the critical launch window.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital and brand equity while the OS matures or the market shifts.
2. Low-price entry: Undercut the leader significantly to justify the app deficit as a "budget" option (high risk to margin, but viable volume play).
3. Niche focus: Target a specific vertical where the app count matters less (e.g., enterprise management), rather than a general consumer launch.

Pre-mortem: Launch occurs, early adopters buy due to ads, immediately encounter missing apps, return devices, and publish negative reviews. The narrative solidifies as "expensive toy with no use cases." Inventory piles up; the brand takes a lasting reputation hit.

Blockers:
1. Price parity with superior competitor: The proposal explicitly states pricing "in line with the market-leading tablet" despite having a "small fraction" of the apps. This violates basic value exchange.
2. Ecosystem dependency: Tablet success is non-linear with app count; being below a critical threshold makes the device unusable for mainstream buyers regardless of ad spend.

Risks: Leadership distraction, marketing burn without conversion, channel conflict.

The plan fails the value test on its own stated facts. It asks users to pay a premium for an inferior experience based on future promises.

DECISION: NO-GO
