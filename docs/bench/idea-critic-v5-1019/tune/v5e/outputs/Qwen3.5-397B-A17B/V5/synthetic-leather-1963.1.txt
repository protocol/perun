First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it admits higher cost, potential comfort deficits, and adoption friction without identifying a compensating advantage over existing leather.

Steelman: The synthetic offers superior durability that justifies a premium price if marketed effectively to manufacturers.

Attack:
1. **Economics:** The material costs more than leather. Without a verified willingness from consumers to pay a significant premium for "durability" over comfort and tradition, shoe manufacturers face margin compression or sales loss. The proposal assumes marketing spend can overcome this unit economics gap, which is unproven.
2. **Adoption:** Manufacturers are reluctant to retool factories. Asking them to incur capital expenditure for a material that is more expensive and potentially less comfortable creates a negative value proposition for the buyer (the manufacturer).
3. **Market Fit:** Fashion volatility is a known risk. A "durable" material becomes a liability if styles shift, leaving manufacturers with obsolete, expensive inventory compared to biodegradable or easily sourced leather.
4. **Comfort:** If wearers find it less comfortable, repeat purchases will decline regardless of marketing. Durability does not compensate for pain in footwear.

Alternatives:
- **Do nothing:** Continue using leather, avoiding cost increases and retooling risks.
- **Niche application:** Target industrial or protective footwear where durability outweighs comfort and fashion, rather than premium fashion uppers.
- **Cost reduction:** Delay launch until production costs fall below leather, creating a genuine economic edge.

Blockers:
- **Negative Unit Economics:** Higher cost + lower comfort = no compelling reason for manufacturers to switch. The proposal offers no data suggesting consumers value this specific durability enough to absorb the cost.
- **Channel Resistance:** Manufacturers have no incentive to change processes for a inferior (cost/comfort) input.

Risks:
- Fashion trends shifting away.
- Consumer rejection of comfort trade-off.

The proposal relies on marketing spend to solve a fundamental product-market fit and economic disadvantage. Without a stated edge (e.g., unique aesthetic, water resistance, or lower long-term cost despite higher upfront price), the claim fails the basic operator test.

DECISION: NO-GO
