First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a clear customer pain point (removing the jack) but fails to state a defensive edge that makes this specific $159 solution beat existing alternatives or justify the anger. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Launching proprietary wireless earbuds at $159 creates a seamless, high-margin ecosystem lock-in that turns a hardware liability into a recurring revenue stream, offsetting jack-removal backlash with superior convenience.

However, the risks identified are not merely potential; they are structural blockers under the stated facts.
1. **Value Mismatch:** Replacing a free included item with a $159 optional accessory is a net value destruction for the baseline user. Without proof that users *want* to pay for this specific convenience, the price point alienates the very customers angered by the jack removal.
2. **Adoption Friction:** "Looking odd" and "easy to lose" are fatal for a mass-market accessory intended to replace a universal standard. If the form factor invites ridicule, it becomes a brand liability rather than an aspirational product.
3. **Technical Deficit:** Five hours of battery life is insufficient for a "fully wireless" primary audio solution, especially when wired alternatives offer infinite playback.

The proposal offers no evidence that the target market accepts these trade-offs. It assumes the brand power alone overrides the loss of utility and the price shock. A cheaper, staged approach (e.g., selling the dongle separately first, or launching a lower-cost model) reaches the same upside (ecosystem entry) with less risk. The current plan bets the entire audio transition on an unproven, expensive, and fragile product configuration.

Blockers: The $159 price creates a net-negative value perception compared to the removed free good; the 5-hour battery fails to match wired reliability; the design risk threatens brand equity without a stated mitigation.
Risks: Customer anger, loss of units, ridicule.

The plan fails as proposed because it substitutes a universal, free utility with an expensive, limited, and risky alternative without demonstrating demand for that specific trade-off.

DECISION: NO-GO
