First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it offers a higher-priced device with a smaller compatible base than existing failures, relying on a market (legal downloads) that does not yet exist.

Steelman: A premium, high-capacity device creates a new category by superior user experience, forcing software adoption rather than following install base.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids capital risk but cedes emerging digital media to incumbents.
2. License to Windows: Expands addressable market immediately but dilutes hardware margin and control.
3. Wait for legal downloads: Reduces content risk but misses first-mover branding opportunity.

Fit: Assumes a strategy where hardware drives ecosystem loyalty, not immediate unit volume. This contradicts the "small market share" constraint unless the device itself converts new users to the computer platform, which is unproven.

Pre-mortem: The device fails because the $400 price point exceeds the perceived value of MP3 players, and the lack of legal content makes the 5GB capacity irrelevant. The one-way door is the sunk cost of custom tooling for a niche drive. Cost of delay is low; the market is not yet ripe.

Evidence: The proposal admits no legal downloads at scale and a tiny compatible base. Competitors are cheaper and failing. The math (high price × tiny base × no content) yields negative ROI.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: Higher price and smaller base than failing competitors is a losing formula without a proven differentiator beyond "capacity," which users cannot utilize due to lack of legal content.
2. Content vacuum: Selling a 5GB vessel with no legal way to fill it renders the core feature useless.

Risks: Component sourcing delays, brand dilution if the product flops.

Kill criteria: If legal download licensing is not secured before prototype completion, or if pre-orders from existing computer owners do not cover 20% of tooling costs within 3 months of announcement, halt production.

The proposal relies on creating demand for a solution to a problem (storage) that is secondary to the actual bottleneck (content availability). Without a guaranteed content pipeline or a larger hardware base, this is a speculative bet with no defensive moat.

DECISION: NO-GO
