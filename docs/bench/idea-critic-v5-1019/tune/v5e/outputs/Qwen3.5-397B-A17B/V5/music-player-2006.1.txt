First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; being late with similar features, higher costs (marketing, new hardware), and a fractured ecosystem (dropping copy-protection alienates partners) offers no reason buyers would switch from the dominant, loyal incumbent.

**Steelman:** The only defensible version is that superior hardware (screen, wireless) and a seamless ecosystem integration outweigh the lateness and loss of label support, creating a premium experience the leader cannot match.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid massive capex and partner backlash; maintain status quo.
2.  **License/Partner:** Enhance existing players via software deals rather than building hardware/store from scratch.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** High cost, high risk, requires convincing labels to relax restrictions and users to abandon existing libraries.

**Fit:** Entering a saturated market without a unique, defensible advantage contradicts prudent resource allocation. The "large marketing budget" is a cost, not a strategy.

**Pre-mortem:** The device fails because labels restrict wireless sharing (the key feature), rendering it no better than competitors. The store launches with a limited catalog due to partner alienation over dropped copy-protection. Users refuse to switch ecosystems for marginal hardware gains.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the leader has "enormous share," "loyal ecosystem," and that the company is "late." It explicitly states there is "no clear price advantage" and that partners (labels) may restrict the primary differentiator (sharing).

**Blockers:**
1.  **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits no price advantage and relies on features (wireless, screen) that labels may block. Need alone is not an edge.
2.  **Self-Sabotage:** Dropping the copy-protection standard alienates the very partners (labels/stores) required to supply content, directly threatening the store's viability.
3.  **Dependency Risk:** The core differentiator (wireless sharing) is contingent on label approval, which is explicitly flagged as uncertain.

**Risks:** Execution difficulty, high burn rate, brand damage from a failed launch.

**Kill Criteria:** If labels do not guarantee unrestricted sharing rights before hardware tooling begins, the project must stop. If pre-orders do not indicate 20% conversion from competitor users within 3 months, halt marketing spend.

The plan fails as proposed because it attacks a monopoly without a verified wedge, relying on permissions it may not get.

DECISION: NO-GO
