First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a "very large sum" sunk cost but offers no stated edge over ordinary cigarettes on taste, ease of use, or regulatory clearance; need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: A heated tobacco product could capture health-conscious smokers if the taste gap is negligible and regulators allow "cleaner" claims.

Attack:
1. **Taste & Usability:** The proposal admits smokers may dislike the taste and lighting mechanism. In a commodity market where habit is paramount, a "complicated" inferior experience is a fatal blocker, not a risk. Users will not trade convenience for a vague "cleaner" promise if the core sensory reward is diminished.
2. **Regulatory Trap:** Promoting it as a "cleaner way to smoke" invites immediate regulatory scrutiny on health claims. Without pre-approved language, the launch could be enjoined, turning the marketing budget into a liability.
3. **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** The "very large sum" already spent is irrelevant to future profitability. It biases the decision toward launching a flawed product rather than cutting losses or re-engineering.
4. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A "test market" is already suggested but the proposal asks to "launch... and promote widely" immediately after, ignoring the high probability of test failure given the admitted taste/usability risks.

Blockers:
- **User Rejection:** Stated risk that smokers dislike taste/ignition creates a high probability of market failure against a superior incumbent (ordinary cigarettes).
- **Regulatory Block:** Promoting health-adjacent claims ("cleaner") without stated regulatory approval is a legal blocker.

Risks:
- High development costs cannot be recovered if the product fails.
- Complexity reduces adoption speed.

The proposal attempts to convert a "test market" phase directly into a "wide promotion" launch despite admitting core product deficiencies. A product that tastes worse and is harder to use than the competitor cannot win on "cleanliness" alone, especially when that claim invites regulatory attack. The sunk cost is a trap; the lack of a verified taste advantage is a killer.

DECISION: NO-GO
