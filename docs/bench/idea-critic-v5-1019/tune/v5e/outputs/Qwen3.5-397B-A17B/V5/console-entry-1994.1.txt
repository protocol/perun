First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "easier tools" and "cheaper discs" are generic advantages that do not overcome the massive switching costs of loyal customers and incumbent exclusive franchises.

Steelman: A new entrant can disrupt the duopoly by lowering barriers for third-party developers, creating a software library distinct from the incumbents' first-party strongholds.

Attack: The claim fails the "killer app" test. Console markets are driven by must-have software, not manufacturing economics. Without owned intellectual property (IP) to drive hardware adoption, the platform relies entirely on external developers taking a risky bet on an unproven ecosystem. The risks identified are not merely operational; they are structural blockers. Entering a network-effect market without a unique content hook or an existing user base is a near-certain path to massive losses on subsidized hardware.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoids catastrophic R&D and marketing spend; preserves capital for other ventures.
2. Partner with existing software houses: Secure exclusives before announcing hardware (not stated in proposal).
3. Proceed as proposed: High probability of failure due to lack of software pull and entrenched competition.

Fit: This contradicts basic platform strategy; hardware is a loss leader for software. Without a confirmed slate of system-selling games, the hardware has no value proposition to the consumer.

Pre-mortem: The console launches, developers hesitate due to low install base, consumers refuse to buy hardware without games, the company accumulates unsold inventory and writes off the division. This is a one-way door with high financial damage.

Evidence: The proposal admits "no experience" and reliance on "outside developers" who are not contractually bound. The incumbents' "loyal customers" and "famous in-house series" represent a moat this plan does not bridge.

Blockers:
1. No owned or contracted "killer app" IP to drive initial hardware sales.
2. Reliance on uncommitted third-party developers in a market dominated by exclusives.
3. Negative hardware margins with no proven path to recoupment via software sales.

Risks: Execution difficulty, brand dilution, timing collision with incumbent 3D launches.

The absence of a secured content strategy is a fatal flaw. Cheaper manufacturing is irrelevant if no one buys the device.

DECISION: NO-GO
