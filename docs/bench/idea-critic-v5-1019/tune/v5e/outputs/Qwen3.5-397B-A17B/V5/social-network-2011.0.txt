First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state an edge; "circles" and "hangouts" are features, not a defensible wedge against a locked-in incumbent with hundreds of millions of users.

Steelman: Forced integration of superior grouping and video tools into existing high-traffic products can overcome network effects by leveraging current utility rather than requiring standalone migration.

Attack:
1. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves core search/mail dominance. A "cheaper alternative" would be acquiring a niche network or building APIs for others, avoiding the massive brand risk of forced integration. The proposal does not explain why these fail to reach the same upside.
2. **Fit & Incentives:** Tying executive bonuses to adoption of a specific feature, rather than overall user satisfaction or retention, creates perverse incentives to degrade core products (search/mail) to force social usage. This directly conflicts with the company's primary value proposition.
3. **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. The cause is likely user backlash against "forced" social layers in utility tools, driving them to competitors, compounded by the company's proven inability to sustain social engagement (three prior failures).
4. **Evidence Gap:** The proposal cites no evidence that "circles" or "hangouts" are the specific blockers preventing users from joining. Without proof that these specific features unlock the market, the plan relies on hope, not data.
5. **Blocker:** The combination of a history of three failed social attempts and a strategy that risks annoying the existing user base of core products constitutes a factual blocker. The plan assumes integration equals adoption, contradicting the historical evidence that distribution alone did not work previously.

Risks (non-blocking but severe): Network effects favoring the incumbent, execution difficulty, brand damage.

Kill Criteria Check: The proposal lists risks but offers no kill criteria, caps, or pilot metrics to limit the blast radius of forcing social features onto core products.

The plan repeats past failures with higher stakes and misaligned incentives. It lacks a stated edge that overcomes the incumbent's lock-in and ignores the specific lesson that distribution via core products has already been tried and failed to create a lasting network.

DECISION: NO-GO
