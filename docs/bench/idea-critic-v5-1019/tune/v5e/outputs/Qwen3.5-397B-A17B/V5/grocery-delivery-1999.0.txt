First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; low margins and zero operating data cannot support a $1B irreversible bet before validating unit economics.

Steelman: Secure dominant market share in 1999 by locking in real estate and infrastructure capacity before competitors, leveraging abundant venture capital to outlast rivals in a land grab.

Attack:
1. **Options:** "Do nothing" in new cities preserves cash to fix the broken unit economics in the first city. A "pilot warehouse" approach tests automation at 1% of the cost. The proposed "all-in" strategy fails the reversibility test; building a dozen white elephants is harder to unwind than scaling successful pods.
2. **Fit:** The strategy contradicts the basic operational reality of groceries: density drives margin. Expanding to a dozen cities dilutes management focus and capital away from solving the critical "customer-density" problem in the initial market.
3. **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 2001. Cause: The $1B is spent on fixed automation designed for hypothetical volume. Actual order density remains too low to fill trucks or justify warehouse overhead. Cash runs out before the model works because the "rapid pace" burned the runway on unproven assumptions.
4. **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no operating track record" and "low-margin business." Committing $1B without a single day of profitable data is mathematically unsound. The risk of "capital intensity" is not a future possibility; it is the immediate result of signing these contracts.

Blockers:
- **Fact:** Zero validated unit economics.
- **Fact:** $1B commitment is irreversible (one-way door) while the core business model (density vs. margin) is unproven.
- **Fact:** Expansion to 12 cities actively worsens the primary constraint (density) by fragmenting orders.

Risks:
- Automation technology may not handle grocery variability (mitigated by pilot).
- Competitor response (irrelevant if unit economics fail).

Kill Criteria Check: The proposal offers no kill criteria, no pilot cap, and no metric to stop the bleed. It demands full commitment before learning. A sound operator never bets the company on an unproven hypothesis when a staged, cheaper alternative exists. The absence of a learning loop before the $1B spend is a fatal flaw.

DECISION: NO-GO
