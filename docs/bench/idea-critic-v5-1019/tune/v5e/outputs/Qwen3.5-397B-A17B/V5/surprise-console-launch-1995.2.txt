First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; launching early with few games, at a higher price ($399 vs rival's $299), and excluding most retailers offers no advantage to buyers or partners, only friction.

Steelman: Launching in May secures shelf space and mindshare before the competitor arrives, accepting short-term pain for long-term dominance.

Attack:
1. **No Product-Market Fit:** A console without games is a toy. Developers explicitly did not plan for this date. Launching with "few games" guarantees negative word-of-mouth that early adopters will spread for months, poisoning the brand before the full library arrives.
2. **Channel Suicide:** Shipping only to "a few retailers" while alienating the rest invites retaliation. Excluded partners will prioritize the competitor's launch, burying this console in Q4 when volume matters most.
3. **Price Disadvantage:** Being $100 more expensive than the rival is fatal without a superior library or hardware advantage. The proposal admits the machine is hard to program for 3D, negating any technical edge.
4. **Developer Trust:** Forcing an unplanned release date burns developer goodwill. If the hardware is difficult and the timeline impossible, the resulting software will be low-quality, reinforcing the "no games" problem.

Alternatives:
- **Do Nothing (September Launch):** Aligns with developer schedules, ensures a robust game lineup, maintains retailer relationships, and allows price matching or competitive bundling.
- **Staged Rollout (Regional):** If early data is needed, limit to one region without announcing a national shift, preserving partner trust.

Pre-mortem: By Christmas 1995, the console is perceived as overpriced and empty. Retailers refuse to stock holiday bundles. Developers delay titles to the next generation. The early launch is cited in case studies as a catastrophic self-inflicted wound.

Blockers:
- **No Edge:** Higher price + fewer games + angry retailers = immediate failure.
- **Developer Readiness:** Explicitly stated as unprepared; forcing release guarantees poor software quality.
- **Channel Conflict:** Alienating the majority of retailers removes the distribution engine required to beat the competitor in Q4.

Risks: Brand damage, developer exodus, inventory glut.

DECISION: NO-GO
