First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge over rapidly improving ground cellular networks; high cost and poor indoor performance are disadvantages, not advantages.

Steelman: This is the only solution for truly remote global coverage where terrestrial cells cannot reach, catering to niche emergency and expedition use.
Attack: The proposal targets "international business travelers," a mass market that requires indoor reliability and affordable pricing, not a niche remote-coverage segment. The economics rely on rapid mass adoption to service $5 billion in debt, which the $3,000 handset and high call rates preclude.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoid sinking further capital into a product with negative unit economics for the target mass market.
2. Pivot to niche: Target maritime, aviation, and government/defense sectors where price sensitivity is lower and outdoor usage is standard.
3. Proceed as proposed: High likelihood of bankruptcy due to missed subscriber targets and inability to service debt.

Fit: The strategy assumes a mass-market adoption rate incompatible with the product's limitations (bulk, indoor failure) and price point.

Pre-mortem: The company launches, fails to attract enough subscribers to cover debt service because business travelers refuse bulky phones that don't work in offices, and defaults on loans within two years. This is a one-way door; the satellite constellation cost is sunk, but operational losses will accelerate insolvency.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits ground networks are spreading (substitutes improving), handsets work poorly indoors (core utility failure for business users), and price limits the market ( TAM contraction). Debt payments require fast growth, but the barriers to entry for the target customer are prohibitive.

Blockers:
1. Economic mismatch: Debt service requires mass adoption; product features and price restrict it to a niche too small to support the balance sheet.
2. Functional deficit: "Works poorly indoors" disqualifies the primary use case for business travelers who spend most time inside.

Risks: Ground network expansion speed, handset miniaturization timeline.

The proposal's own facts confirm the target market will not adopt the product at a rate sufficient to survive. The lack of a viable path to profitability for the stated target audience is a fatal flaw.

DECISION: NO-GO
