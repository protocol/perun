First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it adds cost and friction (broadband requirement, mandatory fee) to a market where rivals offer free or publisher-managed online play, with no evidence that users will pay for a unified identity or voice chat in 2002.

Steelman: A unified, high-quality online ecosystem creates a sticky network effect that justifies a subscription fee by offering superior social features (friends list, voice) unavailable elsewhere.

Attack: The "edge" relies on unproven assumptions. In 2002, broadband penetration is low; mandating it excludes the vast majority of the installed base. Competitors' "free" model sets a powerful price anchor of $0. The company has zero operational experience in running hosting/support, introducing massive execution risk. The "unified identity" value proposition is theoretical; players may not care enough to pay an annual fee on top of the console and game costs.

Options:
1.  **Do nothing:** Let publishers handle online play (current rival strategy). Cost: $0 new ops. Risk: Fragmented experience. Reversibility: N/A.
2.  **Publisher-partnered model:** Offer tools for publishers to integrate voice/friends, keeping service free or optional. Cost: Low. Risk: Less control. Reversibility: High.
3.  **Proposed paid service:** Cost: High (ops, support). Risk: High (adoption, execution). Reversibility: Low (brand damage if it fails).
The proposed plan does not beat "do nothing" or the cheaper partner model on stated facts.

Fit: Assumes a north star of "premium ecosystem," but this is not stated. If the goal is market share, this strategy contradicts it by raising barriers.

Pre-mortem: The service launches, but few subscribe due to broadband limits and price resistance. The company bleeds money on underutilized servers and support staff, distracting from core console sales. This is a one-way door for reputation; a failed paid service looks worse than no service.

Evidence: No data cited showing users will pay for this specific bundle in 2002. No proof the company can build the required ops.

Blockers:
1.  **No stated edge:** The proposal admits rivals are free; it offers no fact proving users will switch to paid.
2.  **Capability gap:** The company has never run these ops; this is a factual deficit, not just a risk.
3.  **Market mismatch:** Mandating broadband in 2002 ignores the actual installed base.

Risks: Execution failure, low adoption, brand damage. These are confirmed by the blockers.

DECISION: NO-GO
