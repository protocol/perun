First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; needing demonstration does not explain why this specific plan beats existing retailer channels or why it will succeed where the industry has failed.

**Steelman:** Company-run stores are the only viable way to control the customer experience and demonstrate the digital-music strategy sufficiently to reverse sales losses, justifying the high fixed costs.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** The proposal ignores cheaper, lower-risk alternatives. Dedicated "shop-in-shop" displays within existing retailer locations or pop-up kiosks could provide demonstration without the burden of prime mall leases and full inventory risk. These alternatives address the "demonstration" need without the catastrophic fixed-cost exposure during a recession.
2.  **Fit & Evidence:** The company has a "poor retail track record" and is currently losing money. Expanding into the lowest-margin sector (computer retail) with the highest cost structure (prime leases) during a recession contradicts basic survival logic. There is no evidence provided that the digital-music strategy requires a full store rather than a display unit.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: Fixed lease obligations drained cash reserves during a recession-induced sales dip, while channel conflict caused resellers to deprioritize the brand entirely, collapsing the remaining volume.
4.  **Blocker Test:** The combination of "recent run of losses," "low margins," "high lease costs," and "recession" creates a mathematical blocker. The cash burn rate of prime real estate cannot be sustained by a struggling manufacturer with no proven retail competency. The risk is not theoretical; the financial constraints are current facts that make the plan insolvent as proposed.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal offers no cap, pilot, or metric. It asks for a full chain rollout. A sound operator would demand a single pilot store with a strict break-even date before approving a chain. Without this, the risk is unmanaged.

**Conclusion:** The plan fails the blocker test. The financial reality (losses + high fixed costs + recession) outweighs the strategic desire for control. The company cannot afford to learn retail on the job while bleeding cash.

DECISION: NO-GO
