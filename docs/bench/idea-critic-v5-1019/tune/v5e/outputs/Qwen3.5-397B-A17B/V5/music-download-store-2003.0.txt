First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; selling scarce, expensive units against a ubiquitous, free alternative with lower margins and higher friction (platform lock-in) contradicts basic market dynamics.

**Steelman:** A premium, integrated ecosystem where ease of use and legal safety justify a per-song cost that free sharing cannot match, driving hardware loyalty.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** At 99 cents with major label payouts, margins are razor-thin. Without a subscription recurring revenue model (which rivals are backing), unit economics rely entirely on volume that free competitors already capture.
2.  **Friction:** Limiting the store to proprietary computers excludes the vast Windows market, capping adoption while file-sharing remains platform-agnostic.
3.  **Supply Chain Risk:** Dependence on label licenses creates a single point of failure; if labels withdraw to launch their own stores, the product becomes a shell.
4.  **Market Fit:** Consumers have normalized "free." Convincing them to pay per unit rather than a flat subscription fee for unlimited access is an uphill battle against established behavioral shifts.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Continue selling hardware; let software be a commodity. Risk: Hardware becomes irrelevant if content ecosystems shift to subscription-only rivals.
*   *Subscription Model:* Aligns with rival strategy and consumer desire for unlimited access, reducing per-transaction friction.
*   *Open Platform:* License the store to Windows immediately to maximize volume, sacrificing hardware exclusivity for market share.

**Pre-mortem:** The store launches, labels demand higher rates eroding the already thin margin, Windows users refuse to buy the computer just for music, and the company pivots to subscriptions two years later after burning capital.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits rivals are doing subscriptions (better value) and free sharing exists (better price). It offers no fact explaining why a user would choose this specific paid, restricted, per-song model over those options. Need without an edge is a failure.
*   **Margin Contradiction:** The math (99c - label fee) leaves little room for error, marketing, or development, making the business model fragile from day one.

**Risks:** Label withdrawal, platform isolation, consumer rejection of per-song pricing.

DECISION: NO-GO
