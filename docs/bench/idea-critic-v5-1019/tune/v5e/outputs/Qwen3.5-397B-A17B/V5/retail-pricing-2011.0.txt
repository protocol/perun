First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge proving this specific leadership transplant (electronics to apparel/home) beats the incumbent discount model for this specific demographic, relying instead on a track record in a different category.

**Steelman:** Eliminating coupon dependency and modernizing the store layout will restore margin integrity and attract higher-value shoppers, replicating a proven electronics retail success.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The core customer base is explicitly "older" and "coupon-driven," with most sales historically occurring at a discount. The proposal offers no evidence that this demographic values "everyday pricing" over known discounts, nor that the electronics CEO's success translates to soft goods. The assumption that behavior trained over decades can be broken instantly without a pilot is unsupported.
2.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves current cash flow while testing small changes. "Cheaper alternative": A pilot in 5-10 stores would validate the pricing elasticity and shop-in-shop concept before committing the entire chain. The proposal rejects staging, increasing irreversibility.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assuming failure: Revenue collapses because loyal customers refuse to pay full price, new customers do not arrive fast enough to replace them, and the capital cost of rebuilding stores drains liquidity before the brand perception shifts.
4.  **Blocker Test:** The proposal admits "most sales have historically come from items sold at a discount" yet plans to end discounts chain-wide without a test. This contradicts the known revenue driver. The lack of a pilot for a strategy that alienates the primary customer base is a factual blocker; it ignores the verified state of customer behavior.
5.  **Kill Criteria:** Not applicable as the plan fails the initial blocker test.

**Blockers:**
*   The plan removes the primary historical sales driver (discounts) for the core demographic without evidence they will accept everyday pricing.
*   Zero pilot testing for a chain-wide behavioral shift creates unacceptable execution risk.

**Risks:**
*   Brand alienation of older customers.
*   Capital exhaustion during rebuild.
*   Leadership domain mismatch (electronics vs. department store).

The proposal demands a high-stakes, one-way door decision based on cross-industry analogy rather than verified data for this specific customer base. It fails to beat "do nothing" or a "pilot first" approach on the stated facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
