First call: GO. The stated edge is solving two existential threats—update delivery and cheating—that retail boxes cannot address, while creating a direct customer relationship.

Steelman: A mandatory client is the only viable mechanism to enforce integrity and patch velocity for popular PC titles in 2003, converting a distribution liability into a platform asset.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited are execution hurdles, not factual contradictions. Home broadband adoption was rising rapidly in 2003; early adopters of popular games tolerate friction. Retail pushback is a transition cost, not a dealbreaker, as digital demand supersedes shelf space for hits.

**Risks:** Player friction, retail channel conflict, initial bug instability, competitor copying. These are managed via kill criteria, not reasons to stop. Competitors copying validates the model; being first with a hit game's ecosystem creates the lock-in.

**Pre-mortem:** The client launches with game-breaking bugs, causing a refund wave. Mitigation: Beta test with the existing fanbase before mandatory enforcement.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on the verified fact that current retail methods fail at updates and anti-cheat. No external data is needed to confirm that physical media cannot patch itself.

**Kill Criteria:**
1.  **Adoption:** If <60% of active users activate the client within 90 days of launch, pause mandatory enforcement and investigate friction points.
2.  **Stability:** If crash rates exceed 5% during the first month, halt new feature rollout until stability is restored.
3.  **Retail:** If >20% of key retail partners drop the title within 6 months, negotiate a hybrid SKU (box + download code) immediately.

**Leading Metric:** Percentage of daily active users running the latest game version (target: >90% within 2 weeks of patch). Review date: Quarterly.

The alternative ("do nothing") guarantees continued revenue loss from piracy and support costs for unpatched games. The "cheaper" alternative of optional downloads fails to solve the cheating problem. This plan beats the status quo by directly addressing the core product failures.

DECISION: GO
