First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it assumes exposure equals revenue without proving users or partners will tolerate the mechanism, and it explicitly admits the feature is hard to withdraw.

Steelman: Sharing user activity drives ad revenue via social proof, mitigated by notifications and opt-outs.

Attack:
1. **Incentive Misalignment**: Partners fear public embarrassment from negative reactions to sensitive purchases. An opt-out does not prevent the initial notification shock or the stigma of being on a "monitoring" list.
2. **Irreversibility**: The proposal admits the feature is hard to withdraw. Launching a system that erodes trust (the core asset of a social network) creates a one-way door where the cost of failure exceeds the potential ad upside.
3. **Privacy Backlash**: "Sensitive" purchases combined with a default-on share model (even with opt-out) invites regulatory scrutiny and user exodus. The "notification step" is a friction point that reduces adoption while failing to prevent the perception of surveillance.

Alternatives:
- **Do Nothing**: Preserve trust; explore non-intrusive ad formats.
- **Opt-In Only**: Require explicit user consent before sharing any data. This reduces initial scale but eliminates the backlash risk and partner embarrassment.
- **Aggregated Insights**: Share anonymized trends with partners, not individual user actions with friends.

Pre-mortem: The feature launches, users revolt over privacy violations, major partners demand exit clauses due to brand safety concerns, and the company is forced to dismantle the system after significant reputation damage.

Blockers:
- The proposal states the feature is "hard to withdraw," creating an unacceptable downside risk for a speculative revenue gain.
- No evidence is provided that users want purchase data shared; the assumption that "more data = more revenue" ignores the trust cost.

Risks: User churn, partner abandonment, regulatory action.

Kill Criteria: If user opt-out rates exceed 20% in week one, or if any major partner threatens contract termination, the feature must be disabled immediately. However, given the irreversibility concern, the risk cannot be adequately capped post-launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
