First call: NO-GO. A search advertiser with no hardware DNA proposing a $50M acquisition of a pre-revenue, six-person OS team in a carrier-dominated market lacks a stated edge over incumbents; need for mobile web growth is not an edge.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the only viable open-source mobile OS now secures the future search interface before handset makers lock into proprietary ecosystems, leveraging openness to bypass carrier gatekeepers.

**Options:**
1. **Acquire:** High cost ($50M+), extreme risk (adoption, carrier resistance), low reversibility.
2. **Partner/License:** Lower cost, moderate risk, higher reversibility; avoids operational drag of building an OS.
3. **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, risk of being locked out of mobile search, but preserves capital for proven channels.
The proposal fails to demonstrate why acquisition beats partnering or waiting for the market to mature.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of "search everywhere," but assumes a mobile future that is not yet economically proven for an ad model.

**Pre-mortem:** In 2008, the OS is finished but no major carrier or handset maker adopts it due to fear of Google's data ambitions; the $50M is sunk, and the team dissipates. This is a one-way door with a high cost of delay only if competitors move first (no evidence they are).

**Evidence:** All figures ($50M, 6 people) are assumed from the proposal. No data exists on the startup's technical maturity or carrier interest. The "open source" lever is unproven in mobile.

**Blockers:**
1. **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits the market is dominated and carriers control distribution. It offers no fact showing why *this* tiny team can break that lock where others failed.
2. **Misaligned Capability:** A search company has no verified capability to run an OS vendor business or manage carrier relationships.

**Risks:** No revenue model, long payback, adoption failure. These are severe but secondary to the lack of a winning mechanism.

**Kill Criteria:** If no major handset maker signs a letter of intent within 12 months, terminate the project. Leading metric: Number of committed devices shipping with the OS by Q4 2006.

The absence of a concrete mechanism to overcome carrier/handset inertia makes this a speculative gamble, not a sound operator's bet.

DECISION: NO-GO
