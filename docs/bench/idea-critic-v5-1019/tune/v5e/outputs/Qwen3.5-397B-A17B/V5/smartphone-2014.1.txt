First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; entering a saturated two-platform market with a higher price, fewer apps, and a gimmicky 3D feature does not beat existing flagships.

Steelman: A device leveraging unique 3D navigation and instant product identification could create a niche for high-intent shoppers, bypassing app store dependency via web tools.

Attack:
1.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A cheaper tablet extension reaches shoppers without the risk of carrier exclusivity or competing directly with flagship ecosystems. The proposed phone costs more to produce (cameras, tracking) yet offers less utility (no mainstream apps).
2.  **Fit:** While aligned with retail goals, the method (hardware competition) contradicts the constraint of a smaller app store. Users prioritize communication and social apps over shopping features; a phone missing these is a non-starter regardless of shopping tools.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The device fails because carriers reject unsold inventory due to high unsubsidized pricing, and users return it due to app incompatibility. The 3D feature is a novelty that drains battery without solving a core need.
4.  **Evidence:** The market is dominated by two platforms; history shows forked OS attempts fail without massive ecosystem investment. The "app gap" is a known blocker, not a solvable risk here.
5.  **Blockers:** The core claim relies on users accepting a flagship price for a device lacking flagship apps. This contradicts verified consumer behavior where app availability drives platform choice. The "one-touch" feature can be implemented on existing devices via an app, rendering the hardware unnecessary.

Risks (non-blocking but severe): Carrier exclusivity limits reach; 3D tech may be a fad. However, the lack of a value proposition relative to cost is a definitive blocker.

Kill Criteria: If the projected attach rate of the shopping feature does not exceed the hardware subsidy cost within Q1, the project terminates.

The proposal attempts to solve a software/ecosystem problem with expensive hardware, ignoring the primary driver of smartphone adoption: the app ecosystem.

DECISION: NO-GO
