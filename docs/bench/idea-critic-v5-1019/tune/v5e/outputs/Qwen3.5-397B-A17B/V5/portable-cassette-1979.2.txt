First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (portable music) but fails to state a defensible edge: it relies on existing parts competitors can copy, lacks market research, and faces internal skepticism from the very channel (dealers) required for distribution. Need without a stated advantage over current solutions or a barrier to entry is not a viable plan.

Steelman: A low-cost, ultra-portable device leveraging existing components can create a new personal listening category for youth, bypassing the need for recording if the price and form factor are compelling enough.

However, the attack reveals critical blockers based on the provided facts:
1.  **No Defensive Moat:** Being built entirely from existing parts means competitors can instantly copy the design. Without a unique technology or brand lock-in stated, the first-mover advantage is negligible against established hi-fi rivals.
2.  **Channel Hostility:** Dealers and staff doubt the product. In 1979, retail staff education is crucial. If the channel believes the product is "antisocial" or broken (no recording), they will not push it, dooming shelf visibility regardless of consumer demand.
3.  **Inventory Risk:** The requirement for a large first production run to hit the price target creates massive downside exposure. If the "antisocial" perception holds true, the company is left with unsellable inventory that cannot be easily pivoted (no speaker/recording to fall back on).
4.  **Unverified Demand:** The explicit admission that "no market research shows demand" combined with the high fixed cost of the initial run violates basic capital discipline. You cannot bet a large production run on a hypothesis that your own sales force rejects.

Risks like "antisocial behavior" are secondary; the primary failure is the combination of high inventory risk, zero competitive protection, and a hostile distribution channel. A cheaper alternative exists: pilot a small batch to validate demand and dealer acceptance before committing to the large run required for the target price. The proposal as stated demands an irreversible large bet on unproven premises with no defensive strategy.

Blockers: No competitive moat (copyable parts); hostile distribution channel; large irreversible inventory bet without demand validation.
Risks: Consumer rejection of headphone-only format; competitor speed-to-market.

DECISION: NO-GO
