First call: NO-GO. The proposal addresses a real need (different management for declining vs. growing units) but lacks a stated edge: splitting brands immediately after a 60% price hike compounds customer anger rather than isolating it.

**Steelman:** Separating the brands now allows streaming to pivot aggressively without being dragged down by DVD logistics, while giving the DVD unit focused leadership to manage its decline profitably.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing & Perception:** Executing a brand split two months after a massive price increase looks like a deceptive tactic to hide the cost of the DVD service rather than a strategic evolution. Customers will perceive this as "double billing" for what was previously one ecosystem.
2.  **Friction:** Forcing existing users to create new accounts, manage separate queues, and handle two bills creates immediate friction. In a churn-sensitive environment post-price-hike, this friction is a catalyst for cancellation, not just confusion.
3.  **Value Destruction:** Severing data sharing (ratings/recommendations) destroys the unique competitive advantage of the combined model (hybrid discovery) without a proven replacement.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "do nothing" approach regarding branding, while managing the units internally with separate P&Ls, achieves the management goal without the customer-facing chaos. A softer "unbundling" with a single login and bill would test the market reaction without the nuclear option of a full brand divorce.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The 60% price hike occurred in July; this split is proposed for September. The proximity guarantees the market will link the two events as a coordinated extraction of value.
*   **Fact:** The proposal admits to "customer confusion" and "two logins/bills" as risks but offers no mitigation other than "different management." This is an operational benefit sold as a customer feature.

**Risks:** Mass churn due to perceived greed; loss of cross-pollination between formats; brand dilution for the streaming service by association with the "dying" DVD brand if the split is seen as punitive.

**Kill Criteria:** If customer support tickets regarding "billing confusion" or "cancellation" spike >20% in the first 14 days post-launch, revert to a single-brand, unified billing interface immediately.

The operational logic is sound, but the customer experience strategy is catastrophic given the recent pricing context. The plan fails as proposed because it prioritizes internal structure over external perception during a crisis of trust.

DECISION: NO-GO
