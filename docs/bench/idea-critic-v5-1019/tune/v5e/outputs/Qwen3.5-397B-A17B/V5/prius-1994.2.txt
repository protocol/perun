First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: cheap fuel, zero regulatory mandate, and no competitor movement indicate no immediate market pull or cost advantage to justify the massive upfront investment and technical risk.

Steelman: Being first with a proven, durable hybrid powertrain in 1997 creates an insurmountable technological moat and brand leadership before regulators force the issue.

Attack:
1.  **Economics:** With cheap fuel, the payback period for the consumer on the higher vehicle cost is likely decades, killing volume sales. The proposal admits "no near-term demand signal."
2.  **Technology:** Nickel-metal-hydride durability is unproven for mass production. A failure here triggers catastrophic warranty exposure that outweighs first-mover prestige.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital while competitors validate the technology. A "pilot fleet" limits warranty risk compared to a "mass-production" commitment.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The program fails because battery degradation after 3 years leads to class-action lawsuits and brand destruction, while sales remain negligible due to fuel prices.
5.  **Evidence:** The proposal's own facts (cheap fuel, unproven battery, no mandate) contradict the core claim of viable mass production.

Blockers:
*   **Unit Economics:** Cheap fuel removes the consumer incentive to pay the hybrid premium; the proposal offers no data showing otherwise.
*   **Technical Viability:** Unproven battery durability for volume production presents a binary failure risk (mass recalls) that the plan does not mitigate.
*   **Market Demand:** Explicitly stated as absent; no regulatory or competitive pressure exists to create it.

Risks:
*   High R&D sunk costs if the technology pivots.
*   Reputation damage if the "bold public schedule" is missed.

Kill Criteria Check: The proposal names risks (cost, reliability, demand) but offers no caps, pilots, or metrics to manage them, instead committing to full mass production. The stated facts directly point toward these risks materializing as failures (e.g., no demand signal = no sales). This is not a calculated bet; it is a gamble on unproven premises without a safety net. A sound approach would be a limited pilot to prove durability and gauge price sensitivity before a corporate-level mass-production commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
