First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it attempts to split the difference between two distinct segments without a fact showing why a "middle" product beats the existing extremes for young men.

**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men who reject the "diet" label but want fewer carbs than regular soda, willing to pay a premium for this specific positioning.

**Attack:**
1.  **Segmentation Failure:** The core risk is that the product satisfies neither group. Regular drinkers want taste (sugar); diet drinkers want zero calories. A "half-sugar" drink offers compromised taste with non-zero calories, likely appealing to no one. The proposal admits this risk but offers no evidence (e.g., focus groups) that young men specifically desire this compromise.
2.  **Price Barrier:** Launching at a premium price creates a high hurdle for trial. If the taste is compromised (as suspected in risk #1), the premium price ensures rapid rejection. There is no stated advantage justifying a higher cost than the flagship regular cola.
3.  **Trend Timing:** The low-carb trend is flagged as a potential fad. Investing in a large campaign and new SKU for a fading trend is capital inefficient. If the trend dies, the product has no standalone merit.
4.  **Competition:** A rival is already launching a similar drink. Entering a crowded, unproven niche with a "me-too" product at a premium price is a weak strategic position.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal fails to identify a specific fact showing why young men prefer half-sugar over zero-sugar or full-sugar. Need alone is not an edge.
*   **Contradictory Economics:** Premium pricing requires superior perceived value. A product defined by what it *lacks* (half the sugar) rather than what it *adds* struggles to command a premium, especially against established alternatives.

**Risks:**
*   Cannibalization of regular sales without gaining diet users.
*   Trend expiration before ROI is achieved.
*   Confused brand messaging.

The proposal relies on the assumption that "middle" is a viable market position without evidence. It fails the "beat do nothing" test because "do nothing" preserves margin while monitoring if the mid-calorie trend proves real via the rival's experiment.

DECISION: NO-GO
