First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price, unproven demand, and regulatory ambiguity are negatives, not advantages over existing transport.

Steelman: A novel, self-balancing device creates a new micro-mobility category for early adopters despite cost and legal gray areas.

Attack:
1. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" costs $0 and avoids regulatory fights. Walking, cycling, and cars are proven, cheaper, and legal. The proposal offers no evidence that $5,000 buyers exist in sufficient volume to fill a 40,000-unit factory.
2. **Fit:** Launching a niche, expensive product with unclear legal status contradicts mass-market viability. The "conspicuous" risk directly limits adoption to a tiny fringe.
3. **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: The device is banned from sidewalks and roads simultaneously, rendering it unusable. The high price restricts sales to novelty seekers, leaving the factory idle. The "institutional" market proves too small to sustain production.
4. **Evidence:** The proposal admits legal status is "unclear," price is "high," and non-institutional use is "unproven." These are not assumptions; they are stated facts within the proposal that contradict the core claim of a successful launch. There is no data showing demand at $5,000.
5. **Blockers:** The stated facts themselves are blockers. A product with no clear legal operating environment cannot launch successfully. A $5,000 price point for an unproven utility with no identified mass market is a financial failure waiting to happen. The factory capacity (40,000 units) assumes demand that the proposal explicitly states is unproven.

Risks (non-blocking if blockers weren't present): Rider conspicuousness, limited cargo capacity. However, since the legal and economic fundamentals are broken, these are secondary.

Kill Criteria: If legal status remains undefined in key markets within 6 months, or if pre-orders do not cover 10% of factory capacity before full production starts, the project must pivot or stop.

The proposal asks to commit to massive fixed costs (factory) based on hope rather than verified demand or legal clearance. It fails the basic operator test: do not build a factory for a product you cannot legally guarantee people can ride, nor prove they will buy at that price.

DECISION: NO-GO
