First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it attempts to sell a product category (small step-throughs) to a market explicitly defined as preferring "large machines," without citing evidence of latent demand for commuters or students that incumbents ignore.

**Steelman:** The US market contains an unaddressed segment of commuters and students who need affordable, easy-to-ride transport and are currently underserved by the "rough," large-machine offerings, making non-traditional retail channels a viable entry point.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The claim that Americans will buy small bikes contradicts the stated fact that the US market favors large machines. No data is provided to prove the existence of the "commuter/student" segment or their willingness to buy a Japanese brand. The "edge" is assumed, not proven.
2.  **Blocker (Product Viability):** The proposal admits the company's larger models fail at US highway speeds. While the 50cc is intended for city use, the brand's reputation for reliability is unproven in the US. If the brand is unknown and the only available larger models are defective for US conditions, the brand equity required to sell the small bike may be impossible to build.
3.  **Better Way:** "Do nothing" preserves scarce foreign currency. A cheaper alternative is exporting only to markets with existing demand for small bikes or licensing technology, rather than the high-cost, high-risk move of opening a subsidiary and forging a new retail channel.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The subsidiary opens, spends the currency reserve, and finds zero demand because the "rough" motorcycle image deters the target demographic, or the 50cc is legally classified as a moped requiring different licensing, stalling sales. The company runs out of cash.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal lists risks (brand unknown, product mismatch, currency cost) but offers no specific metric, pilot, or kill criterion to validate the core assumption that Americans want small bikes. It asks for a full subsidiary launch based on an unverified hypothesis. The absence of a pilot or cap on the currency spend, combined with the direct contradiction of stated market preferences, constitutes a blocker.

Blockers: Stated market preference contradicts product; no evidence of target segment; admitted product failure (larger models) threatens brand; no pilot/cap on currency risk.
Risks: Channel rejection, regulatory classification of 50cc, brand stigma.

DECISION: NO-GO
