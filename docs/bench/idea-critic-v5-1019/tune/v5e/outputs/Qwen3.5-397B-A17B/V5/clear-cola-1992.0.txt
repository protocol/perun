First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a need (slowing growth) and a positive test signal, but fails to state a defensible edge proving this specific extension beats the "do nothing" or "line extension" alternatives on a national scale given the explicit risks of non-repeat and cannibalization.

**Steelman:** A clear cola leverages "purity" trends to capture novelty seekers and lapsed drinkers without alienating core fans, creating a net-new revenue stream that outweighs internal cannibalization.

**Attack:**
1.  **Evidence Gap:** Test markets notoriously overstate demand for novelty items (the "novelty bump"). The proposal admits buyers may not repeat. Without a verified repeat-purchase rate from the test (not just initial trial), national launch is a bet on a one-time event, not a brand extension.
2.  **Cannibalization vs. Growth:** The proposal admits the flagship may be cannibalized. If the clear cola merely shifts existing volume while costing millions in new marketing, the net margin likely declines. There is no evidence presented that the "purer" positioning converts non-cola drinkers.
3.  **Competitive Moat:** If the concept works, rivals can launch copies instantly (low barrier to entry). The proposal offers no exclusive asset (patent, unique supply chain) to defend the position.
4.  **Door Class:** This is a one-way door with high sunk costs (national campaign, production retooling). Once launched, retracting damages brand equity more than never launching.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal lists risks but offers no kill criteria (e.g., "Launch only if repeat purchase > X% in test"). It asks for a binary "go" based on incomplete data. A sound operator does not bet the brand on a "positive response" that explicitly includes warnings about non-repeat and cannibalization. The absence of a capped pilot or a specific repeat-purchase threshold makes the national rollout reckless.

**Blockers:**
*   Missing verified repeat-purchase data (admitted risk of non-repeat).
*   No mathematical proof that incremental volume exceeds cannibalization + marketing cost.
*   No defensive moat against immediate copycats.

**Risks:** Novelty fade, brand dilution, wasted capex. These are currently unmitigated blockers because the proposal assumes the test market "positive response" equals national viability without adjusting for the stated flaws.

DECISION: NO-GO
