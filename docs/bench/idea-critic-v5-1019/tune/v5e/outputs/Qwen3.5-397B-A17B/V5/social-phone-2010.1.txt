First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high cost and no apps are disadvantages against incumbents, and sunk costs do not create value.

**Steelman:** Leveraging a popular messaging brand to lock teens into a closed ecosystem via a single carrier, bypassing the need for a broad app store initially.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Edge:** Teenagers prioritize social connectivity and cost. A mandatory high-cost data plan removes the price advantage required for this demographic. Lacking an app store while rivals offer thousands creates an immediate functional deficit with no compensating benefit stated. "Sunk costs" of two years on the wrong OS is a liability, not an asset; it suggests further investment in a losing technical strategy.
2.  **Better Way:** The cheaper, safer alternative is porting the popular messaging brand to existing platforms (iOS/Android) or the company's own established mobile platform, avoiding the risk of a new OS and carrier lock-in. Doing nothing preserves capital until a viable differentiator exists.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The phones launch, teens reject the high monthly fee and lack of apps, sales flatline, and the brand equity of the acquired messaging phone is diluted by association with a failed hardware platform.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits rivals have large catalogs and the price point is high for the target. These are confirmed blockers.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price:** Data plan cost equals full smartphone plans, violating the budget constraints of the target demographic (teenagers).
*   **Feature Gap:** No app store at launch vs. rival large catalogs creates an insurmountable utility gap.
*   **Strategy:** Building a new OS instead of using the company's own platform increases risk and cost without stated justification.

**Risks:** Carrier exclusivity limits reach; brand confusion between messaging and hardware.

**Kill Criteria:** If the target demographic cannot afford the total cost of ownership or refuses a device without key third-party apps, the venture fails. The proposal provides no metric to prove otherwise.

The plan fails on price, features, and strategic fit. Sunk costs are irrelevant to future viability.

DECISION: NO-GO
