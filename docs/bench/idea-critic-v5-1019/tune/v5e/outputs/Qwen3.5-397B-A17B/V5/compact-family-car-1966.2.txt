First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state a competitive edge; "new plant" and "dedicated network" are costs, not advantages against established low-price rivals.

**Steelman:** A dedicated 1100cc compact captures a specific family segment unreachable through existing channels, justifying the new plant's capacity.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Stated Edge:** Rivals already sell small cars at low prices. The proposal offers no fact explaining why buyers would switch to this new entrant (e.g., superior fuel economy, unique feature, brand trust). Need alone is not an edge.
2.  **Capacity Trap:** Building a dedicated dealer network is slow and capital-intensive. This delays volume ramp-up, directly contradicting the requirement to run the new plant near capacity immediately to pay off investment.
3.  **Market Mismatch:** With risks that buyers prefer either cheaper or larger cars, an mid-range 1100cc option without a defined value proposition is the most vulnerable position.
4.  **Alternatives:** Utilizing existing dealer networks or contracting production to test demand before building dedicated infrastructure would reduce risk and improve reversibility. The proposal rejects these implicitly but offers no evidence why they fail.

**Blockers:**
*   **Missing Edge:** No fact demonstrates how this car beats existing low-price rivals.
*   **Contradictory Logic:** The plan requires high immediate volume (to pay off the plant) but proposes a slow-to-build sales channel (dedicated dealers) in a crowded market.

**Risks:**
*   Dealer network cost overruns.
*   Preference shift to cheaper/larger segments.
*   Plant underutilization.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal lists risks but treats them as hurdles rather than blockers. However, the absence of a stated advantage over incumbents is a factual gap that prevents a "GO." The internal contradiction between the need for immediate capacity utilization and the slow rollout of a new dealer network is a structural failure in the plan as proposed.

Without a verified reason for customers to choose this car over cheaper incumbents, and with a go-to-market strategy that jeopardizes the plant's financial viability, the claim fails the operator test.

DECISION: NO-GO
