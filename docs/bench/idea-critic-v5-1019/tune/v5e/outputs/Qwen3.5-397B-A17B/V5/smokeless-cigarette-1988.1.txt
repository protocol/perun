First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a core functional risk (smokers may dislike taste and lighting mechanism) and a regulatory blocker (health claims) without evidence that these are solved. Need alone is not an edge; a "cleaner" cigarette that tastes bad or cannot be legally marketed as such has no stated advantage over existing products.

**Steelman:** A heated tobacco product offers reduced smoke and a familiar ritual, potentially retaining smokers who want a cleaner option, justifying the sunk development costs if adoption is proven.

**Attack:**
1.  **Product-Market Fit:** The proposal admits smokers "may dislike the taste" and find it "complicated." In 1988, convenience is the primary driver of cigarette adoption. A product requiring new behavior (lighting a carbon tip) with inferior sensory feedback fails the basic utility test against standard cigarettes.
2.  **Regulatory Trap:** Promoting it "widely as a cleaner way to smoke" invites immediate regulatory scrutiny. Without pre-approved health claims, marketing is legally constrained, destroying the "widespread promotion" strategy.
3.  **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** The "very large sum" spent is irrelevant to future viability. Launching a flawed product to recover costs compounds the loss.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids reputational damage and regulatory fines; preserves capital.
*   **Limited Pilot:** Run a strictly monitored test market *without* wide promotion to validate taste and usability first. This isolates the functional risk before committing to a full launch.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, smokers reject the taste, regulators issue cease-and-desist orders on "cleaner" claims, and the brand suffers a high-profile failure. The complexity creates a barrier to entry that no amount of marketing can overcome.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Core Utility:** The proposal explicitly states users may dislike the fundamental experience (taste/lighting).
*   **Regulatory Exposure:** The marketing plan relies on claims likely to be objected to by regulators.

**Risks:** High development cost loss (already sunk), brand reputation damage.

**Kill Criteria:** If test market data shows <20% repeat purchase rate due to taste or complexity, or if regulators formally object to the "cleaner" framing, the project stops immediately.

The proposal asks to "launch and promote widely" despite admitting the product might be disliked and the marketing illegal. This is a speculative gamble with confirmed downsides.

DECISION: NO-GO
