First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it admits inferior content, poor quality, limited distribution, and high costs while threatening a profitable core business without a clear path to offset cannibalization or beat incoming giants.

**Steelman:** Offering free streaming acts as a defensive retention tool to slow subscriber churn as the market shifts, leveraging existing cash flow to buy time for digital transition despite current limitations.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves high-margin DVD profits longer. A "paid add-on" tests willingness to pay without devaluing the core product or accelerating cannibalization. The proposed "free inclusion" subsidizes a low-value product with high-value revenue, accelerating the decline of the cash cow without guaranteeing digital dominance.
2.  **Fit:** The strategy contradicts the immediate north star of profitability. Entering a capital-intensive war against tech giants with an inferior product (old titles, poor quality) while licensing costs are high is a direct route to margin collapse.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 24 months. Cause: Rapid cannibalization of DVD revenue by a free, low-quality stream that users treat as a bonus rather than a primary service, while licensing costs bleed cash. Competitors with deep pockets and better content capture the premium digital market, leaving the company with neither DVD nor streaming dominance.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the catalogue is small/old, quality is poor, broadband is limited, and rights are expensive. These are not risks; they are stated facts that negate the value proposition.

**Blockers:**
*   **Negative Unit Economics:** High licensing costs + technology investment vs. low perceived value (old/poor quality) creates a loss leader with no stated conversion or upsell mechanism to justify the burn.
*   **Strategic Exposure:** Giving away a substitute for the profitable core product (DVD) without a differentiated digital advantage accelerates revenue collapse before the digital model can sustain itself.

**Risks:** Competitor entry, broadband adoption rates. These are secondary to the fundamental flaw of devaluing the core product prematurely.

**Kill Criteria:** If cannibalization exceeds 5% of DVD revenue within 6 months without a corresponding increase in total subscriber lifetime value, the program must halt.

The plan fails the basic test of beating "do nothing" or a "paid tier" on the stated facts. It trades certain high-margin revenue for uncertain, low-quality digital presence against stronger rivals.

DECISION: NO-GO
