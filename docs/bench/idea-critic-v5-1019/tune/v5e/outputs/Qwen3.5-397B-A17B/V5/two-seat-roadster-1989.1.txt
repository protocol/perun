First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a market void but fails to state an edge; "affordable" and "lightweight" are aspirations, not facts proving this maker can beat incumbents or overcome the stated shrinkage.

Steelman: A mid-size manufacturer leverages existing front-engine, rear-drive components to produce a low-cost, lightweight roadster, capturing neglected demand before safety regulations make the segment impossible.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital in a shrinking segment. A cheaper alternative is a special edition of an existing model, not a new platform. The proposal does not explain why a new launch beats modifying current stock.
2.  **Fit:** The company lacks sports-car heritage. Entering a niche requiring specific brand equity without it increases marketing costs and failure risk.
3.  **Evidence:** The claim of "affordability" is unsupported. Safety rules are cited as a cost driver, yet no calculation shows how this maker absorbs those costs better than mass-market players who already abandoned the niche.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The car launches, early enthusiasts buy the limited run, and sales collapse because the broader market rejects a two-seat car with no practical utility. The company is left with obsolete tooling.
5.  **Blockers:** The core claim rests on "affordable" production. Without evidence of a cost advantage (e.g., shared parts ratio, specific manufacturing technique), the math does not work against established safety costs. The stated fact that "mass-market makers regard [this] as a dead niche" suggests they possess data this proposal ignores. A plan that contradicts the consensus of well-funded competitors without a stated technical or cost breakthrough is a blocker.

Risks: Market shrinkage, limited buyer pool, regulatory costs, lack of heritage, sales fade. These are severe but would be risks if the edge were proven. Since the edge is missing, they confirm the NO-GO.

The proposal asks to enter a dying segment without explaining *how* this specific company succeeds where others failed. It relies on hope, not a stated advantage.

DECISION: NO-GO
