First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; higher local prices and an entrenched competitor negate the "discount" advantage without evidence of a structural cost benefit or unique assortment that beats incumbents.

Steelman: Rapidly saturating a neighboring market with 130+ stores leverages existing lease opportunities to achieve immediate scale before competitors react, accepting short-term logistical friction for long-term dominance.

Attack:
1.  **Fit & Edge:** Entering a market with higher prices than home contradicts the core "discount" identity unless the retailer can undercut the entrenched local leader. The proposal admits local prices are higher on some goods but fails to prove the retailer can sell cheaper *there* after absorbing cross-border logistics, new regulatory compliance, and bilingual labeling costs. Without a proven cost advantage, the brand value proposition collapses.
2.  **Better Way:** "Do nothing" or a pilot of 5–10 stores is superior. Opening ~140 sites in two years while simultaneously building an unproven supply chain and inventory system creates a single point of catastrophic failure. A staged approach allows validation of the cross-border model without risking capital on 130 leases simultaneously.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: The new inventory system crashes under real-world load, causing stockouts in 80% of stores while lease obligations mount. The entrenched competitor engages in a price war that the new, inefficient supply chain cannot sustain. The brand is perceived as "expensive foreigner" rather than "trusted discount."
4.  **Evidence Gap:** No data supports the claim that the domestic loyal following translates to this new country or that the supply chain can handle the complexity immediately. The "pace" risk is acknowledged but not mitigated.

Blockers:
-   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits higher local prices and new costs but claims a discount model will work. This is a mathematical contradiction without proof of superior margin or sourcing.
-   **Unproven Core Systems:** Launching 130 stores on a "new" and "unproven" inventory system is operational suicide.

Risks:
-   Currency/regulatory friction.
-   Brand dilution if prices aren't actually lower.

The plan fails the basic test of having a defensible advantage over the status quo or a safer alternative.

DECISION: NO-GO
