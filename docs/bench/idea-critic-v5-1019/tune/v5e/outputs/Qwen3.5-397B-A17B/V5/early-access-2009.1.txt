First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a rough, unfinished state with no marketing budget and a solo developer incapable of supporting a large audience; these stated facts remove the route to upside (sales) without offering a counter-mechanism.

**Steelman:** Releasing immediately converts player feedback into a development roadmap, validating demand before further unpaid work.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Capacity vs. Volume):** The proposal admits the developer "cannot support a large audience." A public sale inherently risks attracting more users than one person can support. Negative reviews from unsupported early adopters will permanently tank the product's reputation, killing future sales even if the game improves.
2.  **Blocker (Value Proposition):** Buyers in 2009 expect playable products. Selling a "rough" game without a publisher's trust signal or marketing budget to manage expectations relies on buyers ignoring the very flaws the proposal admits exist. The stated risk that "players may stop buying" is not a possibility but a near-certainty given the lack of content completion.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" alternative (finish privately) or a "free technical demo" avoids the reputational suicide of selling broken software. A free demo gathers the same feedback without the contractual obligation of a sale or the backlash of charging for incompleteness.
4.  **Fit:** The strategy lacks a stated edge. "Need money/time" is not an edge; it is a constraint. Without a unique hook or existing community, a rough solo game cannot beat established competitors or justify its price tag.

**Pre-mortem:** The game launches, players encounter bugs and lack of content, leave negative reviews citing "unfinished" and "no support," and the developer burns out trying to answer forums. The project dies in three months.

**Conclusion:** The proposal's own facts confirm the risks are active blockers. Selling an unsupported, rough product with no marketing is a guaranteed failure mode. The plan must be revised to either finish the core loop privately or release a free demo first.

DECISION: NO-GO
