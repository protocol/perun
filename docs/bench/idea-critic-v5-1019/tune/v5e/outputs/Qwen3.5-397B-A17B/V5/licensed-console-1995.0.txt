First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; small royalties and dominant incumbents mean this plan does not beat existing options for the licensee or consumers.

Steelman: Licensing converts fixed R&D risk into variable royalty income, allowing market entry without capital expenditure.
Attack: The economics fail. "Small royalties" cannot motivate a toy company to fight "established console makers" who dominate shelf space and mindshare. Without the licensor's marketing muscle, the brand connection fails, rendering the royalty stream negligible. The licensee bears the loss risk while the licensor gains little upside.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Preserves capital, avoids brand dilution in a low-margin segment.
2. Direct launch: High cost, but retains control over branding and margin (requires capital).
3. Proposed license: Low cost, but cedes control for negligible return; likely to fail due to misaligned incentives.

Fit: Entering a consumer market via a passive partner contradicts the need for strong brand positioning against dominators.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, sells poorly due to weak marketing, and the licensor is associated with a cheap, forgotten device. One-way door: Brand damage is irreversible. Cost of delay: Minimal; the console market is not waiting for this specific passive entry.

Evidence: The proposal's own facts contradict its success. Dominant competitors require aggressive marketing to displace; the plan explicitly leaves marketing to a partner with "small" financial incentive. The math does not work: Low incentive + High competition = No effort = No sales.

Blockers:
1. Incentive mismatch: Small royalties cannot drive the aggressive marketing needed to unseat incumbents.
2. No stated edge: The proposal admits no advantage in cost, feature, or distribution over current leaders.

Risks: Brand dilution, opportunity cost of management attention.

Kill criteria: If the licensee cannot guarantee minimum marketing spend equal to 20% of projected revenue, stop. However, the structural flaw (low royalty vs. high competition) is fatal regardless of specific metrics. The plan relies on the partner succeeding where the licensor fears to tread, without paying them enough to try.

DECISION: NO-GO
