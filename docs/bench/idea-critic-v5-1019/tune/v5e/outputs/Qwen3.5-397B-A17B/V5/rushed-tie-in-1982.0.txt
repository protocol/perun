First call: NO-GO. A stated need (Christmas release) without a stated edge (why this specific plan beats existing market options or ensures quality) is a failure as proposed.

**Steelman:** Compressing development to five weeks with a single engineer leverages the narrow window between the July deal closure and the Christmas sales peak to maximize revenue before demand decays.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capacity Mismatch:** The proposal assumes one programmer can deliver a "hit" quality product in 35 days. Normal development cycles are months or years. A single resource cannot absorb the complexity of a film license game in this timeframe without catastrophic quality reduction.
2.  **Inventory Risk:** Ordering a "very large production run" based on a rushed, low-quality product creates a massive downside. If the game is poor (highly probable given the timeline), the company is left with high fixed costs (license + inventory) and zero revenue.
3.  **Missing Edge:** The proposal offers no mechanism to ensure the game is good, only that it is fast. Speed without quality is not a competitive advantage; it is a liability. The "edge" of being first is nullified if the product is unplayable.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Five weeks is far shorter than normal development.
*   **Contradiction:** The plan requires "hit" quality (implied by the high license fee) but allocates resources for a prototype or broken build. The stated facts show the target users (gamers) will likely reject a broken product, removing the route to upside.
*   **Alternative:** A smaller, capped production run or a delayed launch to ensure quality reaches the same upside (sales) with significantly less downside risk (inventory write-off).

**Risks:** Poor quality, unsold inventory, brand damage. These are not mere risks here; the timeline makes poor quality a near-certainty, transforming the inventory risk into a guaranteed financial loss.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal lacks a pilot, a quality gate, or a capped initial run. It commits to maximum exposure (large run) with minimum validation (5 weeks, 1 dev). The stated facts already demonstrate the core claim (successful Christmas hit) is unsupported by the execution plan.

DECISION: NO-GO
