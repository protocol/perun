First call: NO-GO. The stated price ($700) and the requirement for licensees to profit on hardware contradict the established market dynamic where incumbents sell at a loss to secure software royalties.

**Steelman:** Licensing shifts capital risk to partners, enabling rapid market saturation if the high price signals premium value to early adopters.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $700, the console is 3-4x the cost of competitors. Early adopters pay for novelty, but not typically for a 300% premium without exclusive, must-have software launched day-one. The proposal mentions "high-end graphics" but no killer app lineup.
2.  **Incentive Misalignment:** Incumbents subsidize hardware to lock users into their ecosystem. Here, licensees must make a profit on the box *and* pay royalties. This double-margin structure makes the final price uncompetitive compared to rivals selling at a loss. Licensees will hesitate to manufacture a product that is both expensive to make and expensive to buy.
3.  **Market Friction:** Entering a crowded market with a premium-priced, licensed hardware model ignores the "razor-and-blades" standard. Without a installed base, game developers will not prioritize the platform, reducing the console's value proposition further.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids catastrophic capital burn in a hostile market.
*   **Subsidized Model:** The start-up raises capital to subsidize hardware, selling near cost to drive adoption, recouping via software. This matches competitor tactics but requires significant upfront funding.
*   **Niche Peripheral:** Target a specific high-end segment without attempting to set the "standard" immediately.

**Pre-mortem:** The console launches, sells poorly due to price, licensees cancel production runs to avoid inventory losses, and the start-up runs out of cash before securing a second wind of developer support.

**Blocker:** The economic model (profit on hardware + royalty) guarantees a retail price that excludes the mass market required to make the software ecosystem viable. The proposal's own facts (price, licensee profit requirement) contradict the goal of setting the "standard."

**Risks:** Lack of exclusive titles, competitor retaliation, licensee churn.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders do not cover licensee tooling costs within 6 months, or if no major third-party developer commits to day-one launch titles.

DECISION: NO-GO
