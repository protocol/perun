First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it offers "larger screen" and "marketing spend" against an entrenched ecosystem, which are not structural advantages over the incumbent's network effects.

**Steelman:** A late entrant can displace a dominant music platform by leveraging superior hardware integration and a unified store-subscription model to bypass partner friction, assuming wireless sharing creates a viral loop the leader cannot match.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A "cheaper alternative" would be licensing the existing dominant store rather than building a competing ecosystem from scratch. Building a store and device simultaneously doubles execution risk.
2.  **Fit:** Dropping the copy-protection standard the company previously championed alienates current partners (labels, device makers) without a guaranteed replacement volume. This burns bridges before new ones are built.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The device fails because labels restrict the key differentiator (wireless sharing) due to piracy fears, leaving a costly device with a restricted library. The "large marketing budget" cannot buy loyalty if the content ecosystem is fractured.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no clear price advantage" and "late to market." In network-effect businesses, being late without a 10x cost benefit or exclusive content is usually fatal. The "larger screen" is a marginal feature, not a category killer.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Strategy:** Abandoning the copy-protection standard undermines existing partner relationships required to secure the music rights needed for the store.
*   **No Stated Edge:** "Marketing budget" and "larger screen" do not overcome the incumbent's ecosystem lock-in or the "late to market" deficit.
*   **Dependency Risk:** The core value prop (sharing) relies on label approval, which the proposal admits is uncertain ("labels may restrict").

**Risks:** High capital burn, partner retaliation, low adoption due to late entry.

**Kill Criteria:** If label agreements for unrestricted sharing are not signed before hardware tooling begins, the project stops. If pre-orders do not reach 10% of the incumbent's quarterly sales within 6 months, pivot to software-only.

The proposal asks to fight a war on two fronts (hardware + store) with no unique weapon against a fortified enemy.

DECISION: NO-GO
