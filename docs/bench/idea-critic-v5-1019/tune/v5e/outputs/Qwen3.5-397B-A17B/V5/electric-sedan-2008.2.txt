First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it asserts a vision but offers no fact showing why this specific plan beats established competitors who already possess capital, factories, and supply chains.

Steelman: Committing exclusively to EVs in 2008 creates a first-mover brand monopoly if the company can secure production. However, the attack on this claim is fatal based on the provided facts. The proposal explicitly admits the company lacks the capital for the factory and batteries, yet the plan requires buying a large factory. This is a mathematical contradiction: you cannot commit to a capital-intensive strategy without the capital to execute it.

Alternatives: "Do nothing" preserves cash. A "cheaper alternative" would be contracting existing manufacturers (low capex, high margin loss) or building a niche sports car first (proven by the industry's actual history) to fund the sedan later. The proposed "all-in" factory purchase beats neither option on the stated facts because it is impossible to fund.

Fit: While the vision aligns with a future north star, the 2008 timing clashes with the stated reality of scarce charging stations and dominant incumbents. Without a bridge to solve the capital gap, the vision is irrelevant.

Pre-mortem: The company buys the factory, exhausts its cash on retooling, cannot afford battery packs at scale, and declares bankruptcy before the first unit ships because incumbents undercut prices using existing efficient lines.

Evidence: The proposal's own figures contradict the core claim. It states the need for "far more capital than the company has" while simultaneously proposing to "buy" the primary capital asset (the factory).

Blockers:
1. Capital Insufficiency: The plan requires purchasing a large factory and funding battery production, but the facts state the company has "far less capital" than needed. This is a hard stop.
2. No Stated Edge: There is no fact provided that explains how a cash-poor newcomer defeats established makers with deep pockets and existing infrastructure.

Risks: Charging scarcity, incumbent retaliation, and lack of mass production experience are severe risks, but the capital blocker makes them moot.

DECISION: NO-GO
