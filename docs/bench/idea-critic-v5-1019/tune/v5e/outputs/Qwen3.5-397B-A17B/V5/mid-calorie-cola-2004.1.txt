First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it offers a compromised product at a higher price to a segment already served by a superior low-calorie alternative, relying on a potentially fleeting trend.

**Steelman:** A mid-calorie cola captures young men who reject the "diet" label but want fewer carbs than regular soda, leveraging a premium price to signal quality amidst a low-carb cultural shift.

**Attack:**
1.  **Options:** "Do nothing" protects margins and avoids cannibalizing the growing diet segment. The cheaper alternative is reformulating existing lines or marketing diet cola differently to men. This plan costs more (premium ingredients, large campaign) for uncertain gain.
2.  **Fit:** Assuming the goal is growth, this misreads the binary nature of the market: consumers choose between taste (regular) and weight control (diet). A middle ground satisfies neither, creating a "jack of all trades, master of none" product.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The product fails because the low-carb trend proves transient, the premium price blocks trial among price-sensitive young men, and the core diet audience rejects the extra calories while regular drinkers reject the altered taste.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the target demographic already buys diet versions and that a rival is entering the same risky space. The "half-sugar" claim is a disadvantage, not a feature, for both adjacent segments.
5.  **Blockers:** The stated facts reveal a fatal flaw: the product solves no urgent problem for the target users better than existing options. The "premium price" combined with "half-sugar" creates a negative value proposition compared to established diet colas. The risk of the trend being a fad is a known variable that invalidates the long-term ROI required for a "large campaign."

**Blockers:**
*   No stated edge over existing diet options for the target demographic; the proposal admits the audience already buys diet.
*   Premium pricing on an inferior (half-sugar) product creates a negative value proposition.

**Risks:**
*   Low-carb trend is a fad.
*   Cannibalization of diet sales without net new growth.
*   Rival saturation in a niche market.

The proposal fails the fundamental test of offering a distinct advantage. It attempts to split the difference in a binary choice market, charging more for less satisfaction.

DECISION: NO-GO
