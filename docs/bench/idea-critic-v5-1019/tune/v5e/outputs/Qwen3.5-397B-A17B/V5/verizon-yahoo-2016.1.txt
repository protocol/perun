First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it attempts to solve a "need" (scale) by acquiring a deteriorating asset without a credible mechanism to reverse the decline or leverage the carrier's specific strengths.

**Steelman:** Combining two distressed media assets creates sufficient scale to technically bid on large campaigns, theoretically forcing a breakthrough in mobile ad relevance through aggregated data.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Edge:** The proposal admits the target has falling traffic, falling revenue, repeated management failures, and a declining business model. The acquirer has zero history in consumer media. There is no fact presented suggesting this specific carrier can fix what specialized media CEOs could not. Need is not an edge; desperation is not a strategy.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves $4.8B. Investing that capital into building mobile-first ad tech or partnering with an existing innovator offers higher upside with no legacy drag. The cheapest alternative (organic build) avoids the integration risk entirely.
3.  **Fit:** The move contradicts a sound north star for a carrier: leveraging network infrastructure. Instead, it pivots to a low-margin, high-churn software business where the acquirer has no competence.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** One year post-close, the combined entity's revenue continues to drop faster than the sum of its parts due to cultural clash and talent flight. The $4.8B is written down as goodwill impairment. This is a two-way door only in name; the capital is gone, and the distraction is permanent.
5.  **Evidence:** The proposal's own facts constitute the blocker: "falling for years," "repeated management changes," and "no history in consumer media." These are not risks to be managed; they are evidence that the core claim (we can make this work) is false as proposed.

**Blockers:**
*   The target's fundamental business mechanics are broken (falling traffic/revenue) with no stated plan to fix them other than "combine."
*   The acquirer lacks the specific competency (consumer media) required to execute the turnaround.
*   No stated edge exists to beat incumbent platforms; scale alone does not create relevance.

**Risks:** Integration failure, continued revenue decline, capital misallocation.

The proposal asks to spend $4.8B on a problem it cannot solve, using an asset that is actively rotting, without a single fact demonstrating why this buyer is the one to succeed.

DECISION: NO-GO
