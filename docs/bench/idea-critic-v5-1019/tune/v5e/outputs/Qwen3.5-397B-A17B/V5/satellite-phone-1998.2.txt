First call: NO-GO. The plan lacks a stated edge over rapidly improving ground networks; high cost and poor indoor performance are fatal disadvantages, not differentiators.

Steelman: This is the only solution for true global coverage where terrestrial cells cannot reach, serving urgent communication needs for specific high-value users regardless of cost or device bulk.

Attack: The proposal fails the "cheaper alternative" test. Ground cellular is spreading fast, offering lower costs, better indoor reception, and smaller handsets. The target market (international business travelers) increasingly finds coverage in major hubs without needing satellites. The $3,000 handset and high per-minute rates severely limit the total addressable market, making it impossible to service the massive $5 billion debt quickly enough. The risk of being undercut by ground infrastructure before reaching profitability is extreme.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Avoid sinking more capital into a likely failing venture; let ground networks mature.
2. **Pivot to niche:** Target only maritime, aviation, or remote exploration sectors where ground cells are impossible, ignoring the mass business traveler market.
3. **Proceed as proposed:** High risk of bankruptcy due to slow adoption and debt burden.

Fit: Assuming the goal is profitable global communication, this plan misaligns by targeting a price-sensitive mass market with a premium, limited-product.

Pre-mortem: The company launches, sells few units due to price and bulk, ground networks cover 90% of travel routes within two years, and the company defaults on its loans. This is a one-way door with massive financial loss.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits ground networks are spreading, handsets work poorly indoors, and debt payments require fast growth. These facts contradict the feasibility of the plan.

Blockers:
- **Market mismatch:** High price and poor performance vs. rapidly improving, cheaper ground alternatives.
- **Debt structure:** $5 billion debt requires immediate, massive scale that the limited market cannot support.

Risks: Execution delays, further tech obsolescence.

Kill criteria: If subscriber growth does not meet debt service requirements within 12 months, cease operations. However, the initial facts suggest this threshold is unreachable.

DECISION: NO-GO
