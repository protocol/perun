First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; entering a keyboard-dominated market with no phone experience, no apps, and limited reach via exclusivity offers no factual advantage over incumbents, only assumptions.

Steelman: A premium, keyboard-less device can succeed if the touchscreen interface and integrated music experience create a category shift that makes physical keyboards obsolete, leveraging brand loyalty to overcome the lack of apps and carrier restrictions.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Protect current player sales but risk erosion as competitors add music to phones.
2. License OS to multiple carriers: Faster reach but loses control of design/software, commoditizing the hardware.
3. Proposed plan: High control, high margin per unit, but capped volume and unproven demand for a keyboard-less premium device.

Fit: Assumes the north star is redefining the mobile experience rather than maximizing immediate unit share. This conflicts with the reality of business-market dominance by keyboard devices.

Pre-mortem: The device fails because enterprise users reject the lack of a physical keyboard for email, the premium price excludes early adopters without app utility, and the single carrier limits market penetration enough to starve the ecosystem. This is a one-way door with high delay cost if competitors lock in music integration first.

Evidence: All claims regarding "touchscreen superiority" and "willingness to pay premium" are assumed. No data exists showing consumers will abandon physical keyboards or pay more for a phone with fewer features (no apps). The revenue share model is theoretical.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: The proposal admits no phone experience and removes a key feature (keyboard) relied on by the target premium market (business), without evidence that touch compensates.
2. Contradictory constraints: Demanding design control and revenue share from a carrier while offering a niche, unproven device with limited reach (exclusivity) creates a negative value proposition for the carrier.

Risks: Market rejection of touch-only input; app ecosystem failure; brand damage from high-price failure.

The proposal's own facts highlight fatal gaps: no apps, no keyboard, no experience, limited reach. These are not risks to be managed but blockers to the core value proposition. Without proof that touch replaces keyboards or that carriers will accept these terms for an unproven device, the plan fails as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
