First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a market need (CD storage, 3D graphics) but fails to state a defensible edge against incumbents who control loyal customer bases, famous IP, and have deeper pockets to absorb hardware losses. Entering a two-player oligopoly without a unique distribution channel, exclusive content, or proven platform experience is speculative, not strategic.

Steelman: A new entrant can disrupt the duopoly by leveraging cheaper disc manufacturing to lower barriers for third-party developers, creating a software library distinct from cartridge-based rivals.

Attack:
1. **Options:** "Do nothing" avoids catastrophic capital loss. The cheapest alternative is licensing technology to an existing player rather than building a standalone console. The proposed path requires winning over developers who already have established relationships with incumbents.
2. **Fit:** Without a stated north star beyond "entering the market," this diverts resources from the company's core competency in consumer electronics into a high-burn entertainment war.
3. **Pre-mortem:** The console launches, sells at a loss, and fails to attract key developers because the install base is too small. The company is stuck with unsold inventory and no recurring software revenue to offset hardware subsidies.
4. **Evidence:** The proposal admits zero experience as a platform holder and acknowledges simultaneous competition from an incumbent's 3D launch. These are not risks; they are active blockers. The "cheaper manufacturing" advantage is nullified if the hardware must be sold below cost to compete, a war the newcomer cannot fund longer than the incumbents.
5. **Blockers:** The lack of a "must-have" exclusive franchise or a pre-existing developer ecosystem means the value proposition relies entirely on speculation. The incumbents' loyalty and IP are facts that remove the route to upside as proposed.

Blockers: No proven route to secure essential third-party content against entrenched rivals; no capital advantage to sustain negative hardware margins longer than incumbents; zero platform experience.
Risks: Market rejection, developer apathy, rapid obsolescence.

The proposal relies on hopes (developers will come) rather than facts (developers are committed). As proposed, it is a capital destruction event.

DECISION: NO-GO
