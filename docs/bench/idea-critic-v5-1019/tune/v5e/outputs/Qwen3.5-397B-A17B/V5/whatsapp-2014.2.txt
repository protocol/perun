First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it admits no monetization plan, charges a fee the target refuses to scale, and relies on fear of displacement rather than a proven advantage over existing buyer services.

Steelman: Acquiring the target is the only viable path to prevent total obsolescence in mobile messaging, securing hundreds of millions of users who are already migrating away, despite the astronomical price tag.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Risk gradual irrelevance as users shift to the competitor; cost is low now but potentially existential later.
2. Build internal clone: Cheaper financially, but likely too slow to capture the network effect already held by the target.
3. Acquisition: Highest cost and risk, but offers immediate access to the user base and talent.

Fit: Assumes the north star is "universal connectivity" regardless of platform. If the strategy is "profitable growth," this fails. If it is "survival," it fits.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition fails because the founders leave immediately due to culture clash, regulators break up the deal, or the massive debt/equity dilution crushes stock value without generating new revenue streams for a decade. This is a one-way door; reversal is impossible once closed.

Evidence: The $19B price is 12x the valuation from one year ago ($1.5B). Revenue is minimal; the $1 fee is explicitly rejected as a scale model by the target. No evidence exists that these users will tolerate ads or pay more.

Blockers:
1. No monetization path: The target refuses ads and has minimal revenue. Buying a cost center with no clear route to profit violates basic capital allocation logic based on stated facts.
2. Price discrepancy: A 12x valuation jump in 12 months with no revenue inflection suggests speculative mania, not fundamental value.

Risks: Founder retention, regulatory scrutiny, integration failure.

Kill criteria: If the target's user growth rate drops below 10% month-over-month within six months post-close, or if the founders depart within 12 months, the acquisition thesis is broken.

The absence of a monetization plan is not just a risk; given the refusal to show advertising and the tiny fee, it is a factual blocker to ROI. Fear of displacement does not justify buying a black box at record prices without a defined mechanism to recoup costs.

DECISION: NO-GO
