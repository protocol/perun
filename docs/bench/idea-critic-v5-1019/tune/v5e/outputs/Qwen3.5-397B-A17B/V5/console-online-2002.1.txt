First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it asks users to pay for a feature rivals provide free, despite the company having zero prior experience running the required hosting and support operations.

**Steelman:** A unified, high-quality paid network creates a premium ecosystem that justifies the cost through superior reliability and social features, differentiating the console from fragmented free alternatives.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits "few homes have broadband" in 2002. Mandating broadband for a paid service immediately caps the addressable market to a tiny fraction of owners, contradicting the goal of building a large player base.
2.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "publisher-led" model carries zero operational risk and aligns with current user expectations (free online play). A cheaper alternative is a hybrid model: free basic matchmaking with optional paid premium features, allowing market validation before committing to massive infrastructure costs.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: The company burns cash building data centers and support teams for a service only 5% of users can access, while competitors gain market share by remaining free and accessible via dial-up. The lack of operational history makes service outages likely, destroying the "premium" reputation instantly.
4.  **Blocker:** The combination of a mandatory subscription, a mandatory hardware requirement (broadband) that most users lack, and the company's explicit lack of operational experience constitutes a factual failure of the core business model as proposed. The risks are not hypothetical; they are stated constraints that prevent the service from reaching critical mass.

**Risks:** High price sensitivity, infrastructure inexperience, low broadband penetration. These are not merely risks; given the current market state, they are blockers to viability.

**Kill Criteria:** None can save this specific proposal because the foundational assumptions (market readiness, user willingness to pay for infrastructure the company cannot yet reliably deliver) are contradicted by the provided facts.

**Dissent Ledger:** The proposal ignores the "free" competitor baseline and the "dial-up" reality of 2002. It bets the company's reputation on an unproven operational capability.

**Remaining Risk:** Total market rejection and unsustainable operational burn.

DECISION: NO-GO
