First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price and locked supply are disadvantages, not advantages, against existing cheap juicers and fresh produce.

Steelman: A premium, hassle-free juicing ecosystem where device subsidies are recouped via high-margin proprietary bags, justified by freshness verification and convenience.

Attack:
1. **Options:** "Do nothing" (buy a $100 juicer and fresh fruit) costs significantly less per serving with zero waste or connectivity friction. The proposed model forces a high upfront capex ($699) plus a recurring premium for a commodity (fruit). No cheaper alternative reaches the same upside because the "upside" (convenience) does not justify the massive price differential.
2. **Fit:** Assumes consumers value app-connected freshness checks over cost and flexibility. In 2016, the "smart appliance" trend often added friction without solving core utility.
3. **Pre-mortem:** The device sits unused because the subscription is too expensive compared to grocery stores. The internet requirement creates a single point of failure (server shutdown kills the hardware). The market for $699 countertop appliances is tiny; the addressable market for daily juice drinkers who refuse to buy fresh fruit is smaller.
4. **Evidence:** The proposal admits concerns about price and market size but offers no data showing users will pay a 5x-10x premium for pre-packed bags. The "freshness check" is a solution in search of a problem; visual inspection is free.

Blockers:
- **Unit Economics:** The combined cost of device amortization and proprietary bags likely exceeds the value proposition of fresh juice, creating a negative ROI for the consumer immediately.
- **Market Reality:** No stated evidence proves a large enough segment exists that prefers locked, expensive subscriptions over flexible, cheap fresh produce.

Risks:
- Connectivity dependency rendering hardware useless.
- Subscription churn due to price sensitivity.
- Limited flavor variety compared to open markets.

The proposal fails the "beat do nothing" test. The high price and locked ecosystem are structural disadvantages, not differentiators. Without evidence of a specific, underserved niche willing to pay this premium, the plan is financially unsound.

DECISION: NO-GO
