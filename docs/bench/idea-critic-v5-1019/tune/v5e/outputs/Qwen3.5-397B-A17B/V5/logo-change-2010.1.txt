First call: NO-GO. The proposal offers no stated edge (cost, speed, or evidence advantage) over the incumbent identity; it relies entirely on the assumption that "contemporary" equals "plain," without proving this shifts buyer behavior or revenue.

Steelman: A minimalist rebrand modernizes perception and signals a strategic shift to a younger demographic.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves brand equity at zero cost. A "staged pilot" (testing the new mark in specific channels) offers the same modernization signal with reversible risk. The proposal's "all-at-once" approach is strictly riskier and more expensive than a pilot, failing to beat the cheaper alternative.
2.  **Fit:** While "contemporary" is a valid goal, the method (no announcement, total immediate swap) contradicts the reality of a "mature retailer" with "loyal customers." Alienating the core base to chase an unproven demographic is a strategic mismatch.
3.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the design was "not tested with customers." In the face of stated risks (backlash, attachment to the old square), proceeding without data is negligence, not boldness.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Loyal customers perceive the change as a betrayal of heritage; social media amplifies the confusion; sales dip because the recognizable "blue square" anchor is diminished. The "no advance announcement" strategy guarantees maximum shock and minimum context for the change, fueling the backlash.
5.  **Blocker:** The explicit lack of customer testing combined with the high probability of alienating the existing loyal base (a stated fact) constitutes a blocker. The plan fails as proposed because it ignores the primary asset (recognition) without verifying the new asset's value.

Blockers: No customer testing despite stated risks of backlash; "all-at-once" rollout eliminates the ability to pivot based on early feedback; no evidence that the new design outperforms the old one with the target audience.
Risks: Permanent loss of legacy customers; brand confusion; negative media cycle.

Kill criteria are irrelevant as the plan cannot start. The absence of a pilot or metric is a fatal flaw in the strategy itself, not just a missing detail.

DECISION: NO-GO
