First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the economics are unproven and predicts a large short-term margin hit without stating a specific edge (e.g., verified data showing current repeat rates are suppressed solely by shipping fees, or a calculated lifetime value that guarantees payback). Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: An annual fee creates sticky loyalty and increases order frequency enough to offset shipping losses through volume and retention.

Attack: The "heavy buyers" risk is not a possibility; it is a stated probability in the proposal ("heavy buyers will order more often"). If heavy buyers drive the revenue but also trigger the cost overrun, the model collapses immediately. The proposal offers no cap, pilot, or kill criterion to test this before a full launch. It explicitly states the economics are unproven yet asks for a full commitment.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retains current margin, accepts current friction.
2. Pilot: Offer the membership to a small, controlled segment (e.g., top 5% of users) with a hard cap on total subsidies to validate the unit economics before a general launch.
3. Threshold-based free shipping: Lower friction for large orders without an upfront fee or unlimited liability.

Pre-mortem: The program launches, heavy users exploit the unlimited shipping, costs exceed the $79 fee by 30% in Q1, and the company cannot cancel contracts without reputational damage, forcing a sustained cash burn.

Evidence: The proposal itself provides the blocking evidence: "economics are unproven" and "large short-term margin hit." There is no counter-evidence provided to suggest the margin hit is an acceptable investment rather than a loss.

Blockers:
1. Unproven economics with admitted high risk of cost exceeding revenue.
2. No pilot or cap proposed to validate the model before full exposure.
3. High reversibility risk (hard to cancel) combined with unverified assumptions.

Risks: Customer adoption rates, competitor response, operational strain. These are secondary to the financial blocker.

The proposal fails the basic operator test: do not make an irreversible, costly bet with unproven math. The lack of a staged approach or kill criteria makes this a blind gamble.

DECISION: NO-GO
