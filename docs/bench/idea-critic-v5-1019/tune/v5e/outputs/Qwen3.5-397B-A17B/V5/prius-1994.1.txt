First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: cheap fuel, zero regulatory mandate, and no competitor activity remove the usual drivers (cost, compliance, first-mover urgency), while unproven battery durability creates massive warranty exposure without a near-term demand signal to offset it.

Steelman: Committing now secures a decade-long lead in hybrid technology before regulations force the industry's hand, establishing brand dominance despite short-term losses.

Attack: The "first-mover" advantage is illusory without market pull. With fuel cheap and regulators inactive, consumers have no financial incentive to pay a premium for unproven technology. The novel power-split transmission and NiMH packs introduce complex failure modes; without durability data, warranty costs could exceed the entire program budget. A cheaper alternative exists: accelerate R&D and prototype testing silently while waiting for either fuel prices to rise, regulations to tighten, or a competitor to validate the market. This "fast follower" approach captures 80% of the benefit with 10% of the financial risk.

Pre-mortem: By 1998, the car launches to tepid sales due to high sticker price and consumer range anxiety. Early adopters experience catastrophic battery failures, triggering a recall that bankrupts the division and destroys brand trust. The company is forced to halt production, having spent billions on a solution the market did not want.

Evidence Check: The proposal admits "no near-term demand signal" and "battery durability is unproven." These are not risks to be managed; they are factual blockers to a mass-production commitment. Mass production requires proven unit economics and reliability; this plan has neither. The absence of a pilot program or capped initial run confirms the proposal ignores basic product validation.

Blockers:
1. No demand signal + high cost = guaranteed financial loss.
2. Unproven battery durability + mass scale = catastrophic warranty liability.
3. Cheap fuel + no regulation = no consumer incentive to adopt.

Risks: Competitor entry, technology obsolescence, supply chain immaturity.

The proposal fails the basic test of unit economics and market fit. It commits to full-scale production before validating the core technology or customer need.

DECISION: NO-GO
