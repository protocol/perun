First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a strategic need (cross-business franchise) but fails to state an edge explaining why this specific asset, at this price and creative inflection point, beats existing internal options or cheaper alternatives.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the franchise secures exclusive IP for parks and merchandise, and a $4B price is justified if the new trilogy revitalizes the brand despite the founder's exit.

**Attack:**
1.  **Valuation Mismatch:** $4B is immense against current film output. With mixed recent reviews and the creative lead departing, the core asset (future film quality) is unproven. Paying a premium for past glory without a guaranteed creative successor is speculative, not strategic.
2.  **Revenue Leakage:** The proposal admits much merchandise value is already licensed out. This severely caps the immediate "synergy" upside for the consumer products division, undermining the primary financial justification for the premium price.
3.  **Fan Risk:** Without the founder and with recent mixed reception, new entries face high rejection risk. If fans reject the trilogy, the $4B investment yields negative returns across all three business units (films, parks, products).
4.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "develop internal IP" options avoid the $4B cash/stock drain and the specific creative vacuum risk. A cheaper, staged partnership (e.g., distribution deal only) was not considered but would test the water without full acquisition risk.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial:** The cost ($4B) relies on future film success which is actively threatened by the stated risks (founder exit, mixed reviews). The math does not work on current facts.
*   **Strategic:** The stated fact that merchandise rights are largely licensed out removes the immediate synergy value needed to justify the price today.

**Risks:** Fan rejection, creative failure, overpayment. These are severe, but the blockers above are factual contradictions within the proposal's own premises. The plan fails as proposed because the stated evidence (licensed merch, retiring founder, mixed reviews) contradicts the claim that this is a sound buy at $4B now.

DECISION: NO-GO
