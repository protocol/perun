First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge proving this hardware bet beats existing alternatives or overcomes the company's lack of experience, relying instead on a real need which is insufficient.

Steelman: A dedicated, cellular-enabled device removes friction for book buyers and solves piracy fears via a closed ecosystem, creating a new market where none existed.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Zero risk, zero capex, but cedes digital future to competitors.
2. Software app: Cheaper, uses existing hardware, but cannot guarantee the "paper-like" experience or control piracy as tightly.
3. Proposed hardware: High cost, high risk, unproven demand, but offers total ecosystem control.

Fit: Assumes the north star is "owning the reading experience" rather than just "selling books." This is an unverified assumption.

Pre-mortem: The device fails because the hardware subsidy (free cellular) burns cash faster than low-margin ebook sales can recover, and publishers, fearing cannibalization of print, withhold key titles. It is a one-way door with massive sunk costs. Delaying one year to license an existing reader platform would save capital while testing demand.

Evidence: The proposal admits the company has "never designed consumer hardware" and earlier e-readers "sold poorly." These are facts, not risks. The margin squeeze is stated as a likely outcome, not a hypothetical.

Blockers:
1. Capability Gap: The company explicitly lacks hardware design experience, a core requirement for success.
2. Economic Contradiction: The plan combines "low price per title" (squeezing margins) with "free cellular" (high operating cost) without a unit economics model showing profitability.
3. Supply Chain Risk: Publishers' fear of piracy and cannibalization is stated as a fact; without signed commitments, the store may launch empty.

Risks: Cannibalization of print sales, low adoption due to niche hardware.

The stated facts regarding inexperience and negative unit economics (low revenue + high connectivity cost) constitute immediate blockers. The proposal asks to jump into a capital-intensive, low-margin hardware business with no prior skills and no proof that the economic model works. This is not a calculated bet; it is a leap into the dark based on hope rather than evidence.

DECISION: NO-GO
