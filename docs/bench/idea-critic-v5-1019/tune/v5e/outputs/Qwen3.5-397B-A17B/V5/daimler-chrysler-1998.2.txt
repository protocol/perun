First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a cyclical peak (US truck boom) to justify a permanent structural merger, lacking a stated edge that offsets the cultural and operational fracture risks.

Steelman: Combining premium engineering with high-margin US volume creates a diversified global giant resilient to regional downturns.

Attack: The "merger of equals" structure is likely a facade masking a culture clash where neither side can impose discipline. The US partner's profitability is tied to a specific, cyclical segment (light trucks) that may revert, leaving the combined entity with bloated costs and no premium buffer. Platform sharing between distinct brand tiers often dilutes the premium value proposition without achieving sufficient mass-market savings quickly enough to cover integration friction.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve the German firm's margins and the US firm's cyclical upside without integration debt.
2. Strategic Alliance: Share specific platforms or powertrains without merging balance sheets or cultures.
3. Acquisition: One side clearly buys the other to enforce a single culture and strategy.

Fit: Global scale is a vanity metric if integration costs erase synergies. The North American mass market entry via a high-cost premium partner is contradictory.

Pre-mortem: The merger fails because decision-making stalls between two incompatible boards, the truck cycle turns down, and the premium brand loses its exclusivity trying to cut costs for mass volume. This is a one-way door with massive reversal costs.

Evidence: The proposal admits "very different corporate and labour cultures" and "dependence on a cyclical boom." These are not risks to be managed; they are active blockers to the "merger of equals" thesis. A true edge requires a mechanism to resolve the culture clash or hedge the cycle, which is absent.

Blockers:
1. Structural contradiction: "Merger of equals" prevents decisive cultural integration needed to survive a cycle turn.
2. Timing risk: Basing a permanent merger on a cyclical peak without a hedging mechanism.

Risks: Brand dilution, labor strikes, platform incompatibility.

Kill criteria: If US truck sales drop >10% within 18 months, the merger thesis collapses.

DECISION: NO-GO
