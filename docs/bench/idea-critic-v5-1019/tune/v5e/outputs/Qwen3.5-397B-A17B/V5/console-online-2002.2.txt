First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it adds cost and friction (broadband requirement, mandatory fee) to a market expecting free access, without citing evidence that users will pay for features rivals offer gratis.

Steelman: A unified, high-quality network with voice chat and friends lists creates a superior social ecosystem that justifies a subscription, differentiating the console as a premium service hub rather than just hardware.

However, the attack reveals fatal blockers based on the provided facts:
1.  **Infrastructure Mismatch:** The plan *requires* broadband, yet the facts state "few homes have broadband." This immediately caps the addressable market to a tiny fraction of owners, making the service commercially non-viable at launch.
2.  **Pricing Contradiction:** Rivals leave online play free or publisher-managed. This proposal mandates a console-wide paid subscription. With no cited evidence of willingness to pay, this directly contradicts established market expectations, likely driving users to competitors.
3.  **Operational Inexperience:** The company has "never run" hosting or support operations. Building this from scratch while launching hardware creates immense execution risk with no buffer for failure.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Match rivals by letting publishers handle online play. Cost: Low. Risk: Fragmented experience. Reversibility: High.
*   **Hybrid Model:** Offer basic matching for free, charge only for premium voice/features. Cost: Medium. Risk: Lower adoption friction. Reversibility: High.
*   **Current Proposal:** Cost: High (infra + ops). Risk: Massive adoption failure due to broadband/price barriers. Reversibility: Low (brand damage).

The proposal fails the "beat do nothing" test. It imposes high barriers (broadband, subscription) on a user base that largely lacks the infrastructure and expects free service. The operational inexperience compounds the likelihood of a botched launch. The lack of a pilot, cap, or metric to validate demand before a full rollout is a critical omission. The facts provided suggest the target users cannot (broadband) and will not (price expectation) adopt this as proposed.

Blockers: Infrastructure scarcity (few homes have broadband); Market pricing mismatch (paid vs. free expectation); Operational inexperience.
Risks: Low adoption, high churn, brand damage.

DECISION: NO-GO
