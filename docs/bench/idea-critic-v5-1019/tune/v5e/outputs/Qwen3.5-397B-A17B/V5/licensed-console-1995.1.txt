First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; small royalties and dominant incumbents offer no path to beat existing options, and need alone is insufficient.

**Steelman:** Licensing converts fixed R&D risk into variable royalty revenue, enabling market entry without capital expenditure or marketing execution by the licensor.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves brand equity and avoids dilution. A "cheaper alternative" exists: direct sales of a niche peripheral or software bundle, capturing full margin rather than a fraction. The proposal fails to prove licensing beats doing nothing given the tiny royalty.
2.  **Fit:** Entering the toy/console market via a third party disconnects the core brand from the consumer experience. If the licensee fails to market hard (a stated risk), the licensor's brand suffers association with a forgotten product, damaging the core computer business.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: The console launches, sells poorly due to weak marketing, and is discontinued. The licensor earned negligible royalties but lost brand control. The "one-way door" is brand perception; once associated with a low-quality toy, the professional computer image degrades.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits royalties are "small" and marketing is ceded. No evidence suggests the licensee can overcome established dominance. The stated facts (dominant rivals, low margin, no marketing control) directly contradict the success condition.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal offers no advantage over current market leaders or the "do nothing" baseline. Small royalties do not justify the brand risk.
*   **Self-Contradiction:** The plan relies on a licensee to fight dominant incumbents but admits the licensee may not market hard. The stated facts show the target users (consumers) are already served by dominant players; without a unique value prop or marketing push, they will not switch. This is a blocker, not a risk.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, opportunity cost of engineering time, reliance on third-party execution.

**Kill Criteria:** None needed; the plan fails on stated facts. The lack of a marketing guarantee and minimal financial upside removes the route to success.

DECISION: NO-GO
