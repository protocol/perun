First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; low price alone is not an advantage when the core function (cleaning) is unproven and the target users (floor-care companies) already rejected the prototype.

**Steelman:** A low-cost, randomized robot can capture the mass market by prioritizing "good enough" automation at a disruptive price point, bypassing the need for complex mapping technology that early adopters do not yet value.

**Attack:**
1.  **Evidence Contradiction:** Two industry experts (floor-care companies) rejected the prototype. The proposal offers no new data suggesting consumer preferences differ radically from professional judgment regarding coverage reliability.
2.  **Capability Gap:** The company has zero consumer-product experience. Launching a hardware product requiring retail distribution, consumer support, and brand trust without this foundation is a fatal operational risk, not merely a "risk to manage."
3.  **Functional Defect:** Randomized movement fails the primary job-to-be-done (a clean floor) compared to rivals. Selling a defective core feature as a "low cost" alternative is a race to the bottom against established players who can also lower prices.
4.  **Trust Barrier:** Buyers are explicitly noted as distrustful. A cheaper, less effective robot amplifies distrust rather than alleviating it.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; avoid entering a hardware market without expertise.
*   **Pivot to Niche:** Target a specific, low-expectation use case (e.g., industrial spot cleaning) rather than whole-home consumer vacuuming.
*   **Partner/License:** Sell the technology to an existing consumer appliance manufacturer rather than launching a standalone brand.

**Blockers:**
*   **Market Rejection:** Direct evidence from target partners indicates no interest.
*   **Operational Incompetence:** No existing capability to execute a consumer launch.
*   **Product Inferiority:** The proposed solution (random) is objectively worse than the alternative (mapping) on the primary metric (coverage), with no proven consumer willingness to accept this trade-off.

**Risks:** Brand destruction, inventory write-offs, liability from home accidents.

**Conclusion:** The proposal asks to bet the company on an unproven consumer hypothesis with a superior competitive alternative already available, while lacking the fundamental organizational skills to execute. The lack of a pilot, cap, or metric is secondary to the fact that the core premise contradicts available evidence.

DECISION: NO-GO
