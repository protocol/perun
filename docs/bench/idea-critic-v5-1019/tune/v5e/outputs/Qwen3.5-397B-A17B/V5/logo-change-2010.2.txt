First call: NO-GO. The proposal offers no stated edge—no evidence that the new design increases contemporary perception, sales, or relevance compared to the incumbent. It relies entirely on aesthetic preference without a business case.

Steelman: A simultaneous, unannounced rebrand maximizes shock value to instantly signal a break from the past, forcing immediate market attention.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves brand equity at zero cost. A "staged pilot" (e.g., digital-only first) tests reception with minimal downside. The proposal's "all-at-once" approach beats neither; it incurs maximum implementation cost and maximum backlash risk without validating the core assumption that customers want this change.
2.  **Fit:** A mature retailer's north star is typically sustainable growth and loyalty. Alienating the core base (who recognize the blue square) to chase a vague "contemporary" label contradicts this unless data proves the old logo is actively repelling new growth. No such data exists.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Loyalists view the unannounced change as erasing heritage, sparking a social media boycott. Competitors mock the generic "neutral typeface." Sales dip as trust erodes. Because there was no testing, the company cannot pivot back without admitting a costly error. This is a one-way door with high delay cost (lost trust) and zero reversibility once physical stock is updated.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the design was "not tested." The risks (backlash, attachment to old square) are stated as facts by stakeholders, not hypotheticals. Proceeding without testing treats known risks as irrelevant.

Blockers:
*   **No stated edge:** The plan fails to demonstrate how it beats the status quo or cheaper alternatives on any metric other than "shock."
*   **Contradictory facts:** The proposal acknowledges high customer attachment to the specific element being removed (the square) yet offers no mitigation or validation.

Risks: Brand alienation, sales decline, irreversible loss of heritage equity.

Kill criteria: None proposed. A go requires a capped pilot with a specific retention metric before full rollout. As proposed, the lack of testing and the "big bang" execution constitute a failure of basic operational hygiene for a mature brand.

DECISION: NO-GO
