First call: GO. The stated edge is clear: a garage-sized van built on an existing compact-car platform offers car-like driving dynamics for families, a specific advantage over the bulky full-size vans and station wagons currently available. This reuses proven technology to enter an unserved niche at a lower capital cost than a clean-sheet design.

Steelman: A front-wheel-drive minivan leveraging existing compact-car components creates a new "family hauler" category that fits in standard garages, solving the size/driveability trade-off of current options.

Options analysis:
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue selling station wagons (too small) and full-size vans (too large/clunky). Risk: Slow erosion as families seek better utility.
2.  **New large van:** High cost, high risk, ignores the garage constraint.
3.  **Proposed Minivan:** Moderate cost (converted factory, existing platform), high potential upside by defining a new category. It beats "do nothing" by addressing the unmet need for garage-compatible family transport.

Fit: Aligns with the survival stage; uses the government guarantee to pivot rather than just sustain legacy lines.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if marketing positions it as a "commercial van," alienating families. This is a messaging risk, not a product flaw. The "one-way door" is the factory conversion, but reusing the compact platform limits downside exposure compared to a new chassis. Delay costs first-mover advantage in a category rivals have ignored.

Evidence: The proposal cites the government loan (solving cash constraints) and the existing compact platform (solving R&D time/cost). The risks raised are hypothetical: "no category exists" is the opportunity, not a blocker; "rivals could copy" validates the market potential; "limited space" is the trade-off for garage fitment, which is the core value proposition.

Blockers: NONE. The risks listed are uncertainties inherent to innovation, not factual contradictions of the plan's viability. The lack of cash is mitigated by the stated government guarantee.

Risks: Market education (family vs. commercial perception), execution speed before rivals react. These become kill criteria: if early buyer testing shows strong commercial-only perception, pivot messaging.

DECISION: GO
