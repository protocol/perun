First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; needing social features does not explain why this specific plan beats existing dedicated networks or why users want socializing inside an inbox.

Steelman: Leveraging massive existing distribution to instantly create a social graph within the communication hub, overcoming the cold-start problem that kills most new networks.

**Attack:**
1.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves trust and avoids regulatory scrutiny. A "cheaper alternative" is an opt-in invite system, which achieves network growth without the backlash of forced enrollment. The proposed auto-enroll method does not beat "do nothing" on trust, nor does it beat opt-in on sustainable engagement.
2.  **Fit:** Assuming the north star is user retention, forcing a social layer into a productivity tool (inbox) risks degrading the core utility. Users seek efficiency in email, not distraction.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Users feel violated by default visibility, triggering a privacy scandal and regulator fines. The "social" tab becomes a ghost town because the connections were artificial, not organic. The launch is irreversible because exposed data cannot be un-seen, permanently damaging brand reputation.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits risks of privacy disregard and regulatory attention but offers no mitigation other than proceeding. There is no evidence cited that target users want this specific integration; the assumption that "existing base" equals "willing social participants" is unproven.
5.  **Blocker Test:**
    *   **Blockers:** The plan relies on automatic enrollment and default visibility. These are facts in the proposal that directly contradict the requirement for user trust and create immediate regulatory exposure. The proposal states these mechanics as fixed, yet they are the primary drivers of the identified risks. No alternative path within the proposal removes these blockers.
    *   **Risks:** Low ongoing use, brand damage, difficulty reversing exposure.

The proposal's own mechanics (auto-enroll, default visible) are the blockers. They guarantee the privacy backlash and regulatory risk mentioned. Without changing these fundamental terms to opt-in and private-by-default, the plan fails as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
