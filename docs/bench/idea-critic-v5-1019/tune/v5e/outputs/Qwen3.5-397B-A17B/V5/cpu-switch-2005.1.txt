First call: GO. The stated edge is survival: the current supplier cannot deliver chips cool and efficient enough for thin laptops, while the dominant architecture offers a roadmap that enables the required form factor. Need alone is insufficient, but the physical inability of the current path to meet market demands (thinness/efficiency) creates a definitive advantage for switching.

**Steelman:** Migrating to the dominant architecture is the only viable path to produce competitive thin laptops within two years, and a translation layer mitigates the immediate loss of software compatibility better than staying on a dead-end roadmap.

**Attack & Analysis:**
*   **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" guarantees product irrelevance as competitors ship thin, cool devices. Sticking with the current supplier fails the core requirement (efficiency/heat). No cheaper alternative reaches the same upside (viable thin laptops).
*   **Fit:** Assumes the north star is selling desirable hardware; without this move, the product line dies.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the translation layer is too slow for key apps or if the sales freeze lasts longer than the transition. However, these are execution risks, not strategic blockers. The risk of "weakening distinctiveness" is irrelevant if the devices cannot be built otherwise.
*   **Evidence:** The proposal states the current supplier *has not* delivered and *lags* the competitor. This is a hard fact of capability, not speculation.
*   **Blockers:** None. The risks (sales freeze, rebuild effort, speed, brand dilution, past pain) are all manageable execution challenges or known trade-offs, not facts that prove the plan fails as proposed. The "painful earlier transition" is a warning to manage better, not a reason to avoid the necessary change.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If the translation layer cannot run core applications at >80% native speed by the midpoint of the two-year window, pivot to native recompilation incentives.

The plan beats "do nothing" (product death) and has no factual blocker. The risks are real but do not invalidate the necessity of the move given the supplier's failure.

DECISION: GO
