First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; small royalties and dominant incumbents mean "need" without a path to beat existing options.

**Steelman:** Licensing converts fixed RISK into variable royalty, allowing market entry with zero capital outlay while leveraging a partner's distribution.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Mismatch:** The licensor earns "small" royalties; the licensee bears all manufacturing and marketing costs. With established competitors dominating, the licensee has little margin to market aggressively. If sales lag, the licensee cuts marketing first to preserve margins, dooming the product. The licensor cannot force effort.
2.  **Brand Dilution:** Consumers may not connect the low-cost toy console with the computer maker's brand. If the product fails (likely given the risks), it associates the licensor with "cheap toys" rather than serious computing, damaging core brand equity for negligible gain.
3.  **No Control:** Leaving marketing entirely to the licensee surrenders the only lever to overcome market dominance. Without a co-marketing commitment or minimum spend, the "small royalty" model guarantees passivity.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves brand reputation and capital. A joint venture with shared marketing costs and control would align incentives but requires more than "licensing."

**Pre-mortem:** The console launches, sells poorly due to lack of visibility, the licensee discontinues it within a year, and the computer maker is remembered for a failed toy rather than its computers.

**Blockers:**
*   **Incentive Failure:** The financial structure (small royalty vs. full marketing cost) makes it rational for the licensee to under-invest in promotion, ensuring failure against dominant rivals.
*   **Brand Risk:** High probability of negative brand association with no upside control.

**Risks:** Market dominance by rivals; consumer confusion. These are exacerbated by the lack of control.

**Kill Criteria:** None proposed. The plan commits to a structure where failure is the rational outcome for the partner.

**Decision:** The proposal fails because it relies on a partner to spend heavily on a low-margin product in a saturated market without contractual leverage to ensure that spend. It trades brand risk for pennies.

DECISION: NO-GO
