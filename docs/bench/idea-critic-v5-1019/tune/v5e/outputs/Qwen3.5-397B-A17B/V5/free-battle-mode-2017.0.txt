First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a market need but fails to state an edge; "reusing assets" and "two months" are constraints, not advantages against an incumbent who already owns the genre.

**Steelman:** Rapidly deploy a free-to-play variant using existing assets to capture market share before the rival solidifies dominance, monetizing via cosmetics to offset cannibalization.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Edge:** The studio lacks free-to-play operational experience (live ops, balance, anti-cheat). Entering a winner-take-all genre against an established rival without a distinct mechanic or cost advantage is statistically fatal. Reusing paid assets often creates tonal dissonance in a battle-royale context.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves the paid game's value. A cheaper alternative is a limited-time event within the existing game to test retention without the overhead of a separate title and economy.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The mode launches, the rival copies any novelty instantly, the studio fails to balance the economy due to inexperience, and the free title drags down the brand value of the paid predecessor without generating sufficient cosmetic revenue to cover server costs.
4.  **Evidence Gap:** The claim that "two months" is sufficient for a competitive last-player-standing mode contradicts industry standards for netcode, anti-cheat, and balancing required to retain players in this genre. No evidence is provided that the reused assets support the scale or performance needed.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits a rival owns the genre and the studio lacks F2P experience. Without a specific differentiator (e.g., unique mechanic, existing community lock-in), the plan relies on hope, not strategy.
*   **Resource Mismatch:** The timeline (2 months) is likely insufficient to build a stable, cheat-resistant F2P infrastructure from scratch, creating a high risk of immediate technical failure upon launch.

**Risks:** Cannibalization of the core paid product; brand dilution; financial loss from server costs without conversion.

The absence of a unique value proposition and the admission of inexperience in the specific business model required constitute a fundamental flaw in the strategy as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
