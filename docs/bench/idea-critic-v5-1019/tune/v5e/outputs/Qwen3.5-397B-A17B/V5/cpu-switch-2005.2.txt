First call: GO. The stated edge is existential: the current supplier cannot deliver chips cool and efficient enough for the company's thin form factor, while the dominant architecture offers an immediate roadmap advantage. Need alone is insufficient, but a physical inability to ship competitive hardware constitutes a definitive edge over the status quo.

Steelman: Migrating to the dominant architecture is the only viable path to maintain product relevance in thin laptops, with a translation layer mitigating immediate software incompatibility.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal acknowledges severe risks (sales freeze, developer friction, performance loss, brand dilution, past trauma), but none are factual blockers proving the plan fails as proposed. They are execution risks, not contradictions of the core claim that the current roadmap is dead.
**Risks:** High. Sales may stall during the announcement window; translated apps may underperform; distinct brand identity may erode.

**Kill Criteria:**
1.  **Metric:** If the translation layer delivers less than 70% native performance on key benchmarks by the first developer preview date.
2.  **Date:** 12 months from project start.
3.  **Action:** Pivot to a hybrid lineup or abandon the thin form factor entirely if performance targets are missed.
4.  **Leading Metric:** Developer adoption rate of native tools within 6 months of announcement.

**Pre-mortem:** The transition fails because the company announces too early, causing a complete sales collapse before new units ship, and the translation layer proves too slow for professional workflows, driving users to competitors. The "distinctiveness" risk is secondary to the primary failure mode: shipping nothing.

**Better Way Analysis:** "Do nothing" results in product obsolescence as the current supplier cannot meet thermal/efficiency needs. Waiting for the current supplier to improve is a bet against a decade of evidence. A partial transition creates support nightmares. The all-in approach with a translation bridge is the least bad option.

**Evidence Check:** The proposal states the supplier "has not delivered" and "lags." Accepting these premises as the driving facts, the decision is forced. The risks are managed via the translation layer and a two-year window, which allows for a controlled burn rather than sudden death.

The absence of a pilot or specific metric in the original text is not a blocker; the kill criteria above rectify this. The plan survives the attack because the alternative (staying) guarantees failure based on the stated hardware constraints.

DECISION: GO
