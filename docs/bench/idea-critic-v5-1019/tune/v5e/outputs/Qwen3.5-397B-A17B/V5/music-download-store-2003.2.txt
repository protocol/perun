First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it enters a market where the dominant substitute (free file-sharing) is superior on price (zero vs. 99 cents) and availability, while the proposed solution is artificially constrained to a single hardware ecosystem with thin margins.

Steelman: A curated, legal, high-fidelity alternative with seamless integration could convert users fatigued by the friction and legal risk of piracy, provided the hardware lock-in drives sufficient device sales to subsidize the retail operation.

Attack:
1.  **Economics:** At 99 cents with major label payouts, margins are negligible. Without a subscription recurring revenue model (which rivals are pursuing), the unit economics rely entirely on selling more hardware. If the store fails to move devices, the retail arm bleeds cash.
2.  **Adoption Barrier:** Restricting the store to only the company's computers ignores the vast majority of potential music buyers on Windows. This caps the addressable market to existing niche device owners, unlikely to generate the volume needed to persuade labels to maintain licenses.
3.  **Competitive Moat:** Rivals are betting on subscriptions (access over ownership). This proposal doubles down on ownership in a sharing economy. If labels withdraw licenses due to low volume, the entire value proposition collapses instantly.
4.  **Risk Realization:** The risk of label withdrawal is high because the proposed volume (limited by platform exclusivity) may not meet label expectations compared to the reach of file-sharing or subscription services.

Pre-mortem: The store launches, but Windows users cannot access it. Sales volumes remain too low to cover licensing overhead. Labels, seeing better deals with subscription rivals, withdraw catalogs. The store becomes a ghost town, failing to drive incremental hardware sales enough to offset the operational loss.

Blockers:
-   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits free sharing is "widespread" and cheaper. It offers no fact explaining why a user would pay 99 cents for a restricted file when a free unrestricted one exists, other than assumed "convenience" which is unproven at this price point.
-   **Market Cap:** Limiting to own computers contradicts the need for massive volume to satisfy labels.

Risks: Label withdrawal, margin compression, platform irrelevance.

The plan relies on changing consumer behavior without a compelling economic or functional advantage over the status quo (free) or the emerging alternative (subscription).

DECISION: NO-GO
