First call: NO-GO. The proposal offers no stated edge; it combines two declining businesses with different customer bases and uncertain synergies while facing growing big-box rivals.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the larger chain provides immediate scale and brand assets to revitalize the post-restructuring discounter faster than organic growth, assuming merger savings offset the target's decline.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Edge:** The proposal admits both chains have declining sales and the target is underinvested. Buying a larger, failing business does not fix the acquirer's weak sales; it compounds them. There is no fact stated showing this specific target beats "doing nothing" or organic repair.
2.  **Customer Mismatch:** The explicit risk that customers differ undermines the core synergy of selling the older chain's brands in discount stores. If the discount shoppers do not want department-store brands, the revenue plan fails.
3.  **Financial Gravity:** Merging two declining top-lines rarely creates growth; it usually accelerates cash burn during the chaotic integration of corporate functions and store conversions.
4.  **Competitive Context:** Big-box rivals are growing. This plan forces management to focus on internal integration (closing stores, merging HR/IT) while agile competitors capture market share.
5.  **Uncertain Savings:** The proposal admits cost savings are "uncertain." In a turnaround, uncertain savings cannot fund the acquisition premium or the capital required to fix underinvested stores.

**Blockers:**
*   **Lack of Stated Edge:** The proposal fails the primary test: it does not state a fact proving this plan beats the status quo or alternatives given the dual decline.
*   **Contradictory Synergy:** The plan relies on cross-selling brands to customer bases explicitly noted as different.

**Risks:** Execution complexity, culture clash, accelerated cash burn, distraction from core turnaround. These are severe, but the lack of a basic economic engine (the edge) is the fatal flaw.

**Kill Criteria:** If the combined same-store sales do not stabilize within 12 months post-merger, or if integration costs exceed 15% of projected savings, the plan must be halted. However, without a stated edge, the plan should not start.

Buying a larger, declining company to fix a smaller, declining company is not a strategy; it is desperation masked as scale. The facts provided show no mechanism for value creation.

DECISION: NO-GO
