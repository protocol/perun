First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; paying a premium for an unproven rookie with no professional track record contradicts the principle of buying proven upside, especially when signature shoes rarely sustain sales beyond one season.

**Steelman:** Secure a generational talent before the market prices him, using controversy (rule-breaking colors) to manufacture free media attention that offsets the high salary and creates a distinct brand identity separate from running.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve capital, but cede basketball relevance to competitors signing established stars.
2.  **Sign established veteran:** Higher certainty of performance, lower media shock value, likely higher upfront cost without the "rookie discount" gamble.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Highest risk (injury/performance), highest potential reward (cultural icon), unique marketing hook (banned colors).

**Fit:** Assumes the goal is rapid category disruption rather than steady share gain. If the north star is immediate profitability, this fails. If it is brand dominance, it aligns only if the player becomes an icon.

**Pre-mortem:** The player suffers a career-ending injury in year one; the "banned" colorway generates a fine but no consumer demand; the shoe sells only to collectors, not athletes; the budget drain prevents signing other necessary talent.

**Evidence:** All critical claims are `assumed`. There is no data confirming this specific rookie's potential, the league's actual enforcement response to the specific colorways, or consumer appetite for a rookie's signature shoe. The historical data cited (rare multi-season sales) actively contradicts the plan's success probability.

**Blockers:**
*   **Historical Precedent:** The proposal admits signature shoes for single players rarely sell beyond a season. Without evidence this player is an exception *before* signing, the core revenue model is unsupported.
*   **Unverified Edge:** Paying "far more than rivals" for an unproven asset is a negative expected value bet without inside information on the player's future performance.

**Risks:** Injury, league fines, budget exhaustion, flop product.

**Kill Criteria:** None defined in the proposal (e.g., sales thresholds to trigger continuation).

The plan relies entirely on future events that are currently unknown and statistically unlikely based on the provided historical context. It commits significant resources to a hypothesis with no current validation.

DECISION: NO-GO
