First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; matching the rival's price with a smaller screen, an untested OS, and no native email removes the company's core advantage in business communications while offering no compensating benefit.

Steelman: A 7-inch device creates a new, portable form factor that leverages a proprietary OS to build a distinct ecosystem, betting that size and future software potential outweigh current app deficits.

Attack:
1. **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves the high-margin phone business. The rival's large tablet dominates the current market. A cheaper, phone-paired accessory might work, but this proposal prices it as a standalone competitor without standalone utility.
2. **Fit:** A business leader launching a product that undercuts its own primary value proposition (email) contradicts the established north star of enterprise dominance.
3. **Pre-mortem:** The device fails because enterprise buyers reject a "tablet" that cannot function as a communicator without a specific phone, and consumers reject an expensive toy with no apps. The OS remains a ghost town.
4. **Evidence:** The proposal admits the OS is untested, developers are uncommitted, and the price matches a superior (larger) rival product. These are not risks; they are stated facts that guarantee failure.
5. **Blockers:**
   - **No Edge:** Price parity with a larger, established rival tablet while offering fewer features (no email) is a fatal economic flaw.
   - **Core Contradiction:** Selling a business device without built-in email destroys the brand's primary equity.
   - **Ecosystem Void:** An untested OS with no developer commitment cannot support a standalone hardware launch at a premium price.

These are not mitigatable risks; they are fundamental flaws in the unit economics and value proposition as stated. The plan requires a complete pivot (lower price, bundled phone, or delayed launch until apps exist) to survive. As proposed, it is a guaranteed loss.

DECISION: NO-GO
