First call: GO. The stated edge is leveraging a dominant, high-margin hardware ecosystem (the portable player) to solve a critical user pain point (fragmented, risky free sharing) with a frictionless, legal alternative that competitors (subscriptions) are not yet offering in a simple, owned format.

**Steelman:** A curated, legal store selling individual songs at a psychological price point ($0.99), integrated exclusively with the market-leading portable player, creates a seamless "hardware + content" loop that converts hardware users into recurring revenue customers while denying rivals a foothold.

**Attack & Analysis:**
*   **Free Sharing:** Free is strong, but illegal and fragmented. Convenience and safety are the sellable commodities here.
*   **Label Licenses:** High risk of withdrawal, but labels are desperate for *any* revenue stream as CD sales collapse. The exclusivity of the hardware platform gives the maker leverage to negotiate.
*   **Margins:** Thin per-song margin is irrelevant if the primary goal is driving hardware sales and locking users into the ecosystem. The store is a feature, not the profit center.
*   **Platform Lock-in:** Limiting to own computers is a feature, not a bug, in 2003. It forces hardware adoption, creating a defensible moat against Windows-based rivals.
*   **Subscription Rivals:** Subscriptions require constant payment for temporary access. Ownership (per-song) aligns better with current consumer psychology regarding music collections.

**Blockers:** None. The proposal's own facts (strong hardware sales, label desperation, lack of simple legal alternatives) support the move. The risks (margins, licensing) are manageable execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The "free" alternative lacks the integration and safety this proposal offers.

**Risks:** Label cooperation falters; rivals clone the model quickly once proven. These become kill criteria: if major labels refuse licensing within 6 months, or if a rival launches a compatible store within 12 months, the strategy must pivot.

**Decision:** The plan turns a hardware advantage into a service monopoly. It beats "do nothing" (losing users to piracy) and "subscriptions" (wrong model for the era).

DECISION: GO
