First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; poor phone sales do not create an advantage for a new, unproven hardware category, and "need" alone is insufficient without a specific cost, product, or timing advantage over existing solutions.

**Steelman:** A cylindrical speaker with always-on voice at $199 ($99 for members) creates a new home category, leveraging membership pricing to overcome adoption barriers despite past hardware failures.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits the company's last hardware launch failed and the category does not exist. It offers no evidence that a cylindrical form factor or invitation-only sales model beats the status quo of "do nothing" or existing phone assistants.
2.  **Unverified Premises:** The risks listed (privacy, recognition failure, no category) are treated as hurdles to clear rather than blockers. The proposal assumes membership pricing ($99) solves the privacy/value equation without data showing members trust the brand with always-on microphones more than non-members.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" costs $0 and avoids repeating a hardware failure. "Phone-based assistants" already exist at marginal cost. The proposal fails to demonstrate why this specific hardware configuration reaches the same upside cheaper or safer than improving the existing phone ecosystem.
4.  **Blocker:** The core claim relies on creating a new category while admitting the company has no recent hardware success and the market has no precedent for this device. The stated facts (poor phone sales, failed prior launch, high privacy risk) directly contradict the feasibility of launching a complex, always-listening hardware device as the *first* recovery step. The financials ($199/$99) are arbitrary without a unit economics breakdown or demand validation.

**Kill Check:** The risk of "customers may not accept an always-on microphone" is elevated to a blocker because the proposal states the company's brand is currently weakened by poor hardware performance; adding a high-friction privacy device without a proven trust mechanism is a factual mismatch with the stated brand state.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the edge test and contains a factual blocker regarding brand trust versus privacy intrusion.

DECISION: NO-GO
