First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; buying a low-revenue OS to fix a failing giant appears financially irrational without a unique strategic advantage explicitly defined.

Steelman: Acquiring the small firm provides immediate ownership of a modern, object-oriented core and the founder's expertise, bypassing years of failed internal development to rapidly halt market share loss.

Alternatives: Doing nothing guarantees continued decline. Licensing offers a modern core without acquisition risk but forfeits control and differentiation. Internal rebuild has already failed twice.

Fit: Assumes the priority is speed to market and technical modernization over short-term financial metrics.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the technical integration of the new core proves impossible due to architectural mismatches, or if the founder's return triggers internal rebellion, stalling the transition. This is a one-way door; the capital is spent and cannot be recovered if the integration fails. Delay costs further market share erosion.

Evidence: The proposal states internal replacement failed twice, validating the need for an external solution. The target's low sales are a fact, but in this context, they represent an acquisition discount rather than a product failure, provided the technology is sound. The high price relative to revenue is a blocker only if valued as a standalone business; it is justified only if valued as a strategic rescue asset.

Blockers: NONE. The apparent financial mismatch (high price/low revenue) is resolved by reclassifying the purchase as a strategic asset acquisition rather than a revenue-generating business buy. The risks regarding migration and the founder are execution challenges, not facts proving failure.

Risks: Integration complexity, cultural clash with the founder, and developer adoption. These become kill criteria: if a working prototype integrating the new core is not achieved within 12 months, or if key developer retention drops below 80% post-announcement, the strategy must pivot.

The unique edge is the combination of a ready-made modern core and the specific leadership required to execute the turnaround, which licensing cannot provide and internal teams have failed to deliver.

DECISION: GO
