First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a capital-intensive path without a stated edge over incumbents, relying on assumptions that contradict the company's verified lack of capital and experience.

**Steelman:** Committing exclusively to EVs and buying a large factory creates a first-mover advantage in a nascent market, forcing scale before competitors react.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capital Mismatch:** The proposal admits the company lacks funds for the factory and batteries, yet mandates buying a large factory. This is a factual contradiction; you cannot commit to an expense you cannot afford.
2.  **Infrastructure Gap:** In 2008, charging stations are scarce. A full-size sedan requires reliable long-distance charging to compete with gas cars. Without a built-in charging network or partnerships, the product is unusable for its target market.
3.  **Execution Risk:** Mass production is distinct from design. Buying a factory does not transfer the institutional knowledge required to run it. Established makers can respond with deeper pockets and existing supply chains.
4.  **No Stated Edge:** The proposal offers no specific advantage (technology, cost, or access) that allows this young carmaker to beat established buyers who already have factories and capital. Need alone is not an edge.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing/Contract Manufacturing:** Partner with an existing manufacturer to build the first run. This avoids the massive capital outlay of buying a factory and mitigates the lack of production experience.
*   **Niche Entry:** Start with a low-volume, high-price roadster to prove the technology and brand before attempting a mass-market sedan.

**Pre-mortem:** The company buys the factory, exhausts its capital on retrofitting, fails to achieve production volume due to inexperience, and runs out of money before selling a single unit because customers cannot charge the cars.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The company has less capital than required for the factory and batteries.
*   **Fact:** Charging infrastructure is insufficient for mass-market adoption in 2008.
*   **Contradiction:** The plan requires an action (buying a large factory) that the stated financial position makes impossible.

**Risks:** Competitor response, production failure, market rejection. These are secondary to the immediate financial and infrastructural blockers.

**Conclusion:** The plan fails the basic test of feasibility. It proposes a massive fixed-cost commitment without the means to pay for it or the ecosystem to support the product.

DECISION: NO-GO
