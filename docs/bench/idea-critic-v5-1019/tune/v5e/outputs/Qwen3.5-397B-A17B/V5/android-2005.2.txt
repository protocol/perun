First call: NO-GO. A search-ad company with no hardware DNA proposing a $50M acquisition of a pre-revenue, six-person OS team in a carrier-dominated market lacks a stated edge. The proposal admits no revenue model, no adoption path, and fierce incumbency; "free software" is a tactic, not a defensible moat against handset makers who fear commoditization or carriers who demand control.

Steelman: Acquiring the only viable open-source mobile OS early secures the future gateway to search, preventing a competitor from locking it down, and leverages openness to bypass traditional licensing costs.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve $50M, wait for the market to mature, and license search later. Cost: $0. Risk: Missing the platform shift. Reversibility: N/A.
2. Partner/License: Pay a fraction to embed search in existing OSs. Cost: Low. Risk: Limited integration. Reversibility: High.
3. The Proposal: Cost: $50M+. Risk: Total failure (adoption/carrier blockade). Reversibility: None (sunk cost).

The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" or "partnering" on current facts. It requires assuming carriers will cede control and handset makers will adopt a stranger's OS without immediate profit—assumptions contradicted by the stated market structure. The "long multi-year payback" with "no revenue model" is a blocker for a company with no hardware experience; the burn rate alone threatens the core business before any ad revenue materializes.

Pre-mortem: The project burns $200M over five years, handset makers reject the OS as a threat, carriers block it, and the team disperses. The core search business suffers from diverted focus. This is a one-way door with a high price of delay (capital loss), but the cost of acting now is certain financial drain versus uncertain upside.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: "Free software" does not overcome carrier/handset inertia without a distribution partner or hardware capability, which the acquirer lacks.
2. Financial mismatch: $50M for 6 people with no revenue is unjustifiable without a clear path to monetization, which is explicitly admitted as missing.

Risks: Carrier resistance, handset maker apathy, multi-year cash burn.

Kill criteria: None can be set because the fundamental premise (adoption) has no supporting evidence in the proposal.

DECISION: NO-GO
