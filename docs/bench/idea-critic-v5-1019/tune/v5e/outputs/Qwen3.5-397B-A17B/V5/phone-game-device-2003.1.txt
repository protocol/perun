First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; a higher-priced device with inferior ergonomics and a smaller library cannot beat the incumbent on these facts alone.

**Steelman:** A phone-maker can leverage brand loyalty to enter the gaming market by offering a hybrid device, accepting hardware compromises for dual utility.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Edge:** The target market is led by a dedicated competitor with a massive library and lower price point. The proposal admits the price is "far above" the leader and the library is non-existent (publishers uncommitted). Need is not an edge. Without a unique advantage (e.g., exclusive content, superior tech, or ecosystem lock-in explicitly stated), this fails the basic test of beating the status quo.
2.  **User Experience Blockers:** The risks cited are not theoretical; they are fundamental design failures. Removing the battery to change games is a catastrophic usability flaw that breaks the core loop of gaming. Awkward ergonomics for calls negate the primary value of a phone. These are not "risks" to mitigate; they are disqualifiers in a consumer hardware launch.
3.  **Economic Reality:** At $300, significantly above the market leader, the value proposition relies entirely on software availability. With publishers uncommitted, the device launches empty. High hardware cost + no software = guaranteed failure.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A "cheaper alternative" would be licensing games to existing phones or waiting for technology to converge naturally without forcing a bulky, battery-swapping hybrid.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to poor reviews citing battery swapping and awkward handling. Publishers refuse to port games due to low install base. Sales flatline. Inventory is written off.

**Blockers:**
*   **Usability:** Changing games requires battery removal (stated fact).
*   **Price/Value:** Price is far above the leader with no compensating library (stated fact).
*   **Supply:** Publishers are uncommitted (stated fact).

**Risks:** Market rejection, brand damage.

**Kill Criteria:** None can save this as proposed because the core mechanics are broken.

**Dissent Ledger:** The proposal confuses "capability" (can run games) with "viability" (will people buy it over the better, cheaper option). The battery swap mechanic alone invalidates the product category.

**Remaining Risk:** Total product failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
