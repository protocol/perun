First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a market need but fails to state an edge; "reusing assets" and "two months" are constraints, not advantages against an incumbent who already owns the genre.

Steelman: A stripped-down, free entry point leveraging existing art could acquire users cheaply if the core loop is instantly fun without the paid campaign. However, the attack reveals fatal gaps in the stated facts.

Alternatives: Doing nothing preserves the paid game's value. A cheaper alternative is a free weekend for the existing title, which tests retention without building a separate economy. The proposal must beat "do nothing," but the risk of cannibalization is high and unmitigated.

Fit: Entering a live-service genre requires ongoing content and balance, conflicting with the "two-month" build assumption. The studio lacks the stated capability (free-to-play business experience) to sustain the model after launch.

Pre-mortem: The mode launches, players try it once, find the monetization grindy or the balance poor compared to the rival, and leave. The separate title fragments the community, killing matchmaking for both games. The cost of delay is low; rushing a half-baked competitor damages the brand more than waiting.

Evidence: The proposal admits the studio has no free-to-play experience and the rival dominates. These are facts, not risks. Building a complex economy in two months with no prior experience contradicts industry norms.

Blockers:
1. **Capability Gap**: The team has no experience running a free-to-play business, yet the plan relies entirely on cosmetic monetization success.
2. **Timeframe Impossibility**: Two months is insufficient to build, balance, and economize a competitive last-player-standing mode that can challenge an established rival.
3. **Cannibalization**: No mechanism is proposed to prevent the free mode from devaluing the core paid product.

Risks: Rival dominance, player retention failure, brand damage. These are exacerbated by the blockers.

The proposal's own facts (no experience, short timeline, strong rival) directly contradict the likelihood of success. This is not a risk to be managed with kill criteria; it is a plan destined to fail as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
