First call: NO-GO. The proposal offers a massive cost increase (1GB vs MBs) without a proven edge; "fast search" is a feature, not a defensible business model against entrenched incumbents who can copy it.

Steelman: A 1GB free tier forces users to keep all mail, creating a unique, searchable life-log that drives higher ad engagement than competitors' deletion-heavy models.

Attack:
1. **Economics**: Storage costs in 2004 are high. If users fill 1GB, the cost per user may exceed the lifetime value of text ads, especially with no direct revenue. The math relies on low utilization or ad rates not yet proven at this scale.
2. **Privacy**: Scanning private mail for ads is a novel, untested violation of user trust. A single scandal could destroy the brand before it gains traction.
3. **Market**: Incumbents have network effects (address books, established habits). An invitation-only launch slows adoption, giving incumbents time to react or copy the storage bump without the ad-scanning baggage.
4. **Alternatives**: A paid tier with 1GB or a smaller free tier (e.g., 100MB) reduces risk while testing search value. "Do nothing" preserves capital until storage costs drop further.

Blockers:
- **Unit Economics**: The proposal lacks evidence that ad revenue per user exceeds the marginal cost of 1GB storage. Without this, the model bleeds money per active user.
- **Trust Barrier**: No mitigation is offered for the inherent privacy conflict of scanning mail; this is a fundamental product flaw, not just a risk.

Risks:
- Incumbents copying the storage offering.
- Low ad fill rates in a new format.
- Invitation-only growth being too slow to reach critical mass.

The proposal assumes users will tolerate privacy invasion for storage, but offers no data that this trade-off works. It also assumes ad technology can monetize this specific behavior profitably. These are unproven premises that block the plan as stated. A pilot with explicit user consent for scanning or a hybrid monetization model is required before a full launch commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
