First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge beyond one customer's request; it assumes dominance will protect against massive capital exposure and technological uncertainty without evidence of broader market adoption or a hedge against the supersonic threat.

**Steelman:** Building a 2.5x larger wide-body jet with a convertible freighter design captures emerging high-volume routes and secures long-term utility, leveraging current dominance to fund the transition before supersonic renders subsonic obsolete.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Ruin:** The development and factory outlay exceeds all prior commitments. With only one airline ordered, the company bets its entire existence on a single client's continued solvency and demand accuracy. If that order cancels, bankruptcy is near-certain.
2.  **Technological Gamble:** Relying on an unproven high-bypass engine from a supplier introduces a single point of failure. If the engine fails certification or performance targets, the airframe is useless.
3.  **Strategic Obsolescence:** The industry expectation is that supersonic airliners will replace subsonic jets. Investing billions in a larger subsonic jet may create a "white elephant" just as the market shifts, making the new factory a stranded asset.
4.  **Market Thinness:** Only one airline has ordered. The assumption that others will follow is unverified. Without multiple launch customers, the unit economics cannot support the massive fixed costs.

**Blockers:**
*   **Single Customer Dependency:** The proposal admits only one airline has ordered. This fact alone blocks approval; the financial risk is too concentrated.
*   **Unproven Core Technology:** The engine is unproven. Committing to production before engine validation violates basic engineering risk management.
*   **Competing Paradigm:** The stated expectation of supersonic replacement directly contradicts the long-term viability of a massive subsonic investment without proof that supersonic will be too expensive or limited for high-density routes.

**Risks:** Cost overruns, delayed certification, supersonic adoption faster than expected, freighter conversion never utilized.

**Kill Criteria:** Secure binding orders from at least two additional major airlines within 6 months; achieve successful ground testing of the high-bypass engine prototype within 12 months; independent analysis confirming subsonic demand remains dominant for 20+ years despite supersonic entry.

DECISION: NO-GO
