First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; poor styling ratings and an aging platform offer no advantage over existing buyers' options, while cost cuts further erode value.

Steelman: A budget-friendly, utility-focused vehicle leveraging existing minivan economics to serve a niche camping demographic despite aesthetic compromises.

Attack: The core claim fails on evidence already in hand. Early customer clinics explicitly rated the styling poorly; for a "youthful" demographic, visual appeal is the primary purchase driver, not just utility. Relying on an aging platform with compromised design limits differentiation and invites reliability stigma without the performance to justify it (no off-road ability). The break-even target of 30,000 units is unsupported given the negative market signal from clinics. A cheaper alternative exists: do nothing and wait for a clean-sheet design, or refresh the current minivan with accessories, avoiding the sunk cost of a flawed crossover program.

Blockers:
1. Stated fact: Early customer clinics rated styling poorly. This directly contradicts the "youthful" positioning required to move volume.
2. Stated fact: Cost cuts compromised design on an aging platform. This removes the route to quality or distinctiveness needed to justify a new model launch.

Risks:
1. Failure to meet 30,000 sales due to poor reception (elevated to blocker by clinic data).
2. Brand dilution by launching a compromised product in a visible segment.

The proposal admits the target users (youthful buyers) rejected the look in clinics. Proceeding ignores this direct evidence of market rejection. No kill criteria or pilot can fix a fundamental design rejection before launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
