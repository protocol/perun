First call: GO. The proposal targets a specific, emerging demographic (young postwar buyers) with a stated edge: sporty styling at a low base price, enabled by reusing proven mechanical parts from an existing reliable compact car. This creates a cost advantage competitors cannot easily match without building a new platform from scratch.

**Steelman:** Launch a customizable, sporty coupe/convertible using existing compact underpinnings to capture the untapped youth market at a disruptive price point, supported by massive awareness advertising.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the emerging youth segment to competitors; risk brand irrelevance as the current buyer base ages.
2.  **New Platform:** Build a dedicated sports car chassis. Higher cost, higher price, misses the "low price" requirement, slower time-to-market.
3.  **This Plan:** Reuse mechanics (low cost/risk), invest in styling and marketing (high upside). Beats doing nothing by capturing new volume; beats a new platform by hitting the price target.

**Fit:** Aligns with the need to refresh the brand image and capture the next generation of buyers.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the "cheap" parts perception overwhelms the sporty styling, or if the ad spend fails to convert interest into orders. However, the low base price reduces the barrier to trial, and the long options list allows high-margin customization to offset launch costs.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on the verified existence of the reliable compact car (mechanical base) and the demographic shift (baby boomers entering the market). The financial risk is capped by the shared tooling; the variable cost is the ad spend, which is reversible if scaled poorly, though the initial launch is a one-way door for brand perception.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (unproven demand, cheap perception, heavy spend) are execution risks, not factual blockers. The plan addresses the "cheap" risk via styling and the "demand" risk via the low price entry point. The heavy spend is the necessary cost of creating a new category.

**Kill Criteria:** If first-year sales do not cover the marginal cost of the ad campaign plus a 10% margin on the vehicle, halt further ad spend and reduce options complexity.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The objection that sharing parts with an economy car will make the car look "cheap" was tested against the styling directive (long hood, short deck). The visual differentiation is sufficient to decouple the perceived value from the donor car, provided the marketing emphasizes performance and style over economy.

DECISION: GO
