First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it admits no large paying market exists and relies on academic experimentation, which cannot justify raising unit costs on every consumer chip in a fierce competitive race.

Steelman: Embedding general-purpose compute capabilities now creates an indispensable platform monopoly before rivals realize scientific computing is the next growth frontier, turning every gamer into a subsidized supercomputer node.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws based strictly on provided facts. The "better-way" analysis shows "do nothing" is superior: academic users are already hacking existing APIs, proving demand can be explored without costly silicon changes. Building a full compiler and toolchain for a non-existent market diverts critical engineering resources from the core life-or-death rivalry in gaming, where revenue currently originates. The cost structure is inverted: raising unit prices on mass-market goods without committed customers violates basic unit economics.

Pre-mortem: The program fails because the added silicon area reduces gaming performance or increases prices, causing gamers to switch to the rival's cheaper/faster chips. The scientific features remain unused by the mass market, and the niche academic users refuse to pay the premium required to recoup the R&D, leaving the company with higher costs and lower market share in its only revenue stream.

Blockers:
1. No committed customers: The proposal explicitly states no large paying market exists. Building supply without demand is a fundamental failure mode.
2. Core business threat: Engineering distraction during a "core race" with a main rival risks losing the sole existing revenue stream (gaming/workstation) for a speculative future.
3. Negative unit economics: Raising unit cost on every chip without a price-insensitive buyer guarantees margin erosion or volume loss.

Risks (converted to kill criteria if proceeding): Niche demand remains niche; toolchain adoption stalls.

The proposal fails the blocker test. It bets the entire current business on a hypothetical future without a bridge (paying customers). A sound operator would wait for a specific, paying anchor tenant before altering silicon architecture and cost structures.

DECISION: NO-GO
