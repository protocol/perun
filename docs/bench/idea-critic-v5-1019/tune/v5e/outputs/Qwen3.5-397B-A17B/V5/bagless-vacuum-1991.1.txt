First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a higher price, no manufacturing history, powerful incumbents who can copy the tech, and unproven buyer willingness to pay a premium. Need alone is not an edge; the stated facts show no advantage over existing options.

Steelman: A superior bagless technology justifies a premium price if early adopters value performance enough to ignore brand and distribution gaps.

Attack: The proposal admits buyers "may not pay extra." Without evidence of demand at the proposed price point, the core revenue assumption is unsupported. Incumbents copying the idea neutralizes the unique selling proposition before scale is reached. No manufacturing record introduces execution risk that could delay launch or degrade quality, further eroding trust against established brands.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids capital loss in a crowded market with unproven demand.
2. License the tech to an incumbent: Leverages their distribution and manufacturing, reducing risk, though ceding margin.
3. Low-cost pilot: Build a tiny batch to test price elasticity before founding a full company.

Fit: Assumes a strategy of disruptive innovation, but the lack of a "stated edge" (e.g., exclusive patent, pre-orders) makes this a gamble, not a strategic move.

Pre-mortem: The company launches, fails to secure shelf space, burns cash on manufacturing inefficiencies, and folds when a major brand releases a similar model at half the price. This is a one-way door with high cost of delay if the market shifts to bagless anyway, but the risk of total loss dominates.

Evidence: The proposal itself provides the blockers: higher cost, no track record, copycat risk, and uncertain demand. These are not risks to manage; they are facts that break the business model as proposed.

Blockers:
1. Stated fact: Buyers may not pay the premium required to cover higher costs.
2. Stated fact: Incumbents can copy the idea, removing the moat.
3. Stated fact: No manufacturing record implies high execution failure probability.

Risks: Market education costs, supply chain setup.

The proposal's own facts confirm the target users have no proven desire for this specific solution at the required price, and the competitive landscape allows immediate neutralization.

DECISION: NO-GO
