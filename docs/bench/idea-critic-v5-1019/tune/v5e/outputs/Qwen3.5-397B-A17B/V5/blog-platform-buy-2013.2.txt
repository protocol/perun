First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a high price ($1.1B) against little revenue, with no stated edge explaining why this specific asset beats existing options or justifies the multiple given the integration history and ad-hostility.

Steelman: Acquiring the platform provides immediate access to a young demographic that the portal cannot organically reach, betting that independence preserves culture while back-end ad monetization can be slowly introduced without triggering churn.

Options:
1. **Acquire:** High cost ($1.1B cash), high risk (integration failure, user exodus due to ads), low reversibility (cash spent, brand damage).
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, status quo relevance decay, fully reversible.
3. **Partner/Integrate Lightly:** Lower cost, tests ad tolerance without full ownership risk, highly reversible.
The proposal fails to demonstrate why the $1.1B purchase beats "Do Nothing" or a cheaper partnership, especially given the stated inability to integrate past acquisitions.

Fit: Assumes the north star is "young audience share" rather than "profitable young audience." If the users dislike ads and content is hard to sell, the strategic fit for an ad-driven portal is contradictory unless a new monetization model (not stated) exists.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition closes; the portal imposes ad standards to justify the cost; young users flee to the next free platform; the founder leaves due to culture clash; the portal is left with an expensive, adult-content-labeled asset it cannot monetize. This is a one-way door with massive delay cost (capital locked for years).

Evidence: The proposal's own facts constitute the blocker. The price ($1.1B) vs. "little revenue" creates an immediate financial hole. The stated fact that "users dislike ads" directly contradicts the plan to "grow advertising." The history of failed integrations suggests the "keep it independent" pledge will likely fail under executive pressure.

Blockers:
1. **Economic Mismatch:** $1.1B price tag lacks a stated path to ROI given "little revenue" and high ad-resistance.
2. **Strategic Contradiction:** The core asset value (young users) is explicitly threatened by the proposed monetization method (ads), which the users dislike.
3. **Execution History:** Past integration failures make the "independent" survival condition unlikely.

Risks: Founder departure, adult content liability, culture clash. These are secondary to the fundamental economic and strategic blockers.

DECISION: NO-GO
