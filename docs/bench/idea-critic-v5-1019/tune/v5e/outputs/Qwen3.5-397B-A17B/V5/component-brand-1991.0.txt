First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "unprecedented" is a liability, not an advantage, and need alone does not justify the cost.

**Steelman:** Component branding creates consumer pull that forces OEMs to prioritize this chip over clones, justifying the ad subsidy.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves the full budget for R&D or direct OEM incentives. A cheaper alternative is technical differentiation (speed benchmarks) marketed to IT buyers, not end consumers. The proposal fails to prove consumer ads beat these lower-cost paths.
2.  **Fit & Evidence:** The claim that buyers care about invisible components is an unsupported assumption. No evidence is given that consumers influence B2B procurement or that they can distinguish "similar part numbers."
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Consumers ignore the logo; OEMs resent the interference and reduce orders; the budget is wasted with zero sales lift. This is a one-way door with high sunk cost.
4.  **Blocker Test:** The proposal admits benefits "may not show in sales." Without a verified mechanism linking consumer awareness to corporate purchasing decisions, the core ROI calculation is imaginary. The risk of OEM resentment is a concrete negative stated in the proposal that removes the route to upside (OEM cooperation).

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal names risks (unprecedented channel, budget share, OEM resentment, uncertain benefit) but offers no kill criteria, pilot cap, or metric to validate the "consumer pull" hypothesis before full spend. It asks for a massive commitment based on silence where evidence is required.

**Verdict:**
Blockers: The proposal explicitly states benefits are unproven ("may not show") and identifies a direct conflict with partners (OEM resentment) without a mitigation plan. The "unprecedented" nature confirms a lack of market validation.
Risks: Total budget waste, channel alienation.

This is a speculative bet with no stated edge over existing strategies and explicit admission of unproven outcomes. It fails the operator test for immediate approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
