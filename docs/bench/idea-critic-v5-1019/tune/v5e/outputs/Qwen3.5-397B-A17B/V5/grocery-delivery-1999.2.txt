First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits irreversible capital before validating the core hypothesis that unit economics can be positive in a low-margin, high-density-dependent business.

**Steelman:** Rapidly building a national moat via automated infrastructure prevents competitors from securing prime real estate and customer loyalty, leveraging abundant capital to win the "land grab" before the market matures.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" beyond the current metro preserves cash while learning unit economics. A "staged rollout" (one warehouse per quarter) tests density requirements and automation reliability at 1% of the cost. The proposed plan beats neither on risk-adjusted returns; it merely accelerates exposure to unproven variables.
2.  **Fit:** The strategy contradicts the stated lack of operating track record. Scaling an unproven model does not fix broken unit economics; it amplifies losses.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 2001. Cause: Massive fixed costs (warehouses, trucks) deployed in cities with insufficient order density to cover variable delivery costs within 30-minute windows. The $1 billion sunk cost prevents pivoting to a sustainable model (e.g., larger slots, pickup points) before bankruptcy.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no operating track record" and "low-margin business." These are facts, not risks. Low margins require proven efficiency before scaling; no track record means efficiency is unknown. Committing $1B based on assumptions rather than data from the first city is a fundamental violation of capital allocation logic.
5.  **Blocker:** The plan explicitly rejects learning unit economics before scaling. Since profitability in low-margin grocery delivery is entirely dependent on unknown density and operational efficiency metrics, expanding before measuring them guarantees that the $1B investment is based on fiction. This is a factual blocker: you cannot scale what you have not proven works.

**Blockers:**
- Committing $1B to fixed assets before validating unit economics in the initial market.
- Ignoring the stated lack of operating track record while executing a complex, capital-intensive rollout.

**Risks:**
- Customer density fails to materialize.
- Automation technology proves unreliable at scale.
- Margins remain too thin to service debt on $1B infrastructure.

DECISION: NO-GO
