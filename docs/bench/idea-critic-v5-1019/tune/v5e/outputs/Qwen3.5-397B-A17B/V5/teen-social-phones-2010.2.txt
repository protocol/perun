First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it offers a high-cost, feature-poor device to price-sensitive users without addressing the core cost barrier or leveraging the company's upcoming platform.

Steelman: Launching a restricted, carrier-locked social device now captures the youth market immediately, avoiding cannibalization of the main platform launching later.

Attack:
1. **Economics:** Requiring a full smartphone data plan for a device costing as much as a smartphone destroys the value proposition for price-sensitive youth. The "edge" of social networking is nullified if the total cost of ownership exceeds a standard smartphone that offers games and an app store.
2. **Timing & Strategy:** Launching two limited devices before the main platform fragments the brand and confuses carriers, especially given the history of internal conflict between teams. It forces carriers to support a dead-end OS before the real product arrives.
3. **Features:** No app store and no games for young people is a non-starter in 2010, where entertainment is a primary driver. This is not a niche; it is a broken product.
4. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" and wait for the main platform is superior. It avoids channel conflict, leverages the new OS, and allows a unified go-to-market strategy. A cheaper, staged alternative (e.g., a software layer on existing hardware) is not proposed but would be safer.

Blockers:
- **Cost Mismatch:** The target demographic cannot afford the required data plan + device cost, while competitors offer more for less.
- **Feature Deficit:** Lack of apps/games removes the primary utility for the target audience.
- **Internal Friction:** Known conflict between teams threatens execution without a unified platform strategy.

Risks:
- Market rejection due to price.
- Carrier resistance to supporting two OS launches in one year.
- Cannibalization of the upcoming main platform launch.

The proposal fails the basic economic test for its target audience and introduces unnecessary strategic complexity. It does not beat "do nothing."

DECISION: NO-GO
