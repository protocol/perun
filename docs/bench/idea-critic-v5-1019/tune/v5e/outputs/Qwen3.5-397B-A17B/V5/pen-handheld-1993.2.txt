First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "leading a new category" is an ambition, not a competitive advantage over existing notebooks or emerging palmtops, especially given the admitted unreliable core mechanism.

**Steelman:** A $700 pen-based device can define a new market if the CEO's publicity creates sufficient initial demand to overcome technical immaturity and high cost.

**Attack:**
1.  **Core Mechanism Failure:** The proposal admits handwriting recognition is "unreliable in testing." For a device where this is the *main* input, this is a functional blocker, not a risk. Users cannot tolerate a primary interface that fails frequently.
2.  **Price vs. Value:** At $700 (1993 dollars), the price is premium. Without proven software or reliable input, the value proposition collapses compared to established alternatives.
3.  **Form Factor:** Being "too big for a pocket" contradicts the "handheld" and "personal" promise, reducing portability—the key utility of such a device.
4.  **Ecosystem Void:** "Little software" and an "unproven category" mean high adoption friction. Heavy publicity cannot sustain a product that fails basic usability tests.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids brand damage from a flawed flagship launch.
*   **Delay/Refine:** Wait until recognition reliability meets a minimum viable threshold before committing to mass production and heavy marketing.
*   **Niche Pilot:** Release a limited developer unit to build software and gather data without the risk of a consumer-facing failure.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to massive hype, users immediately encounter input failures, reviews destroy the product's reputation, the category is deemed a gimmick, and the company loses significant capital and credibility.

**Blockers:**
*   Unreliable handwriting recognition (stated fact) makes the primary input unusable.
*   High price + poor utility = negative value proposition.
*   Pocket-size failure undermines the core use case.

**Risks:** (All become blockers given the stated facts) Unproven demand, lack of software, execution difficulty.

The proposal relies on hope (publicity) to overcome demonstrated functional deficits. A sound operator does not launch a product with a broken primary feature at a premium price.

DECISION: NO-GO
