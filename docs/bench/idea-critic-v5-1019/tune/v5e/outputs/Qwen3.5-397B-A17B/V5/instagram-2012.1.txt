First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge beyond defensive fear; paying $1B for zero revenue and no business model fails basic operator logic without a proven path to monetization or integration synergy.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the sole dominant mobile photo threat neutralizes a competitor that could erode the core business's future relevance, buying time to fix internal mobile weaknesses.

**Attack:**
1.  **Valuation Blocker:** The price implies a massive multiple on non-existent revenue. Without a defined business model, this is speculation, not investment. The "threat" is unproven as a business, only as a user attention sink.
2.  **Alternative:** Build or partner. The buyer has immense resources; copying features or licensing technology is cheaper than a $1B outright purchase of an unproven entity.
3.  **Cultural Risk:** The proposal admits high risk of destroying the very culture that made the app successful. Integrating a dozen-person startup into a giant often kills the innovation asset being bought.
4.  **Regulatory & Execution:** Regulatory review adds uncertainty, and the buyer's own weak mobile product suggests they may fail to leverage the acquisition effectively.

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition closes, the founders leave due to culture clash, the user base migrates to the next new platform, and the company is left with a $1B write-down and no improved mobile strategy.

**Evidence:** The facts provided show zero revenue, no business model, and a valuation detached from financial fundamentals. The "threat" is qualitative (user growth), not quantitative (financial loss).

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial:** No revenue or business model to justify a $1B price tag based on stated facts.
*   **Strategic:** No stated edge showing how this specific acquisition beats building internally or waiting for a lower valuation post-IPO volatility.

**Risks:** Culture destruction, regulatory blocks, failure to integrate, overpayment for a fad.

**Kill Criteria:** If the app fails to monetize within 12 months post-acquisition, or if key founder retention drops below 6 months, the thesis fails.

The absence of a business model and the astronomical valuation relative to any financial metric constitute a fundamental blocker. Defensive fear is not a strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
