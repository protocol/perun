First call: NO-GO. The proposal offers no stated edge over the current market position; it relies on aesthetic modernization while explicitly removing the brand's primary shelf cue (the orange with a straw) for a loyal, older demographic.

Steelman: A minimalist redesign with a cap-shaped orange and vertical typography will attract new buyers without alienating the core base, justified by a massive ad campaign.

Attack: The plan fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests immediately. The proposal admits the old image is the "main shelf cue" and the buyers are "older" and "loyal." In retail, shelf recognition is the primary conversion mechanism for CPG. Removing the dominant visual anchor for a demographic resistant to change, while simultaneously rotating the brand name (reducing readability), creates a high probability of sales collapse. The "large advertising campaign" is a cost, not an edge; it attempts to buy back recognition the design voluntarily discarded.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Maintains shelf visibility and loyalty at zero risk.
2. Staged pilot: Test the new design in a limited region to measure recognition loss before a nationwide rollout.
3. Hybrid: Retain the straw/orange iconography while updating other elements.

The proposal beats none of these. It assumes advertising can instantly retrain deep-seated visual habits of an older demographic, a claim unsupported by the provided facts. The risk of shoppers failing to recognize the carton is not a "risk" here; given the facts, it is a near-certain outcome that acts as a blocker. The cost of the redesign plus the ad spend, combined with the high likelihood of lost sales from confused loyalists, means the numbers do not work as proposed.

Blockers:
1. Removal of the stated "main shelf cue" for a "loyal and older" base guarantees recognition failure.
2. No evidence that minimalism converts this specific demographic better than the established cue.
3. Vertical rotation reduces readability, compounding the recognition deficit.

Risks:
1. Competitor exploitation of shelf confusion.
2. Irreversible loss of market share if the rollout is nationwide and immediate.

Kill criteria are irrelevant as the plan cannot proceed. The lack of a pilot or metric is secondary to the fundamental flaw: destroying the product's primary identifier without proof of a superior replacement.

DECISION: NO-GO
