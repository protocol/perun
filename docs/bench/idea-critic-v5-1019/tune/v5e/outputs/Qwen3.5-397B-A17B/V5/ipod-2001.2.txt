First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it offers a higher-priced device with a smaller compatible base than existing failures, relying on a market (legal downloads) that does not yet exist.

Steelman: A premium, high-capacity device creates a new category by solving storage limits, betting that superior integration will eventually force industry licensing and expand the user base beyond the current small computer share.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Avoids capital risk but cedes the emerging digital audio space to cheaper, fragmented competitors.
2. **Cheaper alternative:** Partner with existing PC makers or license software to widen the addressable market immediately, reducing unit cost.
3. **Proposed plan:** High risk, high cost, limited initial reach. It does not beat "do nothing" on current facts because the ecosystem required for success (legal music, large install base) is absent.

Fit: Assumes a strategy of vertical integration to control user experience, but this contradicts the immediate reality of a tiny market share. Without a stated mechanism to bridge the gap between "small share" and "mass adoption," the fit is broken.

Pre-mortem: The product launches to critical acclaim for design but fails commercially because users cannot legally fill the 5 GB drive, and non-owners cannot use it. The high price prevents impulse buys, and component shortages delay launch past the holiday window. Cost of delay: Missing the window to define the category before competitors solve the storage issue.

Evidence: All claims regarding market share, pricing, and lack of licensed music are accepted as stated facts. No internal data exists to contradict the premise that the current base is too small to sustain a $400 accessory.

Blockers:
1. **No stated edge:** The proposal admits to higher price, smaller compatible base, and missing content ecosystem compared to the status quo. Need alone is not an edge.
2. **Ecosystem dependency:** Success requires legal downloads and software expansion, neither of which are secured or guaranteed by the plan.

Risks: Component sourcing delays, consumer rejection of price, failure to secure music licenses.

The plan fails the primary test: it does not explain why this specific approach wins *today* against cheaper alternatives or doing nothing. It relies entirely on future conditions (licensing, market expansion) that are not yet true.

DECISION: NO-GO
