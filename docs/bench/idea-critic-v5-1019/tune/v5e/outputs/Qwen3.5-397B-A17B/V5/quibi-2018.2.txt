First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (mobile video) but fails to state an edge; "high production budgets" and "A-list talent" are cost drivers, not differentiators in a market saturated with free, abundant short-form content.

Steelman: A premium, mobile-native service leveraging studio IP and adaptive technology can overcome subscription fatigue by offering unmatched quality and convenience that free platforms cannot replicate.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or licensing content to existing giants (YouTube, Netflix) offers immediate revenue with zero customer acquisition cost. Building a new siloed app requires billions in CAPEX/OPEX to fight entrenched habits. The "cheaper path" (licensing) reaches the same upside (monetizing IP) without the execution risk of a standalone platform.
2.  **Fit:** The strategy conflicts with the stated "subscription fatigue." Adding another paid tier for content format (short-form) that users already consume for free contradicts market behavior.
3.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no proven demand for premium short-form." Raising $1B based on an unproven hypothesis while competitors give similar formats away is financially unsound. The "technology feature" (orientation adaptation) is a commodity, not a moat.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The service launches, burns cash on talent, and fails because users refuse to pay for short videos when free alternatives exist. The high fixed costs of A-list talent make the unit economics impossible at a viable subscriber count.

Blockers:
-   **No Stated Edge:** High cost and A-list talent do not constitute a competitive advantage against free, abundant substitutes; they are liabilities without proven willingness-to-pay.
-   **Contradictory Market Data:** The proposal explicitly cites "no proven demand" and "subscription fatigue" as risks but proceeds with a capital-intensive launch anyway.
-   **Superior Alternative:** Licensing content to existing platforms achieves monetization with significantly lower risk and capital requirement.

Risks:
-   Execution difficulty in acquiring subscribers.
-   Rapid changes in mobile consumption habits.

The absence of a pilot, a proven willingness-to-pay metric, or a path to profitability that beats the "licensing" alternative constitutes a fatal flaw. The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test on stated facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
