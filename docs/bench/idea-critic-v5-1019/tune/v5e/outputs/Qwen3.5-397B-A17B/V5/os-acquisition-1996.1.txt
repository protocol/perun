First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on acquiring a company with negligible revenue and market traction to solve a critical, existential failure where two internal attempts have already collapsed. There is no stated edge demonstrating why this specific small vendor's technology succeeds where the maker's own vast resources failed, nor why it beats licensing an established competitor's system.

Steelman: Acquiring this specific modern, object-oriented system provides the only viable shortcut to a competitive platform, leveraging external innovation to bypass internal stagnation, with the founder's return ensuring technical continuity.

Attack: The core claim fails on evidence. The target has "sold few units," meaning the technology lacks market validation. Paying "a few hundred million" for unproven software with high migration risk contradicts basic capital allocation logic when "licensing another vendor" offers a proven, lower-risk alternative. The "difficult history" of the founder introduces unnecessary organizational volatility during a crisis. The proposal admits the maker failed twice internally; substituting internal failure with external obscurity at a premium price is not a strategy, it is desperation.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Continued share erosion, likely bankruptcy.
2. License established vendor: Lower cost, proven stability, immediate availability, no integration of toxic personnel.
3. Acquire target: High cost, unproven tech, high migration risk, cultural poison pill.

The proposal does not beat "do nothing" because it accelerates cash burn without guaranteeing a working product. It does not beat licensing because it trades certainty for gamble.

Blockers:
1. Price vs. Value: Paying hundreds of millions for a company with few sales and no market fit is financially indefensible based on stated facts.
2. Superior Alternative: Licensing exists, offering the same upside (modern OS) with less risk and cost, blocking this specific acquisition path.
3. Execution Risk: Migrating developers to an unproven core after two internal failures compounds the likelihood of a third failure.

Risks: Founder conflict, migration failure, continued market share loss.

The stated facts show the target users (developers) have not adopted this system, and the cost exceeds reasonable bounds for the asset's proven worth. A cheaper, safer alternative reaches the same upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
