First call: GO. The stated edge is a 100x storage advantage (1GB vs MBs) combined with fast search, solving the user pain of routine deletion. This creates a defensible wedge against incumbents who rely on storage scarcity.

**Steelman:** A search company leverages its core indexing competency to offer unlimited utility (storage + search) where competitors offer scarcity, monetizing via high-intent text ads rather than subscriptions.

**Attack & Findings:**
*   **Skeptic:** The cost of 1GB/user in 2004 is non-trivial. However, if the search giant already operates massive storage clusters for web crawling, the marginal cost of email storage is likely lower than standalone email providers. The privacy objection is valid but was a known trade-off for free services in 2004; the value exchange (free GBs for ad scanning) is explicit.
*   **Better Way:** "Do nothing" cedes the high-value user to competitors eventually. A paid model fails against entrenched free habits. The invitation-only launch is the correct "better way" to manage server load and create scarcity-driven demand without capital expenditure on infinite immediate scaling.
*   **Kill Criteria:** The proposal lacks a specific stop condition, but the "invitation-only" constraint acts as a natural circuit breaker. If ad revenue per user does not exceed storage/ops cost within the first cohort expansion, the model fails.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The math works if the company has existing storage infrastructure (highly probable for a "search company"). No fact provided proves the target users reject ad-scanning when given 1GB free.
*   **Risks:** Privacy backlash, high initial capex if infrastructure isn't shared, slow adoption due to invite-only. These are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The mature market is a strength here; users are trained to need email but are underserved on storage.

**Decision Logic:**
The proposal beats "do nothing" by capturing users desperate for space. It beats competitors by offering what they technically cannot (at that price point) without breaking their legacy business models. The ad-funding model aligns with the company's existing competency. The risks are manageable via the invite-only gate.

DECISION: GO
