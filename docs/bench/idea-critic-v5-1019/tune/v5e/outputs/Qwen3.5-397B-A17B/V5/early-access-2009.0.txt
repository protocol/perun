First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a rough state, zero marketing budget, and no support capacity; it offers no stated edge (e.g., unique mechanic, existing cult following, or pricing advantage) that makes this specific plan beat "finish first" or "free demo" in a 2009 market where unfinished paid games were often reviewed poorly.

**Steelman:** Releasing early monetizes development immediately, turning players into co-designers to ensure feature fit while funding completion.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** In 2009, the "Early Access" model was not a recognized, trusted category on major PC platforms (Steam Greenlight arrived in 2012). Selling an explicitly "unfinished" product without a publisher's seal of quality risks brand death before launch. The proposal admits the state is "rough," which contradicts the requirement for a paid product to provide immediate value.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" (finish privately) avoids negative reviews. A "free alpha" gathers feedback without the backlash of charging for broken software. The proposal fails to explain why charging *now* is superior to building a wishlist via free content.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The game launches; players encounter bugs and lack of content; they demand refunds and leave negative reviews on forums and emerging aggregators. The solo dev, already unable to support a large audience, is overwhelmed by support tickets, halting development entirely. The "rough state" becomes the permanent public perception.
4.  **Blocker:** The stated fact that "a solo developer cannot support a large audience" directly contradicts the mechanism of a public sale, which invites a large audience. Without a plan to cap sales or manage expectations (which 2009 distribution channels did not easily support for paid indie titles), the operational constraint blocks the revenue model.

**Risks:** Negative word-of-mouth destroying future potential; burnout from support duties; revenue falling short of expectations due to refund demands.

**Kill Criteria:** If a free demo does not generate significant organic traffic within 4 weeks, do not sell. If support tickets exceed 10 per day, halt sales.

The proposal relies on a market behavior (paying for unfinished indie games) that was not yet established in 2009, while ignoring the explicit operational constraint of the developer.

DECISION: NO-GO
