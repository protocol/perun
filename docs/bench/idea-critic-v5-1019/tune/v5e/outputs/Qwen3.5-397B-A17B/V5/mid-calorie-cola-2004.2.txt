First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it offers a compromised product at a premium price to a segment already served by diet cola, without evidence that young men specifically demand "half-sugar" over "no-sugar."

Steelman: A mid-calorie option captures consumers who find diet cola's taste unsatisfying but want fewer carbs than regular soda, creating a new category before rivals dominate.

Attack: The "compromise" positioning is the fatal flaw. Consumers reducing carbs typically seek elimination, not reduction, making the product redundant for dieters and insufficient for low-carb adherents. The premium price creates a barrier for a "trial" product that may fail to satisfy either core need (taste vs. health). If the low-carb trend is a fad, the specialized inventory becomes stranded. A rival's similar launch suggests the "first mover" advantage is already contested, negating the urgency to launch without a differentiated mechanism.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Protect margin on existing lines; wait for trend clarity.
2. Reformulate existing diet: Improve taste without adding calories (cheaper, lower risk).
3. Proposed mid-calorie: High cost, high confusion, unproven demand.

Fit: Assuming the goal is growth in a shrinking sugar category, this plan attacks the wrong constraint (calorie count vs. taste perception).

Pre-mortem: The product launches, confuses shoppers who grab diet or regular instead, fails to gain repeat purchases due to lack of distinct value, and is discounted heavily within six months, damaging brand equity.

Evidence: The proposal admits risks (satisfaction gap, price sensitivity, fad potential) but offers no data to refute them. No pilot or metric is defined to validate the "young men" hypothesis.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: The plan does not explain why "half-sugar" beats "no-sugar" for the target demographic.
2. Contradictory economics: Premium pricing for a potentially inferior (compromised) product deters the necessary trial volume.

Risks: Trend reversal, cannibalization of diet sales, brand dilution.

Kill criteria: None proposed. Without a verified demand signal or a clear advantage over the "do nothing" or "taste-improved diet" alternatives, the plan fails the operator test.

DECISION: NO-GO
