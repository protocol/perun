First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a critical fact: the company is known for reliable, economical small cars with no presence above the mid-price segment. Luxury purchasing is driven by brand prestige and status signaling, not just engineering quality or lower price. A flagship sedan from an economy manufacturer, even with a separate dealer network, lacks the inherent brand equity required to compete with European incumbents who hold decades of prestige. The "stated edge" of lower price is insufficient to overcome the stigma of the parent brand in the luxury segment; buyers seeking status will likely still choose established European marques.

Steelman: Creating a distinct brand and dealer network isolates the luxury product from the economy reputation, allowing engineering parity at a lower cost to undercut competitors.

Attack: The proposal admits development has already cost heavily and relies on a single flagship model. This creates a fragile financial position with high fixed costs (new dealer network) and low revenue diversity (one model). The risk that buyers reject the brand is not merely a possibility; it is the primary hurdle of luxury market entry. Without an existing halo or heritage, "matching quality" is a commodity play, not a luxury play. The separate dealer network increases the burn rate before proving demand.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids massive capital expenditure on unproven luxury demand.
2. Acquire an existing luxury brand: Instantly gains prestige and dealer network, though at high upfront cost.
3. Joint venture: Shares risk and leverages partner prestige.

Pre-mortem: The brand launches, but sales remain negligible because status-conscious buyers ignore the badge. The heavy development costs and new dealer overhead bankrupt the division, dragging down the profitable core business.

Blockers: The stated fact that the company sells "almost nothing above the mid-price segment" combined with "decades of prestige" held by rivals indicates the core claim (that a new brand can match them on quality/refinement alone) fails to address the primary driver of luxury sales: brand heritage. The thin line-up cannot support the cost of a separate dealer network.

Risks: Brand rejection, high burn rate, single-product dependency.

Kill criteria: If pre-orders do not cover 20% of first-year production capacity within six months of unveiling, halt dealer network expansion.

DECISION: NO-GO
