First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the core economics are unproven and forecasts a large short-term margin hit without defining a cap, pilot scope, or break-even volume. A need (shipping friction) is stated, but no edge proves this specific price point covers the variable cost of heavy buyers.

Steelman: An annual fee prepaid by customers funds increased order frequency, where the lifetime value of retained users exceeds the subsidized shipping costs.

Attack: The "heavy buyer" risk is not a possibility; it is a mathematical certainty if the value proposition works. If $79 covers two-day shipping for only 10 orders, an 11th order incurs a direct loss. Without historical data to model elasticity or a hard cap on eligible orders per member, the retailer is underwriting unlimited liability for a fixed revenue. The "hard to reverse" nature of prepaid annual contracts locks the company into these losses for 12 months before correction is possible.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain current friction but protect margins.
2. Free shipping threshold (e.g., orders over $50): Increases average order value to offset shipping costs, limiting exposure to small, frequent losses.
3. Capped pilot: Offer the membership to a small, invited segment with a strict cancellation clause if loss ratios exceed a set percentage.

The proposal fails because it treats a variable cost (shipping) as a fixed revenue model without a verified conversion rate or cost-per-order ceiling. The risks listed in the text (margin hit, unproven economics) are not future possibilities; they are immediate structural flaws in the plan as written. A "go" decision requires a bounded experiment, not a full launch. Since the proposal explicitly states the economics are unproven and offers no mechanism to validate them before scaling, it cannot proceed as described.

Blockers: Unproven economics with unlimited variable cost exposure; no defined pilot cap or kill metric.
Risks: Margin erosion, inability to reverse prepaid contracts.

DECISION: NO-GO
