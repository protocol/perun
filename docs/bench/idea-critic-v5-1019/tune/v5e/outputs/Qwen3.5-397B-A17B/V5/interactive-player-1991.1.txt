First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; a $1,000 price point without a unique, compelling advantage over rapidly improving personal computers or cheaper consoles is a non-starter in 1991.

**Steelman:** A dedicated $1,000 appliance succeeds by offering a seamless, high-fidelity interactive experience that general-purpose PCs cannot yet match and game consoles do not target, creating a new "living room computer" category.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $1,000, this device costs more than high-end PCs which offer broader utility (productivity, programming) alongside emerging multimedia. The "interactive disc" library is small and unproven, offering insufficient value to justify the premium over a console ($200–$400) or the utility of a PC.
2.  **Category Confusion:** Positioned between a console and a PC, the device risks appealing to neither. Console buyers want games; PC buyers want utility. A "multimedia player" is an undefined category for consumers who already own devices that play music and photos or are about to gain those capabilities via PC upgrades.
3.  **Content Chicken-and-Egg:** A small library of interactive titles creates a negative feedback loop. Without a massive installed base, developers won't create content; without content, consumers won't buy the expensive hardware.
4.  **Technological Obsolescence:** PCs are rapidly gaining multimedia abilities. A dedicated hardware box with no upgrade path (other than buying new discs) is vulnerable to being outpaced by general-purpose machines within months.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price Disadvantage:** The $1,000 price is explicitly cited as "far above game consoles" while offering less utility than PCs. This is a fatal economic mismatch.
*   **Undefined Value Proposition:** Buyers "may not understand what the product is for." A $1,000 consumer electronic device cannot launch with an ambiguous identity.

**Risks:** Small library, rapid PC competition, category confusion. These are severe, but the price and lack of a clear "why" are immediate blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** If the device cannot demonstrate a killer app that *only* it can run and that justifies 5x the cost of a console, the project fails.

DECISION: NO-GO
