First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge proving this specific leadership fit beats the incumbent model for this specific customer base; success in electronics does not transfer to department stores without evidence, and need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Radical simplification and branded shops will restore margin and brand prestige by eliminating coupon dependency, leveraging the CEO's prior specialty retail success.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost, extreme risk (total chain rollout), irreversible brand damage if failed.
2. **Do Nothing:** Maintain falling sales but preserve current customer habits; low immediate risk.
3. **Pilot Program:** Test new pricing in 5–10 stores; moderate cost, high learning value, fully reversible.
The proposed plan does not beat "Do Nothing" on safety nor "Pilot" on risk-adjusted upside.

Fit: Assumes the North Star is margin restoration via brand elevation. However, the stated fact that "most sales historically come from items sold at discount" contradicts the assumption that the customer base values "everyday pricing" over coupons.

Pre-mortem: The plan fails because the core demographic, trained to wait for sales, perceives "everyday low price" as "no discount" and stops buying entirely. Revenue collapses before brand perception shifts. This is a one-way door; once trust in coupons is broken, it cannot be easily restored. The cost of delay is continued slow decline, which is preferable to immediate catastrophic failure.

Evidence: The proposal cites the CEO's electronics success as proof. This is an unsupported claim when applied to a different sector with different customer economics. No data shows department store shoppers behave like electronics buyers.

Blockers:
1. **Customer Behavior Mismatch:** The stated fact that most sales are discount-driven directly contradicts the core mechanism of everyday pricing. Removing the primary purchase trigger without verified demand for the new model guarantees revenue loss.
2. **No Pilot:** Rolling out an untested, sector-transplanted strategy to the entire chain simultaneously ignores the specific risk of alienating the existing base.

Risks: Pace of change, brand confusion, competitor exploitation of the transition.

The proposal's own facts (heavy discount dependency) point toward failure, not success. The lack of a pilot converts a risk into a blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
