First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (business features) but fails to state an edge: it does not explain why this specific machine will beat existing business computers that already have lowercase and 80 columns, nor how it will overcome the massive switching costs for current owners and software makers.

Steelman: A dedicated business successor with essential text features is required to capture the enterprise market, necessitating a rapid transition from the hobbyist-focused predecessor.

Attack: The plan relies on a complex new design with a tight one-year schedule and unproven reliability. Crucially, it ignores the ecosystem lock-in: existing software makers may not port applications, and current owners may refuse to upgrade, leaving the new machine with no software and no users. Phasing out the only successful product before the successor proves reliable risks catastrophic revenue loss. The "edge" is merely feature parity with competitors, not superiority.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Continue selling the successful unit; risk losing future business market share to competitors.
2. Adapter/Peripheral: Build an external 80-column card or terminal adapter (cheaper, faster, reversible).
3. Parallel Launch: Keep the original running while developing the new one slowly to ensure reliability and software support.

Pre-mortem: The new machine launches late with bugs; software makers ignore it due to small install base; sales collapse because the original was discontinued too soon; the company runs out of cash.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: Feature parity is not a strategy against established business incumbents.
2. Ecosystem risk: No plan to ensure software migration; without apps, business users will not buy.
3. Revenue risk: Phasing out the only successful product before the new one is proven creates a single point of failure.

Risks: Hardware reliability, schedule slippage, user resistance.

Kill criteria: If a prototype cannot run key business software within 6 months, or if pre-orders from major software vendors are not secured before discontinuing the old model, stop the phase-out.

The proposal demands killing the cash cow for an unproven, complex successor with no clear advantage over existing business machines other than fixing basic omissions.

DECISION: NO-GO
