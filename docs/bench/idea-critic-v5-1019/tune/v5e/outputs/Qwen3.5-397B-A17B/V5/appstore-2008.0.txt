First call: GO. The stated edge is a curated, secure native ecosystem on a device where the CEO previously dismissed web apps as sufficient, creating a unique value proposition of quality and safety that open rivals lack.

Steelman: A single, company-run store offering native apps with a 30% revenue share and strict review ensures security, stability, and product polish while incentivizing developers with monetization and distribution.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Rely on web apps; risk losing developers to rivals with native ecosystems.
2. Open sideloading: Maximizes developer freedom but invites security risks, instability, and carrier backlash.
3. Partner-led stores: Dilutes brand control and user experience consistency.

Fit: Aligns with a premium product vision where "polish" is a north star; open distribution contradicts this.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if review bottlenecks stall key apps, or if the 30% fee drives top developers to rivals. However, the "walled garden" prevents the instability that could kill the phone's reputation. This is a one-way door for platform identity; delay cedes the developer mindshare to incumbents.

Evidence: The proposal cites existing rival ecosystems as proof of demand. The 30% model is standard for retail distribution. Security concerns are mitigated by the review process itself.

Blockers: NONE. The concerns (security, carrier objections, workload) are operational hurdles addressed by the proposed solution (review team, negotiation), not facts proving the plan fails. The risk of hurting polish is paradoxically solved by the strict approval process.

Risks: Developer adoption speed, review scalability, carrier friction. These become kill criteria: if top 50 apps are not present within 6 months, or if review time exceeds 48 hours consistently, the model must pivot.

The strongest attack is that 30% is too high compared to open distribution. This fails because developers pay for access to a premium, secure user base and simplified billing, which sideloading does not provide. The curated nature is the product feature, not a bug.

DECISION: GO
