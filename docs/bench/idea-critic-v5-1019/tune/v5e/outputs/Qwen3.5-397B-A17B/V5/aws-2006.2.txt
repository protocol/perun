First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state an edge; thin retail margins and heavy capex do not inherently beat incumbent hardware vendors, and "unproven market" combined with zero internal platform experience makes this a speculative gamble rather than a defensible expansion.

Steelman: The company can monetize its spiky infrastructure excess by selling raw compute cycles cheaper than incumbents, leveraging existing assets to create a high-margin revenue stream that funds further growth.

Attack: The claim collapses under the weight of operational reality. Selling to developers requires a distinct reliability culture and support model that a retail organization lacks; one major outage destroys developer trust permanently. The "spiky" nature of internal infrastructure suggests inconsistent availability, which contradicts the "pay-per-use" reliability promise. Furthermore, entering a capital-intensive hardware market while investors are already questioning heavy spending invites a crisis of confidence. The risk of distraction is existential: if the platform fails, it drains capital from the core retail business without a proven path to recovery.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital, focus on core retail margins, avoid investor backlash.
2. Partner: License excess capacity to an established vendor instead of building a competing platform.
3. Internal optimization: Use spiky capacity to improve retail performance rather than selling it.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because retail-grade reliability cannot meet developer SLAs, leading to churn and reputational damage that spills over to the core brand. The capital drain forces a retreat, validating investor skepticism.

Blockers:
- No stated edge: Thin margins and high capex do not constitute a competitive advantage against incumbents.
- Capability gap: Zero experience running a developer platform at scale creates an unbridgeable execution risk.
- Investor conflict: Heavy capital spending is already questioned; this exacerbates the issue without guaranteed ROI.

Risks: Market unproven, distraction from core business, margin dilution, reliability failures. These are not merely risks; given the lack of edge and experience, they function as blockers. The proposal offers no kill criteria, no pilot cap, and no evidence that the target market wants *this* provider specifically.

DECISION: NO-GO
