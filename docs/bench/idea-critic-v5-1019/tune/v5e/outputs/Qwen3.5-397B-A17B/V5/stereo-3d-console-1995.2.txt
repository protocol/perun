First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "between generations" is a gap, not an advantage, and the stated facts (monochrome display, discomfort, health risks, weak library) describe an inferior product compared to existing handhelds and upcoming home consoles.

Steelman: A low-cost ($180) bridge device leveraging stereoscopic 3D to extend the brand during a hardware transition.

Attack:
1.  **No Competitive Edge:** The "edge" must beat current buyers' options. Red monochrome visuals are technically inferior to full-color handhelds available in 1995. Being "neither portable nor comfortable" removes the core value of a handheld and the convenience of a home console.
2.  **Health Blocker:** The proposal admits users "may get eye strain or headaches." In consumer electronics, a known physical adverse effect is a fatal flaw, not a manageable risk. This directly contradicts the requirement for a mass-market product.
3.  **Ecosystem Failure:** "Few games at launch" guarantees poor adoption. Without a killer app, the hardware is a novelty.
4.  **Cannibalization/Obsolescence:** With a next-gen home console due in two years, this device risks confusing consumers and dividing developer resources for a short window.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve brand reputation and focus resources on the next home console. Cost: $0. Risk: Low.
*   **Accelerate Home Console:** Bring the next generation forward. Cost: High. Risk: Technical delays.
*   **Standard Handheld Refresh:** Update existing portable tech. Cost: Medium. Risk: Low.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, reviewers cite headaches and poor visuals, sales flatline, and the brand suffers a "failure" stigma entering the next generation.

Blockers:
*   **Health Hazard:** Stated fact of eye strain/headaches makes the product unsellable as a mainstream device.
*   **Inferior Value Proposition:** Red monochrome vs. color competitors; stationary vs. portable. The proposal's own facts show it loses on every feature dimension.

Risks:
*   Low software library (mitigated by blocker: hardware won't sell regardless).
*   Short lifecycle (mitigated by blocker: no reason to start).

The proposal's own evidence confirms the target users will reject the core experience (discomfort, bad visuals). This is not a risk to manage; it is a fundamental product failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
