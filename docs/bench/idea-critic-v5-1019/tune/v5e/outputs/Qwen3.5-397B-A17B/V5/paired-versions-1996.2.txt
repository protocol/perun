First call: GO. The stated edge is not the dual-release itself, but the enforced social mechanic: trading via link cable turns a declining hardware constraint into a unique value proposition that single-cart competitors cannot match. Need alone is not an edge; the specific mechanism that forces interaction is.

Steelman: Releasing two versions is the only viable way to mandate peer-to-peer trading on this specific hardware, creating a viral loop that offsets the console's aging status by making the software a social necessity rather than a solitary product.

Options analysis:
1. **Do nothing:** Safe, but likely fails to stand out in a shrinking market; no viral mechanic.
2. **Single version with optional trading:** Weakens the incentive; players can complete the game alone, removing the social pressure to buy cables or recruit friends.
3. **Dual versions (Proposal):** Highest perceived risk of "price grab," but highest upside for engagement and unit sales (2x SKUs per dedicated fan). It beats "do nothing" because it manufactures demand through scarcity and social obligation.

Fit: Assumes the goal is maximizing engagement and sell-through in a mature/declining lifecycle stage. This fits a "last hurrah" strategy where revitalizing the ecosystem is critical.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the creature disparity is too severe, causing anger rather than desire, or if the installed base is too small to find trading partners. However, the "fear of missing out" on specific creatures drives the behavior. This is a two-way door for future titles, but a one-way investment for this specific launch window. Cost of delay is losing the holiday window or the remaining active user attention.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the assumption that the link cable capability is underutilized and that players value completionism over cost sensitivity. No external data contradicts this; the mechanic is self-validating if the creatures are desirable.

Blockers: NONE. The risks (popularity decline, price grab perception) are execution risks, not factual blockers. The publisher's uncertainty is a condition to be tested, not a fact that disproves the model. The "price grab" concern is mitigated if the value of completionism exceeds the cost of two carts.

Risks: Perceived greed, low install base for trading. These become kill criteria: monitor sell-through ratio of Version A vs. B and secondary market prices for "rare" creatures.

DECISION: GO
