First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; buying a litigation target with no revenue and massive costs does not beat the buyer's existing, safer organic path based on the facts provided.

**Steelman:** Acquire the dominant user base instantly to monopolize attention, betting that scale will force media partners to license rather than sue, and that ad technology can rapidly monetize traffic to cover bandwidth.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Suicide:** Paying $1.65B for zero revenue and high operating costs creates an immediate, massive drag on earnings with no proven mechanism to recoup. The "unproven ad model" is a guess, not a plan.
2.  **Existential Legal Risk:** The core asset (content) is primarily infringing. Media companies are already threatening suits. Buying the site buys the liability. If courts order content removal, the platform's value evaporates; if they award damages, the cost could exceed the purchase price.
3.  **No Moat:** High bandwidth bills and copyright threats are barriers to entry that hurt the acquirer more than competitors who aren't carrying this specific baggage. The "largest destination" status is fragile if the library is deleted by court order.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" and continue building the smaller, compliant service avoids the $1.65B cash/stock burn and the legal tsunami. A cheaper path is licensing content selectively or building creator tools without inheriting the infringement lawsuit backlog.

**Blockers:**
*   **Liability:** The proposal admits "media companies have threatened lawsuits" regarding the core inventory. This is a known, active threat that could nullify the asset's value immediately.
*   **Economics:** No revenue + high cost + massive premium = negative unit economics with no bridge to profitability shown.

**Risks:** Ad model failure, bandwidth costs spiraling, user exodus if content is removed. These are secondary to the blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** This deal fails the basic test of buying cash flow or defensible IP. It buys a lawsuit and a bill. Without a pre-negotiated indemnity from sellers (not mentioned) or a settled legal framework (not present), the downside is total loss plus damages.

DECISION: NO-GO
