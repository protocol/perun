First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a "large premium" and "far behind" positioning without a stated edge—such as exclusive technology, immediate revenue synergy, or a unique path to overtaking the incumbent—that justifies a $6 billion cash outlay. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Acquiring the platform instantly provides the missing display infrastructure to challenge the search leader before the window closes.

**Blockers:**
1. **Financials:** The proposal admits a "large premium" but offers no recomputed ROI, payback period, or margin analysis showing the $6 billion cost is recoverable given the "fast-changing" market risks. Spending the largest cash reserve on a premium asset without a proven return model fails the basic capital allocation test.
2. **Strategic Conflict:** The plan acknowledges "conflicts with existing agency customers" but provides no mechanism to resolve them. Alienating current revenue streams to chase a speculative display future contradicts the duty to protect the core business.
3. **Market Timing:** The target market is "fast-changing," yet the solution is a massive, irreversible cash buy. The competitor's recent agreement does not force this specific expensive reaction; a cheaper, staged partnership or build strategy could achieve similar upside with less downside.

**Risks:**
Technology obsolescence, customer churn due to conflicts, and integration failure in a volatile market. These are severe but secondary to the lack of a financial and strategic bridge in the proposal itself.

The proposal's own facts (premium price, customer conflicts, volatile market) point toward value destruction rather than creation. The absence of a stated edge to overcome these admitted negatives makes this a failed claim as proposed. A sound operator would demand a revised plan with a capped pilot, a clear conflict-resolution strategy, and a verified financial model before committing cash.

DECISION: NO-GO
