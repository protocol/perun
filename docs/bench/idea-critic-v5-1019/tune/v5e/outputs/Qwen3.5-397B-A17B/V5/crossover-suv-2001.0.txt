First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; poor styling ratings and a compromised design on an aging platform offer no advantage over existing buyers' options, while the "youthful" target conflicts with the minivan base.

Steelman: A rugged, affordable camping vehicle for youth can succeed if distinct styling and utility overcome platform limitations.

Attack: The core claim fails on verified premises within the text. Early clinics explicitly rated styling poorly, directly contradicting the requirement for a "distinctive" look to drive sales. The platform is aging, and cost cuts further compromised the design, undermining durability perceptions critical for camping. Crucially, the vehicle lacks real off-road ability, rendering the "camping" value proposition hollow against true SUVs. The break-even target of 30,000 units is unjustified given these fatal flaws; demand will likely miss this threshold due to the product's identity crisis (neither a capable off-roader nor a stylish urban cruiser).

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids sunk costs on a likely failure.
2. Redesign on a new platform: Higher cost, but addresses styling and capability gaps.
3. Market existing minivan with accessories: Cheaper, tests camping demand without new tooling.

Fit: Assumes a need for a youth crossover, but the execution contradicts the vision of a desirable, capable vehicle.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because young buyers reject the "uncool" styling and lack of off-road utility, while families prefer dedicated minivans or SUVs. Sales plummet below 15,000, missing the 30,000 break-even. This is a one-way door with high inventory risk. Delay allows for a proper platform update.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits poor clinic ratings, an aging platform, compromised design, and no off-road ability. These are internal contradictions to the success criteria.

Blockers:
1. Poor styling ratings directly threaten the "distinctive" sales driver.
2. Lack of off-road ability invalidates the core camping use case.
3. Aging platform + cost cuts = high risk of quality failures.

Risks: Market rejection, brand damage, inventory write-downs.

Kill criteria: If a refreshed clinic does not show >60% positive styling response and verified off-road capability is not added, stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
