First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the plan yields lower margins, invites copying via standard parts, relies on uncontrolled outside software, and enters a channel (retail) where the company has zero experience, without stating a specific edge that allows this mainframe-centric organization to beat established small-computer makers on their own turf.

Steelman: A segregated team using off-the-shelf components and open architecture can bypass internal inertia to capture the emerging personal computer market faster than competitors, leveraging retail reach despite lower per-unit margins.

Attack: The "separate team" assumption is fragile; mainframe culture often bleeds into satellite projects, bloating costs and slowing the one-year timeline. Rivals copying the design is not a risk but a certainty given the open architecture; without a proprietary moat or first-mover scale, the company becomes just another assembler with higher overhead. Dependence on outside software cedes control of the user experience to third parties, potentially fragmenting the brand promise. Most critically, the lack of retail experience is a structural deficit, not a learning opportunity; established players already own shelf space and distributor relationships. The proposal offers no evidence that lower margins can sustain the company's existing service-heavy cost structure.

Alternatives: Doing nothing preserves high-margin mainframe focus. Acquiring an existing small-computer maker provides immediate retail channels, software ecosystems, and design expertise, avoiding the "build from scratch" delay and cultural clash. Partnering with a current retailer under the company's brand mitigates channel risk without building a distribution network from zero.

Pre-mortem: The project launches late due to internal resource conflicts, the open design is cloned by cheaper Asian manufacturers within six months, and the lack of software control leads to compatibility nightmares that destroy brand reputation. The retail push fails due to poor placement, leaving inventory stranded.

Blockers: The proposal explicitly states the company has no retail experience and admits margins will be lower, yet offers no mechanism to offset these deficits or a specific advantage over incumbents. The reliance on outside suppliers for software contradicts the company's core competency of vertical integration, creating a strategic mismatch.

Risks: Cultural contamination of the skunkworks team, rapid commoditization of hardware, and channel conflict with existing sales forces.

DECISION: NO-GO
