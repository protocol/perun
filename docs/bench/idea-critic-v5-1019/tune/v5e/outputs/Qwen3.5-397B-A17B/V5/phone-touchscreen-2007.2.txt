First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; premium price without a keyboard contradicts the dominant business-phone standard, and no evidence is provided that buyers want a music-centric device over established keyboard phones.

Steelman: A converged device offering superior web and music integration can create a new category, bypassing carrier control to deliver a user experience keyboard phones cannot match.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Safe, but risks music player erosion by competitors; low cost, low risk.
2. License OS to carriers: Faster reach, but cedes design control and margin; high risk to brand integrity.
3. Proposed plan: High risk, high cost, potential high reward if the "edge" of integration works.

Fit: Assumes the north star is "best user experience" rather than "market share." If the goal is volume, this fails.

Pre-mortem: The device fails because business users reject the lack of a physical keyboard, and the premium price alienates consumers who view it as "just a phone." The single carrier limits adoption to one network's subscribers, stalling momentum. No third-party apps make it a dead end compared to emerging smart platforms.

Evidence: The proposal admits "no phone-industry experience" and "no third-party applications." It asserts carriers "normally dictate" design, yet claims one will agree to cede control without citing a specific carrier commitment or market data proving demand for a keyboard-less premium phone. The risk of "eroding player sales" is hypothetical; the risk of failing in the phone market is concrete.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: The proposal claims a premium price is viable but offers no fact showing buyers will pay more for a keyboard-less phone than for established business devices.
2. Contradictory constraints: Demanding carrier revenue share and design control while having no industry track record is a factual mismatch with the stated norm that carriers dictate terms.

Risks: Lack of apps, limited reach due to exclusivity, unproven form factor.

The proposal relies on assumptions (carrier acquiescence, user desire for no keyboard) that contradict current market facts. Without a pilot, specific carrier agreement in hand, or data validating the "music-first" phone hypothesis, the plan fails the blocker test.

DECISION: NO-GO
