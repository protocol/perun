First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; needing demonstration does not explain why this specific plan beats existing channels or why it will succeed where the industry has failed.

**Steelman:** Company stores are the only way to control the customer experience and demonstrate the new digital-music strategy, directly reversing sales losses caused by retailer neglect.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** The claim fails to beat "do nothing" or cheaper options. Dedicated demo kiosks within existing retailers or a partner-program to train staff cost a fraction of prime mall leases and solve the "staff push cheaper machines" issue without fixed overhead.
2.  **Fit & Evidence:** The company has 3–5% share and recent losses. High-fixed-cost expansion during a recession contradicts basic survival logic for a struggling minority player. The "poor retail track record in the industry" is a stated fact that acts as a strong prior against success; the proposal offers no mechanism to overcome this systemic failure.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: The company burns cash on leases while volume remains too low to cover overhead. Resellers, feeling threatened, deprioritize the brand further. The recession deepens, and fixed costs become unsustainable, accelerating bankruptcy.
4.  **Blockers:**
    *   **Financial:** Running losses + high lease costs + recession = immediate cash flow crisis.
    *   **Strategic:** No evidence provided that customers will travel to standalone stores rather than buy convenient alternatives at electronics retailers.
    *   **Historical:** The stated "poor retail track record in the industry" is a negative fact already true that removes the route to upside unless a specific differentiator (beyond "we will try") is proven.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal names risks (lease costs, channel conflict) but treats them as manageable rather than fatal. However, the math of losses plus high fixed costs in a downturn is a blocker, not a risk. The alternative (kiosks/partnerships) reaches the same upside (demonstration) cheaper and safer.

**Verdict:** The plan commits a struggling company to its highest-cost channel during a downturn without proving why it will defy industry-wide retail failures. The cheaper alternative achieves the demonstration goal without the existential fixed-cost risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
