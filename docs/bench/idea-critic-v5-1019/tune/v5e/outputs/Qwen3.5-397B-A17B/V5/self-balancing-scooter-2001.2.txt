First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price, unproven demand, and regulatory ambiguity without a specific advantage over existing transport make this a speculative bet rather than a sound operation.

**Steelman:** A premium, self-balancing transporter can create a new urban mobility category by leveraging brand prestige to overcome early adoption barriers.

**Attack:**
1.  **Options:** "Do nothing" costs zero and avoids regulatory liability. A cheaper, smaller pilot (e.g., 500 units for institutional trials) tests the "unproven use" risk without committing to a 40,000-unit factory. The current plan bets the company on unverified consumer behavior.
2.  **Fit:** Building massive capacity before validating sidewalk legality or consumer comfort contradicts prudent staging. If regulations ban usage in key areas, the factory becomes a stranded asset.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Regulations classify the device as a motor vehicle requiring licenses/insurance, killing casual use. The $5,000 price point limits sales to novelty seekers, failing to reach critical mass for network effects. Riders feel conspicuous, limiting daily utility.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits legal status is "unclear," price is "high," and uses are "unproven." These are not risks to be managed; they are missing facts required to justify a $5,000 SKU and a 40,000-unit capacity. No data shows buyers will pay this premium for an unproven form factor.
5.  **Blocker Test:**
    *   **Blockers:** The core claim (viable mass market product) contradicts the stated facts (unclear legality, unproven use, high price). A factory sized for 40,000 units assumes demand that the proposal explicitly states is unverified. This is a factual mismatch between capacity and evidence.
    *   **Risks:** Regulatory bans, social stigma, limited utility. These are severe but secondary to the lack of proof for the base assumption.

**Kill Check:** The proposal lists "unproven uses" and "unclear legal status." These are not mere risks; they are blockers because the plan proceeds as if they are resolved. A sound plan would start with a capped, time-boxed pilot to prove legality and utility before scaling.

**Decision:** The plan fails as proposed because it commits to massive scale without evidence that the product is legal or desired.

DECISION: NO-GO
