First call: GO. The stated edge is accessing an unserved "casual and non-gamer" demographic with a price point and interaction model (motion) that high-definition rivals cannot match without sacrificing their core value proposition. Need alone is not an edge; the specific combination of low cost, novel input, and family-friendly software creates a distinct category rather than competing in a saturated "graphics-power" race.

Steelman: By ignoring the specs war, the console creates a new market blue ocean where it faces no direct competition, relying on volume over hardware margin.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Remain a distant third in a two-horse race defined by graphical fidelity; likely eventual exit.
2. Compete on power: Requires massive R&D spend to catch leaders, risking financial collapse with no guarantee of displacing incumbents.
3. Proposed path: Lower upfront risk via modest hardware, betting on software novelty to drive adoption.

Fit: Moves the north star from "best graphics for hardcore gamers" to "widest adoption across all demographics."

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the motion controller is perceived as a gimmick rather than intuitive, or if third-party developers refuse to build software, leaving a content drought. However, the low price mitigates consumer risk, and first-party titles can bridge the initial gap. This is a two-way door; if the casual market responds, the platform scales; if not, losses are capped by the low-cost hardware strategy.

Evidence: The proposal cites specific skepticism (hardcore gamers, publishers) and risks (toy perception, no HD). These are known variables, not hidden blockers. The "thin margin" is a feature, not a bug, enabling the low price required to penetrate the casual market.

Blockers: NONE. The risks listed (limited support, toy perception) are execution challenges inherent to disrupting a category, not facts proving the plan fails as proposed. The plan explicitly accepts these to gain the upside.

Risks: Third-party hesitation, niche perception. These become kill criteria: if attach rates for motion games do not exceed X% by launch +6 months, pivot strategy.

The proposal correctly identifies that winning the existing game is impossible, so it changes the game. The skepticism confirms the differentiation is sharp enough to confuse incumbents, which is a leading indicator of potential disruption.

DECISION: GO
