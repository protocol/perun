First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but states no edge; it offers no fact showing why a feature would beat existing live-action films or why audiences would pay ten times the price for an unproven format.

**Steelman:** A full-length feature could unlock a new premium market segment, leveraging existing characters to generate revenue far exceeding short films if audience endurance holds.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves steady profits. A "cheaper alternative" (serializing shorts into a compilation) tests feature appetite at 1/10th the cost with lower risk. The proposal fails to explain why this staged approach is inferior.
2.  **Fit:** The studio's current model is "steady, thin profits." A 10x budget spike contradicts this financial reality without a guaranteed revenue multiplier.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: The audience leaves at the 40-minute mark; the budget overruns by 50% due to uncharted production complexity; the studio exhausts short-film reserves and collapses before release.
4.  **Evidence:** The only data points are negative: insiders advise against it, and no precedent exists for feature-length animation success. The "risk" of audience boredom is treated as a possibility, but without a pilot, it is a fundamental unknown that blocks the investment.
5.  **Blockers:** The proposal admits the budget relies entirely on current thin earnings, creating a single point of failure. If the feature flops, the studio dies. There is no stated mechanism to recoup costs if the "feature" format fails. The lack of a pilot or smaller-scale test is a critical gap; jumping straight to a 10x bet without validating the core hypothesis (audience endurance) is financially irresponsible.

**Blockers:**
- No validated demand for feature-length animation; insiders explicitly advise against it.
- Financial structure relies on thin margins to fund a 10x spike with no fallback; failure means total insolvency.
- Cheaper, reversible alternatives (compilations, pilots) exist but are not addressed as rejected.

**Risks:** Budget overrun, production complexity, audience fatigue. These are secondary to the blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** None proposed. A go requires a capped pilot proving audience retention >80 minutes before full funding.

DECISION: NO-GO
