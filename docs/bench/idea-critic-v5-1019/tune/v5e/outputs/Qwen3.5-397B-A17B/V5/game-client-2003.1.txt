First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: it solves internal distribution pain but offers no fact-based advantage to buyers over existing boxed games, while introducing friction (mandatory online, downloads) that contradicts 2003 market realities (spotty broadband, retail dominance).

Steelman: A mandatory client secures revenue and integrity long-term by shifting control from retailers to direct digital distribution.

Attack: The plan fails as proposed because it ignores the "blocker" of market readiness. In 2003, home broadband was not universal; forcing a mandatory online client and large downloads alienates the core installed base relying on dial-up or limited connectivity. The proposal admits players dislike mandatory accounts and retail partners may drop support. These are not mere risks; they are stated facts that remove the route to upside (sales volume) immediately. The "edge" of updates/anti-cheat does not outweigh the loss of retail shelf space and player access in this specific year.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Maintain retail sales, accept update/cheat friction. (Safe, preserves revenue).
2. Optional client: Offer the client for updates/anti-cheat but keep boxed games functional without it. (Captures early adopters, avoids retail backlash).
3. Proposed mandatory client: Forces migration, risks retail delisting and player revolt.

Pre-mortem: The client launches with bugs, broadband users struggle with downloads, retailers drop the games due to conflict, and sales collapse before the network effect of the store can materialize. The cost of delay is low; waiting for broadband penetration to rise is safer than forcing a premature transition.

Blockers:
- Stated fact: Home broadband is not universal in 2003.
- Stated fact: Players dislike mandatory online accounts.
- Stated fact: Retail partners may drop boxed games.
These facts contradict the core mechanism (mandatory adoption) required for the plan to work.

Risks: Competitors copying, early bugs (mitigatable if the blocker wasn't present).

The proposal's own facts show the target users (gamers in 2003) and channels (retail) are not ready for a mandatory shift. The lack of a stated edge that overcomes these specific 2003 constraints makes this a failure as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
