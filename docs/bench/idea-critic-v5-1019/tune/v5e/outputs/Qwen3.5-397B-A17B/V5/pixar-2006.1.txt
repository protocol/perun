First call: NO-GO. The stated price ($7.4B) against a one-film-per-year output creates an immediate valuation mismatch that no strategic synergy can instantly justify without a proven pipeline expansion.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio is the only way to secure its creative talent and IP against a rival, preserving the conglomerate's animation future despite the premium.

**Attack:**
1.  **Valuation Blocker:** At one film annually, recouping $7.4B requires unprecedented per-film profitability or immediate output scaling. The proposal offers no evidence that the studio can scale production without degrading the quality that drives its value.
2.  **Key Person Risk:** The deal makes the conglomerate dependent on a few creative leaders who just rejected a partnership extension due to economics. Integrating them as leaders of the failing hand-drawn unit assumes immediate cultural compatibility that recent negotiation breakdowns contradict.
3.  **Governance Hazard:** Converting the studio's largest shareholder into a major buyer shareholder creates a conflicted insider with leverage over the very entity paying them, complicating fiduciary duties and future dissent.
4.  **Alternative:** A revenue-sharing joint venture or exclusive distribution right could secure the IP flow without the $7.4B balance sheet risk or integration trauma.

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition closes, but the creative team chafes under corporate oversight, production slows to two years per film, and the stock price collapses under the weight of amortized goodwill with no hit films to offset it. The "synergy" with the hand-drawn unit fails due to distinct medium expertise.

**Evidence Check:** The proposal admits the price is "very high" and the studio produces "about one film a year." These stated facts alone contradict the financial viability of the purchase as proposed. The risk of dependence is not hypothetical; it is structural to the asset being bought.

**Blockers:**
*   Financial mismatch: $7.4B price vs. 1 film/year output lacks a stated scaling plan.
*   Governance conflict: Seller becomes major shareholder without stated mitigation.

**Risks:** Culture clash, creative attrition, pipeline stagnation.

The absence of a concrete plan to increase output or mitigate the shareholder conflict renders this specific all-stock proposal untenable.

DECISION: NO-GO
