First call: NO-GO. The proposal states the studio has never run a free-to-play business, yet the entire revenue model depends on cosmetic monetization mechanics they do not possess. Need alone is not an edge; without a stated advantage in execution or market access, entering a rival-owned genre with unproven economics is a failure waiting to happen.

**Steelman:** Reusing assets allows a rapid, low-cost entry into a popular genre to capture users via a free tier, monetizing later through cosmetics.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capability Gap:** The proposal admits zero experience with free-to-play operations (live ops, economy balancing, anti-cheat for high-stakes modes). Building the mode is not the product; running the economy is.
2.  **Cannibalization:** Releasing a free last-player-standing mode directly undermines the value proposition of the paid cooperative game without a proven conversion funnel.
3.  **Timeline:** Two months is insufficient to build, test, and tune a competitive multiplayer economy from scratch, especially with no prior data.
4.  **Competition:** A rival already dominates the genre; entering second with no unique hook and unproven monetization is a "me-too" failure.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Protects the paid game's revenue and avoids operational distraction.
*   *DLC Expansion:* Add competitive elements to the existing paid game, leveraging the current user base and avoiding the free-to-play learning curve.
*   *Partnership:* License the IP to a studio with existing free-to-play infrastructure.

**Pre-mortem:** The mode launches, players flock to it, but the cosmetic store generates negligible revenue due to poor pricing or lack of desire. The studio lacks the team to iterate quickly, the mode dies, and the brand is diluted by a low-quality free entry. Meanwhile, paid game sales drop as users wait for the free option.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** "Studio has not run a free-to-play business before."
*   **Impact:** The core revenue mechanism (cosmetics) requires expertise explicitly stated as missing. This is not a risk; it is a missing capability required for the plan to function.

**Risks:** Cannibalization, short timeline, strong incumbent.

The lack of operational experience in the specific business model proposed is a fatal flaw that cannot be mitigated by asset reuse.

DECISION: NO-GO
