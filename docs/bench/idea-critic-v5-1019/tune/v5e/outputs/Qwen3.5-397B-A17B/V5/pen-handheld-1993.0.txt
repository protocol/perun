First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price, unreliable core technology, and poor form factor against unproven demand constitute a failure case as proposed.

Steelman: Launching first defines the category, leveraging CEO publicity to overcome early technical debt and software scarcity before competitors arrive.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost, high technical risk, one-way door on brand reputation if the device fails publicly.
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, zero risk, but cedes potential category leadership to rivals.
3. **Wait/Refine:** Delay launch until recognition accuracy improves and form factor shrinks; lower risk, but risks being second-to-market.

Fit: Assuming the goal is category leadership, this plan attempts to seize it but ignores the prerequisite of functional utility. A leader with a broken core feature (handwriting) destroys the category rather than leading it.

Pre-mortem: The device launches to massive hype. Early users encounter unusable handwriting recognition, rendering the primary input method frustrating. The high price amplifies buyer remorse. Reviews destroy the brand's credibility for innovation. The "pocket" size issue makes it inconvenient for daily carry, leading to low adoption. Software developers, seeing poor sales and technical instability, abandon the platform. The company loses millions and exits the handheld market entirely.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the core mechanism (handwriting recognition) is "unreliable in testing." It admits the price is "high" and the size is "too big." These are not risks; they are confirmed defects in the current state.

Blockers:
1. **Core Function Failure:** The primary input method is unreliable. A computer without reliable input cannot function as a "personal digital assistant."
2. **Value Mismatch:** The price ($700) is high for a device that is too large and lacks software. The stated facts show the value proposition is negative.
3. **Form Factor:** Being "too big for a pocket" violates the fundamental utility of a "handheld" device intended for mobility.

Risks: Unproven category, software scarcity (these are secondary to the functional blockers).

The proposal's own facts confirm the product does not work as intended (unreliable input) and fits neither the user's pocket nor budget. No kill criteria or pilot can fix a product launched with a broken core feature and negative initial reception.

DECISION: NO-GO
