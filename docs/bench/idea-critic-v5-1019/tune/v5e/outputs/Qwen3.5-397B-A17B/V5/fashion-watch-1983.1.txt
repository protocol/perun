First call: GO. The proposal leverages a stated edge: positioning an inexpensive plastic watch as a "fashion item" rather than a "plain timekeeper" creates a new value proposition that bypasses direct competition on horological quality and allows for higher margins despite low production costs. This reframing addresses the reputation risk by decoupling the product from the company's traditional quality metrics.

Steelman: Launching twelve distinct fashion models creates a portfolio broad enough to capture volatile trends, mitigating the risk of any single design failing, while the "fashion" label justifies the aggressive one-million unit target by expanding the total addressable market beyond traditional watch buyers.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Safe for reputation but cedes the emerging low-cost, high-volume segment to competitors.
2. Plain timekeeper launch: Directly triggers the reputation risk and invites immediate price wars with established brands.
3. Limited pilot: Reduces exposure but fails to achieve the economies of scale required to make the "inexpensive" model profitable.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the market rejects the "fashion" categorization, viewing the plastic construction solely as "cheap," causing the reputation damage the strategy seeks to avoid. However, the proposal's explicit pivot to "fashion items" is the specific mechanism to prevent this. The risk of changing tastes is mitigated by the twelve-model spread; if one fails, others may succeed. The threat of price cuts by rivals is less relevant if the product is sold on style, not just utility.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (reputation, aggressive targets, taste volatility, competition) are inherent to the strategy but are addressed by the core tactical shift to "fashion." No fact in the proposal demonstrates that the target users explicitly reject plastic fashion items.

Risks: Execution difficulty in marketing to a new demographic; potential brand dilution if the fashion angle fails to resonate. These become kill criteria: monitor sell-through rates by model within the first quarter and brand sentiment scores. If sentiment drops below baseline or sell-through misses 50% of the run-rate required for the million-unit goal, pivot or halt.

The aggressive target is a function of the new market positioning; without it, the volume is impossible, but with it, the volume is the only path to profitability. The proposal holds together logically.

DECISION: GO
