First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a market gap but fails to state a defensible edge; "affordable" and "lightweight" are aspirations, not facts proving this plan beats existing used roadsters or why mass-market makers are wrong beyond their own risk aversion.

Steelman: A mid-size maker can profitably fill a vacuum left by premium brands by leveraging existing non-sports components to keep costs low, appealing to nostalgia rather than pure performance.

Attack:
1. **No Stated Edge:** The proposal claims the niche is "dead" to mass-makers due to safety costs and shrinking volume. It offers no evidence that this specific company can solve the safety-cost equation cheaper than incumbents who already rejected it. Without a proprietary method to bypass safety weight/cost penalties, "affordable" is a contradiction.
2. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A "cheaper alternative" is importing/selling existing stock or a limited edition using an existing chassis, which reduces R&D risk. The proposal ignores these.
3. **Fit:** Entering a shrinking segment with no heritage contradicts standard growth strategies unless the "north star" is purely brand halo, which is not stated.
4. **Pre-mortem:** The car launches, enthusiasts buy the first run, but safety compliance drives the price up to match established used alternatives. Sales collapse because the "affordable" promise was broken by regulatory reality.
5. **Evidence:** Zero data provided on component sharing, projected margin, or specific safety loopholes. The claim that "mass-market makers regard [it] as dead" is treated as a barrier, not a challenge with a solution.

Blockers:
- The proposal states safety rules make convertibles costly but claims the car will be "affordable" without explaining the mechanism to resolve this contradiction. This is an internal factual conflict.
- No evidence supports the claim that this specific maker can succeed where others see a "dead niche."

Risks:
- Segment shrinkage continues.
- Lack of heritage reduces perceived value.
- Two-seat limitation caps volume.

The plan relies on solving a cost problem (safety) that stopped larger competitors, without explaining how. As proposed, it is a gamble on an undefined advantage.

DECISION: NO-GO
