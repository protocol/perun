First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; while buyer confusion exists, the plan does not demonstrate why consumer advertising—a method with no precedent for components—will overcome the structural barrier that buyers cannot choose chips directly.

**Steelman:** If end-users demand a specific component by name, computer makers will be forced to source it to sell units, shifting power from OEMs to the supplier.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The claim relies on buyers caring about an invisible part. In 1991, microprocessors are technical specs, not lifestyle brands. There is no evidence provided that consumers possess the literacy or motivation to distinguish "clone" part numbers, making the demand generation hypothesis unproven.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves the current B2B relationships without alienating partners. A cheaper alternative is technical co-marketing focused on IT purchasers, not the general public.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. The campaign burns a massive share of the marketing budget. Computer makers, resentful of creative interference, quietly sideline the logo or refuse participation. Consumers remain indifferent. Sales flatline. The company loses its war chest for actual R&D or trade incentives.
4.  **Blocker Test:** The proposal admits benefits "may not show in sales." This is not a risk; it is a missing mechanism. The plan spends heavily on an unproven behavioral change (consumer pull) without a pilot, metric, or fallback. The cost is immediate and large; the upside is speculative. A plan that risks a "large share of the marketing budget" on an unprecedented tactic with no guaranteed route to revenue fails the operator test.

**Blockers:**
*   No stated edge proving consumer advertising works for invisible components; the "unprecedented" nature is a negative signal, not an opportunity, without data.
*   The cost structure (large budget share) creates a single point of failure with no defined kill criterion or pilot phase.

**Risks:**
*   OEM resentment leading to channel conflict.
*   Total budget waste if consumer indifference persists.

This is a high-stakes gamble disguised as a strategy. Without a small-scale test to prove consumers actually care, committing major funds is irresponsible.

DECISION: NO-GO
