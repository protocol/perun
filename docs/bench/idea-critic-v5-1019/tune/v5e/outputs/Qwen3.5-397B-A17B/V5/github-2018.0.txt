First call: NO-GO. A loss-making target with no permanent CEO, active community hostility, and a free alternative cannot justify a $7.5B premium without a stated edge proving this specific buyer unlocks value competitors cannot.

Steelman: Only the buyer's massive enterprise distribution can monetize the target's free user base fast enough to offset the $7.5B cost and neutralize the free competitor.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids integration risk but cedes the developer cloud to the free competitor.
2. Partner instead of buy: Gains integration without the $7.5B balance sheet hit or cultural backlash.
3. Build internal: Impossible given the target's existing network effects.

Fit: Assumes the buyer's north star is "owning the developer workflow." If the goal is purely revenue, the math fails immediately given the target's losses.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition triggers a mass exodus to the free competitor due to distrust, leaving the buyer with a hollowed-out platform and a massive goodwill debt. The target remains a siloed loss leader. This is a two-way door only if the community stays; once they leave, the asset is worthless.

Evidence: The proposal admits the target is loss-making and the community is hostile. No evidence is provided that the buyer can reverse this sentiment or monetize free users faster than the status quo. The $7.5B price implies a multiple on revenue that assumes successful monetization of a resistant user base.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: The proposal offers no fact showing why this buyer succeeds where others (or the status quo) fail to monetize the loss-making entity.
2. Negative synergy: The buyer's historical hostility + current community distrust creates a high probability of immediate value destruction (defection) upon announcement.

Risks: Regulatory scrutiny, integration failure, price erosion.

Kill criteria: If developer activity drops >5% within 6 months post-close, or if enterprise conversion remains <1% after 18 months, the thesis is broken.

The absence of a concrete mechanism to convert hostility into revenue, combined with the massive premium for a loss-making asset, makes this a speculative bet rather than a sound operational move.

DECISION: NO-GO
