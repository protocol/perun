First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: it does not explain why buyers, who currently cannot identify or choose chips, would suddenly care enough to demand a specific component by name, nor how this overcomes the unprecedented nature of B2B component branding.

Steelman: Creating artificial consumer pull for an invisible component will force computer makers to adopt the chip to satisfy demand, justifying the ad subsidy cost.

Attack:
1.  **Fit & Edge:** The proposal admits buyers "do not know which chip is in their computer." Without a pre-existing differentiator (speed, reliability) that consumers already value, creating demand from zero is speculative. The "edge" is assumed, not stated.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves the budget. A cheaper alternative is targeting technical buyers or computer makers directly with performance data, rather than funding generic consumer ads. The proposal fails to prove why the expensive consumer route beats direct B2B influence.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** If the campaign launches, computer makers may resent the branding constraints and refuse participation, or consumers may ignore the logo entirely. The result is a massive budget drain with no sales lift, exactly as the raised risks suggest.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal offers no evidence that component branding works or that consumers care. The risks (unprecedented approach, maker resentment, budget share, uncertain benefits) are acknowledged but not mitigated by facts.

Blockers:
*   **No Stated Edge:** The core claim relies on creating consumer demand for an unknown product without explaining the mechanism or proof that this is possible. Need alone is not an edge.
*   **Contradictory Premise:** The plan assumes buyers will "ask for its chip by name" despite the stated fact that they currently "do not know which chip is in their computer." This gap is a blocker, not a risk.

Risks:
*   Computer maker resentment.
*   Budget inefficiency.
*   Unproven category demand.

The proposal asks to bet a large share of the marketing budget on an unproven behavioral shift without a demonstrated reason why it would succeed. The absence of a pilot, cap, or metric is secondary to the missing strategic edge.

DECISION: NO-GO
