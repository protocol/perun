First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (business features) but fails to state an edge explaining why this specific complex, rushed redesign will succeed where the current machine's limitations persist, especially given the explicit risks of alienating the existing user base and software ecosystem without a proven migration path.

**Steelman:** A dedicated business machine with lowercase and 80-column display is essential to capture the enterprise market, requiring a rapid, clean break from the consumer-focused predecessor to establish credibility.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "adapter" options are ignored. Adding an external 80-column card or a software-based lowercase workaround to the *existing* reliable hardware is cheaper, faster, and preserves the installed base. The proposal offers no evidence that a full hardware redesign beats these incremental fixes within a year.
2.  **Fit & Pre-mortem:** Phasing out the only successful product before the new one is proven is a one-way door with catastrophic revenue risk. If the complex new design slips (highly probable given the tight schedule) or suffers reliability issues, the company has no product to sell. The assumption that users will voluntarily migrate ignores switching costs and software incompatibility.
3.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the new hardware reliability is "unproven" and the schedule is "tight." These are not risks to be managed; they are blockers when the strategy involves killing the cash cow. There is no data showing business users prefer a new, unproven platform over a patched existing one.

**Blockers:**
*   **Self-Contradiction:** The plan relies on unproven hardware reliability to replace the only revenue source.
*   **Superior Alternative:** Incremental upgrades to the current machine achieve 80% of the value with near-zero risk of alienating users or breaking revenue, which the proposal fails to refute.
*   **Missing Edge:** No fact suggests a full redesign beats a peripheral/patch solution for speed or market acceptance.

**Risks:** Migration failure, revenue gap during transition, new hardware defects. These are severe, but the blockers above are fatal based on the stated facts. The proposal attempts to solve a feature gap with a structural gamble that ignores safer, cheaper paths.

DECISION: NO-GO
