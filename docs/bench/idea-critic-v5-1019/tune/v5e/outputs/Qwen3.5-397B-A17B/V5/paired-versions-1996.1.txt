First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a shrinking hardware base and introduces friction (buying two units, finding a trading partner) without a stated edge proving players value social completion enough to overcome these barriers.

Steelman: Releasing two versions forces social interaction, creating a viral loop that compensates for the console's decline by making the game a social necessity rather than just software.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Release one complete version; lower risk, but no viral mechanic.
2. Single version with unlockable bonus creatures via in-game challenges: Retains completion goal without forcing hardware duplication or immediate social dependency.
3. Two versions (proposed): Highest friction, highest potential reward if social proof works.

Fit: Assumes the "north star" is maximizing engagement through social pressure. If the console is dying, the installed base may be too small to sustain the required trading network, causing the core loop to fail immediately.

Pre-mortem: The game launches, players buy one version, realize they cannot find friends with the other version or the cable, feel cheated by the "price grab," and the publisher's sales fears are realized. The delay cost is low; waiting to validate demand with a single version loses little.

Evidence: All figures are assumed. The claim that players *will* trade relies on unproven behavior for this specific demographic on this specific aging hardware. The risk of looking like a "price grab" is explicit in the proposal and unmitigated.

Blockers:
1. The stated risk that "trading needs a second player and a cable" combined with a "declining popularity" creates a critical mass failure mode. If the user base is fragmented and shrinking, the probability of finding a trading partner drops below the threshold required for the game's core value proposition to function. The proposal offers no solution for this isolation.
2. The "price grab" perception is a stated negative that removes the route to upside (word-of-mouth) unless proven otherwise. The proposal does not answer this.

Risks: Publisher uncertainty, execution difficulty of linking two SKUs.

Kill criteria: If a pilot with a small batch shows less than 20% of buyers attempting to trade within 30 days, halt the two-version strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
