First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; a machine that is slower, has less software, and costs multiples more than established competitors offers no defensible advantage to office workers.

Steelman: This device targets a niche willing to pay a massive premium for a superior graphical interface despite performance and ecosystem deficits.

Attack: The facts provided destroy the business case. Office buyers prioritize productivity and compatibility. A machine running "slowly" with "little third-party software" actively hinders work compared to faster, text-based or established graphical competitors. The price ($10,000) creates an insurmountable barrier without a corresponding value multiplier, which is absent here. Furthermore, the existence of a cheaper, similar internal alternative creates a direct conflict; launching the expensive, inferior version first cannibalizes the future cheaper option or guarantees immediate obsolescence upon its release.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids sinking capital into a non-competitive product.
2. Launch the cheaper internal machine: Captures the graphical interface market at a viable price point with better margins.
3. Delay this launch: Wait until performance improves or software library grows.

Fit: This contradicts basic market entry logic. You cannot lead with your worst, most expensive product when a better, cheaper version is already in the pipeline.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, sales are negligible due to price and speed, the internal cheaper machine renders it instantly outdated, and the company loses both development costs and market credibility.

Blockers:
1. Price/Performance Mismatch: The unit is slower and has less software than competitors but costs several times more. This is a factual failure of value proposition.
2. Internal Cannibalization: A cheaper, similar machine is already in development. Launching the expensive version now makes no strategic sense.

Risks: Market rejection, inventory write-downs, brand damage.

The proposal fails the fundamental test of offering a reason to buy. There is no edge, only disadvantages.

DECISION: NO-GO
