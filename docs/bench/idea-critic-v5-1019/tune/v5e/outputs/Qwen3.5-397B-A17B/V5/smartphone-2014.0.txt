First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; entering a saturated two-platform market with a higher price, fewer apps, and carrier exclusivity offers no defensible advantage over existing flagships.

Steelman: A niche device leveraging unique 3D hardware and shopping integration could capture high-value customers despite OS limitations.

Attack: The "edge" relies on unproven consumer demand for 3D displays and assumes shopping features outweigh the absence of mainstream apps. In 2014, app ecosystem lock-in is the primary driver of smartphone choice; a forked OS without top-tier apps is a non-starter for mass adoption, regardless of hardware novelty. The "one-touch" shopping feature is a software function that does not require a dedicated, expensive handset.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital, avoid direct combat with entrenched duopoly.
2. Cheaper alternative: Launch the shopping feature as an app on existing dominant platforms to capture value immediately without hardware risk.
3. Proposed plan: High cost, high risk, low reversibility (hardware inventory), dependent on changing consumer behavior regarding 3D and app stores.

Fit: While it aligns with a commerce vision, it contradicts the market reality where platform compatibility is the north star for smartphone buyers.

Pre-mortem: The device fails because carriers refuse to subsidize a non-standard OS, consumers reject the unsubsidized price given the app gap, and the 3D feature is viewed as a gimmick. This is a one-way door with massive inventory liability.

Evidence: The proposal admits the market is saturated, dominated by two platforms, and the device lacks their mainstream apps. These are fatal blockers for a flagship-priced device. The "edge" is assumed, not evidenced by buyer behavior.

Blockers:
1. App Gap: Flagship price requires flagship app support; the proposal explicitly lacks this.
2. No Stated Edge: Novelty (3D) does not overcome utility loss (missing apps) at a premium price point.
3. Carrier Exclusivity: Limits reach in a market where network effects dominate.

Risks: Execution difficulty, 3D adoption rates (all become irrelevant due to blockers).

Kill Criteria: N/A (No-Go).

DECISION: NO-GO
