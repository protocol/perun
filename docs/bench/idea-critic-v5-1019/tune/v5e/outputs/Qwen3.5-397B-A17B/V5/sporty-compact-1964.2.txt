First call: GO. The proposal targets a specific, emerging demographic (young postwar buyers) with a stated edge: sporty styling at a low price via parts reuse, a combination competitors lack.

Steelman: Leverage existing compact economics to deliver unmatched style-to-cost ratio for a new generation, monetizing customization to offset low base margins.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited are execution uncertainties, not factual contradictions.
**Risks:** Unproven volume demand; perceived "cheapness" from parts sharing; high upfront ad spend. These are addressed by the long options list (margin buffer) and the specific styling cues (long hood/short deck) which visually distance the product from the economy donor.

**Alternatives:**
1. *Do nothing:* Cede the emerging youth segment to competitors; risk brand stagnation as buyers age.
2. *New platform:* Prohibitive cost and time; misses the immediate demographic wave.
3. *Sporty trim only:* Insufficient to create the "sporty" identity required; fails the styling test.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it is because the marketing fails to separate the car's image from its economy roots, or the options list is poorly priced. The "one-way door" is the ad spend; the "two-way door" is production volume (scalable). Cost of delay is losing the first wave of this demographic to rivals.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on the verified existence of the reliable compact base and the demographic shift. The "unproven demand" is the very opportunity; waiting for proof cedes the market. The parts sharing is a feature (low price), not a bug, provided the styling successfully masks it.

**Kill Criteria:** If early pre-orders do not show a attach rate on options sufficient to cover the ad spend within 6 months, pivot marketing or trim levels. Leading metric: Option take-rate % and cost-per-acquisition vs. lifetime value.

The plan beats "do nothing" by capturing a new market segment with minimal incremental engineering cost. The risks are manageable via the proposed options strategy and marketing focus. No factual blocker exists.

DECISION: GO
