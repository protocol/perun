First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the synthetic costs more than leather, offers no stated edge (performance, scarcity, or unique aesthetic) to justify the premium, and faces immediate friction from manufacturers and potential wearer discomfort. Need alone is not an edge.

**Steelman:** This material offers a unique, durable aesthetic that defines a new "future" category, allowing early-adopter brands to command a price premium that offsets higher costs and retooling, making comfort secondary to style.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** If the material costs more than leather and requires factory retooling, the shoe manufacturer's margin shrinks unless they can raise prices significantly. In 1963, leather is a trusted, scalable commodity. Without a functional advantage (waterproofing, weight) or a massive marketing pull that guarantees sales, manufacturers will reject the risk.
2.  **Comfort & Adoption:** Wearers rejecting the product due to comfort kills repeat purchases. Marketing cannot force comfort. If fashion moves away, the high cost becomes a sunk loss with no resale value, unlike leather.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" (stick with leather) preserves margins and comfort. A "pilot" with one avant-garde designer is cheaper than "spending heavily on marketing" to the whole industry, yet the proposal skips this for a broad, expensive push.

**Blockers:**
*   **Cost Disadvantage:** The proposal states the synthetic costs *more* than leather. Without a cited functional benefit that buyers value more than the price difference, the unit economics fail immediately.
*   **No Stated Edge:** The text lists risks (cost, comfort, factory change) but provides no fact showing why a buyer would choose this over leather today.

**Risks:** Fashion volatility, manufacturer inertia, consumer rejection of comfort. These are secondary to the cost blocker.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot with a single partner does not yield a 20% repeat order rate within 6 months, stop.

The proposal asks to spend heavily on a product that is more expensive, potentially less comfortable, and requires partner investment, with no stated reason why the market wants it. It fails the basic operator test of having a competitive advantage.

DECISION: NO-GO
