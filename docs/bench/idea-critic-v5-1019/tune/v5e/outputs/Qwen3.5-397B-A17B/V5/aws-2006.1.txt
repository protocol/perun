First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but states no edge; thin retail margins and zero developer-platform experience offer no stated advantage over incumbent hardware vendors.

Steelman: The retailer can monetize its spiky, sunk infrastructure costs by renting excess capacity, turning a cost center into revenue.

Attack:
1. **No Stated Edge:** Incumbents possess brand trust, developer relationships, and dedicated support structures. The proposal admits the market is unproven and the team lacks scale experience. A "cheap" option built on retail-grade reliability is not a defensible product for enterprise developers.
2. **Strategic Conflict:** Investors already question heavy capital spending. Diverting focus to a low-margin, high-complexity B2B service distracts from the core retail business during a critical build-out phase.
3. **Operational Mismatch:** Retail reliability (99.9% during sales) differs fundamentally from developer platform expectations (99.99% continuous). Failure here damages the core brand.
4. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital and focus. Selling hardware via partners is a lower-risk alternative to building a service platform.

Pre-mortem: The project launches, attracts only price-sensitive hobbyists, suffers outages that make retail headlines, and is shut down after burning $50M in CAPEX that could have optimized retail logistics.

Blockers:
- **No Edge:** The proposal explicitly states no experience and no unique advantage over incumbents.
- **Capital Contradiction:** Pursuing a capital-intensive new line contradicts the stated investor concern regarding heavy spending.

Risks (converted to kill criteria if pursued later):
- Margin dilution > 2%.
- Core retail site latency increase > 50ms during spikes.
- Developer churn > 10% monthly.

The proposal fails the primary test: it does not explain why *this* company, *now*, with *these* constraints, beats existing options. It relies on hope that "if we build it, they will come," despite admitting the market is unproven and the team is inexperienced. Without a stated edge (e.g., proprietary technology, exclusive access to cheap power, or a massive existing developer waitlist), this is speculation, not strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
