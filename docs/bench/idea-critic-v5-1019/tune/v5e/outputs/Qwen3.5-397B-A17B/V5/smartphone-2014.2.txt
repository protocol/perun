First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price without the leading apps or ecosystem is a disadvantage, not a differentiator, in a saturated two-platform market.

**Steelman:** A niche device leveraging unique 3D hardware and shopping integration to capture high-value retail customers despite app limitations.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Edge:** The core value proposition (shopping) does not justify a flagship price point when the device lacks mainstream apps (social, messaging, banking) required for daily primary use. Users will not carry a second $600+ device just for product scanning.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or integrating the camera/scanning feature into existing successful tablets/e-readers is cheaper, reaches more users, and avoids the massive risk of carrier exclusivity and OS fragmentation.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The device fails because developers ignore the tiny install base, the app gap widens, and the "3D" novelty wears off, leaving a costly brick. The "one-touch" feature is a software function, not a hardware mandate.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the market is saturated and dominated by two platforms. History shows forked OSs fail without a massive content library or significantly lower price.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price vs. Value Mismatch:** Selling at flagship prices without flagship apps contradicts basic market dynamics stated in the proposal.
*   **No Stated Edge:** "3D display" is a gimmick, not a systemic advantage over incumbents. The proposal offers no fact showing buyers want this enough to abandon the dominant ecosystems.

**Risks:** Carrier exclusivity limits reach; app gap frustrates users; high R&D cost for low volume.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders do not exceed 100k units in the first month, halt production. Leading metric: Daily Active Users of the shopping feature vs. device returns.

The plan commits real money to a hardware gamble with no clear path to ecosystem viability. The "shopping" feature should be a software layer on existing devices, not a walled garden hardware play.

DECISION: NO-GO
