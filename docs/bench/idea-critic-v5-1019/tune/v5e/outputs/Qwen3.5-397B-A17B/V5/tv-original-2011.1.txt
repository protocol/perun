First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge where data proves demand for this specific genre, star, or release model; raw viewing logs of licensed content do not equal validation for a proprietary high-cost gamble.

Steelman: Leverage proprietary granular viewing data to bypass the pilot system, commissioning a proven creative team to secure exclusive prestige content that locks in subscribers before competitors react.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Continue licensing; margins shrink as costs rise, risking long-term irrelevance.
2. Traditional Pilot: Test one episode; reduces financial exposure but sacrifices secrecy, speed, and the "all-at-once" value proposition.
3. Proposed Plan: High cost, high risk, potential market-defining monopoly on talent and format.

Fit: Assumes the goal is transitioning from a distributor to a studio. If the north star is immediate profitability, this fails. If it is existential differentiation, it fits, provided the data actually supports the specific creative choices.

Pre-mortem: The show launches to critical indifference; the "all-at-once" model confuses users accustomed to weekly anticipation; the massive sunk cost of two seasons prevents cancellation after a weak start, draining the budget for other acquisitions. This is a one-way door; the money is gone upon commitment. Delay costs include losing the attached talent and allowing a competitor to launch a similar original first.

Evidence: The claim relies entirely on "viewing data." Without seeing the specific correlation between current viewing habits and the proposed drama's attributes, this is an assumption. The cost is a "large share" of the budget, creating a single-point failure risk.

Blockers:
1. No evidence presented that the existing data predicts success for *original* prestige drama, only licensed content consumption.
2. Committing to two full seasons without a pilot removes the industry-standard kill switch before major spend.

Risks: Audience rejection of the release model; talent underperformance; budget overrun.

Kill Criteria: If the data does not explicitly show a surplus of demand for the specific genre/star combination that licensed content cannot satisfy, the premise collapses. The lack of a pilot requires a stricter pre-commitment metric which is absent.

DECISION: NO-GO
