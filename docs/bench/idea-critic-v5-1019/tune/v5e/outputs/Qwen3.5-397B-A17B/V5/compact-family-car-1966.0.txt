First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state an edge: it does not explain why an 1100 cc car sold through a costly new dealer network beats existing low-price rivals or larger alternatives.

Steelman: A dedicated 1100 cc compact, sold via exclusive dealers from the new plant, captures families seeking specific value incumbents miss.

Attack:
1. **Options:** "Do nothing" avoids the sunk cost of a new dealer network. A "cheaper alternative" is utilizing existing distribution channels or modifying an existing model to fill the plant, avoiding the dual capital drain of new product + new network. The proposal admits rivals already sell small cars at low prices; without a stated differentiation (e.g., superior fuel economy, specific feature), entering a price war with higher fixed costs (new network) is financially unsound.
2. **Fit:** The risk that buyers prefer cheaper or larger cars is not mitigated. If the market splits between budget and size, a mid-tier 1100 cc car with premium distribution costs faces a "stuck in the middle" scenario.
3. **Evidence:** The proposal itself provides the blocker: "rivals already sell small cars at low prices" and "dealer network is costly." The math implies higher break-even volume with lower margin potential unless a specific advantage exists. No evidence suggests buyers are dissatisfied with current low-price options.
4. **Pre-mortem:** The plant runs below capacity because the dedicated network fails to generate sufficient volume against established rivals, while the high overhead of the new dealerships destroys profitability per unit.

Blockers:
- **Economic Contradiction:** High fixed costs (new plant + new dealer network) combined with a market where rivals already compete on low price creates a negative margin trajectory without a stated volume or price advantage.
- **Missing Edge:** No fact explains why this specific configuration wins against "cheaper" or "larger" preferences.

Risks:
- Channel conflict or cannibalization if the dedicated network isn't truly distinct.
- Capacity underutilization if the niche is smaller than projected.

The proposal asks to commit to high fixed costs in a contested segment without defining the winning mechanism. It fails the "beat do nothing" test because the "do nothing" option preserves capital while the proposed path compounds risk (product + channel) without a proven differentiator.

DECISION: NO-GO
