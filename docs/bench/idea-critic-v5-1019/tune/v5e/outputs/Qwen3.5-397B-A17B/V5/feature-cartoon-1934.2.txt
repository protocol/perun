First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (growth) but states no edge: it offers no fact showing why a feature film would succeed where shorts currently earn "thin" profits, nor why audiences would pay ten times the implied unit cost for a cartoon when insiders and family advise against it. Need without a stated advantage over the current baseline is a failure as proposed.

Steelman: A feature film could unlock a new revenue tier if audiences accept long-form animation, leveraging existing characters to dominate a vacant market niche.

Attack:
1. **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves steady, albeit thin, profits with zero risk of ruin. The "cheaper alternative" is a series of high-budget shorts to test appetite; this proposal skips validation.
2. **Fit:** The strategy conflicts with the studio's current financial reality ("thin profits"). Funding a 10x budget item from thin margins creates immediate liquidity risk without guaranteed returns.
3. **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: The audience treats it as a novelty and leaves early; the budget overruns by 50% due to unproven production pipelines; the studio exhausts short-film reserves and cannot fund operations.
4. **Evidence:** The proposal admits insiders and family advise against it. There is no data cited showing audience demand for feature-length cartoons. The cost is stated (10x), but the revenue upside is assumed, not calculated.
5. **Blocker Test:**
   - **Blockers:** The proposal explicitly states that current earnings are "thin" yet must fund a 10x cost increase. This is a mathematical contradiction; the funding source is insufficient for the proposed scale without external capital or stopping short production, neither of which is addressed. The unified advice from insiders constitutes a stated negative fact removing the route to success.
   - **Risks:** Audience rejection, budget overrun, opportunity cost of shorts.

The funding mechanism fails on the stated facts: you cannot fund a 10x expansion from "thin" profits without jeopardizing the core business. The lack of a stated edge (why this wins now) combined with the explicit funding contradiction makes this a definitive no-go as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
