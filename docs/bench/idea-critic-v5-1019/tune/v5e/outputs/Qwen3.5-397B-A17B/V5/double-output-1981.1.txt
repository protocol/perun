First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores the fundamental mismatch between supply and collapsing demand; producing more cars without a proven mechanism to sell them accelerates cash burn.

**Steelman:** Doubling production leverages fixed costs to lower unit price, creating a war chest of inventory to capture immediate market share when the economy rebounds, preventing factory shutdown.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cash Flow Contradiction:** The company has "limited cash" and faces "rising interest rates." Doubling production requires immediate upfront capital for parts and labor. With only 3,000 of 7,500 cars sold, working capital is already trapped. Financing more unsold inventory at higher rates will deplete cash reserves faster than any potential future sale can replenish them.
2.  **Channel Blockage:** Dealers "may not be able to finance more stock." If the distribution channel cannot absorb the current 4,500 unsold units due to credit constraints, pushing double the volume creates a logistical bottleneck. Cars produced cannot be moved to dealers, stacking up at the factory and incurring storage costs without generating revenue.
3.  **Demand Ignorance:** The recession is actively hurting demand. Increasing supply in a shrinking market without a specific, funded stimulus plan (e.g., zero-percent financing subsidized by the manufacturer) assumes sales will follow production, which contradicts the stated economic reality.

**Blockers:**
*   **Liquidity Crisis:** The plan requires significant immediate cash outflow in a high-interest, low-cash environment. The math of burning cash to build inventory that cannot be sold or financed by dealers guarantees insolvency before a recovery occurs.
*   **Distribution Failure:** The explicit constraint on dealer financing capacity means the additional production has nowhere to go, turning variable costs into sunk storage costs immediately.

**Risks:**
*   Inventory obsolescence if the recession lasts longer than anticipated.
*   Brand damage from visible overstock and potential factory layoffs later if the gamble fails.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   If dealer floorplan financing capacity does not increase by 50% within 30 days, halt production expansion.
*   If cash reserves drop below 2 months of operating expenses, cease doubling efforts.

The proposal fails because it treats a demand and liquidity crisis as a production volume issue.

DECISION: NO-GO
