First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge beyond low price; "cheapest" is a positioning claim, not a structural advantage against cheaper scooters and used cars, and the risks explicitly note buyers may reject this stigma.

**Steelman:** A dedicated high-volume factory enables a price point low enough to convert scooter families despite safety concerns and land protests.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** Scooters and used cars are explicitly cheaper. The proposal fails to explain why a family would trade the proven utility and lower cost of a used car for a new, stigma-labeled "cheap" car with unproven safety.
2.  **Fit & Risks:** The "cheapest in the world" marketing directly fuels the stated risk that buyers do not want a car known as the cheapest. This is a self-inflicted blocker, not a mere risk. Furthermore, farmer protests threaten the dedicated factory model, jeopardizing the volume targets required to sustain the $2,500 price.
3.  **Evidence Gap:** The proposal asserts high volume targets but cites no evidence of demand at this price point given the availability of superior used alternatives. The cost-cutting necessary to hit $2,500 amplifies the safety doubt, creating a fatal trust gap for families.

**Blockers:**
*   **Stated Negative:** The proposal admits buyers may not want a car known as "the cheapest," yet the entire strategy relies on marketing it as such. This contradicts the route to upside.
*   **Alternative Superiority:** Used cars and scooters are stated as cheaper; the proposal offers no fact showing why the new car beats them beyond novelty, which is insufficient for family transport.

**Risks:** Land protests halting construction; safety failures destroying brand trust; inventory pile-up due to stigma.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders from current scooter owners do not exceed 50% of capacity within six months of announcement, or if land acquisition is not legally resolved within one quarter, the project must pivot to a higher-spec model or halt.

The plan as proposed relies on a marketing hook that alienates the target audience and ignores cheaper, safer alternatives without a stated defensive edge.

DECISION: NO-GO
