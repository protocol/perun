First call: NO-GO. The proposal breaks compatibility with a huge installed base and relies on unproven compiler technology and slow emulation to bridge the gap, offering no stated edge over rivals extending the dominant architecture.

Steelman: A clean-slate 64-bit design optimized for compiler-scheduled parallelism could theoretically outperform incremental extensions of the dominant architecture if compilers mature and emulation overhead is minimized.

Options:
1. **Proposed:** High cost, high risk (compiler difficulty, emulation speed), low reversibility (breaks compatibility).
2. **Do Nothing/Extend:** Low cost, low risk, high reversibility. Rivals extending the dominant architecture preserve the installed base.
3. **Hybrid:** Extend existing architecture to 64-bit while researching new designs. Lower risk, preserves ecosystem.

Fit: The strategy ignores the north star of ecosystem leverage. A new architecture without native software support fails to move the market, regardless of theoretical performance.

Pre-mortem: The project fails because emulation proves too slow for critical applications, compiler development stalls due to complexity, and rivals successfully extend the dominant architecture, locking in the installed base. This is a one-way door with massive sunk costs. The cost of delay is low; waiting allows compiler research to mature or rivals to reveal their extension strategies.

Evidence: All claims regarding risks (slow emulation, hard compilers, huge installed base) are stated in the proposal itself. No evidence suggests these hurdles can be overcome quickly or cheaply. The proposal admits the risks without providing a mitigation plan that outweighs the "do nothing" or "extend" alternatives.

Blockers:
1. **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits the dominant architecture has a huge installed base and rivals may extend it. It offers no fact showing why breaking compatibility beats extending the current standard. Need without an edge is a NO-GO.
2. **Self-Contradictory Viability:** The core mechanism (emulation) is admitted to be potentially slow, and the enabling technology (compilers) is admitted to be hard to write. These are not just risks; they are stated negatives that remove the route to upside.

Risks: Market rejection, developer fatigue, rival dominance.

The proposal fails the blocker test: it does not beat "do nothing" or "extend" on stated facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
