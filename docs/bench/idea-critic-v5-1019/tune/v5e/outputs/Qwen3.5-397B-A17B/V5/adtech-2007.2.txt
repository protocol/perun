First call: NO-GO. The proposal cites a strategic need (countering a rival's move) but offers no stated edge—such as a unique technology advantage, cost synergy, or proven integration path—that justifies a $6 billion cash outlay at a large premium. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Acquiring the platform immediately closes the gap with the search leader by owning essential display infrastructure before the market shifts further.

However, the attack reveals a fatal blocker. The proposal explicitly admits "conflicts with the buyer's existing agency customers." In 2007, the digital ad ecosystem relied on neutral intermediaries; a software company owning both the demand (via its own products) and the supply/intermediary (the acquired agency and platform) creates an unresolvable channel conflict. If existing agency customers flee due to this conflict, the acquired asset's revenue base collapses, destroying the ROI required to justify the $6 billion price. The proposal offers no mitigation for this self-inflicted wound.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retains current agency relationships but cedes display share to the rival. Cost: Opportunity loss. Risk: Competitive lag. Reversibility: High (can buy later).
2. Partner/License: Access technology without ownership or channel conflict. Cost: Lower capital. Risk: Less control. Reversibility: High.
3. The Proposal: Cost: $6B cash + premium. Risk: Channel collapse, tech obsolescence. Reversibility: Low (cash gone, integration hard).

The "do nothing" or "partner" options preserve the core business while testing the display waters. The proposed acquisition bets the entire cash reserve on a conflicted model in a "fast-changing" market. The stated fact that existing customers may conflict is a blocker because it directly undermines the revenue assumptions needed to support the purchase price. Without a concrete plan to resolve the channel conflict *before* closing, the math does not work. The risk of alienating the current base outweighs the speculative gain of catching the rival, especially when the rival's deal is merely an agreement, not a finished integration.

Blockers: Stated channel conflict threatens the acquired asset's revenue base, invalidating the premium valuation.
Risks: Tech obsolescence, integration failure, cash depletion.

DECISION: NO-GO
