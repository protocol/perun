First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state a defensible edge: it offers no fact showing why a retailer with thin margins, no developer platform experience, and heavy existing capital scrutiny can beat established hardware vendors or justify the distraction.

**Steelman:** The retailer leverages its massive, spiky internal infrastructure to achieve utilization rates competitors cannot match, turning a cost center into a profit engine via pay-per-use pricing.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capability Gap:** Running a reliable, multi-tenant developer platform is fundamentally different from running a single-tenant retail backend. The proposal admits zero experience here; reliability expectations for external developers are far higher than internal tolerance.
2.  **Market & Competition:** Incumbents own the developer relationships and trust. The market is "unproven," meaning the retailer would be spending heavily to educate customers while fighting established giants, all while investors are already questioning capital intensity.
3.  **Strategic Distraction:** With thin margins in the core business, diverting engineering focus and capital to an unproven, operationally distinct venture risks diluting the primary revenue source without a guaranteed upside.
4.  **No Stated Edge:** The proposal relies on the existence of spare capacity, not a unique advantage. Spare capacity alone is not a product; without a proven mechanism to sell it against incumbents, it is merely a hope.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capability:** Zero experience in developer platforms vs. high reliability requirements.
*   **Strategy:** Direct conflict with investor sentiment on capital spending and core business focus.
*   **Evidence:** No data showing target users prefer a retailer over established vendors or that the "unproven" market will materialize fast enough to justify the risk.

**Risks:** Margin dilution, brand damage from reliability failures, and catastrophic distraction from the core retail business during a period of thin margins.

The risks are existential to the core business, while the upside is speculative and unsupported by a competitive moat. The absence of a pilot, a specific differentiator, or a path to acquiring developer trust makes this a bet on luck rather than strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
