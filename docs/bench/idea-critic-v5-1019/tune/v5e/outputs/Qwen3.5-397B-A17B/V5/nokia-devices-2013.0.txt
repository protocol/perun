First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits smartphone sales are falling and the rival platform dominates apps; buying a shrinking asset to solve an ecosystem deficit lacks a stated edge where buyers prefer the rival today.

Steelman: Acquiring the handset maker secures the primary hardware outlet for the OS and prevents immediate defection to the rival, buying time to court developers.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Risk the manufacturer switches to the rival, collapsing OS distribution instantly.
2. **Buy division (Proposal):** Costs €5.4B; inherits falling sales and integration chaos; high risk other makers abandon the OS in fear of competition.
3. **Subsidize/Partner:** Pay the manufacturer to stay exclusive without ownership. Cheaper, reversible, avoids integration risk, but offers less control.

Fit: Assuming the north star is OS survival, owning a losing hardware business contradicts the need for broad adoption. If other manufacturers flee due to conflict of interest, the OS dies faster.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition closes, but the inherited division continues losing money. Competing hardware vendors immediately drop the OS to avoid supporting a rival owner. The app gap remains because developers follow the market leader's volume, not this specific hardware. The €5.4B is sunk; the OS becomes a niche player or dies.

Evidence: The proposal itself states sales are falling and the rival has the apps. No evidence suggests hardware ownership attracts developers; history shows developers follow users, not corporate structure.

Blockers:
1. **Negative self-fulfilling prophecy:** The proposal aims to stop the manufacturer switching, but the act of buying them likely triggers other manufacturers to switch (a fact implied by "other handset makers' reaction"). This removes the route to upside (broad OS adoption).
2. **Ecosystem mismatch:** The core problem is apps; the solution is hardware. The proposal offers no mechanism where owning a declining factory fixes the app deficit.

Risks: Integration failure, culture clash, continued financial bleeding.

Kill criteria: If other major handset makers announce platform abandonment within 6 months, or if app growth does not outpace the rival's within 12 months, divest immediately.

The plan fails as proposed because it trades cash for a liability that accelerates the very ecosystem collapse it seeks to prevent.

DECISION: NO-GO
