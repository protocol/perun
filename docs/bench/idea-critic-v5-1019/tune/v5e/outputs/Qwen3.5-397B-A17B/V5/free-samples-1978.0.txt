First call: NO-GO. The initial four-city test demonstrated little demand, and the proposal offers no stated edge—such as a price advantage, unique access, or new evidence—that renaming and sampling would overcome this fundamental market rejection. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Renaming and targeted free sampling in a mid-sized city will validate latent demand that the previous branding and launch strategy obscured, providing a low-cost signal before national investment.

Attack:
1.  **Skeptic:** The proposal assumes "renaming" solves a product-market fit issue without evidence that the name was the failure point. The initial test showed "little demand," suggesting the utility itself, not the label, is the barrier. Free samples to offices assume a use case (office notes) that the market has already implicitly rejected in the first test.
2.  **Better-way:** "Do nothing" or liquidate the inventory is superior to spending on a second guess. A cheaper alternative is a micro-test of the *new name only* via survey or mock-up before incurring manufacturing and distribution costs for physical free samples.
3.  **Fit/Pre-mortem:** If the rollout fails, the company loses the cost of samples plus the opportunity cost of delay. The "disappointing sales" in four cities is a strong leading indicator that office workers do not see a need. Assuming a mid-sized city will react differently without a changed variable (other than a name) is speculative.
4.  **Evidence:** The only hard fact is "disappointing sales" and "little demand." The proposal's condition for rollout ("if most recipients say they would buy it") relies on stated intent from free recipients, which is a notoriously poor predictor of actual purchase behavior, especially for a product already flagged as unwanted.

Blockers: The proposal relies on stated intent from free samples to justify national rollout despite prior evidence of no demand. This contradicts the basic economic principle that revealed preference (actual sales in the first test) outweighs stated preference (survey responses after freebies). The lack of a stated edge to overcome the initial failure makes the plan financially unsound as proposed.

Risks: Wasting budget on samples; false positive from free users; national rollout failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
