First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state an edge: a specific fact showing why this $1,500 device beats existing smartphones or dedicated cameras for everyday users. Need alone is not an edge; without a stated advantage in cost, utility, or access that displaces current solutions, the plan fails as proposed.

Steelman: An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to refine the product with high-tolerance early adopters before a mass-market release, mitigating initial social friction.

Attack: The stated facts contradict the path to mass adoption. The proposal explicitly lists "no clear everyday use for most people" as a known risk. If the core value proposition for the general public is undefined at the time of the elite launch, the product cannot transition from a niche novelty to an "everyday consumer product" as planned. The high price ($1,500) combined with privacy objections and short battery life creates a barrier that the "invitation-only" strategy does not lower; it merely delays the inevitable rejection by the broader market. The alternative of "do nothing" or waiting until a clear everyday use case is proven and battery technology improves offers a safer path with zero reputational risk from a failed public launch.

The proposal admits the target users (the general public) have no clear use for it. This is not a future risk; it is a present fact stated in the proposal that removes the route to the stated upside (becoming an everyday product). A cheaper alternative (waiting for use-case clarity) reaches the same upside (eventual success) with less wasted capital on a premature rollout.

Blockers: The proposal's own evidence states there is "no clear everyday use for most people," which directly contradicts the goal of positioning it as an "everyday consumer product."
Risks: Privacy backlash, social rejection, battery limitations. These are secondary to the primary blocker of undefined utility.

DECISION: NO-GO
