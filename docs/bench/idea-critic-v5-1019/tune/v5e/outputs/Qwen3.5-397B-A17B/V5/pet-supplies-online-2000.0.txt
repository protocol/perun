First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on selling products below cost to acquire customers while simultaneously undertaking massive capital expenditures (TV ads, acquisitions, warehouses) in a sector with inherently low margins and high shipping costs. There is no stated edge where this specific mix beats existing well-funded incumbents also spending heavily; it is a cash-burn race without a defined path to profitability.

Steelman: Aggressively capture market share via below-cost pricing and brand dominance through TV sports ads to achieve monopoly power before capital markets tighten.

Attack: The plan fails on basic arithmetic. Selling below cost (negative gross margin) means every additional customer increases the loss. Funding this with an IPO is precarious given the stated risk that "capital markets could close." If markets close mid-campaign, the company cannot fund the inventory losses, the ad buy, or the competitor acquisition, leading to immediate insolvency. The "growth at all costs" model ignores the unit economics: high shipping costs on heavy items exacerbate the loss per unit. Buying a competitor adds integration risk and debt without fixing the core margin defect.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve cash, fix unit economics, and seek niche profitability.
2. Staged growth: Focus on high-margin products only, using digital ads (cheaper, measurable) instead of national TV.
3. Strategic partnership: Lease warehouse space and delay acquisition until profitability is proven.

Pre-mortem: The IPO launches, but a market shift halts secondary funding. The company is locked into expensive TV contracts and warehouse leases while bleeding cash on every shipment. Competitors with better margins undercut them further. The company runs out of cash within 12 months.

Blockers:
1. Negative gross margins combined with high fixed-cost expansion (TV, warehouses) create an unsolvable cash flow gap if capital markets tighten.
2. No evidence that below-cost pricing yields loyal customers rather than churn once prices rise.

Risks: Capital market closure, competitor retaliation, shipping cost volatility.

Kill criteria: Halt if IPO proceeds do not cover 24 months of projected operating losses at current burn rates; stop ad spend if customer lifetime value does not exceed acquisition cost within 6 months.

DECISION: NO-GO
