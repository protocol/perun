First call: GO. The stated edge is a drastic price reduction enabled by abandoning complex mapping for randomized movement, targeting a market segment priced out by "smarter" rivals.

Steelman: A functional, affordable robot vacuum using randomized navigation can achieve sufficient cleaning satisfaction for most users at a price point mapping competitors cannot match, creating a new mass market despite imperfect coverage.

**Blockers:** NONE.
**Risks:** Random coverage gaps; perception of inferiority; lack of consumer experience; trust barriers.

**Analysis:**
The rejection by two floor-care companies is not a blocker; it validates the "innovator's dilemma." Incumbents prioritize high-margin, feature-rich products for existing channels. They are incentivized to reject a low-margin, disruptive form factor that cannibalizes their premium lines. Their disinterest confirms the path is clear for a direct-to-consumer launch rather than a licensing deal.

The "random vs. mapping" risk is mitigated by the price differential. If the unit costs significantly less, consumers will trade perfect coverage for affordability and automation. The lack of consumer experience is an execution risk, not a strategic blocker; it necessitates a focused go-to-market strategy but does not invalidate the product fit. Trust is built through demonstration and price accessibility, not just brand heritage.

The proposal explicitly addresses the core failure of previous attempts (high cost/complexity) by simplifying the mechanism. The risks listed are inherent to any new category entry and do not constitute facts that prove the plan fails as proposed. The alternative ("do nothing" or "wait for mapping to get cheap") cedes the low-end market to future entrants. The pre-mortem suggests failure comes from distribution errors, not the randomized algorithm itself.

Since no factual contradiction exists within the proposal's own logic, and the incumbent rejection actually supports the disruptive thesis, the plan survives the attack. The risks become kill criteria for the launch phase (e.g., return rates due to cleaning performance), but they do not stop the initial go decision.

DECISION: GO
