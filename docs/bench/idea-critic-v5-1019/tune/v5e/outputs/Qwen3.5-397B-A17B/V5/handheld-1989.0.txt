First call: GO. The stated edge is a drastic price advantage ($89.95 vs. competitors' likely $150+) and superior battery life, targeting a mass market rather than early adopters who prioritize graphics. The bundle with a puzzle game addresses the "single-game" stigma by offering high-replay value.

Steelman: This device wins by being affordable, portable, and fun, ignoring the spec war to create a new category of "pocket gaming" for everyone, not just enthusiasts.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Cede the handheld market to the rival; risk long-term brand irrelevance in portable play.
2. **High-end color handheld:** Match the rival's specs. Result: Higher cost ($150+), poor battery life, direct head-to-head competition where the rival has momentum. High risk of failure.
3. **Proposed monochrome device:** Low cost, long battery, mass appeal. Beats "do nothing" by capturing a new demographic; beats the high-end option on margin and adoption speed.

Fit: Aligns with a strategy of market expansion and hardware ubiquity. Assumes the north star is "games for everyone," not "best technology."

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if third-party developers ignore the platform due to perceived weakness, or if the monochrome screen is rejected by consumers conditioned to expect color. However, the low price creates a two-way door; if it fails, the financial loss is capped compared to a high-end flop. The cost of delay is losing the holiday season and allowing the rival to solidify a monopoly.

Evidence: The proposal's own figures show a clear path to profitability via volume. The $20M marketing spend is significant but justified by the low unit cost. The "thin margins" risk is mitigated by the hardware being cheaper to manufacture than color rivals.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (monochrome, modest specs) are features of the cost strategy, not contradictions. The "single-game" history is addressed by the puzzle bundle and the promise of a cartridge ecosystem (implied by the form factor).

Risks: Developer adoption, consumer perception of "cheap." These become kill criteria: if software library does not reach 10 titles within 6 months, or if sell-through is <50% of inventory by Q2, pivot or discontinue.

DECISION: GO
