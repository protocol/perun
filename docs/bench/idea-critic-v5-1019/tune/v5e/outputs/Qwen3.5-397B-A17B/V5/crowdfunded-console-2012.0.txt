First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but states no edge; "low-cost" and "open" are not advantages against incumbents who already subsidize hardware and possess massive libraries.

Steelman: A niche, developer-friendly console can succeed by targeting underserved homebrew creators and retro enthusiasts willing to trade polish for openness, bypassing traditional retail.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Zero risk, zero reward.
2. **Software-first:** Build the OS/emulator for existing PCs/phones to validate the ecosystem before hardware commitment. Lower cost, higher reversibility.
3. **Proposal:** High capital risk, irreversible hardware tooling, dependent on unproven developer adoption.

Fit: Assumes "open platform" attracts developers, but history shows developers follow users, not openness. Without a stated exclusive hook (e.g., unique hardware feature or existing community), this fails to move the needle against established ecosystems.

Pre-mortem: The campaign funds initial tooling, but supply chain delays drain reserves. The device ships late with a bare library. Developers abandon the platform because the user base is too small to justify porting costs. Backers demand refunds; reputation is destroyed. This is a one-way door for capital; once spent on molds and chips, it cannot be recovered. Cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of failure.

Evidence: The proposal admits "no marketing budget" and "hardware delays are common." In 2012, mobile gaming was exploding, and consoles required massive exclusives. The stated facts contradict the core claim that a low-budget team can compete on hardware alone. The risk of "developers may not build games" is treated as a possibility, but given the lack of budget and installed base, it is a near-certainty based on the proposal's own constraints.

Blockers:
1. **No stated edge:** "Low-cost" cannot beat subsidized incumbents; "open" does not solve the chicken-and-egg developer problem without an existing user base.
2. **Resource mismatch:** The plan requires hardware execution and marketing reach the team explicitly lacks ("no marketing budget," "hardware delays common").

Risks: Supply chain failure, developer apathy, rapid mobile market shifts. These are confirmed by the proposal's own admissions.

Kill criteria check: The proposal lists risks but offers no mitigation or kill switch. The stated facts (no budget, high competition, inexperience) directly point toward the failure modes listed.

DECISION: NO-GO
