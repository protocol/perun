First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high cost and no apps offer no advantage over rivals with large catalogs.

**Steelman:** Leveraging acquired brand loyalty to lock teens into a closed ecosystem via a single carrier, betting social features outweigh app absence.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Edge:** Teens are price-sensitive. A data plan costing as much as a full smartphone plan removes the primary adoption lever. Rivals offer mature app stores; this offers none. "Social networking" is a feature, not a platform substitute in 2010. The "new OS" fragments engineering without proven benefit over the company's existing mobile platform.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A cheaper alternative is porting the messaging experience to existing platforms (iOS/Android) or the company's current OS, avoiding the sunk cost fallacy of the new OS.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Launch fails because teens cannot afford the plan and refuse a phone without apps. The carrier exclusivity limits reach just as social virality requires maximum distribution.
4.  **Evidence Check:** The proposal admits rivals have large catalogs and the price point is high for the target demographic. These are stated facts, not risks. The "two years sunk" is a past cost, not a future benefit.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price vs. Demographic:** The stated cost equals a full smartphone plan, which the proposal explicitly notes is "high for teenagers." This directly contradicts the target market's ability to pay.
*   **Value Deficit:** No app store at launch when rivals have large catalogs creates an immediate functional gap with no compensating stated edge (e.g., significantly lower hardware cost or exclusive content).
*   **Platform Fragmentation:** Using a new OS instead of the company's own proven platform adds execution risk without a stated strategic necessity.

**Risks:** Carrier dependency, slow app ecosystem growth. These are secondary to the immediate blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** If the target user (teen) cannot afford the monthly entry price, the product cannot gain traction. The proposal's own facts confirm this mismatch. The sunk cost of development is irrelevant to future viability; it is a loss to be cut, not a reason to proceed.

DECISION: NO-GO
