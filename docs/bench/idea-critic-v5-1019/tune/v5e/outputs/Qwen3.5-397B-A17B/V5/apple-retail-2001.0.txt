First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; needing demonstration is a product deficit, not a retail advantage, and low margins with high leases worsen existing losses.

**Steelman:** Direct stores are the only viable channel to demonstrate the integrated music strategy and stop third-party staff from pushing competitors, potentially reversing brand erosion.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** The "better way" is a dedicated demo zone within existing retailer footprints or a traveling roadshow. These achieve the "demonstration" goal without the catastrophic fixed cost of prime mall leases during a recession. The proposal fails to prove these cheaper options cannot work.
2.  **Fit & Pre-mortem:** A company with 3–5% share and recent losses cannot absorb the cash burn of a retail rollout. If the recession deepens (highly probable), fixed leases become anchors while sales volume remains too low to cover overhead. The pre-mortem shows bankruptcy accelerated by real estate liability, not solved by it.
3.  **Evidence Gap:** The claim that third-party staff *must* be replaced rather than incentivized or bypassed via in-store branding is assumed, not proven. The "poor retail track record in the industry" is a stated fact that acts as a blocker; entering a failing model with weaker finances is irrational.
4.  **Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal offers no cap, pilot, or metric. It commits to a full chain immediately. There is no "stop" condition if the first store loses money, which is the baseline expectation given the sector's margins.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Mismatch:** Recent losses + low margins + high fixed lease costs = immediate cash flow crisis.
*   **Industry Reality:** Stated "poor retail track record" combined with "recession" creates a near-certain failure mode for a low-share player.
*   **Missing Edge:** Demonstration does not require ownership; the proposal proves no unique advantage to owning the lease versus controlling the demo experience within existing channels.

**Risks:** Channel conflict with remaining resellers, inventory obsolescence, brand damage from empty stores.

The plan fails the operator test: it bets the company's remaining liquidity on a high-fixed-cost channel in a downturn without a pilot or a proven unique advantage over cheaper alternatives.

DECISION: NO-GO
