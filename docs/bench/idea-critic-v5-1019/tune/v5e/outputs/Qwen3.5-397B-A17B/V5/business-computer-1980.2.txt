First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (business features) but fails to state an edge: why this specific maker, with a single consumer product and unproven hardware reliability, can beat established business competitors or retain its own base during a complex transition.

**Steelman:** A feature-complete successor is the only path to capture the lucrative business market before competitors lock it out, leveraging the brand's existing momentum.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Strategy:** Phasing out the only successful product before the successor is proven violates basic portfolio hygiene. If the new design fails (high complexity, tight schedule), the company has zero revenue stream.
2.  **Evidence Gap:** The proposal admits hardware reliability is "unproven." Launching a complex, unreliable machine to skeptical business users (who value stability over novelty) is a direct path to brand destruction.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or "add features to current line" preserves cash flow while testing the waters. The proposal rejects protecting the current revenue engine without proving the new one works.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The launch slips due to complexity; the machine ships with bugs; business users reject it; the original user base flees because support shifts to the failed new model. Company collapses.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Reliability:** Business users will not adopt unreliable hardware; the proposal offers no mitigation other than "launch within the year," which increases risk.
*   **Cannibalization without Safety:** Phasing out the *only* successful product before the successor proves market fit creates a single point of failure for the entire company's revenue.

**Risks:** Schedule slippage, software ecosystem fragmentation, alienation of existing users. These are severe but secondary to the structural blocker of killing the cash cow prematurely.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal lacks a pilot, a reliability metric, or a phased rollout. It demands an all-in bet on an unproven, complex design while dismantling the only asset that works. The absence of a "go-if" condition (e.g., "go only if beta reliability hits X%") makes this a reckless gamble rather than a strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
