First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high license cost and compressed timeline create negative unit economics without evidence of guaranteed demand.

**Steelman:** A single developer can deliver a commercially viable product in five weeks that justifies a massive inventory run before the holiday window closes.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capacity vs. Scope:** Five weeks is insufficient for a "hit film" adaptation, which implies complex assets and mechanics. A single programmer cannot match audience expectations, guaranteeing poor quality.
2.  **Inventory Risk:** Ordering a "very large production run" based on an unfinished, low-quality product exposes the company to catastrophic inventory write-downs. The high license fee increases the break-even point, making a sales miss fatal.
3.  **Missing Edge:** The proposal relies entirely on the film's existing popularity but offers no mechanism (e.g., unique gameplay, marketing lock-in) to convert that interest into sales despite the likely poor execution. Need is not an edge.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids inventory loss and protects brand reputation; forfeits holiday revenue.
*   **Staged Run:** Produce a minimal batch to test market reception before committing to a large run. This reduces downside risk while capturing some upside.
*   **Delay:** Push release to ensure quality, missing Christmas but potentially selling better long-term.

**Pre-mortem:** The game releases in December with bugs and shallow gameplay. Critics pan it, consumers return it, and the warehouse is full of unsold cartridges. The high license fee ensures the project loses money despite the film's fame.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Five weeks is objectively insufficient for high-quality development of a major IP title by one person.
*   **Fact:** Large production runs require confirmed demand; none exists for an unproven, rushed product.
*   **Fact:** The proposal admits high license fees and short timelines, yet claims these will result in success without a stated differentiator.

**Risks:** Poor quality, unsold inventory, brand damage. These are not mere risks here; the proposal's own constraints (time, staff) make the negative outcome the baseline expectation.

**Kill Criteria:** If a playable demo is not ready and tested within two weeks, cancel the large run.

The plan fails as proposed because the constraints guarantee the failure mode (poor quality + high inventory) without a counterbalancing advantage.

DECISION: NO-GO
