First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a need (slowing growth) and a positive test signal, but fails to state a defensible edge against the specific risks of flavor-color association and cannibalization that would guarantee nationwide success over the status quo.

**Steelman:** A clear cola leverages "purity" trends to capture novelty seekers and health-conscious drinkers without alienating core fans, creating a net-new revenue stream that outweighs internal cannibalization.

**Attack:**
1.  **Flavor-Color Link:** The core risk is physiological/psychological: consumers equate dark color with cola taste. If the clear liquid tastes like "diet" or "weak" cola due to this bias, repeat purchase (the true driver of CPG success) will collapse after the novelty wears off. The proposal admits this link exists but offers no mitigation strategy other than marketing spend.
2.  **Cannibalization vs. Expansion:** With slowing category growth, any volume gain is likely zero-sum. Without evidence that clear cola draws users from *other* categories (water, tea) rather than the flagship, the net impact is negative margin (new SKUs + marketing cost) for flat revenue.
3.  **Test Market Validity:** Test markets often over-index on novelty. The "positive response" may be purely trial-based. The proposal lacks a kill criterion for repeat rates before committing to a nationwide launch.
4.  **Competitor Response:** A nationwide launch signals the category opportunity to rivals immediately. If the concept is easily copied (likely, as it's a formulation/color change), first-mover advantage is minimal without a patented moat, which is not stated.

**Blockers:**
*   **Missing Edge:** No stated fact proves the product overcomes the flavor-color bias or achieves net-new volume rather than cannibalization.
*   **Unit Economics:** The plan commits to a "large marketing campaign" without a verified projection that Lifetime Value (LTV) of a repeat buyer exceeds the Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) in a non-growing category.

**Risks:** High probability of flagship erosion; novelty cliff after 3 months; rapid competitive imitation.

**Conclusion:** The proposal relies on hope that "purer" positioning overrides deep-seated sensory expectations. Without a pilot proving *repeat* purchase rates distinct from *trial* rates, and without a model showing net-positive category expansion, the nationwide bet is unjustified.

DECISION: NO-GO
