First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price and slow performance are disadvantages, not advantages, against established competitors.

**Steelman:** This machine offers a revolutionary graphical interface that simplifies computing for non-technical office workers, justifying a premium despite lower raw speed.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $10,000, the unit costs several times more than leading business PCs. Without a massive productivity multiplier proven by third-party software (which is currently absent), the ROI is negative. Office managers will reject a slower, expensive machine when faster, cheaper alternatives exist.
2.  **Software Void:** Business utility depends on applications (spreadsheets, word processing). "Little third-party software" means the hardware is a solution without a problem. Users cannot adopt a platform that lacks tools for their daily work.
3.  **Internal Cannibalization:** A cheaper machine with a similar interface is already in development internally. Launching the expensive version first alienates early adopters who will soon see a better deal, damaging brand trust and inventory value.
4.  **Performance Deficit:** "Runs slowly" is a fatal flaw for business workflows where time is money. A graphical interface that lags frustrates users rather than empowering them.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for the internal cheaper model or for third-party software to mature.
*   **Pivot:** Target a niche willing to pay for novelty (e.g., design studios) rather than general office workers, but only if performance improves.
*   **Delay:** Launch only when the software ecosystem and price point align with business realities.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price:** $10,000 exceeds market tolerance for unproven tech with no software support.
*   **Performance:** Slower speed than competitors removes the primary business incentive to upgrade.
*   **Ecosystem:** Lack of software renders the machine useless for most office tasks.

**Risks:** Market rejection, inventory write-downs, brand damage from internal cannibalization.

The proposal fails the basic test of business logic: it sells a slower, more expensive product with no supporting ecosystem while a better internal alternative looms. There is no stated edge to overcome these deficits.

DECISION: NO-GO
