First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (mobile video) but fails to state an edge: it offers premium short-form content in a market where free, abundant short-form already exists, without explaining why users would pay a subscription fee amidst rising "subscription fatigue."

**Steelman:** A high-fidelity, orientation-agnostic platform leveraging A-list talent could capture high-value users underserved by chaotic, free user-generated content.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or leveraging existing platforms (YouTube, Instagram) for premium shorts is significantly cheaper and reversible. Building a dedicated tech stack and raising $1B+ creates massive fixed costs before validating demand.
2.  **Fit:** The strategy conflicts with the stated market reality of "subscription fatigue." High production budgets for sub-10-minute episodes likely yield poor ROI compared to long-form or low-cost viral content.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: Users refuse to add another subscription for content format they already consume for free; high burn rate from A-list talent contracts drains capital before a subscriber base forms.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no proven demand" and cites "price" and "competition" as risks. These are not just risks; they are blockers based on the stated facts. The "vertical/horizontal" tech feature is a commodity, not a differentiator sufficient to overcome payment friction.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal offers no fact demonstrating why this specific paid model beats free alternatives or existing subscriptions. Need alone is not an edge.
*   **Contradictory Economics:** High production costs + subscription fatigue + unproven demand = negative unit economics as proposed.
*   **Superior Alternative:** A cheaper, staged pilot (e.g., licensing content to an existing app) reaches the same upside (testing demand) with far less capital and risk.

**Risks:** Execution difficulty, talent retention, market timing. These become kill criteria if the blockers were resolved, but the blockers themselves prevent approval.

The plan commits real money without a validated route to revenue. The absence of a pilot or cap is a critical flaw given the unproven nature of the core hypothesis.

DECISION: NO-GO
