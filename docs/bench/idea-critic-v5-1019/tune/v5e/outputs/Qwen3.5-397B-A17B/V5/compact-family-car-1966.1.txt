First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a real need but offers no stated edge: it does not explain why an 1100 cc car sold through a costly new dedicated network beats existing low-price rivals or meets the specific preference shift toward cheaper or larger vehicles.

Steelman: A dedicated 1100 cc model leverages the new plant's capacity to capture a specific family segment that current low-price rivals ignore, justifying the dealer network cost through higher volume and brand focus.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** High capex (new dealers), high risk (plant utilization, price war), low reversibility.
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero new cost, maintains status quo, risks plant underutilization.
3. **Shared Network:** Sell the 1100 cc through existing channels or partners; lower cost, faster launch, tests demand before committing to dedicated infrastructure.

Fit: Assuming the goal is plant utilization and market expansion, building a parallel sales structure contradicts efficiency unless the product is radically distinct, which is not stated.

Pre-mortem: The plant runs below capacity because the dedicated network takes too long to build; meanwhile, rivals drop prices further, making the new car unprofitable. Buyers choose larger used cars or cheaper competitors. This is a one-way door with high delay cost.

Evidence: The proposal admits rivals already sell small cars at low prices and that buyers may prefer cheaper or larger options. It offers no data showing a gap in the 1100 cc family segment large enough to support a dedicated network. The cost of the dealer network is acknowledged as a risk without a mitigation plan.

Blockers:
1. **No Stated Edge:** The plan fails to demonstrate how it wins against established low-price rivals or addresses the stated buyer preference for cheaper/larger cars.
2. **Economic Contradiction:** Building a costly dedicated network increases the break-even point, directly conflicting with the need to compete on price against existing rivals.

Risks: Plant underutilization, price war, misaligned product size.

Kill Criteria: If a shared-network pilot cannot secure 20% of target plant capacity within 6 months, stop the dedicated network build.

The proposal asks for a massive, irreversible investment (dedicated network) without proving the product can survive the price competition it explicitly acknowledges. The lack of a stated advantage over existing options is a fatal flaw in the current form.

DECISION: NO-GO
