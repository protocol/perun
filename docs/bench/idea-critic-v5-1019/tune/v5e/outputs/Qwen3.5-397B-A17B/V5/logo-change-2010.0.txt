First call: NO-GO. The proposal offers no stated edge over the current asset; it relies on an untested aesthetic shift while actively discarding the brand's primary recognition marker (the dark blue square) without evidence that the new design drives contemporary perception better than the old one.

**Steelman:** A simultaneous, unannounced swap to a minimalist wordmark instantly signals a modern era, avoiding the "dated" look of the enclosed square before competitors do.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" retains 100% recognition. A "staged pilot" (e.g., online only) tests the "contemporary" claim with near-zero reputational risk. The proposal rejects these for a "big bang" approach that maximizes downside.
2.  **Fit:** If the goal is to look contemporary, alienating the loyal base that funds the transition is counter-strategic. Contemporary brands iterate; they do not vanish and reappear overnight.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Loyalists perceive the removal of the blue square as erasing heritage, not updating it. Social media amplifies this as "corporate soullessness." Sales dip due to confusion; the brand looks desperate, not modern.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits zero customer testing. The "risk" of backlash is treated as a possibility, but the *removal* of the recognized asset is a certainty. Without data showing the new mark tests better, the move is a gamble, not a strategy.
5.  **Blocker:** The core claim (looking contemporary) contradicts the method (hiding the change). A rebrand requires narrative; an unannounced swap creates confusion. The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" or "staged test" on risk/reversibility. The lack of testing on the specific fear (loss of recognition) is a fatal gap when the solution removes the very thing being recognized.

**Blockers:**
*   No evidence the new design achieves the goal better than the old; removing the known identifier (blue square) without validation guarantees recognition loss.
*   "No advance announcement" ensures the narrative is controlled by critics, not the brand.

**Risks:** Backlash, sales drop, brand confusion (all elevated to blockers due to lack of testing).

DECISION: NO-GO
