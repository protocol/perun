First call: NO-GO. An unprofitable retailer inviting competitors to undercut its own prices on the same page, without a stated mechanism to protect margins or brand trust, lacks a competitive edge; it accelerates losses rather than solving the profitability gap.

**Steelman:** Transform the site into a liquid marketplace where inventory depth drives customer retention, monetizing traffic via fees when direct sales margin is thin.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economic Suicide:** The proposal admits sellers will undercut prices. For an unprofitable entity, losing the sale *and* the margin to a competitor while bearing the platform cost is a negative-sum game. There is no "edge" stated where the fee compensates for the lost gross profit on displaced own-inventory sales.
2.  **Brand Cannibalization:** Used copies directly cannibalize new sales. Without a stated strategy to segment these markets or protect supplier relationships, this alienates the very publishers required for core inventory.
3.  **Trust Liability:** The proposal acknowledges less control over service quality. For a retailer struggling to prove its model, introducing variable third-party failure rates risks the remaining customer trust. A "fee per sale" does not mitigate the reputational damage of a bad third-party transaction.
4.  **Missing Edge:** The proposal relies on "growth" and "liquidity" as abstract benefits. It fails to state a specific fact showing why *this* unprofitable retailer can attract better sellers than existing channels or why customers prefer this mixed environment over competitors. Need without a distinct advantage is a failure mode.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Contradiction:** The core mechanism (competitors undercutting prices) directly worsens the stated problem (unprofitability) by displacing own-inventory sales with lower-margin fee income, with no math showing net positive contribution.
*   **Supplier Revolt:** The plan invites conflict with publishers/suppliers who may view used competition on their own product pages as hostile, risking supply chain disruption for an unprofitable company.

**Risks:** Customer confusion, quality control failures, and accelerated cash burn. These are secondary to the fundamental economic flaw.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot shows that the margin gained from fees on third-party sales is less than the gross profit lost on displaced own-inventory sales within 90 days, the program must terminate.

The proposal asks to compound uncertainty with direct competition on the primary asset (the product page) without a defensive moat.

DECISION: NO-GO
