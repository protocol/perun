First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge proving this plan beats existing low-cost options for non-gamers; "casual audience" is a need, not a competitive advantage against established PC or handheld markets.

Steelman: A motion-controlled, low-cost console can unlock a massive blue-ocean market of non-gamers by prioritizing intuitive interaction over graphical fidelity, creating a unique value proposition distinct from the hardcore race.

**Blockers:**
NONE. The risks cited (skepticism, toy perception, untested audience) are uncertainties, not proven facts contradicting the core claim. The thin margin is a strategic choice for volume, not a mathematical impossibility based on the text.

**Risks:**
1.  **Third-party support:** Hardcore publishers may ignore the platform, leaving a content gap.
2.  **Market perception:** Being labeled a "toy" could limit long-term software attachment rates.
3.  **Untested demand:** The casual audience may not convert to hardware buyers despite low price.
4.  **Technical limitation:** Lack of HD output may date the console quickly as TV standards shift.

**Kill Criteria & Strategy:**
Since no factual blocker exists, the decision rests on accepting the risk of an unproven market. The proposal implicitly bets on a two-way door: if the casual audience does not respond, the low hardware cost limits downside exposure compared to a high-end failure. The "edge" is the differentiation itself; no other console is targeting non-gamers with motion control at this price point. The skepticism of hardcore gamers is irrelevant if the target demographic is truly distinct.

The pre-mortem suggests failure occurs if the motion controller is seen as a gimmick rather than a genuine interface improvement. However, the proposal addresses this by launching with simple, family-friendly games designed specifically for the hardware, mitigating the "toy" risk through software curation.

The cost of delay is high; competitors are pouring resources into the graphics race. Waiting for HD adoption or third-party validation cedes the casual segment entirely. The only path to validate the "untested audience" is a market launch.

DECISION: GO
