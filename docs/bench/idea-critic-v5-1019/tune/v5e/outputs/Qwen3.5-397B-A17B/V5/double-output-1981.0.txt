First call: NO-GO. Doubling production while sales lag by 60% during a recession with rising interest rates destroys cash without a stated edge.

**Steelman:** The claim implies that flooding the market now secures future dominance or forces dealer acceptance before competitors react, betting that demand will rebound sharply enough to absorb the massive inventory buildup.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cash Flow vs. Demand:** With 4,500 unsold units (7,500 produced - 3,000 sold), inventory turnover is critically low. Doubling output adds variable costs (materials, labor) immediately while revenue remains stalled by the recession. High interest rates make financing this expanded inventory prohibitively expensive for both the manufacturer and dealers.
2.  **Dealer Capacity:** Dealers cannot finance more stock in a high-rate environment. Forcing inventory onto them risks bankrupting the distribution network, killing the only route to market.
3.  **Missing Edge:** The proposal offers no reason why producing more unsold cars beats "doing nothing" or cutting losses. There is no cost advantage, no locked-in demand, and no product superiority cited that would clear the backlog once the economy recovers. Need alone is not an edge.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Sales (3,000) are less than half of production (7,500).
*   **Fact:** Recession and rising rates suppress demand and increase capital costs.
*   **Contradiction:** The plan increases cash burn exactly when liquidity is most constrained and demand is weakest. The math guarantees insolvency before any hypothetical recovery.

**Risks:**
*   Dealer network collapse due to forced inventory financing.
*   Total cash exhaustion prior to economic turnaround.
*   Asset devaluation of unsold stock sitting in lots.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   If monthly sales do not exceed monthly production within one quarter, stop all expansion.
*   If cash reserves drop below 3 months of operating expenses at current burn, halt production increases.

The proposal fails the basic operator test: it accelerates cash outflow while the inflow mechanism (sales) is broken by macroeconomic forces. It lacks a mechanism to convert extra units into cash.

DECISION: NO-GO
