First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (direct distribution) but fails to state an edge: pricing below a incumbent with 100M+ subscribers while burning cash and sacrificing guaranteed licensing revenue offers no defensible advantage in content depth, technology, or margins.

**Steelman:** Sacrificing immediate, high-margin licensing revenue to build a scaled direct-to-consumer platform is necessary to survive industry shifts, using price to accelerate adoption despite short-term losses.

**Attack:**
1.  **Options:** "Do nothing" (continue licensing) guarantees billions in low-risk cash flow. The proposed path trades certain revenue for speculative subscriber growth in a crowded market. A cheaper alternative exists: license selectively while building tech incrementally, avoiding a "big bang" withdrawal from rivals.
2.  **Fit:** While strategic alignment with direct-to-consumer trends is clear, the execution method (immediate total withdrawal) contradicts financial stability required for a multi-year loss.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The venture fails because the tech platform crashes under load (no prior experience), the cash burn exceeds projections before reaching critical mass, and rivals simply outspend on content. The "low price" triggers a price war the conglomerate cannot win without destroying its theatrical/cable base.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits zero experience running a tech platform and relies on unproven subscriber targets (tens of millions) against an entrenched leader (100M+). The math of replacing billions in licensing fees with low-price subscriptions requires unrealistic penetration rates to break even.
5.  **Blockers:** The core claim fails on its own stated facts. The plan requires funding a "multi-year operating loss" while simultaneously losing "billions" in licensing revenue. Without a stated source of capital to cover this double-gap (lost revenue + new burn), the plan is mathematically insolvent as proposed. The lack of a tech track record is a secondary blocker for a service requiring flawless execution to compete.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capital Gap:** No funding source identified to replace billions in lost licensing revenue *plus* fund multi-year operating losses.
*   **Capability Void:** Zero prior experience running the required technology platform creates an unmitigated execution risk that threatens the entire value proposition.

**Risks:** Cannibalization of existing profitable units; price wars; failure to hit subscriber targets.

DECISION: NO-GO
