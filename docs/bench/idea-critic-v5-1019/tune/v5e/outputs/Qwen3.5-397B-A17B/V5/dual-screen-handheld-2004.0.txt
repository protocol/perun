First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge proving this hardware configuration beats the rival's superior graphics and media features for the target broad audience; "lower price" is a constraint, not a functional advantage that offsets the developer confusion and unproven touch mechanics cited in the risks.

Steelman: Differentiate via novel interaction (dual-screen/touch) to unlock non-traditional gameplay at a lower price point, leveraging legacy hardware to maintain core revenue while expanding the market.

Attack: The proposal admits the hardware is "unusual" and may "confuse developers," yet offers no evidence of existing developer buy-in or a simplified SDK to mitigate this. If developers cannot easily create software, the device has no content, rendering the screen innovation useless. The risk that "touch-screen games are unproven" is treated as a possibility rather than a fatal flaw; without a killer app demonstrating the value of touch, the second screen is merely a cost burden ("two screens add cost") that competitors will mock as a "gimmick." The plan to keep the older handheld on sale dilutes marketing focus and does not solve the new device's identity crisis.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Maintain dominance with the existing library and brand loyalty; avoid the cost of educating developers on unproven tech.
2. Match rival: Upgrade graphics to prevent brand erosion among core gamers, accepting a higher price point.
3. Proposed: High risk of fragmenting the developer base and confusing consumers without a proven software hook.

Pre-mortem: The device launches, developers struggle with the dual-screen paradigm, resulting in a dry launch library. Consumers perceive the touch screen as a toy, core gamers dismiss it for weak graphics, and the "gimmick" label sticks, forcing a price cut that destroys margins.

Blockers: The proposal explicitly states the hardware may confuse developers and the core mechanic (touch) is unproven, yet provides no strategy to validate demand or secure software support before launch. A plan relying on unproven technology without a validation step fails the evidence test.

Risks: Market rejection, developer abandonment, margin compression.

DECISION: NO-GO
