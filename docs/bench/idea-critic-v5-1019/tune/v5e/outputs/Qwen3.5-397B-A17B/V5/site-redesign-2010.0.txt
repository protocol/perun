First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "slowing traffic" is a problem, not an advantage over incumbents, and favoring publishers alienates the core community that drives the site's value without evidence they will accept it.

Steelman: A complete rewrite with a publisher-focused algorithm and immediate cutover is the only way to reverse traffic decline and monetize via publishers before a rival captures the market.

Attack:
1. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves the loyal base while testing small changes. A "staged rollout" allows rollback if the new mechanics fail, directly mitigating the stated risk of "no rollback." The proposed "big bang" approach fails the reversibility test against a safer alternative that reaches the same upside (traffic recovery) with less existential risk.
2. **Fit:** The core community values existing voting mechanics. Changing these to favor publishers contradicts the stated user value proposition, likely accelerating exodus to the growing rival rather than stopping it.
3. **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: The rewrite takes too long, the new algorithm depresses engagement from loyal users, and the single-point launch reveals critical bugs with no fallback, causing immediate mass migration to the rival.
4. **Evidence:** No data supports that "favoring publishers" increases total traffic or that a rewrite is technically necessary versus iterative updates. The risk of a "large engineering risk" is admitted but not mitigated.

Blockers:
- The plan explicitly removes rollback capability ("move all users at once") while admitting "no rollback" is a major risk. This is a self-contradictory failure condition.
- The strategy antagonizes the stated "loyal community" (the current asset) to chase an unproven publisher benefit, lacking evidence this trade-off yields net growth.

Risks:
- Engineering delays in a full rewrite.
- Community revolt due to changed voting mechanics.
- Rival captures users during the transition.

The proposal fails because it chooses the highest-risk execution method (big bang) for a strategy that undermines its own base, with no stated edge to justify bypassing safer, reversible alternatives.

DECISION: NO-GO
