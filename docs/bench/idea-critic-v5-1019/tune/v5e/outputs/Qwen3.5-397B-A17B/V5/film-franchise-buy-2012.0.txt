First call: NO-GO. The proposal cites mixed recent reviews, the loss of the creative lead, and a price tag ($4B) that dwarfs current film output without demonstrating a specific advantage to overcome these stated negatives. Need alone is not an edge.

**Steelman:** Acquiring this franchise provides unique, owned IP capable of saturating parks, products, and screens, creating a synergistic revenue engine that justifies the premium despite recent creative instability.

**Attack:**
1.  **Creative Void:** The founder's retirement is a stated fact, not a risk. Without the primary architect, the probability of fan rejection increases significantly, directly threatening the trilogy's box office and the downstream park/product viability. The proposal offers no successor strategy.
2.  **Financial Overreach:** At $4B, the cost exceeds the company's current entire film output value. Paying a premium for a "mixed review" asset with a departing leader contradicts basic capital allocation logic.
3.  **Licensed Constraints:** With much merchandise value already licensed out, the immediate synergy upside is capped, delaying ROI while the acquirer waits for contracts to expire or pays to break them.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves $4B for internal development or acquiring a franchise with an active creative lead and stronger recent momentum. A cheaper, staged partnership to test new content before a full buyout reaches similar upside with less downside.

**Blockers:**
*   **Creative Blocker:** The stated retirement of the founder removes the core value driver (creative vision) required to fix the stated "mixed reviews" and prevent "fan rejection." The plan has no answer for this.
*   **Financial Blocker:** The price ($4B) is unsupported by the asset's current performance (mixed reviews) and future stability (no lead).

**Risks:** Fan rejection, integration failure, opportunity cost of capital. These are secondary to the blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** If a successor creative team is not identified and contracted before signing, or if a pre-acquisition audit shows licensed merchandise cannot be reclaimed within 3 years, the deal fails.

The proposal asks to bet the studio's annual output value on a fading asset with no captain.

DECISION: NO-GO
