First call: GO. The stated edge is clear: a garage-fit van with car-like handling built on an existing compact platform offers a unique value proposition (convenience + familiarity) that current full-size vans and station wagons cannot match, specifically targeting families who need space but reject commercial aesthetics.

**Steelman:** Launching a front-wheel-drive, garage-compatible van on a proven compact platform leverages existing assets to create a new category, minimizing capital risk while maximizing utility for families.

**Attack & Analysis:**
*   **Category Risk:** The absence of a category is an opportunity, not a blocker. Station wagons are too small; full-size vans are too bulky. This fills the gap.
*   **Financial Constraint:** The company has little cash. However, reusing the compact-car platform and converting an existing factory significantly lowers development and tooling costs compared to a clean-sheet design. This aligns with the financial reality.
*   **Perception Risk:** Buyers fearing a "commercial" look is a valid concern, but the proposal explicitly targets the "family" segment with car-like driving dynamics, directly countering this perception. Marketing must emphasize "family vehicle," not "work van."
*   **Competition:** Rivals can copy, but first-mover advantage in a new segment defined by garage-fit convenience creates a defensible niche before clones arrive.
*   **Space Limitation:** Reusing a compact platform limits absolute volume, but the "garage-fit" requirement necessitates this trade-off. The benefit (fitting at home) outweighs the cost (slightly less cargo than a full-size van).

**Blockers:** None. The financial constraint is mitigated by the reuse strategy. The market gap is evident.
**Risks:** Brand perception (mitigated by positioning), competitor response (mitigated by speed).

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders or early clinic testing show >40% of target families perceive it as a "commercial van" rather than a "family car," pivot marketing or halt. If the converted factory cannot meet quality standards within 10% of the compact car's baseline, stop.

The proposal addresses the core constraints (cash, factory) while exploiting a clear market void. The risks are manageable and do not invalidate the core logic.

DECISION: GO
