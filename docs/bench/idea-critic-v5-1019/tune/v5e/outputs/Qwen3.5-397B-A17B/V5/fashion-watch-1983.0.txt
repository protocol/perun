First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a high-volume target for a low-cost plastic product but offers no stated edge explaining why this specific maker, known for quality movements, would win against established fashion brands or why customers would trust their plastic watches over competitors'. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Leveraging movement expertise to create durable, stylish plastic watches that redefine accessibility without sacrificing reliability, capturing a new demographic before competitors react.

Attack:
1.  **Fit & Edge:** The proposal lacks a mechanism to overcome the stated reputation risk. Selling "inexpensive plastic" under a "quality" brand without a distinct sub-brand strategy or marketing narrative invites brand dilution. No evidence suggests the market views this manufacturer as a fashion authority.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves brand equity. A "pilot launch" of two models in a single region reduces exposure to volatile fashion tastes and tests the aggressive 1M unit target without committing to a full twelve-model collection.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: The watches are perceived as cheap toys, damaging the core movement business; inventory piles up as fashion trends shift within six months; established brands undercut prices, triggering a margin war the manufacturer cannot win.
4.  **Evidence Gap:** The 1M unit target is asserted without data on distribution capacity, price point elasticity, or pre-orders. The "fashion" angle is undefined.

Blockers:
*   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits reputation risks and aggressive targets but provides no fact showing why this plan beats existing fashion options or protects the core brand.
*   **Contradictory Positioning:** A "quality" movement maker launching "inexpensive plastic" fashion items creates immediate brand confusion with no mitigation strategy offered.

Risks:
*   Fashion volatility.
*   Price wars with incumbents.
*   Inventory obsolescence.

These risks are manageable only if the core claim (that this specific launch will succeed) holds water. It does not, as proposed. The lack of a differentiation strategy or brand protection plan is a fatal flaw for a quality-focused entity entering a fad-driven market. A pilot or sub-brand strategy is required before a full twelve-model rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
