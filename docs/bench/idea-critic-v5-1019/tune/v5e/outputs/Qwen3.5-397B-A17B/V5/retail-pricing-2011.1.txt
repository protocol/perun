First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge proving this specific executive's electronics success translates to a coupon-dependent department store demographic; past success in a different category is not an edge for this specific context.

**Steelman:** Leveraging a proven "branded shop" operator to modernize a dying chain by eliminating margin-eroding promotions and creating a curated experience.

**Attack:**
1.  **Category Mismatch:** Electronics buyers value innovation and specs; department store shoppers here are explicitly "coupon-driven." Removing coupons without a transition period ignores the core behavioral trigger of the existing revenue base.
2.  **Execution Risk:** The plan mandates a simultaneous, chain-wide rollout with zero pilot. This violates basic operational hygiene for a turnaround; if the pricing model alienates the base, the entire chain fails at once with no data to pivot.
3.  **False Equivalence:** Assuming the "branded shop" model works identically for soft goods/fashion as it did for hard goods/electronics without testing is an unsupported assumption.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Continued slow decline, but preserves cash flow while testing occurs.
*   *Pilot:* Test the new pricing/shop model in 5-10 stores to validate if the electronics CEO's playbook converts department store shoppers before burning capital chain-wide.
*   *Hybrid:* Gradually reduce coupon frequency while introducing branded shops, measuring elasticity.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales collapse 40%. The core customer base, confused by the lack of expected discounts, migrates to competitors. The company cannot reverse the changes quickly enough due to the scale of the simultaneous rollout, leading to liquidity crisis.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Pilot:** The absence of a test for a fundamental behavioral shift (coupon to everyday low price) in a distressed asset is a factual blocker. The risk is not theoretical; it is a guaranteed blind spot.
*   **Unsupported Claim:** The proposal assumes the electronics success vector applies directly to department stores without evidence.

**Risks:** Customer churn, brand confusion, inventory mismatch.

**Kill Criteria:** If a 5-store pilot does not show flat or positive comp sales within 90 days, halt expansion.

The lack of a pilot for a strategy that directly attacks the historical revenue driver (discounts) makes this unrecoverable if wrong.

DECISION: NO-GO
