First call: NO-GO. A monochrome screen and modest hardware in 1989, against a color rival, lacks a stated edge; need alone is not an advantage.

Steelman: Win by prioritizing battery life and a $89.95 price point to capture the mass market with a specific killer app, ignoring the hardware arms race.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids risk but cedes the portable segment to the color rival.
2. High-end color handheld: Matches the rival but increases cost, reduces battery life, and raises the price barrier, likely failing to reach the volume needed.
3. Proposed plan: Cheapest entry, longest playtime, but visually inferior.

Fit: Assumes the north star is "ubiquitous adoption" rather than "technical superiority." If the goal is market share, this fits; if it is brand prestige via tech, it fails.

Pre-mortem: The device fails because retailers and consumers equate "black and white" with "obsolete," refusing to stock or buy it despite the price. The puzzle game is seen as a gimmick, not a system seller. This is a two-way door (can discontinue), but the $20M marketing spend is sunk. Cost of delay: The rival solidifies a monopoly on portable gaming, making future entry impossible.

Evidence: No internal data provided to prove users prefer battery over color. The claim that "thin margins" are acceptable relies on unproven volume assumptions.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: The proposal admits inferior hardware and screen. Without evidence that the target demographic prioritizes battery/price over color, the plan fails the basic requirement of beating the incumbent's value proposition on *stated* facts.
2. Contradictory history: The company's previous handhelds were single-game units. Moving to a platform model is a strategic pivot not supported by evidence of capability or developer interest in the proposal.

Risks: Visual obsolescence, developer apathy, margin erosion.

Kill check: The proposal states the rival has a color screen and stronger hardware. It offers no fact showing users will reject color for battery. This is a blocker, not a risk, because the core value proposition (play anywhere) is arguably *better* served by the rival's superior tech unless battery life is catastrophically poor on the rival (unproven).

DECISION: NO-GO
