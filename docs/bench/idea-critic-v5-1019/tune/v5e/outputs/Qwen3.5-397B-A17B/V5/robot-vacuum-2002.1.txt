First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; low price alone is not an advantage without evidence that target users prefer cheap, incomplete cleaning over effective, mapped cleaning.

**Steelman:** A low-cost, randomized robot succeeds by targeting price-sensitive buyers who value convenience over perfect coverage, creating a new market segment distinct from premium mapping rivals.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Edge:** The proposal admits two industry experts rejected the concept. It offers no data showing consumers differ from these experts. "Low cost" is a feature, not a strategy, unless the margin supports it and buyers accept the trade-off. The proposal states no fact proving users want "good enough" cleaning at half the price.
2.  **Risks as Blockers:** The lack of consumer experience is a fatal blocker for a direct launch. Building hardware, supply chains, and brand trust from zero is a capital-intensive failure mode for a B2B robotics firm. The risk of "random coverage" failing user expectations is high; without a pilot proving acceptance, this is a blocker, not a risk.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or licensing the tech to an existing player beats a standalone launch. A cheaper, staged alternative (a limited regional pilot) is missing. The proposal demands a full launch without validating demand.
4.  **Evidence:** The only evidence cited (two rejections) contradicts the go decision. The proposal assumes a market exists without proof.

**Blockers:**
*   No consumer product capability: The company cannot execute the distribution, support, or marketing required.
*   No validated demand: Expert rejection stands unrefuted by consumer data.
*   No stated edge: Price reduction does not guarantee adoption if the core function (cleaning) is perceived as flawed.

**Risks:** Brand damage, inventory write-offs, safety/liability issues in homes.

**Kill Criteria:** None proposed. A launch without a "stop" metric for returns or satisfaction is reckless.

**Decision:** The plan fails the operator test. It requires capabilities the firm lacks, ignores negative expert feedback, and offers no proof of a market wedge. It is a speculative gamble, not a strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
