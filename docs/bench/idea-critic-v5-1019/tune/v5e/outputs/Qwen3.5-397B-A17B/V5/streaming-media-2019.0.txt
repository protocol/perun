First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (direct consumer access) but fails to state an edge: being cheaper and later than a dominant incumbent with 100M+ subscribers is not a competitive advantage, it is a deficit.

**Steelman:** Withdrawing content to launch a underpriced, loss-making service is the only way to capture long-term consumer value before licensing windows expire.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Stated Edge:** The plan relies on "low price" against a market leader. Without a unique content window, superior tech, or bundled value explicitly stated as beating the status quo, price alone cannot drive the required migration to offset billions in lost licensing revenue.
2.  **Unverified Capacity:** The company has "never run" a technology platform of this scale. Assuming execution success without a partner or acquired capability is a fatal gap, not a risk.
3.  **Cannibalization Math:** The proposal admits to losing licensing revenue and cannibalizing cable/theatrical income. It offers no evidence that the projected "tens of millions" of subscribers (vs. the leader's 100M+) generates enough margin to cover the triple hit of lost licensing, operating losses, and internal cannibalization within a reasonable timeframe.
4.  **Alternatives Ignored:** "Do nothing" (continue licensing) preserves billions in immediate cash flow. A joint venture or white-label partnership could test direct-to-consumer waters without the full capex/opex burn and tech build risk. These alternatives reach similar upside with less downside.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Contradiction:** The stated facts (loss of licensing revenue + multi-year operating loss + cannibalization) contradict the feasibility of reaching profitability with only "tens of millions" of low-price subscribers. The numbers do not work as proposed.
*   **Capability Gap:** The core mechanism (streaming platform) is unproven for this entity, and the plan offers no mitigation other than "fund it," which is not a strategy.

**Risks:** Execution failure, brand damage from a buggy launch, inability to raise capital for sustained losses, and permanent loss of leverage with distributors.

The proposal asks to trade guaranteed billions for a speculative, under-capitalized, and operationally unproven venture with no stated advantage over the incumbent.

DECISION: NO-GO
