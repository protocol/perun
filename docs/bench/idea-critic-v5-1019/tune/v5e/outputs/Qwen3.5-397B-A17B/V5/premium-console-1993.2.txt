First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; a $700 price point against established competitors selling at a loss is a fatal disadvantage for a new entrant without a proven library.

**Steelman:** Licensing shifts capital risk to partners, enabling rapid market saturation if the royalty model aligns incentives.
**Attack:** The model creates a misalignment fatal to adoption. Established competitors subsidize hardware to drive game sales; this proposal forces licensees to charge a 2-3x premium ($700) to cover costs and generate profit. Early adopters will not pay a massive premium for an unproven platform with no games, while licensees cannot lower prices without losing money. The "standard" cannot be set if the hardware never reaches critical mass due to price.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Avoid certain failure; preserve capital.
2.  **Subsidize hardware:** Start-up absorbs hardware losses to lower consumer price, earning only on games (mimics competitors). High cash burn risk.
3.  **Proposed model:** High price, low volume, no ecosystem.

**Fit:** Aiming to set a "standard" requires volume. A luxury niche product ($700) cannot become the industry standard.

**Pre-mortem:** The console launches, sells 5,000 units, and is discontinued within a year. Licensees demand refunds or sue over unsold inventory. The brand is dead before the second generation.
**Door:** One-way door on reputation and partner relationships.
**Delay cost:** Minimal; the market is not waiting for a $700 device.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the price is "far above existing consoles" and competitors "sell hardware at a loss." These are not risks; they are blockers. The math does not work: Licensees need margin + Start-up needs royalty + High BOM = $700. Competitors Price = BOM - Subsidy. The gap is insurmountable for a new entrant.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Price/Volume Mismatch:** $700 price prevents the volume required to establish a "standard."
2.  **Incentive Conflict:** Licensees cannot compete on price against subsidized rivals while paying royalties.

**Risks:** None need listing; blockers are fatal.

**Kill Criteria:** N/A (Decision is No-Go).

DECISION: NO-GO
