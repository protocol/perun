First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: while data exists, the plan does not explain why this specific data guarantees success where traditional pilot testing fails, nor why a straight-to-series order for an unproven format beats the industry standard of de-risking via a pilot.

Steelman: Leveraging proprietary viewing data to bypass traditional gatekeepers and secure a first-mover advantage in original prestige content, locking in top talent before competitors react.

However, the risks outweigh the potential upside given the execution method.
1. **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "pilot first" approaches are significantly cheaper and safer. A single-season order or a pilot episode would test the "all-at-once" consumption hypothesis and the audience's appetite for this specific show without committing 50% of the content budget to an unproven model. The proposal fails to demonstrate why these cheaper alternatives cannot achieve the same strategic goal of entering the original content market.
2. **Fit & Evidence:** While the strategy aims to differentiate via data, the evidence (viewing data) has never been validated for greenlighting full series in a vacuum. Historical data predicts retention of existing content, not necessarily the creation of new cultural phenomena. Relying solely on backward-looking metrics for a forward-looking creative gamble is a fundamental flaw.
3. **Pre-mortem:** If the show fails, the company loses a massive portion of its budget with no salvageable asset (no weekly rollout to build buzz, no pilot to shop elsewhere). The "all-at-once" release removes the ability to adjust marketing or production based on early episode reception.
4. **Blocker:** The core blocker is the absence of a validation step. The proposal commits irreversible capital to a hypothesis (data replaces pilots; binge-watching works for drama) that remains unproven. A cheaper alternative (pilot or one season) reaches the same upside (testing the market) with far less downside.

The risks (uncertain audience, high cost) are not merely theoretical; they are exacerbated by the refusal to stage the investment. Without a pilot or a smaller initial commitment, the plan fails the basic test of capital efficiency.

DECISION: NO-GO
