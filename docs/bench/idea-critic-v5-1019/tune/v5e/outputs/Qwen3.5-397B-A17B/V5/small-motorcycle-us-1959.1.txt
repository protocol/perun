First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it attempts to sell a product category (small motorcycles) that the target market explicitly rejects in favor of large machines, without evidence of latent demand or a mechanism to shift perception.

Steelman: The small, reliable step-through can create a new "easy riding" segment for non-enthusiasts if distribution moves beyond traditional motorcycle dealers to sporting-goods stores, bypassing the "rough" image.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** Enter US with 50cc only via non-traditional retail. High risk of brand irrelevance; low capital efficiency if sales fail.
2. **Do Nothing:** Preserve scarce foreign currency; avoid exposure to unproven market and potential mechanical failures of larger models at highway speeds.
3. **Alternative:** Export only larger models to establish brand presence first, or license technology to a local manufacturer to test waters without currency exposure.

Fit: Entering a shrinking, hostile market with an unknown brand contradicts prudent capital allocation unless a specific, verified behavioral shift is identified. No such shift is stated.

Pre-mortem: The venture fails because Americans view 50cc bikes as toys or inferior goods, not transport. Inventory piles up in sporting-goods stores lacking service infrastructure. The larger models fail on highways, damaging the brand's reputation for durability before the small bike gains traction. Currency reserves are depleted with no return.

Evidence: All claims regarding US preference for large bikes and the brand's obscurity are accepted as stated facts. The proposal offers no counter-evidence (e.g., surveys showing interest in commuting, data on student transport needs). The risk of larger models failing at US speeds is a stated fact that undermines the full product line's viability.

Blockers:
1. **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits the market prefers large bikes and the brand is unknown, yet offers no fact-based reason why this specific small bike would succeed where the category has failed. Need is not an edge.
2. **Product Viability:** The stated risk that larger models cannot handle US highway speeds suggests the company's core engineering may not fit the US infrastructure, casting doubt on the entire brand entry.

Risks: Currency depletion, brand damage, inventory obsolescence. These are severe but secondary to the lack of a fundamental reason to win.

DECISION: NO-GO
