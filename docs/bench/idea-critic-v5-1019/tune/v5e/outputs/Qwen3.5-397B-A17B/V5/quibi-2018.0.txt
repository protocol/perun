First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (mobile video) but fails to state an edge; "high budgets" and "A-list talent" are cost drivers, not differentiators in a market saturated with free, abundant short-form content.

**Steelman:** A premium, mobile-native studio can command subscription fees by delivering cinematic short-form narratives that free platforms cannot match, leveraging vertical/horizontal tech to reduce friction.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Stated Edge:** The core value proposition relies on production quality, yet the target format (under 10 minutes) is dominated by free user-generated and ad-supported content. The proposal admits "subscription fatigue" and "no proven demand" for *premium* shorts. High cost does not equal high value here; it increases the break-even point in a category where users currently pay $0.
2.  **Contradictory Evidence:** The proposal itself states risks include "no proven demand" and "price." These are not future uncertainties; they are current market realities cited as facts. A plan requiring $1B+ to solve a problem the market hasn't asked to pay for is a blocker.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "wait" alternative is superior. Let competitors burn capital proving the model. A cheaper alternative exists: license existing premium shorts or produce low-cost pilots to test willingness-to-pay before raising $1B.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The service launches, users try the free trial, enjoy the quality, but cancel because the library is too small relative to the price, or they realize they can get similar entertainment for free on existing platforms. The $1B burns on content that cannot acquire retained subscribers.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal admits "no proven demand" for the specific product (premium short-form subscription).
*   **Fact:** "Subscription fatigue" is cited as a rising concern, directly opposing the revenue model.
*   **Fact:** Free alternatives are "abundant."
*   **Result:** The plan requires spending $1B to overcome a lack of demand that the proposal itself confirms exists. This is a fundamental economic failure, not an execution risk.

**Risks:** Competition for attention, high churn, content costs. These are secondary to the primary blocker: paying for what is currently free without a proven reason to switch.

DECISION: NO-GO
