First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; a price several times higher than competitors, combined with slower performance and a software vacuum, offers no defensible advantage to office workers.

Steelman: This machine justifies its premium by replacing complex command-line training with an intuitive graphical interface, theoretically boosting long-term productivity despite higher upfront costs and lower raw speed.

Attack:
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $10,000, the cost is prohibitive without immediate, proven productivity gains. The "slower" speed directly contradicts the efficiency claim needed to justify the price.
2.  **Software Ecosystem:** Business utility depends on third-party software (spreadsheets, databases). "Little software" means the hardware is useless for core office tasks regardless of interface quality.
3.  **Internal Cannibalization:** A cheaper, similar internal project exists. Launching the expensive version first guarantees the cheaper version will render this launch obsolete immediately, destroying early adopter trust and inventory value.
4.  **Market Timing:** Office workers prioritize function over form. Without a killer app, the mouse and GUI are novelties, not necessities worth a 300%+ premium.

Blockers:
*   **Price/Performance Mismatch:** Higher cost and lower speed than established competitors with no compensating software ecosystem.
*   **Internal Alternative:** A cheaper, similar machine is imminent, making this specific configuration commercially non-viable upon arrival.
*   **Lack of Edge:** No stated fact suggests buyers prefer this slow, expensive unit over cheaper, faster, software-rich alternatives.

Risks:
*   Market rejection of GUI paradigm (mitigated by internal cheaper option testing).
*   Software developers ignoring the platform due to low install base.

The proposal fails the fundamental test of beating "do nothing" or waiting for the cheaper internal alternative. It asks the market to pay a massive premium for inferior speed and an empty ecosystem. The existence of a cheaper internal alternative with similar features is a fatal flaw in the current pricing and positioning strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
