First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price and unclear value proposition against rapidly improving PCs and cheaper consoles make failure likely.

Steelman: A dedicated $1,000 appliance offers superior, immediate multimedia integration that PCs cannot match in 1991 due to configuration complexity, targeting early adopters willing to pay for convenience.

Options:
1. **Do Nothing:** Avoid capital risk while the market defines itself; PCs naturally evolve into this role.
2. **PC Add-on:** Sell a lower-cost drive/peripheral for existing computers, leveraging installed bases.
3. **Proposal:** High-cost standalone unit requiring new content creation and consumer education.
The proposal fails to beat "Do Nothing" or the cheaper add-on strategy on cost or risk.

Fit: Assumes a market desire for a dedicated living room hub, but the "North Star" of affordable home computing is better served by evolving PC architectures. The $1,000 price point excludes the mass market required to sustain a new media format.

Pre-mortem: The device launches to poor sales because consumers view it as an expensive toy with no software library. Developers refuse to build titles without an installed base. Within two years, PCs include CD-ROMs as standard, rendering the standalone unit obsolete. This is a one-way door with high sunk costs in hardware tooling and licensing.

Evidence & Calculation: The $1,000 price is ~3-4x contemporary consoles. With a small title library, the value per dollar is critically low. The risk of buyer confusion is confirmed by the lack of a clear category definition ("between console and PC").

Blockers:
1. **Price vs. Value:** No evidence suggests consumers will pay a 300% premium over consoles for unproven interactive media.
2. **Content Chicken-and-Egg:** Small library discourages buyers; low sales discourage developers. The proposal admits this risk but offers no solution.
3. **Rapid Obsolescence:** PCs are gaining these capabilities natively; a dedicated static hardware device cannot compete with general-purpose evolution.

Risks: Market education failure, format war losses, inventory write-downs.

Kill Criteria: If pre-orders do not cover 20% of manufacturing tooling costs within 3 months of announcement, cancel production.

DECISION: NO-GO
