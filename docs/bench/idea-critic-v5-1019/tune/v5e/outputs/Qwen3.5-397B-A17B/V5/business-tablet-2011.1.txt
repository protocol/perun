First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; matching a rival's price with a smaller screen, an untested OS, and no native email for a business-focused brand offers no defensible advantage.

Steelman: A niche, phone-tethered 7-inch device could validate a new OS ecosystem cheaply before full integration.

Attack:
1.  **Fit:** The core brand promise is "business email." Shipping a device explicitly without built-in email contradicts the company's primary market strength and validates competitor fears rather than alleviating them.
2.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves brand integrity. A "cheaper alternative" would be porting existing business apps to the rival's established OS or delaying launch until native apps are ready. The current plan costs the same as the rival's superior (larger screen, app store) product but delivers less utility.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. Cause: Business users reject a "tablet" that cannot function independently for its primary use case (email), while consumers reject it due to the high price and lack of apps. The "tethering" requirement is a friction point, not a feature.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the OS is untested and the app store is missing. It concedes the price matches the rival's larger, established tablet. There is no evidence provided that users want a $500 (implied) accessory that requires a specific phone to function.
5.  **Blockers:**
    *   **Value Mismatch:** Selling a business device without email is a factual contradiction of the brand's leading position.
    *   **Price/Value Gap:** Matching the rival's price for inferior hardware (smaller screen) and software (no apps, no email) guarantees market rejection.
    *   **Platform Risk:** An untested OS with no developer commitment is a fatal flaw for a consumer computing device in 2011.

Risks (non-blocking if blockers were absent): Developer adoption, ecosystem growth. However, the blockers render these moot. The plan fails on its own stated facts: it charges a premium for a crippled experience in the company's own area of expertise.

DECISION: NO-GO
