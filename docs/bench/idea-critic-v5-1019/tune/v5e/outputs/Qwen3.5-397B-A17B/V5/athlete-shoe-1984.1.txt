**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain status quo; safe but cedes basketball ground to rivals with proven stars.
2.  **Sign a veteran:** Higher certainty of performance, lower marketing shock value, likely higher upfront cost due to proven status.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Highest risk (injury, flop, fines), highest potential reward (cultural icon), unique "rebel" narrative.

**Fit:** Assumes the brand needs a disruptive narrative to enter the basketball space, not just product performance.

**Pre-mortem:** The player suffers a career-ending injury in year one, leaving the company with dead inventory and wasted budget. The league bans the shoes immediately, removing the marketing hook without generating sustained sales.

**Evidence:** No data provided on the player's college dominance, market research on "rebel" appeal, or legal assessment of league fines. The claim that signature shoes rarely sell beyond a season is accepted as fact but not countered with a specific mechanism for this exception.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Exposure:** The deal consumes a "large share" of the budget for an asset with zero professional validation.
*   **Regulatory Risk:** Building a line around rules the league actively enforces creates immediate inventory liability if banned.
*   **Lack of Edge:** Paying "far more" than rivals for less proof of value is economically unsound without a guaranteed differentiator beyond the contract size itself.

**Risks:** Injury, player underperformance, consumer rejection of non-conforming aesthetics, budget cannibalization from the core running line.

**Kill Criteria:** If the player does not make the opening day roster or if the league issues a formal ban before the first game, the deal must be terminated or renegotiated immediately.

The proposal relies entirely on a speculative "what if" without a safety net or verified demand for rule-breaking aesthetics. It fails the operator test of capital preservation against unproven assets.
