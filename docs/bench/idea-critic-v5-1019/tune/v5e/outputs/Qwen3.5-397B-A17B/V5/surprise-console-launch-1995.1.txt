First call: NO-GO. A hardware launch without software, at a premium price against a cheaper rival, lacks a stated edge; "being first" with an empty library is a disadvantage, not an advantage.

Steelman: Launching in May secures shelf space and mindshare before the competitor arrives, forcing them to react to our presence rather than us reacting to theirs.

Attack: The proposal admits critical failures: the device is hard to program, developers were not consulted (ensuring a software drought), and the price is 25% higher than the rival's. Launching early amplifies these flaws. Retailers excluded from the initial limited shipment will likely prioritize the competitor's broader launch later, viewing this early move as chaotic rather than strategic. Without games, the hardware is a toy; without developer buy-in, there are no games. The "first mover" advantage here is purely negative: being the first to disappoint customers with a $399 empty box.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing until September: Match the competitor with a full library and potentially adjusted pricing.
2. Delay to Q4: Miss the competitor but ensure software readiness.
3. Current Plan: Guarantees a reputation for being expensive, difficult, and empty.

Pre-mortem: By Christmas 1995, the console is labeled a failure. Retailers refuse to stock it due to the earlier slight, developers stick to the easier, cheaper rival platform, and early adopters demand refunds due to lack of software. The brand is tainted before the real race begins.

Evidence: The proposal itself states developers "did not plan" and the machine is "hard to program." These are internal facts confirming the software gap cannot be closed by simply moving the date. The price difference is a stated fact ($399 vs $299 implied).

Blockers:
1. No software pipeline: Developers cannot create games in four months if they haven't planned for it, especially on hard-to-program hardware.
2. Price disadvantage: $100 premium with no content to justify it.
3. Channel alienation: Excluding retailers creates immediate distribution friction.

Risks: Competitor dominance, brand damage, developer abandonment. These are not mere risks; the stated facts confirm them as near-certainties under this plan.

DECISION: NO-GO
