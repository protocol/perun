First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a higher price, no manufacturing history, powerful incumbents who can copy the tech, and unproven buyer willingness to pay a premium. Need alone is not an edge; the stated facts show no advantage over existing options.

Steelman: A superior bagless technology justifies a premium price if buyers value the elimination of recurring bag costs enough to overcome brand loyalty and distribution barriers.

Attack:
1. **Options**: "Do nothing" costs zero and avoids capital risk. Licensing the technology to an incumbent (Alternative A) leverages their manufacturing and distribution immediately, capturing upside without building a factory. The proposed path (build from scratch) is the most expensive and risky.
2. **Fit**: Starting a manufacturing company contradicts the constraint of having "no manufacturing record." Entering a mature market with a premium product requires a distribution moat or brand equity, neither of which exists here.
3. **Pre-mortem**: Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: Incumbents release a cheaper cyclone model using their existing supply chains while the startup burns cash trying to tool up production. Retailers refuse shelf space to an unknown brand at a higher price point.
4. **Evidence**: The proposal admits buyers "may not pay extra." Without data proving otherwise, this is a fatal gap. The cost disadvantage is stated as fact ("noticeably more").
5. **Blocker Test**:
   - **Blockers**: The stated facts confirm the product costs more than competitors, the company cannot manufacture efficiently yet, and incumbents can copy the feature. These facts directly contradict the requirement for a competitive edge. The lack of proven demand at the premium price point is a blocker when combined with high fixed costs.
   - **Risks**: Execution difficulty, brand building. These are secondary to the fundamental economic mismatch.

The proposal fails because it chooses the hardest path (building a manufacturer) against entrenched rivals with a more expensive product, based on unproven customer willingness to pay. A licensing model would achieve the technology's adoption without the fatal manufacturing and distribution risks. The stated facts provide no reason to believe this specific plan will succeed where a cheaper, licensed alternative would not.

DECISION: NO-GO
