First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; rivals already offer open ecosystems, and the CEO previously argued web apps were sufficient, making this a costly pivot without a defined advantage over the status quo or competitors.

Steelman: A curated, single-vendor store creates a premium, secure environment that prevents fragmentation and malware, distinguishing the device from chaotic open markets while capturing revenue via a 30% cut.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Maintain stability and carrier relationships; risk falling behind as users demand native features web apps cannot provide.
2. Web apps only: Aligns with CEO's vision and avoids security risks; fails to match rival performance and functionality.
3. Open sideloading: Matches rival freedom; invites security disasters and carrier backlash, threatening the product's "polish."

Fit: Assumes the north star is shifting from "web sufficiency" to "ecosystem lock-in." If the strategy remains web-first, this contradicts leadership.

Pre-mortem: The review bottleneck delays critical apps, carriers block the phone for bypassing services, and a single security breach destroys the brand's reputation for stability. The 30% fee drives developers to rivals with lower cuts or open distribution.

Evidence: No data provided proves users will pay for native apps over free web alternatives, nor that the company can scale reviews fast enough. The 30% model is unproven for this specific hardware cohort.

Blockers:
- No stated edge: The proposal admits rivals have ecosystems and the CEO advocated web apps. Without evidence that a walled garden beats open distribution *for this specific device*, the core claim fails.
- Contradictory premise: The CEO's public stance against native apps undermines internal alignment and market messaging.

Risks: Carrier revolt, security breaches, review backlog, developer abandonment due to fees.

Kill criteria: If carrier partnerships are not secured within 3 months, or if the first 100 reviewed apps take longer than 2 weeks each, the program halts.

The absence of a unique value proposition compared to existing open platforms, combined with internal strategic contradiction, constitutes a fatal flaw.

DECISION: NO-GO
