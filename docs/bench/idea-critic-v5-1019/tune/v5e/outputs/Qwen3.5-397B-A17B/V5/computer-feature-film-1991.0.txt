First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a critical need (feature production) but fails to state a specific edge explaining why this small company, with no feature experience and slow rendering capabilities, can succeed where a large studio previously failed to hire their talent away, other than the mere existence of the offer.

Steelman: Leveraging the studio's distribution and capital to bypass internal resource limits while betting that the "toy's point of view" concept is strong enough to overcome the technical inexperience of the team.

However, the risks presented are not merely hypothetical; they are structural blockers based on the stated facts. The company has never produced anything longer than a short, yet the proposal commits immediately to a feature-length film without a stated intermediate step (like a special or short series) to validate pipeline scalability. Rendering is explicitly described as "slow and costly," which threatens the budget of a first-time feature where inefficiencies are highest. Furthermore, ceding control of distribution to a partner that previously tried to poach the lead animator creates a misaligned incentive structure; the partner holds the "stronger hand" and may prioritize their own interests over the small studio's survival if the film struggles.

The "better way" is to reject the immediate feature commitment. Instead, the company should negotiate a deal for a high-profile short or a limited series to prove the rendering pipeline and storytelling endurance at a lower cost basis before risking the entire company on a single, unproven feature. The current proposal bets the farm on a "one-way door" decision without a pilot, cap, or verified metric for rendering throughput. The fact that the partner tried to hire the lead away suggests they value the talent, not necessarily the small studio's unproven feature capability; accepting this deal as proposed risks losing creative control or facing bankruptcy due to technical debt.

Blockers: No prior feature experience combined with known slow/costly rendering creates a high probability of budget overrun and failure; no intermediate validation step exists in the plan.
Risks: Partner misalignment, technical bottlenecks, market rejection of CG features.

DECISION: NO-GO
