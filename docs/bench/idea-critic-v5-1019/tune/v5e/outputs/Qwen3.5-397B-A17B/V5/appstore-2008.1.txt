First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; rivals already possess open ecosystems, and the CEO's own stance favors web apps, making this a costly catch-up play without a defined advantage.

Steelman: A curated, single-vendor store transforms security risks and carrier friction into a premium trust signal, offering developers monetization and users stability that open, fragmented rivals cannot match.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** High cost (review staff), high risk (carrier backlash), low reversibility (ecosystem lock-in). Beats "do nothing" only if curation creates a unique brand premium.
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, zero risk, maintains CEO's web-app vision. Loses developer mindshare to rivals.
3. **Web-App Only:** Low cost, aligns with CEO, avoids security/carrier issues. Fails to match rival native performance.

Fit: Contradicts the CEO's public vision and current stage (first-gen hardware). Assumes users value "polish" over "features," which is unproven against rivals' expansive libraries.

Pre-mortem: The review bottleneck stalls innovation; carriers block the device; developers reject the 30% cut for a tiny user base. This is a one-way door with high delay cost (losing the developer war immediately).

Evidence: All figures (30%, single store) are assumptions. No data proves users will pay for a smaller, curated set versus a larger, open one. The "polish" argument is speculative.

Blockers:
1. **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits rivals have ecosystems. It offers no fact showing why a *smaller*, *taxed*, *delayed* ecosystem beats an *open*, *existing* one. Need without an edge is a NO-GO.
2. **Strategic Contradiction:** Directly opposes the CEO's stated thesis (web apps suffice) without new evidence to overturn it.
3. **Resource Mismatch:** A first-year phone maker lacks the operational scale to review "every app" effectively compared to established players.

Risks: Security breaches, carrier rejection, developer revolt. These are secondary to the lack of a competitive edge.

Kill Check: The proposal states rivals have open distribution. Entering later with higher friction (review) and higher cost (30% tax) contradicts the path to upside unless "curation" is proven to be the primary buyer driver. The proposal offers no such proof.

DECISION: NO-GO
