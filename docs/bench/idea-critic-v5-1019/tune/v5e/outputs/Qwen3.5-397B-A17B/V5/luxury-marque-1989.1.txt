First call: NO-GO. A real need for lower-priced luxury is not a stated edge; the proposal admits the brand is known for economy cars, which directly contradicts the requirement for prestige pricing, and offers no fact showing buyers will ignore this history.

Steelman: A distinct brand identity can sever the "economy" association, allowing a high-quality, lower-priced flagship to capture value-conscious luxury buyers ignored by European incumbents.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The core claim relies on matching European quality while pricing "well below." The proposal itself states development has "already cost heavily." If the unit economics require a low price to compete, but the sunk costs and separate dealer network investment demand high margins to recoup, the numbers likely do not work as proposed. The "thin line-up" of a single flagship is a critical vulnerability; luxury buyers expect a range of options, and a single model cannot sustain a dealer network or brand perception against decades of entrenched competition.

The risk that "buyers may not pay" is elevated to a blocker because the proposal offers no evidence (e.g., market research, waiting lists) that the target demographic exists for *this* specific brand architecture. Without a proven route to overcome the "economy car" stigma, the stated fact of the company's reputation removes the route to the upside. The separate dealer network cost is a massive fixed burden that a single model cannot support if sales volume lags due to brand perception.

Blockers: 1) The stated brand reputation (economy) directly contradicts the luxury pricing strategy with no evidence of customer willingness to override it. 2) A single flagship model cannot economically sustain a new, separate dealer network given the heavy development costs already incurred.

Risks: European incumbents' prestige; execution difficulty of a new brand.

The proposal asks to commit to a full dealer network and brand launch based on an unproven assumption that brand separation works instantly. This is a one-way door with insufficient evidence.

DECISION: NO-GO
