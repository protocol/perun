First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge over rapidly improving ground cellular networks; high cost and poor indoor performance are disadvantages, not advantages.

**Steelman:** This is the only solution for true global coverage where terrestrial cells cannot reach, serving high-value travelers who need connectivity everywhere immediately.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" and wait for ground cellular expansion is cheaper and offers better indoor performance. The "cheaper alternative" (roaming agreements on existing GSM networks) is already operational and improving monthly.
2.  **Fit:** The strategy targets "international business travelers," but the $3,000 handset price and bulky form factor contradict the needs of this demographic, who value convenience and compactness.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 24 months. Cause: Ground cellular coverage reaches 80% of business destinations before the satellite network achieves critical mass. Debt payments ($5B principal + interest) force aggressive pricing or equity dilution just as competitors lower roaming rates.
4.  **Evidence & Math:** $5B sunk cost is irrelevant to future viability; it is a liability. To service debt, the project needs massive, immediate subscriber growth. However, the addressable market is capped by the $3,000 entry barrier and poor indoor utility. The math does not work: high fixed costs + limited market size + superior, cheaper substitutes = negative cash flow.
5.  **Blockers:** The core claim fails because the stated facts show a superior, cheaper alternative (ground cellular) is actively encroaching on the target use case. The proposal admits sales channels are not ready, yet debt payments require immediate growth. This is a factual contradiction.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal names risks (ground network spread, price limits) but offers no mitigation. These are not just risks; they are active blockers based on current trajectories. A "pilot" cannot fix the fundamental physics of indoor signal loss or the macro-economic reality of $5B debt service against a shrinking premium niche.

**Verdict:** The plan relies on a window of opportunity that is closing faster than the execution timeline. The debt structure makes "waiting for adoption" impossible, while the product limitations prevent rapid adoption.

DECISION: NO-GO
