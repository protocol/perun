First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; matching the leader's price with a fraction of the apps and zero OS share offers no reason for buyers to switch.

Steelman: Leverage the computer maker's brand and distribution to force a new tablet ecosystem into the market despite a weak app catalog, betting that heavy ad spend and developer outreach will close the gap post-launch.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost, extreme risk (platform failure), low reversibility (brand damage).
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, no risk, preserves capital for a viable product later.
3. **Low-Cost Niche:** Price significantly below the leader to target price-sensitive users; lower margin but reduces the "unproven" barrier. This beats the proposed plan on stated facts because the current price point guarantees rejection given the app deficit.

Fit: Entering a dominated market without a differentiated value proposition (price or features) contradicts basic survival strategy. The "small app catalog" is a fatal flaw at a "market-leading" price.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because consumers refuse to pay premium prices for an incomplete ecosystem. Developers ignore the outreach because the user base remains tiny. The brand becomes associated with "failed tablet." This is a one-way door for reputation; delay costs less than a botched launch.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the app catalog is a "small fraction" and the OS has "almost no market share." These are internal contradictions to a premium pricing strategy. No external data is needed; the math of value (Price vs. Utility) fails immediately.

Blockers:
1. **Price-Utility Mismatch:** Charging market-leading prices for a product with significantly lower utility (apps) is a factual path to zero sales.
2. **No Stated Edge:** The proposal offers no advantage (cost, feature, access) to overcome the incumbent's network effect.

Risks: Leadership transition, shopper perception (these are secondary to the fundamental pricing flaw).

Kill Criteria: If the app count is <20% of the leader, the price must be <70% of the leader. The proposal violates this.

DECISION: NO-GO
