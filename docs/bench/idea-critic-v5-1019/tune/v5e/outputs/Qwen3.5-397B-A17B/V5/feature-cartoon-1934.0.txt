First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a need (higher profits) but fails to state an edge: no fact suggests audiences will pay for or sit through a feature-length cartoon, nor that the studio's current thin margins can sustain a 10x cost overrun without catastrophic failure.

Steelman: If the studio can leverage its existing short-film audience to validate feature-length demand before committing the full budget, the risk of total loss decreases. However, the proposal as written commits to the full production immediately.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Continue steady, thin profits. Safe, but limits growth.
2. **Pilot/Serial:** Release a sequence of connected shorts to test feature-length engagement at a fraction of the cost.
3. **Proposed Plan:** Commit 10x budget upfront. High risk of ruin if the core assumption (audience endurance) is false.

Fit: The goal is profit growth, but the method threatens the entire enterprise's solvency. A sound strategy moves toward the north star without betting the farm on an unproven hypothesis.

Pre-mortem: The film is completed, but audiences leave at the 40-minute mark. The studio has exhausted its cash reserves from shorts, cannot fund new content, and goes bankrupt. This is a one-way door; the money spent on a failed feature cannot be recovered to fund shorts.

Evidence: The proposal admits insiders and family advise against it, citing the exact risks (audience attention, budget overrun) that constitute the core uncertainty. There is no data provided to contradict these warnings. The "steady but thin" earnings make the studio uniquely vulnerable to a single large failure.

Blockers:
1. **Unproven Core Assumption:** The plan assumes audiences will sit for a feature, yet explicitly lists this as a major risk with no mitigation or testing phase.
2. **Financial Fragility:** Funding a 10x cost project from "thin profits" means a single failure results in insolvency. The math of "thin margins" vs. "massive bet" does not work without a safety net or staged investment.

Risks: Budget overrun, creative failure, market rejection. These are not just risks; given the financial structure, they are fatal triggers.

The proposal lacks a staged approach (e.g., a test segment) or a cap on initial exposure. It demands an all-in bet on an unverified market behavior.

DECISION: NO-GO
