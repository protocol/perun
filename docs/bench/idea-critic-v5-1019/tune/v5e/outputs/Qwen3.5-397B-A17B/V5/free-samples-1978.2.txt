First call: NO-GO. The initial four-city test demonstrated weak demand; renaming a failed product and distributing free samples without a proven mechanism to convert interest into sales ignores the core evidence that the market does not currently value the offering.

**Steelman:** Repositioning the adhesive as a novel office tool via targeted sampling could uncover latent demand missed by the initial retail launch, validating a niche before national spend.

**Attack:**
1.  **Evidence Contradiction:** The proposal admits the first test showed "little demand." A rename and free samples do not address *why* demand was low (e.g., price, utility, habit). If the product lacks utility, sampling merely wastes capital on education for a non-solution.
2.  **Metric Flaw:** The success criterion is "most recipients say they would buy." Stated intent in a free-sample scenario is notoriously unreliable compared to actual purchase data. Offices accepting free goods creates a false positive signal; it does not prove willingness to pay.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "pivot" option is stronger. Before spending on a city-wide sample campaign, a low-cost experiment should test the *renamed* value proposition with a direct purchase offer (no free samples) to validate if the new narrative actually drives transactions.
4.  **Cost vs. Risk:** Free samples to an entire mid-sized city's offices represent a significant sunk cost with no recovery mechanism if the "intent" metric proves false. The risk of scaling based on vanity metrics (survey says "yes") after a hard metric failure (sales were "no") is extreme.

**Blockers:**
*   **False Success Metric:** Relying on stated intent ("would buy") rather than actual pre-orders or paid pilots contradicts the hard evidence of the previous sales failure.
*   **Ignored Root Cause:** The proposal assumes "positioning" is the only variable, ignoring the possibility that the product simply solves no urgent problem for office workers.

**Risks:** High burn rate on sampling; national rollout based on inflated interest; brand damage from pushing an unwanted product.

**Kill Criteria:** If a small-scale test offering the renamed product for *payment* (not free) yields less than 5% conversion, the project stops.

The plan substitutes hope (survey intent) for evidence (sales). It fails to validate the fundamental economic unit before scaling distribution.

DECISION: NO-GO
