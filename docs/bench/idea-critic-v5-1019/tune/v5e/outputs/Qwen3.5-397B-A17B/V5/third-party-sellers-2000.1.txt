First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge showing why this specific marketplace model beats existing alternatives or solves the profitability problem better than focusing on owned inventory margins, especially given the explicit risks of cannibalization and brand erosion without proposed mitigations.

Steelman: Aggregating third-party supply instantly expands selection and generates fee revenue without inventory cost, potentially creating a network effect that draws more buyers.

Attack:
1. **Cannibalization vs. Margin:** The proposal admits used copies may cannibalize new sales. For an unprofitable retailer, shifting volume to lower-margin used goods (or losing the sale entirely to a competitor on the same page) directly worsens the path to profitability unless the fee structure overwhelmingly compensates, which is not detailed.
2. **Brand Trust & Control:** Allowing competitors and unknown sellers on product pages introduces uncontrolled variables in service quality. One bad actor damages the retailer's trust, which is the primary asset for a non-profitable entity trying to gain scale. No mechanism for quality control or insurance is offered.
3. **Supplier Retaliation:** Publishers and suppliers may object to their goods being undercut by used markets or competitors on the retailer's own site, risking supply chain disruption for the core inventory business.
4. **Missing Edge:** The proposal describes a feature (marketplace) but fails to articulate why *this* retailer, currently struggling with profitability, is the right vehicle to launch it now against established players or internal focus needs. It assumes liquidity will appear without a customer acquisition strategy for the seller side.

Blockers:
- **Economic Contradiction:** The plan risks shifting sales from owned (higher potential margin) to third-party (fee only) or used (lower value) without a proven model to net positive profit growth.
- **Strategic Risk:** Alienating key suppliers (publishers) threatens the core business viability.

Risks: Brand degradation, customer confusion, operational complexity in dispute resolution.

The risks are not just theoretical; they are direct threats to the existing business model. Without a pilot, a specific fee structure analysis, or a supplier alignment plan, the proposal fails the basic test of protecting the core while attempting to expand.

DECISION: NO-GO
