First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a pricing backlash by increasing friction, effectively punishing the most loyal customers who value both formats.

Steelman: Separating the brands allows distinct strategic focus and cost structures for a declining physical business versus a growth-oriented digital one.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current plan:
1. **Timing & Incentives:** Executing a complex brand split, including separate logins and billing, immediately after a 60% price hike is catastrophic. It compounds customer anger with inconvenience, signaling that the company wants to make it harder to stay subscribed.
2. **Value Destruction:** Siloing ratings and recommendations destroys the unique value proposition of a unified library. It forces users to rebuild preference profiles, degrading the user experience for no immediate gain.
3. **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "delay" alternative is superior. If the DVD business requires different management, achieve this via internal organizational restructuring without fracturing the customer-facing product. A brand split should only occur once the streaming service is dominant enough to stand alone and the DVD decline is less emotionally charged for the user base.
4. **Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch fails. The cause will be mass churn driven by the perception that the company is forcing customers into two cumbersome accounts right after raising prices. The "loss of cross-service recommendations" is not just a risk; it is a guaranteed degradation of product utility.

The proposal treats an operational need (separate management) as a customer-facing product change at the worst possible moment. The stated risks (confusion, reaction to price) are not merely potential; they are certain outcomes of this specific execution plan. The plan fails to beat "do nothing" because it sacrifices customer goodwill and product utility to solve an internal org chart problem.

Blockers: The combination of a recent 60% price increase and the immediate introduction of high-friction account separation creates a near-certain churn event that outweighs strategic benefits. The removal of shared data actively harms the product's core value.

Risks: Brand dilution, operational overhead of two sites.

DECISION: NO-GO
