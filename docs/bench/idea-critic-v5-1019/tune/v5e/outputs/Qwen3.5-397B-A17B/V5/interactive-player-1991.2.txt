First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; a $1,000 price point without a proven library or clear category definition cannot beat existing consoles or evolving PCs.

**Steelman:** A premium bridge device offering unique interactive media capabilities unavailable on consoles or PCs, justifying the cost through exclusive content.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $1,000, the unit costs significantly more than game consoles and approaches low-end PC prices. Without a massive installed base of interactive discs, the value proposition collapses. Buyers will not pay a premium for a "player" when a PC offers creation and productivity plus emerging multimedia.
2.  **Category Confusion:** Positioned between a console and a PC, the device risks appealing to neither. Console buyers seek affordable entertainment; PC buyers seek utility. A "multimedia player" is an abstract concept for 1991 living rooms.
3.  **Content Chicken-and-Egg:** A small library of interactive titles ensures low adoption; low adoption ensures developers do not build titles. This is a fatal loop unless the manufacturer subsidizes content heavily, which is not stated.
4.  **Rapid Obsolescence:** PCs are rapidly gaining multimedia abilities. A dedicated hardware box with no upgrade path will be undercut by general-purpose computers within months.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for PC multimedia standards to mature and console makers to add disc drives. Cost: $0. Risk: Missing first-mover status (which may be a poison chalice here).
*   **Software/Addon Strategy:** Partner with existing PC or console manufacturers to add disc capabilities to their units. Lower risk, shared cost.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sells poorly due to price and confusion, developers abandon the format, and the company is left with unsold hardware and a damaged brand.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits the library is small and the price is high. There is no fact presented that suggests buyers want this specific combination at this price over alternatives.
*   **Economic Mismatch:** The cost structure ($1,000) exceeds the perceived value of a passive player in a market dominated by active consoles and versatile PCs.

**Risks:** Market confusion, content drought, rapid PC commoditization. These are not merely risks; given the current facts, they are near-certainties that block success.

DECISION: NO-GO
