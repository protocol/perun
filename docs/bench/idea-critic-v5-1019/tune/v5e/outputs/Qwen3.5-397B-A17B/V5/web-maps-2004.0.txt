First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state an edge: it offers no fact showing why this plan beats established map sites given the stated costs, lack of revenue, and technical limits of 2004 browsers.

Steelman: Leveraging the acquirer's search dominance to subsidize free, browser-based mapping with satellite imagery creates a defensible moat that desktop competitors cannot match.

Attack:
1. **Economics:** Licensing map data and satellite imagery is explicitly "costly" with "no direct revenue model." Without a stated subsidy mechanism (e.g., search ad integration), the unit economics are negative on day one. A cheaper alternative is "do nothing" or licensing only specific high-value layers rather than building a full suite.
2. **Technical Feasibility:** The proposal admits "browsers of the time are slow at interactive graphics." Building a fluid mapping experience in 2004 web tech against native desktop performance is a high-risk execution bet with no proof of concept cited.
3. **Competition:** "Established map sites already have users." Entering a crowded market with a slower, costlier product requires a distinct advantage (e.g., exclusive data or superior speed), neither of which is present. The "free" angle is not an edge if the experience is unusable due to browser slowness.

Pre-mortem: The project burns cash on licenses, launches a sluggish web app that frustrates users accustomed to desktop speed, and fails to monetize, leading to shutdown.

Blockers:
- **Negative Unit Economics:** High licensing costs + zero revenue model = immediate financial bleed with no stated offset.
- **Technical Mismatch:** 2004 browser limitations directly contradict the requirement for "interactive graphics," making the core value proposition (smooth mapping) likely unachievable as proposed.

Risks:
- User acquisition costs against incumbents.
- Satellite imagery update latency.

The proposal's own facts confirm the target users face a slow experience and the business faces undefined costs. These are not mere risks; they are structural failures in the current plan. A "go-if" is impossible without a defined subsidy strategy and a technical breakthrough not currently in evidence.

DECISION: NO-GO
