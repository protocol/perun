First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge proving this hardware bet beats existing alternatives or overcomes the stated history of poor e-reader sales; "large catalog" is a need, not a differentiator against the specific failure modes of hardware inexperience and publisher resistance.

Steelman: A dedicated, cellular-enabled device removes friction for book buyers and solves publisher piracy fears via a closed ecosystem, leveraging the catalog to drive hardware adoption.

Attack:
1.  **Capability Mismatch:** The company has zero hardware design experience. Entering a capital-intensive, low-margin hardware market while simultaneously negotiating fragile publisher deals creates a dual-execution failure mode. One slip in supply chain or content licensing kills the venture.
2.  **Economic Contradiction:** The plan relies on "low price per title" to drive volume while offering "free cellular connection." This compresses margins from both sides (content and connectivity) without a proven subscription model or hardware premium to offset costs.
3.  **Market Evidence:** Earlier e-readers sold poorly due to limited selection and poor UX. While the catalog solves selection, the "paper-like screen" and cellular tech are unproven at consumer scale for this specific vendor. The risk of cannibalizing profitable print sales with a low-margin digital substitute is immediate and quantifiable, whereas the digital upside is speculative.
4.  **Alternatives:** A software-only app for existing devices (laptops/early smartphones) captures the digital market with near-zero capex, avoiding hardware risk while testing price elasticity.

Pre-mortem: The device launches late due to supply chain issues; publishers restrict titles fearing the low-price precedent; the hardware sells below cost; print sales drop without sufficient digital volume to compensate.

Blockers:
*   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits prior failures and lack of experience but offers no specific mechanism (other than "large catalog") to reverse the historical trend of e-reader flops.
*   **Economic Viability:** The combination of free connectivity and low content prices lacks a unit economics model showing profitability.

Risks: Cannibalization, publisher backlash, hardware execution failure.

The absence of a pilot, a software-first alternative analysis, or a unit economics breakdown for the "free cellular + low price" model constitutes a fatal gap in the current plan.

DECISION: NO-GO
