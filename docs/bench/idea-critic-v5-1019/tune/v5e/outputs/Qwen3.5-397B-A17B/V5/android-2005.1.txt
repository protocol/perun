First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: no unique asset, distribution channel, or cost advantage is presented that would allow a search-ad company with no hardware experience to displace entrenched OS vendors and overcome carrier control. Need alone (mobile web growth) is not an edge.

Steelman: Acquiring the team now secures the only viable open-source mobile OS before competitors do, enabling a future where ubiquitous mobile usage drives search ad volume, justifying the $50M entry fee as a long-term infrastructure play.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws based strictly on provided facts. The "better way" analysis shows "do nothing" dominates: the company can optimize existing mobile web experiences for current OSs at a fraction of the cost without assuming the massive execution risk of building an ecosystem from scratch. The proposal admits zero revenue model, total dependence on hostile carriers and handset makers, and a multi-year payback with no intermediate milestones.

The pre-mortem is clear: the project fails because handset makers refuse to adopt a platform backed by an advertising competitor rather than a neutral vendor, and carriers block it to protect their own portals. The $50M price tag for a six-person team with no product and no path to monetization is unjustifiable without a specific, verified technical advantage or existing commitment from a major partner—neither of which is stated.

The risks listed (no revenue, adoption hurdles, carrier resistance) are not merely potential issues; given the stated facts of market dominance by incumbents and lack of hardware expertise, they function as blockers. The proposal offers no mechanism to reverse these dynamics. A cheaper, staged alternative (partnering or software-only optimization) reaches the same upside (increased mobile search) with significantly lower capital exposure and no need to solve the hardware distribution puzzle.

Blockers: Stated lack of revenue model; stated inability to overcome carrier/handset control; no stated edge over "do nothing" or cheaper software-only alternatives.
Risks: Multi-year payback; team size vs. market scale.

DECISION: NO-GO
