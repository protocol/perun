First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the core assets (networks) are incompatible and the target is data-limited, offering no stated edge on cost, speed, or product superiority to justify a $36 billion premium over organic build or partnership.

**Steelman:** The acquisition is the only viable path to instantly secure a sticky, high-margin enterprise segment that cannot be replicated quickly, justifying the integration hell as a temporary toll for market dominance.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or a commercial MVNO agreement captures the revenue without the $36B capex risk or technical debt. A "cheaper alternative" exists: license the push-to-talk platform rather than buying the whole incompatible network.
2.  **Fit:** Running legacy equipment indefinitely contradicts the industry's north star of network simplification and data evolution. The "incompatible technology" is a permanent drag on margins, not a temporary glitch.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Integration stalls due to technical friction; the legacy network becomes a money pit while competitors launch superior data-native push solutions. The "large goodwill" becomes a massive write-down when synergies fail to materialize.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the technology is "incompatible" and "limited for data." In 2005, data growth is the primary value driver; buying a data-limited asset at a premium is structurally flawed.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fatal Flaw:** The core claim relies on acquiring a customer base via a network that cannot serve their future data needs and cannot be easily merged. The cost to integrate two incompatible networks while running legacy gear creates a negative unit economics scenario that the proposal does not solve.
*   **Superior Alternative:** A partnership or platform licensing deal achieves the revenue goal without the balance sheet toxicity of $36B in goodwill and stranded assets.

**Risks:** Integration timeline slippage, customer churn during migration, rapid obsolescence of the acquired tech stack.

**Kill Criteria:** If a partnership model cannot be structured within 90 days, abandon the M&A entirely. Leading metric: Cost per integrated subscriber vs. organic acquisition cost.

DECISION: NO-GO
