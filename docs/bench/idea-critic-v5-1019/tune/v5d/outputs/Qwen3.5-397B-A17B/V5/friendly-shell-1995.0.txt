First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "hard to use" is a need, not an advantage over existing solutions, and the high hardware requirement excludes the majority of the target home market.

Steelman: A cartoon house shell makes computing intuitive for novices by leveraging spatial metaphors, justifying a $100 premium despite hardware constraints.

Attack:
1. **Fit & Market Reality:** The proposal requires a "high-end PC," yet the problem targets "many new home users" who statistically possess lower-spec machines. This creates an immediate addressable market mismatch. The solution solves the problem only for the fraction of users who can already afford the hardware to run it, ignoring the bulk of the "hard to use" demographic.
2. **Timing & Obsolescence:** A "major new operating-system release" is imminent. Launching a $100 add-on shell months before a native OS interface update is commercially suicidal. Users will not pay for a temporary layer that the upcoming OS release will likely render redundant or incompatible.
3. **Ecosystem Viability:** "Few third-party add-ons" indicates a weak ecosystem. A shell dependent on programs appearing as "rooms" fails if those programs do not adopt the metaphor. Without critical mass, the house remains empty, offering no utility.
4. **User Segmentation:** While novices might benefit, "experienced users" finding it "patronizing" suggests a brand risk for a company leading in PC OS. Alienating the power user base to chase a niche novice segment with a hardware-barrier product is a net negative.

Blockers:
- The hardware requirement contradicts the target demographic's likely assets.
- The imminent OS release creates a near-certain obsolescence window, destroying ROI.
- No stated edge explains why this beats the upcoming native OS features or free/cheaper alternatives.

Risks:
- Low adoption due to hardware costs.
- Brand damage from patronizing design.
- Ecosystem failure due to lack of add-ons.

The plan fails on timing and market fit. The upcoming OS release alone blocks this as a standalone $100 product.

DECISION: NO-GO
