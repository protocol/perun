First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "largest mobile maker" status does not automatically translate to gaming dominance against an entrenched leader with a superior library and lower price point.

**Steelman:** Leveraging massive mobile distribution to force a hybrid device into the market, betting that phone utility offsets gaming awkwardness.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fundamental Usability Flaw:** The requirement to remove the battery to change games is a catastrophic user experience failure. It breaks the core loop of handheld gaming (quick swapping) and risks data loss or power interruption. This is not a minor friction point; it renders the "handheld" function broken by design.
2.  **Product Conflict:** A device "awkward to hold for calls" fails its primary function as a phone while offering an inferior experience as a game console. It satisfies neither use case well.
3.  **Economic Disadvantage:** At $300, it is significantly more expensive than the market leader's dedicated device, yet offers fewer games and worse ergonomics. Price premium requires superior value, which is absent.
4.  **Ecosystem Void:** Without committed publishers, the hardware is a shell. The market leader's "huge library" is a moat that price and phone features cannot easily bridge, especially when the hardware experience is compromised.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid brand damage from a flawed hybrid.
*   **Dedicated Device:** Launch a proper handheld without phone compromises (higher cost, but viable product).
*   **Software Only:** Partner with the leader to put games on existing phones (lower risk, leverages installed base).

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, reviewers pan the battery-swap mechanic and awkward form factor, gamers stick to the cheaper, better-supported leader, and the project is discontinued within a year, wasting R&D and harming the mobile brand's reputation for usability.

**Blockers:**
*   **Design Failure:** Changing games requires battery removal (stated fact). This makes the product non-viable as a game console.
*   **Value Proposition:** Higher price + worse ergonomics + smaller library = no reason to buy.

**Risks:** Publisher apathy, brand dilution.

The usability flaw alone is a fatal blocker. No amount of mobile market share compensates for a product that is difficult to use for both its intended purposes.

DECISION: NO-GO
