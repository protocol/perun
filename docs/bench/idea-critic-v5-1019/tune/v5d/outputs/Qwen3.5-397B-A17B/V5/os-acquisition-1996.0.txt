First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: acquiring a low-revenue OS with high migration risk and a contentious founder does not inherently beat licensing or internal rebuilds based on the facts provided.

Steelman: Buying this specific OS is the only viable path to instantly secure a modern, object-oriented core that internal teams failed to build, leveraging the founder's unique vision despite the cost.

Attack:
1. **Fit & Evidence:** The target has "sold few units," suggesting the technology lacks market validation. Paying "a few hundred million" for unproven tech with high integration risk contradicts sound capital allocation without a clear synergy plan.
2. **Alternatives:** Licensing offers a modern core without the baggage of a difficult founder or the cost of acquisition. "Do nothing" preserves cash while the failure of two internal attempts suggests a need for a different internal approach, not necessarily an external buy.
3. **Pre-mortem:** The integration fails because the founder's return reignites internal conflict, and the technical debt of migrating existing software proves too costly, leaving the company with two incompatible ecosystems and depleted reserves.
4. **Blockers:** The core claim relies on the acquired OS being superior enough to justify the price and risk. However, the facts state it has low sales and high migration risk. The "difficult history" of the founder is a stated negative that removes the route to smooth execution. No kill criteria or pilot cap is offered to mitigate the massive upfront cost.

The proposal asks for a massive, irreversible spend on an asset with poor market traction and high cultural/technical friction. The risks are not hypothetical; they are stated facts (failed internal attempts, difficult founder, low sales). Without a concrete plan to resolve the migration risk or the founder's integration, the deal fails the blocker test. The alternative of licensing or a structured partnership offers the tech upside without the existential cultural and financial risk.

Blockers: Stated facts show high cost, low asset validation, and guaranteed cultural friction that the plan does not address.
Risks: Total integration failure, capital depletion, developer exodus.

DECISION: NO-GO
