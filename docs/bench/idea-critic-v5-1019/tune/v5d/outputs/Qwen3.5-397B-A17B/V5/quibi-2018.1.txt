First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (mobile video) but fails to state an edge; "high budgets" and "A-list talent" are cost drivers, not differentiators in a market saturated with free, abundant short-form content.

Steelman: A premium, mobile-native studio can command subscriptions by delivering exclusive, high-fidelity narrative content that free platforms cannot replicate, leveraging vertical/horizontal tech to solve a specific UX pain point.

Attack:
1.  **Fit & Edge:** The core claim relies on users paying for what they currently get free. With "subscription fatigue" explicitly cited as a rising concern, the plan lacks a stated mechanism to overcome this inertia. High production costs increase the break-even point without proving higher willingness to pay.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or licensing content to existing giants (who already have the users) offers lower risk and immediate revenue. Building a new tech stack and content library from scratch for a "mobile-only" niche is the most expensive path with the highest customer acquisition cost.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: The target audience refuses to add another $15/month bill for 10-minute videos when TikTok/YouTube offer endless free alternatives. The "vertical/horizontal" feature is a hygiene factor, not a value proposition worth the subscription price.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no proven demand for premium short-form." Proceeding without a pilot or validated metric for paid conversion is speculative gambling, not operating.

Blockers:
-   **No Stated Edge:** The plan admits no proven demand while proposing a premium price point against free incumbents. High cost + unproven demand + fatigue = negative unit economics as proposed.
-   **Contradictory Logic:** Solving "subscription fatigue" by adding a costly new subscription service is logically inconsistent without a demonstrated unique value that forces migration.

Risks:
-   Massive capital burn ($1B+) before product-market fit is established.
-   Talent contracts becoming liabilities if the platform fails to gain traction.
-   Technology feature proving insufficient to drive switching behavior.

This is a classic "build it and they will come" fallacy scaled to billions. Without a pilot proving users will *pay* specifically for this format over free options, the capital commitment is unjustified.

DECISION: NO-GO
