First call: NO-GO. An unprofitable retailer inviting competitors to undercut its own inventory on the same page destroys its only path to margin without a verified mechanism to capture sufficient fee revenue or guarantee service quality.

**Steelman:** Aggregating third-party supply instantly expands selection and creates a high-margin fee stream, turning the site into a definitive marketplace before competitors do.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cannibalization vs. Margin:** The proposal admits used copies cannibalize new sales. For an unprofitable seller, losing a high-margin new sale to a low-fee used transaction is immediate value destruction unless the fee exceeds the lost gross profit. No math supports this.
2.  **Trust & Control:** Allowing competitors and unknown sellers on product pages introduces service variability. One bad actor damages the retailer's brand, which is the only asset retaining customers. The proposal lists "misbehave" as a risk but offers no enforcement mechanism, escrow, or performance bond.
3.  **Supplier Retaliation:** Publishers and suppliers may view this as channel conflict, potentially cutting off the primary inventory source. The proposal acknowledges this objection but offers no mitigation strategy.
4.  **No Edge:** The "edge" is selection, but at the cost of price erosion and brand risk. Without profitability, the company cannot afford a price war it facilitates.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Contradiction:** The core mechanic (competitors undercutting own prices) directly attacks the retailer's existing revenue stream without a proven model where fees compensate for lost gross profit.
*   **Unmitigated Brand Risk:** No mechanism exists to prevent service failures by third parties from destroying customer trust in the primary brand.

**Risks:** Supplier boycotts, race-to-the-bottom pricing, operational complexity of managing disputes.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot shows that third-party sales reduce total gross profit per ASIN (After Fee) compared to organic sales, or if customer support tickets related to third-party orders exceed 5% of volume, the program must terminate.

**Decision:** The proposal invites self-destruction by prioritizing selection over the unit economics required for survival. It lacks the controls to prevent brand damage and the financial modeling to prove fee revenue offsets cannibalization.

DECISION: NO-GO
