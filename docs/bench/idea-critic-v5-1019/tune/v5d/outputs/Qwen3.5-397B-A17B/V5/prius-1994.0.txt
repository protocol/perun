First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; cheap fuel and absent regulation remove the economic and compliance drivers, while unproven durability creates immediate liability without a compensating market pull.

**Steelman:** A bold first-mover commitment forces rapid resolution of battery and transmission unknowns, securing a defensible IP moat before competitors react, betting that regulatory or price shifts will occur before launch.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Edge:** With cheap fuel and no mandates, the customer value proposition is negative (higher cost, unproven reliability) versus incumbent internal combustion engines. "First" is not an edge if the product is economically irrational for the buyer.
2.  **Blocker (Evidence):** The proposal admits battery durability is "unproven" and warranty exposure is high. Committing to a fixed 1997 mass-production schedule before validating the core component's lifespan is a factual contradiction; you cannot guarantee volume delivery of a reliable product when the failure rate is unknown. This is not a risk; it is a broken plan.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "limited pilot" alternative captures the learning value without the catastrophic warranty exposure of mass production. A capped, time-boxed fleet test (e.g., 500 units) validates durability before locking the corporate schedule.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** By 1998, early units suffer massive battery failures. With no demand signal and cheap gas, recalls bankrupt the program, and the brand becomes synonymous with unreliability, ceding the future to competitors who waited for technology maturity.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** The plan commits to mass production despite admitting the core technology (battery) durability is unproven. The schedule assumes a solution to a problem not yet solved.
*   **Risks:** High cost, warranty blowout, zero demand. These are severe but secondary to the fundamental feasibility gap.

A sound operator does not bet the corporation on a fixed date for unproven physics without a demand signal. The plan must be revised to a capped pilot with explicit kill criteria based on durability data before a mass-production commitment is made.

DECISION: NO-GO
