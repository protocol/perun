First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (hands-free computing) but fails to state an edge: a specific fact showing this $1,500 device beats existing smartphones or cameras for everyday users today. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to refine the product with enthusiastic early adopters before a general release, mitigating initial social friction.

Attack: The stated facts contradict the path to "everyday consumer product." The risks raised are not merely potential; they are structural blockers confirmed by the proposal's own admission.
1. **No Clear Use:** The proposal explicitly states there is "no clear everyday use for most people." Without a defined utility that surpasses current tools, the product cannot sustain mass adoption regardless of price or exclusivity.
2. **Social & Privacy Friction:** A wearable camera facing public privacy objections and social acceptance issues creates a hostile environment for an "everyday" device. These are not solvable by an invitation-only program; they are fundamental to the form factor.
3. **Economic Mismatch:** A $1,500 price point for a device with "short battery life" and undefined daily utility offers negative value compared to established alternatives.

Alternatives:
- **Do Nothing:** Avoids reputational damage from a failed consumer launch and preserves R&D for enterprise or niche industrial applications where privacy/social norms differ.
- **Pivot to Enterprise:** Target specific verticals (medicine, logistics) where the high cost is justified by workflow efficiency and privacy concerns are manageable.
- **Delay:** Wait until battery technology improves and use cases are proven by third-party developers before attempting a consumer push.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, receives media backlash over privacy ("Glassholes"), fails to retain users due to lack of utility, and is discontinued within 18 months, damaging the brand's consumer trust.

Blockers: The proposal's own admission of "no clear everyday use" is a factual blocker. A product cannot succeed as an "everyday consumer product" if it lacks an everyday use case. The high price and social friction reinforce this failure mode.

Risks: Battery life, privacy regulation, manufacturing scale.

The plan fails the core requirement of product-market fit as described in its own text.

DECISION: NO-GO
