First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state a competitive edge; "new compact family car" without a stated advantage in cost, performance, or features cannot beat existing low-price rivals or justify a costly dedicated dealer network.

Steelman: A dedicated 1100cc compact leverages the new plant's capacity to capture family buyers underserved by current cheap small cars, using a specialized network to ensure brand focus.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" avoids the sunk cost of a bespoke dealer network. Selling through existing channels or sharing networks with rivals reduces fixed costs, directly addressing the "costly to build" risk. A cheaper alternative (using existing distribution) reaches the same upside (plant utilization) with lower risk.
2.  **Fit & Evidence:** The claim assumes buyers prefer this specific 1100cc offering over cheaper or larger options. No evidence supports this preference. The risk that buyers want "cheaper or larger" is a direct contradiction to the core value proposition, not just a side risk.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The project fails because the dedicated network drains cash before volume materializes, while rivals undercut price or offer more space. The plant runs below capacity because the product lacks a differentiator.
4.  **Blocker Test:** The proposal admits rivals sell small cars at low prices and buyers may prefer cheaper/larger options. Without a stated edge (e.g., superior fuel economy, unique styling, lower production cost per unit), the plan relies on hope rather than a defensible market position. The high fixed cost of the dealer network acts as a multiplier for any demand shortfall.

Blockers:
-   No stated edge against existing low-price rivals or substitute larger cars.
-   High fixed cost (dedicated network) exacerbates the risk of low adoption.

Risks:
-   Plant underutilization.
-   Channel conflict or inefficiency if dedicated network fails to generate volume.

The plan commits significant capital to distribution and production without a proven reason for customers to choose this specific car over established alternatives. It must beat "do nothing" or a lower-cost distribution strategy, which it currently does not.

DECISION: NO-GO
