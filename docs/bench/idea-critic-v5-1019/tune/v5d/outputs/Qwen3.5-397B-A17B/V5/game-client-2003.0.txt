First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge over the current retail model; it trades proven box sales for unproven digital friction without demonstrating why players would accept mandatory accounts in 2003's limited broadband environment.

Steelman: A mandatory client secures revenue via updates and anti-cheat while building a future platform monopoly.

Attack:
1. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves retail relationships and avoids alienating users without broadband. A "voluntary client" for updates achieves 80% of the benefit with 0% of the retail risk. The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" on immediate facts.
2. **Fit:** In 2003, home broadband is not universal. Making the client mandatory excludes a massive portion of the addressable market immediately, contradicting the goal of selling "very popular" games.
3. **Evidence:** The proposal admits players dislike mandatory accounts and large downloads. There is no evidence provided that this specific developer has enough leverage to force this behavior change without destroying sales volume.
4. **Pre-mortem:** The client launches with bugs (admitted risk); retail partners drop the games due to channel conflict; players with dial-up cannot play; sales collapse before the "store" vision ever matures.
5. **Blocker:** The core claim relies on forcing a behavioral shift (mandatory online) that the proposal itself states users resist and infrastructure may not support. Without a pilot, cap, or metric to prove users will comply, the plan fails as proposed. The risk of retail channel collapse is a factual threat, not a hypothetical.

Blockers:
- No stated edge proving mandatory adoption works despite user hostility and infrastructure gaps.
- Direct conflict with admitted user preferences and 2003 connectivity limits.
- Cheaper alternative (voluntary updater) exists but was not chosen.

Risks:
- Retail partner retaliation.
- Early client bugs destroying brand trust.
- Competitor copying (low barrier if the model works).

The proposal attempts a high-stakes, one-way door pivot based on assumptions rather than verified demand or infrastructure readiness. It blocks itself by ignoring the "do nothing" and "voluntary" baselines which offer safer paths to the same update/anti-cheat goals.

DECISION: NO-GO
