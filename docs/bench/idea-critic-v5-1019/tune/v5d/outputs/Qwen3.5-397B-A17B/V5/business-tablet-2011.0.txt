First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; matching a rival's price with a smaller screen, untested OS, and missing core business features (email/calendar) offers no defensible advantage to the target business segment.

**Steelman:** A 7-inch device leverages portability to create a new category, using phone-pairing to bypass initial OS immaturity while relying on future app growth.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Edge:** Business users prioritize productivity. Shipping without native email/calendar contradicts the company's core strength and value proposition. Pairing dependency creates friction competitors don't impose.
2.  **Pricing Failure:** Matching the price of a superior (larger screen, mature OS, full app store) product is not a strategy; it is a discount without the price cut.
3.  **Untested Risk:** The new OS is unproven. Relying on developers to commit to a niche, fragmented ecosystem (phone-dependent) against an entrenched rival is speculative, not strategic.
4.  **Better Way:** "Do nothing" preserves brand reputation. A "cheaper alternative" would be pricing significantly below the rival to offset hardware/software deficits, which this proposal explicitly rejects.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, reviewers pan the lack of native email and high price, business customers reject it as a "toy," and the new OS fails to gain developer traction due to low install base. The company loses money and brand equity.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Price matches rival's larger, more capable tablet.
*   **Fact:** Core business features (email) are missing unless paired.
*   **Fact:** No stated advantage over the rival's large app store and mature platform.

**Risks:** Developer abandonment, brand dilution, inventory write-downs. These are secondary to the fundamental lack of value proposition.

**Conclusion:** The plan fails the basic test of offering a reason for a customer to switch. It attacks the leader with inferior specs, higher friction, and equal cost. There is no "stated edge" in cost, product, or timing. The absence of native email for a "business email" leader is a self-inflicted wound that no amount of future potential fixes today.

DECISION: NO-GO
