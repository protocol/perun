First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge proving this specific executive's electronics success translates to a coupon-dependent department store demographic; need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Eliminating coupons and rebuilding stores will attract new, higher-margin shoppers faster than it alienates the legacy base, leveraging the CEO's unique branding success to reverse sales declines immediately.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Contradictory Evidence:** The proposal admits most sales historically come from discounted items and customers are "trained to wait." Removing the primary purchase trigger without proof of replacement demand guarantees an immediate revenue collapse.
2.  **Missing Edge:** Success in branded electronics does not constitute a stated edge for mid-market apparel/home goods where price sensitivity is the defined core behavior. The plan assumes transferable loyalty without evidence.
3.  **Fatal Execution Risk:** Rolling out a total model change across the entire chain simultaneously, with no pilot, removes the ability to detect failure before it becomes irreversible. Given the "falling sales" context, a failed chain-wide launch likely triggers insolvency before correction is possible.

**Risks:**
*   Legacy customers churn before new demographics are acquired.
*   Capital exhaustion from rebuilding stores before ROI is proven.
*   Competitors undercutting the new "everyday" prices while the chain is locked into a high-cost remodel.

**Analysis:**
The pre-mortem is clear: sales plummet as coupon users stop buying, new customers do not arrive in sufficient volume to offset the loss, and the chain runs out of cash mid-renovation. The cost of delay (waiting for a pilot) is negligible compared to the cost of a chain-wide failure. A cheaper, safer alternative exists: test the pricing and shop-in-shop concept in 5-10 diverse stores for two quarters. This plan rejects that in favor of a "big bang" approach that contradicts the stated historical data of its own customer base. The absence of a pilot is not a mere missing metric; in this context, it is a blocker because the stated facts (coupon dependence) predict immediate failure upon implementation.

DECISION: NO-GO
