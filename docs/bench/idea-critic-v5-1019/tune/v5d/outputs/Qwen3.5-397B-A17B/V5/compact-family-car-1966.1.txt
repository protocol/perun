First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state a competitive edge; "new plant" and "dedicated network" are costs, not advantages over existing low-price rivals.

Steelman: A dedicated 1100cc compact captures a specific family segment willing to pay for quality unavailable in current cheap rivals, utilizing the new plant efficiently.

Attack:
1. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" avoids the massive fixed cost of a new dealer network. A "cheaper path" exists: sell through existing networks or partner with current dealers to test demand before building dedicated infrastructure. The proposal ignores this lower-risk entry.
2. **Fit & Capacity:** Running a new plant near capacity requires immediate, high-volume sales. With rivals already dominating the low-price small car market, assuming instant uptake for a new, unproven model is speculative. If buyers prefer cheaper or larger cars (as the risk assessment admits), the plant becomes a financial anchor.
3. **Evidence Gap:** There is no data showing buyers want *this* specific 1100cc configuration over existing options. The "dedicated network" assumption presumes success before validating demand.

Pre-mortem: The plant opens, but the dedicated network fails to attract enough volume because customers stick to known brands or choose larger vehicles. The high fixed costs of both the plant and the new network drain cash, forcing a shutdown.

Blockers:
- **No Stated Edge:** The proposal offers no reason why this car beats established low-price rivals other than existence. Need is not an edge.
- **Costly Assumption:** Building a dedicated dealer network before proving product-market fit violates basic capital efficiency; it bets the company on an unverified hypothesis.

Risks:
- Preference for cheaper/larger cars (stated).
- Inability to fill plant capacity (stated).
- High burn rate from new network construction.

The plan commits to maximum fixed costs (plant + network) without a pilot or a proven differentiator. It must beat "do nothing" or a staged rollout using existing channels. It does not.

DECISION: NO-GO
