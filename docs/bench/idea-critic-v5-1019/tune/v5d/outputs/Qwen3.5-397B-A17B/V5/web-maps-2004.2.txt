First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state an edge; "free" is not an advantage without a defined payer, and slow browsers plus high licensing costs suggest immediate technical and financial failure against incumbents.

Steelman: A free, browser-based map with satellite imagery and routing will displace desktop tools and capture users from established sites by leveraging the acquirer's search dominance to offset licensing costs.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** High cost (licensing), high risk (browser performance, no revenue), low reversibility (brand damage if slow).
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, zero risk, maintains desktop revenue.
3. **Hybrid:** Keep desktop for heavy users, add static web maps only. Cheaper, lower risk, but misses the "interactive" upside.

Fit: Assumes the goal is user acquisition via search integration. If the north star is immediate profitability, this fails.

Pre-mortem: The application loads too slowly in IE6, users abandon it for faster desktop alternatives, and licensing fees bleed cash with no ad revenue to cover them. This is a one-way door for reputation; delay costs market share to faster-moving rivals.

Evidence: All figures are assumed. Browser speed limits and licensing costs are stated as facts in the proposal. No revenue model is cited.

Blockers:
1. **No Revenue Model:** The proposal explicitly states "no direct revenue model" while admitting "costly" licensing. Without a stated mechanism to convert search traffic to profit, the math fails immediately.
2. **Technical Feasibility:** The proposal admits browsers are "slow at interactive graphics." If the core value (interactive maps) cannot be delivered smoothly, the product has no value proposition.

Risks: Incumbent dominance, high burn rate, user adoption friction.

The absence of a revenue model to cover known high costs is a factual blocker, not a risk. The technical limitation of browsers in 2004 contradicts the requirement for "interactive" performance unless a specific breakthrough is cited (none is). A cheaper alternative (static maps) exists but does not meet the full claim; however, the primary claim fails on its own financial and technical terms.

DECISION: NO-GO
