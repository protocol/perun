First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: paying a 100x revenue multiple (since revenue is zero) for an unproven business model on a single platform does not mathematically beat building or buying cheaper alternatives based on the facts provided.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the sole mobile threat neutralizes a existential risk to the core business pre-IPO, buying immediate distribution and talent that cannot be replicated internally fast enough.

**Attack:**
1.  **Valuation Blocker:** The $1B price is purely speculative. With zero revenue and no business model, the cost of failure is total loss of capital. A "cheaper alternative" exists: building a competitor or acquiring a smaller player costs a fraction of this. The proposal admits the buyer's mobile product is weak; throwing $1B at a symptom rather than fixing the core engineering capability is a strategic band-aid.
2.  **Execution Risk:** Integrating a dozen-person culture into a massive public entity frequently destroys the very value being purchased (talent flight). The proposal admits this risk but offers no mitigation.
3.  **Regulatory & Timing:** Doing this immediately before an IPO invites intense regulatory scrutiny that could delay or derail the public listing, the primary financial goal.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, the founders leave within six months due to culture clash, the user base migrates to the next new platform, and the company is left with a $1B write-down just as shareholders sue over IPO prospectus omissions regarding mobile strategy.

**Evidence Check:** The claim of "dominant threat" relies on current momentum, not defensible moat. No evidence suggests this app has a monetization path superior to what the buyer could build. The "edge" is fear, not economic advantage.

**Blockers:**
*   **Valuation:** $1B for $0 revenue with no defined monetization model fails basic capital allocation logic.
*   **Strategic Fit:** Buying a feature (photos) rather than a platform does not solve the underlying mobile product weakness.

**Risks:** Culture destruction, regulatory delay of IPO, total capital loss.

**Kill Criteria:** If the app fails to monetize within 18 months or loses 20% of DAU post-acquisition, the project must be shuttered.

The absence of a business model and the extreme valuation relative to any tangible asset constitute a factual failure of the proposal as stated.

DECISION: NO-GO
