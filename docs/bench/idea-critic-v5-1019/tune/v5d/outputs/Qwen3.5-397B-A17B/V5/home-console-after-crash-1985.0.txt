First call: NO-GO. The stated facts describe a collapsed market where retailers refuse to stock video games; launching a console, even rebranded, directly confronts a verified distribution blockade without a guaranteed mechanism to force shelf space.

Steelman: Repositioning the hardware as a toy robot bundle with a stock buy-back guarantee bypasses retailer skepticism and the "passed fad" stigma, while the lockout chip ensures quality control to prevent another crash.

However, the attack reveals a fatal blocker in the economics of the proposal as stated. The "take-back promise" combined with "retailers clearing stock" creates an unlimited liability scenario. If the product fails to gain traction—which is the prevailing market condition—the company faces massive returns with no revenue offset, potentially bankrupting the venture before it establishes a foothold. The proposal relies on the assumption that the robot/light gun disguise is sufficient to overcome the "video game" stigma, but this is unproven. Furthermore, limiting outside publishers via a lockout chip in a new market with "no console track record" reduces the software library depth, making the hardware less attractive compared to "cheap home computers" that offer open development and broader utility.

The alternative of "do nothing" or waiting for the market to heal carries zero financial exposure. A cheaper, safer alternative would be a limited regional test without the unlimited buy-back clause, or licensing the technology to an established American toy manufacturer who already has shelf space, rather than bearing the full risk of direct distribution.

The plan fails the blocker test: the combination of a saturated clearance market, an unproven rebranding strategy, and an uncapped financial liability (the buy-back promise) means the numbers do not work as proposed. The risk of total loss outweighs the potential upside given the stated market collapse.

Blockers: Unlimited liability from stock buy-backs in a collapsing market; no proven method to force retailer adoption despite rebranding.
Risks: Developer alienation due to lockout chip; competition from home computers.

DECISION: NO-GO
