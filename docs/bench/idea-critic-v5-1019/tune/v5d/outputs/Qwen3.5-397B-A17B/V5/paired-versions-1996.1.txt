First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; a declining platform and split content without proven demand is a financial liability, not a strategy.

**Steelman:** Splitting the game forces social interaction via hardware links, creating a viral loop that offsets the aging console's decline by making the software a social necessity rather than a solitary toy.

**Attack:**
1.  **Platform Decay:** Releasing on a dying handheld caps the total addressable market. If the user base shrinks faster than the viral loop grows, the trading mechanic fails due to a lack of partners, rendering the "complete the collection" goal impossible and frustrating users.
2.  **Consumer Backlash:** Selling two incomplete versions looks like a price grab. Without a strong brand, this triggers negative sentiment, reducing the initial sales velocity needed to sustain the ecosystem.
3.  **Friction:** The requirement for a second player and a physical cable creates a high barrier to entry. If early adopters cannot find trading partners immediately, word-of-mouth turns negative.
4.  **Publisher Confidence:** The publisher's uncertainty is a blocker. Launching a complex, dual-SKU strategy requires full marketing commitment to explain the value proposition; half-hearted support guarantees failure.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for a newer platform or release a single complete version to maximize satisfaction on the current install base.
*   **Single Version + Online/Local Hub:** Release one game and facilitate trading through a centralized mechanism or simpler local play, reducing consumer confusion.

**Pre-mortem:** The game launches, sales are mediocre due to the "cash grab" perception. Players buy one version, realize they cannot finish the game without finding a specific friend with the other version and a cable, then abandon the game. The publisher loses money on doubled manufacturing and marketing costs for two SKUs.

**Blockers:**
*   No evidence that the remaining user base is dense enough to support mandatory trading.
*   Publisher uncertainty indicates a lack of resources to educate the market on the dual-version mechanic.
*   High friction (cable + second player) on a declining platform creates a likely deadlock.

**Risks:** Brand damage, inventory bloat, player frustration.

DECISION: NO-GO
