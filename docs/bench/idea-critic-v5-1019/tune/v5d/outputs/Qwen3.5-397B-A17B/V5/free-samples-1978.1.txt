First call: NO-GO. The initial four-city test demonstrated little demand, indicating the product lacks a stated edge over existing solutions; renaming and sampling do not address the core lack of utility.

Steelman: Rebranding and targeted free sampling in a single mid-sized city could uncover a specific use case among office workers that broad testing missed, providing low-risk validation before a national spend.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** Rename and sample one city. Cost: Moderate (samples + rebrand). Risk: High (ignores prior failure). Reversibility: Medium (brand confusion).
2. **Do Nothing:** Halt rollout. Cost: Zero. Risk: Lost potential revenue. Reversibility: High (can restart later).
3. **Pivot Application:** Seek industrial/non-office uses for the adhesive. Cost: Low (research). Risk: Medium (new market). Reversibility: High.

Fit: The proposal assumes office workers need re-positionable notes, contradicting the primary evidence of "disappointing sales" and "little demand." A rename does not create utility where none was perceived.

Pre-mortem: The rollout fails because the product solves a non-existent problem for office workers. The "rename" creates confusion without adding value, and the "free samples" are discarded as clutter. This is a two-way door, but the cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a failed re-launch.

Evidence: The only hard data is the failed four-city test showing little demand. The proposal offers no new evidence that user needs have changed or that a name change alters utility. The assumption that "most recipients would say they would buy" is speculative and historically unsupported by the initial test.

Blockers:
- **Contradictory Evidence:** The core claim (national rollout potential) is directly contradicted by the stated fact of "little demand" in the initial test. A rename does not fix a functional mismatch.
- **False Premise:** The plan relies on stated intent ("say they would buy") rather than actual purchase behavior, which already failed.

Risks: Wasting budget on samples; reinforcing a negative brand perception; delaying a necessary pivot to a viable use case.

The plan attempts to solve a product-market fit failure with marketing tactics. Without a demonstrated change in product utility or user need, the outcome will mirror the initial failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
