First call: NO-GO. The proposal cites a competitive reaction ("the search leader just agreed to buy another platform") as the primary edge, rather than a standalone product, cost, or access advantage that beats current market offerings. Need alone is not an edge; paying a large premium in cash for a fast-changing technology without a stated defensible moat is a strategic vulnerability, not a lead.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform immediately closes the gap with the search leader, preventing permanent exclusion from the display market before it matures.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or building organic capability avoids a $6B cash outflow and integration risk in a volatile sector. A cheaper, smaller partnership could test the waters without the "large premium" burn.
2.  **Fit & Evidence:** The "fast-changing technology market" contradicts locking capital into a specific platform at a peak price. The proposal admits "conflicts with existing agency customers," introducing immediate revenue churn risk that offsets the acquisition's value.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months: The integration alienated key agency clients, the technology became obsolete due to market shifts, and the $6B cash reserve (needed for other R&D) was depleted. The "large premium" leaves no margin for error.
4.  **Blocker Test:** The proposal explicitly states the technology market is "fast-changing" and the price is a "large premium." Buying a depreciating asset at a peak price with known customer conflicts constitutes a factual failure of the core investment thesis as presented. The reactive motive (chasing a competitor) is not a valid economic edge.

**Blockers:**
*   Paying a "large premium" for an asset in a "fast-changing" market without a stated technological moat creates immediate negative expected value.
*   Known "conflicts with existing agency customers" guarantees revenue loss upon integration, undermining the acquisition's ROI.

**Risks:**
*   Cultural clash and talent flight post-acquisition.
*   Rapid obsolescence of the ad-serving tech.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   Metric: Retention of 90% of acquired platform's top 20% of advertisers within 12 months.
*   Date: May 2008.
*   Action: Divestiture if retention <80%.

**Decision:** The reactive nature, premium pricing in a volatile market, and admitted customer conflicts form a factual blocker to approval as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
