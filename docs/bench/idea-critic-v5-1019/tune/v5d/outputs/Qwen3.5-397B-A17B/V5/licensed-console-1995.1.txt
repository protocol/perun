First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; entering a dominated market with low royalties, no marketing control, and weak brand linkage offers no defensible advantage over doing nothing.

**Steelman:** Licensing leverages existing IP to enter the consumer market with minimal capital risk, betting on the licensee's distribution to overcome incumbents.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital and brand integrity. A direct, controlled launch (though costlier) ensures brand alignment and marketing intensity. The proposed path cedes control of the primary success driver (marketing) to a partner with misaligned incentives (toy volume vs. platform ecosystem).
2.  **Fit:** A "small royalty" model contradicts the need for a breakout consumer hit to shift market share. It caps upside while exposing the core brand to failure via a third party.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sells moderately due to low price, but fails to build a loyal user base or brand equity. The licensee moves to the next toy trend; the computer maker is stuck with an orphaned platform association, having earned negligible profit while diluting its professional brand.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits established makers dominate and the licensee may not market hard. These are not risks to be managed; they are stated facts that invalidate the strategy. No evidence suggests the toy company can disrupt console giants without aggressive, aligned marketing.
5.  **Blockers:** The core claim fails because the stated terms (low royalty, no marketing control) guarantee a outcome worse than "do nothing." The plan relies on a partner doing what they are explicitly flagged as unlikely to do (market hard).

**Blockers:**
*   Stated fact: Licensee may not market hard, yet marketing is left entirely to them.
*   Stated fact: Royalties are small, offering no significant upside to justify brand risk.
*   Stated fact: Market is dominated; no edge provided to displace incumbents.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, opportunity cost of capital, failure to establish consumer foothold.

The proposal asks to bet on a weak partner in a hard market with capped rewards. It beats neither the status quo nor a focused direct strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
