First call: NO-GO. A loss-making manufacturer with single-digit share proposing high-fixed-cost retail in a low-margin sector during a recession lacks a stated edge; need for demonstration does not equal a viable business model.

Steelman: Direct retail is the only way to control the customer experience and demonstrate a complex digital-music strategy that third-party staff actively undermine.

Attack: The proposal ignores the "fixed cost trap." Leases are long-term liabilities; sales are cyclical. With recent losses, the company cannot absorb the cash burn of prime mall locations if adoption lags. The "poor industry track record" is not just a risk but a leading indicator of structural failure for this model. Channel conflict may trigger retaliatory delisting by current resellers, causing an immediate revenue cliff before new stores mature. The "digital-music strategy" is an unproven hypothesis; betting the company's liquidity on it via real estate is a one-way door with catastrophic downside.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve cash, accept suboptimal demos.
2. Shop-in-shop kiosks: Low-cost demos inside existing retailers, aligning incentives rather than fighting them.
3. Online configuration + limited demo units: Reduce inventory risk while maintaining customization.

Fit: A recessionary environment demands variable cost structures, not massive fixed overhead. This plan contradicts basic survival logic for a struggling firm.

Pre-mortem: Two years later, the company is bankrupt. Cause: $500M in lease obligations and inventory write-downs while sales missed targets by 40% due to the recession. The digital strategy failed to gain traction, and resellers dropped the brand due to channel conflict.

Blockers:
1. Financial incapacity: Recent losses + high lease costs + recession = high probability of insolvency.
2. Strategic mismatch: Low-margin hardware cannot support high-cost retail without massive volume or ecosystem lock-in (not yet proven).
3. Channel suicide: Alienating current primary sales channel without a guaranteed replacement.

Risks: Inventory obsolescence, brand dilution if stores feel empty, execution failure.

The plan commits real money without a pilot, cap, or proven unit economics. It bets the firm on a fixed-cost model in a downturn.

DECISION: NO-GO
