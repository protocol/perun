First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it offers a high-cost data plan to price-sensitive youth while removing the primary value drivers of a smartphone (apps and games), making it inferior to existing cheap smartphones or feature phones.

Steelman: The only defensible version is that this device serves as a tightly controlled, carrier-subsidized gateway to a specific social network, where the lack of distractions (no games/apps) is the selling point. However, the proposal does not claim this differentiation, nor does it explain why the cost barrier (data plan equal to a smartphone) is acceptable to the target demographic.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids channel conflict with the main platform launching later and prevents internal team friction.
2. Software-only approach: Launch the social network as an app on existing low-cost phones, removing the hardware and data plan barrier entirely.
3. Revised hardware: A Wi-Fi-only device or one with a minimal data tier, acknowledging the price sensitivity.

Fit: This contradicts the likely north star of user growth for young people by introducing massive friction (cost) and removing utility (no apps). It also risks cannibalizing or confusing the market ahead of the main platform launch.

Pre-mortem: The project fails because parents refuse to pay smartphone data rates for a limited device, and teens reject a phone with no games or apps. The internal conflict escalates as the delayed project drags resources away from the main platform launch.

Blockers:
1. Economic mismatch: The cost of the required data plan equals a full smartphone, yet the device offers significantly less utility (no apps/games). This makes the value proposition negative for price-sensitive buyers.
2. Strategic conflict: Launching a limited, delayed product immediately before a major full-platform launch creates market confusion and internal resource contention.

Risks: High probability of channel conflict, brand dilution, and failure to gain traction due to the "walled garden" limitation without a corresponding price advantage.

The proposal fails the basic economic test for its target audience and introduces unnecessary strategic risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
