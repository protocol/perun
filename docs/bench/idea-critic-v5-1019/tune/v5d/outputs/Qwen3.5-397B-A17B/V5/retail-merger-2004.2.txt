First call: NO-GO. The proposal offers no stated edge; it combines two declining businesses with different customers, uncertain savings, and superior rivals, creating a larger distressed entity rather than a turnaround.

Steelman: Acquiring the larger chain provides immediate scale and brand assets that, if integrated rapidly, could stabilize the discount chain's weak sales before competitors dominate the market.

Attack: The core claim fails on the stated facts. Both entities suffer declining sales and underinvestment; merging them aggregates distress rather than curing it. The "edge" of selling older brands in discount stores assumes customer overlap that the proposal explicitly states does not exist ("customers differ"). Cost savings are admitted as "uncertain," removing the only financial justification for a merger of this magnitude. With big-box rivals growing, the combined entity faces stronger competition while distracted by complex integration of incompatible formats and corporate functions. The plan lacks a mechanism to reverse sales declines or fund necessary store upgrades post-restructuring.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve cash, focus on organic turnaround of the restructured discount chain. Lower risk, avoids integration chaos.
2. Targeted asset purchase: Buy only specific locations or brands from the department chain, avoiding the liability of its entire declining operation. Cheaper, reversible.
3. Strategic partnership: Test brand cross-selling without full merger. Validates customer fit before capital commitment.

Pre-mortem: The merger consumes cash reserves on integration costs while sales continue to drop due to format confusion and neglected stores. The distinct customer bases reject the combined offering, leading to a second insolvency within 24 months. This is a one-way door with high cost of delay only if a specific asset is about to be lost to a rival, which is not stated.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: Combining two declining sales curves with different customers yields no competitive advantage against growing big-box rivals.
2. Contradictory logic: The primary synergy (selling brands in discount stores) is invalidated by the stated fact that customers differ.
3. Uncertain economics: Admission that cost savings are "uncertain" removes the financial basis for the deal.

Risks: Integration failure, brand dilution, capital exhaustion, accelerated decline.

DECISION: NO-GO
