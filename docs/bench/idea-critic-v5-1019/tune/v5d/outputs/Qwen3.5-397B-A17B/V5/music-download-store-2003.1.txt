First call: GO. The stated edge is leveraging a high-margin, loyal hardware install base to solve a critical usability gap (legal, reliable music) that free file-sharing fails to provide due to complexity and legal risk.

**Steelman:** A curated, legal store integrated directly into the dominant portable player creates a seamless "just works" ecosystem that free alternatives cannot match, driving hardware sales while monetizing software.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Skeptic:** Margins are thin, and platform lock-in limits initial reach. However, the primary goal is hardware differentiation, not immediate store profit. Free sharing lacks reliability and legality; subscriptions (the alternative) suffer from ownership anxiety in this era.
*   **Better-way:** "Do nothing" cedes the user experience to piracy, eroding hardware value. Subscriptions are a valid alternative but conflict with the user desire to own and transfer files to the specific device. The per-song model aligns perfectly with the existing "album" mental model but at lower friction.
*   **Fit:** This moves the north star of "1,000 songs in your pocket" from a technical feat to a legal reality.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if labels withhold key catalogs or if the copy protection alienates users. However, the proposal secures licenses first. The risk of rivals choosing subscriptions is actually an advantage; it fragments the market while this plan offers a unified ownership model.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The facts state the player is "selling well," proving demand. The "widespread" nature of file-sharing confirms the market need but highlights the legal void this fills. No factual blocker exists: the margin concern is offset by the halo effect on hardware sales (the real profit center). The platform restriction is a feature, not a bug, for ecosystem locking.

**Decision:** The plan beats "do nothing" and the subscription alternative by offering immediate ownership compatible with the leading hardware. Risks are manageable execution details, not fundamental flaws. The kill criterion is clear: if major labels refuse licensing within the negotiation window, pivot. Until then, the integrated ecosystem is the only viable path to legitimize digital music for this user base.

DECISION: GO
