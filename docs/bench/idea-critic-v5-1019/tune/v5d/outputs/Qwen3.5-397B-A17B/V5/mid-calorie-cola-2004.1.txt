First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it assumes a mid-point preference that the risks explicitly contradict, offering no evidence that "half-sugar" beats existing diet options or justifies a premium price.

Steelman: A mid-calorie cola captures young men who reject the "diet" label but want fewer carbs than regular soda, creating a new category before rivals do.

Attack: The core assumption—that a distinct segment wants exactly half-sugar—is unproven and directly challenged by the risk that dieters already have a solution (diet cola) and regular drinkers want full sugar. The "half-sugar" position risks satisfying neither group (the "straddling" failure). The premium price further reduces trial likelihood for an unproven taste profile. With a rival already entering the space and the low-carb trend potentially fleeting, the cost of delay is low compared to the high risk of a failed national launch. The proposal admits the trend may be a fad yet commits to a large campaign anyway.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids sunk costs if the trend fades; preserves brand equity.
2. Limited regional test: Validates the "young male" demographic and price sensitivity before a national campaign.
3. Reformulate existing brand: Cheaper than a new launch, though carries brand dilution risk.

Fit: Assumes the goal is growth via trend-capturing; however, without proof of demand, this misaligns with capital preservation.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, young men try it once due to marketing, find the taste unsatisfying compared to regular or the stigma of diet remains, sales flatten, and the inventory is written off as the low-carb trend collapses. This is a two-way door only if the initial spend is capped; the proposed "large campaign" makes it effectively one-way.

Blockers:
- No stated edge: The proposal offers no fact showing why half-sugar wins over diet or regular.
- Contradictory evidence: The proposal's own risks admit the target audience may not exist.

Risks: Trend volatility, cannibalization, price resistance.

Kill criteria: None proposed. A pilot with a <15% repeat purchase rate among young men should trigger an immediate stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
