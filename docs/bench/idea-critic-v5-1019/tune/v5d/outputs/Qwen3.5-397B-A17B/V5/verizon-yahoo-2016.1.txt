First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; acquiring a second declining asset does not create a competitive advantage against dominant incumbents.

**Steelman:** Combining two struggling legacy media portfolios creates sufficient scale to pivot effectively into mobile advertising and challenge market leaders.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Edge:** The proposal admits the target's traffic and revenue are falling, management is unstable, and the buyer lacks consumer media experience. Buying decline does not fix decline; it compounds it. There is no stated mechanism (technology, talent, or unique data) explaining why this specific combination succeeds where the target failed alone.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. Investing the $4.8 billion directly into mobile infrastructure or acquiring a growing mobile-native entity offers a clearer path to the stated goal without the drag of legacy decay.
3.  **Fit:** The strategy contradicts the "north star" of building a scalable mobile business. You cannot scale a shrinking foundation. The carrier's core competency (wireless) does not naturally extend to fixing broken media management.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Integration fails because the carrier imposes wireless culture on unstable media teams, accelerating talent flight. Ad revenue continues to slide post-merger, leaving the carrier with $4.8 billion in stranded assets and no mobile ad scale.
5.  **Evidence:** The facts provided (falling traffic, falling revenue, repeated management changes, no buyer experience) directly contradict the core claim that this acquisition will build a competitive business. The numbers show a value trap, not a turnaround opportunity.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal offers no fact showing how combining two failing businesses beats the status quo or competitors. Need without an edge is a NO-GO.
*   **Contradictory Evidence:** The target's verified decline and the buyer's inexperience remove the route to the stated upside.

**Risks:** Integration complexity, continued revenue erosion, culture clash.

This is a classic "fallacy of scale." Without a specific turnaround mechanism or unique asset, this is buying a problem, not a solution. The absence of a pilot, cap, or metric is secondary to the fundamental flaw: the plan fails on its own stated facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
