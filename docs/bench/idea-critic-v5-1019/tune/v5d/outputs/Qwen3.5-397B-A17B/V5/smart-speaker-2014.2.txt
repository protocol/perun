First call: NO-GO. The proposal cites a recent hardware failure and a non-existent category without stating a unique edge over existing phone assistants; need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: A dedicated, always-listening cylinder offers superior audio and frictionless shopping integration that phones cannot match, leveraging membership pricing to overcome hardware skepticism.

Attack: The core claim relies on customers accepting an always-on microphone despite explicit privacy risks and poor prior hardware performance. The "invitation only" launch limits scale needed to fix the acknowledged voice recognition failures. Phone-based assistants already solve the stated use cases (music, questions, lists) without new hardware costs or privacy intrusions. The $99 member price masks a likely loss leader strategy without a defined path to profitability beyond vague "shopping list" additions.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids repeating the recent hardware failure and preserves capital.
2. Improve phone app: Enhance existing mobile assistant features at a fraction of the hardware cost and risk.
3. Partner with existing speaker makers: Integrate software into established hardware rather than manufacturing a new form factor.

Fit: Assumes a strategy of hardware ecosystem expansion, but contradicts the immediate reality of a failed phone launch and low consumer trust.

Pre-mortem: The product fails because privacy concerns stall adoption, voice accuracy frustrates early users, and the niche form factor proves unnecessary given smartphone ubiquity. This is a one-way door with high inventory risk. Cost of delay is low; waiting allows voice tech to mature and privacy norms to clarify.

Evidence: The proposal admits the last hardware launch went badly, voice recognition often fails, and no category exists. These are stated facts, not assumptions. No evidence suggests customers want this specific form factor or will trust an always-on mic from a company with a recent hardware miss.

Blockers:
1. Recent hardware failure directly contradicts the capability to execute this launch successfully now.
2. No stated edge over free, existing phone-based assistants for the core features.
3. Privacy risk (always-on mic) is a known negative with no mitigation plan provided.

Risks: Category creation failure, voice accuracy issues, inventory overhang.

The plan fails the blocker test: it repeats a recent failure mode without a demonstrated advantage over the status quo.

DECISION: NO-GO
