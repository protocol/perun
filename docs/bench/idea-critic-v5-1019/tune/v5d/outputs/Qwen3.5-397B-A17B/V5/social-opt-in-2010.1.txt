First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; automatic enrollment and default visibility are distribution tactics, not product advantages over existing social networks, and need without an edge is insufficient.

Steelman: Leveraging the existing user base and inbox context creates immediate network density and utility that standalone social networks cannot match, justifying default visibility to overcome cold-start problems.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High risk of regulatory backlash and user churn due to privacy violations; low reversibility once data is exposed.
2. **Do Nothing:** Preserves trust and regulatory standing; forfeits potential social engagement upside.
3. **Opt-In Pilot:** Lower user acquisition speed but validates demand without triggering privacy alarms; fully reversible.

Fit: Assuming the north star is user growth and engagement, violating core trust (privacy) to achieve it is a strategic mismatch for a communication utility.

Pre-mortem: The feature launches, users immediately revolt over unexpected visibility, regulators launch investigations into data practices, and the company is forced to disable the feature, leaving a permanent stain on brand trust. This is a one-way door for reputation; financial costs are reversible, trust is not. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a forced rollback.

Evidence: The proposal admits risks regarding privacy disregard and regulatory attention as probable outcomes of the *method* (automatic enrollment), not just possibilities. No evidence suggests users want social features in email, only that they exist.

Blockers:
1. **Method Contradiction:** The proposed mechanism (automatic enrollment with default visibility) directly triggers the admitted primary risks (privacy violation, regulatory scrutiny). The plan relies on the very actions that constitute its failure mode.
2. **No Stated Edge:** Distribution is not differentiation. Without proof that users prefer social networking inside email, the plan assumes value where none is proven.

Risks: Low adoption, brand damage, legal fines.

The plan fails because its execution strategy guarantees the negative outcomes it acknowledges. A valid approach would require an opt-in model to test demand before exposing user data. As proposed, it is a bet on regulatory leniency and user apathy rather than product value.

DECISION: NO-GO
