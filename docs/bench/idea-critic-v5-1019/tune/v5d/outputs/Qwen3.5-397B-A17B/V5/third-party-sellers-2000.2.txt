First call: NO-GO. An unprofitable retailer inviting competitors to undercut its own inventory on the same page sacrifices its only path to margin without a verified mechanism to capture sufficient fee revenue to offset the loss of direct sales control and brand trust.

**Steelman:** Transforming from a fragile retailer into a high-margin marketplace platform creates a defensible moat where revenue scales via fees rather than inventory risk, forcing immediate liquidity even if it cannibalizes low-margin direct sales.

**Attack:** The proposal assumes the fee per sale will exceed the margin lost when a customer chooses a cheaper third-party used or competitor item over the retailer's own new stock. For an unprofitable entity, this is a fatal cash-flow gamble. If the fee (e.g., 15%) is lower than the retailer's gross margin on new items (e.g., 30%), every converted sale to a third party reduces total contribution margin. Furthermore, "customer trust" is the sole asset of an unprofitable online store; allowing unknown sellers to fulfill orders introduces uncontrolled variance in delivery and quality that can permanently sever customer retention before profitability is reached. The risk of supplier retaliation (cutting off inventory) is immediate and existential for a company that still relies on its own stock for the majority of revenue. There is no stated "edge" proving this specific timing works better than waiting for positive cash flow to absorb the transition shock.

**Blockers:**
1. **Margin Dilution:** No evidence provided that fee revenue > lost gross margin from cannibalization. For an unprofitable firm, negative contribution growth is a hard stop.
2. **Existential Supply Risk:** Suppliers may delist the retailer immediately upon seeing competitors on their product pages, starving the core business.
3. **Trust Asymmetry:** One bad third-party experience can destroy the brand equity required to survive until profitability.

**Risks:** Execution complexity, price wars, regulatory scrutiny.

**Decision:** The plan fails the basic unit economics test for an unprofitable entity and introduces immediate existential threats to supply and brand without a guaranteed offsetting upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
