First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; needing demonstration does not prove owned retail beats cheaper alternatives, especially given the company's losses and the industry's poor retail track record.

**Steelman:** Owned stores are the only way to control the customer experience enough to sell a premium music strategy, justifying high fixed costs despite losses.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** The claim fails to beat "do nothing" or cheaper options. Dedicated kiosks inside existing retailers, staff training incentives, or pop-up demo zones achieve product demonstration without multi-year prime mall leases. These preserve capital during a recession.
2.  **Fit & Evidence:** A company with 3–5% share and recent losses cannot absorb the high fixed costs of prime real estate if sales dip. The proposal admits low industry margins and high lease costs but offers no corrected ROI showing profitability at current volume.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: A recession hits six months post-launch. Foot traffic drops, but lease obligations remain. Cash burn accelerates, forcing bankruptcy or a fire sale. The "digital music strategy" is abandoned because the hardware vehicle sank the company.
4.  **Blocker:** The core financial premise is broken. Expanding fixed costs (leases) while variable margins are low and the company is already losing money creates an immediate liquidity trap. The proposal cites no evidence that owned retail yields sufficient margin uplift to offset lease costs and channel conflict angering current resellers.

**Risks:** Recession exposure, inventory obsolescence, reseller retaliation, execution failure.

**Verdict:** The plan commits scarce capital to a high-fixed-cost model without proving it generates more profit than low-capital alternatives. The lack of a specific financial edge over kiosks or partner training, combined with the company's fragile cash position, makes this a fatal gamble.

DECISION: NO-GO
