First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits a core factual contradiction: the target's primary value (push-to-talk service) relies on technology explicitly stated as "incompatible" with the buyer's network and "limited for data."

Steelman: The acquisition secures a sticky, high-margin business customer base that competitors cannot easily replicate, justifying the $36 billion premium if the service can be migrated or dual-run profitably.

Attack: The plan fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests immediately. It identifies the asset's value (push-to-talk) and simultaneously identifies the mechanism of its destruction (incompatibility with the buyer's network). Running two incompatible networks indefinitely contradicts the merger's purpose of synergy; migrating a specialized, popular service to an incompatible legacy infrastructure typically results in service degradation and customer churn. The "large goodwill" cited is not an asset here but a liability marker, representing the gap between the purchase price and the tangible, integratable assets.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids $36B risk and integration chaos.
2. Partner/Resell: Access the business customers via MVNO agreement without buying the incompatible network.
3. Acquire only the customer list (if legally separable) and migrate them to existing plans, avoiding the network debt.

Pre-mortem: Two years post-merger, the specialized push-to-talk service fails on the integrated network due to latency or coverage gaps inherent in the legacy tech. Business customers, who rely on instant connectivity for safety and operations, flee to niche competitors. The $36 billion becomes a write-down as the "high-value" base evaporates, leaving the carrier with stranded legacy equipment and massive debt.

Blockers:
1. The proposal states the target's core value proposition is technically incompatible with the buyer's infrastructure. There is no stated plan, cost estimate, or timeline for resolving this incompatibility, nor evidence that the service can survive migration.
2. The "limited for data" constraint suggests the target network cannot support future growth, making the CapEx required to upgrade it potentially prohibitive compared to the $36B entry price.

Risks: Integration complexity, culture clash, debt load. However, the technical incompatibility of the revenue-generating engine is a fatal flaw, not a manageable risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
