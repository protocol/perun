First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; higher local prices and an entrenched competitor mean the retailer's domestic loyalty and discount model do not automatically translate to a winning position abroad. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Rapidly leasing 130–150 sites allows immediate scale to challenge the incumbent before they react, leveraging existing capital to bypass slow greenfield development.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A "pilot" (5–10 stores) tests cross-border logistics, bilingual labeling, and price sensitivity with minimal exposure. The proposed "big bang" approach fails the reversibility test; if the new inventory system fails or price perception is wrong, 150 leaking leases create an existential crisis versus a contained pilot failure.
2.  **Fit & Evidence:** The plan assumes domestic loyalty transfers internationally without proof. It assumes a new, unproven supply chain can handle complex cross-border regulation and bilingual requirements at day one. No evidence suggests the target market values this specific retailer's assortment over the entrenched competitor's, especially if prices are higher on some goods.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The expansion fails because the new inventory system cannot handle local regulatory nuances, causing stockouts or compliance fines. Simultaneously, the "discount" brand perception fails to resonate against a local incumbent who better understands local pricing elasticity, leaving the retailer with 150 underperforming leases and no exit strategy.
4.  **Blockers:** The core claim relies on speed to beat the incumbent, but the *stated facts* show higher prices on some goods and a dominant local rival. Without a verified price advantage or unique assortment, speed only accelerates cash burn. The lack of a proven supply chain for this specific cross-border context is a factual gap that blocks execution as proposed.

Risks (non-blocking but severe): Brand dilution, regulatory non-compliance, capital exhaustion.

Kill Criteria: If a 5-store pilot cannot achieve positive unit economics within 12 months, stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
