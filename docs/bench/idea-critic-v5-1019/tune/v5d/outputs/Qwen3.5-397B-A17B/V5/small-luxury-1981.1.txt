First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; sharing a mass-market platform while charging a premium price against established rivals offers no defensible advantage in cost, performance, or exclusivity that beats current options.

Steelman: Leveraging an existing parent platform drastically reduces development time and capital expenditure, allowing rapid entry into the emerging compact luxury segment with a price point potentially lower than bespoke competitors, assuming the "luxury" trim successfully masks the shared underpinnings.

Attack: The core claim fails on value perception. Luxury buyers purchase status and engineering distinctiveness, not just trim. If the platform is visibly or reputationally linked to mass-market vehicles, the "premium" price becomes unjustifiable, creating a value gap. The risk that salespeople will not champion the car indicates an internal recognition that the product dilutes the brand rather than extending it. Without a unique engineering feature or exclusive design language that decouples the car from its mass-market roots, the "added trim" is insufficient to command luxury margins.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve brand prestige and avoid cannibalization or dilution risks.
2. Develop a dedicated compact platform: Higher cost and time, but ensures brand integrity and justifies premium pricing.
3. Acquire or partner with an existing small-car luxury niche player: Faster than building, clearer brand positioning than sharing a mass-market floorpan.

Pre-mortem: The car launches, critics expose the shared components with the parent's economy model, and the luxury press mocks it as a "gold-plated economy car." Sales stall because the target demographic (American luxury seekers) still perceive it as inferior to European bespoke rivals, while the core European buyer base feels the brand has sold out. The brand's overall prestige drops, hurting flagship sales.

Blockers: The proposal admits the premium price is hard to justify against rivals without a unique engineering edge. Relying on "trim" to bridge the gap between mass-market hardware and luxury pricing is a fundamental economic mismatch that contradicts the definition of the target market's values.

Risks: Brand dilution, sales channel resistance, failure to deceive or satisfy the new demographic.

DECISION: NO-GO
