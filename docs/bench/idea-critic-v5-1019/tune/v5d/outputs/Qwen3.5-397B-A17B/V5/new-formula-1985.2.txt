First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; superior taste in blind tests is not an edge when the product being replaced possesses non-taste value (heritage, identity) that the test explicitly ignored.

Steelman: Replacing the formula maximizes market share by delivering the objectively preferred taste experience, assuming taste is the sole driver of cola consumption.

Attack: The evidence cited (blind taste tests, surveys, focus groups) is fundamentally flawed for this specific decision because it isolates taste from brand context. The proposal admits testing did not inform participants of the original's removal. This creates a false positive: consumers chose "sweeter" assuming the original remained an option or as a standalone sip, not as a permanent deletion of a cultural icon. The identified risks (emotional attachment, heritage, no fallback) are not merely potential downsides; they are direct contradictions to the test conditions. By withdrawing the original entirely, the company converts a "variety" preference into a "loss" scenario, triggering reactance that taste metrics cannot predict.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Retain share erosion but preserve brand equity and bottler stability.
2. Launch as extension: Introduce the sweeter formula as a new SKU alongside the original to capture taste-preferers without alienating heritage loyalists.
3. Proposed plan: Replace entirely. This fails to beat "do nothing" because it trades a known, manageable decline for a catastrophic, unquantified reputational risk based on invalid data.

Fit: This contradicts the north star of brand stewardship by discarding the core asset (the original formula) based on data that explicitly excluded the asset's primary value proposition (heritage).

Pre-mortem: The launch triggers a consumer backlash rooted in perceived corporate arrogance and loss of identity. Sales plummet not due to taste, but due to boycott. No fallback exists to appease angry loyalists, forcing a humiliating reversal that permanently damages brand trust.

Blockers: The core evidence (taste tests) is invalid for the specific action proposed (total replacement) because it omitted the critical variable (removal of the original). The plan relies on data that contradicts its own execution context.

Risks: Total brand alienation, bottler revolt, irreversible loss of heritage positioning.

DECISION: NO-GO
