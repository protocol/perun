First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (adult growth) but offers no stated edge; it attempts to overwrite a deeply entrenched "kids/value" brand identity with price and volume alone, ignoring the core mismatch between the chain's image and the premium positioning.

**Steelman:** The chain can leverage its massive scale to fund an ad blitz so dominant that it forcibly resets adult perception of the brand to "premium," overcoming the value expectation through sheer exposure.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit:** The strategy contradicts the "child-centered image." A premium burger at a higher price point creates cognitive dissonance for adults who associate the brand with cheap toys and value. You cannot advertise your way out of a fundamental brand identity mismatch without first altering the product or experience ecosystem.
2.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves margins. A "better way" would be introducing a limited-time adult item at core pricing to test demand without the brand friction of a permanent "premium" label, or acquiring a distinct adult-oriented sub-brand.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The campaign launches, spending the record budget. Adults try the burger once due to curiosity but do not return because the environment (playplaces, kids' marketing) feels incongruent with a "premium" dining experience. The high price deters regulars, cannibalizing core sales. The result is massive sunk cost with no retention.
4.  **Evidence:** The risks raised in the proposal itself (customers come for kids/value, adults see it as fast/not premium) are treated as solvable by ad spend, but no evidence is offered that advertising alone shifts deep-seated brand archetypes.

**Blockers:**
*   **Brand-Price Mismatch:** The proposal admits adults see the chain as "fast, not premium" yet relies on a "priced above core" product as the primary lever. This is a contradiction; higher prices reinforce the "wrong fit" perception rather than solving it.
*   **Unproven Demand:** Committing the "largest budget" before validating if adults will pay a premium at *this specific brand* violates basic capital allocation. The high spend is a risk multiplier, not a solution.

**Risks:** Cannibalization of core value customers; permanent brand confusion; massive financial loss if the ad spend fails to convert trial to loyalty.

**Decision:** The plan fails as proposed because it treats a structural brand identity problem as a marketing volume problem. The lack of a pilot or staged approach, combined with the contradictory pricing strategy, creates an unacceptably high probability of failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
