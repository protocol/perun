First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; higher local prices and an entrenched competitor mean the retailer's domestic loyalty and discount model do not automatically translate to winning this specific market.

Steelman: Rapidly acquiring 130–150 sites creates immediate scale to challenge the incumbent before they can react, leveraging existing lease availability.

Attack:
1.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A "pilot" (5–10 stores) tests cross-border logistics and bilingual labeling at 5% of the cost. The proposed "big bang" approach bets the entire international strategy on unproven systems and supply chains. The pilot reaches the same learning upside with negligible downside; the massive rollout does not beat "do nothing" on risk-adjusted returns.
2.  **Fit:** Expansion fits a growth vision, but the execution method (simultaneous launch of ~140 stores) contradicts operational prudence for a first-time international entrant.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: The new inventory system fails under real-world cross-border complexity, causing stockouts in 100+ locations simultaneously. Cash burns on leases while shelves sit empty. The entrenched competitor undercuts prices on key goods, exploiting the newcomer's supply chain fragility.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the supply chain and inventory systems are "new" and "not yet proven." It also admits the need to learn regulation and labeling. There is no evidence that the target users prefer this retailer over the incumbent or that the price perception risk is manageable.
5.  **Blocker Test:**
    *   **Blockers:** The core claim relies on unproven systems supporting a massive footprint immediately. The facts state the systems are new and the logistics are unknown. Scaling unproven operations to 140 sites is a factual path to systemic failure. The alternative (pilot) achieves the strategic goal of market entry without the catastrophic risk of total system collapse.
    *   **Risks:** Price perception, regulatory hurdles, competitor retaliation. These are manageable with a pilot but fatal at scale if the foundation cracks.

The absence of a pilot phase for unproven cross-border systems, combined with the commitment to nearly 150 stores before validating the supply chain, constitutes a blocker. The plan fails as proposed because it ignores the cheaper, safer alternative that validates the specific unknowns (logistics, systems) before full deployment.

DECISION: NO-GO
