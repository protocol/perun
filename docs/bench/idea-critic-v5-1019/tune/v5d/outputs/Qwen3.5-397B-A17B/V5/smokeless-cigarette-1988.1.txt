First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a real need (cleaner smoke) but offers no stated edge over existing cigarettes on the critical dimensions of taste, simplicity, or regulatory clearance; "cleaner" alone is not an advantage if the experience is worse or illegal to claim.

**Steelman:** A heated tobacco product can capture health-conscious smokers if the taste is acceptable and regulators permit "cleaner" marketing without demanding proof of reduced harm.

**Attack:**
1.  **Taste & Usability Blocker:** The proposal admits smokers "may dislike the taste" and finds it "complicated." In a mass market, a superior sensory experience is the primary driver of adoption. A product that tastes worse and is harder to use than the incumbent fails the basic utility test, regardless of smoke reduction.
2.  **Regulatory Blocker:** Promoting it as "cleaner" invites immediate regulatory scrutiny. Without pre-approved health claims (which are not stated as secured), marketing it this way risks fines, forced withdrawal, or being classified as an unapproved drug/device. The cost of development is sunk; spending more on a launch that may be banned is throwing good money after bad.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A "pilot" is already suggested but cannot overcome the fundamental product flaws (taste/complexity) identified in the premise.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, smokers try it once, reject the taste and the lighting mechanism, regulators issue a cease-and-desist on "cleaner" claims, and the company faces massive write-offs on top of the already "very large sum" spent.

**Blockers:**
*   **Product-Market Fit:** Stated risk of user rejection due to taste and complexity is a direct failure of core value proposition.
*   **Regulatory Viability:** Marketing strategy relies on claims likely to be rejected without explicit prior approval, creating legal liability.

**Risks:** High development costs are sunk, creating pressure to launch, but this does not validate the path forward.

**Kill Criteria:** If blind taste tests show <80% preference vs. ordinary cigarettes, or if regulators do not pre-approve "cleaner" messaging, the project stops.

The plan fails because it asks to launch a product with known inferior user experience and unverified regulatory standing.

DECISION: NO-GO
