First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "cheapest" is a price position, not a defensible advantage against cheaper scooters and used cars, and the risks explicitly state buyers may reject the stigma.

Steelman: A dedicated high-volume factory enables a $2,500 price point, successfully converting scooter families by offering the absolute lowest-cost four-seat enclosure despite safety concerns and land protests.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High capex, high reputational risk, dependent on quelling farmer protests and overcoming the "cheap" stigma.
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoids sunk costs in a contested factory site and potential brand damage from safety failures.
3. **Alternative (Staged):** Utilize existing manufacturing capacity or partnerships to validate demand at low volume before committing to a dedicated, protest-prone factory.

Fit: While the vision of motorizing families is sound, the specific tactic of a new dedicated factory contradicts the risk profile of land acquisition delays and unproven demand for a "cheap" badge.

Pre-mortem: The project fails because farmer protests halt construction indefinitely, or the market rejects the vehicle as unsafe "tin," leaving the company with a stranded, specialized factory. This is a one-way door with massive irreversible capital expenditure. The cost of delay is low compared to the cost of building the wrong factory in the wrong place.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits buyers may not want a car known as "cheapest" and that scooters/used cars are cheaper alternatives. There is no evidence provided that the safety cuts are acceptable or that the land protests will resolve.

Blockers:
1. **Unresolved Land Risk:** The factory site faces active farmer protests; building there is not currently feasible.
2. **Negative Market Signal:** The proposal states buyers may reject the "cheapest" positioning, yet offers no strategy to counter this other than price.
3. **Superior Alternative:** Used cars and scooters remain cheaper; the proposal fails to demonstrate why a new, potentially unsafe car beats these established options.

Risks: Safety reputation damage, construction delays, brand dilution.

The absence of a mitigation strategy for the land protests and the explicit acknowledgment of potential market rejection constitute fatal flaws. The plan commits to a high-fixed-cost model before validating demand or securing the site.

DECISION: NO-GO
