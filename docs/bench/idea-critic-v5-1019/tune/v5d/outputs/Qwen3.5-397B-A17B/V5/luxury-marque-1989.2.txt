First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a critical fact: the company is known for reliable, economical small cars with no presence above the mid-price segment. Luxury purchasing is driven by brand prestige and status signaling, not just hardware quality. A brand defined by "economy" actively repels luxury buyers who pay for exclusivity; the stated brand equity contradicts the core requirement for luxury success.

Steelman: Launching a distinct brand with a separate dealer network isolates the new product from the parent's economy reputation, allowing the flagship's superior engineering to establish a new prestige identity at a disruptive price point.

Attack: The "separate brand" defense fails against the stated reality of the parent company's market position. While the brand name might differ, the corporate ownership and manufacturing origin are difficult to hide in the launch phase, especially for a single-model lineup. The proposal admits development has "already cost heavily," creating pressure to succeed quickly, yet a single flagship model cannot sustain a luxury dealer network or convince skeptics without a broader portfolio. European incumbents possess decades of heritage; a new entrant with one car and an economy DNA cannot match this immediately, regardless of engineering parity. The risk that "buyers may not pay luxury prices" is not a hypothetical risk but a near-certain outcome given the stated brand perception.

Alternatives: Doing nothing preserves capital. Acquiring an existing luxury marque would provide instant heritage. Staging the launch with a shared network reduces initial capex. None of these are pursued in the current plan.

Blockers: The stated fact that the company sells "almost nothing above the mid-price segment" combined with the reliance on a "single flagship model" creates a fatal gap: there is no bridge for consumers to accept a luxury price tag from an economy manufacturer, and one car cannot carry the weight of building a new luxury ecosystem alone. The brand perception acts as a ceiling that a separate nameplate cannot instantly shatter without a multi-model commitment and massive marketing spend not evidenced here.

Risks: Dealer network insolvency due to low volume; brand dilution if the launch fails; inability to recoup heavy development costs.

DECISION: NO-GO
