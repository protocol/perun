First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (feature production) but lacks a stated edge proving this specific plan beats existing buyer options or mitigates the extreme capability gap.

**Steelman:** A small CG studio leverages a major studio's distribution to produce the first all-CG feature, using a toy-centric story to mask technical limitations while proving the medium's viability.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capability Mismatch:** The company has never produced anything longer than a short. Feature rendering scales non-linearly; "slow and costly" for shorts implies prohibitive costs and timelines for 90 minutes with 1991 hardware. No evidence suggests the team can scale operations 10x without collapsing quality or budget.
2.  **Strategic Vulnerability:** The larger partner controls distribution and holds the "stronger hand." Without a proven feature track record, the small studio cannot negotiate favorable terms, risking total loss of IP or bankruptcy if the film underperforms.
3.  **Unproven Market:** No all-CG feature exists. While innovative, the risk isn't just technical; it's whether audiences accept the aesthetic for a full narrative. The proposal offers no pilot or intermediate step to validate this.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves the successful short-film business. A cheaper alternative is producing a high-quality short or special effects sequence to prove feature viability before committing to a full film.

**Pre-mortem:** The project runs 300% over budget due to rendering bottlenecks. The studio forces creative changes to cut costs, ruining the vision. The film releases to mixed reviews, the small studio is dissolved or absorbed, and the lead animator leaves.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capacity:** Zero evidence the team can scale from shorts to features; the jump is historically fatal for similar studios.
*   **Leverage:** Entering a binding deal with a dominant partner without a proven feature product guarantees unfavorable terms that threaten survival.

**Risks:** Technical failure, market rejection, partner exploitation. These are not mere risks; the lack of scaling proof is a factual blocker.

**Kill Criteria:** None proposed. A go-if would require a funded pilot short proving the rendering pipeline scales, but the proposal demands the full feature now.

The absence of a proven scaling path and the imbalance of power create a failure scenario that cannot be mitigated by optimism.

DECISION: NO-GO
