First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a clear cost disadvantage ("cost noticeably more") and no stated edge (e.g., patented protection, proven demand for premium pricing, or exclusive access) that allows it to beat established, cheaper incumbents. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: A superior, bagless technology justifies a premium price if buyers value the innovation enough to overcome higher costs and lack of brand trust.

Attack:
1.  **Options:** "Do nothing" costs $0. Licensing the tech to an incumbent leverages their manufacturing and distribution, avoiding the "no manufacturing record" risk. The proposal to self-manufacture is the highest risk, highest cost option with no stated advantage over licensing.
2.  **Fit:** Assuming the goal is market penetration, entering with a higher-priced product from an unknown manufacturer against copy-capable incumbents contradicts basic market entry logic without a moat.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The company fails because incumbents copy the cyclone feature, offer it at a lower price point due to scale, and the new company cannot sustain manufacturing costs or retail shelf space.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits higher costs, no manufacturing history, and strong incumbents. It offers no evidence of buyer willingness to pay the premium or legal protection against copying.
5.  **Blocker Test:**
    *   **Blockers:** The stated facts show the plan fails as proposed: higher cost + no brand + no manufacturing record + copyable idea = no route to profitability against incumbents. The alternative of licensing reaches the same upside (selling the tech) cheaper and safer.
    *   **Risks:** Execution difficulty, unproven demand, one-way door (capital commitment).

The proposal lacks a stated edge to overcome its admitted disadvantages. A cheaper, safer alternative (licensing) exists that avoids the manufacturing and distribution risks. Without a patent moat or pre-sold demand cited, self-manufacturing is a blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
