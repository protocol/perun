First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "hard to use" is a need, not an advantage over existing solutions or the imminent OS update.

Steelman: A cartoon house shell makes computing intuitive for novices, justifying a $100 premium on high-end hardware.

Attack: The plan fails on three verified facts within the proposal. First, the hardware requirement ("high-end PC") contradicts the target market reality ("most home PCs" lack memory), creating an immediate addressable market of near zero. Second, the timing is fatal: a "major new operating-system release" arriving in months will likely integrate interface improvements, rendering a separate $100 shell obsolete before adoption scales. Third, the "patronizing" risk alienates experienced users who influence household purchases, while the "few add-ons" constraint limits longevity.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Wait for the upcoming OS release to solve interface issues natively at no extra cost.
2. Cheaper path: Develop a lightweight launcher rather than a full graphical shell, reducing memory footprint and price.
3. Current plan: High cost, high risk, low reversibility due to development spend, and dependent on hardware penetration that does not exist.

Fit: This diverts resources from the core OS business to fight a hardware limitation the company cannot solve and a timeline it cannot beat.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, users realize their machines cannot run it, early adopters feel insulted by the "cartoon" aesthetic, and the free OS update cancels the value proposition entirely. Sales flatline; the project is cancelled within a quarter.

Blockers:
1. Hardware mismatch: Target users do not own the required "high-end PC."
2. Timing conflict: Imminent free OS release undermines the paid product's value.
3. No stated edge: Need alone does not justify the cost or hardware barrier.

Risks: Brand damage from perceived patronization; lack of ecosystem (add-ons).

The hardware constraint and imminent competitor (internal OS) release are factual blockers that prevent success as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
