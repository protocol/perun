First call: NO-GO. While test markets showed positive response, the proposal lacks a stated edge proving this specific extension beats the status quo given the high probability of cannibalization and novelty decay.

**Steelman:** A clear cola captures a latent demand for "purity" that color-conscious consumers currently cannot satisfy, creating a net-new revenue stream that outweighs internal cannibalization.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cannibalization vs. Growth:** The proposal admits shoppers link cola flavor with color. If the core product identity is tied to darkness, a clear version may confuse the brand rather than expand it. Without data proving these are *new* buyers rather than flagship drinkers switching temporarily, the net revenue impact is likely negative.
2.  **Novelty Trap:** The "positive response" in test markets is a classic novelty spike. The proposal admits repeat purchase is uncertain. Launching nationwide with a *large* marketing campaign locks in high fixed costs before verifying retention. If repeats fail, the brand suffers both financial loss and reputation damage from a "failed experiment."
3.  **Competitive Vulnerability:** A nationwide launch signals the category to rivals immediately. If the concept works, copies will arrive before the brand establishes dominance, diluting the first-mover advantage without a defensible moat (e.g., patent or unique supply chain).
4.  **Test Market Fallacy:** Test markets often overstate demand due to heightened awareness. Scaling to a national campaign amplifies this error.

**Better Way:** Execute a staged regional rollout with capped marketing spend. Measure repeat purchase rates over 90 days before committing to a national campaign. This preserves capital if the "purity" angle proves to be a one-time curiosity.

**Kill Criteria:** Halt expansion if repeat purchase rate falls below 20% of initial trial or if flagship sales drop more than 5% in test regions without net total volume growth.

**Blockers:** The proposal commits to a large national campaign despite admitting unverified repeat purchase rates and high cannibalization risk. The financial exposure is disproportionate to the validated evidence.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, wasted marketing spend, accelerated competitive entry.

DECISION: NO-GO
