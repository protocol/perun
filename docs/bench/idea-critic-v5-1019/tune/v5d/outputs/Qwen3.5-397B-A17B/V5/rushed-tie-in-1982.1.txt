First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high license cost and extreme time compression do not constitute a competitive advantage over existing market offerings, but rather a financial vulnerability.

Steelman: A single developer can rapidly synthesize a functional product from a hit film's brand to capture immediate holiday demand before the cultural moment fades.

Attack: The plan relies on two unverified assumptions: that one person can deliver quality in five weeks (normally impossible), and that the brand alone guarantees sales despite poor quality. The "very large production run" amplifies the risk of total capital loss if the game fails. There is no cheaper alternative considered, such as a smaller initial batch or a delayed release to ensure quality. The cost of delay (missing Christmas) is high, but the cost of failure (unsold inventory + wasted license) is catastrophic.

Evidence: The proposal admits the timeline is "far shorter than normal" and quality "may be poor." These are not risks; they are near-certainties given the constraints. No evidence suggests the film's audience will accept a substandard game.

Blockers:
1. Capacity mismatch: One programmer cannot reasonably build a high-quality title in five weeks; the stated facts contradict the core claim of feasibility.
2. Financial exposure: A "very large production run" based on a likely low-quality product creates a guaranteed loss scenario if sales underperform, which is probable given the quality risk.

Risks: Brand damage, total inventory write-off, wasted license fee.

The plan fails the blocker test. The capacity constraint is a factual impossibility for the stated goal, and the financial structure (high fixed costs + high variable risk) has no mitigation. A capped, time-boxed pilot (small run) would be required to proceed, but the proposal explicitly demands a large run now.

DECISION: NO-GO
