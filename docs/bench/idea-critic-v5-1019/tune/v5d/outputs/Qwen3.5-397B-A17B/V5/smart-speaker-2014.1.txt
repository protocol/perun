First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; a poor phone launch, unproven voice tech, privacy fears, and existing mobile assistants offer no factual advantage over current buyer options. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: A cylindrical speaker at $199 ($99 for members) creates a new home category by leveraging exclusive access and member pricing to overcome privacy and reliability concerns where phones fail.

**Attack:**
1.  **Options:** "Do nothing" costs $0 and avoids repeating a recent hardware failure. Mobile assistants already exist at no extra hardware cost. The proposed device must beat free, functional software with expensive, unproven hardware. It does not.
2.  **Fit:** The company's last hardware launch failed badly. Entering a non-existent category with known technical flaws (voice recognition failures) and high consumer friction (always-on mic) contradicts a prudent recovery strategy.
3.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the category does not exist and the tech often fails. There is no evidence customers want an always-listening device after a poor phone reception.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The product fails because privacy concerns stall adoption, voice errors frustrate users, and the "invitation only" model prevents the volume needed to improve the AI, leaving the company with unsold inventory and compounded brand damage.
5.  **Blocker Test:**
    *   **Blocker:** The stated facts show the core technology (voice recognition) often fails and the target behavior (always-on mic) faces explicit customer resistance. The proposal offers no solution to these negatives, only a price discount.
    *   **Risk:** High execution difficulty, no prior success, strong incumbents (mobile).

The plan fails as proposed because its own evidence (tech failure, privacy risk, prior hardware flop) contradicts the core claim that this will succeed where the phone did not. A cheaper alternative (improving the mobile app) reaches the same upside without the hardware risk.

Blockers: Core tech acknowledged as unreliable; customer acceptance of always-on mic unproven and risky; no stated edge over existing mobile assistants.
Risks: Repeating hardware failure; privacy backlash; category creation costs.

DECISION: NO-GO
