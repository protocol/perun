First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it assumes exposure equals revenue without proving users or partners will tolerate the mechanism.

Steelman: Transparently sharing user purchases with friends, coupled with an opt-out, drives ad relevance and viral commerce, increasing revenue.

Attack: The "notification and opt-out" defense is insufficient against the core failure mode: social backlash. In 2007, privacy norms treat purchase history as private by default, not public by exception. Making sensitive data (medical supplies, gifts, debt payments) visible to a user's entire network invites immediate harassment and embarrassment. The "opt-out" places the burden of privacy on the victim rather than the platform, signaling that surveillance is the default state.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserves trust; revenue growth relies on traditional banner metrics.
2. Aggregated insights: Share anonymized trends ("3 friends bought this") without exposing individual transactions. Lower risk, preserves utility.
3. Explicit opt-in: Only share if a user actively clicks "share purchase." Harder to scale, but safe.

Fit: This contradicts the foundational social contract of a network: a safe space for connection. Turning the feed into a public ledger of consumption erodes that safety.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-launch, major partners demand exit clauses after users mock their brands in comments attached to purchase receipts. A viral story breaks about a user being outed or harassed due to a sensitive purchase visible in the feed. The feature is quietly disabled, but the brand damage regarding "privacy invasion" is permanent, stalling future monetization efforts.

Evidence: No data is provided showing users *want* this visibility. The risks listed in the proposal (invasion of privacy, partner embarrassment) are admitted facts, not hypotheticals. The proposal offers no mechanism to prevent the admitted "hard to withdraw" scenario once the backlash occurs.

Blockers:
1. The proposal admits the feature is "hard to withdraw" while simultaneously admitting high risks of user backlash and partner embarrassment. Launching a sticky feature with known, severe reputational risks and no proven demand is operationally negligent.
2. No stated edge proves this specific invasive method beats aggregated or opt-in models.

Risks: Permanent brand damage, partner churn, regulatory scrutiny.

DECISION: NO-GO
