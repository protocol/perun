First call: NO-GO. A search-ad company with no hardware DNA proposing a $50M acquisition of a pre-revenue, six-person OS team in a carrier-dominated market lacks a stated edge over existing vendors; the "free software" angle is a tactic, not a defensible moat against entrenched handset makers.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the only viable open-source mobile OS now creates a defensive floor to prevent search exclusion from the emerging mobile web, betting that controlling the stack is cheaper than paying distribution tolls later.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Rely on WAP and existing handset browsers; risk being shut out as mobile data grows.
2.  **Partnerships:** Pay carriers/handset makers for search placement; high marginal cost, low control.
3.  **Acquisition (Proposed):** High upfront capex ($50M+), massive execution risk, but offers total control over the user interface and default search.

**Fit:** Aligns with the mission to organize information, but assumes a "mobile-first" future that is not yet financially proven in 2005. The strategy relies on a shift to data-heavy smartphones which carriers are currently resisting.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because handset makers refuse to adopt a competitor's OS, carriers block non-certified devices, and the team burns cash for five years without market share, distracting from the core high-margin search business. This is a one-way door with a multi-year payback.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits no revenue model, no adoption path, and explicit resistance from gatekeepers. The $50M price tag is speculative for six people with no product.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Distribution Path:** Handset makers have zero incentive to install a rival's OS; carriers control the pipe and reject unvetted software. The proposal offers no mechanism to overcome this duopoly.
*   **Misaligned Core Competency:** A software/search firm has no supply chain, retail, or carrier negotiation experience required to ship phones.
*   **Financial Disconnect:** $50M for a pre-revenue team with no clear path to monetization violates prudent capital allocation given the stated risks.

**Risks:** Long development cycle, fragmentation, burning cash without ad revenue lift.

**Kill Criteria:** If no major handset partner signs a letter of intent within 18 months, or if carrier certification blocks release by 2007, terminate the project.

The absence of a verified distribution channel and the mismatch in operational capabilities constitute a fatal flaw in the current plan.

DECISION: NO-GO
