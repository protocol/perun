First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge over existing handhelds or home consoles; "between generations" is a gap, not an advantage.

Steelman: A low-cost ($180) entry point creates a new stereoscopic 3D category using existing display tech.
Attack: The execution negates the edge. Red monochrome visuals are inferior to full-color competitors. The form factor (tabletop with eyepiece) destroys portability (handheld's core value) and comfort (home console's core value).

Options:
1. Do nothing: Wait for the next home console. Cost: $0. Risk: Missed window. Reversibility: N/A.
2. Enhanced Handheld: Color screen, standard form factor. Cost: Higher. Risk: Low. Reversibility: High.
3. Proposal: Red 3D tabletop. Cost: $180. Risk: High (usability, health). Reversibility: Low (hardware inventory).
The proposal does not beat "do nothing" or a standard handheld upgrade on utility.

Fit: Assumes a strategy of "hardware experimentation." If the north star is "market share" or "user satisfaction," this fails.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, users experience headaches within 15 minutes, and the library remains empty because developers refuse to optimize for red-only 3D. Units gather dust. It is a two-way door (can stop production), but brand damage is immediate. Cost of delay is negative; waiting avoids a flop.

Evidence: The proposal admits critical flaws: "red-on-black only," "neither portable nor comfortable," "eye strain," "few games." These are not risks; they are stated characteristics that invalidate the value proposition. No evidence suggests users want this specific compromise.

Blockers:
1. Usability contradiction: The device claims to be a console but removes the primary benefits of both portability and comfortable home use.
2. Health barrier: Acknowledged eye strain/headaches are a hard stop for mass-market adoption.
3. Content vacuum: "Few games at launch" ensures low attachment rates, killing the ecosystem immediately.

Risks: Brand dilution, inventory write-downs.

The stated facts confirm the product is worse than existing alternatives in every key dimension (visual quality, comfort, portability, library) without a compensating price or feature advantage.

DECISION: NO-GO
