First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price, slow performance, and software scarcity are explicit negatives with no compensating advantage cited.

Steelman: This machine offers a superior, intuitive interface that reduces training costs enough to justify a 5-10x price premium over existing text-based business PCs.

Attack: The proposal admits the machine runs slowly and lacks software, the two primary drivers of business productivity. At $10,000, it costs several times more than competitors while offering less utility. The existence of a cheaper internal alternative with similar capabilities destroys any potential margin or strategic uniqueness. Without a specific, verified advantage (e.g., exclusive software, unmatched reliability, or specific enterprise integration), the "graphical interface" alone cannot overcome the massive cost and performance deficits. The risk of cannibalization by the internal cheaper project makes this launch financially suicidal.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital, wait for the cheaper internal option or market prices to drop.
2. Launch internal cheaper machine: Capture the GUI benefit without the price/speed penalty.
3. Launch this $10k unit: High cost, low performance, high risk of immediate obsolescence.

Fit: Assuming the goal is profitability and market share, this contradicts basic economic logic for 1983 office workers who prioritize speed and compatibility over novelty at any cost.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, sells negligible units due to price and lack of software, and is discontinued within a year, damaging the brand's business credibility. This is a one-way door for brand reputation; delay costs little as the market is nascent.

Evidence: The proposal itself contains the disqualifying facts: price is "several times" higher, speed is "slow," software is "little," and a superior internal alternative exists. No external data is needed to see the math fails.

Blockers:
1. Price/Performance Mismatch: Costs significantly more while performing worse than established competitors.
2. Internal Cannibalization: A cheaper, similar internal project renders this SKU redundant before launch.
3. No Stated Edge: The proposal offers no fact showing why a buyer would choose this over cheaper/faster options.

Risks: Market rejection, inventory write-off, brand damage.

DECISION: NO-GO
