First call: NO-GO. The proposal doubles down on a recent 60% price hike by adding severe friction (two logins, two bills, no shared data) immediately after angering the base. There is no stated edge where this specific execution beats the status quo or a simpler unbundling; it trades strategic clarity for operational hostility.

**Steelman:** Separating brands allows focused management of a declining DVD asset versus a growth streaming asset, preventing legacy constraints from stifling innovation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing & Friction:** Executing a complex split (new brand, site, billing) weeks after a massive price increase ignores the primary risk: customer backlash. Forcing existing users to manage two accounts validates fears of a "cash grab" rather than strategic focus.
2.  **Value Destruction:** Explicitly severing ratings and recommendations destroys the unique value proposition of a combined library, making the service strictly worse for users who utilize both formats.
3.  **Alternatives:** A "do nothing" approach regarding the interface, or a soft-separation with single sign-on and shared data, achieves the management goal without the customer experience penalty. The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" on net customer value.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. Cause: Mass churn driven by the perception that the company is punishing loyalists to subsidize streaming, exacerbated by the inconvenience of dual logins. The cost of delay is low; the cost of immediate execution is reputational catastrophe.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The plan explicitly removes cross-service recommendations and forces separate billing immediately after a 60% price hike.
*   **Contradiction:** The rationale cites "customer confusion" as a risk, yet the solution (two brands, two logins) maximizes confusion and friction.
*   **Missing Edge:** No evidence suggests this specific hostile split retains more value than a unified backend with separate branding.

**Risks:** Total brand erosion, accelerated churn, regulatory scrutiny on deceptive bundling/unbundling practices.

**Conclusion:** The proposal confuses organizational convenience with market strategy. While separation may be inevitable long-term, this specific implementation—stripping data synergy and adding billing friction atop a price hike—is a self-inflicted wound. It fails the "beat do nothing" test.

DECISION: NO-GO
