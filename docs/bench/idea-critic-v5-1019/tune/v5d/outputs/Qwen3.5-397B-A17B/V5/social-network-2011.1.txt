First call: NO-GO. The proposal cites no stated edge where this plan beats the incumbent's hundreds of millions of users; need alone is not an edge, and three prior failures suggest the core claim lacks viability.

Steelman: Integrating social layers into dominant search/mail products forces distribution, while circles and hangouts offer sufficient differentiation to overcome network effects and past failures.

Attack:
1. **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves search/mail utility. A cheaper alternative is acquiring a niche network or building standalone tools without forcing integration. Forced integration risks degrading the core products (search/mail) to feed a failing social layer, a negative upside the plan ignores.
2. **Fit:** The strategy assumes users want a unified identity across search and social, contradicting the company's history of three failed social attempts. No evidence suggests "circles" or "hangouts" overcome the incumbent's lock-in.
3. **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: users resent forced profiles, abandoning core tools or ignoring the social layer entirely. Bonuses tied to adoption incentivize dark patterns (spammy invites) rather than organic value, accelerating churn. This is a one-way door; once trust in core products is damaged by forced social features, it is hard to regain.
4. **Evidence:** The proposal admits three previous failures and an incumbent with massive network effects. It offers no corrected figures showing why this attempt differs fundamentally other than "integration," which is a distribution tactic, not a product edge.
5. **Blocker:** The plan fails the "stated edge" test. It relies on forcing users via existing products rather than winning them with superior value. The history of failure combined with the risk of annoying existing users of core products constitutes a factual blocker: the proposed method (forced integration) actively threatens the company's current revenue engines without a proven mechanism to displace the incumbent.

Blockers: No stated edge to beat the incumbent; forced integration threatens core products; history of three failures unaddressed.
Risks: Network effects, user annoyance, bonus-driven dark patterns.

DECISION: NO-GO
