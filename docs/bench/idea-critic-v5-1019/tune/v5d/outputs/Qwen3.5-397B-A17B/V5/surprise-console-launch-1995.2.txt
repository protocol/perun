Attack:
1. **Options:** "Do nothing" and launch in September with a full library and competitive $299 price beats a May launch with empty shelves at $399. A cheaper alternative is a limited "developer preview" kit without retail backlash.
2. **Fit:** A console's north star is a vibrant ecosystem. Alienating retailers and starving developers directly contradicts this.
3. **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: The console launches, consumers see no games and a high price, buy the rival's cheaper unit in September, and retailers refuse to stock the brand later due to prior exclusion. This is a one-way door; lost retailer relationships are hard to repair.
4. **Evidence:** The proposal admits the machine is hard to program, developers are unprepared, and the price is $100 higher. These are not risks; they are stated facts that guarantee poor initial sales.
5. **Blocker Test:**
   - **Blockers:** The stated facts show the product cannot succeed on its own merits at launch (no games, high price). The plan relies on "being first" when the product is objectively inferior to the upcoming rival in every consumer-facing metric (price, content). The stated fact that retailers may retaliate creates a distribution blockade the plan does not solve.
   - **Risks:** Developer churn, brand damage.

The proposal fails because it trades a strong September launch (potential parity) for a disastrous May launch (certain failure). Being first with a broken value proposition cedes the market to the competitor immediately upon their arrival. There is no edge, only liabilities.
