First call: NO-GO. Removing a universal, reliable interface (headphone jack) to force adoption of an unproven, expensive ($159), and fragile accessory (easy to lose, short battery) lacks a stated edge beyond internal chip utilization; customers perceive this as a downgrade, not an upgrade.

Steelman: Eliminating the jack accelerates a wireless future, leveraging proprietary silicon for seamless pairing to create a sticky ecosystem premium that wired audio cannot match.

Attack: The proposal substitutes a functional standard with a luxury convenience item. At $159, the price is prohibitive compared to free wired alternatives, creating immediate customer resentment rather than desire. The "odd" design and high loss risk amplify negative sentiment. Five-hour battery life fails all-day utility, requiring a case that adds friction. The "edge" is entirely manufacturer-centric (chip usage), not user-centric. No evidence suggests users demand this trade-off; the risk of anger is a near-certainty, not a speculation.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Keep the jack, offer wireless earbuds as an optional accessory. Lower risk, retains customer goodwill.
2. Include a dongle: Remove the jack but provide a free adapter. Mitates anger, allows transition at user pace.
3. Proposed plan: Highest cost to user, highest friction, highest reputational risk.

Pre-mortem: Launch fails as tech press ridicules the design and consumers revolt over the lost jack and high price. Sales lag, brand trust erodes. This is a one-way door; once the jack is removed, returning it admits defeat. Cost of delay is low; waiting for battery tech to improve or prices to drop reduces risk.

Blockers:
- The proposal offers no user-value edge to justify removing a standard feature; it forces a costly, inferior (battery/durability) solution.
- Price point ($159) creates a barrier against the "free" alternative, ensuring negative reception.

Risks: Public ridicule, loss of customers, perception of greed over innovation.

Kill criteria: If pre-order sentiment analysis shows >40% negative mentions regarding the jack removal within 48 hours of announcement, halt the jack removal for this model. Leading metric: Attachment rate of earbuds vs. phone sales.

DECISION: NO-GO
