First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it concedes lower margins, no retail experience, supplier dependency, and easy copyability without explaining why this specific approach beats existing small-computer makers or the company's current high-margin model. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: A segregated team using commodity parts and open architecture can bypass internal inertia to capture the emerging personal market faster than competitors, accepting lower margins as the cost of entry.

However, the attack reveals critical flaws based strictly on provided facts. The proposal admits rivals can instantly copy a design built from standard parts. Without a proprietary moat or stated distribution advantage, the company enters a race to the bottom against incumbents who already possess retail networks and lower cost structures. The company's core competency (vertical integration) is explicitly discarded, yet no replacement capability (retail experience) exists. Depending on outside software creates a strategic vulnerability where the platform's value is controlled by third parties, while the company bears the hardware risk.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve high margins and avoid diluting the brand in a low-margin, high-risk segment where the company has no competitive advantage.
2. Acquire an existing small-computer maker: Instantly gain retail channels, software ecosystems, and market share without building from scratch or revealing an open design to copycats.
3. Partner rather than build: License the company's manufacturing prowess to existing players for a fee, avoiding retail and software risks entirely.

The proposal fails to demonstrate how a late entrant with no retail experience, lower margins, and an easily copied product will succeed. The "open design" feature, intended to speed development, simultaneously removes barriers to entry for competitors while the company lacks the distribution muscle to win volume wars. The risks are not hypothetical; they are admitted facts in the proposal that directly contradict the path to profitability. There is no evidence that a small team can overcome the structural disadvantages of entering a commodity market without a unique lever.

Blockers: No stated edge to overcome lower margins and lack of retail experience; admitted ease of copying removes potential moat.
Risks: Supplier dependency, brand dilution, execution failure in unfamiliar channels.

DECISION: NO-GO
