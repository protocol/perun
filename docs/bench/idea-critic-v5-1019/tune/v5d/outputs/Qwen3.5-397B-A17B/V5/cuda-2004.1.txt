First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it admits no large paying market exists and relies on niche academic experimentation while raising unit costs on every consumer chip.

Steelman: Embedding general-purpose compute capabilities now creates a dominant platform monopoly before rivals react, turning every gaming console into a latent supercomputer for future high-margin enterprise workloads.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Maintain cost leadership in the core gaming race; wait for market demand to justify silicon area.
2. Dedicated silicon: Build a separate, expensive card for scientists only, avoiding cost penalties on mass-market units.
3. Software-only: Optimize existing graphics APIs for science without adding hardware area or compiler costs.

Fit: This contradicts the immediate survival need to win the core gaming hardware race. Diverting engineering focus and increasing unit costs without committed customers threatens the primary revenue stream.

Pre-mortem: The program consumes capital and engineering bandwidth for years. The gaming division loses price/perf parity due to increased die size, causing market share collapse to the rival. The scientific market fails to materialize at consumer volumes, leaving the company with bloated, expensive chips nobody wants. This is a one-way door with massive delay costs; waiting preserves cash and focus.

Evidence: The proposal itself states "no large paying market exists yet" and "academic users are experimenting with hacked graphics APIs." There is no evidence of customer willingness to pay the premium required to offset the added unit cost.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: The proposal admits no current market advantage, only a speculative future one, while actively weakening the current core business position via higher unit costs.
2. Contradictory economics: Raising costs on 100% of units to serve a 0% revenue segment violates basic unit economics without a bridge funding mechanism or committed anchor tenant.

Risks: Engineering distraction, loss of gaming market share, cash burn on unproven toolchain.

The absence of a paying customer base and the explicit admission of increased unit costs on the core product constitute a fatal flaw in the current economic model.

DECISION: NO-GO
