First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a market gap but fails to state an edge; need without a distinct advantage over existing solutions or a reason why mass-market makers are wrong is insufficient.

Steelman: A mid-size maker can profitably revive the affordable roadster niche by leveraging lightweight engineering to bypass heritage requirements and safety cost barriers.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" avoids capital risk in a shrinking segment. A "cheaper alternative" exists: updating a current coupe chassis rather than a dedicated rear-drive platform. The proposal does not demonstrate why these fail to capture the same upside.
2.  **Fit:** No stated north star connects this niche product to the company's broader stage or vision. Without a strategic anchor, this is a vanity project, not a portfolio move.
3.  **Evidence:** The claim that "mass-market makers regard [this] as a dead niche" is accepted as fact, yet the proposal offers no counter-evidence (e.g., waiting lists, resale value data) proving demand exists *now*. Relying on "early enthusiasts" admits a limited total addressable market that may not sustain production volumes.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: The car launches, safety compliance inflates the price beyond "affordable," and sales stall after the initial enthusiast wave because the two-seat limitation excludes practical buyers. The company lacks the heritage to command a premium, forcing margins to zero.
5.  **Blockers:** The proposal explicitly admits the segment has shrunk, the company has no heritage, and safety rules increase costs. These are not risks to be managed; they are stated facts that remove the route to profitability as proposed. There is no mechanism described to overcome the "no heritage" deficit or the "safety cost" penalty while remaining "affordable."

**Verdict:**
Blockers: The combination of a shrinking segment, lack of brand heritage, and high regulatory costs creates a negative unit economics scenario as proposed. No evidence suggests this specific entrant can succeed where others fled.
Risks: Limited buyer pool, fading demand post-launch.

The plan fails the basic test of beating "do nothing" or a simpler derivative project. It commits resources to a declining category without a defined competitive moat.

DECISION: NO-GO
