First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge over incumbents; need alone is not an advantage, and the capital gap is a factual blocker.

Steelman: Committing exclusively to EVs in 2008 leverages first-mover branding, but buying a large legacy factory creates immediate insolvency before production begins.

Options:
1. **Proposal**: Buy large factory, full commitment. Cost: Catastrophic (capital mismatch). Risk: Immediate failure. Reversibility: Low (sunk assets).
2. **Do Nothing**: Preserve cash. Cost: Opportunity loss. Risk: Irrelevance. Reversibility: High.
3. **Contract Manufacturing**: Lease capacity or partner. Cost: Variable. Risk: Margin compression. Reversibility: High.
The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" because the funding gap is unsolved.

Fit: Assumes a vision of total electrification, but the strategy (buying heavy assets) contradicts the constraint (little capital).

Pre-mortem: The company buys the factory, exhausts cash on retrofitting, cannot fund battery R&D or charging infrastructure, and enters receivership before the first sedan rolls out. This is a one-way door with high delay cost if waited, but immediate death if taken now.

Evidence: The proposal admits the company lacks the capital for the factory and batteries. In 2008, charging infrastructure is negligible, and incumbents have deep pockets. No evidence suggests buyers prefer an unproven EV over established hybrids given the scarcity of chargers.

Blockers:
1. **Capital Mismatch**: The plan requires buying a large factory and funding battery packs, but the company explicitly "has far less capital than needed." This is a mathematical impossibility as proposed.
2. **Infrastructure Void**: Scarce charging stations remove the route to upside for mass adoption; the plan offers no solution.

Risks: Incumbent response, production inexperience, unproven demand. These are secondary to the funding blocker.

The strongest attack survived: Even if the brand vision is correct, the specific mechanism (buying a large factory now) guarantees bankruptcy due to the admitted capital shortfall. A cheaper alternative (contracting) exists but was not proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
