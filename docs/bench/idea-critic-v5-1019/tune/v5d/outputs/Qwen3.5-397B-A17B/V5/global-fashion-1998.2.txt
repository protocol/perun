First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a massive need but offers no stated edge (cost, product, access, or timing advantage) over existing local buyers to justify beating them, especially given the unproven nature of the channel.

Steelman: Simultaneous global launch captures first-mover advantage in online fashion before incumbents react, leveraging venture capital to scale faster than any single-market competitor could.

Attack: The proposal admits online fashion buying is unproven and venture money may run out. Launching in multiple continents simultaneously multiplies logistics and marketing costs without validated demand. If the channel fails or adoption is slow, the burn rate across multiple offices ensures insolvency before product-market fit is found. The risk of delay is guaranteed by the complexity of coordinating multi-country launches without prior operational proof.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Zero capital risk, but misses the potential opportunity.
2. Single-market pilot (e.g., UK or Germany only): Drastically reduces logistics/marketing burn, validates the "unproven" online buying behavior, and preserves capital for scaling only if metrics prove viability. Reversibility is high; failure is contained.
3. Staged rollout: Launch in one region, refine the model, then expand.

Fit: While the vision is global, the strategy ignores the constraint of unproven demand. A north star of "largest retailer" is not moved by spreading thin; it is moved by dominating a beachhead first.

Pre-mortem: The company runs out of cash in 18 months because logistics costs in three countries tripled projections while sales remained flat due to consumer hesitation. This is a one-way door for the invested capital; once spent, it cannot be recovered.

Evidence: The proposal itself cites "online fashion buying is unproven" and "venture money may run out." These are internal contradictions to a "go" decision on a global scale.

Blockers:
1. The stated fact that the core behavior (online fashion buying) is unproven contradicts the requirement for a massive, immediate, multi-continent bet.
2. The stated risk of running out of money is guaranteed by the proposed high-burn, multi-office structure without prior validation.

Risks: Execution difficulty, competitor response, supply chain failures.

Kill criteria: None proposed; none needed as the plan fails on stated facts. A single-market pilot with a cap on spend and a clear conversion metric is the only viable path.

DECISION: NO-GO
