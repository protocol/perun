First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a market gap but lacks a stated edge explaining why this specific plan beats existing competitors or internal alternatives given the explicit recession risk and massive infrastructure costs.

Steelman: Launching a distinct mid-priced division with dedicated dealers captures a growing segment before a potential downturn, leveraging extensive research to ensure product-market fit.

Attack:
1. **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option avoids catastrophic fixed costs during a recession. A cheaper alternative exists: badge-engineering existing models into the mid-price slot using current dealerships, capturing the same upside without the capital expenditure of a new network. The proposal fails to demonstrate why a standalone brand is necessary over this lower-risk approach.
2. **Timing & Evidence:** The plan relies on a "growing" segment that "could shrink" alongside a known "recession risk." Launching a high-fixed-cost venture (new dealers, tooling) immediately before a potential economic contraction contradicts basic operational hygiene. The massive ad spend increases burn rate precisely when liquidity matters most.
3. **Fit:** The risk of cannibalizing existing low and premium lines is acknowledged but not mitigated. Without a unique mechanical or cost advantage, the new brand merely splits internal sales volume while adding overhead.

Pre-mortem: The launch coincides with the 1957 recession. Consumers trade down to economy cars or hold existing vehicles. The new dealer network, lacking volume and burdened by high startup costs, collapses, dragging the parent company's balance sheet into insolvency. The "extensive research" focused on styling, not macroeconomic resilience.

Blockers:
- **Economic Timing:** Launching a capital-intensive new division immediately prior to a probable recession creates an unsustainable cost structure.
- **Superior Alternative:** Utilizing existing distribution channels (current dealers) to serve the mid-market offers the same revenue potential with significantly lower risk and capital outlay. The proposal fails to justify rejecting this safer path.

Risks: Brand overlap, segment contraction, dealer profitability. These are compounded by the lack of a phased rollout or a "go-if" economic trigger.

The plan commits to maximum fixed costs (new brand, new dealers) in a volatile environment without a stated competitive edge that necessitates this specific expensive structure over a leaner alternative.

DECISION: NO-GO
