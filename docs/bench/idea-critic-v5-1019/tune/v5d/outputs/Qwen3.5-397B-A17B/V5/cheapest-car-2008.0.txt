First call: NO-GO. A "cheapest" label without a stated edge on safety, reliability, or total cost of ownership fails against used cars and scooters; need alone is not an advantage.

**Steelman:** Launching the world's cheapest car at $2,500 will succeed by unlocking latent demand from scooter families via a dedicated high-volume factory.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** Used cars offer proven safety and status at similar price points; scooters cost a fraction to buy and run. The proposal admits these are cheaper but offers no mechanism (e.g., financing, specific fuel efficiency, weather protection) to overcome their value.
2.  **Fit & Risk:** Marketing explicitly as "the cheapest" invites a quality stigma that alienates aspirational buyers moving up from scooters. Farmer protests threaten the dedicated factory's timeline, creating a single point of failure for the high-volume model.
3.  **Evidence Gap:** The proposal cites "basic four-seat" but provides no data on crash safety or durability. If cost-cutting compromises safety, one high-profile accident could destroy the brand before launch. The "cheapest" claim is a vulnerability, not a feature, without proof of acceptable standards.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The factory is delayed indefinitely by land disputes; early units suffer reliability issues due to aggressive cost targets; sales stall because families prefer the known quantity of a 5-year-old used car over a risky new "budget" vehicle.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal relies entirely on price ($2,500) but admits alternatives (used cars, scooters) are cheaper or better value. There is no stated product advantage (safety, efficiency, financing) to justify switching.
*   **Execution Threat:** Land protests pose an immediate, unresolved threat to the dedicated factory required for the volume targets.

**Risks:** Brand damage from "cheap" perception; safety liabilities; supply chain fragility.

**Decision:** The plan fails the operator test: it enters a competitive market with a negative differentiator ("cheapest") and a critical path dependency (land) under active attack. It does not beat "do nothing" or "buy used."

DECISION: NO-GO
