First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price, unproven demand, and regulatory ambiguity without a clear advantage over existing transport make this a speculative bet rather than a sound operator's choice.

Steelman: A novel, self-balancing transporter creates a new micro-mobility category, leveraging inventor prestige to overcome early adoption barriers despite regulatory and cost hurdles.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** $5k price, 40k unit capacity. High capital risk, unverified market fit, potential regulatory ban.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid sunk costs until legal status and use cases are proven; let competitors test the water.
3.  **Pilot/Limited Run:** Produce <5,000 units for institutional buyers only to validate utility and navigate regulations before consumer launch.

**Fit:** Assumes a strategy of market creation without evidence that customers value this specific solution enough to pay a premium or endure social friction.

**Pre-mortem:** The product fails because cities ban it from sidewalks/roads immediately, leaving no legal place to ride. The high price limits adoption to enthusiasts, failing to reach the volume needed to sustain the factory. Riders feel conspicuous and stop using it, killing word-of-mouth.

**Evidence:** All claims regarding demand, legal status, and user comfort are marked `assumed`. The proposal admits risks are "unclear" and "unproven" yet commits to full-scale production. No data supports the $5,000 price point elasticity or the 40,000 unit demand.

**Blockers:**
*   **Regulatory Void:** The core utility depends on legal access to public spaces, which is explicitly "unclear." Launching without resolved legal status risks immediate confiscation or bans, rendering the inventory worthless.
*   **Unproven Demand vs. Fixed Cost:** Committing to a 40,000-unit factory capacity based on "unproven" use cases and "high" price sensitivity is a fundamental financial mismatch. The plan assumes demand exists to fill capacity, contradicting the stated risk.

**Risks:** Social stigma, limited cargo capacity, high price point. These are secondary to the blockers but compound the failure mode.

The plan commits massive capital to a product with no legal footing and unverified demand. A sound operator would validate legality and secure institutional orders before sizing a factory.

DECISION: NO-GO
