First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it offers a compromised product at a premium price to a segment already served by diet cola, without evidence that "half-sugar" solves a specific unmet need better than existing options.

Steelman: A mid-calorie cola captures young men who reject the taste of artificial sweeteners in diet drinks but want fewer carbs than regular soda, leveraging a first-mover advantage before a rival launches.

Attack:
1. **Fit & Edge:** The core claim fails the "beat what buyers have today" test. If the goal is fewer calories, diet cola exists. If the goal is taste, regular cola exists. A "half-sugar" premium product assumes a non-existent preference for paying more to get neither full taste nor zero calories. No evidence suggests young men value "mid-calorie" enough to override price sensitivity and brand loyalty.
2. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves margins while the low-carb trend is validated as more than a fad. Reformulating existing lines or waiting for the rival to test the market (letting them absorb the education cost) is cheaper and safer.
3. **Pre-mortem:** The product launches, confuses consumers who don't understand why it costs more than regular cola but tastes worse, and is discontinued within a year as the low-carb trend fades. The "mid-calorie" category never forms.
4. **Evidence:** The proposal admits the low-carb trend "may be a fad" and that a rival is entering. Launching a premium product into a potentially vanishing market against an incumbent rival is financially unsound without proof of demand.

Blockers:
- **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits diet cola grows; it offers no fact showing why a premium, half-sugar drink beats diet cola for calorie-conscious users or regular cola for taste-conscious users.
- **Contradictory Economics:** Premium pricing for a compromised product (less sugar, likely altered taste) deters the very trial needed to succeed.

Risks: Trend longevity, consumer confusion, cannibalization of regular soda without gaining diet users.

The plan commits real money to an unproven category with a structural disadvantage (price vs. value). It fails to beat "do nothing" or "wait for rival data."

DECISION: NO-GO
