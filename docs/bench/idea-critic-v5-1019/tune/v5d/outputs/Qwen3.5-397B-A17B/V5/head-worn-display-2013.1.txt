First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state an edge; high price, privacy hostility, and undefined daily utility mean it does not beat existing solutions (smartphones) for the mass market as proposed.

**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to refine privacy norms and battery technology with a controlled user base before a general release, mitigating social risk through exclusivity.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Edge:** The proposal positions the device as an "everyday consumer product" immediately. However, the stated facts confirm "no clear everyday use for most people." A product cannot be everyday if the use case is undefined. The $1,500 price point creates a barrier incompatible with mass adoption without a proven killer app.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or delaying the general public release until a specific high-value use case (e.g., enterprise navigation, medical recording) is validated is superior. The current plan risks brand damage by forcing a social experiment on the general public before solving the core utility gap.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume the general launch fails. Cause: Public backlash against covert recording ("privacy objections") combined with ridicule due to lack of utility ("social acceptance"). The invitation-only phase is insufficient to solve societal norms if the subsequent step is immediate mass availability.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the risks (privacy, price, utility, social acceptance, battery) without offering a mechanism in the *general public* phase to overcome them. Selling to early adopters does not validate the "everyday" claim for the majority.

**Blockers:**
*   **Missing Edge:** The stated facts explicitly say there is "no clear everyday use for most people," yet the goal is to position it as an "everyday consumer product." This is a direct contradiction that guarantees market rejection.
*   **Contradictory Strategy:** You cannot mitigate "social acceptance" and "privacy objections" via an invite-only program and then immediately expose the device to the general public where those risks are highest.

**Risks:** High price sensitivity, regulatory bans on recording, battery frustration leading to churn.

**Decision:** The plan fails the basic test of product-market fit as described. The absence of a defined everyday use case is a fatal flaw for a consumer launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
