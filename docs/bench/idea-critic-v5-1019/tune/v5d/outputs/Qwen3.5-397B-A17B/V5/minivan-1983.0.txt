First call: GO. The stated edge is clear: a garage-fit van with car-like handling built on an existing compact platform offers a unique value proposition (convenience + familiarity) that current station wagons and full-size vans do not, specifically targeting families underserved by the "commercial" stigma of existing vans.

**Steelman:** Leverage the government-backed liquidity and existing compact-car infrastructure to create a new "minivan" category before rivals react, converting a dormant factory to minimize capex while delivering a vehicle that solves the garage-size vs. capacity trade-off for families.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" risks stagnation as the station wagon market saturates. Building a new platform is too costly given the cash constraint. Reusing the compact platform is the *only* financially viable path, even if it limits space; the trade-off is acceptable if the vehicle fits a garage, which is the primary constraint for the target buyer.
2.  **Fit:** This moves the north star of "saving the company" by creating a high-margin, defensible niche. The risk of buyers seeing it as a commercial van is mitigated by marketing it strictly as a family car (front-wheel drive, car styling).
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure would stem from execution delays or failing to convince dealers to stock a "weird" vehicle. However, the cost of delay is catastrophic given the recent near-collapse; speed to market is essential.
4.  **Evidence:** The government guarantee provides the necessary runway. The existing platform reduces development time and cost. The "no category exists" risk is actually an opportunity for first-mover advantage.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (no category, cash, copycats, space limits) are inherent to innovation and do not contradict the core logic; they are managed by the proposed strategy (new category creation, platform reuse, speed). The cash constraint is addressed by the loan guarantee and factory conversion.

**Risks:** Market education (solving the "commercial van" perception), platform limitations on cargo volume. These become kill criteria: if initial dealer feedback confirms strong "commercial" perception despite marketing, pivot messaging immediately.

**Decision:** The proposal turns constraints (cash, platform) into advantages (speed, cost) to attack an unmet need.

DECISION: GO
