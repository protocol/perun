First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a market gap but fails to state a competitive edge; "affordable" and "lightweight" are aspirations, not proven advantages against incumbents or the "dead niche" consensus of mass-market makers.

Steelman: A mid-size maker can profitably revive the two-seat roadster by leveraging existing front-engine/rear-drive components to undercut heritage brands on price while offering a purist driving experience absent in modern heavy convertibles.

Attack:
1. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" avoids capital risk in a shrinking segment. A cheaper alternative is a sporty trim of an existing two-door model, sharing tooling and safety certification costs, reaching similar buyers without a dedicated platform.
2. **Fit:** The company lacks sports-car heritage. Entering a niche defined by brand legacy without it requires a massive marketing spend not accounted for, eroding the "affordable" premise.
3. **Evidence & Math:** The proposal admits the segment has shrunk and sales may fade after early enthusiasts buy. With no data on the size of the remaining enthusiast pool or the cost of meeting new safety rules for low-volume convertibles, the unit economics are undefined. "Affordable" contradicts the high fixed costs of low-volume safety compliance.
4. **Pre-mortem:** The car launches to press praise but fails commercially because the total addressable market is too small to cover tooling, and the lack of brand heritage prevents premium pricing needed to offset low volume.

Blockers:
- **No stated edge:** The proposal offers no fact proving this specific maker can succeed where others see a "dead niche."
- **Contradictory constraints:** "Affordable" clashes with the high cost of low-volume safety compliance and the admitted lack of heritage.
- **Negative market trajectory:** The proposal itself states the segment is shrinking and demand is finite, with no plan to expand beyond early enthusiasts.

Risks: Rapid sales fade, brand dilution, unsustainable unit costs.

Without a verified cost advantage, a defined market size, or a strategy to overcome the heritage deficit, the plan fails as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
