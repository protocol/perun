First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high licensing costs and existing data do not prove an untested original format will succeed where incumbents use pilots.

Steelman: Leveraging unique viewing data to bypass traditional pilot testing allows immediate capture of pent-up demand for binge-worthy prestige content, differentiating the service from cable competitors.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Continue rising licensing costs with no ownership; safe but margin-compressive.
2. Traditional pilot: Test one episode, gauge reaction, then order series; lower cost, slower time-to-market, mimics broadcasters.
3. Proposed plan: High cost, high speed, total differentiation; beats do nothing only if data accurately predicts full-series retention, which is unproven.

Fit: Assumes the north star is shifting from "aggregator" to "studio/destination." If the strategy remains pure aggregation, this misaligns.

Pre-mortem: The series launches to silence. Without weekly water-cooler moments, buzz dies instantly. The "all-at-once" model confuses users accustomed to schedules. The $100M+ spend yields no subscriber lift, bankrupting the original content initiative. This is a one-way door; the money is gone. Cost of delay: Competitors may license similar talent first, but waiting a quarter to analyze data patterns is negligible compared to the loss of the entire budget.

Evidence: The claim relies on "viewing data." However, historical data reflects behavior within a library of licensed hits, not appetite for a specific, unknown original narrative. No evidence confirms users will stay for a whole season of an unproven show. The cost is a "large share" of the budget, creating a single-point failure risk.

Blockers:
1. No pilot or proof-of-concept: The proposal explicitly removes the industry-standard de-risking mechanism without substituting a verified alternative.
2. Unvalidated assumption: Viewing data for licensed content is treated as predictive for original commissions without evidence of correlation.
3. Financial concentration: Committing a "large share" of the budget to one unproven asset violates basic portfolio diversification for a first attempt.

Risks: Audience confusion, lack of sustained engagement, total capital loss.

The absence of a pilot and the concentration of financial risk constitute a factual failure of the plan as proposed. The data cited is insufficient to support the leap.

DECISION: NO-GO
