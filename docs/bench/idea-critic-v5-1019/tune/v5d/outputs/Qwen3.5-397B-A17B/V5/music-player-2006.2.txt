First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; being late with a larger screen and wireless sharing does not overcome an entrenched ecosystem and loyal user base without a clear price or exclusive content advantage.

Steelman: A superior hardware form factor and seamless wireless sharing can disrupt the market leader by offering a significantly better user experience that justifies switching costs.

Attack: The "edge" is weak. A larger screen is incremental, not transformative. Wireless sharing is a feature, not a platform killer, especially if labels restrict it as noted. Dropping copy-protection alienates existing partners (labels, other device makers) without a guaranteed replacement revenue stream. The proposal admits no price advantage and late market entry. The "large marketing budget" is a cost, not a strategic differentiator against a dominant incumbent with deep pockets.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids massive capex and partner backlash; preserves current licensing revenue.
2. License software only: Put the new interface on existing dominant hardware; test the UI edge without hardware risk.
3. Niche focus: Target a specific demographic underserved by the leader rather than a head-on assault.

Pre-mortem: The device launches, but users refuse to switch because their existing libraries are locked to the competitor's ecosystem (which this proposal abandons by dropping the old standard). Labels sue or block sharing features. The marketing spend burns cash with low conversion.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the leader has "enormous share," "loyal ecosystem," and that "labels may restrict sharing." It confirms the company is "late" and has "no clear price advantage." These are fatal blockers as stated.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: Need alone is not strategy; the proposal offers no reason why users would switch given the ecosystem lock-in.
2. Partner alienation: Dropping the promoted copy-protection standard destroys relationships with labels and other manufacturers without a secured alternative.
3. Late entry: Entering a mature market without a disruptive price or exclusive content model is historically futile.

Risks: Label litigation, low adoption, massive cash burn.

The plan fails the basic test of beating "do nothing" on the stated facts. It requires a fundamental strategic pivot (e.g., securing exclusive content, a radical price model, or a partnership) before proceeding.

DECISION: NO-GO
