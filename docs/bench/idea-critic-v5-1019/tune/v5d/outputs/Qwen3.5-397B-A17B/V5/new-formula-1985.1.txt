First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: while the new formula wins on taste, the plan to *entirely withdraw* the original ignores the verified fact that the brand's value exceeds its flavor profile. Taste is not the sole driver of purchase for a century-old flagship.

**Steelman:** Replacing the formula with a sweeter, blind-test-winning variant will reverse market share loss by aligning the product with consumer taste preferences.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fatal Flaw in Evidence:** The taste tests were blind; they measured liquid preference, not brand loyalty. The proposal admits testing did not inform participants of the original's removal. This invalidates the data for a *replacement* strategy. Consumers may prefer the rival's taste in a sip test but reject the loss of their heritage brand in a real-world purchase.
2.  **Better Way:** Introduce the sweeter formula as a *new* line extension (e.g., "New Cola") while retaining the original. This captures the taste-preference segment without alienating the heritage base. This is cheaper, reversible, and respects the "emotional attachment" risk explicitly raised.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** If launched as proposed, loyalists will perceive the removal as a betrayal, not an improvement. The emotional backlash will overshadow taste gains, causing a boycott that the sweeter formula cannot offset. The "no fallback" risk becomes a certainty: once withdrawn, the original cannot be easily restored without admitting failure.
4.  **Blocker:** The plan fails on its own terms. It relies on taste test data that explicitly excludes the primary variable: the emotional cost of removing the original. The stated risk of "emotional attachment" combined with the methodology gap (blind tests only) creates a factual contradiction: the evidence supports a *new product*, not a *replacement*.

**Decision:** The proposal commits an irreversible error by conflating taste preference with brand equity acceptance. The absence of a fallback and the flawed testing methodology regarding the removal scenario constitute a blocker.

Blockers: Taste test data is invalid for a replacement strategy as it excluded the variable of original formula removal; no fallback exists for the high-probability emotional backlash.
Risks: Bottler revolt, permanent brand damage, competitor capitalization on nostalgia.

DECISION: NO-GO
