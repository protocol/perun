First call: NO-GO. The proposal offers no stated edge; it combines two declining models without a mechanism to reverse the trend or beat growing big-box rivals.

Steelman: Merging creates immediate cost synergies and cross-selling opportunities that stabilize cash flow faster than organic turnaround.
Attack: Synergies are "uncertain" per the proposal, while the core disease—declining sales in underinvested stores—is doubled, not cured. Converting stores requires capital the post-restructuring buyer likely lacks. Selling older brands in discount stores assumes customer overlap that the proposal explicitly states does not exist ("customers differ").

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserves cash, avoids integration chaos, but accepts slow decline.
2. Organic pivot: Invest in remodeling existing discount stores to fight big-box rivals directly. Lower risk, focused capital.
3. Acquisition (Proposed): Highest cost, highest integration risk, unproven revenue synergy. It fails to beat "do nothing" on risk-adjusted returns because it adds debt and distraction to a weak balance sheet without a proven growth engine.

Fit: A post-restructuring company's north star is solvency and sustainable same-store sales growth. This deal prioritizes size over health, violating the stage-appropriate strategy of stabilization.

Pre-mortem: By 2006, integration costs have blown out, key talent from both chains has fled due to culture clash, and the combined entity faces a liquidity crisis as big-box rivals undercut prices on the now-confused merchandise mix. The deal is a one-way door; unwinding the merger would be catastrophic. Cost of delay is low; waiting allows the acquirer to strengthen its core before attempting expansion.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits sales are declining for both, stores are underinvested, savings are uncertain, and customers differ. These are not risks to be managed; they are factual blockers to the core thesis of revenue growth.

Blockers:
1. Conflicting customer bases prevent the proposed cross-selling strategy.
2. Uncertain cost savings cannot justify the capital outlay for a distressed acquirer.
3. No stated edge against growing big-box rivals; the merger creates a larger loser, not a stronger competitor.

Risks: Integration failure, brand dilution, liquidity crunch.

Kill criteria: None can be set because the premise is flawed. Do not proceed.

DECISION: NO-GO
