First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "premium price" and "no keyboard" are disadvantages against established business phones, and "no phone experience" is a liability, not an asset. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: A device leveraging existing music dominance and design control can disrupt carrier-led stagnation if the user experience is sufficiently superior to justify the premium and offset the lack of a physical keyboard.

Attack: The claim fails the evidence test. There is no proof that consumers will abandon physical keyboards for touchscreens on a primary communication device, nor that a single carrier exclusivity deal provides enough reach to sustain a premium price point without third-party apps. The "edge" relies entirely on the assumption that the music player integration compensates for phone inexperience, but phone makers are already adding music players, neutralizing this advantage. The proposal admits the device has no apps, a critical gap against competitors.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Protect current player sales; avoid high-risk entry into a carrier-dominated market.
2. License OS to multiple carriers: Gain reach, lose design control (violates core constraint).
3. Partner with existing phone maker: Gain experience, dilute brand control.

Fit: Assumes a shift from business-utility to consumer-experience phones, but this is unproven.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs because business users reject the lack of a keyboard, the single carrier limits volume, and the lack of apps makes it a toy, not a tool. The premium price becomes unjustifiable.

Blockers:
- No stated edge: The proposal lists disadvantages (no keyboard, no apps, no experience) without a factual basis for why this specific combination beats existing solutions.
- Contradictory evidence: Phone makers are already eroding player sales; adding a phone without apps may accelerate this erosion without capturing phone market share.

Risks: High price sensitivity, keyboard preference, limited distribution, app ecosystem void. These are not just risks; without a proven differentiator, they are fatal flaws in the current plan.

The plan commits to a premium price and exclusivity without demonstrating a unique value proposition that overcomes the admitted deficits. It bets on a behavioral shift (touch over keyboard) without evidence.

DECISION: NO-GO
