First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a need but fails to state an edge; "low-cost" is insufficient when buyers must source peripherals for a device lacking native display or input, and incumbents (laptops/tablets) already offer complete solutions at competitive prices.

Steelman: A $35 bare-board computer creates a mandatory entry point for student programming by stripping away non-essential hardware costs, relying on existing household peripherals to complete the setup.

Attack: The core claim collapses under the "complete solution" test. In 2012, the marginal cost savings of $35 versus a used laptop or cheap tablet does not offset the friction of sourcing compatible keyboards, displays, and power supplies. The proposal admits the charity lacks manufacturing and support experience, creating a high risk of supply chain failure (small batch sell-outs) and user abandonment due to lack of documentation or help desks. The "risk" of selling out is actually a distribution failure, not a validation of demand. Without a stated advantage in availability, curriculum integration, or total cost of ownership (including peripherals), the product loses to "do nothing" or buying existing cheap tablets.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Schools continue using existing labs or cheap laptops; zero execution risk.
2. Subsidized laptops: Negotiate bulk rates for complete used laptops; higher unit cost but immediate usability.
3. Software-only: Distribute programming environments for existing hardware; zero hardware risk.

Pre-mortem: The project launches, the first batch sells out quickly due to hype, but the charity cannot scale manufacturing. Students who buy boards cannot find compatible power supplies or displays. Frustrated parents return the units. The charity burns its reputation and capital on logistics it cannot handle, while the goal of "rekindling interest" fails because the barrier to entry (assembly) was too high.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: The proposal offers no fact showing why a $35 incomplete board beats a $100 complete laptop for the target user.
2. Capability gap: The charity explicitly lacks the required manufacturing and support experience to deliver the hardware reliably.

Risks: Supply chain inability to scale; user friction from peripheral sourcing; competition from complete devices.

DECISION: NO-GO
