First call: NO-GO. A loss-making target with leadership instability, active community hostility, and a free alternative requires more than a stated intent to run independently to justify a $7.5B premium; no specific edge is proven.

Steelman: Acquiring the dominant platform neutralizes a potential rival, secures the primary developer funnel for a historically hostile giant, and prevents the free alternative from becoming the default standard.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Risk the free competitor capturing market share while the target drifts without CEO leadership.
2. Partner deeply: Integrate tools without the cultural toxicity and $7.5B price tag of full ownership.
3. Build internal: Impossible to replicate the network effect quickly; cedes ground to competitors.

Fit: Assumes the buyer's north star is "developer adoption" over short-term profitability. If the goal is immediate ROI, this fails.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition triggers a mass exodus to the free competitor due to distrust, leaving the buyer with a hollow, expensive shell. The "independent" promise is viewed as a trojan horse, accelerating the very consolidation regulators fear.

Evidence: The proposal admits the target is loss-making, lacks a CEO, and faces vocal distrust. The $7.5B price is asserted without revenue multiples or synergy math. The "free competitor" threat is noted but not quantified.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: The proposal offers no specific fact (cost, product, timing) showing this deal beats the status quo or the cheaper "partner" alternative. Need alone is not an edge.
2. Contradictory premises: Buying a community-driven asset while the community explicitly distrusts the buyer, with no concrete plan to bridge that gap beyond "run independently," suggests the core value (the network) may evaporate post-close.

Risks: Developer defection, regulatory blocks, cultural clash, overpayment for a leaderless, unprofitable entity.

The absence of a pivot plan, a specific synergy calculation, or a mechanism to win over the hostile community makes this a speculative bet disguised as a strategy. A cheaper, staged partnership could test the waters without the irreversible $7.5B commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
