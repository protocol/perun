First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high cost and no apps make it inferior to existing rivals for the target demographic.

Steelman: Leveraging a popular messaging brand to lock teens into a closed ecosystem via a single carrier could work if the social utility outweighs the lack of apps and high price.

Attack:
1.  **No Edge:** Teenagers value network effects and content. Rivals offer large app catalogs; this proposal offers none. A "social-networking phone" with no third-party apps is functionally crippled compared to competitors.
2.  **Price Mismatch:** The target audience (teenagers) is price-sensitive. A mandatory data plan costing as much as a full smartphone plan removes the economic incentive, especially without the utility of an app store to justify the expense.
3.  **Platform Fragmentation:** Building a new in-house OS instead of using the company's existing mobile platform increases development risk and delays time-to-market, while offering no consumer benefit. It fractures the company's own ecosystem.
4.  **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** Two years of sunk development is a reason to cut losses, not to double down on a flawed product. It does not create future value.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid launching a product likely to fail against established app-rich competitors.
*   **Cheaper Path:** Port the messaging features to the company's existing mobile platform or an established OS, lowering costs and leveraging existing app ecosystems.
*   **Staged Launch:** Launch with an app store or a clear path to one, and a lower-cost data tier specifically for teens.

Blockers:
*   **No App Store:** The core value proposition for teens (social apps) cannot be delivered without third-party developers.
*   **High Cost:** The pricing model excludes the target demographic's budget constraints.
*   **Wrong OS:** Using a new OS adds unnecessary risk and cost without a compensating advantage.

Risks: Market rejection due to lack of apps; inability to compete on price; brand damage from launching a crippled product; wasting further capital on a sinking project.

The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" or simpler alternatives. It asks teens to pay a premium for less functionality.

DECISION: NO-GO
