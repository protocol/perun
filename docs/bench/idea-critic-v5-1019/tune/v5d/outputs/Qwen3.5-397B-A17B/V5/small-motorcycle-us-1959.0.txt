First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it admits Americans favor big bikes, the brand is unknown, and the product may fail at US highway speeds. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: A small, friendly bike can create a new "commuter/student" niche outside the hostile "big bike" culture, using non-traditional retail to bypass image barriers.

Attack: The core mechanism relies on Americans wanting small bikes for commuting, yet the proposal states the market is small and preferences lean large. The product's potential mechanical failure at highway speeds is a factual blocker if US geography requires highway use, which it typically does. The "unknown brand" risk is compounded by the "rough image" of the category; a weak entrant with a potentially defective product cannot overcome this without a massive marketing spend, which contradicts the "scarce foreign currency" constraint.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserves capital, avoids reputation damage from potential mechanical failures.
2. License technology to an established US manufacturer: Reduces currency risk and leverages existing distribution, though margins are lower.
3. Export only larger models: Aligns with stated US preferences but ignores the company's current strength in small bikes.

Pre-mortem: The subsidiary opens, sales are negligible because students cannot highway-commute safely on 50cc bikes, larger models break down during test runs confirming durability fears, and the parent company drains its foreign currency reserves with no ROI.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the market is small, the image is rough, the brand is unknown, and the product may not hold up. These are internal contradictions to a "GO" decision. No external data supports a hidden demand.

Blockers:
1. Product suitability: The 50cc engine likely cannot handle US highway distances/speeds required for commuting, rendering the primary use case invalid.
2. Economic constraint: Scarce foreign currency makes a high-risk, unproven market entry irresponsible without a guaranteed upside, which is absent.

Risks: Brand rejection, distribution failure, currency loss. These are secondary to the blockers.

The plan fails the basic test of product-market fit and financial prudence as stated. It bets on changing consumer behavior and overcoming mechanical limitations with no evidence either is possible.

DECISION: NO-GO
