First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "priced well below" European leaders contradicts the requirement to match them on quality and refinement while building a separate dealer network from scratch. Low price signals low status in luxury, undermining the core claim of matching incumbents.

Steelman: A distinct brand identity can overcome economy-car stigma if the product offers equal quality at a disruptive price point, forcing a market re-evaluation.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current facts.
1. **Fit & Edge:** Luxury buyers pay for prestige and heritage, not just refinement. A "mass-market" parent brand actively subtracts value in this segment. Being "priced well below" confirms a lack of brand equity, positioning the car as "cheap luxury" rather than "accessible prestige." There is no evidence that cost savings can be passed to consumers without destroying margins required to fund the new dealer network.
2. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A "better way" would be upgrading the existing top-tier model with premium materials (a halo trim) to test waters without the sunk cost of a separate dealer network and clean-sheet engineering. The proposed path incurs massive fixed costs (dealers, R&D) before selling a single unit.
3. **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: The flagship launches, critics praise the engineering, but buyers refuse to pay near-luxury prices for a logo associated with economy. The separate dealers sit empty, bleeding cash. The single model cannot sustain the network overhead. The brand becomes a synonym for "trying too hard," damaging the parent's reliability reputation.
4. **Evidence Gap:** The proposal admits development has "already cost heavily," suggesting a sunk-cost fallacy driving the decision rather than future ROI. No data proves buyers will trust this specific manufacturer with a luxury purchase solely based on a new badge.

**Blockers:**
- **Economic Contradiction:** Matching European quality/refinement while pricing "well below" and funding a new dealer network likely results in negative unit economics. The math does not work as stated.
- **Brand Physics:** The parent brand's "economy" equity is a negative multiplier in the luxury segment; a new badge alone is unproven to reverse this without decades of marketing spend not accounted for.
- **Line-up Risk:** A single flagship cannot support a standalone dealer network's operational costs.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, massive capital burn, rejection by target demographic.

The plan fails the basic economic test of the luxury segment as described.

DECISION: NO-GO
