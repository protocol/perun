First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price and locked supply chain are disadvantages versus existing cheap juicers and fresh produce, not advantages.

Steelman: A premium, hassle-free juicing ecosystem where freshness is guaranteed by proprietary packs and device verification, justifying a subscription model for busy, affluent consumers.

Attack:
1. **Options:** "Do nothing" (buy a $50 juicer and grocery store fruit) costs 90% less upfront and offers infinite variety. The "cheaper alternative" (standard juicer) reaches the same upside (fresh juice) without internet dependency or vendor lock-in. The proposal fails to explain why a user would accept higher recurring costs and hardware risk for marginally less prep time.
2. **Fit:** Assumes a market willing to pay a luxury hardware premium for a commodity outcome (juice). No evidence suggests the "freshness check" feature solves a painful enough problem to warrant a $699 entry fee plus subscription markup.
3. **Pre-mortem:** The device sits unused because the subscription is too expensive per cup compared to local juice bars or DIY. The "internet required" constraint causes failure when Wi-Fi drops or servers lag, rendering a $699 appliance a paperweight. One-way door: custom tooling and inventory commit tens of millions before product-market fit is proven.
4. **Evidence:** No data provided on customer willingness to pay a 5x hardware premium or recurring subscription markup over spot-market produce. The "freshness" claim is unverified against actual spoilage rates of store-bought fruit used within 24 hours.

Blockers:
- **No stated edge:** The proposal admits higher costs (device + subscription) and more constraints (internet, proprietary packs) than alternatives, with no compensating advantage proven (e.g., significantly superior taste, nutrition, or time savings that users value at this price point).
- **Economic mismatch:** High fixed cost ($699) plus high variable cost (subscription) vs. low-cost alternatives creates a negative value proposition without a demonstrated unique mechanism.

Risks: Market size overestimation, subscription churn due to price, hardware reliability issues, internet dependency friction.

The plan commits significant capital to a model that is strictly more expensive and less flexible than the status quo, with no evidence of a compensating superpower.

DECISION: NO-GO
