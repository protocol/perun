First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a clear need (integration) but offers no stated edge explaining why this specific move beats the current supplier arrangement today, while explicitly listing unproven performance, software compatibility risks, and high transition costs without a mitigation plan.

**Steelman:** Leveraging proven phone silicon architecture for laptops will unlock superior performance-per-watt and ecosystem unity, outweighing transition friction.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits the laptop chips are "unproven" against the supplier's. Without benchmark data showing the phone-derived design meets laptop-class thermal and sustained performance demands, the core value proposition is an assumption, not a fact.
2.  **Better Way:** "Do nothing" maintains guaranteed software compatibility and avoids the stated engineering drain. A hybrid approach (select models only) is safer than a full line migration but isn't proposed.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** If the transition fails, the company faces a fragmented ecosystem where key professional software breaks, causing immediate revenue loss and brand damage. The "one-way door" of ending the supplier relationship removes the fallback option before success is verified.
4.  **Blockers:** The explicit admission that existing software compatibility is a risk and the chips are unproven constitutes a blocker. A plan to migrate a mature software ecosystem cannot proceed on the hope that compatibility will hold; it requires verified emulation layers or native ports before approval. The cost of engineering time is cited as a negative with no offsetting ROI calculation provided.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** Unproven performance vs. incumbent; undefined software compatibility path; no ROI to justify engineering drain.
*   **Risks:** Buyer confusion; supplier retaliation; thermal throttling in laptop chassis.

The proposal asks to burn a bridge (supplier relationship) and spend significant capital based on unverified assumptions about performance and compatibility. It fails the "must beat do nothing" test because the "do nothing" option carries zero compatibility risk and zero transition cost, whereas this plan guarantees both.

DECISION: NO-GO
