First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "unprecedented" indicates no proof that component buyers care about brand, and the cost is high with unproven demand.

**Steelman:** If consumers begin demanding specific internal components by name, computer makers will be forced to source from the branded supplier to maintain sales volume, justifying the ad subsidy.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Edge:** The proposal admits consumer advertising for components is unprecedented. Without evidence that end-users make purchasing decisions based on invisible internal parts, there is no edge over the status quo of selling to manufacturers. Need is not an edge.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves the entire marketing budget for proven B2B channels. A cheaper alternative is negotiating direct volume discounts or technical exclusivity with computer makers, which guarantees shelf space without gambling on consumer psychology.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Consumers ignore the logo; computer makers resent the brand overshadowing their own marketing and refuse to participate or quietly comply without pushing sales; the budget is exhausted with zero lift in unit sales.
4.  **Evidence:** The risks listed (resentment, no sales impact) are treated as possibilities, but the proposal offers no data showing consumers *can* be educated to care about a chip. The claim that this will drive sales is an assumption, not a fact.
5.  **Blocker Test:**
    *   **Blockers:** The proposal commits a "large share of the marketing budget" to an unproven mechanism with no stated evidence that the target audience (consumers) influences the purchase of this specific product type. The core claim (consumers will ask for the chip) contradicts the stated fact that buyers currently do not know or care which chip is inside. This is a fundamental disconnect between the mechanism and the market reality as described.
    *   **Risks:** Manufacturer resentment, budget drain, ineffective messaging.

The plan fails as proposed because it bets the farm on a behavioral shift (consumer demand for components) that the proposal itself admits has no precedent and offers no evidence to support. It does not beat "do nothing" or direct B2B incentives on the stated facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
