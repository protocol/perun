First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; automatic enrollment and visible defaults are coercive distribution, not a product advantage over existing social networks.

Steelman: Leveraging the existing user base and inbox context creates immediate network density and utility that standalone social networks cannot match, overcoming the cold-start problem.

Attack:
1. **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves trust and avoids regulatory scrutiny. A "opt-in" alternative reaches the same upside (network effects) without the backlash of forced enrollment. The proposed plan does not beat "do nothing" on trust, nor does it beat "opt-in" on sustainability.
2. **Fit:** A search company's north star is organizing information and maintaining user trust. Forced social exposure contradicts the implicit contract of private communication (email).
3. **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Users feel violated by default visibility, leading to mass opt-outs and negative press. Regulators investigate anti-competitive bundling and privacy violations. The feature becomes a "ghost town" inside the inbox because social interaction is not the primary intent of email users.
4. **Evidence:** The proposal admits risks of privacy disregard and regulatory attention but proceeds anyway. No evidence is provided that users *want* social features in their inbox or that visible defaults are necessary for adoption.
5. **Blocker Test:**
   - **Blockers:** The plan relies on a negative premise (users won't join voluntarily) to justify coercive mechanics (auto-enroll, visible defaults). This removes the route to sustainable upside (voluntary engagement) and invites immediate existential risk (regulatory action, trust erosion). The alternative of "opt-in with incentives" reaches the same upside cheaper and safer.
   - **Risks:** Low ongoing use, difficulty reversing exposure.

The core mechanism (coercion) contradicts the product's foundational value (privacy/trust). The risks are not theoretical; they are inherent to the proposed execution. A capped pilot with explicit opt-in is required before any automatic enrollment. As proposed, this fails the blocker test.

DECISION: NO-GO
