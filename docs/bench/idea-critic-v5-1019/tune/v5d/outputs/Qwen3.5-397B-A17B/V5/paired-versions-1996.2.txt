Steelman: Releasing two versions creates artificial scarcity that compels social trading via the link cable, transforming a single-player RPG into a communal event that drives word-of-mouth and requires two purchases to complete the game.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Release one version. Lower risk, but no forced social mechanic; likely fades into obscurity on aging hardware.
2. Single premium edition: Includes all creatures. Removes the trading necessity entirely; kills the core loop.
3. Two versions (Proposed): Highest execution risk and consumer friction, but the only path to creating a "must-trade" phenomenon that offsets hardware decline with software virality.

Fit: Assumes the goal is cultural impact and maximizing software attach rate over hardware lifespan. A standard release does not move this north star.

Pre-mortem: The game fails because parents refuse to buy two cartridges for one child, viewing it as greedy. The link cable requirement creates a barrier if few own one. Cost of delay is high; waiting for a newer console guarantees death against established competitors.

Evidence: No sales data or market surveys are provided. The claim that trading will occur relies entirely on the assumption that completionism outweighs the cost of a second unit and cable.

Blockers: NONE based strictly on provided facts. The risks (declining popularity, price grab perception) are uncertainties, not proven failures. The publisher's uncertainty is a state of mind, not a factual barrier to the mechanic working.

Risks: Consumer backlash regarding "cash grab"; low adoption of link cables limiting the trading loop; cannibalization of sales if players choose only one version and stop playing.

Kill Criteria: If pre-orders for the second version do not reach 40% of the first version's volume within 30 days of launch, halt production of the second SKU and pivot to a single "complete" edition. Leading metric: Ratio of link cable sales to game unit sales.
