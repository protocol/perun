First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a capital-intensive path (buying a large factory, full BEV lineup) without stating an edge over incumbents who already possess factories, supply chains, and brand trust; need alone is not an edge.

**Steelman:** A dedicated BEV strategy with a purchased legacy factory accelerates scale and signals total commitment, bypassing the slow ramp of greenfield construction.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capital Mismatch:** The proposal admits the company lacks capital for the factory and batteries, yet makes these the core commitment. Buying a large factory from established makers contradicts the "young carmaker" constraint; legacy assets often carry hidden liabilities and retooling costs that exceed greenfield builds for novel architectures.
2.  **Infrastructure Gap:** In 2008, charging scarcity is a hard blocker for mass-market sedans. Without a proprietary network or partnership (not mentioned), the product is unusable for key segments.
3.  **Execution Risk:** Mass production experience is zero. Jumping straight to a "large factory" maximizes fixed costs before proving demand or manufacturing competence. A smaller pilot or contract manufacturing would reduce downside.
4.  **Competitive Response:** Established makers have deep pockets to slash prices or flood the market if threatened, a risk this undercapitalized entity cannot survive.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserve cash, wait for battery costs to drop and infrastructure to mature.
*   *Contract Manufacturing:* Use existing capacity (e.g., NUMMI, Toyota/GM joint venture) to avoid massive CapEx.
*   *Niche Entry:* Start with a high-margin low-volume roadster to fund the sedan later.

**Pre-mortem:** The company buys the factory, burns cash on retooling, fails to secure battery supply at scale, and goes bankrupt before delivering 1,000 units because the charging network never materialized.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Proposal states capital is insufficient for the required factory and batteries.
*   **Fact:** 2008 charging infrastructure is scarce, limiting market viability.
*   **Logic:** Committing to a large fixed asset without the funds or experience to operate it guarantees insolvency.

**Risks:** Incumbent price wars, supply chain failure, production hell.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the basic test of solvency and fit. It proposes spending money the company does not have on assets it cannot efficiently operate in an unready market.

DECISION: NO-GO
