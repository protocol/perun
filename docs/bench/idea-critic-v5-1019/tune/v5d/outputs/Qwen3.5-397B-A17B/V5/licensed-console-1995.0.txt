First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; small royalties and dominant incumbents offer no path to beat existing options or justify brand dilution.

Steelman: Licensing converts fixed R&D and marketing costs into variable royalty revenue, allowing market entry without capital risk.

Attack:
1. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves brand equity and avoids cluttering the market with low-margin hardware. A direct, high-end release (though costlier) builds brand value; this low-cost license actively erodes it.
2. **Fit:** A "small royalty" contradicts the need for significant revenue impact given the "small market share." It fails to move the needle.
3. **Pre-mortem:** The licensee, lacking the maker's core competency, under-markets the device. Consumers associate the cheap plastic console with the licensor's premium brand, damaging reputation. Sales flop; royalties are negligible.
4. **Evidence:** The proposal admits royalties are "small" and marketing is ceded to a partner who "may not market hard." These are not risks; they are stated facts that guarantee failure. The core claim (successful consumer product) contradicts the stated mechanism (passive, low-margin licensing).
5. **Blockers:** The plan relies on a partner to solve the maker's primary deficit (marketing/brand reach) while offering them minimal incentive (small royalty) against dominant competitors. This is a structural failure, not an execution risk. The stated facts show the target users will likely reject an unmarketed, generic console from a minor PC maker.

Blockers:
- Incentive mismatch: Licensee has little motive to fight dominant incumbents for small royalties.
- Brand erosion: Low-cost hardware dilutes the maker's existing brand equity with no compensatory upside.
- No stated edge: The plan offers no advantage over established consoles.

Risks:
- Consumer confusion regarding brand identity.
- Opportunity cost of distracting leadership from core business.

This is a passive strategy in an active, winner-take-all market. It surrenders control of the brand experience for negligible returns.

DECISION: NO-GO
