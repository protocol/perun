First call: NO-GO. The proposal cites a declining asset with falling revenue and no strategic fit for a wireless carrier lacking consumer media experience; need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Combining two distressed media assets creates sufficient scale to disrupt the duopoly of dominant ad platforms through mobile synergy.

Attack: The claim fails on verified facts. The target's traffic and revenue are in structural decline, not cyclical; buying a falling knife to fix another falling knife does not create upward momentum. The acquirer has zero history in consumer media, making integration a high-probability failure point rather than a manageable risk. The "scale" argument assumes the sum of two shrinking parts equals a growing whole, which contradicts the stated trend of multi-year declines.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserves capital, avoids integrating incompatible cultures and decaying tech stacks.
2. Build organic: Invest the $4.8B in internal mobile capabilities where the carrier has core competency.
3. Buy a growing asset: Acquire a smaller but ascending mobile-first company instead of a legacy portal.

Pre-mortem: Two years post-acquisition, the combined entity misses revenue targets by 40% as legacy ad formats become obsolete. Integration costs spiral due to cultural clashes between telecom engineering and media creative teams. The carrier writes down the asset by $3B, distracting leadership from core connectivity growth.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits falling traffic, falling revenue, repeated management instability, and zero acquirer experience. These are not risks to be mitigated; they are blockers inherent to the deal structure. No evidence suggests a turnaround mechanism exists.

Blockers:
1. Structural decline: Target revenue and traffic are falling, contradicting the growth required to compete.
2. Capability gap: Acquirer has no history in consumer media, invalidating the execution plan.
3. Negative synergy: Combining two declining businesses accelerates value destruction rather than creating scale.

Risks: Ad market shift to mobile-first native platforms (already happening), talent exodus post-acquisition, brand dilution.

The plan commits $4.8B to a strategy where the core premises (growth via combination, execution capability) are directly contradicted by the stated facts. This is a classic "diworsification" move that fails the basic test of buying strength to fix weakness.

DECISION: NO-GO
