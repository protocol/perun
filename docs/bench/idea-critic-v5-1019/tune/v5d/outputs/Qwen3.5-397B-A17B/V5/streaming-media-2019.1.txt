First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (direct audience access) but fails to state an edge: a lower price alone does not beat incumbents who already possess scale, data, and habit, especially when funded by burning cash and sacrificing guaranteed licensing revenue.

**Steelman:** Sacrificing short-term guaranteed licensing cash flow to build a proprietary direct-to-consumer relationship is the only way to survive the industry's inevitable shift away from third-party distribution, provided the content library is strong enough to pull subscribers despite a smaller catalog than rivals.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** The plan trades certain, high-margin licensing revenue for uncertain, low-margin subscription revenue plus massive operating losses. Without a stated differentiation beyond price, the "churn" of subscribers leaving after watching specific titles will outpace acquisition, making the "tens of millions" target mathematically improbable before cash runs out.
2.  **Capability Gap:** The company has never run a global technology platform. Building this while simultaneously marketing to millions introduces execution risk that could degrade the user experience, driving users back to established, stable competitors regardless of price.
3.  **Cannibalization:** The proposal admits to cannibalizing cable and theatrical revenue. Without a precise model showing that the Lifetime Value (LTV) of a direct subscriber exceeds the sum of lost licensing fees plus cannibalized box office/cable income, this is value destruction, not creation.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "hybrid" approach (licensing non-core content while launching a niche, premium service) or a joint venture with an existing tech player offers the same strategic pivot with significantly lower capital exposure and execution risk.

**Blockers:**
*   **Missing Edge:** Price is not a moat against entrenched networks effects; the proposal offers no unique mechanism to retain users cheaper than competitors can acquire them.
*   **Negative Unit Economics:** The plan explicitly relies on multi-year losses without demonstrating a path to profitability that exceeds the opportunity cost of the forgone licensing deals.
*   **Unproven Capacity:** The core competency (content creation) does not transfer to the required competency (platform engineering), creating a high probability of launch failure.

**Risks:** Massive cash burn, brand damage from a poor tech launch, and irreversible loss of licensing partners who will back competing services.

The absence of a verified unit-economic model and a credible technology roadmap constitutes a failure to prove the plan works as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
