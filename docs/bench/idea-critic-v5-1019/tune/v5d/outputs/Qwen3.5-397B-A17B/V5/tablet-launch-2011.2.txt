First call: NO-GO. A need for tablets exists, but the proposal states no edge over the market leader; matching price with a tiny app catalog and an unproven OS is a recipe for irrelevance.

Steelman: Leverage the computer maker's brand and distribution to force a foothold, betting that heavy ad spend and developer outreach can rapidly close the app gap before consumers reject the unproven OS.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids burning cash on a likely failure during leadership transition.
2. Low-price entry: Undercut the leader significantly to compensate for the weak app catalog (hardware loss leader).
3. Delay: Wait for leadership stability and a matured app ecosystem.

Fit: Assumes the goal is immediate market share, but launching an unproven product during leadership transition contradicts stable growth.

Pre-mortem: The tablet launches, reviewers praise the hardware but pan the lack of apps. Consumers, seeing no advantage over the dominant leader, refuse to buy. Heavy ad spend accelerates awareness of these flaws. Inventory piles up, forcing a fire sale that damages the parent brand. The OS fails to gain traction, and the acquisition becomes a write-off. This is a one-way door; once the brand is stained as "unproven," recovery is costly. Delay costs only opportunity time, whereas failure costs capital and reputation.

Evidence: The proposal admits the app catalog is a "small fraction" and the OS has "almost no market share." In platforms, apps drive users, and users drive developers. Without a critical mass of apps, price parity offers no value. The "leadership transition" introduces execution risk exactly when focus is needed most.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: Price parity with inferior utility (apps) guarantees failure against a dominant network effect.
2. Timing: Launching during leadership transition invites strategic drift when the product needs absolute focus.

Risks: Consumer rejection of unproven OS; developer apathy despite outreach; cash burn without ROI.

The plan commits real money without a mechanism to overcome the network effect deficit. It must be revised to include a distinct advantage (e.g., aggressive pricing, unique hardware integration, or delayed launch until app parity improves).

DECISION: NO-GO
