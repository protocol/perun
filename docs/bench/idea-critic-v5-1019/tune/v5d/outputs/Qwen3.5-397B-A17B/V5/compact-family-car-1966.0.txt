First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state a specific edge (cost, product, access, or timing advantage) that allows this new 1100 cc car to beat established rivals selling small cars at low prices. Need alone is insufficient without a stated differentiator.

Steelman: A dedicated 1100 cc car, built at the new plant and sold via a proprietary network, captures the family segment by offering superior availability or brand experience that generalist rivals cannot match.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" avoids the massive capital outlay for a dedicated dealer network. A "cheaper alternative" exists: utilizing existing distribution channels to sell a competitive small car immediately, avoiding the fixed cost of new infrastructure. The proposal does not explain why the dedicated network is essential to the upside.
2.  **Fit & Capacity:** The new plant requires near-capacity utilization to pay off. Relying on a single new model to fill a new plant while simultaneously funding a new dealer network creates a dangerous cash-flow bottleneck. If buyers prefer cheaper or larger cars (as the risk assessment admits), the plant becomes a stranded asset.
3.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits rivals already sell small cars at low prices. Without a stated mechanism to undercut them on cost or outperform them on value despite the added expense of a new network, the unit economics likely fail.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The project fails because the dealer network drains capital before volume scales, forcing price increases that make the car uncompetitive against incumbents.

Blockers:
*   **Missing Edge:** No stated fact explains how this car beats existing low-price rivals despite the added cost of a dedicated dealer network.
*   **Capital Conflict:** The requirement to fill the new plant conflicts with the high fixed cost of building a new sales channel, creating a financial gap not addressed in the plan.

Risks: Buyer preference shifts, price wars with incumbents, underutilized plant capacity.

The plan commits to high fixed costs (plant + network) without a proven route to the volume necessary to sustain them. A cheaper, staged approach using existing channels must be validated first.

DECISION: NO-GO
