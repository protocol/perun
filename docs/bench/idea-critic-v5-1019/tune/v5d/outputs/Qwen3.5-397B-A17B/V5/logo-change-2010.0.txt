First call: NO-GO. The proposal offers no stated edge over the current asset; it relies on subjective "contemporary" aesthetics without evidence of customer demand or competitive necessity.

Steelman: A simultaneous, unannounced rebrand maximizes shock value to signal an immediate break from the past, forcing market attention.

Attack: This approach ignores Chesterton's Fence; the "dark blue square" is the primary recognition heuristic for loyal customers. Removing it entirely while offering no tested alternative destroys brand equity instantly. The "no advance announcement" strategy converts a marketing risk into a certainty of confusion, as social media backlash will fill the information vacuum with negative narratives before the company can explain. The proposal admits the design was untested; deploying an untested core asset across all channels simultaneously is a one-way door with catastrophic reversal costs.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retains equity, zero cost.
2. Phased rollout: Test the new wordmark in limited channels, measure sentiment, and iterate.
3. Hybrid evolution: Gradually introduce the new typeface while retaining the recognizable blue square anchor.

Pre-mortem: The launch triggers immediate social media outrage over "erasing history." Sales dip as confused customers fail to recognize the brand on shelves or apps. The company is forced to revert, admitting failure and looking indecisive.

Blockers:
1. Untested core asset: The proposal explicitly states the new design lacks customer validation, violating the basic requirement for high-stakes brand changes.
2. Simultaneous deployment: Rolling out to every channel at once removes the ability to mitigate failure, turning a manageable risk into a systemic threat.
3. No stated edge: "Looking contemporary" is not a strategic advantage without data showing the current logo is actively repelling the target demographic.

Risks: Total loss of brand recognition, alienation of the core loyalist base, irreversible reputational damage from perceived arrogance.

Kill criteria: If a blind test with 500 loyal customers shows less than 80% recognition of the new mark compared to the old, the project stops.

The absence of testing and the "big bang" deployment method constitute factual failures in the plan's construction.

DECISION: NO-GO
