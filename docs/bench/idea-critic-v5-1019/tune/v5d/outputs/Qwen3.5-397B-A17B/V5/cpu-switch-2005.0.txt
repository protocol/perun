First call: GO. The stated edge is survival and competitiveness: the current roadmap cannot deliver thin, efficient laptops, while the dominant architecture offers immediate performance and efficiency advantages that buyers demand today. Need alone is insufficient, but the explicit failure of the current supplier to meet thermal and efficiency requirements creates a definitive product gap that only this switch fills.

Steelman: Migrating to the dominant architecture is the only viable path to produce competitive thin laptops within two years, using translation to preserve the existing software ecosystem during the transition.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (sales freeze, developer friction, performance overhead, brand dilution, past pain) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. None prove the plan fails as proposed; they are manageable risks. The "painful earlier transition" is a historical fact, not a current impossibility, especially given the maturity of translation layers compared to past attempts.

Risks: Sales dip during announcement, slower translated app performance, potential brand commoditization, and developer resistance. These are mitigated by the two-year timeline and the necessity of the move for product viability.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the translation layer performs too poorly for pro users or if the brand loses its premium perception entirely. However, staying on the current path guarantees failure in the thin laptop segment due to thermal constraints.

Door: Two-way door for the strategy (can pivot back if the market rejects it, though unlikely given the hardware deficit), but one-way for the specific engineering investment. Cost of delay is high: continuing with inefficient chips cedes the growing thin-laptop market to competitors immediately.

Evidence: The proposal states the current supplier's roadmap lags and fails on thermals/efficiency. This is the core fact driving the decision. No alternative exists to fix the thermal/efficiency gap in the required timeframe.

Kill Criteria: If, after 12 months, translated application performance is below 70% of native speed for key pro workflows, or if sales of new thin models miss targets by >20% due to brand confusion, pivot to a hybrid approach or accelerate native app incentives. Leading metric: % of top 100 apps running natively vs. translated by month 18.

DECISION: GO
