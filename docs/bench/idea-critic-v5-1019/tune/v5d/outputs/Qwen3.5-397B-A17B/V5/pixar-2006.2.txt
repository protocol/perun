First call: NO-GO. The stated price ($7.4B) against a one-film-per-year output creates an unjustifiable valuation gap without a stated edge proving immediate scalability or library monetization that beats current market alternatives.

Steelman: Acquiring the studio is the only way to prevent a rival from securing the talent and to instantly pivot the buyer's struggling hand-drawn unit to a hit-making CGI model.

Options:
1. **Buy:** Highest cost, highest integration risk, irreversible capital allocation.
2. **Do Nothing/Partner:** Retain cash, risk losing the studio to a competitor, continue hand-drawn underperformance.
3. **Minority Stake/Co-pro:** Lower cost, shared risk, preserves creative independence, but fails to secure exclusive control.

Fit: Aligns with the need for hits but contradicts fiscal prudence given the "very high" price relative to output.

Pre-mortem: The deal fails because the "one film a year" constraint cannot be scaled without breaking the creative culture that drives value, leaving the buyer with massive debt and no increased volume. The founder, now a major shareholder, blocks necessary operational changes. This is a one-way door; the cost of delay is losing the partner, but the cost of acting is potential insolvency or decades of amortization drag.

Evidence: The proposal admits the price is "very high" against "one film a year." No evidence suggests the studio can increase throughput without degrading quality. The risk of culture clash and dependence on specific leaders is acknowledged but unmitigated in the "buy outright" structure.

Blockers:
1. **Valuation Mismatch:** The core math ($7.4B for ~1 film/year) fails as proposed. The upside relies on unstated assumptions about scaling production or library value not present in the facts.
2. **Concentration Risk:** Dependence on a few leaders and a single major shareholder (the founder) creates a single point of failure that an outright purchase exacerbates rather than solves.

Risks: Culture clash, integration failure, stock dilution.

The plan commits real money without a capped first step or a mechanism to validate the scaling hypothesis before full acquisition. A cheaper, staged alternative (e.g., exclusive multi-picture deal with option to buy) reaches the same upside (securing the pipeline) with less risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
