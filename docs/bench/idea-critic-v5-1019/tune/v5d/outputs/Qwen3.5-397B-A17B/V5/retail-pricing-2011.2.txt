First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge proving this specific leadership transfer (electronics to department stores) beats the incumbent discount model for this specific older, coupon-trained demographic.

Steelman: Eliminating coupon dependency and modernizing the store layout will attract higher-margin shoppers and restore brand value faster than incremental discounting.

Attack: The core failure mode is immediate revenue collapse. Since "most sales" historically come from discounted items, removing discounts without a transition period alienates the entire existing base before a new one is proven. The electronics success relied on a different customer psychology (tech early adopters vs. budget-conscious department shoppers) and a different era (pre-omnichannel dominance). Applying a "shock therapy" approach across the whole chain without a pilot ignores the specific risk that the old base leaves before the new base arrives.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Continued slow decline, but preserves current cash flow.
2. Full immediate switch (Proposed): High risk of catastrophic revenue drop; irreversible brand damage if it fails.
3. Staged pilot: Test the "everyday pricing" in 5-10 stores to measure elasticity and churn before chain-wide rollout. This offers the same upside potential with drastically lower downside risk.

Fit: While modernization aligns with a general "north star" of health, the method contradicts the reality of the current customer base's behavior.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-launch, sales are down 40%. The older customers stopped shopping immediately due to sticker shock, and the new demographic did not materialize because the chain lacks the brand equity of the CEO's previous electronics venture. Cash flow dries up, forcing bankruptcy before a pivot is possible.

Evidence: The proposal admits most sales are discount-driven and offers no data showing this specific customer base desires everyday pricing. The lack of a pilot is a fatal flaw given the total dependence on the old model.

Blockers:
1. No pilot test despite high risk of alienating the primary revenue source.
2. Assumption that electronics retail success transfers directly to mid-market department stores without validation.
3. Immediate chain-wide rollout removes the ability to learn and adjust.

Risks: Total loss of existing customer base; insufficient time to build new brand perception.

DECISION: NO-GO
