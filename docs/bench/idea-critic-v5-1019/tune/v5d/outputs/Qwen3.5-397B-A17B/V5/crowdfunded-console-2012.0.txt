First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state an edge; "low-cost" and "open" are not advantages against established incumbents with subsidized hardware and massive libraries, especially given the team's lack of marketing budget and development partnerships.

**Steelman:** A niche, developer-friendly console can succeed by targeting underserved homebrew creators and retro enthusiasts who value openness over AAA graphics, leveraging crowdfunding to validate demand before mass production.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or building software for existing open platforms (PC, Raspberry Pi) offers the same "open" benefit with zero hardware risk and immediate access to users. A cheaper, staged alternative (software-first) reaches the same upside without the capital exposure of manufacturing.
2.  **Fit:** The strategy assumes a market for new hardware in 2012, yet the proposal admits strong competition from phones and established consoles. Without a stated exclusive content deal or unique hardware capability, the "open" angle does not move a north star of user acquisition against free/cheap mobile games.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The project fails because hardware delays consume the crowdfunding funds before shipping, leaving backers angry and no product to sell publicly. The "one-way door" of manufacturing commits capital before verifying developer interest.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no marketing budget" and "developers may not build games." These are not risks to be managed; they are blockers. A platform without content and without visibility cannot acquire users. The math fails: hardware margins are thin without scale, and scale requires marketing/content, which are explicitly missing.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Edge:** The proposal offers no fact showing why this console beats existing options for buyers or developers.
*   **Resource Contradiction:** The plan requires marketing and developer adoption to succeed, yet explicitly states these are absent.
*   **Superior Alternative:** A software-only approach on existing hardware achieves the "open platform" goal without the manufacturing blocker.

**Risks:** Hardware delays, funding shortfalls, lack of games. These are secondary to the fundamental lack of a competitive edge.

The plan attempts to solve a hard problem (platform adoption) with fewer resources than the incumbents, without a differentiating factor that compels switching.

DECISION: NO-GO
