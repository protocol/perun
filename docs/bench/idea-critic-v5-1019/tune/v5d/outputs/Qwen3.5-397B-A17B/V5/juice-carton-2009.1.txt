First call: NO-GO. The proposal offers no stated edge over the current shelf presence; it removes the brand's primary recognition cue (the orange with a straw) to adopt a minimalist aesthetic that risks alienating the loyal, older demographic without a proven upside in sales or efficiency.

Steelman: A modernized, minimalist design with a functional orange-shaped cap will signal premium quality and innovation, driving a sales surge that outweighs rebranding costs.

Attack: The plan fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests. The primary shelf cue—the orange with a straw—is explicitly identified as the brand's main recognition factor. Removing it to launch nationwide simultaneously creates a massive risk of shelf invisibility. The proposal admits the buyer base is loyal and older; this demographic typically penalizes abrupt visual breaks from tradition. There is no evidence provided that "minimalism" resonates with this specific group or that the new cap compensates for the loss of the primary visual anchor.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retains 100% of current recognition and loyalty at zero cost.
2. Staged pilot: Test the new design in a limited region to measure recognition failure before a national rollout.
3. Hybrid evolution: Gradually introduce the new cap while retaining the orange imagery to bridge the visual gap.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-launch, sales have collapsed because loyal shoppers walked past the cartons, failing to recognize the brand on the shelf. The advertising campaign was insufficient to retrain decades of muscle memory. The cost of reprinting and unsold inventory is catastrophic.

Blockers:
- The plan removes the stated "main shelf cue" without evidence that the new design works better for the specific "older, loyal" demographic.
- Nationwide simultaneous launch eliminates the ability to detect recognition failure before total commitment.

Risks:
- Alienation of the core demographic.
- High sunk cost if the design fails.

Kill Criteria: If a regional pilot shows a >5% drop in repeat purchases within 4 weeks, halt the rollout.

The absence of a pilot, a stated edge for the new design, or evidence that the target demographic desires minimalism makes this a reckless bet on a core asset.

DECISION: NO-GO
