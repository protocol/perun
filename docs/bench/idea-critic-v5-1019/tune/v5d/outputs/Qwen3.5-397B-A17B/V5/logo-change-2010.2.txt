First call: NO-GO. The proposal offers no stated edge over the current asset; it substitutes a recognized symbol (the blue square) with a generic element while removing the primary differentiator, relying entirely on untested assumptions about contemporary taste.

Steelman: A radical, immediate rebrand shocks the market into perceiving the retailer as modern, leveraging the "small blue square" as a subtle heritage nod while the new wordmark signals change.

Attack:
1. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" retains 100% brand equity at zero cost. A "staged pilot" (testing in one channel or region) offers the same potential upside with drastically lower risk and reversibility. The proposal's "all channels at once" approach is the most expensive and least reversible option, failing to beat the cheaper alternative of testing first.
2. **Fit:** While "contemporary" is a valid north star, destroying the primary recognition asset (the large blue square) without evidence that the target demographic dislikes the old logo contradicts the goal of retaining loyal customers while attracting new ones.
3. **Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch failed. Cause: Loyal customers felt alienated by the sudden disappearance of the iconic logo, interpreting the "small square" as an error or dilution, while new customers saw only a generic wordmark indistinguishable from competitors. Social media amplified this confusion into a narrative of "selling out" or "decline."
4. **Evidence:** The proposal explicitly admits the design was "not tested with customers." In branding, untested removal of a decades-old asset is a factual blocker, not a mere risk. The "risk" of backlash is a near-certainty given the lack of validation.

Blockers:
- **Untested Core Assumption:** The plan assumes the new design works better without data, while explicitly removing the asset known to drive recognition.
- **Superior Alternative Exists:** A staged rollout or customer testing achieves the same goal (modernization) with less risk and cost, blocking the "big bang" approach.

Risks:
- Immediate revenue drop from confused loyalists.
- Irreversible loss of brand equity if the new mark fails to resonate.

The absence of a pilot, testing, or phased rollout is a fatal flaw for a high-stakes brand change. The proposal cannot proceed as written.

DECISION: NO-GO
