First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; adding a social layer to webmail via automatic enrollment does not demonstrate why this specific approach beats existing standalone social networks or respects the primary utility of an inbox.

**Steelman:** Leveraging the existing user base and default visibility creates immediate network effects, solving the cold-start problem that kills most social features, while integrating communication and connection in one workflow.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Incentives:** Webmail is a utility for private, task-oriented communication. A social layer with default public visibility fundamentally conflicts with the user's expectation of privacy in an inbox. This misalignment risks churn in the core product to chase a secondary social metric.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves trust. A "opt-in" pilot with strict privacy defaults achieves network growth among willing users without the regulatory and reputational blast radius of automatic enrollment. The proposed "automatic" path is not the cheapest or safest way to test the hypothesis.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Regulators fine the company for GDPR/privacy violations regarding automatic data usage; users revolt over unexpected visibility of contacts, leading to mass opt-outs or account deletions; the feature becomes a "ghost town" because inbox usage patterns (sporadic, task-based) do not support sustained social engagement.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits risks of privacy disregard and regulatory attention but proceeds anyway. No evidence suggests users *want* this specific integration, only that they *exist*.
5.  **Blocker:** The core mechanism (automatic enrollment + default visibility) creates an immediate, high-probability regulatory and trust blocker that contradicts the company's established role as a secure communication provider. The risk is not theoretical; it is baked into the launch strategy. A cheaper, safer alternative (opt-in) exists that tests the same upside without the existential threat.

**Blockers:** Automatic enrollment with default public visibility creates unavoidable regulatory exposure and violates user trust expectations for email, threatening the core product.
**Risks:** Low engagement due to context mismatch; difficulty reversing exposed connections; brand damage.

DECISION: NO-GO
