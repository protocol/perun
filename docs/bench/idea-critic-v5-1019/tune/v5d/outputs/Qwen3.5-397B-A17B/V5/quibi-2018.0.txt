First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (mobile video) but fails to state an edge; "high production budgets" and "A-list talent" are cost drivers, not differentiators in a market saturated with free, abundant short-form content.

**Steelman:** A premium, mobile-native studio can command subscriptions by delivering cinematic short-form narratives that free platforms cannot match technically or artistically.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Stated Edge:** The core value proposition relies on "professional" quality in a category where users increasingly prefer authentic, low-fi UGC. High costs do not equal high demand. The proposal admits "no proven demand" for *premium* short-form.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or licensing existing content to current giants (YouTube, Netflix) offers lower risk and immediate revenue without building a new tech stack for a unproven format.
3.  **Fit & Timing:** Launching a new subscription service in 2018 amidst "subscription fatigue" contradicts market headwinds. The "vertical/horizontal" tech feature is a hygiene factor, not a reason to pay.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The service launches with great shows, but users refuse to add another $10/month bill for 10-minute clips they can get for free elsewhere. The $1B burn rate forces a fire sale within 18 months.
5.  **Evidence:** The proposal itself cites "no proven demand" and "subscription fatigue" as risks. These are not hypotheticals; they are stated facts that directly undermine the revenue model.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal explicitly states "no proven demand for premium short-form."
*   **Contradiction:** The plan requires massive upfront capital ($1B+) based on an unproven hypothesis while ignoring the stated market resistance to new subscriptions.
*   **Alternative:** A cheaper, staged pilot (e.g., a single show on an existing platform) could test demand without the $1B exposure. The refusal to stage this implies the numbers do not work at a smaller scale.

**Risks:** Execution difficulty, talent retention, and rapid shift in mobile consumption habits are secondary to the primary blocker: paying for what is freely available.

**Decision:** The lack of a stated edge combined with explicit admission of unproven demand and market fatigue creates a fatal flaw. The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test.

DECISION: NO-GO
