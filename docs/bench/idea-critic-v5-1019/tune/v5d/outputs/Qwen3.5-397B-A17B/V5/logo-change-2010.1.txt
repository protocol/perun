First call: NO-GO. The proposal offers no stated edge over the current brand equity; it relies on an untested aesthetic shift while voluntarily discarding the primary asset (the blue square) that drives recognition.

Steelman: A simultaneous, unannounced global rebrand creates immediate shock value, forcing the market to perceive the retailer as contemporary overnight without the dilution of a phased rollout.

However, the plan fails the blocker test. The proposal explicitly admits the new design was "not tested with customers" while simultaneously acknowledging the risk that "loyal customers are attached to the old logo." In retail, brand recognition is the primary driver of foot traffic and trust. Discarding a decades-old, high-equity asset (the blue square) for an untested alternative is not a strategy; it is a gamble with the company's core revenue engine.

The "all channels at once" and "no advance announcement" approach removes all safety valves. If the untested design fails to resonate or triggers the predicted backlash, the error is instantly global and irreversible in the short term. There is no pilot, no A/B test, and no fallback. The cost of delay (waiting to test) is negligible compared to the cost of a failed global launch, which could include lost sales, inventory devaluation due to confused branding, and permanent erosion of loyal customer trust.

A cheaper, safer alternative exists: a phased rollout starting with digital channels or a specific sub-line, accompanied by customer testing. This reaches the same upside (modernization) with significantly lower risk. The current plan demands we bet the entire brand on an unverified hypothesis when a validated path is available.

Blockers:
1. The core asset (blue square) is being discarded for an untested replacement despite known customer attachment.
2. The "no advance announcement" strategy guarantees that any negative reaction will be immediate and global, with no mitigation possible.

Risks:
1. Instant social media backlash alienating the core demographic.
2. Loss of shelf recognition leading to immediate sales drop.

Kill Criteria: If a blind test with 500 loyal customers shows a >15% preference for the old logo over the new, the project stops.

DECISION: NO-GO
