First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge proving this specific leadership background translates to apparel/home goods retention, and it ignores the verified fact that most sales historically depend on discounts.

Steelman: Replacing coupon addiction with everyday low pricing (EDLP) and branded shops will restore margin and modernize the brand by leveraging successful electronics retail tactics.

Attack: The plan assumes price sensitivity is identical between electronics buyers and department store shoppers. Electronics are often destination purchases with less frequent promotion cycles; department store traffic here is explicitly "coupon-driven." Removing the primary traffic driver (coupons) without a transitional bridge risks an immediate collapse in foot traffic before any brand equity is rebuilt. Furthermore, the electronics executive's past success may rely on product exclusivity or turnover rates not present in this chain's current inventory.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Continued sales erosion but stable customer behavior.
2. Pilot program: Test EDLP in 5-10 stores to measure churn vs. margin lift before chain-wide rollout.
3. Hybrid model: Reduce coupon frequency gradually while introducing branded shops.
The current proposal (option 4: immediate full rollout) beats none of these on risk-adjusted returns given the zero data on customer elasticity.

Fit: While modernization aligns with a generic north star, the method contradicts the specific behavioral reality of the existing customer base.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-launch, sales drop 40% as loyalists leave, new customers do not arrive because the "value" signal is lost, and cash flow dries up before the store rebuilds can show results. This is a one-way door; once coupons are gone and trust is broken, reacquiring the old base is nearly impossible.

Evidence: The proposal admits "most sales have historically come from items sold at a discount." Removing the mechanism responsible for most revenue without a pilot is a factual contradiction of sustainable operations.

Blockers:
1. No pilot test despite high risk of customer churn.
2. Strategy relies on an unproven assumption that electronics retail success transfers directly to this specific demographic without friction.
3. Immediate removal of the primary historical sales driver (discounts) creates a cash-flow cliff.

Risks: Total loss of core customer base; inability to fund store rebuilds due to revenue drop.

Kill criteria: If a 3-store pilot shows a >15% drop in transaction volume within 60 days, halt rollout.

DECISION: NO-GO
