First call: NO-GO. The stated price ($7.4B) against a one-film-per-year output creates an unjustifiable valuation gap without a stated edge proving immediate scalability or library monetization that beats the status quo.

Steelman: Acquiring the studio is the only way to prevent a rival from securing its talent and to instantly revitalize the conglomerate's failing hand-drawn unit by installing proven creative leadership.

Options:
1. Buy: Highest cost, highest integration risk, irreversible capital commitment.
2. Do Nothing: Lose partnership, rival potentially gains advantage, continued underperformance in-house.
3. Revised Deal: Cash-and-stock mix with earn-outs tied to film performance to mitigate valuation risk.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of dominating family entertainment, but the financial mechanics as proposed threaten the conglomerate's stability more than the current partnership loss would.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition fails because the "one film a year" bottleneck cannot be scaled without diluting quality, leaving the $7.4B price tag unsupported by revenue growth. Culture clash causes key creative leaders to exit post-acquisition, leaving the conglomerate with overpaid IP and no talent. This is a one-way door; the capital is gone forever.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the price is "very high" relative to output (one film/year). No evidence suggests the studio can increase production frequency without breaking its success formula. The risk of the founder becoming a major shareholder creates a governance conflict that could paralyze future strategic shifts.

Blockers:
1. Valuation Mismatch: $7.4B for ~$500M-$800M annual revenue (estimated) implies a multiple that assumes impossible growth rates not evidenced in the proposal.
2. Production Bottleneck: The core asset produces only one unit of revenue annually; buying it does not solve the volume problem inherent in the conglomerate's needs.

Risks: Culture clash, founder influence, key person dependency.

Kill Criteria: If the studio cannot greenlight two projects within 12 months post-acquisition, the integration is failing. Leading metric: Greenlight velocity and retention of key creative staff at 6 months.

The financial exposure outweighs the strategic necessity given the lack of a scalable production model in the current facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
