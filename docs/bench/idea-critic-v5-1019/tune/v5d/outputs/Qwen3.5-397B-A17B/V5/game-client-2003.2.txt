First call: GO. The stated edge is solving two existential threats—update delivery and cheating—that retail boxes cannot address, while creating a direct customer relationship retailers block.

Steelman: A mandatory client is the only viable mechanism to enforce integrity and patch velocity for popular PC titles in 2003, turning a distribution liability into a platform asset.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited are execution hurdles, not factual contradictions. Home broadband adoption was rising rapidly in 2003; early friction is the cost of solving the core product failure (cheating/bugs). Retail pushback is a channel conflict, not a plan failure; the leverage of "very popular" games forces compliance or migration to digital.

**Risks:** Player churn due to friction; retail delisting; client stability; competitor copying. These are managed via the kill criteria below, not by abandoning the plan. "Competitors may copy" is irrelevant; first-mover lock-in with existing popular IPs creates the moat.

**Alternatives:**
1. *Do nothing:* Cheating destroys the player base; updates remain slow. Status quo is fatal.
2. *Optional patcher:* Fails to stop cheaters who bypass it.
3. *Retail-only updates:* Logistically impossible for frequent fixes.
The proposal beats "do nothing" because the alternative is product death.

**Pre-mortem:** The client launches with game-breaking bugs, causing a mass refund demand and retailer revolt.
**Door:** Two-way. If the client fails, the company can revert to retail-only patches for new sales while supporting legacy users, though cheating will persist.
**Cost of Delay:** High. Every month without anti-cheat accelerates community collapse.

**Kill Criteria:**
1. **Metric:** Client crash rate >5% or cheat detection false-positive rate >1% within 30 days of launch.
2. **Threshold:** If active daily users drop >15% in the first week post-mandatory switch due to client issues.
3. **Action:** Roll back mandatory requirement to "strongly recommended" and deploy emergency patch team.
**Leading Metric:** Percentage of active users successfully updating within 24 hours of release. Review date: 45 days post-launch.

The plan addresses the only things that matter: keeping the game playable and fair. Friction is the price of survival.

DECISION: GO
