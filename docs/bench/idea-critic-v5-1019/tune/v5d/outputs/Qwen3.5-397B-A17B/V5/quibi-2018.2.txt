First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (mobile video) but fails to state an edge; "high production budgets" and "A-list talent" are cost drivers, not differentiators in a market saturated with free, abundant short-form content.

Steelman: A premium, orientation-agnostic video service could capture high-value users fatigued by ads and user-generated clutter if they uniquely value cinematic short-form.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" costs $0 and avoids the massive burn rate of A-list talent. Existing platforms (YouTube, Instagram, TikTok) already solve the vertical/horizontal viewing issue via software rotation or native formats at a fraction of the cost. A cheaper alternative is licensing existing premium shorts to current platforms rather than building a new tech stack and content library from scratch.
2.  **Fit:** The strategy conflicts with the stated risk of "subscription fatigue." Adding another paid silo for content that users currently consume for free requires proving a willingness to pay that the proposal admits is "unproven."
3.  **Evidence:** The claim that users will pay for "professional" shorts contradicts the market reality where professional creators thrive on ad-supported or freemium models. The "technology feature" (orientation adaptation) is a commodity, not a moat; it does not justify a $1 billion capex.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The service launches, burns cash on talent, and fails because users refuse to open a new app for content they can get elsewhere, or they churn after one month due to price. The "one-way door" is the sunk cost of production contracts.
5.  **Blocker:** The proposal explicitly states "no proven demand" and "subscription fatigue" while requesting >$1 billion. This is a factual contradiction: scaling unproven demand with maximum fixed costs is a failure mode, not a plan. The numbers do not work without a validated willingness-to-pay metric, which is absent.

Risks (non-blocking but severe): Competition for attention, high churn, content cost inflation.

The lack of a pilot, a capped test, or evidence of demand conversion makes this a speculative gamble, not an operational plan. The absence of a stated edge over free alternatives is fatal.

DECISION: NO-GO
