First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge proving this specific partner beats organic growth or a cheaper alliance; "global scale" is a generic desire, not a competitive advantage against incumbents, and the cyclical peak of the US partner creates an immediate valuation trap.

Steelman: Combining premium engineering with mass-market distribution creates a dominant global portfolio that shares platforms to cut costs while accessing the profitable North American truck segment.

Attack:
1. **Valuation & Timing:** The US partner is "highly profitable at the moment" on trucks/SUVs, suggesting a cyclical peak. Buying at the top of a boom with expensive stock risks massive write-downs when the cycle turns.
2. **Cultural Incompatibility:** "Very different corporate and labour cultures" between a German premium maker and a US mass-market producer historically leads to integration paralysis, not synergy.
3. **Strategic Dilution:** Entering the "mass market" dilutes the German firm's premium positioning without guaranteeing volume retention if the truck boom bursts.
4. **False Equality:** A "merger of equals" often masks a power struggle, delaying critical decisions during the integration phase.

Alternatives:
- **Do Nothing:** Preserve premium margins and avoid cyclical exposure.
- **Alliance:** Share platforms without merging balance sheets or cultures.
- **Organic Entry:** Build US capacity slowly to control quality and culture.

Pre-mortem: The truck boom collapses within 18 months; the US division becomes a loss-maker. Cultural clashes prevent platform sharing, doubling R&D costs. The "merger of equals" devolves into a hostile takeover attempt, causing executive exodus and brand erosion.

Evidence: The proposal admits dependence on a "cyclical light-truck boom" and "very different cultures" as facts, not risks. These are inherent blockers to the stated goal of stable global synergy. No kill criteria or caps are offered to mitigate the cyclical bet.

Blockers:
- The US partner's profitability is tied to a cyclical peak, making the stock swap overpriced.
- Irreconcilable cultural differences prevent the required platform sharing.
- No stated edge proves this merger outperforms a simple supply alliance.

Risks: Brand dilution, integration failure, cyclical downturn.

DECISION: NO-GO
