First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state an edge; "low-cost" and "open" are not advantages against incumbents who subsidize hardware and control distribution, especially given the team's lack of marketing budget and developer relationships.

Steelman: A niche, developer-friendly console can succeed by targeting underserved indie creators ignored by major platforms, leveraging crowdfunding to validate demand before mass production.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" costs zero. A "software-first" approach (building the OS for existing PC/mobile hardware) eliminates hardware delay risk and capital requirements while testing the "open platform" value proposition. This cheaper alternative reaches the same upside (developer adoption) without the fatal hardware bottleneck.
2.  **Fit:** The goal is a successful consumer product. Shipping hardware without a marketing budget or secured developer pipeline contradicts the basic economics of the console business, where software sales drive hardware viability.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The project fails because hardware delays drain crowdfunding goodwill, the device ships late with few games, and the "open" tag fails to attract developers who prefer established stores with existing users.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no marketing budget" and "hardware delays are common." In 2012, the barrier to entry for console developers was high; without a guaranteed user base, developers will not port games. The math does not work: hardware margins are thin, and the cost of customer acquisition is unaddressed.
5.  **Blocker:** The plan relies on a crowdfunding campaign to fund hardware production despite having no marketing budget to drive that campaign. This is a factual contradiction; a campaign without marketing fails. Additionally, the lack of a committed developer pipeline means the product has no content at launch, a fatal flaw for a gaming console.

Risks (non-blocking but severe): Incumbent retaliation, supply chain failures, platform fragmentation.

The proposal attempts to solve a hardware and ecosystem problem with a funding mechanism that requires prerequisites (audience, buzz) the team explicitly lacks. The "software-first" alternative is safer, cheaper, and validates the core claim without the existential risk of manufacturing.

DECISION: NO-GO
