First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it admits no monetization plan, minimal revenue, and a price 12x the prior valuation without explaining how this specific asset beats building or buying cheaper alternatives.

Steelman: Acquiring the target is the only way to instantly neutralize the existential threat of mobile messaging displacing the core service, buying time to figure out monetization later.

Options:
1. **Acquire:** Cost $19B, risk extreme (integration, regulation, culture), reversibility low (cash/stock spent).
2. **Build/Clone:** Cost low, risk medium (speed to market), reversibility high.
3. **Do Nothing:** Cost zero, risk catastrophic (core displacement), reversibility N/A.
The proposal fails to prove why Option 1 beats Option 2 on speed or Option 3 on survival probability given the lack of revenue.

Fit: Assumes the north star is "mobile retention" but contradicts the stated constraint of "no monetization plan." A sound idea must move a stated north star; buying a charity-sized revenue stream for a fortune does not inherently move value unless the displacement fear is an immediate fact, not a projection.

Pre-mortem: The deal closes, founders leave within 18 months due to culture clash, regulators block data integration, and the $19B becomes a sunk cost with no ROI path. This is a one-way door; the cost of delay is the potential loss of users, but the cost of error is bankruptcy-level waste.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the target has "no monetization plan" and "minimal revenue." The $19B price is a fact; the value is an assumption. The "fear" of displacement is a hypothesis, not evidence of current core failure.

Blockers:
1. **No stated edge:** The proposal offers no fact showing this plan beats building or waiting. Need without an edge is a NO-GO.
2. **Contradictory economics:** Paying 12x valuation for an asset explicitly refusing the buyer's primary monetization channel (ads) with no alternative plan creates a negative expected value based on stated facts.

Risks: Founder retention, privacy regulation, integration failure, price inflation.

Kill criteria: None proposed. A cap or pilot is impossible at this scale, but the lack of a monetization hypothesis is fatal.

DECISION: NO-GO
