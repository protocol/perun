First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state an edge; "low-cost" and "open" are not advantages against established incumbents with subsidized hardware and locked ecosystems.

Steelman: A niche, open console can succeed by targeting underserved developers and enthusiasts who value modifiability over AAA graphics, leveraging crowdfunding to validate demand before mass production.

Options:
1.  **Proposed Plan:** High risk. Requires simultaneous success in hardware manufacturing, developer recruitment, and marketing with zero budget beyond crowdfunding. Competes directly with subsidized giants.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids capital loss. The market remains served by phones and existing consoles.
3.  **Software-First Alternative:** Build the open OS for existing PC/Raspberry Pi hardware first. Cost is near-zero, reversibility is high, and it validates the developer community without hardware supply chain risk. This reaches the same upside (community adoption) cheaper and safer.

Fit: Assumes "open platform" moves a north star of developer freedom, but without a stated strategy confirming this beats current user habits, it is an assumption.

Pre-mortem: The project fails because hardware delays drain crowdfunding goodwill before shipment, or because major developers ignore the platform due to low install base, leaving a device with no games. It is a one-way door for capital; once spent on tooling, it is unrecoverable. Delay costs nothing; rushing costs everything.

Evidence: The proposal admits "no marketing budget," "hardware delays are common," and "established competitors." These are not risks to be managed; they are blockers to the core claim of viability. The math does not work: acquiring users and developers without capital in a winner-take-all market is impossible as proposed.

Blockers:
1.  **No stated edge:** "Low-cost" cannot beat subsidized hardware from incumbents; "open" has not been proven to attract sufficient developer volume to sustain a platform.
2.  **Fatal resource gap:** Explicitly stating "no marketing budget" while entering a crowded consumer hardware market guarantees invisibility.
3.  **Superior alternative exists:** A software-only rollout on existing hardware achieves the "open platform" goal without the hardware execution risk.

Risks: Supply chain failure, developer apathy, crowdfunding shortfall.

The plan commits real money to a hardware venture with no competitive moat and no marketing engine. It must be rejected in favor of a software-first validation or a revised plan identifying a specific, defensible wedge.

DECISION: NO-GO
