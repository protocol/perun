First call: NO-GO. The proposal cites a competitive reaction ("market leader just agreed") as the primary edge, not a stated product, cost, or timing advantage that makes this specific $6B asset beat existing alternatives. Need alone is not an edge.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform instantly grants scale to challenge the search leader before the market consolidates further.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or build/buy smaller avoids a $6B cash outflow at a "large premium." A cheaper, staged alternative exists unless the proposal proves only this specific asset unlocks the upside. It does not.
2.  **Fit & Conflicts:** The buyer serves agency customers; the target *is* an agency. This creates immediate channel conflict. The proposal acknowledges this but offers no mechanism to resolve it, risking existing revenue to chase display ads.
3.  **Evidence & Math:** The price ($6B) is asserted without ROI, payback period, or unit economics relative to the "fast-changing" market. In 2007, display ad technology obsolescence is a high probability; paying a large premium for assets that may devalue rapidly is a factual blocker if the growth trajectory isn't guaranteed. The proposal admits the market is "fast-changing" yet commits massive irreversible capital.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Integration fails due to culture clash (tech company vs. agency); channel conflicts cause key agency clients to churn; the technology shifts again within 18 months, rendering the premium payment a sunk cost.

**Blockers:**
*   **Channel Conflict:** The plan admits conflicts with existing customers but provides no solution. This threatens current revenue streams without guaranteed replacement.
*   **Unsupported Premium:** No evidence shows this specific $6B asset is the *only* path to compete. A cheaper alternative likely reaches similar scale without the agency conflict.
*   **Market Volatility:** Committing maximum cash to a "fast-changing" sector at a "large premium" lacks a safety margin or kill criterion for technology shifts.

**Risks:** Integration failure, overpayment, client churn.

The proposal relies on fear of missing out (competitor action) rather than a verified economic edge. The channel conflict is a stated negative that removes the route to upside unless solved, which the plan does not do.

DECISION: NO-GO
