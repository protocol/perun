First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve an ecosystem deficit (apps/developers) via vertical integration of a declining hardware asset, a mismatch of mechanism to problem.

Steelman: Acquiring the handset maker secures a reference device to demonstrate OS capabilities and prevents immediate defection to the rival platform, buying time to court developers.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** High risk of manufacturer defection, likely causing immediate OS irrelevance.
2. **Subsidize developers directly:** Cheaper, reversible, and targets the root cause (app gap) rather than the symptom (hardware sales).
3. **Acquire handset division:** Massive capital outlay (€5.4B) for a asset with falling sales; high integration friction; unproven ability to attract developers through hardware ownership alone.

Fit: Assuming the north star is ecosystem vitality, owning a shrinking hardware unit does not inherently generate developer interest. Developers follow users; users follow apps. This plan bets that controlling a specific hardware pipe will reverse network effects, contradicting the stated reality that the rival holds "most of the apps."

Pre-mortem: The acquisition closes, but the handset line continues to lose market share due to the app gap. The company is now stuck with a bloated workforce and a €5.4B sunk cost, while competitors mock the "walled garden" of a single, unpopular phone. The app ecosystem never tips because the installed base remains too small to justify porting costs.

Evidence & Math: The proposal admits smartphone sales are "falling" and the rival has "most" apps. Spending €5.4B to protect a declining share against a dominant network effect lacks a stated edge. No evidence suggests hardware ownership reverses developer inertia; historically, it creates a conflict of interest with other handset partners, potentially accelerating their exit.

Blockers:
1. **Mechanism Mismatch:** The core failure mode is lack of apps; the solution is buying hardware. The proposal offers no causal link showing how owning a factory fixes the app deficit, while the cost diverts resources from direct ecosystem subsidies.
2. **Negative Momentum:** Buying a "falling" asset to stop a "market-leading" rival without a unique differentiator is a value trap, not a turnaround strategy.

Risks: Partner alienation, integration drag, continued sales decline.

DECISION: NO-GO
