First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; cheap fuel and absent regulation remove the economic and compliance drivers, while unproven durability creates immediate warranty liability without a compensating market pull.

**Steelman:** Being first establishes long-term brand dominance in efficiency and forces competitors to play catch-up, securing a monopoly on hybrid technology before regulations inevitably shift.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** With cheap fuel, the premium cost of the novel power-split transmission and NiMH batteries cannot be recouped through customer savings. The value proposition relies entirely on future regulation or altruism, neither of which justifies a 1997 mass-production commitment today.
2.  **Technical Risk:** Nickel-metal-hydride durability is unproven in this configuration. A public bold schedule locks the company into a launch date before real-world aging data exists. Failure modes (battery degradation) directly threaten the brand's core reliability reputation with no fallback.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital while monitoring regulatory shifts. A "limited pilot" (non-public) tests durability without exposing the corporation to mass warranty claims or reputational damage from a premature public failure. The current plan beats neither alternative on risk-adjusted returns.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The program launches in 1997; within 18 months, battery packs fail prematurely in moderate climates. Warranty costs skyrocket, exceeding the R&D budget. The public "bold schedule" becomes a headline for corporate overreach, damaging stock value and delaying the actual technology maturity by years due to rushed fixes.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits cheap fuel and no regulatory mandate. There is no factual basis for customer adoption at the required price point.
*   **Contradictory Evidence:** The "unproven durability" fact directly contradicts the feasibility of a "bold public schedule" for mass production. You cannot commit to volume before verifying the core component's lifespan.

**Risks:** High execution difficulty, potential for catastrophic warranty exposure, and market rejection.

**Decision:** The plan fails the basic operator test: it commits massive capital to an unproven technology with no immediate market driver and a fixed public deadline that ignores technical uncertainty.

DECISION: NO-GO
