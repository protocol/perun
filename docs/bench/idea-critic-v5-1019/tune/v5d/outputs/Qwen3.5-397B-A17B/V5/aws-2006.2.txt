First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; thin retail margins and no developer platform experience do not beat incumbent hardware vendors in an unproven market.

Steelman: The retailer can monetize its spiky internal infrastructure by selling excess capacity, turning a cost center into revenue while leveraging existing scale.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High capital risk, unproven demand, direct competition with established vendors, potential distraction from core retail.
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoids margin dilution and investor skepticism; maintains focus on core retail profitability.
3. **Internal Optimization:** Use spiky capacity to improve retail reliability or lower costs without the overhead of a public developer platform.

Fit: Assumes a north star of "infrastructure dominance" which contradicts the stated reality of investor concern over capital spending and lack of relevant experience.

Pre-mortem: The venture fails because reliability expectations for external developers exceed internal tolerances, causing brand damage to the core retail business. Capital allocated here starves retail innovation during a margin-sensitive period. This is a one-way door with high delay cost if the market proves false.

Evidence: The proposal admits no experience running a developer platform, an unproven market for hourly servers, and existing strong incumbents. Investor sentiment is already negative regarding capital spend. No data suggests this specific retailer has a cost or technology advantage over established vendors.

Blockers:
1. **No Stated Edge:** The proposal offers no fact showing why this retailer wins against incumbents given its lack of experience and thin margins. Need without an advantage is a failure condition.
2. **Contradictory Context:** Heavy capital spending is already questioned by investors; this plan increases that exposure without a proven ROI model.
3. **Capability Gap:** Running a reliable public cloud is fundamentally different from managing internal spiky infrastructure; the proposal admits zero experience here.

Risks: Distraction from core business, margin dilution, reliability failures damaging the retail brand, and capital misallocation.

The absence of a pilot, cap, or metric is not the blocker; the blocker is the lack of any stated reason this specific company would succeed where incumbents exist and the market is unproven. A sound operator cannot approve a capital-intensive entry into a new domain with no competitive advantage and negative investor sentiment.

DECISION: NO-GO
