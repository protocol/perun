First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (business features) but fails to state an edge: why this specific complex design will succeed where the current machine's limitations exist, given the tight schedule and unproven reliability.

Steelman: A dedicated business machine with lowercase and 80-column display, launched within a year, will capture the enterprise market and justify phasing out the legacy model.

Attack:
1.  **Feasibility vs. Schedule:** The design is "complex" and reliability "unproven," yet the launch is set for "within the year." History suggests complex hardware rarely meets aggressive deadlines without compromising the very reliability business users demand.
2.  **Ecosystem Friction:** Phasing out the existing machine risks alienating current owners and software makers. If the new machine isn't perfectly compatible or if the transition is forced too quickly, the ecosystem may fracture, leaving the new product without software support.
3.  **Revenue Cliff:** Ending the current product before the new one is proven and widely adopted creates a potential revenue gap. The proposal admits this risk but offers no mitigation other than hope for a quick launch.
4.  **Alternatives:** A cheaper, safer alternative exists: release an enhanced peripheral or a modular upgrade for the existing machine to test business demand without the cost and risk of a full redesign and immediate phase-out.

Pre-mortem: The project fails because the complex design causes delays, missing the one-year window. The resulting rushed hardware suffers reliability issues, causing business returns. Meanwhile, the phase-out of the old machine angers the existing user base, leading to a collapse in software support and total market rejection.

Blockers:
*   The stated timeline ("within the year") contradicts the admitted complexity and unproven reliability of the new design.
*   The plan to "phase out" the existing machine lacks a bridge for software makers, threatening the ecosystem required for business adoption.

Risks: Revenue drop during transition; failure to gain software traction; hardware defects.

Kill Criteria: If a working prototype with 99% reliability isn't achieved in 6 months, halt the phase-out plan. Leading metric: Pre-orders from top 10 software vendors by Month 4.

DECISION: NO-GO
