First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state an edge; "circles" and "hangouts" are features, not a defensible advantage against a locked-in incumbent with hundreds of millions of users.

Steelman: Forced integration of superior grouping and video tools into a dominant search/mail ecosystem creates unavoidable utility that overrides network effects.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves core product sentiment. A "cheaper alternative" is acquiring a niche network or building standalone tools without risking the core search/mail reputation. The proposal does not demonstrate why forced integration beats these safer paths.
2.  **Fit & Incentives:** Tying executive bonuses to adoption of a specific feature set, rather than user satisfaction or retention, creates perverse incentives to spam users, directly amplifying the stated risk of annoying the existing base.
3.  **Evidence & History:** The company has three prior social failures. The proposal offers no new evidence that market dynamics have shifted or that this specific architecture solves the previous failure modes. It relies on coercion (integration) rather than pull.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** If this fails, the likely cause is user backlash against forced integration, degrading trust in core search and mail products. The cost of delay is low; the cost of reputation damage is high.
5.  **Blocker Test:** The stated fact that "network effects favour the incumbent" combined with "history of failed social products" and no stated edge (only features) constitutes a blocker. The plan relies on forcing users rather than winning them, which contradicts the requirement for organic network growth. The bonus structure guarantees the exact behavior (annoyance) that leads to churn.

Blockers:
-   No stated edge over the incumbent beyond feature parity; features do not break network effects.
-   Incentive structure (bonuses tied to adoption) directly drives the primary risk (user annoyance).
-   History of three failures without new evidence of viability.

Risks:
-   Reputation damage to core search/mail products.
-   Wasted engineering capacity on a low-probability win.

The plan as proposed lacks a mechanism to overcome the incumbent's moat and actively incentivizes the behavior most likely to cause failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
