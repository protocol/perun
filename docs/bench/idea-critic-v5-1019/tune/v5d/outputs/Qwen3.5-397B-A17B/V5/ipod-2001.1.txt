First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it offers a higher-priced device with severe compatibility restrictions (small computer share, no consumer experience) in a market where cheaper alternatives already struggle and legal content is scarce. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: A premium, high-capacity device creates a new category by offering vastly superior storage (5GB) than flash-based competitors, betting that capacity outweighs price and compatibility friction.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoid capital risk in a failing category.
2. Cheaper alternative: License software to Windows immediately to expand the base.
3. Proposed: High price, Mac-only, 5GB. This fails to beat "do nothing" because the addressable market is too small to sustain the supply chain risks without a Windows bridge or content deals.

Fit: Assumes a strategy of vertical integration, but without a content ecosystem or broad OS support, the "north star" of user adoption is unreachable. The restriction to a tiny computer base contradicts the goal of mass-market penetration.

Pre-mortem: The product launches to applause from the niche base but fails commercially because users cannot legally fill the 5GB drive, and Windows users (the majority) cannot buy it. The door is one-way regarding tooling costs; delay costs nothing as the market isn't ready.

Evidence: The proposal admits no legal downloads at scale and a tiny compatible base. These are blockers, not risks. A 5GB drive is useless without content; a $400 price is unjustifiable without a Windows market.

Blockers:
1. Content Void: No legal source to fill the drive removes the primary value proposition (capacity).
2. Market Cap: The installed base of compatible computers is insufficient to recover tooling costs before cash runs out.
3. Price/Value: Higher price than competitors with strictly lower utility (compatibility).

Risks: Supply chain timing, brand dilution.

The plan commits real money to a product that cannot function as intended (filled with music) for the vast majority of potential buyers, restricted to a user base too small to sustain the business case. It requires a pivot to Windows compatibility and a content strategy before approval.

DECISION: NO-GO
