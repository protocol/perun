Evidence: The proposal itself provides the blockers: higher cost, lower comfort, and manufacturer resistance. No evidence suggests buyers want this trade-off.

Blockers:
1. **Negative Unit Economics:** The material costs more than leather while offering perceived lower value (comfort) and requiring customer CapEx (factory changes).
2. **No Stated Edge:** Durability alone does not overcome the combined friction of higher price, retooling costs, and comfort deficits in the general premium shoe market.

Risks: Fashion trends shifting, unproven consumer acceptance.

The proposal asks to spend heavily on a product that is objectively worse (cost/comfort) and harder to adopt (factory changes) than the incumbent, with no specific beachhead identified. This is not a bet; it is a burn.
