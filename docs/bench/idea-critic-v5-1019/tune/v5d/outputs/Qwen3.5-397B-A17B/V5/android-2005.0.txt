First call: NO-GO. A search-ad company with no hardware DNA proposing a $50M acquisition of a pre-revenue, six-person OS team in a carrier-dominated market lacks a stated edge; the core claim relies on hopeful conversion to search ads without a mechanism to overcome entrenched handset makers or carrier gatekeepers.

Steelman: Acquiring this team secures a free, open-source OS to standardize mobile web access, breaking carrier/handset bottlenecks and exponentially growing search inventory before competitors lock the market.

However, the proposal fails the blocker test. The facts state the market is dominated by established vendors and handset makers have "no reason to adopt" a new platform. The proposal offers no specific leverage (e.g., exclusive search integration, subsidies, or existing developer momentum) to change this calculus. Relying on "free software" alone is not an edge when distribution is physically controlled by carriers who prioritize reliability and revenue share over openness. The $50M price tag for six people implies a valuation based entirely on future search ad upside, yet the path to that upside is blocked by the very distribution constraints the proposal admits exist.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital; wait for market clarity. Cost: $0. Risk: Competitor moves first.
2. Partner/License: Collaborate without acquisition. Cost: Low. Risk: Weak control.
3. Acquisition (Proposed): Cost: $50M+. Risk: Total loss if adoption fails.

The proposed path does not beat "do nothing" on stated facts because the adoption mechanism is missing. The risk of carrier resistance and lack of handset maker incentive are not just "risks" but active blockers given the absence of a stated strategy to overcome them. A pre-mortem reveals total capital loss as the OS fails to ship on any major device due to carrier rejection.

Blockers: No stated mechanism to force handset/carrier adoption despite admitted market resistance; valuation relies on unproven conversion to search ads without distribution.
Risks: Multi-year payback, no revenue model, open-source dilution of control.

Kill criteria: If no major handset maker commits to shipping the OS within 18 months post-acquisition, terminate the project and pivot assets. Leading metric: Number of active devices running the OS by Q4 2006.

DECISION: NO-GO
