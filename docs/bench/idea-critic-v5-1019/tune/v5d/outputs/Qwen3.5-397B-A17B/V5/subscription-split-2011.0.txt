First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a pricing backlash and strategic divergence by fracturing the customer experience immediately after a 60% price hike, compounding friction with operational separation.

**Steelman:** Separating the brands allows distinct management focus and prevents the declining DVD legacy from dragging down the streaming valuation, forcing each to stand on its own merits.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing & Friction:** Executing a brand split, separate billing, and dual logins immediately after a massive price increase is catastrophic for retention. It validates customer anger by making the service harder to use, signaling abandonment rather than improvement.
2.  **Value Destruction:** Severing shared ratings and recommendations destroys the primary data advantage of a combined library. It forces users to rebuild preference profiles, reducing engagement on both platforms.
3.  **Cost vs. Benefit:** While management focus is valuable, the cost of building duplicate infrastructure (websites, accounts, billing) and the certainty of churn outweigh the organizational benefits. The "declining" DVD business still generates cash flow; confusing its remaining loyalists accelerates its collapse without guaranteeing streaming success.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "do nothing" approach regarding branding, coupled with internal management restructuring, achieves the strategic goal without alienating the user base. A bundled discount could mitigate the price hike backlash without splitting the entity.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-split, churn spikes as customers refuse to manage two accounts. The new DVD brand is perceived as a "zombie" service, accelerating cancellation. Streaming growth stalls due to the loss of cross-promotion and negative PR surrounding the split. The company is forced to re-integrate at high cost.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal adds significant user friction (two logins, two bills) immediately following a 60% price hike.
*   **Fact:** It explicitly discards shared data (ratings/recommendations), reducing product value for no immediate revenue gain.
*   **Contradiction:** The rationale cites "customer confusion" as a risk, yet the solution (splitting brands/billing) is the primary driver of that confusion.

**Risks:** Accelerated decline of DVD revenue; stalled streaming adoption; brand damage perceived as corporate greed.

**Decision:** The plan solves an internal organizational problem by creating an external customer crisis. It fails to beat "do nothing" or internal restructuring on cost, risk, or reversibility.

DECISION: NO-GO
