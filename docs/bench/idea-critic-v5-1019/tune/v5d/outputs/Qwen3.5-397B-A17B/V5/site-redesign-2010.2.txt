First call: NO-GO. The proposal offers a massive engineering risk and a forced migration without a stated edge over the current stable product; "slowing traffic" is a symptom, not a competitive advantage.

**Steelman:** A complete rewrite with publisher-favored surfacing and forced migration is the only way to reverse traffic decline and satisfy publishers.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves the loyal community while A/B testing surfacing changes. A "staged rollout" mitigates the no-rollback risk. The proposal beats neither on cost or safety.
2.  **Fit:** Alienating the core community (who value current voting mechanics) to favor publishers contradicts the asset (loyal community) driving the existing traffic.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because the core community revolts against changed voting mechanics and forced migration, while the new publisher-centric content fails to attract enough new users to offset the exodus. The rival site absorbs the displaced users.
4.  **Evidence:** No data proves users want publisher-favored content or that a rewrite fixes traffic. The "large engineering risk" is admitted but unmitigated.
5.  **Blocker:** The "move all users at once" strategy creates a one-way door with no rollback, violating basic operational hygiene for a high-risk change. The plan admits this risk but offers no mitigation.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Rollback:** Forced immediate migration on a full rewrite prevents recovery from failure.
*   **Contradictory Value:** Favoring publishers risks the core community value proposition without evidence of gain.

**Risks:**
*   Community revolt due to changed voting mechanics.
*   Engineering delays or bugs in a from-scratch rewrite.
*   Competitor gains share during instability.

**Decision:** NO-GO. The plan lacks a pilot, caps, or rollback. It bets the entire company on an unproven hypothesis with maximum execution risk.

**Kill Criteria:** If a staged pilot of the new surfacing algorithm does not increase time-on-site by 10% within 4 weeks without reducing core user retention, stop.

**Leading Metric:** Core user retention rate during any future test. Review date: 4 weeks from pilot start.
