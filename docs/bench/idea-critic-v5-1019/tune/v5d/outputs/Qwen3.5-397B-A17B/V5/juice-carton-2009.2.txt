First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the old image is the primary shelf cue for a loyal, older demographic, yet offers no stated edge—such as cost savings, new market access, or verified demand for minimalism—to justify risking immediate recognition failure.

Steelman: A modernized design will attract new buyers without alienating the core base if supported by massive advertising.

Attack: The plan relies on advertising to overcome the loss of the primary visual cue. For an older, loyal demographic, shelf recognition is instantaneous; if the cue vanishes, the product becomes invisible before the ad campaign registers. The "edge" is purely aesthetic, while the risk is functional invisibility. The proposal admits the risk (loss of cue) but treats it as solvable by spend, ignoring that the cue itself is the asset being destroyed.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retains shelf visibility and loyalty at zero risk.
2. Staged pilot: Test the new design in a limited region to measure recognition drop-off before a nationwide rollout.
3. Hybrid: Introduce the new cap/graphic elements while retaining the orange-with-straw cue until migration is proven.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-launch, sales have collapsed because older shoppers literally cannot find the product on the shelf. Advertising spend failed to retrain instinctive visual search patterns quickly enough to prevent churn to competitors. The "large campaign" assumption is unverified; no evidence suggests ads can replace decades of conditioned shelf recognition instantly.

Blockers:
1. The proposal explicitly states the old image is the "main shelf cue" and the base is "older," yet plans to remove that cue nationwide immediately. This contradicts the goal of maintaining sales; removing the primary identification mechanism for a vulnerable demographic without a proven transition path is a factual failure of the plan as proposed.
2. No stated edge exists to outweigh the admitted risk; "minimalist design" is not a strategic advantage against "loss of recognition."

Risks: High cost of reprinting, potential brand alienation, advertising inefficacy.

The plan fails because it destroys the product's primary visibility mechanism for its core audience without a validated bridge or a compelling strategic necessity cited in the text.

DECISION: NO-GO
