First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (adult growth) but states no edge; it relies on spending to override a deeply entrenched brand identity rather than leveraging a distinct product or access advantage.

Steelman: A massive ad blitz can forcibly reposition a "kids' brand" as "premium" overnight by overwhelming current perceptions with a superior product.

Attack:
1.  **Fit & Strategy:** The plan contradicts the core constraint: the brand is defined by "child-centered image" and "value." Premium positioning requires trust in quality and atmosphere, which advertising alone cannot manufacture if the underlying brand equity screams "toy."
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves margin. A "better way" would be a sub-brand, a limited-time test in adult-heavy markets, or acquiring a competitor with existing adult equity. The proposal explicitly rejects staging, demanding the "largest budget" before demand is shown.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. Cause: Adults reject the venue as inappropriate for dining regardless of burger quality; loyal value customers flee the price hike; the massive spend burns cash with no ROI because the brand "gravity" pulls the premium offering down to "overpriced kids' food."
4.  **Evidence Gap:** The proposal offers zero evidence that advertising can invert a decade of brand conditioning. It assumes spend equals perception change, a fatal fallacy in consumer goods.
5.  **Blocker:** The plan fails the "cheaper alternative" test. A staged pilot or sub-brand reaches the same upside (testing adult demand) with drastically lower risk and cost. Committing the "largest budget" before validating demand is a blocker under the decision method; it violates the principle of reversible doors.

Blockers:
-   No validation of demand before committing maximum capital (violates staged investment).
-   Cheaper, safer alternatives (pilots, sub-brands) exist that achieve the same learning upside.

Risks:
-   Brand dilution alienating the core child/family base.
-   Price resistance from both new and existing customers.
-   Inability to shake "fast/cheap" perception despite premium pricing.

The absence of a pilot, cap, or metric is a structural failure in the plan itself, not just a missing detail. The proposal demands an irreversible, high-cost bet based on hope rather than evidence.

DECISION: NO-GO
