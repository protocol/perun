First call: GO. The stated edge is unique: a garage-fit, car-driving van on an existing compact platform using a converted factory, arriving immediately after a government rescue where families currently compromise with wagons or oversized vans. No competitor offers this specific blend of convenience and familiarity.

Steelman: Leveraging sunk costs in compact-car tooling and idle factory space to create a new "minivan" category that solves the garage-size constraint for families, distinct from commercial trucks.

Options analysis:
1. Do nothing: Continue losing to wagons; risk another collapse when loan terms tighten.
2. Full-size van update: High cost, fails the garage constraint, retains commercial stigma.
3. New platform development: Cash-poor company cannot afford the R&D or tooling time.
The proposal beats "do nothing" by creating a market rather than fighting in a shrinking one, and beats alternatives by utilizing existing assets (platform, factory) to minimize cash burn.

Fit: Aligns with the survival stage and the implicit north star of "cash-efficient volume growth." It repurposes rescue assets for a consumer breakthrough.

Pre-mortem & Door: If it fails, it will be because marketing positions it as a "cheap truck" rather than a "family car," or the compact platform creates unacceptable cargo compromises. This is a two-way door; if the category doesn't stick, the line can be halted or retooled with less loss than a clean-sheet design. Cost of delay is high: rivals (Ford/GM) are watching the rescue and could pivot if given a quarter to react.

Evidence & Blockers: The proposal relies on the fact that the compact platform exists and the factory is convertible. The risks (no category, cash shortage, copycats) are real but not blockers. "No category" is the opportunity, not a flaw. Cash shortage dictates *this* specific low-capital approach. Copycats are inevitable but first-mover advantage in a new segment is defensible. The platform space limit is a trade-off for the garage fit, which is the primary value prop.

Blockers: NONE. The constraints (cash, factory) drive the solution rather than preventing it.
Risks: Marketing misfire (commercial perception); platform space limitations. These are managed by positioning and design tuning, not reasons to stop.

Kill criteria: If pre-orders from family-focused dealerships do not exceed commercial fleet interest by 2:1 within 6 months of reveal, pivot to fleet-only or cancel. Leading metric: Ratio of retail vs. fleet inquiries at launch.

DECISION: GO
