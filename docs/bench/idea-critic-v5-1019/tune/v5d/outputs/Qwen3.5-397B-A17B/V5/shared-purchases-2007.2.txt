First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a revenue goal but offers no stated edge explaining why this specific mechanism (broadcasting private purchases to friends) would succeed where generic ads exist, while explicitly acknowledging severe privacy backlash and partner embarrassment.

Steelman: Transparently sharing verified purchase activity with explicit user consent and an opt-out will increase ad relevance and social proof, driving higher merchant spend without triggering mass abandonment.

Attack:
1. **Incentive Misalignment**: The "notification step" and "option to decline" admit the action is intrusive. If users truly valued this, they would opt-in by default; requiring an opt-out for sensitive data (purchases) signals the company knows it is overreaching.
2. **Partner Risk**: Merchants pay for positive association. Broadcasting real-time purchases to friends invites immediate, public negative commentary (e.g., cheap gifts, medical supplies, debt) directly on the merchant's brand surface. Partners will likely block this integration to avoid reputational damage, killing the inventory supply.
3. **Irreversibility**: The proposal admits the feature is "hard to withdraw." Once private data flows into news feeds, users cannot "unsee" it. A single high-profile embarrassment (e.g., sensitive health purchase exposed) creates a permanent trust deficit that no amount of revenue can offset.
4. **No Edge**: Competitors can target ads based on browsing without exposing specific transaction details socially. This plan trades long-term platform trust for short-term merchant fees, with no unique advantage cited.

Blockers:
- The proposal itself states the feature is hard to withdraw and risks partner embarrassment; these are admitted facts that contradict the viability of a sustainable ad product.
- No evidence suggests users want purchase history broadcasted; the need for an opt-out confirms the opposite.

Risks:
- Mass user backlash and churn.
- Partner exodus due to brand safety concerns.
- Regulatory scrutiny on data sharing.

The plan fails its own logic: it relies on sharing data users and partners are explicitly flagged as potentially unwilling to share, with no exit strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
