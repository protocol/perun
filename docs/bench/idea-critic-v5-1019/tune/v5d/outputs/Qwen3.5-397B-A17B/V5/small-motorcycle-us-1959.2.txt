First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the product (small 50cc) conflicts with the stated market preference (large machines), the brand is unknown, and the company lacks a product capable of US highway conditions, all while risking scarce foreign currency. There is no stated edge explaining why Americans would suddenly want a small bike from an unknown brand when their current options are larger and established.

Steelman: The US market is not monolithic; a hidden segment of students and commuters exists who are priced out of or intimidated by large motorcycles, and non-traditional retail channels can reach them without triggering the "rough" motorcycle stigma.

Attack: The proposal relies on creating demand where none is stated to exist. The core mechanism for success—selling a 50cc bike in a market that favors large machines—requires a behavioral shift not supported by the provided facts. The admission that larger models fail at US highway speeds removes the ability to cross-sell or build brand prestige through performance, a standard route for market entry. The risk to foreign currency is immediate and tangible; the reward is hypothetical.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserves capital, avoids reputation risk from potential mechanical failures of larger bikes or market rejection of small ones.
2. Export only larger models: Fails due to stated mechanical limitations at US speeds.
3. License production locally: Mitigates currency risk but still faces the demand mismatch.

Pre-mortem: The subsidiary opens, burns through foreign currency on rent and staff, and fails to move inventory because the 50cc bikes are viewed as toys or inferior mopeds, while the brand cannot support a full motorcycle lineup due to mechanical reliability issues. The venture closes within 18 months.

Blockers:
1. Product-Market Mismatch: The proposal states Americans favor large machines and the brand is unknown, offering no evidence or mechanism to overcome this inertia.
2. Capability Gap: The company's larger models cannot handle US highway speeds, limiting the product portfolio to a niche segment with unproven demand.
3. Resource Risk: Tying up scarce foreign currency on a speculative entry into a hostile market violates prudent capital allocation without a guaranteed wedge.

Risks: Cultural rejection, channel conflict, brand dilution.

The plan commits resources to a strategy that contradicts the stated market realities and internal capability limits without a proven wedge to bridge the gap.

DECISION: NO-GO
