First call: NO-GO. The stated facts show zero market validation from the only two potential partners consulted, and the company lacks the core competency (consumer experience) required to execute the pivot.

**Steelman:** A low-cost, randomized robot can succeed by prioritizing affordability and simplicity over perfect coverage, creating a new mass-market category that mapping rivals ignore.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Signal:** Two specialized floor-care companies rejected the prototype. In 2002, these are the exact channel partners needed for distribution and credibility. Their rejection suggests the "randomized" approach fails the basic utility threshold for professionals, casting doubt on consumer acceptance.
2.  **Capability Gap:** The proposal admits "no consumer-product experience." Moving from B2B prototypes to mass-market retail requires supply chains, support infrastructure, and marketing muscles the company does not possess. This is a fatal execution risk, not a manageable hurdle.
3.  **Trust Deficit:** Buyers are already skeptical of robots in the home. A "dumber" (random) robot that cannot guarantee cleanliness exacerbates this trust issue compared to "smarter" mapping rivals, even at a lower price.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows the company to refine the technology until it attracts a partner. Licensing the tech to an existing consumer brand is a safer alternative than building a consumer division from scratch.

**Blockers:**
*   **Validated Demand:** Zero. The only tested audience (experts) rejected it.
*   **Execution Capacity:** None. The company explicitly lacks the required experience.
*   **Product-Market Fit:** The core differentiator (random vs. map) is framed as a liability (incomplete cleaning) by the market experts.

**Risks:**
*   Brand destruction if the product fails in early consumer trials.
*   Capital burn building a retail engine without a guaranteed winner.
*   Competitors with mapping tech dropping prices to squeeze out the low-cost entrant.

The proposal asks to bet the company on an unproven product, in an unproven channel, with an unproven team, against explicit negative feedback from the only available experts. The absence of a pilot, cap, or metric is secondary to the fundamental lack of fit and capability.

DECISION: NO-GO
