First call: GO. The stated edge is clear: the current supplier cannot deliver cool, efficient chips for thin laptops, while the dominant architecture can. This solves an immediate product constraint (thermal/efficiency) that the current roadmap cannot address within the relevant timeframe.

**Steelman:** Migrating to the dominant architecture is the only viable path to competitive thin-and-light hardware within two years, and a translation layer mitigates the software discontinuity risk enough to make the transition feasible.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Continue with an inferior roadmap. Result: Product stagnation in the growing thin-laptop segment; eventual irrelevance.
2.  **Partial Move:** Keep old chips for legacy, new for thin. Result: Fragmented user experience, doubled engineering overhead, brand confusion.
3.  **The Proposal:** High execution risk (translation speed, developer friction, sales freeze), but it aligns the hardware with market physics (thermal limits) and user demand (portability).

**Fit:** Moves the north star of "best user experience" by enabling form factors currently impossible. The risk to distinctiveness is real but secondary to having a sellable product.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the translation layer is too slow for pro users, causing a reputation crash, or if the announcement cannibalizes current sales before new units ship. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental flaws in the direction. The "painful earlier transition" is a warning for *how* to execute, not *whether* to proceed.

**Evidence:** The premise rests on the supplier's failure to deliver cool/efficient chips. If true (accepted as stated fact), the current path is a dead end.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented proves the plan fails as proposed. The risks (sales freeze, slow translation) are manageable via timing and engineering, not reasons to abort.
**Risks:** Developer adoption lag, performance overhead of translation, brand dilution. These become kill criteria: if translation performance drops below 80% native speed for key apps by pilot date, or if pre-orders miss targets by >20%, pivot or extend the dual-architecture window.

The absence of a perfect path does not justify staying on a broken one. The proposal addresses a hard physical constraint (heat/efficiency) that the current strategy cannot solve.

DECISION: GO
