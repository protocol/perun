First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: mixed recent reviews, loss of the creative lead, and high price against current output indicate the asset is deteriorating, not advantaged. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Acquiring this franchise secures essential IP to feed parks and products, betting that new leadership can revive the brand despite recent stagnation.

Attack: The core claim relies on the franchise's ability to generate new hits. However, the facts state recent films drew mixed reviews and the founder—the primary creative engine—is retiring. Without a named successor or a proven mechanism to replicate the founder's vision, the "space-fantasy" appeal may be non-transferable. The $4 billion price assumes future success that the current trajectory contradicts.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids $4B outlay for a declining asset; preserves capital for organic growth or fresher IP.
2. License specific elements: Cheaper, lower risk; tests fan appetite without buying the whole deteriorating company.
3. Wait for distress: If new films fail, price drops; buy later when risk is priced in.

Fit: While the strategy seeks cross-business franchises, buying a weakening asset at a peak price contradicts sound capital allocation. A north star of "growth" is not served by overpaying for past glory.

Pre-mortem: New trilogy flops due to lack of founder's touch; fans reject canon changes; merchandise licenses expire without renewal leverage. The $4B becomes a sunk cost with no park integration ROI. This is a one-way door with massive delay cost if the brand dies during integration.

Evidence: The proposal admits mixed reviews and leadership loss. No evidence suggests a viable succession plan or that fans want more entries without the founder. The high price is unjustified by current film output metrics.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: The plan offers no fact showing why this specific asset beats "do nothing" given the creative vacuum and mixed reception.
2. Contradictory facts: High price vs. declining creative quality and leadership exit.

Risks: Fan rejection, merchandise saturation, execution failure without the founder.

The absence of a succession plan or proof of renewed fan demand, combined with a premium price for a peaking asset, constitutes a failure to demonstrate value over alternatives.

DECISION: NO-GO
