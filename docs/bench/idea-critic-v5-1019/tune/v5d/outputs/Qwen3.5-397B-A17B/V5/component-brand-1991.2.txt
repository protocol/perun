First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it asserts a need (brand differentiation) but offers no evidence that end-users care about invisible components or that computer makers will comply without destroying margins.

**Steelman:** If consumers can be trained to value a hidden component, demand pull will force computer makers to adopt the chip to avoid losing sales, justifying the ad subsidy.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Misalignment:** Computer makers control the shelf and the customer relationship. Subsidizing their ads while dictating content invites resentment and non-compliance. They have no incentive to highlight a supplier over their own brand.
2.  **Unproven Premise:** "Consumer advertising for a component is unprecedented" is cited as a risk, yet the plan relies entirely on this behavior changing. Without proof that buyers distinguish or care about part numbers (which are currently "similar" and confusing), the campaign burns budget on awareness that cannot convert to preference.
3.  **Financial Exposure:** Allocating a "large share" of the marketing budget to an unproven channel creates a single point of failure. If the pull mechanism fails, the core business lacks defensive marketing funds.
4.  **Measurement Void:** The proposal admits benefits "may not show in sales." Launching a massive spend without a pre-committed kill criterion or a pilot to test consumer recall is fiscally irresponsible.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal fails to explain why this specific approach beats the status quo or cheaper alternatives (e.g., direct B2B technical marketing). Need is not an edge.
*   **Contradictory Logic:** It relies on computer makers voluntarily ceding advertising control to a supplier while simultaneously fearing they will resent it. The plan does not resolve this tension.

**Risks:**
*   Channel conflict causing makers to promote competitors.
*   Budget exhaustion with zero ROI.
*   Consumer confusion due to technical complexity.

**Conclusion:** The plan bets the marketing budget on a behavioral shift (consumer care for components) that is explicitly admitted as unprecedented and unproven. It lacks a pilot, a cap, and a mechanism to ensure partner compliance. As proposed, it is a speculative burn of capital without a verified route to the upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
