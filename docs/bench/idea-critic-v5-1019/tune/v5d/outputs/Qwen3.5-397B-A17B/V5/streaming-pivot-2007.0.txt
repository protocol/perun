First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it admits inferior content, poor quality, limited distribution, and high costs while threatening the core profitable business.

Steelman: Launching a free, limited streaming service in 2007 secures future relevance by habituating users to digital delivery before competitors dominate, leveraging the existing subscriber base as a testing ground.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Protects current margins but risks obsolescence if broadband adoption accelerates faster than expected.
2. Paid standalone streaming: Tests willingness to pay without cannibalizing DVD revenue, but fractures the value proposition.
3. Proposed plan (free add-on): Maximizes adoption speed but incurs high licensing/tech costs immediately and accelerates cannibalization of the cash cow with an inferior product.

Fit: Aligns with a long-term vision of digital distribution but contradicts the immediate north star of maximizing profit from the seven million subscribers. The strategic fit relies on an assumed future where broadband becomes universal quickly.

Pre-mortem: The initiative fails because licensing costs drain cash reserves while users reject the poor quality/small catalog, causing them to cancel the entire subscription (DVD + Stream) when a superior competitor launches. This is a two-way door (can shut down streaming), but the cost of delay is low given the current lack of broadband ubiquity.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the catalog is small/old, quality is poor, and broadband is not universal. These are facts, not risks. The core claim—that this drives value—contradicts the stated evidence that the product is objectively worse than the existing DVD service and future competitors.

Blockers:
1. Negative Unit Economics: High licensing costs + tech spend for a "free" feature with no stated revenue uplift or retention metric proves financial loss per user.
2. Product Inferiority: Offering a worse experience (poor quality/small catalog) under the same brand risks diluting the premium perception of the core service.
3. No Edge: The plan beats neither "do nothing" (profit protection) nor a paid tier (revenue generation) on stated facts.

Risks: Cannibalization is certain given the "free" pricing; the only question is the rate. Competitor entry is stated as expected, making the small catalog even less defensible.

DECISION: NO-GO
