First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits a core factual contradiction: the assets to be acquired (the target's network) are explicitly incompatible with the buyer's infrastructure and limited for data, yet the plan offers no stated edge or mechanism to resolve this technical debt before assuming $36 billion in liability. Need for business customers is not an edge; it is a baseline requirement that the target already meets independently.

Steelman: The merger is the only viable path to instantly secure a dominant high-value business segment that cannot be organically built due to spectrum or device ecosystem lock-in, justifying the integration hell as a temporary toll.

Attack:
1. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A "partnership/MVNO" model could access the target's business base without assuming the incompatible network's CAPEX or the $36 billion price tag. The proposal fails to demonstrate why owning the broken legacy tech is superior to renting the customer access.
2. **Fit & Pre-mortem:** In 2005, data growth is the north star. Acquiring a network "limited for data" directly opposes this. Assuming failure: The integration of two incompatible technologies drags on for years (a common industry pattern), burning cash on legacy maintenance while competitors launch superior data-native services. The "large goodwill" becomes an immediate write-down when synergy timelines slip.
3. **Evidence:** The proposal's own facts state the technologies are incompatible and the target is data-limited. No evidence is provided that the buyer has a proven playbook to migrate incompatible legacy users without churn, nor that the $36 billion price excludes the cost of a full network rip-and-replace.

Blockers:
- **Fatal Technical Mismatch:** The core asset (network) is incompatible with the acquirer's stack and strategically obsolete (data-limited), creating an immediate negative synergy in network operations.
- **Unjustified Premium:** Paying $36 billion for a customer base that could potentially be accessed via lighter-weight commercial agreements, while inheriting a network that must be scrapped or expensively upgraded, lacks a calculated ROI edge over alternatives.

Risks: Integration paralysis, massive goodwill impairment, churn during migration, opportunity cost of delayed data rollout.

The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test because it acquires a liability (incompatible network) to get an asset (customers) that might be reachable cheaper. Without a specific, funded migration plan or a price adjustment reflecting the network's obsolescence, the deal destroys value.

DECISION: NO-GO
