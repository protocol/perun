First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it imposes higher costs (subscription + broadband) and operational complexity on a company with no prior experience, while rivals offer free or publisher-managed alternatives. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: A unified, paid service creates superior user retention and quality via centralized identity and voice chat, distinguishing the console as a premium ecosystem.

Attack:
1. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or "Publisher-managed" costs less, matches current user expectations (free), and avoids building unproven hosting operations. The proposal beats neither on cost nor risk.
2. **Fit:** The strategy assumes users will pay for what competitors give free, despite low broadband adoption in 2002. This contradicts the market reality stated in the risks.
3. **Pre-mortem:** If the service launches, low broadband penetration and subscription resistance lead to sparse matchmaking pools, ruining the "superior" experience and leaving the company stuck with high fixed operational costs.
4. **Evidence:** The proposal admits the company has "never run" hosting/support operations. Building this from scratch while fighting entrenched free alternatives is a massive execution risk with no proven demand for a mandatory fee.
5. **Blockers:** The core claim fails because the stated facts (rivals are free/publisher-managed, low broadband, user expectation of free play) directly contradict the viability of a mandatory paid model. There is no evidence provided that users will pay for a service they currently get free, nor that the company can operate it profitably. The lack of a "stated edge" beyond "we want to" is fatal.

Blockers:
- No stated edge over free/publisher alternatives; higher cost to user.
- Company lacks operational experience; high execution risk.
- Market conditions (low broadband, free expectation) oppose mandatory paid model.

Risks:
- Sparse player pools due to adoption friction.
- High fixed costs for underutilized infrastructure.

DECISION: NO-GO
