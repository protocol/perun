First call: GO. The stated edge is speed and channel access: a small, separate team using off-the-shelf parts and independent retail can reach the exploding small-computer market in one year, a timeline and distribution model the company's usual integrated practices cannot match.

**Steelman:** A dedicated skunkworks team can bypass internal inertia to launch a standardized, open-architecture PC via retail channels within 12 months, capturing market share before competitors solidify dominance.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The risks are real but non-blocking. Standard parts do invite copying, but in a nascent market, speed to volume and brand trust outweigh first-mover design secrecy. Dependence on outside software and lower margins are structural features of the PC model, not execution flaws; fighting them would revert to the slow, high-cost mainframe approach that fails in this segment. Lack of retail experience is a capability gap, not a fatal flaw; partnering with established retailers mitigates this faster than building an internal sales force.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Cede the personal computer market to agile startups, risking long-term irrelevance as computing shifts away from mainframes.
2.  **Internal Development:** Build a proprietary system using existing processes. This misses the 12-month window and likely results in an over-engineered, over-priced product unsuited for mass retail.
3.  **Acquisition:** Buy an existing PC maker. This is slower, costlier, and creates integration friction that delays market entry.

The proposal beats "do nothing" by securing a foothold in a transformative market. It beats internal development by accepting lower margins and open standards in exchange for speed and channel access. The "separate team" constraint specifically addresses the cultural mismatch, isolating the new venture from the core business's margin expectations and development cycles.

**Blockers:** NONE. The facts provided show a clear strategic necessity where the proposed method is the only viable path to the required speed.
**Risks:** Margin erosion, competitor cloning, supply chain dependence. These are managed by the speed of execution and the scale of the brand, not by altering the fundamental approach.

The plan requires a strict cap on time (12 months) and autonomy for the separate team. Success is measured by unit volume and market presence within 18 months, not by mainframe-style margin targets.

DECISION: GO
