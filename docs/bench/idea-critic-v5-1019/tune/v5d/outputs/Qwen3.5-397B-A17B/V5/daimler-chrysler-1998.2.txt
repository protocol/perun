First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a cyclical peak (US truck boom) to justify a permanent structural union, while ignoring the fatal cultural and operational incompatibilities between a premium German engineering culture and a US mass-market volume culture.

Steelman: Combining complementary brand portfolios and platforms creates a global scale leader that balances premium margins with mass-market volume, securing long-term dominance.

Attack: The "merger of equals" structure is likely a facade masking a takeover, creating immediate governance paralysis. The US partner's profitability is tied to a specific, cyclical segment (light trucks) that may not sustain a premium valuation post-merger. The cultural clash between German precision/hierarchy and US flexibility/volume focus will destroy the anticipated synergies in platform sharing. The "global scale" argument fails if the integration costs and brand dilution outweigh the theoretical benefits.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Preserve the German firm's premium margins and the US firm's current cyclical profits without integration risk.
2. Strategic Alliance: Share specific platforms or technology without a full stock merger, avoiding cultural collision and governance deadlock.
3. Proceed as proposed: High risk of value destruction due to culture clash, governance failure, and cyclical exposure.

Fit: This contradicts the north star of sustainable profitability if the integration destroys the very brand equity (premium vs. mass) that defines both companies.

Pre-mortem: The merger fails because the cultures cannot integrate, leading to talent exodus, brand confusion, and an inability to execute platform sharing. The US truck market corrects, dragging down the combined entity's stock. This is a one-way door with massive reversal costs.

Evidence: The proposal admits "very different corporate and labour cultures" and "dependence on a cyclical light-truck boom." These are stated facts that act as blockers, not just risks. The "merger of equals" claim is contradicted by the inherent power dynamics of a premium brand acquiring a volume player.

Blockers:
1. Stated cultural incompatibility prevents the operational synergy required for the thesis to hold.
2. Reliance on a cyclical peak for the US partner's valuation introduces immediate downside risk that the premium brand cannot absorb.
3. Governance structure ("merger of equals") historically leads to decision paralysis in crises.

Risks: Brand dilution, execution failure, market cyclicality.

Kill criteria: If integration costs exceed 15% of projected synergies within 18 months, or if premium brand margins drop by >5% due to brand confusion, trigger a divestiture.

DECISION: NO-GO
