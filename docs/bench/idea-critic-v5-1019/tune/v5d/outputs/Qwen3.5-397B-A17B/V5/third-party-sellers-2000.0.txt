First call: NO-GO. An unprofitable retailer inviting competitors to undercut its own inventory on the same page surrenders its only leverage—control of the customer experience and margin—without a stated edge proving this drives net profit over lost sales.

Steelman: Aggregating supply creates a "long tail" selection that draws traffic, monetizing real estate via fees rather than inventory risk.

Attack:
1. **Cannibalization vs. Fees:** If a competitor sells a used book for $5 (fee $0.75) while the retailer holds new stock at $15, the retailer loses the $15 sale. The fee rarely covers the lost gross margin on the core item, especially when the retailer is already unprofitable.
2. **Trust & Control:** The proposal admits less control over service quality. For a trust-dependent retailer, one bad third-party transaction damages the brand equity required to sell its own inventory.
3. **Supplier Retaliation:** Publishers and suppliers may view this as channel conflict, potentially restricting supply or raising costs for the retailer's own inventory, crippling the primary business.
4. **Missing Edge:** The proposal cites "growing range" but offers no evidence that third-party selection converts browsers who wouldn't buy from the retailer's own stock. Without a proven demand gap, this is simply subsidizing competitors.

Risks are not blockers if mitigated, but here the risks are intrinsic to the mechanism: the value prop to the customer (cheaper/used options) is directly antagonistic to the retailer's survival (selling new inventory at margin). The "fee per sale" model works only if the retailer's own inventory is insufficient to meet demand; the proposal implies the inventory is "growing," suggesting capacity exists.

Blockers:
- **Economic Contradiction:** The plan relies on competitors winning sales on the retailer's own product pages. For an unprofitable entity, facilitating a competitor's sale is a net loss unless the fee exceeds the lost margin of a direct sale, which is unlikely given price competition.
- **Strategic Misalignment:** No stated edge explains why this specific move turns the profit corner now, rather than focusing on moving existing inventory.

Risks: Supplier revolt, brand degradation, permanent margin compression.

DECISION: NO-GO
