First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a critical negative fact as already true: "development has already cost heavily." This indicates massive sunk costs are driving the decision rather than a validated market edge. While the price advantage is a stated feature, it is not a sufficient edge to overcome the explicitly acknowledged lack of prestige and the high fixed cost of a new dealer network for a single model.

Steelman: A distinct brand can isolate the luxury effort from the economy reputation, using aggressive underpricing to force trial despite the thin lineup.

However, the "better way" analysis reveals a fatal flaw in the current approach. Building a separate dealer network for a single flagship sedan creates an unsustainable cost base before volume is proven. The cheaper, safer alternative is to leverage existing channels or launch with a multi-model lineup to justify infrastructure costs. Launching one car into a prestige-dominated market with a new, unproven distribution layer is economically fragile.

The pre-mortem is clear: The brand launches, but the single model fails to convince buyers to ignore the "economy" stigma, leaving the expensive new dealer network empty and compounding the already heavy development losses. The cost of delay is low compared to the risk of immediate, catastrophic capital deployment on an unproven distribution model.

Blockers:
1. Economic Viability: The proposal admits heavy development costs are already sunk (a negative indicator of discipline) and adds the high fixed cost of a new dealer network for a single product. This structure cannot reach profitability without unrealistic volume assumptions for a debut luxury brand.
2. Market Fit: The stated risk that buyers won't pay luxury prices to an economy maker is not mitigated by price alone; prestige is the primary product in this segment, and the proposal offers no mechanism to manufacture it instantly.

Risks: Brand dilution, dealer bankruptcy, failure to achieve quality parity despite engineering claims.

Kill Criteria: If pre-orders from target luxury demographics do not cover 20% of the new dealer network's operating costs within six months of announcement, terminate the dealer expansion and pivot to a shared-channel strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
