First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "unfinished" is a liability, not a differentiator, and no fact suggests buyers prefer rough alphas over polished competitors in 2009.

Steelman: Releasing early funds development and validates demand through real sales rather than speculation.
Attack: In 2009, the "early access" model is unproven and culturally rejected; players expect complete products. Selling a broken game risks permanent reputation damage ("scam" labeling) that kills future sales.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Finish privately, release complete. Low risk, delayed revenue, high quality perception.
2. Free demo + waitlist: Validate interest without monetizing roughness. Zero revenue risk, builds audience.
3. Proposed sale: Immediate cash, high churn risk, potential brand toxicity.
The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" because the immediate cash is outweighed by the high probability of alienating the only audience the solo dev has.

Fit: A solo dev's north star is sustainable growth and reputation. Releasing a product known to be unfinished contradicts the quality expectation required for a solo brand to survive without a publisher's buffer.

Pre-mortem: The game launches; forums fill with complaints about bugs and missing features. The developer, overwhelmed by support tickets, cannot code new features. Sales halt within a month due to negative reviews. The project is abandoned, and the developer cannot launch a sequel. This is a one-way door for reputation; a bad first impression is irreversible.

Evidence: The proposal admits "no publisher or marketing budget" and "cannot support a large audience." Selling creates an immediate support obligation the dev explicitly cannot meet. The math fails: Revenue = Buyers × Price. If Buyers drop to zero due to negative word-of-mouth (highly likely given the rough state), Revenue is zero, but the reputational cost is permanent.

Blockers:
1. Capacity mismatch: The proposal states the dev "cannot support a large audience," yet selling publicly invites exactly that, guaranteeing failure to deliver support.
2. Market timing: In 2009, no established mechanism exists to frame "unfinished" as a feature rather than a defect.

Risks: Running out of money before completion; losing player trust permanently.

DECISION: NO-GO
