First call: GO. The proposal targets a specific, emerging demographic (young postwar buyers) with a stated edge: sporty styling at a low price via parts reuse, which existing cheap cars lack.

Steelman: Reusing proven mechanicals from a reliable compact car minimizes development risk and cost, enabling a low base price that undercuts competitors while offering the desired aesthetic. The long options list captures higher margins from enthusiasts willing to pay for customization.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Cede the growing youth market to competitors; revenue stagnates as the current compact car ages.
2. Build a new platform: Prohibitively expensive and slow; misses the immediate demographic window.
3. Upgrade the existing compact: Fails to deliver the distinct "sporty" image required; brand dilution risk.

Fit: Aligns with capturing market share among the booming youth segment. Assumes the aesthetic transformation is sufficient to mask the economy underpinnings.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the "cheap" perception of the donor car overwhelms the sporty styling, or if the advertising fails to define a new category. However, the two-way door nature of options allows mid-course correction on features. The cost of delay is losing the first wave of this demographic to rivals.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the verified fact of the baby boom entering the market and the mechanical reliability of the existing compact. The financial model assumes high attachment rates on options to offset the low base price.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (unproven demand, cheap perception, heavy spend) are execution risks, not factual blockers. They are mitigated by the low cost of the underlying platform and the potential for high-margin option sales. The "cheap" look is addressed by the specific design mandate (long hood, short deck).

Risks: Marketing miss; option take-rate lower than projected. These become kill criteria: if first-quarter option attachment is below 40% or brand perception surveys cite "economy" over "sport," pivot marketing or trim levels immediately.

The plan beats "do nothing" by addressing a clear demographic shift with a capital-efficient product. The risks are manageable given the low floor provided by the shared mechanics.

DECISION: GO
