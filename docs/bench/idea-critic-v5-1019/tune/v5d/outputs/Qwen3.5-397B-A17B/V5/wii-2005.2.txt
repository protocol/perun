First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge proving this plan beats existing low-cost alternatives for non-gamers; "casual audience" is a need, not a competitive advantage against established PC or handheld markets.

Steelman: A radically lower price point and intuitive motion interface will unlock a massive, ignored demographic of non-gamers, creating a blue ocean where graphics fidelity is irrelevant.

Attack:
1. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or releasing a standard cheap console retains the core base without alienating third-party publishers. The "cheaper path" of a standard low-spec box avoids the massive education cost required to teach non-gamers how to play.
2. **Fit:** Abandoning the "graphics-power race" contradicts the industry's north star of technological progression. Without high-definition output, the device risks being perceived as a toy rather than a legitimate console, permanently branding the maker as a niche novelty.
3. **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Third-party publishers refuse to support a non-HD, low-margin platform with an unproven controller. The "casual" audience proves unwilling to buy dedicated hardware for simple games available on existing devices. The console becomes a stranded asset with no software library.
4. **Evidence:** The proposal admits hardcore gamers and publishers are skeptical. There is no verified data that non-gamers want a dedicated console; they may prefer PCs or handhelds. The thin hardware margin leaves no room for error or marketing spend to overcome the "toy" perception.

Blockers:
- No stated edge: The proposal identifies a gap (non-gamers) but offers no fact proving this specific hardware configuration beats current solutions for that group.
- Contradictory evidence: Publisher skepticism and lack of HD output directly undermine the software ecosystem required for any console's survival.

Risks:
- Market rejection of motion controls as a gimmick.
- Inability to secure third-party titles due to non-standard architecture and low margins.
- Brand damage from being labeled a "toy" manufacturer.

The absence of a pilot, a verified demand signal for motion-based non-HD gaming, or a strategy to secure software support constitutes a fatal flaw. A cheaper, staged alternative (e.g., an accessory for an existing HD console) would test the hypothesis without betting the company on unproven hardware.

DECISION: NO-GO
