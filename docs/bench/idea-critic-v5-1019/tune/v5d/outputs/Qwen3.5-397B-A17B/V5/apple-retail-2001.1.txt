First call: NO-GO. The proposal cites a strategic need (demonstration) but offers no stated edge proving company-run stores beat existing channels on cost, reach, or conversion given the firm's losses and the industry's poor retail track record. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Direct retail is the only viable path to demonstrate the integrated hardware-software music strategy, creating enough brand pull to offset high fixed costs and reseller friction.

Attack:
1. **Alternatives:** The proposal ignores cheaper, reversible options. Dedicated "shop-in-shop" zones within existing retailer footprints or pop-up kiosks could demonstrate the product without prime mall leases. These reach the same upside (demonstration) with lower capex and no long-term liability.
2. **Fit & Pre-mortem:** A loss-making firm entering a low-margin, high-rent sector during a recession is a classic cash-burn trap. If sales miss targets by 20%, the fixed lease obligations become insolvency drivers. The "poor industry track record" is a stated fact, not a risk to be managed; it is a predictor of failure unless this plan has a unique mechanism to succeed where others failed. None is stated.
3. **Evidence:** The claim that resellers "push cheaper machines" is plausible but unquantified. Without data showing that demonstration directly converts to sales at a rate that covers the massive rent differential, the math fails.

Blockers:
- **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits high costs and low margins but fails to articulate why *this* specific store model generates sufficient incremental margin or volume to overcome the deficit, especially against cheaper alternatives.
- **Contradictory Context:** Launching a capital-intensive, fixed-cost channel while running losses and facing a recession contradicts basic survival logic without a proven, unique conversion advantage.

Risks:
- Channel conflict causing reseller retaliation.
- Inventory obsolescence in a fast-moving tech market.
- Recession reducing discretionary mall traffic.

The plan commits to a high-fixed-cost model without proving it outperforms a staged, lower-cost demonstration strategy. It fails the "beat do nothing" and "beat cheaper alternative" tests on the stated facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
