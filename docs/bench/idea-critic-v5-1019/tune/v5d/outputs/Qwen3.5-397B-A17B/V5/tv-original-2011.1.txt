First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge where data proves this specific director-star combination drives retention better than licensed content; need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Leveraging unique viewership data to bypass traditional pilot testing and secure a competitive moat with exclusive prestige content before rivals can react.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Maintain rising licensing costs, risking margin erosion.
2. Pilot first: Standard industry practice to de-risk creative and audience fit; lower upfront cost, slower time-to-market.
3. Proposed plan: High cost, high speed, zero creative validation.

Fit: Assumes the goal is rapid differentiation and ownership of IP. However, committing two full seasons without a pilot contradicts the "data-driven" claim if the data cannot predict narrative success for unproven formats.

Pre-mortem: The show launches to critical indifference or audience confusion. Without weekly release, cultural conversation fizzles immediately. The massive sunk cost of two seasons prevents cancellation, draining the budget from other potential hits. This is a one-way door; the money is gone.

Evidence: The proposal cites "viewing data" but offers no proof that this data predicts success for original drama, let alone a specific creative team. No online service has done this; the "edge" is unproven speculation. The cost consumes a large share of the budget, creating single-point failure risk.

Blockers:
1. No validation mechanism: Skipping the pilot removes the only industry-standard filter for creative viability. Data on past viewing does not equate to predictive power for original narrative fiction.
2. Over-commitment: Ordering two seasons upfront eliminates the ability to pivot after episode 1 or 2 based on real-world performance.
3. Unproven distribution model: Releasing all at once assumes binge-watching drives subscription retention more than weekly anticipation, which is unverified for this demographic.

Risks: Total budget loss if the show fails; inability to compete if licensed content becomes unavailable; cultural irrelevance without weekly buzz.

The absence of a pilot and the commitment to two seasons creates a fatal lack of reversibility. A sound operator would demand a single-season order or a pilot to validate the data's predictive power before betting the company's content budget.

DECISION: NO-GO
