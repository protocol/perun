First call: NO-GO. The proposal removes a universal, reliable standard (the headphone jack) to force adoption of a nascent, expensive, and fragile alternative without a stated edge that compels buyers to switch today. Need (wireless convenience) is not an edge; incumbents already offer Bluetooth, and the high price ($159 vs. free) combined with high loss risk and short battery life (5 hours) creates a net negative value proposition compared to the status quo.

Steelman: Removing the jack forces a market shift to wireless, allowing the company to capture high-margin accessory revenue and control the user experience via a custom chip, provided the convenience outweighs the friction.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current facts:
1.  **Value Mismatch:** Charging $159 for a product that is easier to lose, has limited battery life, and looks "odd" (inviting ridicule) offers no rational upgrade path from free, reliable wired earphones.
2.  **Customer Hostility:** Removing the jack angers customers by breaking compatibility with existing ecosystems without offering a superior immediate alternative.
3.  **No Moat:** "Automatic pairing" is a feature, not a defensible edge against established Bluetooth standards if the core hardware (battery, form factor) is inferior.

**Blockers:**
*   **Negative Value Proposition:** The proposed solution costs significantly more ($159 vs $0) while delivering less utility (5hr battery, loss risk, social stigma) than the removed feature. This contradicts basic adoption economics.
*   **Unproven Demand:** The proposal admits the product "may be ridiculed" and users "may be angry," yet proceeds without a pilot or lower-risk entry point (e.g., keeping the jack while launching the buds optionally).

**Risks:** High inventory waste if ridiculed; brand damage from perceived arrogance; low attach rate due to price.

The plan fails the "beat what buyers have today" test. A sound operator would not remove the jack until the wireless alternative is undeniably superior in cost, reliability, or battery life, or until the wireless option is proven at a lower price point. Launching both simultaneously (keeping the jack) mitigates the anger risk; removing the jack to force the issue is a gamble with no safety net.

DECISION: NO-GO
