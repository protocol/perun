First call: GO. The stated edge is solving two existential threats—update delivery and cheating—that retail boxes cannot address, while creating a direct customer relationship.

Steelman: A mandatory client is the only viable mechanism to enforce integrity and patch velocity for popular online-capable PC games in 2003, turning a distribution constraint into a platform opportunity.

Attack: The proposal faces significant friction: limited broadband adoption, retail channel conflict, and user aversion to mandatory accounts. Competitors could replicate the feature set. However, "do nothing" guarantees continued failure in anti-cheat and update logistics, eroding the core product value. The "large download" risk is mitigated by the fact that updates are incremental after the initial install, and broadband, while not universal, is the growth trajectory for the target demographic (gamers). Retail pushback is a short-term revenue risk outweighed by the long-term necessity of digital control.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain retail goodwill but lose the game integrity war; high risk of product death.
2. Optional patcher: Fails to stop cheaters who bypass it; fragmented user base.
3. Mandatory client (Proposed): High initial friction, but secures the product future and opens a high-margin store channel.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of maintaining game popularity and longevity.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the client is so buggy at launch that it renders games unplayable, causing a reputational crash. This is an execution risk, not a strategic flaw. The cost of delay is continued cheating and support costs.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (broadband, retail, copying) are market conditions or execution challenges, not facts proving the plan fails as proposed. Broadband is growing; retail can be managed; copying validates the model.

Risks: Launch bugs, retail retaliation, user churn during transition.

Kill Criteria: If the client causes a >20% drop in active users within 3 months due to technical failures, pivot to an optional model. Leading metric: Daily Active Users (DAU) and cheat detection rate.

DECISION: GO
