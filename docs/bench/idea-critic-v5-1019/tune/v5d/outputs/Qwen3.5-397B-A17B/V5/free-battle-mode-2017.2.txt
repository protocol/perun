First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a market need but fails to state an edge; "reusing assets" and "two months" are constraints, not advantages against an incumbent who already owns the genre.

**Steelman:** Launch a free-to-play last-player-standing mode in two months using existing assets to capture market share via cosmetics, leveraging the studio's established art pipeline to undercut competitor development time.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Stated Edge:** The rival dominates the genre. Entering a winner-take-all market with a "fast and cheap" clone offers no unique value proposition (e.g., novel mechanics, distinct art style, or existing community integration) to dislodge the leader.
2.  **Cannibalization Risk:** The proposal admits the free mode could eat into paid game sales. Without a proven conversion funnel from free players to cosmetic buyers (a business model the studio has never run), the net revenue impact is likely negative.
3.  **Operational Mismatch:** Building a live-service economy (cosmetics, anti-cheat, server scaling for battle royale) in two months is unrealistic for a studio with no free-to-play experience. The "two-month" constraint suggests a minimum viable product that will likely fail to retain players against a polished rival.
4.  **Better Way:** The strongest alternative is "do nothing" or a minor update to the paid game. A cheaper, safer path is integrating a limited last-player-standing *feature* into the existing paid title as a bonus mode, testing retention without splitting the community or risking the brand on a standalone free failure.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Edge:** The facts state the rival "owns the genre" and the studio has "no free-to-play business" experience. There is no factual basis in the proposal to believe a rushed, asset-flip clone will succeed where others fail.
*   **Revenue Contradiction:** The plan relies on cosmetic sales, yet the studio lacks the operational history to support this. The risk of cannibalizing the only proven revenue stream (paid game) outweighs the speculative upside.

**Risks:** High execution risk on timeline; brand dilution; splitting the player base.

**Conclusion:** The proposal attempts to fight a market leader with a weaker product (rushed, inexperienced) and a risky monetization shift. It fails the "beat do nothing" test because the downside (cannibalization, wasted dev time) is concrete, while the upside is purely hypothetical.

DECISION: NO-GO
