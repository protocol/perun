First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it offers a higher-priced device with a smaller compatible user base than existing competitors, relying on a market (legal digital downloads) that does not yet exist at scale.

Steelman: The device leverages superior storage density (5GB) to offer a qualitatively better user experience (thousands of songs) that cheaper flash-based players cannot match, betting that hardware superiority will drive computer sales rather than waiting for software compatibility to expand.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoid capital risk but cede the emerging digital music category to incumbents.
2. License to Windows: Immediate addressable market expansion, but dilutes the strategy of using the device to sell proprietary computers.
3. Proceed as proposed: High risk of commercial failure due to price and compatibility limits, but preserves the integrated ecosystem control if the bet on storage density pays off.

Fit: Assumes the north star is selling more proprietary computers, not maximizing player unit volume. If the goal is purely player revenue, this fails. If the goal is ecosystem lock-in, the tiny initial base is a critical weakness.

Pre-mortem: The product fails because the $400 price point exceeds consumer willingness to pay for a peripheral that only works with a minority of computers, and component shortages delay launch past the holiday season. This is a one-way door with high sunk costs.

Evidence: The proposal admits the music industry has not licensed downloads (no content ecosystem) and competitors are cheaper. The "edge" of 5GB is technical, not commercial, without a legal way to fill the drive.

Blockers:
1. No stated route to content: A 5GB drive is useless without a legal, scalable source of music, which the proposal admits does not exist yet.
2. Economic mismatch: Higher price + smaller compatible base + no content = negative expected value based on stated facts.

Risks: Component sourcing delays, consumer rejection of high price, failure to drive computer sales.

The plan commits real money to a product with no clear path to utility (content) or adoption (price/base). The technical advantage (storage) does not overcome the commercial blockers.

DECISION: NO-GO
