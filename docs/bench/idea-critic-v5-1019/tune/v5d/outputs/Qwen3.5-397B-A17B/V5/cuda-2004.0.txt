First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: it raises unit costs and diverts engineering from the core gaming rivalry without a single committed customer or existing large market to justify the expense. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Embedding general-purpose compute capability in every consumer chip now creates a ubiquitous installed base that will inevitably dominate scientific computing once the software ecosystem matures, securing a decade-long monopoly.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Maintain cost leadership in gaming; let academics continue hacking existing APIs. Cost: $0. Risk: Low. Reversibility: N/A.
2. **Targeted High-End:** Build programmable features only for expensive workstation cards. Cost: Low. Risk: Limited reach. Reversibility: High.
3. **Proposal (Mass Market):** Subsidize compute on every unit. Cost: High. Risk: Catastrophic if market fails to materialize. Reversibility: Low (silicon is permanent).

Fit: This contradicts the immediate north star of winning the gaming market share war against the main rival by adding unnecessary cost and distraction.

Pre-mortem: The program burns cash for three years; the compiler remains buggy; gamers refuse to pay the premium for unused features; the rival undercuts price and captures the entire gaming market; the company goes bankrupt before scientific computing becomes viable. This is a one-way door with a high cost of delay (lost gaming share).

Evidence: The proposal admits "no large paying market exists yet" and relies on "academic users experimenting." There is no evidence of demand willing to pay the increased unit cost.

Blockers:
1. **No Stated Edge:** The plan increases costs and distracts from the core business without a current revenue source or committed buyer to offset it.
2. **Negative Unit Economics:** Raising unit costs in a price-sensitive mass market without immediate demand is financially unsound based on the provided facts.

Risks: Engineering distraction, loss of gaming market share, total capital waste on unused silicon area.

The absence of a paying market and the direct threat to the core gaming business constitute a factual blocker. A cheaper, safer alternative is to wait for market signals before committing silicon area on every unit.

DECISION: NO-GO
