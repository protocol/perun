First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but states no edge; a retailer with thin margins, no developer platform experience, and unproven market demand cannot beat incumbent hardware vendors on cost or reliability as proposed.

Steelman: The retailer leverages its massive, spiky internal infrastructure build-out to achieve economies of scale that allow it to offer pay-per-use computing cheaper than incumbents, turning a cost center into a profit engine.

Attack: The claim fails on three stated facts. First, "no one in the company has run a developer platform business at scale" means the operational expertise required to sell reliability to developers is absent; internal spiky usage does not equal external platform stability. Second, "investors already question heavy capital spending" contradicts the need for further undifferentiated heavy lifting to support external tenants without a proven revenue model. Third, "market... is unproven" combined with "large incumbent vendors exist" creates a fatal gap: without a stated differentiation strategy (price, feature, or integration), the plan assumes customers will switch to an unproven retailer over established vendors for no stated reason.

Alternatives: Doing nothing avoids margin dilution and distraction. Optimizing internal infrastructure for retail alone reduces capital spend concerns without adding external complexity. Selling excess capacity via established partners (white-label) would test demand without building a new business line, but the proposal rejects this by insisting on a "distinct" new business.

Pre-mortem: The project fails because reliability SLAs required by enterprise developers are missed due to lack of platform experience, causing churn. Capital spending spikes further, triggering investor revolt, while the core retail business suffers from resource contention during peak spikes. The "unproven" market turns out to be smaller than the cost of acquiring developers away from incumbents.

Blockers: 
1. No stated edge: The proposal admits no unique advantage over incumbents other than existing infrastructure, which is designed for retail spikes, not multi-tenant consistency.
2. Capability gap: Explicit lack of scale developer platform experience blocks execution of a reliability-sensitive business.
3. Capital conflict: Investor skepticism on capex directly conflicts with the heavy investment needed to productize internal tools for external sale.

Risks: Distraction from core retail, margin dilution, brand damage from reliability failures.

DECISION: NO-GO
