First call: NO-GO. A tenfold budget increase with no evidence that audiences will sit through a feature-length cartoon, backed only by internal funds while insiders advise against it, lacks a stated edge over the current steady short-film model.

Steelman: Creating the first full-length animated feature could unlock a new market tier and massive prestige if audiences accept the format.

**Skeptic:** The proposal assumes demand for 80-minute cartoons without data. Shorts succeed due to brevity; features risk boredom. Insiders (family and industry) explicitly oppose this, suggesting unseen structural risks. Funding relies entirely on existing thin profits, creating existential cash-flow danger if the film fails or delays.

**Better-way:** "Do nothing" maintains steady profits with lower risk. Alternatives include producing a medium-length special (3x cost) to test audience endurance before committing to a full feature. Another path is securing external financing to share the risk rather than burning all studio capital.

**Kill-criteria:** If audience testing of longer formats shows drop-off before 40 minutes, stop. If production costs exceed 12x the short budget, halt.

**Pre-mortem:** The film releases to empty theaters because families view cartoons as children's filler, not event cinema. The studio exhausts its cash reserves during overruns, forcing bankruptcy before the next short cycle can recover losses.

**Evidence:** No box office data exists for feature cartoons; all evidence is negative (insider advice, lack of precedents). The budget math (10x cost) vs. unknown ticket multiplier creates a negative expected value based on current facts.

**Blockers:** 
1. No proven market for the core product (feature-length cartoon).
2. Universal opposition from experienced insiders.
3. Funding model threatens entire studio solvency on an unproven hypothesis.

**Risks:** Budget overrun, audience rejection, opportunity cost of halted short production.

The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test. It requires a miracle of audience behavior change while betting the entire company. Without a pilot, external funding, or market validation, this is gambling, not strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
