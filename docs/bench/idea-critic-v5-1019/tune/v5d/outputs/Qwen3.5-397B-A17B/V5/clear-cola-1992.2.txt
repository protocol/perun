First call: NO-GO. A positive test-market response is a need signal, not a stated edge; the proposal fails to explain why "clear" beats existing colorless beverages or why this specific extension captures value rather than diluting the flagship.

Steelman: Clear cola leverages the "purity" trend to capture non-cola drinkers and lapsed users without eroding the core brand's equity, using test data to de-risk a nationwide rollout.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves margin and avoids brand confusion. A "limited regional rollout" (cheaper) tests repeat purchase rates and cannibalization before committing to a "large marketing campaign." The proposal ignores the cheaper, safer staged approach.
2.  **Fit:** If the north star is long-term brand equity, launching a product that risks confusing the core flavor-color association contradicts the strategy. The proposal assumes novelty equals retention, ignoring the high churn risk of "fad" products.
3.  **Evidence:** The claim relies solely on "positive response" in test markets. It lacks data on *repeat* purchase rates versus initial trial. Test markets notoriously overstate demand for novel visuals; without verified repeat metrics, the nationwide projection is unsupported.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in six months. Cause: The product is perceived as a gimmick; novelty buyers do not return, and core customers switch to the clear version temporarily, dropping overall volume. Rivals copy the visual instantly, commoditizing the "clear" attribute before margins recover.
5.  **Blockers:** The proposal commits to a "large marketing campaign" nationwide immediately. This is a one-way door with high sunk costs. The stated risks (cannibalization, low repeat, rival copies) are acknowledged but not mitigated by the plan's structure. A plan that admits high cannibalization risk yet proceeds with a full launch without a containment strategy fails the blocker test.

**Blockers:**
*   Commitment to nationwide launch + large spend before verifying repeat purchase rates (stated risk: novelty buyers may not repeat).
*   No mitigation for stated cannibalization risk; the plan assumes net growth without evidence.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, rapid commoditization by rivals, high customer acquisition cost with low lifetime value.

**Decision:** No-go. The plan must first prove repeat purchase viability and define a cannibalization cap via a limited rollout before approving a nationwide campaign.
