First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (direct consumer access) but fails to state an edge: being cheaper and late against a entrenched leader with 100M+ users is not a defensible advantage, it is a commodity trap.

Steelman: Withdrawing high-value content to force migration to a proprietary, loss-leading platform will capture sufficient market share to offset lost licensing revenue before cash reserves deplete.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" (continue high-margin licensing) or a "hybrid window" (license rivals while building a premium niche) both preserve cash flow. The proposed "all-in" burn eliminates the safety net.
2.  **Fit:** The strategy assumes subscribers value this specific library enough to switch platforms solely for price. Without exclusive *new* hits (theatrical/cable cannibalization risk), the library alone may not sustain retention against a broader competitor.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The platform fails due to technical debt from building a stack from scratch while burning cash on marketing. The loss of licensing revenue creates a cash crunch before subscriber targets are met, forcing a fire sale or shutdown.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits zero experience running a tech platform and relies on "tens of millions" of subscribers in a saturated market. No unit economics prove that the Lifetime Value (LTV) of a low-price subscriber exceeds the Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) plus the opportunity cost of lost licensing fees.
5.  **Blocker:** The plan lacks a stated mechanism to overcome the "content gap" relative to the leader. Being cheaper is not an edge if the product (library breadth + new hits) is perceived as inferior due to the withdrawal from other windows. The math of replacing billions in guaranteed licensing revenue with speculative, low-margin subscription revenue from a cold start does not work on the stated facts.

Risks (non-blocking but severe): Technology execution failure, inability to reach scale before cash runs out, permanent damage to distributor relationships.

Kill Criteria (if attempted): Halt if subscriber growth falls below 20% of target in Year 1, or if cash burn exceeds 150% of forecast while licensing revenue drops >50%.

The absence of a unique distribution edge or a proven tech capability makes this a speculative gamble rather than a sound operation.

DECISION: NO-GO
