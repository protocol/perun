First call: NO-GO. A search advertiser with no hardware DNA proposing a $50M acquisition of a pre-revenue, six-person OS team in a carrier-controlled market lacks a stated edge over incumbent platforms; "free software" is a tactic, not a defensible advantage against entrenched handset makers.

**Steelman:** Acquiring this team is the only way to preemptively secure a mobile gateway for search ads before closed ecosystems lock out third-party indexing, betting that open-source leverage can fracture carrier control faster than organic partnerships.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; rely on existing WAP/mobile web deals. Cost: $0. Risk: Being locked out of future mobile search. Reversibility: N/A.
2.  **Partnerships:** License search to existing OS vendors (Symbian, Windows Mobile). Cost: Low. Risk: Vendors prioritize their own services. Reversibility: High.
3.  **Acquisition (Proposed):** Cost: ≥$50M + multi-year burn. Risk: Total failure if carriers/handset makers reject the OS. Reversibility: Low (sunk cost, reputational damage).

**Fit:** Aligns with the long-term vision of "search everywhere," but contradicts the current stage reality: the company has no distribution channel, no hardware relationships, and no operating system expertise. Moving a stated north star (mobile search volume) requires a distribution mechanism this proposal does not possess.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because handset makers refuse to dilute their differentiation with a commodity OS, and carriers block installation to protect their own portals. The $50M burns while the team tries to build a phone from scratch, distracting from the core search business.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "no revenue model," "carrier resistance," and "handset-maker adoption" issues. These are not risks; they are stated facts that contradict the core claim of viability. The cost ($50M in 2005) is massive for a six-person team with no product, representing a negative ROI based on current facts.

**Blockers:**
1.  **No Distribution Path:** Stated fact that carriers control distribution and have no reason to adopt. Without a carrier deal, the OS dies instantly.
2.  **Capability Mismatch:** A search ad company cannot operate a hardware-adjacent OS venture; the proposal offers no plan to bridge this gap.
3.  **Economic Failure:** $50M for six people with no revenue model violates basic capital efficiency; the "free software" angle does not solve the distribution blocker.

**Risks:** Multi-year payback, open-source governance conflicts, talent retention.

The stated facts show the target users (carriers/handset makers) do not want this, and the acquirer lacks the means to force adoption.

DECISION: NO-GO
