First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a need (slowing growth) and a positive test signal, but fails to state a specific edge proving this plan beats existing alternatives or mitigates the stated, high-probability risks of non-repetition and cannibalization.

**Steelman:** A clear cola leverages "purity" trends to capture novelty seekers and lapsed drinkers without alienating core fans, creating a net-new revenue stream that outweighs internal cannibalization.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves margin and brand equity. A "limited regional rollout" tests repeat purchase rates (the critical unknown) at a fraction of the cost of a nationwide campaign. The proposal ignores the cheaper, safer staged approach.
2.  **Fit:** While extending the brand fits growth goals, a nationwide launch contradicts the risk management required when core mechanisms (color-flavor association) are unproven at scale.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in Q4. Cause: Novelty buyers tried it once, hated the taste (linked to lack of color), and told friends the flagship tastes "chemical" by comparison. Result: Flagship sales drop 5%, new product flops, $50M marketing spend wasted.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits test markets "can overstate demand" and "novelty buyers may not repeat." Yet, it commits to a nationwide launch based *only* on test market data. This is a direct contradiction between the evidence cited and the action proposed.
5.  **Blocker:** The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test because the stated risks (non-repetition, cannibalization) are unmitigated. A nationwide launch locks in these risks before verifying the single most important metric: repeat purchase rate. The alternative (staged rollout) reaches the same upside (validation) cheaper and safer.

**Blockers:**
*   Committing to nationwide spend despite admitted evidence that test markets overstate demand and repeat rates are unknown.
*   Cheaper alternative (staged rollout) achieves validation with less risk.

**Risks:**
*   Brand dilution if the "clear" implication damages flagship perception.
*   Competitor copycats eroding margins before scale is reached.

**Kill Criteria:** If a 3-month regional pilot shows <25% repeat purchase rate, terminate immediately. Leading metric: Repeat purchase rate at 60 days.

DECISION: NO-GO
