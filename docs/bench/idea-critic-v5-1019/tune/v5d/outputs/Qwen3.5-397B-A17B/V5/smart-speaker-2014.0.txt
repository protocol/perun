First call: NO-GO. The proposal cites a recent hardware failure and a non-existent category without stating a unique edge over existing phone assistants; need alone is not an advantage.

Steelman: A dedicated, always-listening cylinder creates a new "ambient computing" category that phone screens cannot match, leveraging member pricing to overcome hardware skepticism.

Attack:
1. **Fit & Edge:** The company's last hardware launch failed. Entering a completely new category (smart speaker) with an unproven form factor (cylinder) and sensitive feature (always-on mic) compounds this risk. Phone assistants exist; the proposal offers no stated reason why users need a second, stationary device.
2. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" avoids repeating a hardware disaster. Improving the existing phone app is cheaper and reaches the installed base immediately.
3. **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Customers reject the always-on microphone due to privacy fears, voice recognition fails in noisy rooms, and the $199 price point is unjustified for a single-function device. The "invitation only" limit masks low demand rather than creating exclusivity.
4. **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no product category like it exists" and "voice recognition often fails." These are not risks to be managed; they are current facts that block the core value proposition. The $99 member price suggests the $199 MSRP is unsustainable, threatening margins.

Blockers:
- **No Stated Edge:** The plan fails to explain why this device beats the phone-based assistants already in users' pockets, especially given the company's recent hardware failure.
- **Contradictory Facts:** The proposal relies on voice recognition working well despite admitting it "often fails," and assumes privacy acceptance despite the "always-listening" controversy.

Risks:
- Privacy backlash against always-on microphones.
- Repeating the recent hardware launch failure.
- High cost of supporting a new, unproven category.

The absence of a pilot, cap, or metric is not a blocker, but the lack of a *stated edge* is fatal. A real need (music/lists) without a defensible advantage over existing solutions is a NO-GO. The cheaper alternative (improving the phone app) reaches the same upside without the hardware risk.

DECISION: NO-GO
