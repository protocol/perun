First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a higher price, no manufacturing history, powerful incumbents who can copy the tech, and unproven buyer willingness to pay a premium. Need alone is not an edge; the stated facts show no advantage over existing options.

Steelman: A superior bagless technology justifies a premium price if early adopters value performance enough to ignore brand and distribution gaps.

Attack: The proposal admits incumbents can copy the idea. If they do, they leverage existing distribution and lower costs, crushing a new entrant with no manufacturing record. The "thousands of prototypes" prove engineering feasibility, not market demand or operational capability. The higher price is a liability, not an asset, without a brand to support it.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids capital loss in a crowded market with high barriers.
2. License the tech to an incumbent: Gains immediate scale and manufacturing, avoiding the "no record" risk, though margins are lower.
3. Partner for manufacturing/retail: Reduces execution risk but still faces the copying threat.

Fit: Assuming the goal is profitable market entry, launching a standalone hardware company with no track record against copy-capable giants contradicts basic survival logic.

Pre-mortem: The company burns cash setting up production, fails to secure shelf space, and is undercut when a major brand releases a similar bagless model at a lower price. This is a one-way door with high cost of delay if the window for "first mover" closes, but the risk of being crushed is immediate.

Evidence: The proposal's own facts are the evidence: higher cost, no manufacturing record, copyable IP, unproven premium demand. No external data contradicts these internal red flags.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: The proposal admits higher cost and no brand/distribution, while incumbents can copy. There is no fact here showing why this specific company wins.
2. Copy risk: The core differentiator is vulnerable to immediate replication by entities with superior resources.

Risks: Execution failure, market rejection of premium pricing, supply chain collapse.

The plan fails as proposed because the stated facts confirm the venture lacks a defensible position against the very threats that kill hardware startups.

DECISION: NO-GO
