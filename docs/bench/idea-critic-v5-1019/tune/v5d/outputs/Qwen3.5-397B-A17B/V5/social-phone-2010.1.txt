First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high cost and no apps offer no advantage over existing rival smartphones with large catalogs.

Steelman: Leveraging a popular messaging brand to lock in teens via a carrier-exclusive, data-centric device could create a closed-loop social ecosystem if network effects outweigh app scarcity.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or porting the messaging strength to existing platforms beats building a new OS with zero apps. The cheaper alternative (software on existing hardware) reaches the same upside (social connection) without the hardware risk.
2.  **Fit:** Teens prioritize social connectivity and cost. A mandatory expensive data plan contradicts the demographic's price sensitivity. A new OS fragments the company's own mobile platform strategy.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The project fails because teens reject a phone with no apps at a premium price, leaving the company with sunk hardware costs and a fragmented software base.
4.  **Evidence:** Rivals have large app catalogs; the proposed data plan costs as much as a full smartphone plan. The proposal admits these negatives as facts.

Blockers:
-   **No Edge:** The plan is strictly worse than rivals on price (high data cost) and utility (no apps), with no compensating fact provided.
-   **Contradictory Strategy:** Building a new OS ignores the company's existing mobile platform, increasing execution risk without stated benefit.
-   **Demographic Mismatch:** High mandatory costs for a price-sensitive teen segment ensures low adoption.

Risks:
-   Sunk cost fallacy driving the decision (2 years development).
-   Carrier exclusivity limiting reach.

The proposal presents a product that is more expensive and less functional than competitors, targeting a demographic unable to afford the premium. The "sunk cost" of development is not an edge; it is a trap. Without an app store or a price advantage, the "social" angle cannot survive against established smartphones.

DECISION: NO-GO
