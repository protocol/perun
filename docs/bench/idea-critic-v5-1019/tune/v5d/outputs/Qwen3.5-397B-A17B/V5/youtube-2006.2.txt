First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; buying a smaller, unprofitable entity with massive liabilities at a premium price contradicts the buyer's existing, safer trajectory.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the market leader instantly secures dominance in a nascent category, betting that user growth and ad innovation will outpace legal and infrastructure costs.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financials:** $1.65 billion for zero meaningful revenue and high burn is speculative. The cost to build or buy later may be lower once the ad model is proven.
2.  **Liability:** The "large volume" of copyrighted material invites existential litigation. Media companies are already threatening suits; inheriting this poison pill could bankrupt the combined entity or force a shutdown of core features.
3.  **Integration:** The buyer already runs a video service. Merging cultures and tech stacks while fighting lawsuits diverts focus from the core search business.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows the market to settle. If the video site survives the lawsuits, its valuation may drop, or the buyer can license content legally without inheriting the infringement baggage.

**Pre-mortem:** In 24 months, courts rule against the platform on copyright grounds. Fines exceed cash reserves, forcing a fire sale or closure. The $1.65 billion stock is worthless, and the buyer's reputation is tarnished by association with piracy.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself admits "no meaningful revenue," "high bandwidth bills," and "threatened lawsuits." These are not risks; they are current, verified facts that invalidate the investment thesis as a sound financial decision today. The "edge" is purely hypothetical market share, which is useless if the platform is legally enjoined.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** No revenue vs. $1.65B price.
*   **Fact:** Active legal threats regarding core content library.
*   **Fact:** High operational burn with no clear path to profitability stated.

**Risks:** Ad model failure, bandwidth costs scaling faster than revenue, culture clash.

**Kill Criteria:** If copyright litigation results in an injunction preventing uploads within 12 months, the acquisition value drops to zero.

The absence of a revenue model and the presence of existential legal threats constitute a blocker. This is not a calculated risk; it is a gamble on a legal outcome that currently favors the plaintiffs.

DECISION: NO-GO
