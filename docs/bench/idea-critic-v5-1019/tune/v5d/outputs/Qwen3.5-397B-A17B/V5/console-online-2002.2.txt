First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it adds cost and friction (broadband requirement, mandatory subscription) to a market where rivals offer free or publisher-managed online play, without explaining why users would pay for this specific bundle over the free alternatives.

Steelman: A unified, high-quality online ecosystem with voice chat and a shared identity creates a superior user experience that justifies a subscription fee, differentiating the console from fragmented, free competitor offerings.

However, the attack reveals critical blockers based on the stated facts:
1.  **Market Mismatch:** The proposal admits "few homes have broadband." Making broadband a hard requirement immediately excludes the vast majority of the potential install base, capping growth before launch.
2.  **Pricing Friction:** Players "expect online play to be free." Introducing a mandatory annual fee on top of the console price creates a double barrier to entry that competitors do not impose. Without a proven willingness to pay, this risks alienating the core audience.
3.  **Operational Risk:** The company has "never run" hosting and support operations of this scale. Building this from scratch while simultaneously launching a hardware platform introduces massive execution risk with no prior evidence of capability.

The "better way" would be to make the service optional or free initially to build the user base, or to partner with existing infrastructure providers rather than building operations from scratch. The current plan forces a high-cost, high-friction model onto an unproven market segment without a clear mechanism to overcome the stated adoption barriers.

Blockers: The combination of low broadband penetration and strong consumer expectation of free online play directly contradicts the mandatory paid, broadband-only model. The lack of internal operational experience compounds this into a fatal execution risk.
Risks: Low adoption rates, high churn, operational failure, brand damage from perceived greed.

DECISION: NO-GO
