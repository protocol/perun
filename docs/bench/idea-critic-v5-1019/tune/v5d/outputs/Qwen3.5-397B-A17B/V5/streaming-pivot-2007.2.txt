First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it admits inferior content, poor quality, limited distribution (broadband), and higher unit economics (licensing) while threatening the core profit engine without a defined defensive moat or immediate revenue uplift.

Steelman: Launching a free, limited streaming service now is a defensive necessity to retain subscribers and gather data before dominant tech giants lock up exclusive rights, even if it temporarily depresses margins.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Protects current cash flow but risks total obsolescence if competitors secure exclusive libraries first.
2. Paid tier only: Tests willingness to pay without cannibalizing DVD revenue, but slows user adoption and data gathering.
3. Proposed plan (Free add-on): Maximizes immediate adoption but burns cash on licensing for a subpar product while actively encouraging users to drop the profitable DVD tier.

Fit: Assuming the north star is long-term survival in digital distribution, the timing is plausible, but the execution (free, low-quality catalog) misaligns with the constraint of high licensing costs.

Pre-mortem: The service fails because licensing costs bleed cash faster than DVD churn saves it; subscribers prefer the higher quality of future entrants, leaving the company with neither the DVD monopoly nor a viable streaming position. This is a one-way door on capital allocation; delay costs market relevance but preserves cash for a stronger future entry.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the catalog is small/old, quality is poor, broadband is not universal, and rights are expensive. These are internal contradictions to a successful launch.

Blockers:
1. Negative unit economics: High licensing costs for a free product with no incremental revenue directly reduce profitability without a proven retention lift.
2. Strategic self-sabotage: Encouraging migration from a high-margin physical model to a loss-leading digital model with inferior product-market fit creates value destruction before scale is achieved.

Risks: Cannibalization of the core business, inability to compete with deep-pocketed tech entrants, technology infrastructure strain.

The plan commits capital to a weak product that undermines the primary revenue source. A "go-if" structure is required: launch only as a paid beta or restrict to specific titles to validate demand before exposing the entire subscriber base to a inferior experience. As proposed, it is financially reckless.

DECISION: NO-GO
