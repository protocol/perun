First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; renaming and gifting do not address the verified lack of demand shown in the four-city test.

**Steelman:** Rebranding and targeted sampling will uncover latent utility in office settings that the initial consumer-focused test missed, validating a national rollout.

**Attack:**
1.  **Evidence Contradiction:** The core claim relies on "most recipients" saying they would buy. Social desirability bias makes verbal intent from free-sample recipients a vacuous metric; it does not predict actual purchase behavior. The previous test already proved low demand; this method measures politeness, not market fit.
2.  **Better Way Ignored:** The proposal skips diagnosing *why* the first test failed. Was it price, distribution, or product utility? Giving away more units without fixing the value proposition burns capital on a known failure mode. "Do nothing" while investigating the specific failure reason is superior to blind re-launch.
3.  **Risk:** A national rollout based on flawed qualitative feedback from a single city risks significant losses in marketing and inventory, repeating the initial failure at scale.
4.  **Missing Edge:** There is no fact presented showing why office workers specifically need this now, nor why a name change alters the physical utility of the adhesive.

**Blockers:**
*   **Metric Failure:** The success criterion ("say they would buy") is uncorrelated with revenue. The plan fails as proposed because the measurement cannot validate the business case.
*   **Contradictory Facts:** The proposal admits the first test showed "little demand" yet assumes a name change and free distribution will reverse this without evidence of a corresponding change in user need.

**Risks:** High cost of samples with zero conversion guarantee; scaling a product with unproven utility.

**Kill Criteria:** If the pilot does not generate a pre-commitment or actual purchase order (not just verbal intent) from >20% of sampled offices within 30 days, stop.

**Decision:** The plan relies on a broken metric and ignores prior negative evidence. It commits resources to a hypothesis that has already been weakened by data, without a mechanism to verify real demand.

DECISION: NO-GO
