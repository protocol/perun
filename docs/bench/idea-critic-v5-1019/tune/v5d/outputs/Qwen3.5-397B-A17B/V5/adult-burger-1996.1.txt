First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (adult growth) but offers no stated edge; it relies on spending to overcome a brand identity explicitly defined as "child-centered" and "value," while ignoring raised risks that the core customer base and target adults both reject the premise.

Steelman: A massive ad spend can temporarily override brand perception, forcing trial of a premium burger among adults before competitors react.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** High price, massive ad spend. Cost: Extreme. Risk: Brand alienation, wasted capital if perception holds. Reversibility: Low (spend is sunk, brand damage sticky).
2. **Do Nothing:** Maintain status quo. Cost: Zero. Risk: Stagnation in adult segment. Reversibility: N/A.
3. **Targeted Pilot:** Launch premium item in select adult-heavy markets with modest local spend. Cost: Low. Risk: Limited data. Reversibility: High.

Fit: Contradicts the "child-centered" north star without a mechanism to bridge the gap.

Pre-mortem: The campaign launches, adults perceive the high price as inconsistent with the "kids' place" brand, regulars feel abandoned, and the massive budget burns with no retention. This is a one-way door for capital; brand reputation damage is hard to reverse.

Evidence: The proposal admits the image is "child-centered" and risks note adults see the chain as "fast, not premium." No evidence suggests ad spend alone flips this deeply held perception. The math fails: High price deters regulars; brand deters adults.

Blockers:
1. **Contradictory Premise:** The plan relies on adults accepting a "premium" price from a brand they explicitly view as "fast, not premium," with no proof ad spend changes this.
2. **Lack of Edge:** Spending more than the industry is not a strategic advantage; it is a bet against verified consumer behavior (value/kids focus) without a product or positioning hook to justify the shift.
3. **Alternative Superiority:** A targeted pilot achieves the learning goal without the catastrophic risk of an industry-record budget on an unproven hypothesis.

Risks: Brand dilution, loss of core value customers, massive financial loss.

The proposal fails the blocker test. It demands irreversible, record-breaking spend to solve a positioning problem that money alone likely cannot fix, ignoring cheaper, safer validation steps.

DECISION: NO-GO
