First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "need" for a phone is not an advantage over existing flagships that already offer full app ecosystems and lower prices.

**Steelman:** A niche device leveraging unique 3D navigation and instant commerce identification could create a high-margin vertical integration play for loyal customers, bypassing app store dependencies.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Edge:** The price matches flagships, but the value is strictly inferior due to the missing mainstream apps. In a two-platform duopoly, app availability is the primary purchase driver, not a novelty 3D screen or a single shopping feature.
2.  **Carrier Constraint:** Exclusivity with one US carrier caps the addressable market immediately, while the unsubsidized price point alienates the carrier's own budget-conscious base.
3.  **Differentiation Failure:** "3D-like display" and "head tracking" are unproven gimmicks in 2014 with no established user demand, whereas the "app gap" is a known, fatal friction point for mainstream adoption.
4.  **Saturation:** Entering a saturated market requires a clear superiority or cost advantage. This proposal offers neither; it offers a more expensive, less compatible alternative to established leaders.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Preserve capital and brand equity; continue selling tablets/e-readers where the company has proven success.
*   *Cheaper Path:* Launch the commerce identification software as an app on existing dominant platforms, capturing the utility value without the hardware risk.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to poor reviews citing the lack of apps. Early adopters return it due to daily usability issues. The carrier, seeing low volume, refuses to renew the exclusivity deal. The project is written off as a costly distraction.

**Blockers:**
*   The core claim (success via differentiation) contradicts the stated fact that the market is dominated by two platforms where app ecosystem completeness is the standard.
*   No evidence suggests users will pay a flagship price for a device with a smaller app store.

**Risks:** High inventory write-downs, brand dilution, carrier relationship damage.

**Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders do not exceed 50,000 units in the first month, halt production immediately.

DECISION: NO-GO
