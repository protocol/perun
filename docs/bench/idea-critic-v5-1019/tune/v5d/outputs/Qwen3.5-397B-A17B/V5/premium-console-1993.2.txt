First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price and fragmented manufacturing are disadvantages against incumbents selling hardware at a loss to dominate software royalties.

Steelman: Licensing accelerates market penetration by leveraging partners' capital and distribution, creating a unified standard without heavy CAPEX.

Attack:
1. **Economics:** Incumbents subsidize hardware to lock in software sales. This model forces licensees to profit on hardware immediately, resulting in a $700 price point. Consumers will not pay a 2-3x premium for an unproven ecosystem when cheaper, established alternatives exist.
2. **Fragmentation:** Multiple manufacturers create compatibility risks, dilute marketing focus, and invite "race to the bottom" quality issues, undermining the "high-end" brand promise.
3. **Incentive Misalignment:** Licensees have little incentive to aggressively market a platform where their margin is capped by royalty fees, especially when they could build their own proprietary systems.
4. **Competitive Reality:** Strong incumbents control the best developers. Without a massive installed base (hard to achieve at $700), developers will not prioritize this new standard, creating a death spiral of low software availability.

Pre-mortem: The console launches late and overpriced. Licensees fail to move units due to cost. Developers ignore the platform due to low install base. The start-up earns negligible royalties and runs out of cash before establishing a foothold.

Blockers:
- **Price Disadvantage:** $700 is prohibitive compared to competitors; no stated value proposition justifies this premium to early adopters over existing high-end PCs or consoles.
- **Channel Conflict:** Licensees needing hardware profit cannot compete with incumbents selling at a loss.

Risks: Developer adoption, brand dilution, execution complexity across partners.

The proposal fails the basic unit economics of the console business model (loss leader hardware, profitable software) and offers no mechanism to overcome the price barrier or coordinate licensees effectively.

DECISION: NO-GO
