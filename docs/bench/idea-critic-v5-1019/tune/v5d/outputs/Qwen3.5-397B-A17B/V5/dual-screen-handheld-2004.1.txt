First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge where need alone exists; broader audience and lower price are goals, not proof this specific dual-screen architecture beats the rival's superior graphics on cost or adoption.

Steelman: Differentiate via novel interaction (touch/dual-screen) to unlock non-core demographics at a lower price point, leveraging legacy hardware to mitigate total market risk.

**Skeptic**: The claim that "unusual hardware may confuse developers" is a verified blocker if the software ecosystem fails. The proposal admits touch-screen games are "unproven." Without a committed library of killer apps demonstrating the dual-screen utility, the hardware is a solution seeking a problem. The "lower price" claim conflicts with "two screens add cost"; unless the screens are significantly cheaper than the rival's high-end graphics pipeline, the margin or price advantage is mathematically unsupported by the text.

**Better-way**: "Do nothing" and compete on the existing library while the rival burns cash on unproven media features. Alternatively, license the rival's stronger graphics tech rather than fragmenting the developer base with a unique, unproven form factor. The proposal fails to demonstrate why a costly hardware pivot is superior to software-led differentiation on established hardware.

**Kill-criteria**: If the cost of two screens exceeds the rival's BOM minus their graphics premium, the price advantage vanishes. If no major developer commits to a touch-first title before launch, the "broadening audience" claim fails.

**Pre-mortem**: The device launches as a "gimmick" because third-party developers treat the second screen as an afterthought, resulting in a library of ports that look worse than the rival's. The core audience rejects it for weak graphics; the new audience ignores it for lack of compelling software.

**Blockers**: 
1. Contradictory economics: "Two screens add cost" vs. "lower price" without explaining the cost-saving mechanism elsewhere.
2. Unproven demand: Admission that touch-screen games are unproven with no mitigation plan.

**Risks**: Developer confusion, gimmick perception, margin compression.

The proposal relies on assumptions that contradict its own risk assessment. The economic model is broken as stated.

DECISION: NO-GO
