First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; acquiring a loss-making asset with leadership voids and active community hostility, while free alternatives exist, offers no immediate advantage over the status quo.

Steelman: The buyer neutralizes the dominant platform to prevent competitor capture, betting that operational independence and capital injection will stabilize leadership and retain users despite cultural friction.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids $7.5B outlay and integration risk; allows free competitors to potentially erode the target's share, weakening the buyer's ecosystem indirectly.
2. Build/Buy smaller: Lower cost, but fails to secure the dominant network effect immediately.
3. Acquisition (Proposed): Highest cost, highest risk of user defection and regulatory delay; only wins if the network effect is truly unassailable and cultural distrust is temporary.

Fit: Assumes the strategic north star is controlling the developer pipeline. Without a named mechanism to convert distrust into trust, this conflicts with the goal of ecosystem growth.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition closes, but key maintainers migrate to free forks citing "embrace, extend, extinguish" fears. The target remains leaderless internally due to culture clash. The $7.5B becomes a sunk cost with declining engagement. This is a one-way door; reversing it requires selling a damaged asset. Cost of delay: Minimal, as the target is currently rudderless and not being acquired by a direct rival today.

Evidence: The proposal admits the target is loss-making, leaderless, and distrusted. No data proves users will stay post-acquisition. The "independent" operation claim is an assumption contradicted by the buyer's historical hostility.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: The proposal admits all negatives (losses, no CEO, distrust, free alternatives) and offers no specific fact showing why this deal beats doing nothing today. Need without an edge is a NO-GO.
2. Contradictory premises: Relying on "independence" to solve "historical hostility" without a verified retention mechanism is a fatal gap.

Risks: Developer defection, regulatory block, permanent brand damage.

Kill criteria: None proposed; cannot approve a bet without a stop condition.

DECISION: NO-GO
