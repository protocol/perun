First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a market gap but fails to state a defensible edge over existing competitors or prove that the timing aligns with economic reality; need alone is not strategy.

Steelman: Launching a distinct mid-priced division with dedicated dealers captures the growing segment before competitors saturate it, leveraging extensive consumer research to ensure product-market fit.

Attack: The plan relies on a "growing" segment that explicitly faces recession risk and potential shrinkage. Introducing a capital-intensive new brand and dealer network immediately before a potential economic downturn creates massive fixed costs without guaranteed volume. The "extensive research" may reflect past conditions, not the 1957 reality. Furthermore, adding a new division risks cannibalizing the firm's existing low and premium lines without a clear mechanism to prevent overlap. The cost of building a new dealer network is a high-fixed-cost burden that reduces flexibility if the segment contracts.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital, avoid dealer friction, and wait for recession clarity.
2. Badge engineering: Use existing dealers and platforms to test the mid-price point with lower capital exposure.
3. Delay: Postpone launch until post-recession demand is verified.

Fit: Assumes the goal is market share growth, but ignores the strategic necessity of capital preservation during instability.

Pre-mortem: The launch coincides with a recession; sales plummet, dealers go bankrupt, and the brand becomes a costly liability that drags down the parent company. This is a one-way door with high irreversible costs.

Evidence: The proposal admits recession risk and segment shrinkage possibility yet proceeds with maximum infrastructure spend. No evidence proves the "perceived gap" persists in a downturn.

Blockers:
1. Economic Timing: Proceeding with high fixed-cost expansion (new dealers, new brand) when the target segment faces recession risk and potential shrinkage contradicts basic capital allocation logic.
2. Lack of Edge: No stated advantage explains why this specific brand will win against established mid-priced leaders in a shrinking market.

Risks: Cannibalization of existing brands, dealer network failure, brand dilution.

The plan commits to irreversible infrastructure spend based on optimistic growth assumptions while acknowledging credible contraction risks. Without a staged entry or recession-proofing, the downside outweighs the upside.

DECISION: NO-GO
