First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: it admits no large paying market exists, no committed customers are lined up, and it raises unit costs for a company dependent on the gaming race. Need alone is not an edge; without a buyer or a cost advantage over CPUs, this is speculation, not a plan.

Steelman: Embedding general-purpose compute in every consumer chip creates a massive installed base that unlocks a future scientific market before rivals can react, turning a commodity into a platform.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve margins and focus on the core gaming rivalry; wait for clear demand signals.
2. Targeted acceleration: Build programmable features only for high-margin workstation cards where scientific users already pay, avoiding mass-market cost penalties.
3. Software partnerships: Fund university research using existing hacked APIs without altering silicon, testing demand before committing to toolchain development.

Fit: This diverts engineering resources from the primary north star of winning the gaming graphics war against the main rival. It assumes a future market materializes fast enough to justify present distraction.

Pre-mortem: The program consumes capital and silicon area, raising prices and reducing gaming performance slightly. Competitors undercut on price/performance for gamers. The scientific toolchain remains unfinished or unused because the academic niche cannot support the ecosystem. The company loses the gaming war due to distraction, and the new market never arrives. This is a one-way door with high delay cost: waiting preserves cash and focus.

Evidence: The proposal itself states "no large paying market," "no committed customers," and "raising unit cost." These are internal contradictions to a go decision. The risks (cost, distraction, niche demand) are confirmed facts, not hypotheticals.

Blockers:
1. No revenue path: The core claim relies on a non-existent market with no committed buyers.
2. Strategic dilution: Engineering distraction threatens the sole existing revenue stream (gaming) against a known rival.
3. Unit economics: Raising costs on every unit without immediate pricing power or demand destroys margin.

Risks: Execution failure, slow adoption, competitor response.

The absence of a pilot, cap, or metric is noted, but the blockers are fatal as proposed. A sound operator cannot approve a multi-year capital intensive program that actively harms the current core business based solely on hope.

DECISION: NO-GO
