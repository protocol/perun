First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge where data proves this specific director-star combination will retain subscribers better than cheaper licensed content; "we have data" is not an edge without the specific finding.

Steelman: The service's unique granular data identifies a specific, underserved audience segment that guarantees this high-cost production will drive net-new subscriptions and reduce churn more effectively than incremental licensing, justifying the skip of a pilot and the two-season commitment.

Attack:
1. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or license a third-party original preserves capital. A single-season order or a traditional pilot tests the "online-only" and "no-pilot" hypotheses at 10% of the cost. The proposal fails to explain why these cheaper paths cannot capture the same upside.
2. **Fit:** Committing a "large share" of the budget to one unproven format (binge-release, no pilot) creates a single point of failure. If the data interpretation is wrong, the service faces insolvency, not just a missed target.
3. **Evidence:** The proposal cites "viewing data" but offers no recomputed ROI, payback period, or specific metric linking past viewing of licensed content to the willingness to pay for *this* specific original genre. Data on what people watched does not prove they will subscribe for a new, expensive drama.
4. **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. The cause is likely that the data reflected availability bias (people watched what existed) rather than demand for this specific high-cost product. The "all-at-once" release may spike churn if the content is consumed too quickly without weekly engagement.
5. **Blockers:** The absence of a pilot or a smaller initial commitment is a structural blocker when the cost is a "large share" of the budget and the format (online prestige drama) is unproven. The proposal asserts the data removes the need for de-risking but provides no evidence that the data is predictive rather than descriptive.

Blockers: No evidence provided that data predicts success for this specific unproven format; cost concentration creates existential risk without a staged entry.
Risks: Audience rejection, budget exhaustion, inability to recover costs without a multi-year subscriber lift.

DECISION: NO-GO
