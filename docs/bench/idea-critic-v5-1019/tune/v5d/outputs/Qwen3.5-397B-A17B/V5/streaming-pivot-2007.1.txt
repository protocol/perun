First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it admits inferior content, poor quality, limited distribution, and high costs while threatening a profitable core business with no immediate revenue uplift.

**Steelman:** Launching a free, limited streaming service in 2007 is a defensive maneuver to retain subscribers by establishing a digital footprint before dominant tech giants lock up exclusive rights, using the existing cash cow to fund the transition.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves high margins while competitors validate the market. A "paid tier" tests willingness to pay without cannibalizing DVD revenue or inflating licensing costs for non-paying users. The proposed "free add-on" burns capital on expensive licenses for a product admitted to be inferior, accelerating cannibalization without a retention guarantee.
2.  **Fit:** The strategy conflicts with the current north star of profitability. Entering a low-quality, high-cost segment against well-funded entrants (who have better tech and leverage) is a direct loss scenario.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The initiative fails because licensing costs drain DVD profits, the poor user experience drives churn rather than preventing it, and the company becomes a "bridge" supplier before being crushed by native digital natives.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal's own facts confirm negative unit economics (high license fees, low value content) and strategic vulnerability. There is no evidence that a small, old catalogue prevents churn better than the current reliable DVD service.
5.  **Blockers:** The plan fails the basic financial test: it increases costs and risks revenue decline with no offsetting income or proven retention benefit. A cheaper, safer alternative (waiting or charging extra) exists.

**Blockers:**
*   Negative immediate ROI: High licensing costs + zero incremental revenue + potential cannibalization of profitable units.
*   Superior alternatives exist: Waiting for market validation or launching a paid pilot reduces risk and preserves capital.

**Risks:**
*   Being locked out of future content rights by competitors.
*   Brand dilution via poor video quality.

**Decision:**
The proposal commits capital to a losing unit economic model without a pilot or pricing mechanism to validate demand. It violates the principle of not attacking one's own profitable core without a clear, funded path to replacement.

DECISION: NO-GO
