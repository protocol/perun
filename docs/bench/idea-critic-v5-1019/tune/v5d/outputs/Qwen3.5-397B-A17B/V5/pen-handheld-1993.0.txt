First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "leading a new category" is an ambition, not a competitive advantage over existing notebooks or emerging competitors, especially given the admitted technical failures.

**Steelman:** A $700 pen-based device can define a new market if handwriting recognition works reliably enough to replace keyboards, justifying the premium through unique mobility and CEO-driven brand momentum.

**Attack:**
1.  **Technical Blocker:** The proposal admits handwriting recognition is "unreliable in testing." For a device where this is the *main* input, unreliability is a fatal functional defect, not a risk. It renders the core value proposition unusable.
2.  **Price vs. Value:** At $700 (a significant sum in 1993), the device competes with full laptops. Without proven software or reliable input, the price cannot be justified.
3.  **Form Factor:** Being "too big for a pocket" contradicts the "handheld" and mobility promise, removing the primary physical advantage over laptops.
4.  **Ecosystem:** "Little software" means early adopters have no reason to buy, creating a dead-on-arrival ecosystem.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for technology to mature (battery, recognition, miniaturization) before entering.
*   **Cheaper Path:** License the OS to existing hardware makers to test the market without bearing manufacturing risk.
*   **Pivot:** Focus on a vertical market (e.g., logistics) where specific utility outweighs general consumer flaws, rather than a mass-market "personal" device.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to heavy publicity, users immediately encounter unusable handwriting recognition, returns skyrocket, and the brand becomes synonymous with overhyped failure. The category is set back by years.

**Blockers:**
*   **Core Function Failure:** Unreliable handwriting recognition makes the primary input method broken.
*   **No Stated Edge:** High price and poor form factor offer no advantage over existing alternatives.

**Risks:** Unproven category, lack of software, size issues.

The plan fails the basic operator test: you cannot sell a broken primary feature at a premium price in an unproven category. The technical deficit is a hard blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
