First call: NO-GO. Falling sales and a dominant rival ecosystem indicate hardware ownership cannot solve the core app-deficit problem.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the handset maker secures the primary hardware channel, preventing an immediate switch to the rival platform and allowing tight integration to improve user experience.

**Attack:** The proposal admits smartphone sales are falling and the rival holds the developer mindshare. Buying a shrinking asset at €5.4B burns cash needed for ecosystem incentives. Integration risks (workforce, culture) will distract leadership while the market leader accelerates. Other handset makers may flee the platform, fearing competition from the owner, fragmenting the very base this move aims to protect. Hardware control does not magically attract developers; apps follow users, and users follow apps. If the device continues to lose market share post-acquisition, the company owns a costly, depreciating liability with no off-ramp.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid €5.4B loss; accept the manufacturer might switch eventually, but preserve capital to court other partners or build software services.
2.  **Licensing/Partnership:** Pay the manufacturer to prioritize the OS without the burden of ownership and integration.
3.  **Ecosystem Fund:** Invest the €5.4B directly in developer bounties and user subsidies to close the app gap, which is the actual bottleneck.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years later, the acquired division posts massive losses, other manufacturers abandon the OS to avoid competing with the owner, and the app gap widens because the installed base shrank faster than integration benefits could materialize. The company is forced to write off the acquisition and exit the hardware market entirely.

**Blockers:**
*   **Falling Sales:** Buying a declining asset contradicts the goal of growth; the premise that ownership stops the switch is unproven and likely false if the device is uncompetitive.
*   **Ecosystem Mismatch:** The core problem is apps (software/network effect), not hardware control. Capital is better spent solving the root cause.
*   **Partner Alienation:** Vertical integration threatens horizontal partners, risking total platform fragmentation.

**Risks:** Integration failure, cultural clash, continued developer apathy.

**Decision:** The plan addresses a symptom (potential defection) by acquiring a liability, ignoring the disease (lack of apps) and creating new enemies (other handset makers).

DECISION: NO-GO
