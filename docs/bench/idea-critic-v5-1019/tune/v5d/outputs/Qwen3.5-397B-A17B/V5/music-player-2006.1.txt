First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; being late with similar features, higher costs, and abandoned partner standards offers no advantage over the incumbent.

**Steelman:** A superior hardware interface and integrated ecosystem can displace a dominant player if the user experience gap is wide enough to overcome switching costs, even without price advantages.

**Attack:**
1.  **Options:** "Do nothing" avoids burning capital on a low-probability bet. A "cheaper alternative" would be licensing the existing dominant store rather than building a competing one. Building a parallel store and device ecosystem from scratch is the most expensive, highest-risk path.
2.  **Fit:** Entering a mature market late requires a breakthrough, not parity. Dropping the industry copy-protection standard alienates current partners (labels, device makers) whose content is essential. If labels restrict the proposed "wireless sharing," the key differentiator vanishes.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The device fails because the store lacks content due to label friction over the new DRM-free approach. The marketing budget burns out before achieving critical mass against the entrenched network effect. The "large screen" and "desktop client" are easily copied by the leader.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits no price advantage, lateness to market, and potential regulatory/content restrictions. These are not risks; they are stated facts that negate the value proposition. A "large marketing budget" is an input, not a strategic edge.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits to being late, lacking price advantage, and offering features (screen, sharing) that are either easily replicated or dependent on third-party permission (labels). Need alone is not an edge.
*   **Self-Sabotage:** Abandoning the promoted copy-protection standard destroys relationships with the very partners needed to stock the store, creating a content gap the competitor does not have.

**Risks:** Label restriction on sharing, entrenched loyalty, high burn rate.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It proposes entering a winner-take-all market late, without a unique advantage, while actively antagonizing supply partners. The absence of a pilot, cap, or metric is secondary to the fundamental strategic void.

DECISION: NO-GO
