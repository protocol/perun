First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real friction but offers no stated edge proving $79 covers the marginal cost of unlimited two-day shipping for heavy buyers; need alone is not an economic advantage.

Steelman: An annual fee prepaid by customers creates liquidity and locks in loyalty, offsetting shipping losses through increased order frequency and lifetime value.

Attack: The core claim fails the blocker test on stated facts. The proposal explicitly admits "economics are unproven" and that "shipping costs may exceed fee revenue." Without a verified unit economic model showing the $79 fee exceeds the expected shipping cost of the average member's annual volume, the plan guarantees a margin hit for every heavy user. The risk of costs exceeding revenue is not a hypothetical "risk"; it is a stated probability that invalidates the "go" decision until proven otherwise. A cheaper alternative exists: a tiered shipping discount or a minimum-order threshold for free shipping, which caps liability while testing price sensitivity. The "hard to reverse" nature of sold memberships creates a one-way door with unquantified downside.

Blockers:
1. Unproven economics: The proposal states costs may exceed revenue with no data to contradict this.
2. Margin hit: Explicitly acknowledged as "large" and "short-term" with no defined cap or funding source.

Risks:
1. Heavy buyer concentration skewing average costs.
2. Inability to reverse the offer without reputational damage.
3. Competitor response eroding the uniqueness of the offer.

The absence of a pilot, cap, or metric is not the blocker; the blocker is the explicit admission that the fundamental math (revenue vs. cost) is unknown and likely negative. A sound operator does not approve a plan where the primary variable (shipping cost) is admitted to potentially exceed the fixed revenue source ($79) without a prior test. The proposal must first run a limited pilot or present a modeled break-even analysis based on current order data before approval. As proposed, it is a bet on an unverified hypothesis with known negative downside.

DECISION: NO-GO
