First call: NO-GO. A hardware launch without software, developer alignment, or broad retail distribution, priced significantly above a direct competitor arriving shortly after, lacks a stated edge to overcome these deficits. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Launching early in May secures shelf space and mindshare before the rival's September arrival, betting that being first outweighs being incomplete.

Attack: The proposal admits fatal gaps. Shipping with "few games ready" ignores that console adoption is software-driven; early buyers will face an empty library, generating negative word-of-mouth that poisons the brand before the competitor even arrives. The $399 price point ($100 above rival) is unjustifiable given the inferior software lineup and creates an immediate losing comparison. Excluding major retailers to manage limited supply guarantees channel retaliation, ensuring poor placement when stock finally arrives. Developers, unprepared for the date, cannot fill the software gap quickly.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing until September: Match the competitor with a full lineup and full retail support.
2. Delay to Q4: Avoid the head-to-head clash entirely and launch with holiday marketing.
3. Current Plan: Guarantees alienated partners, angry consumers, and a "failed launch" narrative.

Pre-mortem: By Christmas 1995, the system is labeled a "dud" due to lack of games and high price. Retailers refuse to dedicate prime floor space because they were snubbed in May. The competitor launches in September with a robust library at $299, capturing the entire market. The cost of delay (waiting until September) is negligible compared to the cost of a botched early entry that destroys brand equity.

Blockers:
1. No software ecosystem: "Few games ready" ensures consumer rejection.
2. Channel hostility: Skipping retailers creates a distribution blockade.
3. Price disadvantage: $100 premium with less value is unsellable.
4. Developer misalignment: Unprepared studios cannot fix the software drought in time.

Risks: Brand damage, inventory stagnation, loss of retailer trust.

The plan fails on its own stated facts. It offers no advantage that survives the reality of the software-driven console market.

DECISION: NO-GO
