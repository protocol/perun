First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a clear market need (feature film) and a unique angle (toy's perspective), but it fails to state a definitive edge that guarantees this specific plan beats existing hand-drawn dominance or mitigates the catastrophic execution risks with current technology.

**Steelman:** Partnering provides the only viable path to distribution and funding for an unproven medium, leveraging the studio's hunger for the lead animator to secure favorable terms despite the company's lack of feature experience.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capability Gap:** The company has zero track record beyond shorts. Scaling to 90 minutes in 1991 implies an exponential, untested increase in render time and storage costs that could bankrupt the project before completion.
2.  **Power Imbalance:** The studio holds distribution and the stronger hand. Without a pilot proving the medium works at scale, the company risks becoming a cheap labor vendor rather than a creative partner, potentially losing IP control if the film stalls.
3.  **Technological Prematurity:** "No one has done it" is a risk, not an edge. In 1991, rendering a single frame could take hours. A feature requires hundreds of thousands of frames. The cost basis is unknown and likely underestimated.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Continue profitable shorts/commercials; wait for rendering hardware to mature.
*   **Pilot First:** Produce a 15-minute special to validate workflow and costs before committing to a feature.
*   **Co-Production:** Seek multiple partners to dilute the single studio's leverage.

**Pre-mortem:** The project runs three years over budget; rendering farms cannot keep up; the studio invokes clauses to take over production, firing the original team. The film releases to mixed reviews due to stiff animation, bankrupting the small company.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Unit Economics:** No data exists to confirm the cost-per-minute of CG animation is viable for a feature in 1991.
*   **Lack of Leverage:** Accepting the deal as proposed cedes control without a proof-of-concept to validate the company's ability to deliver.

**Risks:** Technology failure, partner exploitation, cash flow collapse.

**Kill Criteria:** If a 5-minute test sequence cannot be rendered within 6 months under $500k, terminate the feature effort.

The absence of a pilot or cost verification constitutes a blocker for a first-time feature in an unproven medium.

DECISION: NO-GO
