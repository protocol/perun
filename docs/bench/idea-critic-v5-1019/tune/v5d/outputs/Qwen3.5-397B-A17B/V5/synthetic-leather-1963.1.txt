First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it admits higher cost, potential comfort deficits, and fashion volatility without identifying a compensating advantage over leather.

Steelman: The material's durability offers a unique value proposition for specific high-wear applications, justifying a premium price if marketed as a technical performance upgrade rather than a fashion substitute.

Attack:
1. **Economics:** The synthetic costs more than leather. Without a functional advantage (e.g., waterproofing, weight reduction) explicitly valued by manufacturers, price parity is impossible. The proposal relies on "heavy marketing" to overcome a cost disadvantage, which is inefficient against entrenched supply chains.
2. **Adoption Friction:** Shoe makers are unwilling to retool factories. Introducing a new material requires capital expenditure from manufacturers. They will not incur this cost for a material that is more expensive and potentially less comfortable, absent a guaranteed consumer demand surge which fashion trends make uncertain.
3. **Comfort & Fashion:** If wearers find it less comfortable, returns will destroy brand equity. If fashion shifts, the "durable" nature of the material becomes a liability (unsold inventory), whereas leather retains baseline value.

Alternatives:
- **Do Nothing:** Continue using leather; zero retooling cost, proven comfort, stable pricing.
- **Niche Entry:** Target industrial or military footwear where durability outweighs comfort and fashion, avoiding direct consumer fashion risk.
- **Blending:** Use as a minor component to test durability claims without requiring full factory conversion.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, manufacturers resist retooling due to cost, early adopters return shoes due to discomfort, and the company is left with high inventory costs as trends shift. This is a one-way door with high sunk costs in marketing and inventory.

Blockers:
- **Cost Disadvantage:** Higher unit cost than the incumbent (leather) with no stated functional superiorities to justify the premium.
- **Channel Resistance:** Explicitly stated unwillingness of manufacturers to change factories.
- **Product-Market Fit Risk:** Potential comfort issues and fashion volatility directly threaten the core value proposition.

Risks: Fashion cycles, consumer rejection, inventory obsolescence.

The proposal fails the basic economic test of offering a better or cheaper alternative. It asks partners to spend more for a potentially inferior product based on marketing spend alone.

DECISION: NO-GO
