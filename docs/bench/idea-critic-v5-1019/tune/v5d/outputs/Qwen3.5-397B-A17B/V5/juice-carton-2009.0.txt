First call: NO-GO. The proposal states the old image is the main shelf cue for a loyal, older base, yet offers no stated edge (cost, product, access, timing) proving the new design beats the current one; need for refresh alone is not an edge.

Steelman: A minimalist design with a cap-shaped orange and vertical name will modernize the brand and drive growth without confusing shoppers if supported by heavy advertising.

Attack: The claim relies on advertising to overcome the loss of the primary visual cue (the orange with a straw). For an older, loyal demographic, shelf recognition is instantaneous; removing the core cue creates a high probability of "search failure" where shoppers simply skip the product, assuming it is out of stock or a different brand. The proposal admits this risk but attempts to solve it with spend (advertising) rather than design continuity. A nationwide, all-at-once launch removes the ability to test this hypothesis before committing the full cost.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Zero cost, zero recognition risk.
2. Staged rollout: Test in one region to measure sales impact before national commitment.
3. Hybrid design: Retain the straw/orange cue while introducing the new cap/typography gradually.

The proposal fails because it treats a recognized structural risk (loss of shelf cue for older buyers) as a marketing problem solvable by ad spend. The "all stores at once" approach violates the principle of reversibility; if the cue fails, the brand loses nationwide shelf presence instantly with no fallback. The cheaper, safer alternative (staged rollout or hybrid design) reaches the same upside (modernization) with significantly lower risk of catastrophic sales drop.

Blockers: The stated fact that the old image is the "main shelf cue" combined with an "older" buyer base directly contradicts the feasibility of removing that cue in a single nationwide step without a proven transition mechanism. The plan offers no evidence that the new cap or vertical text functions as an equal cue.

Risks: Sales collapse due to non-recognition; wasted ad spend fighting confusion; alienation of core loyalists.

DECISION: NO-GO
