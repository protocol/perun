First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits irreversible capital before validating the core hypothesis that low-margin grocery delivery can achieve positive unit economics in a single market, let alone twelve.

Steelman: The company aims to secure an unassailable first-mover advantage by building infrastructure capacity ahead of demand, betting that speed to market outweighs the risk of operational inefficiency.

Attack: The plan fails the "door" and "evidence" tests. This is a one-way door decision (building billion-dollar warehouses) based on zero validated learning from the pilot started only weeks ago in June 1999. The proposal explicitly admits to entering new cities "before learning the unit economics." In a low-margin business, unit economics are the only metric that matters; without them, scaling only accelerates cash burn. The "cheaper alternative" is obvious: prove profitability in the first city with temporary or leased infrastructure before locking in billions in fixed costs. The stated risks (capital intensity, thin margins) are not just possibilities; they are mathematical certainties that this plan ignores by substituting capital for knowledge.

Blockers:
1. The plan mandates scaling to a dozen cities before validating unit economics in the first. This contradicts basic operational logic for low-margin ventures.
2. A $1 billion commitment is irreversible and disproportionate to the evidence (a few weeks of operations in one city).
3. No leading metric or kill criterion is defined to validate the model before expansion.

Risks:
Customer density may prove insufficient to fill slots; delivery windows may be too costly to honor; margins may never cover the fixed cost of automation. These are not mere risks but likely outcomes given the lack of data.

The proposal asks to bet the company on an unproven model. A sound operator would cap the experiment, define a clear path to positive unit economics in the first city, and only then consider expansion. As proposed, this is a recipe for total capital destruction.

DECISION: NO-GO
