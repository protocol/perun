First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it presents a desperate defensive reaction to a fear of displacement rather than a plan where the specific facts (price, lack of revenue, anti-ad stance) create a value advantage over current alternatives.

Steelman: Paying a historic premium is the only way to instantly secure hundreds of millions of mobile users and neutralize a competitor that threatens the core business, accepting that monetization must be invented later.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Risks slow erosion of the core user base by mobile-native competitors, but preserves $19B capital.
2. Build/Buy smaller: Develop internal messaging or acquire cheaper, smaller apps; lower cost, but slower scale and higher execution risk.
3. The Proposal: Immediate scale and threat neutralization; extreme financial risk, unproven unit economics, and high integration friction.

Fit: Aligns with a survival imperative if mobile displacement is existential, but contradicts standard capital efficiency principles given the target's refusal to monetize via ads (the buyer's likely core competency).

Pre-mortem: The acquisition fails because the target's culture rejects the buyer's monetization methods, growth stalls without the "cool" factor of independence, regulators block data integration, and the $19B price tag creates an impossible ROI hurdle, distracting leadership for a decade.

Evidence & Math: The price is ~12x the valuation from one year ago ($1.5B to $19B). With minimal revenue and a $1 fee, the payback period is effectively infinite under current operations. The cost per acquired user is roughly $40-$50 (assuming hundreds of millions), vastly exceeding industry norms for non-revenue generating users.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: The proposal admits the target has no monetization plan and refuses ads, yet offers no mechanism to justify the $19B cost other than "fear." A real need (mobile presence) without a stated economic or strategic edge to justify this specific price is a failure.
2. Contradictory fundamentals: Buying an asset explicitly designed to reject the buyer's primary revenue model (ads) at a record multiple creates a structural value destruction scenario unless a new, unproven model is guaranteed.

Risks: Founder retention, cultural clash, regulatory scrutiny, opportunity cost of $19B.

The absence of a plausible path to recoup the investment based on the stated facts constitutes a blocker. Fear is not a strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
