First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state an edge; "free web access" alone does not beat established players who already have users and data, especially given the stated browser performance limits and high licensing costs.

**Steelman:** A free, browser-based map with satellite imagery and routing will displace desktop tools and capture market share from incumbents by removing friction, despite technical constraints.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Edge:** The proposal admits established sites already have users. Without a stated differentiation beyond "free" (which incumbents can match or already offer via ad models) and "web-based" (which is currently slow and technically risky in 2004), there is no defensible wedge. Need is not strategy.
2.  **Evidence & Economics:** Licensing costs are flagged as high with no revenue model. In 2004, bandwidth and rendering costs for satellite imagery are prohibitive without a clear monetization path (ads, enterprise, data licensing). The math does not work on the stated facts.
3.  **Technical Blocker:** The proposal explicitly notes browsers of the time are "slow at interactive graphics." Building a heavy mapping tool on a platform acknowledged as incapable of smooth performance is a fundamental product flaw, not a risk to be managed later.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or licensing the tech for the existing desktop product avoids the massive burn of building a web engine from scratch while browsers mature.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Economic Engine:** High fixed costs (licensing) + zero revenue model = guaranteed cash burn with no exit strategy stated.
*   **Platform Incapacity:** The core value proposition (interactive maps) conflicts with the stated technical reality (browsers are too slow).
*   **No Competitive Edge:** Entering a crowded market with a slower, more expensive-to-host version of existing products.

**Risks:** (Irrelevant due to blockers, but noted) User acquisition costs, data freshness, scaling infrastructure.

**Decision:** The plan fails the basic operator test: it spends heavily on a technically constrained medium with no way to make money, against competitors who already win. It requires a complete rethink of the revenue model and a wait for browser technology to mature before a "GO" is possible.

DECISION: NO-GO
