First call: NO-GO. The stated price ($7.4B) against a one-film-per-year output creates an immediate valuation mismatch that no amount of creative synergy can justify without a guaranteed increase in production volume, which is not stated.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio is the only way to prevent a rival from securing its talent and to instantly revitalize the conglomerate's failing hand-drawn unit by installing proven creative leadership.

**Attack:**
1.  **Valuation Blocker:** At $7.4B for ~1 film/year, the implied value per asset is astronomically higher than industry norms unless the acquisition immediately unlocks a multi-film pipeline. The proposal offers no mechanism or commitment to increase output, making the multiple indefensible on current facts.
2.  **Key Person Risk:** The value is concentrated in a few creative leaders and a founder who will become a major shareholder in the buyer. This creates a hostile internal dynamic where the "acquired" party holds disproportionate leverage over the parent company, effectively inverting the power structure the purchase is meant to secure.
3.  **Culture & Integration:** Merging a high-output, tech-driven CG studio with a struggling hand-drawn unit invites severe culture clash. The proposal assumes leadership transfer solves this, but history shows such mergers often result in talent exodus when creative autonomy is threatened by corporate economics.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing/Re-negotiate:** Accept a narrower distribution deal or profit-sharing model that aligns cost with actual output (1 film/year) rather than buying the entire overhead.
*   **Minority Stake:** Purchase a non-controlling interest to secure rights without assuming full balance sheet risk or cultural integration debt.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, the founder uses their new shareholder status to block cost-cutting or production increases, the single annual film underperforms relative to the $7.4B price tag, and the stock price tanks. The hand-drawn unit remains broken because CG leadership refuses to manage it.

**Blockers:** The math does not work for a single annual asset at this price point; the governance structure (founder as major shareholder) creates an unmanageable conflict of interest.
**Risks:** Talent flight, culture clash, production bottlenecks.

DECISION: NO-GO
