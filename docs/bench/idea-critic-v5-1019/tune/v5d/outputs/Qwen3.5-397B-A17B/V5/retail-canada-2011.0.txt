First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; higher local prices and an entrenched competitor negate the "discount" advantage without proof of a cost structure that undercuts incumbents despite new cross-border logistics costs.

Steelman: Rapid scale (130–150 stores) creates immediate market density to overwhelm the incumbent before they react, leveraging existing lease availability to skip construction delays.

Attack:
1. **No Economic Edge:** Entering a market with higher goods prices while building a new, unproven supply chain implies higher operational costs. Without a verified mechanism to sell cheaper than the dominant rival, the core value proposition fails. "Loyal domestic following" does not transfer across borders automatically.
2. **Compound Risk:** Simultaneously launching a new inventory system, a new supply chain, and 150 stores violates the principle of isolating variables. If the inventory system fails (high probability for unproven tech), the entire physical network starves or overstocks immediately. There is no pilot to validate the bilingual labeling or logistics flow.
3. **Alternative:** A "beachhead" strategy (5–10 stores) would validate the supply chain and price perception with capped capital. The proposed "all-in" approach bets the company on unverified assumptions.
4. **Pre-mortem:** Two years in, the inventory system glitches cause stockouts in 40% of stores; the incumbent drops prices temporarily to squeeze margins; the retailer cannot sustain losses on 150 leases and exits, having burned cash on a supply chain that never achieved efficiency.

Blockers:
- **Missing Edge:** No evidence provided that the retailer can profitably undercut the incumbent given new logistics costs and higher local goods prices.
- **Unproven Core Dependency:** The new inventory-management system is critical yet unproven; scaling it to 150 stores simultaneously creates a single point of catastrophic failure.

Risks:
- Regulatory/labeling delays stalling openings while leases accrue cost.
- Brand irrelevance in a new cultural context despite domestic loyalty.

The plan commits massive fixed costs (leases) before validating the variable engine (supply chain/pricing). It bets on speed over learning in an environment where the competitor is larger and entrenched.

DECISION: NO-GO
