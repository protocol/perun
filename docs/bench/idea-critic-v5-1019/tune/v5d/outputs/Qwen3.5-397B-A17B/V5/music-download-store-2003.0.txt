First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it attempts to sell a priced good against a free, ubiquitous alternative while restricting usage to a single hardware ecosystem and facing supplier instability.

Steelman: A seamless, legal store with universal label support converts users willing to pay for reliability and integration, leveraging the existing hardware install base to lock in value despite higher costs than piracy.

**Blockers:**
1. **No Competitive Edge:** The core value proposition (99¢/song) competes directly with $0 file-sharing. The proposal admits "free file-sharing is everywhere" but offers no fact showing users will pay for light copy-restriction alone. Need without an edge is a failure.
2. **Supplier Risk:** The plan depends on licensing from "all major record labels," yet explicitly cites the risk that "labels may withdraw their licenses." Building a core product on a permission layer that suppliers are actively threatening to remove is a factual contradiction in the plan's stability.
3. **Limited Reach:** Restricting the store to "only on the company's own computers" caps the addressable market to existing owners, ignoring the broader PC market where file-sharing thrives.

**Risks:**
- Margins are thin after label payments, leaving little room for error.
- Rivals backing subscriptions may define the future market, making per-song sales obsolete.
- Copy protection may frustrate users without stopping determined pirates.

**Analysis:**
The proposal fails the "beat do nothing" test. Doing nothing preserves hardware sales without incurring the operational cost and liability of a store that may lose its content library overnight. The "edge" of integration is negated by the platform restriction. The risk of label withdrawal is not a future possibility but a current strategic vulnerability that invalidates the business model's foundation. A cheaper, safer alternative is to wait for the subscription model to mature or negotiate a hardware-bundled royalty rather than building a fragile retail storefront.

The absence of a pilot or metric does not save this; the fundamental economics and supply chain stability are broken as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
