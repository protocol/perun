Steelman: A hybrid device leverages untapped demand for portable access to home-console experiences, creating a unique category that avoids direct spec-based competition with powerful rivals.

However, the attack reveals critical flaws based strictly on provided facts:
1.  **Value Proposition Failure:** Pricing a "modest-power" machine equal to "far more powerful" rivals is indefensible without a killer application or service advantage, neither of which is present in the "thin first-year game line-up."
2.  **Compounding Risk:** The previous console suffered from "confusing positioning." Launching a device that is "both docked... and handheld" with "detachable" parts inherently risks repeating this confusion, especially without a clear marketing narrative provided in the proposal.
3.  **Ecosystem Void:** "Limited third-party support" combined with weak specs suggests a software drought. Relying solely on first-party studios with a "thin" lineup cannot sustain a platform launch against established competitors.
4.  **No Edge Stated:** The proposal lists liabilities (weak specs, confusion, limited games, supply constraints) but offers no fact demonstrating why consumers would choose this over existing powerful consoles or eroding handheld phones. Need for a new product is not an edge.

The risks are not merely potential; the stated facts (weak specs, high price, thin lineup) actively contradict the requirement for market entry success. The "hybrid" concept is a feature, not a strategic edge, when the core value pillar (performance/price ratio) is broken. A cheaper, staged alternative (e.g., a dedicated handheld at a lower price point) or waiting for a stronger software lineup would be safer, but the current plan fails the basic test of offering a compelling reason to buy.

Blockers: The price-to-performance mismatch against rivals and the thin software lineup prevent a viable market entry as proposed.
Risks: Repeating brand confusion, supply chain failure, and inability to recover financial losses.
