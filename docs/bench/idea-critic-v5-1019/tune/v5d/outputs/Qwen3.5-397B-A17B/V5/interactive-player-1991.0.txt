First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price and undefined utility against rapidly improving PCs and cheaper consoles offer no defensible advantage.

Steelman: A dedicated $1,000 hub succeeds by offering superior interactive fidelity unavailable on PCs or consoles in 1991.

Attack: The "edge" relies on exclusive content and performance gaps that are shrinking, not widening. PCs are gaining multimedia natively, eliminating the need for a separate $1,000 box. Consoles are cheaper and closing the gap. The small library is a fatal flaw: without a killer app, the value proposition collapses. Buyers cannot understand a product that solves no clear problem at a premium price.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoid sunk costs in a format war with unclear demand.
2. Partner with PC makers: Embed the tech where the market is already going, cheaper and faster.
3. Wait for costs to drop: $1,000 is prohibitive for a secondary living room device without proven utility.

Fit: Assumes a market for dedicated interactive media that may not exist distinct from computing or gaming.

Pre-mortem: The device launches, sells poorly due to price and confusion, the library stalls, and the company is stuck with unsold inventory as PCs absorb the feature set. This is a one-way door with high capital risk.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the price is far above consoles, the library is small, and buyer confusion is high. These are not risks to mitigate; they are current facts that invalidate the business case. No evidence suggests users will pay a 3-4x premium over consoles for an ambiguous "multimedia" experience.

Blockers:
1. Price mismatch: $1,000 exceeds the psychological barrier for a non-essential living room device, especially against established cheaper alternatives.
2. Value ambiguity: No clear "must-have" function distinguishes it from evolving PCs or game consoles.
3. Content chicken-and-egg: Small library ensures low adoption; low adoption ensures small library.

Risks: Format obsolescence, rapid PC convergence, consumer confusion.

The plan fails on its own stated facts. There is no path to profitability or adoption at this price point with this content library.

DECISION: NO-GO
