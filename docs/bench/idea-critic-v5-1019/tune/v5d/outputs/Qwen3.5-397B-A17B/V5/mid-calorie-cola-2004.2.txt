First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it assumes a permanent market shift without evidence that half-sugar satisfies a distinct need better than existing diet or full-sugar options.

Steelman: A mid-calorie cola captures consumers rejecting both the taste of artificial sweeteners and the carb count of regular soda, leveraging a premium price to signal innovation to young men.

Attack:
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The core premise relies on the "low-carb trend" being a durable structural shift rather than a fad. The proposal admits this risk but offers no data to refute it. If the trend collapses, the product becomes a permanent orphan with no natural audience.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows the company to ride the existing diet cola growth without cannibalizing the core brand or diluting pricing power. A cheaper alternative is reformulating existing SKUs or limited-time offers to test the water without a full campaign launch.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: The low-carb craze faded, leaving the product too sweet for dieters and too expensive/tasting "diet-like" for regular soda drinkers. The rival's similar launch creates a crowded niche that evaporates, leaving high inventory and wasted campaign spend.
4.  **Blocker Test:** The proposal explicitly states the risk that "people who want fewer calories already buy the diet version" and "half-sugar may satisfy neither group." These are not unknown risks; they are stated contradictions to the product's value proposition. The plan offers no mechanism (e.g., blind taste test data, pilot sales figures) proving a "middle" segment actually exists and will pay a premium. Launching a national campaign based on an unproven segment definition, while admitting the product might appeal to no one, is a factual failure of the plan as proposed.

Blockers:
- Stated evidence suggests the target segment may not exist (satisfies neither group).
- Reliance on a trend admitted to potentially be a fad without verification.
- No stated edge proving this beats "do nothing" or cheaper tests.

Risks:
- Cannibalization of diet or regular lines.
- Premium price deterring trial.
- Competitor saturation in a shrinking niche.

The plan commits to a large campaign and national launch without resolving the fundamental question of demand. It fails the operator test: do not bet the brand on a "maybe" segment when the risks are explicitly defined as existential to the product's purpose.

DECISION: NO-GO
