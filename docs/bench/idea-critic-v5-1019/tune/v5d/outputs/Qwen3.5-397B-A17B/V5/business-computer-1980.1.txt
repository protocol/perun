First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (business features) but fails to state an edge: it does not explain why this specific complex design will succeed where the current simple machine dominates, nor how it overcomes the massive friction of migrating existing users and software makers. Need alone is not a strategy.

Steelman: A feature-complete business successor is required to capture the enterprise market, and rapid phase-out is necessary to focus engineering resources.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or releasing an peripheral/adapter for lowercase/80-columns is cheaper, reversible, and preserves the installed base. The proposal dismisses these without evidence they cannot meet the need.
2.  **Fit & Risk:** The plan explicitly admits unproven reliability, high complexity, and a tight schedule. Launching a complex, unproven hardware platform while intentionally alienating the current revenue source (existing owners/software makers) creates a single point of failure. If reliability slips or adoption lags, the company loses both the new market and the old one.
3.  **Evidence Gap:** There is no data showing business users will switch ecosystems for these specific features rather than buying from established competitors who already offer them. The assumption that "phase out" forces migration is unverified and dangerous.

Blockers:
-   **Self-Contradiction:** The plan relies on migrating an installed base that it simultaneously acknowledges "may not move." Without a proven migration path or incentive, the revenue collapse risk is immediate and fatal.
-   **Unproven Core:** Launching complex, unproven hardware on a tight schedule violates basic operational safety; a failure here destroys the brand's only success story.

Risks:
-   Software incompatibility causing ecosystem collapse.
-   Reliability issues damaging reputation.
-   Competitors capturing the business segment with mature solutions while this company distracts itself with a risky transition.

The proposal asks to bet the company on an unproven, complex redesign while actively dismantling the current cash cow. The risks are concrete and acknowledged; the edge is absent.

DECISION: NO-GO
