First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price, slow performance, and scarce software are disadvantages, not advantages, against existing business PCs.

Steelman: This machine offers a revolutionary graphical interface that simplifies computing for non-technical office workers, justifying a premium despite hardware limitations.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** Launch at $10,000. High cost, high risk of rejection due to speed and software gaps. Irreversible brand damage if it fails.
2. **Do Nothing:** Wait for the internal cheaper alternative or market maturity. Zero cost, preserves capital, avoids launching a flawed product.
3. **Internal Pivot:** Redirect resources to the cheaper internal machine mentioned. Lower price point, likely faster adoption, leverages same interface innovation.

Fit: Assuming the goal is market penetration and productivity, a $10,000 slow machine with no software contradicts office efficiency needs.

Pre-mortem: The product launches and sits unsold because buyers choose faster, cheaper competitors with established software ecosystems. The internal cheaper cannibalizes it immediately. This is a one-way door for reputation; a two-way door for capital if stopped now. Cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed flagship launch.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the price is "several times" competitors, performance is "slow," and software is "little." These are fatal blockers for a general office launch. The existence of a cheaper internal alternative proves the current pricing strategy is not the only path to the interface value.

Blockers:
1. **Price/Value Mismatch:** $10,000 for a slow machine with no software is economically indefensible against faster, cheaper options.
2. **Internal Competition:** A cheaper internal machine with similar capabilities makes this specific configuration redundant before launch.

Risks: Market rejection, inventory write-downs, brand damage.

The plan fails on its own stated facts. It cannot beat "do nothing" or the internal alternative.

DECISION: NO-GO
