First call: NO-GO. The proposal addresses a self-inflicted wound (the 60% price hike) with structural fragmentation rather than repairing the value proposition. Splitting the brand immediately after a massive price increase signals abandonment of the legacy customer base, validating fears that the DVD service is being pushed into obsolescence.

Steelman: Separating the brands allows distinct management focus and prevents the declining DVD economics from dragging down the streaming valuation, enabling aggressive streaming growth without legacy constraints.

However, the attack reveals fatal timing and execution flaws. The primary blocker is the conflation of a pricing penalty with a brand divorce. Customers punished by the 60% hike now face fractured utility: two logins, two bills, and broken recommendation synergy. This compounds "loss aversion" precisely when trust is lowest. The proposal admits to losing cross-service recommendations, a key differentiator against emerging competitors, without offering a compensatory benefit.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain combined billing to soften the price hike blowback; manage internal divisions without customer-facing splits.
2. Revert pricing: Restore the combined value before attempting structural separation.
3. Proposed split: Maximizes customer churn risk by adding friction to a price increase.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-split, the DVD brand is seen as a "zombie" service, accelerating cancellations. Streaming growth stalls due to the PR backlash of perceived greed and confusion. The stock drops not because of the strategy's long-term merit, but because the execution alienated the entire installed base simultaneously.

Evidence: The proposal itself lists "customer confusion," "two logins/bills," and "reaction to price increase" as risks. These are not mere risks; they are certainties given the context. No kill criteria or pilot is offered to test this separation before a full rollout. The plan assumes the market will accept friction as "strategic clarity" rather than "service degradation."

Blockers: The plan fails as proposed because it compounds a pricing shock with immediate service fragmentation, guaranteeing heightened churn without a verified path to retention. The lack of shared data undermines the core value of a unified entertainment platform.

Risks: Total brand alienation, accelerated DVD collapse, streaming growth stall.

DECISION: NO-GO
