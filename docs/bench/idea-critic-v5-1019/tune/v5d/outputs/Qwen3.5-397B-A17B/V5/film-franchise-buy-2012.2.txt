First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a strategic need (cross-business franchise) but fails to state an edge explaining why this specific distressed asset, at a $4B premium, beats existing internal options or cheaper alternatives.

Steelman: Acquiring this franchise is the only viable path to secure a unified, high-value IP capable of sustaining theme parks, merchandise, and film output for decades, despite current creative instability.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Valuation vs. Output:** The $4B price tag is explicitly noted as "high against the company's current film output." Without a corrected ROI model or evidence that the trilogy guarantees a return exceeding this premium, the math fails as proposed.
2.  **Creative Void:** The founder's retirement is a stated fact, not a risk. The proposal offers no named successor or creative plan to replace the singular vision driving the franchise, directly threatening the core product (films) required to feed the other businesses.
3.  **Licensing Constraints:** Significant merchandise value is already licensed out. This caps the immediate upside for the consumer products division, undermining the primary synergy argument until contracts expire or are bought out (cost not included).

**Risks:** Mixed recent reviews and potential fan rejection are execution risks, not blockers, provided the creative void is filled. However, without a creative lead, these risks become certainties.

**Analysis:**
The proposal relies on the *potential* of a new trilogy to unlock value, yet the two biggest drivers of that value (creative leadership and unencumbered monetization) are explicitly missing or constrained. A "cheaper, smaller, staged" alternative would be to license the film rights for the trilogy only, avoiding the $4B capital outlay and the risk of owning a stagnant IP without its creator. The proposal does not explain why full ownership is required now versus a production deal. The absence of a succession plan for the retiring founder is a fatal flaw in a creative-led acquisition; you are buying a brand without its engine.

The costs are fixed ($4B), the upsides are capped (licensing), and the core mechanism (creative output) is unstaffed. This fails the operator test for capital allocation.

DECISION: NO-GO
