First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: it enters a hardware market where the company has zero experience, faces publisher resistance, and ignores the historical failure of dedicated e-readers without explaining why this attempt would succeed where others failed. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: A proprietary device with free cellular connectivity removes friction for non-technical users, creating a closed ecosystem that locks customers into the store and alleviates publisher piracy fears through DRM, potentially expanding the total market despite hardware inexperience.

**Blockers:**
1. **Core Competency Mismatch:** The company has never designed consumer hardware. Building a reliable device with cellular integration is a fundamentally different business with high upfront capital risk, contradicting the "low price" strategy unless heavily subsidized without a proven volume base.
2. **Supply Chain Hostility:** Publishers fear piracy and margin erosion. Without an existing track record of digital sales or a dominant market position to force terms, securing content at a "single low price" is unlikely; publishers will likely withhold titles or demand higher royalties, breaking the unit economics.
3. **Historical Precedent:** Earlier e-readers sold poorly due to limited utility and high cost. This proposal repeats the hardware bet without a verified change in consumer behavior or technology cost structure that guarantees a different outcome.

**Risks:**
Cannibalization of print sales is a valid concern but manageable if the digital margin compensates; however, with unproven hardware costs and potential publisher pushback, the net margin is negative. The "free cellular" model creates a massive ongoing operational cost with no confirmed revenue stream to cover it.

**Better Ways:**
The "do nothing" or "software-only" approach (partnering with existing hardware makers like Sony or Palm) avoids the hardware cliff while testing digital demand. A cheaper alternative is launching a desktop/mobile app first to build the digital catalog and user habits before committing to custom silicon.

**Conclusion:**
The proposal asks the company to bet its capital on three unproven variables simultaneously: hardware execution, publisher cooperation, and mass-market adoption of a previously failed form factor. The absence of a pilot, a content partnership, or a hardware partner makes this a speculative gamble, not a strategic expansion. The risks are existential; the edge is theoretical.

DECISION: NO-GO
