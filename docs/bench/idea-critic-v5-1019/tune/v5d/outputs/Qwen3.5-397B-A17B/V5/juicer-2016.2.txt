First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price and forced subscription without proven demand or cost advantage over existing methods is a path to failure, not a breakthrough.

**Steelman:** A premium, hassle-free juicing ecosystem where connectivity ensures safety and freshness, justifying a subscription model for busy, affluent consumers.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Edge:** The "edge" is convenience, but the friction is massive. A $699 hardware barrier plus a perpetual subscription premium over grocery store produce requires irrational loyalty. In 2016, the "connected appliance" novelty is wearing thin without clear utility beyond what a dumb machine offers. The proposal admits concerns about price and market size but offers no evidence that the target demographic values app-verified freshness enough to pay a 300% premium.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" (buy a $150 blender or existing juicer) costs less upfront and has zero recurring fees. Buying fresh produce weekly is cheaper and offers variety, unlike proprietary packs. The proposed model locks users into a single vendor's supply chain, creating a single point of failure.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Why? The hardware margin is eaten by R&D for custom presses. The subscription churn is high because users realize they are paying $8 for $2 of fruit. The "freshness check" feature is perceived as nagging, not value. The market for $700 countertop appliances is microscopic; scaling requires lowering prices, which breaks the unit economics of the custom press.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal cites "well-funded" as a strength, but capital cannot fix a broken unit economy or a non-existent market need. No data is provided on willingness-to-pay for the subscription relative to the hardware cost.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Stated Edge:** Need alone is not an edge. The proposal fails to demonstrate why this specific combination of high CAPEX and high OPEX beats the status quo.
*   **Contradictory Constraints:** High device price limits adoption, yet the subscription model requires massive scale to be viable.

**Risks:** Market rejection, supply chain rigidity, perceived obsolescence of "smart" features.

The plan commits real money to a high-risk, low-reversibility hardware play without validating the core economic hypothesis. The lack of a cheaper, staged entry point (e.g., selling packs for existing machines first) is fatal.

DECISION: NO-GO
