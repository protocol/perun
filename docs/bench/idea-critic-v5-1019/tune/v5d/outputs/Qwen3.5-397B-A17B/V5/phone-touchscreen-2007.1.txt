First call: GO. The stated edge is controlling hardware and software to deliver a superior user experience (full web, music, touch interface) that keyboard-led competitors cannot match, while capturing service revenue—a departure from the carrier-dominated status quo.

Steelman: A premium, integrated device creates a new category that makes existing phones obsolete, justifying exclusivity to secure the necessary design freedom and revenue share.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Music player sales erode as phones add media features; the company becomes a niche accessory maker.
2. License software: Carriers dictate design, diluting the experience and margin; fails to capture service revenue.
3. Build a keyboard phone: Competes directly on incumbents' terms without a differentiator; likely fails to command a premium.

Fit: Aligns with the vision of integrating communication and media; assumes the market values experience over physical keyboards.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the lack of a keyboard renders the device unusable for business users, or if the single carrier cannot distribute enough units to reach critical mass. However, the "no keyboard" constraint is the very mechanism enabling the superior screen and interface, not an oversight. The lack of third-party apps is a temporary constraint of a v1 two-way door; the core value proposition (web, email, music) is built-in.

Evidence: The proposal cites the erosion of player sales as the catalyst. The premium price is justified by the unique integration. The revenue share model offsets the risk of exclusivity.

Blockers: NONE. The lack of phone experience is a risk, not a blocker, mitigated by the company's proven mastery of hardware/software integration in music and computing. The market's attachment to keyboards is a assumption to be tested, but the alternative (doing nothing) guarantees decline.

Risks: Market rejection of touch-only input; limited initial distribution; delayed app ecosystem. These are addressed by the kill criteria: monitor activation rates and churn in the first quarter; if adoption lags behind internal projections by >20%, pivot marketing or accelerate the SDK.

The plan beats "do nothing" and "license" by creating a defensible moat around the user experience and revenue stream.

DECISION: GO
