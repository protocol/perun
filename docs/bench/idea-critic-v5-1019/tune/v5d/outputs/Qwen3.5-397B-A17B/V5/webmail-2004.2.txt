First call: NO-GO. The proposal assumes a cost structure (gigabyte storage) and a revenue model (content-scanned ads) that are unproven at this scale, while entering a mature market with entrenched incumbents who offer "sufficient" free storage. The stated edge ("fast search") is a feature, not a defensible business moat against well-funded competitors who can copy it.

Steelman: A massive storage leap combined with superior search creates a sticky ecosystem that justifies ad-scanning privacy trade-offs, disrupting incumbents by redefining user expectations of "free."

Attack:
1. **Cost Blocker**: The proposal cites "routine deletion" by users as evidence that current limits are acceptable, yet plans to offer 1,000x more storage. If users do not delete, storage costs scale linearly with adoption. With no direct revenue at launch and reliance on unproven ad-matching tech, the burn rate could be catastrophic before monetization validates. The math of "free gigabyte" vs. "text ad CPM" is not shown to close.
2. **Privacy Fatal Flaw**: Scanning private correspondence for ads is a novel, untested value exchange. In 2004, trust is the primary currency of email. A single leak or misuse perception could trigger immediate regulatory scrutiny or mass exodus, destroying the brand before network effects kick in.
3. **Market Reality**: Incumbents have deep pockets. If this model works, they can match the storage instantly, leveraging existing user bases. The "invitation-only" gate slows growth, delaying the data needed to refine the ad algorithm, creating a catch-22.

Alternatives:
- **Do Nothing**: Avoid massive CAPEX/OPEX risk in a low-margin commodity market.
- **Niche Enterprise**: Sell large-storage email to businesses first to fund the tech, avoiding consumer privacy backlash.
- **Incremental Growth**: Offer 100MB (10x current) to test cost/revenue dynamics without betting the company on a 1,000x jump.

Pre-mortem: The service launches, costs skyrocket as users hoard data, privacy groups sue over scanning, advertisers flee due to brand safety concerns, and the company runs out of cash unable to compete with incumbents who simply match the storage limit.

Blockers: Unproven unit economics (cost of 1GB vs. ad yield), unvalidated privacy acceptance model, and lack of defensibility against incumbent replication.
Risks: Regulatory action, brand toxicity, infinite storage liability.

DECISION: NO-GO
