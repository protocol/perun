First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a clear need (direct audience access) but fails to state an edge: a lower price alone does not beat incumbents who already possess scale, data, and habit, especially when funded by burning cash from severed licensing deals.

**Steelman:** Withdrawing content forces migration to a proprietary platform where lower pricing eventually outweighs the friction of switching and the loss of licensing income.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" (continue licensing) guarantees immediate billions in high-margin revenue with zero execution risk. The "cheaper alternative" is a hybrid model (license older content, keep new exclusives) which captures upside without the catastrophic cash burn of a total pullback. The proposal does not demonstrate why the total withdrawal beats a staged approach.
2.  **Fit & Evidence:** The plan relies on acquiring tens of millions of subscribers to offset the loss of licensing revenue. However, the proposal admits the company has never run a technology platform of this scale. The "multi-year operating loss" creates a fixed cost structure that must be covered before the uncertain subscriber target is met. The stated facts show a dependency on unproven execution to replace proven income.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** If the tech platform fails or subscriber growth lags (common in crowded markets), the company loses both the licensing revenue (gone forever) and the theatrical/cable synergy, leading to a liquidity crisis. The "one-way door" of pulling content removes the fallback option immediately.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Contradiction:** The plan replaces guaranteed, high-margin licensing revenue with speculative subscription revenue funded by losses. The math requires near-perfect execution to break even, yet the proposal admits zero prior experience in the core delivery mechanism (tech platform).
*   **Superior Alternative Exists:** A partial licensing strategy retains revenue while testing the direct-to-consumer water. The proposal's "all-in" withdrawal fails the "beat do nothing" test on stated facts because it discards current cash flow for uncertain future gains without a bridge.

**Risks:** Execution failure, cash burn exceeding projections, inability to churn-proof a low-price service.

The absence of a pilot, a hybrid revenue model, or proof of technical capability makes this a bet on perfection in an unproven domain while destroying existing value.

DECISION: NO-GO
