First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits unproven economics and a large short-term margin hit without a stated edge (e.g., verified LTV uplift or competitor gap) to offset the immediate cash burn.

**Steelman:** A $79 annual fee unlocks unlimited two-day shipping, converting shipping friction into a recurring revenue stream that locks in loyalty and increases order frequency enough to cover variable costs within 12 months.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** The proposal explicitly states economics are "unproven." If heavy buyers double frequency but shipping costs triple, the retailer loses money on every loyal order. Without a pilot proving the margin contribution of incremental orders exceeds the shipping subsidy, this is a bet on hope, not math.
2.  **Reversibility:** Selling annual memberships creates a hard lock-in. If the model fails in month three, cancelling existing memberships destroys trust; honoring them guarantees losses for up to a year. This is a one-way door with no emergency exit.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves current margins. A "3-month trial" for select users tests the elasticity and cost basis without committing to a full annual cycle or exposing the whole base to potential rate hikes later.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Unit Economics:** The core claim (fee revenue > increased shipping costs) is admitted as unproven. Launching without a verified positive contribution margin per incremental order is financial negligence.
*   **Irreversible Liability:** Selling 12-month contracts before validating the cost structure creates a fixed liability that cannot be paused if the hypothesis fails.

**Risks:** Margin erosion, cash flow crisis, inability to pivot pricing.

**Verdict:** The absence of a pilot, a cap on exposure, or a validated unit-economic model constitutes a blocker. You cannot approve a structural change to the P&L based on an admitted guess. The plan must first prove the math on a small, reversible sample.

DECISION: NO-GO
