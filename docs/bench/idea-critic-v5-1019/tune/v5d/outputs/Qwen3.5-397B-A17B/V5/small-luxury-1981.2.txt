First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; sharing a mass-market platform while charging a premium price against established rivals offers no fact-based advantage in cost, performance, or exclusivity that beats current buyer options. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: Leveraging an existing platform accelerates time-to-market for a compact entry, capturing new American buyers who cannot afford the flagship models, provided the "luxury" differentiation is perceptible enough to override the mass-market underpinnings.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current facts. The primary blocker is the contradiction between the "luxury" value proposition and the "mass-market compact" foundation. Luxury buyers purchase status and exclusivity; a shared platform with economy cars directly undermines this core utility. The proposal admits salespeople will not even use the main brand name, signaling an internal recognition that the association damages rather than enhances the product. If the sales force cannot justify the premium price against rivals due to the shared architecture, the unit economics fail.

The risks listed are not merely potential downsides; they are probable outcomes based on the stated strategy. "Buyers seeing through the shared platform" is a near-certainty given the scrutiny of this segment. The "prestige suffering" is a direct consequence of diluting the brand with economy components. There is no "do nothing" alternative analysis showing why capturing this specific segment now outweighs the long-term brand erosion. A cheaper alternative exists: develop a distinct, albeit smaller, platform or wait until a dedicated architecture is viable, rather than risking the entire brand equity for a short-term volume play.

The evidence provided suggests the target demographic wants a "smaller sedan," not necessarily a "cheap luxury sedan." The proposal conflates size with price sensitivity without proof. Without a verified mechanism to insulate the luxury perception from the mass-market reality (beyond "added trim"), the plan fails its own test of maintaining the brand's premium status. The absence of a pilot or metric to test brand dilution before full rollout confirms this is a high-stakes gamble with no safety net.

Blockers: The shared mass-market platform contradicts the premium pricing and luxury positioning, creating an unsolvable value gap that rivals do not have.
Risks: Total brand dilution, sales channel rejection, and failure to justify price premium.

DECISION: NO-GO
