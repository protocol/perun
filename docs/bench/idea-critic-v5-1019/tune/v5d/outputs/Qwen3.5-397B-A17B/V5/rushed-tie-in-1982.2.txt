First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a five-week development window for a hit film license, which contradicts standard industry lead times, creating an unmitigated quality risk that threatens the entire investment.

**Steelman:** A single elite programmer can bypass bureaucracy to rapidly translate the film's core appeal into a playable product, capturing the Christmas market before hype fades.

**Attack:**
1.  **Feasibility:** Five weeks is insufficient for design, coding, testing, and debugging a commercial title, even for one person. The likelihood of a ship-ready, bug-free product is near zero.
2.  **Inventory Risk:** Ordering a "very large" production run based on an unproven, rushed product exposes the company to massive losses from unsold inventory. The high license fee increases the break-even point, making failure catastrophic.
3.  **Alternative:** A smaller initial batch or a delayed launch to ensure quality would preserve capital and brand reputation, even if it misses some early sales.

**Pre-mortem:** The game ships late or riddled with bugs, receiving poor reviews. Retailers return the massive inventory. The company absorbs the high license fee plus manufacturing costs, resulting in a significant financial loss and reputational damage.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits five weeks is "far shorter than normal." No evidence suggests this specific programmer can defy industry norms by a factor of 4-5x without quality degradation.

**Blockers:**
*   **Time:** The stated timeline is objectively unrealistic for a commercial release, directly threatening the product's viability.
*   **Scale:** Committing to a large production run before validating the product's quality or market reception is financially reckless given the high sunk costs.

**Risks:** Poor quality, market rejection, inventory glut, brand damage.

**Conclusion:** The plan fails the basic sanity check of development time versus scope. Proceeding as proposed guarantees a high probability of failure. The high license fee demands *more* caution, not less. Do not approve the large production run or the five-week timeline.

DECISION: NO-GO
