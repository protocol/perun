First call: NO-GO. Doubling production while sales lag by 60% during a recession with rising interest rates ignores the fundamental mismatch between supply and demand, risking immediate insolvency.

**Steelman:** The proposal argues that doubling output will lower unit costs via economies of scale, allowing price cuts to stimulate demand and clear the 4,500-unit backlog before cash runs out.

**Attack:**
1.  **Demand Reality:** A recession with rising interest rates suppresses big-ticket purchases. Lowering prices may not offset the cost of capital or consumer hesitation.
2.  **Cash Burn:** Doubling production requires upfront capital for parts and labor. With 4,500 unsold units already straining dealer financing, adding 7,500 more units annually will exhaust limited cash reserves before any new revenue arrives.
3.  **Dealer Capacity:** Dealers cannot finance more stock in a high-rate environment. Flooding them creates a channel blockade, not sales.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves cash but fails to grow. A better way is to halt or reduce production to match sales velocity, preserving cash for marketing or incentives that directly move existing inventory.

**Pre-mortem:** The company doubles output, cash runs out in six months due to inflated working capital needs, dealers refuse further deliveries, and the firm enters bankruptcy with double the unsold inventory.

**Blockers:**
*   **Cash Flow:** The plan increases cash outflow immediately while revenue remains uncertain. The stated "limited cash" cannot support doubled production costs without guaranteed sales, which the recession contradicts.
*   **Channel Constraint:** Dealers are explicitly cited as unable to finance more stock; increasing supply violates this physical/financial constraint.

**Risks:** Inventory obsolescence, brand damage from forced discounting, total liquidity crisis.

**Conclusion:** The proposal fights market gravity by increasing exposure to the very factors (recession, rates) causing the slowdown. It assumes production efficiency solves a demand and liquidity crisis. The blockers are fatal under current facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
