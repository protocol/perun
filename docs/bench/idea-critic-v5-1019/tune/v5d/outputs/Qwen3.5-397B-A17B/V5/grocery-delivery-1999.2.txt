First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it attempts to solve a known need (grocery delivery) with a capital structure that guarantees failure before validating demand or unit economics.

Steelman: Aggressively securing nationwide infrastructure now creates an insurmountable moat against competitors before the market matures.

Attack: The plan violates basic operational logic for a low-margin business. Groceries require high volume and extreme efficiency to survive; this plan locks in massive fixed costs (automated warehouses, fleets) across a dozen cities while the company has zero operating data from its single launch city (June 1999). The "30-minute slot" promise combined with unproven customer density creates a recipe for catastrophic cash burn. The risks cited (capital intensity, thin margins, no track record) are not merely potential issues; they are active blockers when paired with a $1 billion upfront commitment.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital, learn from the first city.
2. Staged expansion: Build one warehouse only after the first city proves positive unit economics.
3. Asset-light: Partner with existing stores rather than building automated hubs.

The proposed plan cannot beat "do nothing" because it assumes success without evidence. A cheaper, staged alternative reaches the same upside (market coverage) with significantly less risk and preserves the option to pivot.

Pre-mortem: The company runs out of cash within 18 months as warehouses sit underutilized in cities where density never materializes. The automation cannot adapt to local buying habits learned too late. This is a one-way door; once the billion is spent on specialized infrastructure, it cannot be recovered.

Blockers:
1. No validated unit economics from the single operating city before scaling to a dozen.
2. $1 billion fixed cost commitment in a low-margin business with no revenue track record.
3. The plan explicitly ignores learning from the initial launch, guaranteeing replication of unknown errors.

Risks: Customer adoption rates, competitor response, supply chain complexity.

The absence of a pilot cap or metric is a fatal flaw here because the scale is irreversible and the margin for error is non-existent.

DECISION: NO-GO
