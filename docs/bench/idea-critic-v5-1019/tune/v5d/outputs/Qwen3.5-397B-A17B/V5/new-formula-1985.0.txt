First call: NO-GO. The proposal confuses sip-level preference with brand-level value, ignoring that the product's core asset is its heritage, not just its taste profile.

**Steelman:** Replacing the formula maximizes market share by aligning the product with verified consumer taste preferences demonstrated in blind trials.

**Attack:** The testing methodology is fatally flawed. By withholding the fact that the original formula would be permanently withdrawn, the tests measured only immediate sensory reaction, not the total value proposition which includes nostalgia, identity, and tradition. Blind tests inherently strip away the brand equity that drives loyalty; applying their results to a branded decision is a category error.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Retain the current formula. Risk: Continued slow share erosion to competitors and diet segments.
2.  **Line Extension:** Launch the sweeter formula as a new variant (e.g., "New Cola") while keeping the original. This captures the taste-preference segment without alienating the heritage base. Reversibility is high; risk is portfolio complexity.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Total replacement. Risk: Catastrophic brand backlash, bottler revolt, and permanent loss of the core demographic. Reversibility is low once the original supply chain is dismantled.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch triggers a public relations crisis. Consumers perceive the removal of the original as an arrogant dismissal of their history. The "sweeter" taste, liked in sips, becomes cloying over whole cans, leading to buyer remorse. Competitors capitalize on the void by marketing themselves as the "real" choice. Sales plummet, not rise.

**Blockers:**
*   **Methodology Mismatch:** The evidence (blind taste tests) explicitly excludes the primary driver of purchase (brand heritage/emotion). The data does not support the specific claim that *replacement* will succeed, only that the *liquid* is preferred in isolation.
*   **Missing Fallback:** The plan to withdraw the original entirely removes the safety valve for negative reaction, turning a manageable risk into an existential threat.

**Risks:** Emotional backlash, bottler friction, misinterpretation of "sip" vs. "drink" satisfaction.

**Kill Criteria:** If focus groups are re-run with full disclosure of the original's removal and show >20% intent to boycott, the plan must stop. Without a line-extension strategy, the risk/reward ratio is unacceptable.

DECISION: NO-GO
