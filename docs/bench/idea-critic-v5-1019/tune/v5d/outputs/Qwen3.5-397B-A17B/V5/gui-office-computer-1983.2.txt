First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price, slow performance, and software scarcity are disadvantages, not advantages, against existing business PCs.

Steelman: This machine offers a revolutionary graphical interface that drastically reduces training time and error rates for office workers, justifying the premium through long-term productivity gains despite higher upfront costs.

Attack:
1. **Price vs. Value:** At $10,000, the unit costs several times more than leading competitors. Without a massive, immediate productivity multiplier (unproven due to lack of software), the ROI fails for standard office tasks.
2. **Performance Bottleneck:** "Runs slowly" directly contradicts the efficiency promise of a GUI. If the interface lag frustrates users, adoption will stall regardless of visual appeal.
3. **Software Void:** Business utility depends on applications (spreadsheets, word processing). "Little third-party software" means the hardware is a solution searching for a problem.
4. **Internal Cannibalization:** A cheaper, similar internal project exists. Launching the expensive, slower version first invites immediate obsolescence and internal conflict, wasting marketing spend.
5. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" and wait for the cheaper internal model or let competitors refine the GUI approach first is safer. The "cheaper machine" alternative reaches the same upside (GUI) with better cost structure.

Blockers:
- **Economic Failure:** Price is multiples of competitors without a proportional, proven performance or software advantage.
- **Strategic Conflict:** An internal, cheaper alternative with similar capabilities makes this specific launch redundant and financially risky.
- **Functional Deficit:** Slow speed and lack of software render the core value proposition (efficiency) unachievable at launch.

Risks:
- Market rejection due to cost.
- Developer apathy due to low initial install base.
- Rapid devaluation once the cheaper internal model releases.

The proposal commits real money to a product that is slower, more expensive, and less useful than current options, while ignoring a superior internal alternative. There is no "go-if" condition that fixes the fundamental mismatch between price and delivered value today.

DECISION: NO-GO
