First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; paying a premium for an unproven rookie with no professional track record contradicts standard risk mitigation, and "breaking rules" is a liability, not a product advantage, without proof that consumers value rebellion over compliance.

Steelman: A charismatic rookie can transcend the sport to become a cultural icon, making the shoe a lifestyle statement rather than just athletic gear, where controversy drives demand rather than suppressing it.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital, accept slow decline in basketball segment.
2. Sign established star: Higher cost, lower upside, safe but undifferentiated.
3. This proposal: Maximum risk, maximum potential cultural disruption.

Fit: Assumes the brand needs a radical identity shift to survive; if the goal is steady growth, this fails.

Pre-mortem: The player suffers a career-ending injury in game one; the league bans the shoes immediately, forcing a costly recall; the "rebel" angle alienates traditional buyers without attracting youth. The door is one-way (five-year commitment); delay costs the chance to sign this specific individual but preserves cash.

Evidence: Zero professional data exists on the player. The claim that "colorways breaking rules" will sell is assumed; no market test confirms consumers want non-compliant gear. The financial commitment represents a large, unverifiable share of the budget against zero revenue proof.

Blockers:
1. No evidence the target market values rule-breaking enough to pay a premium; the core claim relies on an unverified cultural shift.
2. The financial exposure (5 years, record sum) is disproportionate to the single point of failure (one unproven athlete's health and performance).
3. Regulatory risk is treated as a feature, but a ban eliminates the product entirely, a fatal flaw in the supply chain logic.

Risks: Injury, flop performance, brand alienation, regulatory crackdown.

The plan commits massive resources to a single unproven variable with no fallback. The "edge" is purely hypothetical. Without a pilot, a shorter term, or insurance against injury/bans, the math does not work.

DECISION: NO-GO
