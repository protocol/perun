First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it offers a higher-priced device with a smaller compatible base than existing competitors, relying on a market (legal downloads) that does not yet exist.

Steelman: A premium, high-capacity device creates a superior user experience that justifies the price, betting that legal downloads will emerge quickly to unlock its value.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoid capital risk in an unproven, shrinking niche.
2. Cheaper alternative: License software to Windows/Mac to expand the base immediately, reducing unit cost risk.
3. Proposed: High cost, high risk, limited reversibility due to custom hardware sourcing.

Fit: Assumes a strategy of vertical integration to control quality, but contradicts the immediate need for market penetration given the tiny installed base.

Pre-mortem: The device fails because the addressable market is too small to sustain component costs, and legal music sources do not appear in time, leaving users with an expensive brick. This is a one-way door with high delay cost if the window for digital music closes or shifts to streaming.

Evidence: The proposal admits no consumer experience, a small market share, no legal downloads, and higher price. Competitors are cheaper. The math of 5GB vs. price vs. user base does not support profitability without a miracle in licensing or market expansion.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: Higher price + smaller base + no content = no reason for consumers to choose this over cheaper, more compatible options.
2. Market reality: The core value proposition (legal music) is absent and outside the maker's control.

Risks: Component sourcing delays, brand damage from failure in a new category, cash burn.

The plan fails the basic test of offering a compelling reason to buy *today*. It relies on external factors (industry licensing) to validate a premium price, while actively restricting the potential customer pool. Without a guaranteed content pipeline or a larger compatible base, the unit economics cannot work.

DECISION: NO-GO
