First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: paying $1B for zero revenue and no business model does not beat building internally or waiting for a lower valuation post-IPO volatility.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the sole mobile threat neutralizes a existential risk to the core business faster than internal development, buying immediate distribution and talent.

**Attack:**
1.  **Valuation Disconnect:** $1B for ~12 employees and $0 revenue implies a valuation multiple with no mathematical anchor. The "threat" is unproven monetization; paying a premium for user growth alone is speculative gambling, not strategy.
2.  **Integration Risk:** The proposal admits the buyer's mobile product is weak and culture clash is a risk. History shows acquirers often destroy the very agility they buy. If the app's value is its culture, the acquisition likely destroys the asset.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows the target to fail under IPO scrutiny or be bought cheaper later. "Build" leverages existing cash flow without the integration debt. The proposal fails to prove why $1B cash/stock is superior to these options.
4.  **Regulatory & Timing:** Buying a dominant competitor right before its IPO invites immediate antitrust scrutiny, potentially blocking the deal after significant reputational damage.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Business Model:** The core claim relies on future monetization that does not exist. Paying $1B for a hypothesis violates capital discipline.
*   **Valuation Contradiction:** The price exceeds any reasonable projection of near-term cash flow, contradicting the "sound investment" premise.

**Risks:** Culture destruction, regulatory block, overpayment for a fad, internal morale collapse.

**Conclusion:** The plan commits massive capital to an unproven model while ignoring cheaper, safer alternatives. The absence of revenue and a clear path to ROI constitutes a fundamental failure of the proposal as stated. The "threat" argument is fear-based, not evidence-based.

DECISION: NO-GO
