First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge proving this hardware bet beats existing alternatives or mitigates the explicit risks of inexperience and publisher hostility.

Steelman: A dedicated, cellular-enabled device removes friction for book buyers and solves publisher piracy fears via a closed ecosystem, creating a new high-margin revenue stream that offsets print cannibalization.

Attack:
1.  **Capability Gap:** The company has zero hardware experience. Designing reliable consumer electronics with cellular integration is a distinct discipline from retail logistics. Failure here destroys brand trust before the store launches.
2.  **Economic Conflict:** Low prices required to drive adoption directly squeeze margins and antagonize publishers who already fear piracy. Without proven demand, publishers have no incentive to accept lower royalties or digital rights management that limits their control.
3.  **Market History:** Dedicated e-readers have historically sold poorly. The proposal assumes a "paper-like screen" and "free cellular" are sufficient to reverse this trend without evidence of consumer willingness to pay for a single-purpose device.
4.  **Cannibalization:** The plan admits print sales may drop. Without a guaranteed surge in digital volume to offset lower per-unit margins, total profit could decline.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids hardware risk and capital expenditure; waits for the market to mature or partners to emerge.
*   **Software-Only:** License reading software to existing hardware manufacturers (laptops, PDAs). Tests demand without building a device.
*   **Partnership:** Co-brand an existing e-reader rather than building from scratch.

Pre-mortem: The device launches late, costs double the target, battery life fails, and publishers withhold key titles due to pricing disputes. The company is stuck with unsold inventory and a damaged reputation.

Blocker: The combination of zero internal hardware capability and the unresolved conflict between low consumer prices and publisher margin requirements creates a fundamental execution risk that cannot be overcome as proposed. The "free cellular" cost model is undefined against the low-price strategy.

Risks: Market rejection, brand damage, supplier alienation.

DECISION: NO-GO
