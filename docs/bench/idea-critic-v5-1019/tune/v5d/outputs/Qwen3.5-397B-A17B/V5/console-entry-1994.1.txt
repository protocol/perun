First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "easier tools" and "cheaper discs" are table stakes, not a defensible wedge against incumbents with locked ecosystems and hit franchises.

Steelman: A new entrant can displace incumbents by shifting the economic model from expensive cartridges to cheap discs, enabling third-party developers to take risks on 3D games that cartridge costs prohibit.

Attack: The claim fails on three verified fronts. First, the "edge" is theoretical; without a killer app or exclusive content, cheaper manufacturing does not compel developers to abandon established audiences. Second, the timing is fatal: launching simultaneously with an incumbent's own 3D hardware invites a direct feature war the newcomer cannot win without a library. Third, the financial model is unproven; selling hardware at a loss requires deep pockets and a guaranteed software attach rate, which is absent without developer commitments.

Alternatives: Doing nothing preserves capital. Partnering with the existing console maker (despite the cancellation) or licensing the technology to them offers lower risk. A cheaper, 2D-focused device for a niche market is safer but forfeits the 3D ambition.

Pre-mortem: The console launches, developers hesitate due to the small install base, the library remains thin, gamers stick to familiar brands, and the company writes off hundreds of millions in unsold inventory and R&D.

Evidence: The proposal admits zero experience as a platform holder and relies on winning over developers who currently have no incentive to switch. The risk of an incumbent launching a rival 3D system is stated as a fact, not a possibility.

Blockers: 
1. No stated edge: Cost advantages alone do not break network effects without a content strategy.
2. Direct competition: Launching against an incumbent's 3D debut without a unique value proposition guarantees obscurity.
3. Financial exposure: Hardware subsidies require a software success rate the company cannot guarantee.

Risks: Developer apathy, price wars, brand dilution, massive inventory write-downs.

The plan commits real money to a high-risk, low-reversibility bet without a secured content pipeline or a differentiated consumer hook beyond price, which is insufficient to overcome incumbent loyalty.

DECISION: NO-GO
