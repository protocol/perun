First call: GO. The stated edge is a 200x storage leap (1GB vs. MBs) paired with fast search, solving the user pain of constant deletion while incumbents force hoarding discipline. This creates a defensible wedge for ad-targeting data that smaller mailboxes cannot provide.

**Steelman:** A search giant leverages existing ad infrastructure to monetize massive free storage, using scarcity of competitor storage as the primary acquisition hook.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Cedes the "inbox as database" behavioral shift to competitors.
*   *Cheaper Alternative:* Selling storage tiers fails; free adoption is required to train ad algorithms at scale.
*   *Privacy Risk:* Scanning content is the core revenue mechanic. Without explicit opt-in transparency, this invites regulatory backlash. However, the value exchange (free GBs) historically outweighs privacy concerns for mass markets.
*   *Cost Risk:* Storage costs are falling (Moore's Law for disks). The "high cost" objection assumes static hardware pricing, ignoring the trajectory of 2004 drive economics.
*   *Market Maturity:* Incumbents are complacent with small quotas. Disruption comes from changing the unit economics, not feature parity.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if ad relevance is too low to cover storage OpEx before user churn, or if privacy outrage triggers a boycott. The invitation-only launch mitigates server load and creates artificial scarcity to drive demand, acting as a natural circuit breaker.

**Blockers:** None. The proposal's own facts (falling storage costs, high user friction with deletion, existing ad tech) support the model. The risks (privacy, cost) are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws, given the search company's specific capabilities.

**Kill Criteria:** If cost-per-user exceeds ad-revenue-per-user after 12 months at scale, or if privacy litigation halts content scanning, pivot to a paid tier or shut down. Leading metric: Ad yield per GB stored.

The "no direct revenue" critique is invalid; the model relies on user accumulation to enable future ad density. The storage cost is the customer acquisition cost.

DECISION: GO
