First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; it assumes the buyer's mass-market distribution and advertising machinery will automatically translate to a premium, cult-brand context without evidence that this specific mechanism works for this category. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: The acquisition succeeds only if the buyer can scale volume via mass channels faster than competitors erode the brand's niche, without destroying the "quirky" loyalty that drives the premium price.

Attack: The core claim rests on a fatal contradiction. The brand's value is explicitly tied to its "small, quirky network" and "cult image." The buyer's plan is to immediately replace these with "supermarket-focused distribution" and "mass-market advertising." This is not integration; it is lobotomization. The proposal admits the risk of losing the cult image but proceeds with the exact actions that cause it. There is no "go-if" condition to preserve the independent distributors or test the marketing shift; it is a binary swap.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids the $1.7B cash outlay and the high probability of brand destruction.
2. Minority stake or partnership: Gains exposure to growth without forcing a cultural collision that threatens the asset's core value.
3. Acquire and ring-fence: Keep the distribution and marketing separate (harder, but viable). The current plan rejects this.

Pre-mortem: One year post-acquisition, the brand is delisted by specialty stores due to channel conflict, the core demographic rejects the mass-market ads as "sellout," and sales flatline as big soda competitors undercut the now-commoditized product in supermarkets. The $1.7B is sunk.

Blockers:
- The plan explicitly removes the stated source of the brand's value (quirky distribution/cult image) to apply a generic playbook.
- No evidence is offered that mass-market mechanics work for this specific "cult" segment; the sports drink analogy is an assumption, not proof.
- The $1.7B price assumes continued premium growth, which the proposed strategy directly undermines.

Risks: Channel conflict, brand dilution, competitor encroachment. These are certainties under the current plan, not mere risks.

DECISION: NO-GO
