First call: GO. The proposal leverages a distinct strategic edge: asymmetric differentiation via dual-screen interaction and touch input to access a non-core demographic at a lower price point, rather than engaging in a losing specifications war against a well-funded rival.

**Steelman:** By abandoning the race for graphical fidelity and instead offering unique gameplay mechanics through dual screens and touch, the company creates a new market category that the rival's superior hardware cannot address, while the retained legacy device protects the core revenue stream.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Ceding the high-end to the rival while ignoring the mass market invites eventual displacement as the core audience shrinks.
2.  **Match Hardware:** Financially suicidal against a well-funded opponent; results in a "me-too" device with no unique value proposition.
3.  **Risks:** Developer confusion and unproven touch mechanics are execution risks, not strategic blockers. The "gimmick" concern is mitigated by the lower price point, which lowers the barrier to trial. The cost of two screens is offset by avoiding the expensive high-end GPU required to match the rival.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the software library does not demonstrate the unique utility of the second screen within the first year, causing the hardware to be perceived as a novelty toy rather than a gaming platform. However, keeping the older handheld on sale provides a safety net for core gamers unwilling to adapt immediately.

**Evidence & Logic:** The proposal explicitly targets "broadening the audience," acknowledging that the current trajectory limits growth. The rival's strength (graphics) is irrelevant to users prioritizing portability, price, and novel interaction. The dual-screen approach turns a hardware disadvantage (lower raw power) into a software advantage (unique gameplay).

**Blockers:** NONE. The facts presented show a viable path to differentiation that avoids direct conflict with the rival's strengths.
**Risks:** Developer adoption speed; consumer perception of touch as a gimmick. These are managed by the kill criterion of software attach rates within the first two quarters.

The plan beats "do nothing" by opening a new demographic and beats "matching hardware" by preserving capital and defining a new category.

DECISION: GO
