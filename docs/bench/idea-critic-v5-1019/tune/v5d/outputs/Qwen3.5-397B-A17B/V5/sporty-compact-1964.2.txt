First call: GO. The proposal targets a specific, growing demographic (young postwar boomers) with a stated edge: sporty styling at a low price via parts reuse, which existing buyers lack.

**Steelman:** Leverage proven compact mechanics to minimize R&D risk while delivering distinct "sporty" aesthetics and customization to capture an emerging youth market before competitors.

**Attack & Analysis:**
*   **Assumptions:** Young buyers prioritize style over brand prestige; parts sharing won't deter them if the final look is distinct.
*   **Better Way:** Building a new platform is too costly and slow; modifying a luxury car misses the price point. "Do nothing" cedes the segment.
*   **Fit:** Aligns with capturing volume growth in a new demographic.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the "cheap" mechanical base undermines the sporty image or if the advertising fails to define the car as distinct from the economy donor.
*   **Evidence:** The demographic shift is a stated fact. The mechanical reliability is proven.
*   **Blockers:** None. The risks cited (unproven demand, cheap perception, high ad spend) are *risks*, not blockers. They are inherent to entering a new category. The proposal addresses the "cheap" risk via styling (long hood/short deck) and the demand risk via a low base price (limiting downside) and high-margin options.
*   **Kill Criteria:** If first-year sales do not cover the launch campaign cost, or if customization uptake is below 20%, pivot marketing or trim options.

**Decision Logic:**
The plan beats "do nothing" by addressing a clear demographic wave with a capital-efficient solution. The risks are manageable through the proposed low entry price and optional upsells. The heavy ad spend is necessary to define the new category; waiting for proof of demand allows competitors to seize the mindshare. The mechanical reuse is a feature (reliability/cost), not a bug, provided the styling successfully masks the economy roots. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal's own terms.

**Dissent Ledger:** High upfront cash burn; reliance on unproven consumer appetite for "sporty" in this segment.
**Remaining Risk:** Brand dilution if the styling fails to distinguish the car from the compact donor.

DECISION: GO
