First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge over existing handhelds or home consoles; "between generations" is a gap, not an advantage, and the listed constraints (monochrome, non-portable, discomfort) actively degrade the user experience compared to current options.

Steelman: A low-cost ($180) entry point leveraging stereoscopic novelty to bridge hardware cycles.
Attack: The cost saving comes from stripping essential value (color, portability, comfort), creating a product that is worse than both flanking categories. The "novelty" is directly linked to the reported health risks (eye strain), making the core mechanism a liability.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Preserve brand equity, wait for the true next-gen home console.
2. Enhance current handheld: Lower price or refresh library.
3. This proposal: High risk of brand damage for a niche, uncomfortable experience.
This plan does not beat "do nothing" because it introduces negative utility (discomfort) without a compensating unique benefit that users want.

Fit: Assuming the goal is market expansion, alienating users with headaches and a degraded visual format contradicts growth. A product defined by what it lacks (color, portability) fails to move any positive north star.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, reviews cite physical discomfort and poor visuals, sales flatten immediately, and it becomes a cautionary tale that distracts from the upcoming home console launch. This is a one-way door for brand reputation; the cost of delay is zero since the home console is already in the pipeline.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the flaws: "red-on-black only," "neither portable nor comfortable," "eye strain." These are not risks to be managed; they are inherent characteristics of the proposed design that invalidate the value proposition.

Blockers:
1. The stated features (monochrome, eyepiece strain, lack of portability) constitute a net-negative user experience compared to existing alternatives, meaning the product fails its basic function of entertainment.
2. No stated edge exists; "between generations" is a timing constraint, not a product advantage.

Risks: Brand damage, developer apathy due to small install base, inventory write-downs.

The plan commits resources to a product that is objectively inferior to the status quo based on its own specifications.

DECISION: NO-GO
