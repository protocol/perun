First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge over rapidly improving ground cellular networks; high cost and poor indoor performance are fatal disadvantages, not differentiators.

Steelman: This is the only solution for truly remote global coverage where terrestrial cells cannot reach, targeting high-value travelers who must have connectivity regardless of location.

Attack: The "global" advantage is shrinking daily as ground networks expand. The economics rely on rapid mass adoption to service $5 billion in debt, yet the $3,000 handset price and high call rates severely restrict the addressable market to a tiny niche. Bulkiness and indoor failure further reduce utility compared to emerging cellular alternatives. The sales channel unreadiness compounds the risk of missing critical debt service windows.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoid sinking further capital into a likely stranded asset; let terrestrial networks mature.
2. Partner/License: Lease capacity to existing carriers rather than building a consumer brand and hardware ecosystem from scratch.
3. Niche/Government focus: Pivot immediately to maritime, aviation, or defense sectors where price sensitivity is lower and indoor use is irrelevant, abandoning the mass business traveler market.

Pre-mortem: The company launches, fails to attract enough subscribers to cover debt payments due to high prices and poor user experience, and declares bankruptcy within a year as ground coverage renders the value proposition obsolete.

Evidence Check: The proposal admits sales channels are not ready and risks are high regarding debt service. No evidence suggests the target market will pay a 10x premium for inferior indoor service.

Blockers:
1. Economics mismatch: Debt service requires mass adoption; pricing and product limitations restrict market to a niche insufficient to cover costs.
2. Competitive erosion: Ground networks are spreading fast, removing the unique value proposition before commercial launch.
3. Execution gap: Sales channels are unready, threatening immediate revenue upon launch.

Risks: Technology obsolescence, regulatory hurdles, handset supply chain failures.

The plan fails as proposed because the stated facts show the target users likely will not adopt it at the required scale to service the debt, and a cheaper, focused alternative (niche pivot) offers a safer path.

DECISION: NO-GO
