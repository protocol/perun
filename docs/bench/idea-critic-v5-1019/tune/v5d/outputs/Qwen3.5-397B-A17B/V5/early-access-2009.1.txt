First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a rough state, zero marketing, and no support capacity; it offers no stated edge (e.g., unique mechanic, existing cult following, or pricing advantage) that makes this specific plan beat "finish first" or "free demo" in a 2009 market where unfinished paid games were often review-bombed.

Steelman: Releasing early monetizes development cash-flow and validates demand before over-building, treating buyers as co-designers to ensure feature fit.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" (finish privately) avoids reputation damage from a rough launch. "Free alpha" gathers the same feedback without the "paid product" expectation barrier, mitigating the risk of buyers feeling cheated by incompleteness. The proposal fails to explain why *paid* early access is superior to *free* testing given the stated lack of polish.
2.  **Capacity Mismatch:** The claim relies on buyer feedback to drive features, yet the proposal admits the solo dev "cannot support a large audience." If marketing succeeds, support collapses; if it fails, revenue is zero. There is no middle ground where high feedback volume meets low support capacity without burning out the developer or alienating customers.
3.  **Market Reality (2009):** Without a publisher or budget, visibility is near zero. The primary risk isn't just "buyers judging rough state," but that no one sees it at all, while those who do become detractors due to the paywall on an unfinished product. The "edge" of immediate sales is illusory without an existing audience.

Blockers:
-   **Support Contradiction:** The plan requires high engagement (feedback) but explicitly states inability to support the resulting audience. This operational contradiction guarantees failure if the launch gains traction.
-   **Missing Edge:** No fact is presented showing why customers would pay for an unfinished game over competitors or free demos, especially with zero marketing to reach the niche willing to do so.

Risks: Reputation destruction, burnout from support overload, zero sales due to invisibility.

Kill Criteria: If a free demo does not generate 500+ active testers in week one, do not charge. Leading metric: Ratio of positive feedback to support tickets.

DECISION: NO-GO
