First call: NO-GO. The proposed $700 price point lacks a stated edge over existing consoles priced significantly lower, and the licensing model creates a fatal cost structure conflict in a market where leaders subsidize hardware to drive software sales.

**Steelman:** Licensing accelerates market penetration by leveraging established manufacturing capacity, allowing the start-up to focus on architecture and software royalties while partners capture hardware margins.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "beat do nothing" test against current market dynamics. Established competitors sell hardware at a loss to maximize game attachment rates; this proposal requires licensees to profit on hardware *and* pay royalties, forcing a $700 retail price. Early adopters may tolerate high prices for superior tech, but a fragmented licensing model dilutes marketing focus and quality control compared to integrated rivals. The "edge" of high-end graphics is neutralized if the installed base remains tiny due to cost. A cheaper, integrated path (selling direct or subsidizing one partner) reaches the same upside (software royalties) with better price control.

**Pre-mortem:** The venture fails because the high price limits the installed base, causing third-party game developers to ignore the platform, which further reduces console demand—a death spiral. Licensees, unable to compete on price with subsidized rivals, exit the program.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the price is "far above existing consoles" and competitors "sell hardware at a loss." No evidence suggests consumers will pay a 2-3x premium for graphics alone without a critical mass of games.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Cost Structure:** The requirement for licensee hardware profit + startup royalty makes the $700 price unavoidable, directly contradicting the market standard of subsidized hardware.
2. **Market Fit:** No stated edge proves early adopters will accept this price gap over established competitors.

**Risks:** Fragmented support, developer apathy, price sensitivity.

**Kill Criteria:** If a single integrated manufacturer cannot be found to launch a sub-$400 unit within 12 months, or if pre-orders do not reach 50,000 units at the $700 price point before mass production.

The plan as proposed guarantees a non-competitive price point in a volume-driven market.

DECISION: NO-GO
