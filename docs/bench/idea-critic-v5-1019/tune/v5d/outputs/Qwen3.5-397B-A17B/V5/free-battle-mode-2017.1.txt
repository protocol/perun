First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a market need but fails to state an edge; "reusing assets" is a cost saver, not a product advantage against an incumbent who already owns the genre.

**Steelman:** Reusing assets allows a rapid, low-cost entry to capture players priced out of the rival's ecosystem, converting them via cosmetics.
**Attack:** Speed is an illusion. A "last-player-standing" mode requires netcode, anti-cheat, and matchmaking infrastructure that a paid cooperative game likely lacks. Two months is sufficient for a prototype, not a live-service product capable of competing with a popular rival. Without a functioning economy and cheat prevention at launch, the game will bleed players immediately.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Protect the paid game's value; avoid brand damage from a broken free entry.
2.  **DLC Expansion:** Add competitive elements to the existing paid title. Lower risk, monetizes existing users, avoids building a new F2P economy from scratch.
3.  **Acquisition/Partnership:** License tech rather than building netcode in 8 weeks.

**Fit:** Entering a mature market without a stated differentiation strategy (e.g., unique mechanic, specific niche) contradicts the reality of network effects. The rival's popularity creates a barrier that asset reuse cannot breach.

**Pre-mortem:** Launch occurs in two months. The netcode cannot handle peak load; cheaters dominate lobbies within 48 hours. The cosmetic shop remains empty because the player base churns instantly. The studio burns cash on servers with zero ROI, while the paid game's reputation suffers due to association with a "broken" free title.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the studio has "not run a free-to-play business before." This is a critical capability gap. Building the mode is engineering; running the economy is operations. The 2-month timeline ignores the operational learning curve.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Capability Gap:** No prior F2P experience means no existing economy, analytics, or live-ops pipeline.
2.  **Timeline Fallacy:** 2 months is technically insufficient for a competitive, secure multiplayer debut against an entrenched rival.
3.  **Missing Edge:** No stated reason why players would switch from the popular rival to this new entry.

**Risks:** Cannibalization of paid sales; brand dilution; server costs without revenue.

**Decision:** The lack of a stated competitive edge and the unrealistic timeline for a studio without F2P experience constitute fatal flaws.

DECISION: NO-GO
