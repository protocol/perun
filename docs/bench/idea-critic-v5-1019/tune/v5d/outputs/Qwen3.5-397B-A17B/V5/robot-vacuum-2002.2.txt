First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; early rejection by two domain experts without a cited pivot in strategy or evidence of unmet demand suggests the "low cost" angle is insufficient to overcome the core functional deficit (random vs. mapping) and trust barriers.

**Steelman:** A low-price, randomized robot captures the price-sensitive segment that values "some cleaning" over "perfect cleaning," bypassing the need for complex mapping tech.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The company has zero consumer experience. Entering a trust-sensitive category (home access) with an inferior technical approach (random vs. mapping) contradicts the need for reliability. The rejection by two floor-care companies is a strong negative signal; the proposal offers no new data to invalidate their judgment.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" avoids capital destruction in an unproven channel. Licensing the tech to an existing player is a safer alternative that leverages their trust and distribution, though the proposal ignores this.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The product launches, customers return it because random movement misses spots, and the brand is tainted as a "toy" rather than a tool. The lack of mapping makes it impossible to guarantee value, leading to high churn.
4.  **Blockers:** The core claim relies on price overcoming performance. However, for a vacuum, *cleaning efficacy* is the primary utility. If the device cannot reliably clean (admitted risk), low price is irrelevant. The proposal states buyers "may not trust" the robot; without a pilot or third-party validation, this is a fatal barrier for a home device. The rejection by experts stands unrefuted.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** Yes. The proposal admits the product cannot guarantee cleaning (core function failure) and cites no evidence overcoming the expert rejections or the trust deficit. The "low cost" argument does not neutralize the functional failure in a category where performance is paramount.
*   **Risks:** High execution risk due to lack of consumer experience; brand damage.

The plan fails the basic test of offering a viable solution to the primary user need (clean floors) while ignoring negative market validation.

DECISION: NO-GO
