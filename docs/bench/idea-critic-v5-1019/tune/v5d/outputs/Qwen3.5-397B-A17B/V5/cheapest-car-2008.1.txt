First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "cheapest" is a price point, not a defensible advantage against cheaper scooters and used cars, and the core claim relies on unproven demand for a stigmatized product.

Steelman: A dedicated high-volume factory enables a $2,500 price point that successfully converts scooter families to car owners despite safety concerns and land protests.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High capex, high risk of brand stigma ("cheapest"), and potential delays from farmer protests.
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoids sunk costs in a volatile site and unproven market segment.
3. **Alternative (Used/Scooter Upgrade):** Buyers get more value/safety for similar money; this alternative reaches the mobility upside cheaper and safer.

Fit: Assumes volume justifies a new factory, but ignores that the target demographic prioritizes value over novelty. A "cheap" label may actively repel aspirational buyers.

Pre-mortem: The factory is delayed indefinitely by protests, burning capital. The "cheapest" marketing creates a safety stigma, causing families to stick with scooters or buy used cars. The cost-cutting compromises quality, leading to recalls that destroy the brand before launch.

Evidence: The proposal admits farmers are protesting the site (blocker to timeline), safety doubts exist (blocker to adoption), and cheaper alternatives are available (blocker to value prop). No evidence is offered that buyers *want* this specific car over a used one.

Blockers:
1. **Site Viability:** Active farmer protests threaten the dedicated factory's completion; no fallback site or timeline buffer is stated.
2. **Value Proposition:** Used cars and scooters are explicitly cheaper; the proposal offers no feature advantage to offset the "cheap" stigma.
3. **Safety Perception:** Acknowledged doubts about quality without a stated plan to verify or certify safety standards.

Risks: Brand damage, capital lock-up in a single site, failure to convert scooter users.

The plan fails as proposed because the stated facts show the target users have cheaper, safer alternatives, and the production method faces immediate, unresolved physical opposition. A "cheapest" label without a proven safety record is a liability, not an edge.

DECISION: NO-GO
