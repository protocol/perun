First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real friction (shipping fees) but fails to state an edge: it does not demonstrate that the $79 fee covers the marginal cost of increased order frequency from heavy buyers, nor does it present evidence that current users demand this specific price point over simple fee reduction.

Steelman: An annual prepaid fee locks in loyalty and increases lifetime value by removing per-transaction friction, subsidizing heavy users with light users' unused potential.

Attack:
1. **Economics:** The "heavy buyer" risk is a mathematical certainty, not a risk. If shipping costs $10/order and a member orders 8 times (cost $80) vs. a non-member ordering 4 times (cost $40 + fees), the retailer loses money on the most valuable segment immediately. The $79 cap creates unlimited downside liability per user without a stated volume limit or eligibility exclusion.
2. **Reversibility:** Selling an annual contract creates a hard barrier to exit. If margins collapse, cancelling mid-year destroys trust and invites legal churn; waiting a year to fix it compounds losses. This is a one-way door with unproven economics.
3. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves current margins. A "free shipping over $X" threshold drives basket size without uncapping liability. A lower-cost quarterly trial tests elasticity without annual lock-in.

Pre-mortem: Six months in, oil prices spike, shipping costs rise 15%. Heavy users double frequency to maximize the fixed fee. The program bleeds cash daily. Because customers prepaid, we cannot raise rates or cancel without mass refunds and brand damage. We are trapped in a loss-leading contract we cannot escape.

Evidence: The proposal admits economics are "unproven" and highlights "large short-term margin hit" as a known fact, not a possibility. No data suggests $79 covers the break-even frequency for the target demographic.

Blockers:
1. The proposal explicitly states economics are unproven while admitting a high probability of immediate margin negative outcomes for heavy users.
2. No kill criteria, caps, or pilot structure exists to limit the "unlimited downside" liability per user.

Risks: Customer confusion, operational strain on two-day logistics.

Verdict: The plan commits to unlimited liability based on unproven unit economics with high irreversibility. It fails the basic operator test of knowing the break-even volume before selling the contract.

DECISION: NO-GO
