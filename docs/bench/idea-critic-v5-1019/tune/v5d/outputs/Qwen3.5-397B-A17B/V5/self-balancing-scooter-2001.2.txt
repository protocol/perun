First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; high price, unproven demand, and regulatory ambiguity are disadvantages, not advantages, against existing transport.

**Steelman:** A novel, self-balancing transporter will create a new micro-mobility category for early adopters and institutions, leveraging celebrity backing to overcome initial skepticism.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" costs $0 and avoids regulatory fights. Cheaper alternatives (bicycles, scooters) already solve short-distance travel at 5% of the cost with proven legal status. The proposal fails to explain why a $5,000 device beats free (walking) or cheap (bike) options.
2.  **Fit:** The "conspicuous" risk and limited utility (no cargo) contradict mass adoption. Institutional use is too narrow to justify a 40,000-unit factory immediately.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Regulatory bodies ban sidewalk use due to safety conflicts with pedestrians; the high price limits sales to novelty seekers; the "conspicuous" factor prevents daily commuting adoption. The factory sits idle, burning cash.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits legal status is "unclear," price is "high," and use cases are "unproven." These are not assumptions; they are stated facts that invalidate a mass-market launch. No data supports the 40,000-unit capacity need.
5.  **Blockers:** The stated facts show the target market is undefined and the regulatory path is blocked. Launching at $5,000 with unproven demand and unclear legality is a financial hazard. A cheaper, staged pilot (e.g., limited run, specific zones) is a superior alternative that reaches the same learning upside without the capital risk of a full factory.

**Blockers:**
*   Stated fact: Legal status unclear (regulatory risk is immediate).
*   Stated fact: Price high + unproven use cases (no product-market fit evidence).
*   Alternative: Staged pilot achieves validation cheaper/safer.

**Risks:** Social stigma, limited utility, investor reputation damage.

**Kill Criteria:** None proposed; must add: "Stop if <1,000 units sold in 6 months" or "Pause if any major city bans operation."

The plan commits massive capital to an unproven, legally ambiguous product when a smaller, reversible test is available.

DECISION: NO-GO
