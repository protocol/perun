First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: rivals offer free or publisher-managed online play, while this plan adds friction (mandatory broadband, extra cost) without citing evidence that players value a unified identity enough to pay for it. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: A unified, high-quality network with voice chat and friends lists creates a sticky ecosystem that justifies a subscription fee, differentiating the console from fragmented, free alternatives.

Attack:
1. **Fit & Evidence:** The proposal admits the company has never run hosting/support operations. Building this from scratch while fighting incumbents is a massive execution risk. No evidence is provided that the "few homes with broadband" in 2002 constitute a large enough market to sustain the required infrastructure costs.
2. **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "publisher-managed" approach costs less and matches current user expectations (free). A cheaper alternative is a hybrid model: free basic matchmaking with optional paid premium features, rather than a mandatory gate.
3. **Pre-mortem:** If launched, low broadband adoption and price resistance lead to a tiny active user base. High fixed costs for unused server capacity bleed cash. The console becomes known as "offline-first," hurting game sales.
4. **Blocker:** The core claim relies on users paying for what competitors give free, despite the target demographic having low broadband penetration. The proposal states these negatives as facts ("few homes have broadband," "expect online play to be free") and offers no data showing a willingness to overturn them. The financial model (revenue vs. new operational costs) is unstated and likely negative given the constraints.

Risks: Execution failure in new ops, market rejection of paid model, slow broadband adoption. These are severe, but the blocker is the lack of a stated economic edge overcoming the admitted market realities.

The plan fails the operator test: it enters a price-sensitive market with a premium product, no proven demand for the specific premium features, and zero operational history, while competitors undercut on price.

DECISION: NO-GO
