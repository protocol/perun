First call: NO-GO. The proposal cites a strategic need (catching the search leader) but offers no stated edge explaining why paying a large premium in cash for an agency-plus-platform bundle beats the cheaper, safer alternative of building or licensing the technology alone, especially given the explicit risk of alienating existing agency customers.

Steelman: Acquiring the bundle instantly grants scale and technology to rival the competitor's recent move, preventing permanent market exclusion.

Attack:
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves cash and client trust. A "technology-only" acquisition or build avoids the stated conflict with existing agency customers and likely costs less than $6B. The proposal fails to demonstrate why the agency component is essential to the technical goal.
2.  **Fit & Risk:** The market is "fast-changing." Buying a massive, integrated legacy structure at a peak premium creates high irreversibility (one-way door). If the technology shifts, the company is stuck with an expensive, conflicting agency business that drives away current revenue.
3.  **Evidence Gap:** The proposal admits to "conflicts with existing agency customers" as a known negative but offers no mitigation. This is a self-identified blocker: acquiring a asset that actively damages the core business model without a stated plan to isolate or divest it is financially unsound.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** In 12 months, the integration stalls, agency clients flee to competitors due to conflict of interest, and the ad-tech landscape shifts again, leaving the company with $6B less cash and a fractured partner ecosystem.

Blockers:
-   **Self-contradiction:** The plan aims to help the software company but explicitly includes damaging its existing customer relationships (agencies) with no resolution strategy.
-   **No stated edge:** No fact shows why this specific $6B bundled deal is superior to a targeted tech buy or organic build, given the admitted downsides.

Risks:
-   Overpayment in a volatile market.
-   Integration failure.
-   Loss of channel trust.

The presence of a confirmed, unmitigated conflict with the core business model constitutes a failure of the plan as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
