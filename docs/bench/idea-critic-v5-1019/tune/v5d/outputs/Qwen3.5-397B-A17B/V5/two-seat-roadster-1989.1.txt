First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a market gap but fails to state an edge; need without a stated advantage over existing solutions or a reason why mass-market makers are wrong is insufficient.

Steelman: A mid-size maker can profitably fill the void left by discontinued British roadsters by leveraging existing front-engine, rear-drive components to create an affordable, lightweight convertible that appeals to enthusiasts ignored by mass-market safety bloat.

Attack:
1. **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits the segment has shrunk, safety rules raise costs, and the maker lacks heritage. It offers no fact explaining why this specific company can succeed where others failed or why buyers would choose this over remaining incumbents. "Affordable" is a goal, not a proven capability against rising safety compliance costs.
2. **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital in a shrinking niche. A cheaper alternative is a limited-run special edition on an existing platform to test demand without a full launch; the proposal ignores this staged approach.
3. **Fit:** Entering a declining niche with no heritage contradicts a sound north star of growth or brand strengthening unless the "edge" is proven.
4. **Pre-mortem:** The car launches to initial enthusiast praise, but sales collapse after the early adopter wave because the two-seat limitation and high safety-driven price point alienate the broader market needed to sustain production.
5. **Evidence:** The proposal provides no data on current demand, cost structures, or component reuse rates. The risks (shrinking segment, no heritage) are stated as facts, yet the plan proceeds without addressing them.

Blockers:
- The proposal explicitly lists negative facts (shrinking segment, high costs, no heritage) that remove the route to upside, with no counter-evidence or mechanism to overcome them.
- No stated edge beats the "do nothing" or "wait" alternative.

Risks:
- Sales fade after early enthusiasts buy.
- Safety regulations erode affordability.

The plan fails the operator test: it commits resources to a declining niche without a defined advantage.

DECISION: NO-GO
