First call: NO-GO. The proposal states smokers may dislike the taste and lighting mechanism, and regulators may object to health claims. Need without a stated edge over existing cigarettes is insufficient; "cleaner" is undefined against consumer preference for taste and simplicity.

Steelman: A heated tobacco product offers reduced smoke and a familiar ritual, potentially retaining smokers seeking a "cleaner" option without quitting nicotine.

Attack: The core value proposition collapses if the primary user base rejects the sensory experience. The proposal explicitly admits smokers may dislike the taste and the lighting method. Unlike a pure tech upgrade, a consumable requiring daily sensory acceptance cannot succeed if the experience is inferior, regardless of "cleanliness." Furthermore, promoting it as "cleaner" invites immediate regulatory scrutiny on health claims, a risk the proposal acknowledges but offers no mitigation for beyond "test markets."

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids sunk cost fallacy; preserves capital for products with proven demand.
2. Simplify: Remove the carbon tip complexity to match ordinary cigarette ease-of-use before launch.
3. Niche launch: Target only health-conscious smokers without broad promotion to limit regulatory exposure.

Fit: Assumes the north star is market share retention via harm reduction, but the stated risks suggest the product fails the basic "fit" of user preference.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, users try it once, reject the taste/lighting difficulty, and regulators issue warnings about unproven "cleaner" claims. Sales flatline, and the large development cost is written off. This is a one-way door for brand reputation if health claims are debunked.

Evidence: The proposal itself provides the blockers: potential user rejection of core mechanics (taste/light) and regulatory vulnerability. No data suggests these risks are mitigated.

Blockers:
1. Stated user rejection risk: If smokers dislike the taste/method, the product has no market.
2. Regulatory threat: Promoting "cleaner" without explicit approval invites bans or fines.

Risks: High development cost (sunk), execution complexity.

The plan fails as proposed because it relies on users accepting an inferior experience and regulators accepting unverified claims.

DECISION: NO-GO
