First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on an impossible timeline (five weeks) for a complex product, creating a near-certain quality failure that invalidates the high license investment.

**Steelman:** Rapidly deploying a minimum viable product to capture immediate holiday demand before rights expire or market interest fades, leveraging the film's peak popularity.

**Attack:**
1.  **Feasibility:** A hit film adaptation requires significant asset creation, logic mapping, and testing. Compressing months of work into five weeks with one programmer guarantees a broken or unplayable product. Poor quality will destroy the brand value of both the film and the publisher, making the high license fee a total loss.
2.  **Inventory Risk:** Ordering a "very large production run" based on a rushed, low-quality product is financial suicide. If the game fails (likely due to quality), the company is left with massive unsold inventory and no recourse, compounding the sunk cost of the license.
3.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "delay to next year" option preserves capital. A better way is a small, limited initial run to test market reception, or extending development to ensure quality, even if it misses the immediate Christmas peak. Missing Christmas with a good game is better than hitting Christmas with a broken one.

**Blockers:**
*   **Timeline:** Five weeks is objectively insufficient for a commercial-grade game adaptation, regardless of programmer skill. The plan fails on execution certainty.
*   **Inventory Commitment:** Locking in large-scale manufacturing before verifying product quality creates an uncapped downside risk that contradicts the goal of profit maximization.

**Risks:** Brand damage, total loss of license fee, warehouse overstock.

**Kill Criteria:** If a playable, bug-free prototype cannot be produced in two weeks, the project must be halted or scaled down immediately. Production volume must be tied to verified pre-orders or quality milestones, not optimistic projections.

The proposal demands high capital expenditure and brand risk on a foundation of unrealistic scheduling. It fails the basic test of operational viability.

DECISION: NO-GO
