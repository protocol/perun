First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need but fails to state an edge: it does not explain why this specific form factor beats existing smartphones or dedicated cameras for everyday users, nor does it address how the stated risks (privacy, price, utility) are mitigated rather than just listed.

Steelman: An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to refine the product with tech-savvy users before a mass-market release, turning early adopters into advocates who validate the "everyday" use case through real-world usage.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Avoids reputational damage from privacy backlash and hardware failure; zero cost.
2. **Enterprise-first:** Target specific industrial or medical workflows where the hands-free value is quantifiable and privacy concerns are manageable; higher unit price, lower volume, clearer utility.
3. **Proposed plan:** High risk of public rejection due to undefined utility and immediate social stigma ("Glasshole" effect), potentially poisoning the brand for future wearable attempts.

Fit: Assuming the north star is "organizing the world's information," a device that people refuse to wear due to social or privacy constraints fails to move that needle. The "everyday consumer" positioning contradicts the "invitation-only" scarcity and the lack of a killer app.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, early adopters wear it in public, immediate backlash occurs regarding non-consensual recording, retailers ban the device, and the $1,500 price point prevents sufficient volume to iterate software. The project is killed within 18 months, labeled a social failure.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits "no clear everyday use" and "privacy objections" as known facts. Launching a consumer product with acknowledged fatal flaws without a mitigation strategy is not a plan; it is a gamble. The high price ($1,500 in 2013) limits the user base to those least likely to represent "everyday" needs, skewing feedback.

Blockers: The proposal explicitly states there is "no clear everyday use for most people" yet positions it as an "everyday consumer product." This is an internal contradiction that guarantees market failure. A product cannot solve a problem the target market doesn't have.

Risks: Social rejection, privacy regulation, battery limitations, high return rates.

Kill criteria: None provided; none can be effective without a defined use case.

DECISION: NO-GO
