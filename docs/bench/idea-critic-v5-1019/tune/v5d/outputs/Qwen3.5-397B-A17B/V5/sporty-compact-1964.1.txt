First call: GO. The proposal targets a specific, growing demographic (postwar boom) with a stated edge: sporty styling and customization at a low price point, leveraging existing reliable mechanics to minimize development risk.

**Steelman:** Launch a customized, sporty coupe/convertible using proven compact underpinnings to capture young buyers seeking style over utility, funded by aggressive marketing to create immediate category demand.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the emerging youth segment to competitors. Building a new chassis from scratch destroys the cost advantage. The proposed "parts sharing" is a feature, not a bug, enabling the low entry price required for this demographic.
2.  **Fit:** Aligns with capturing market share via volume and options revenue. The "dull" reputation of the base car is irrelevant if the new body style rebrands the image.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the "cheap parts" perception overwhelms the "sporty" styling, or if the ad spend fails to ignite cultural interest. However, the low base price limits downside exposure per unit.
4.  **Evidence:** The demographic shift is a verified fact. The mechanical reliability is established. The risk of "unproven demand" is mitigated by the low marginal cost of re-bodying existing units and the high-margin options list.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented proves the plan fails as proposed. The risks cited (unproven demand, cheap perception, ad spend) are execution risks, not structural blockers. They are managed by the low base cost and the potential for high option attach rates.

**Risks:** Market rejection of the aesthetic; inability to shake the "economy" stigma. These become kill criteria: if initial sales do not meet a specific threshold within the first quarter, or if option uptake is below 20%, the campaign must pivot or cease.

**Decision Method:** The plan beats "do nothing" by addressing a verified demographic need with a cost-effective solution. The "cheap parts" objection is neutralized by the styling proposal. The ad spend is the cost of entry for a new category.

DECISION: GO
