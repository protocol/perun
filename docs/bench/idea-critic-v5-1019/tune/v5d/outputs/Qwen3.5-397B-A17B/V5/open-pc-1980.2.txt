First call: GO. The stated edge is speed and channel access: using off-the-shelf parts and independent retailers allows entry into a fast-growing market within a year, a timeline impossible with vertical integration.

**Steelman:** A segregated team bypassing corporate norms can capture the emerging personal computer market faster than rivals by leveraging existing supply chains and retail networks, accepting lower margins for volume and strategic foothold.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary objection is that standard parts invite copying and erode margins. However, in a nascent market, speed to volume creates a de facto standard that copycats chase. "Do nothing" cedes the small-computer growth trajectory to agile competitors, threatening long-term relevance. Building custom components delays entry, likely missing the window entirely. The risk of supplier dependence is mitigated by the commoditization of those very parts; multiple sources will emerge as the market grows.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This contradicts the current "integrated control" north star but aligns with the survival imperative of market expansion. If this fails, it will be because the mainframe culture suffocates the satellite team or because the company retreats at the first sign of lower margins. The cost of delay is total exclusion from the personal computing era.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*Blockers:* NONE. The proposal explicitly accounts for the cultural clash (small separate team) and the channel gap (independent stores). The financial model accepts lower margins as a trade-off, not an oversight.
*Risks:* Copycats, margin compression, and cultural friction are real but do not invalidate the core logic; they are execution hazards managed by the proposed structural separation.

**Decision:**
The proposal survives the attack because the alternative (slow, integrated development) guarantees failure in a speed-driven market. The structural separation is the critical enabler that neutralizes the company's usual inertia. Proceed immediately with the segregated team model.

DECISION: GO
