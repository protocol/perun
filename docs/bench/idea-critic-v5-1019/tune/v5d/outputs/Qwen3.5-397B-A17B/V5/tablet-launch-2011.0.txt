First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; matching the leader's price with a fraction of the apps and zero OS share offers no reason for buyers to switch.

Steelman: Leverage the computer maker's brand and retail channels to force entry, betting that heavy ad spend and developer incentives will rapidly close the app gap before the market solidifies.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High cost, extreme risk (ecosystem failure), low reversibility (brand damage).
2. **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, risk of missing the tablet wave, fully reversible later.
3. **Low-Price/Niche:** Lower price to offset app deficit, target specific verticals, moderate risk, reversible.
The proposed plan does not beat "do nothing" on stated facts; it burns capital without a unique value proposition.

Fit: Assumes the goal is immediate market share, but launching an unproven OS at a premium price contradicts basic adoption dynamics.

Pre-mortem: The tablet fails because users refuse to pay a premium for a "ghost town" app store. Developers ignore outreach because the installed base remains tiny. The company is stuck with unsold inventory and a damaged brand during leadership transition. This is a one-way door for reputation; delaying avoids locking in a losing position.

Evidence: The proposal admits the app catalog is a "small fraction" and OS share is "almost none." No evidence suggests shoppers value the maker's brand enough to overcome these deficits at a parity price.

Blockers:
1. **No Stated Edge:** Pricing parity with a dominant leader while offering inferior utility (fewer apps) guarantees rejection. A real need (tablets) is not an edge without a differentiator.
2. **Economic Contradiction:** Heavy investment in ads/outreach cannot instantly manufacture an ecosystem; the "chicken and egg" problem remains unsolved by the plan's own metrics.

Risks: Leadership transition instability, developer apathy, consumer perception of the product as "unproven."

Conclusion: The plan commits significant capital to a strategy that fails the basic test of value exchange. Without a price advantage, exclusive content, or a killer feature, this is a speculative burn, not a product launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
