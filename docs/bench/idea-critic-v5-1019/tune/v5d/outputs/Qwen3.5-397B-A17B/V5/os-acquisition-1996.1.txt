First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge: acquiring a low-revenue OS does not inherently beat existing licensing options or internal rebuilds given the migration risks and high price tag.

Steelman: Buying this specific asset is the only viable path to instantly secure a modern, object-oriented core that internal teams failed to build, while neutralizing a potential competitor and gaining unique architectural leverage unavailable via licensing.

Options:
1. **Buy:** High cost, high integration risk, unique IP ownership, resolves "not invented here" failure.
2. **License:** Lower upfront cost, continued dependency, no control over roadmap, fails to solve strategic autonomy.
3. **Do Nothing:** Continued market share erosion, eventual platform obsolescence.

Fit: Assumes the company's survival depends on a differentiated, owned platform rather than commodity licensing. A sound idea must move the north star of "platform ownership"; if licensing achieves parity, this buy is unjustified.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition fails because the technical debt of merging the new core with legacy hardware drivers proves insurmountable, or the founder's return triggers internal cultural collapse, stalling the launch window. This is a one-way door; delay costs another quarter of bleeding market share.

Evidence: The proposal states internal failure twice and falling share. The target has few sales, implying the value is technology, not revenue. The price is "high against small revenue," which is a blocker unless the technology's strategic value (the edge) is proven to exceed the cost of licensing or further internal failure. The proposal does not demonstrate why licensing is insufficient to stop the bleeding immediately.

Blockers:
1. **No stated edge over licensing:** The proposal fails to explain why licensing another vendor's system cannot achieve the same "modern core" outcome at a lower cost and risk, given the target's own commercial failure.
2. **Unproven migration viability:** The risk of migrating existing software is acknowledged but not mitigated; without evidence that this specific OS core is easier to migrate than a licensed alternative, the high price is unjustified.

Risks: Founder friction, integration complexity, cultural clash.

The plan commits real money without proving the acquired tech is superior to the cheaper licensing alternative. The lack of a defined advantage over the "license" option is a fatal flaw in the logic.

DECISION: NO-GO
