First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a capital-intensive path without a stated edge over incumbents, relying on assumptions that contradict the 2008 market reality.

**Steelman:** A dedicated EV-only strategy with a repurposed large factory creates a first-mover advantage in premium electric sedans, leveraging existing infrastructure to accelerate scale.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capital Mismatch:** The proposal admits the company lacks capital for the factory and batteries, yet the plan *requires* buying a large factory. In 2008, credit markets were freezing; financing a massive fixed-asset purchase for an unproven volume product is financially impossible without a stated funding source.
2.  **Infrastructure Gap:** Charging scarcity in 2008 is a hard blocker for mass-market adoption of a full-size sedan, which requires reliable long-distance capability. The proposal offers no solution to this dependency.
3.  **Production Risk:** Buying a large factory does not solve the "little experience with mass production" risk; it amplifies it by adding complex facility management to the learning curve. A smaller, pilot-scale approach would be safer.
4.  **No Stated Edge:** The plan beats neither "do nothing" nor a phased approach. Incumbents have capital, supply chains, and brand trust. A new entrant buying a large factory has none of these and high fixed costs.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve cash, wait for battery costs to drop and charging networks to expand.
*   **Phased Pilot:** Build a limited run in a leased facility to prove manufacturing competence before committing to a large factory purchase.
*   **Partnership:** License the design to an established maker to share production risk.

**Pre-mortem:** The company buys the factory, burns cash on retooling, fails to secure battery supply at scale, and cannot sell enough units due to charging anxiety, leading to insolvency before the first profitable quarter.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Company lacks capital for the stated requirements (factory + batteries).
*   **Fact:** 2008 charging infrastructure is insufficient for the target product.
*   **Fact:** No stated competitive edge to overcome incumbent advantages.

**Risks:** Execution inexperience, incumbent response, capital market freeze.

The proposal fails the basic test of feasibility given the stated constraints. It commits to a high-fixed-cost model without the means to fund it or the market conditions to support it.

DECISION: NO-GO
