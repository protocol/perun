First call: NO-GO. Doubling production while sales lag by 60%, during a recession with rising interest rates and limited cash, lacks any stated edge; it accelerates cash burn without solving demand.

Steelman: The only defensible version of this claim is that massive overproduction is required to achieve unit-cost reductions so drastic that they allow price cuts capable of stimulating demand faster than inventory accumulates, effectively betting the company's remaining cash on a volume-based turnaround before insolvency.

Attack: This fails the blocker test immediately. The proposal ignores the binding constraint: liquidity. With 4,500 unsold units and rising interest rates, financing costs for both the company and its dealers are increasing. Doubling output requires upfront cash for materials and labor that the company explicitly lacks. Furthermore, dealers cannot finance more stock in a high-rate environment. This plan does not beat "do nothing" or "reduce production"; it guarantees earlier bankruptcy by converting scarce cash into depreciating inventory that cannot be sold.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing/Reduce production: Preserves cash, aligns output with demand.
2. Cut prices on existing stock: Clears inventory, generates cash flow without new capital expenditure.
3. Proposed plan: Maximizes cash outflow while demand is at a cyclical low.

Pre-mortem: The company runs out of cash in six months. Warehouses are full of 15,000 unsold cars. Interest payments on inventory loans default. The company enters receivership because it bet on supply-side efficiency in a demand-side collapse.

Blockers:
1. Limited cash vs. increased production costs: The math shows immediate insolvency.
2. Dealer financing constraints: High interest rates prevent channel absorption of new stock.
3. No stated edge: Volume alone does not create demand in a recession.

Risks: Total capital loss, brand destruction, creditor lawsuits.

The proposal commits to a capital-intensive expansion exactly when capital is most expensive and demand is weakest. It solves a cost problem the company may not have while creating a liquidity problem that will definitely kill it.

DECISION: NO-GO
