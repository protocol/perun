First call: NO-GO. The proposal identifies a real need (business features) but fails to state a strategic edge explaining why this specific complex redesign will succeed where the current simple machine dominates, especially given the explicit risk of alienating the existing user base and software ecosystem.

**Steelman:** A timely, reliable successor with lowercase and 80-column display is essential to capture the business market before competitors, leveraging the brand's momentum despite transition risks.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "incremental upgrade" options are ignored. Adding a display controller or keyboard mapping to the existing architecture might solve 80% of the problem with 20% of the complexity and risk.
2.  **Fit & Risk:** Phasing out the only successful product before the new one is proven is a one-way door that risks total revenue collapse if reliability fails or adoption lags. The "tight schedule" combined with "unproven reliability" for a "complex design" is a classic failure triad.
3.  **Evidence Gap:** There is no evidence that business users will switch if it breaks compatibility with existing software. The proposal assumes feature parity equals migration, ignoring ecosystem lock-in.

**Blockers:**
*   **Self-Contradiction:** The plan aims to phase out the revenue-generating product while admitting the new hardware's reliability is "unproven" and the schedule is "tight." Launching an unproven, complex machine to replace a cash cow is financially reckless without a pilot.
*   **Missing Edge:** No argument is made for why a full redesign is necessary over an accessory or minor revision, nor how to overcome software incompatibility.

**Risks:** Ecosystem revolt, revenue gap during transition, hardware failures damaging brand reputation.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal demands a high-stakes, irreversible bet (killing the current product) based on unproven technology and a tight schedule, without a staged approach or compatibility bridge. The absence of a "go-if" condition (e.g., "if reliability testing passes by Q3") or a capped pilot makes this a blind leap. The risks are not just theoretical; they are stated as probable hurdles in the text itself. A sound operator would demand a proven prototype and a compatibility layer before approving a phase-out.

DECISION: NO-GO
