First call: NO-GO. The proposal states a need (inexpensive watch) but offers no stated edge explaining why this specific plastic fashion collection beats existing low-cost competitors or protects the brand's quality reputation.

**Steelman:** Launching 12 fashion-focused plastic models leverages trend agility to capture volume, distinguishing the product from plain timekeepers to justify the 1M unit target despite brand risks.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Edge:** The plan relies on "fashion" to offset "plastic," yet fashion tastes are explicitly cited as a volatile risk. Without a stated distribution or marketing edge to move 1M units rapidly before trends shift, the inventory risk is catastrophic. The proposal admits the brand is known for "quality"; launching "inexpensive plastic" without a distinct sub-brand strategy or clear separation mechanism directly contradicts the core asset, validating the reputation harm risk.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves brand equity. A "cheaper alternative" is a limited pilot (e.g., 2 models, one region) to test the fashion hypothesis and reputation impact before committing to 12 models and a 1M unit supply chain. The current plan skips validation.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: The market perceives the brand as "cheap," not "fashionable." Established brands cut prices, undercutting the margin. The 1M target forces deep discounting, cementing the low-quality perception. The company cannot exit the plastic segment without long-term brand damage.
4.  **Evidence:** The sales target (1M) is aggressive with no cited capacity analysis or pre-orders. The risk of reputation harm is stated as a fact by the proposers themselves, yet the plan offers no mitigation other than hope that "fashion" overrides "plastic."

**Blockers:**
*   **Reputation Contradiction:** The proposal admits the watch is "inexpensive plastic" and the brand stands for "quality," yet offers no structural separation (sub-brand, distinct channel) to prevent brand erosion. This is a self-contradictory core claim.
*   **Unvalidated Scale:** A 1M unit commitment for a volatile fashion product with no pilot or proven demand mechanism is a financial blocker.

**Risks:** Trend misalignment, price wars, inventory bloat. These are secondary to the blockers.

The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test because the stated risks (reputation harm, fashion volatility) are unmitigated and the scale is unjustified by the stated facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
