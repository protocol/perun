First call: NO-GO. The proposal removes a universal, reliable standard (the headphone jack) to force adoption of an unproven, expensive ($159), and functionally inferior (5-hour battery, easy to lose) alternative without a stated edge that compels purchase over existing wired or third-party wireless options.

**Steelman:** Removing the jack accelerates a wireless future by forcing ecosystem lock-in via a custom chip, betting that convenience outweighs the loss of universal compatibility and higher cost.

**Attack:**
1.  **No Stated Edge:** The proposal admits the product looks "odd," costs significantly more than the replaced item (free wired), and has limited battery life. There is no evidence provided that customers *want* this specific trade-off. Need (wireless audio) is not an edge; competitors already offer wireless without removing the jack.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" (keep the jack, sell earbuds optionally) preserves customer goodwill while testing the market. A cheaper, dongle-based transition is safer and reversible.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The product launches, is ridiculed for its appearance and price, and customers revolt over the lost jack, leading to a sales dip for the phone itself. The "custom chip" creates a walled garden that alienates users with non-proprietary headphones.
4.  **Evidence Gap:** The claim that automatic pairing justifies $159 and jack removal is `assumed`. No data shows users value this enough to accept the downsides.

**Blockers:**
*   **Negative Value Proposition:** The proposal explicitly states the replacement is more expensive, less reliable (battery/loss), and aesthetically risky compared to the status quo. This contradicts the core requirement of delivering value.
*   **Missing Demand Evidence:** The plan assumes removing the jack drives earbud sales, but offers no proof users desire this specific form factor at this price point.

**Risks:** Customer anger, ridicule, high return rates, and brand damage are listed as known risks, not hypotheticals. Proceeding without a pilot or lower-cost entry point ignores these verified negatives.

**Decision:** The plan fails the basic operator test: it removes a working feature to sell a worse, more expensive alternative based on an unproven assumption. It must be revised to include a transitional option (e.g., dongle) or validated with customer willingness-to-pay data before proceeding.

DECISION: NO-GO
