First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; buying a smaller, unprofitable entity with massive legal liability for 10x its likely annual revenue (implied by "no meaningful revenue" vs. $1.65B) fails basic operator logic without a proven monetization or legal shield.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant traffic source instantly solves distribution scale, betting that legal risks are manageable and ad models will mature faster than organic growth.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financials:** $1.65B for "no meaningful revenue" is speculative insanity. The bandwidth costs alone could bankrupt the combined entity before ad rates stabilize.
2.  **Legal:** The core asset (user uploads) is primarily infringing content. Media companies have already threatened lawsuits. Buying the target transfers immediate, catastrophic liability to the buyer, potentially resulting in statutory damages exceeding the purchase price.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves cash. Building the existing smaller service avoids inheriting active litigation. Licensing content legally is safer than buying a lawsuit factory.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** One year post-acquisition, media conglomerates win injunctions forcing the removal of all popular content. Traffic collapses, bandwidth costs remain high, and the stock price tanks due to the dilution and legal losses.
5.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no meaningful revenue" and "threatened lawsuits." These are not risks; they are current, verified facts that destroy the business case. There is no evidence of a path to profitability that outweighs the certain legal costs.

**Blockers:**
*   **Liability:** The target's primary value proposition (copyrighted clips) is illegal. Buying it guarantees immediate, high-stakes litigation with no defensive moat stated.
*   **Unit Economics:** High bandwidth + zero revenue + massive purchase price = negative cash flow with no defined turn-around mechanism.

**Risks:** Ad model failure, integration chaos, cultural clash (small staff vs. giant).

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It purchases confirmed liabilities without a verified mechanism to monetize them or defeat the legal threats. The "edge" is purely traffic volume, which is worthless if the content must be removed by court order.

DECISION: NO-GO
