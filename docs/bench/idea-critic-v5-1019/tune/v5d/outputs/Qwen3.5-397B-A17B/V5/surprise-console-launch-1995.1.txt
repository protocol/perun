First call: NO-GO. A hardware launch without software, developer alignment, or broad retail distribution fails the basic requirement of a consumer platform: something to play, somewhere to buy it, at a price customers accept.

Steelman: Launching early in May 1995 secures a "first-to-market" narrative and four months of sales before the competitor arrives, potentially capturing early adopters despite high costs and limited stock.

Attack:
1.  **Software Gap:** A console is useless without games. Shipping with "few games ready" while developers are unprepared for the early date guarantees a barren library. Early adopters will buy, play the scant titles quickly, and demand refunds or lose interest before the competitor even launches.
2.  **Retail Alienation:** Shipping only to "a few retailers" explicitly punishes the broader channel. Excluded retailers, facing a $399 price point ($100 higher than the rival), have zero incentive to support this hardware later. They will likely champion the competitor to retaliate, effectively blocking distribution when mass adoption is needed.
3.  **Developer Hostility:** Forcing an early date on unprepared developers damages long-term relationships. If the hardware is already "hard to program for 3D," rushing the timeline increases the likelihood of poor-quality launch titles, further tarnishing the brand's reputation for quality.
4.  **Price Sensitivity:** At $399 versus the rival's $299, the value proposition relies entirely on exclusive availability. Without a robust game library to justify the premium, the price becomes a barrier rather than a signal of quality.

Pre-mortem: By September, the console is labeled a "expensive failure" with no games and angry retailers. The competitor launches to a full library and wide distribution, capturing the holiday season effortlessly. The early launch burned cash and goodwill without establishing a foothold.

Blockers:
-   **No Software:** The core product (games) is missing; hardware alone cannot sustain a platform launch.
-   **Channel Sabotage:** The strategy actively creates enemies in the retail channel required for future success.
-   **Developer Misalignment:** The timeline contradicts the reality of the development cycle, ensuring a weak software lineup.

Risks: Brand damage, financial loss from unsold inventory, and ceding the holiday market to the competitor.

DECISION: NO-GO
