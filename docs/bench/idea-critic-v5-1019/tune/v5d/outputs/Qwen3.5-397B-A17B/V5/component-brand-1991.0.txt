First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; "unprecedented" indicates no proof that consumers care about invisible components, and need alone is not an advantage.

Steelman: Branding an invisible component forces downstream manufacturers to differentiate on quality, creating a pull-market that locks out clones.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Maintain status quo; clones erode margins slowly. Cost: Low. Risk: Commoditization.
2. Technical lock-in: Change socket designs to block clones. Cost: High engineering. Risk: Alienating partners.
3. Proposed campaign: High cost, unproven consumer demand, potential partner resentment.

Fit: Assumes consumers can be educated to value a part they cannot see. This contradicts the stated fact that buyers currently do not know or care which chip they have.

Pre-mortem: The campaign launches, spending 30% of the budget. Consumers ignore the logo because performance differences are negligible to them. Computer makers resent the ad restrictions and quietly favor cheaper clone suppliers who don't dictate creative. Sales remain flat; the budget is wasted. This is a one-way door for budget allocation; once spent, it cannot be recovered to fund R&D.

Evidence: The proposal admits consumer advertising for components is "unprecedented," meaning there is no historical data to support ROI. The risk of manufacturer resentment is explicit and unmitigated. The claim that buyers will "ask by name" is an assumption with no supporting evidence in the text.

Blockers:
1. No stated edge: The proposal offers no fact proving consumers will value this specific differentiation over price or brand of the computer itself.
2. Contradictory premise: It relies on changing buyer behavior that is explicitly described as non-existent ("buyers do not know").
3. Unverified ROI: A large budget share is committed to an unproven channel with no pilot or metric defined.

Risks: Partner revolt, budget waste, brand dilution if the campaign fails.

The plan commits significant resources based on an unproven hypothesis about consumer behavior without a pilot, cap, or kill criteria. It fails to beat "do nothing" on stated facts because the core mechanism (consumer pull) has no evidence of viability.

DECISION: NO-GO
