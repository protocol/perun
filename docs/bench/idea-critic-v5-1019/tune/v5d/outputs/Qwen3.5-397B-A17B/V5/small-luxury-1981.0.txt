First call: NO-GO. The proposal lacks a stated edge; sharing a mass-market platform while charging a premium price against established rivals offers no inherent advantage in cost, performance, or exclusivity that beats current buyer options. Need alone is not an edge.

Steelman: A compact entry leverages existing engineering to capture price-sensitive buyers who desire the brand's status but cannot afford its flagship models, creating a high-margin volume stream without developing a new chassis.

Attack: The core claim fails on the "luxury" definition. Luxury buyers pay for exclusivity and distinctiveness, not "added trim" on a common foundation. If the platform is visibly shared with mass-market cars, the premium price becomes indefensible, and the brand's prestige suffers via dilution—the very risk the proposal admits. Salespeople refusing to use the main brand name signals internal recognition that the product damages the core asset. Without a unique driving dynamic, technology, or design language that separates it from the parent's mass-market version, this is a badge-engineering exercise that cannibalizes brand equity for short-term volume.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve brand purity and wait for a dedicated small-car platform.
2. Acquire or partner with an existing small premium maker: Instant credibility without platform contamination.
3. Sub-brand strategy: Create a entirely separate dealership network and identity (not just a model name) to isolate the mass-market association.

Pre-mortem: The car launches, critics pan the "glorified economy car" feel, loyalists feel betrayed by the diluted image, and sales stall because the target demographic still perceives it as too expensive for what it is, while the core luxury market migrates to competitors who maintained exclusivity.

Blockers:
- No stated edge: The proposal admits the platform is shared and the price is premium, offering no fact-based reason why this specific combination beats rivals' dedicated small sedans.
- Self-contradiction: The risk that "salespeople will not call it by the main brand" confirms the product fails the primary value proposition (the brand itself).

Risks: Brand dilution, channel conflict, margin compression.

The plan relies on buyers ignoring the mass-market roots despite the sales force's own hesitation. Without a dedicated platform or a completely firewalled sub-brand structure, the prestige damage is a near-certainty that outweighs the potential volume gain.

DECISION: NO-GO
