First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and market trajectory described make commercial failure the base case, not a risk.

**Steelman:** This is the only viable solution for true global coverage where terrestrial networks cannot reach, capturing a high-margin niche of urgent business communication.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Obsolescence:** The proposal admits ground cellular networks are "spreading fast." The target demographic (international business travelers) operates primarily in urban centers and airports where terrestrial coverage is expanding fastest. The value proposition shrinks daily.
2.  **Unit Economics Failure:** At $3,000 per handset and several dollars per minute, the Total Addressable Market (TAM) is microscopic. With $5 billion in debt, the service requires massive, immediate scale to service interest. The high price point physically prevents the necessary volume.
3.  **Product Deficit:** Bulky handsets that fail indoors render the service unreliable for the very executives it targets. If it doesn't work in a conference room or a taxi, it is a novelty, not a utility.
4.  **Go-to-Market Gap:** Sales channels are not ready. You cannot launch a luxury-critical infrastructure product without a distribution engine, especially when competing against subsidized terrestrial phones.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing/Wait:** Let terrestrial networks mature; lease capacity later if a specific gap remains.
*   **Pivot to Niche:** Target only maritime, aviation, or disaster relief (government/enterprise contracts) rather than expensive consumer handsets.
*   **Delay Launch:** Wait for handset miniaturization and cost reduction, even if it means restructuring debt.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-launch, the company files for bankruptcy. Subscriber counts are 1% of projections because business travelers refused to carry a brick that costs $3,000 and doesn't work inside buildings. Terrestrial roaming agreements solved the coverage gap cheaper.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial:** Debt service requirements exceed the revenue potential of the addressable market at the proposed price point.
*   **Product:** Indoor signal failure contradicts the core promise of reliable business communication.
*   **Strategic:** The rapid expansion of the cheaper, superior alternative (ground cellular) invalidates the long-term moat.

This is a one-way door with a high probability of total capital loss. The risks are not merely "execution difficulties"; they are fundamental contradictions between the cost structure, product capabilities, and market reality.

DECISION: NO-GO
