First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a hardware manufacturer with zero platform experience to enter a duopoly dominated by incumbents with loyal user bases and first-party software, while simultaneously fighting a direct 3D competitor and accepting negative hardware margins.

Steelman: A CD-based console can disrupt the market by offering cheaper manufacturing and easier development tools to third parties, bypassing the cartridge bottleneck.

Attack: The "easier tools" advantage is theoretical; incumbents can replicate developer support or leverage existing relationships. The "cheaper discs" benefit is nullified if the hardware sells at a loss and the install base remains small due to a lack of killer apps. Without first-party titles to drive initial adoption, third-party developers will not commit to an unproven platform, creating a death spiral. The concurrent launch of a rival 3D system from an incumbent splits the emerging 3D market, doubling the risk.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoids massive R&D and marketing spend with high probability of failure.
2. Partner with existing software houses: Secure exclusive titles before announcing hardware (not proposed).
3. Proceed as planned: High capital burn, low probability of achieving critical mass against two entrenched rivals.

Fit: This diverges from the company's core competency in consumer electronics manufacturing and enters a software-driven ecosystem war without the necessary content assets.

Pre-mortem: The console launches, sells poorly due to a weak game library, developers abandon the platform for incumbents, and the division incurs hundreds of millions in losses. This is a one-way door with irreversible financial damage.

Evidence: The proposal admits no experience as a platform holder, no owned IP, and thin/negative margins. These are fatal structural flaws, not mere risks. The market dynamics (duopoly, loyal customers) favor incumbents.

Blockers: 
1. No first-party software to drive hardware sales (stated fact).
2. Direct competition from an incumbent's 3D launch (stated fact).
3. Negative hardware margins without a guaranteed install base (stated fact).

Risks: Developer apathy, brand dilution, inventory write-downs.

The absence of a content strategy or a path to profitability despite negative margins makes this a failed proposition as stated.

DECISION: NO-GO
