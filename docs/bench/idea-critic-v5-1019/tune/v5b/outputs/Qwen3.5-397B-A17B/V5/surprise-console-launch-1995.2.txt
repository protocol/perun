First call: NO-GO. Launching early with high price, scarce software, and alienated retailers contradicts the core requirements for a successful console debut.

**Steelman:** An early May launch at $399 secures a "first-mover" mindshare advantage before the competitor's September arrival, allowing the brand to define the 32-bit generation despite limited SKUs.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing (September Launch):** Matches the competitor, allows full game library readiness, ensures nationwide retailer participation, and likely enables a competitive $299 price point. Risk: Losing the "first" headline.
2.  **Proposed Plan (May Launch):** Costs $100 more than the rival, offers few games (the primary purchase driver for consoles), excludes major retailers (inviting retaliation and limiting reach), and frustrates developers.
3.  **Fit:** A console's north star is mass adoption driven by software availability and price parity. This plan fails both. The "first mover" benefit is negligible if the product is overpriced and lacks games; consumers will wait for the cheaper, better-stocked rival.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** By Christmas 1995, the system is labeled a "failed expensive niche" with no software pipeline. Retailers prioritize the competitor. Developers abandon the hard-to-program architecture for the rival's stable timeline. The brand reputation is damaged before the real race begins.
5.  **Evidence:** Historical market dynamics show hardware without software fails. A $100 premium (33% higher) is insurmountable without a killer app library, which explicitly does not exist yet.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price:** $399 vs. rival's $299 is a fatal disadvantage for a new entrant with no established loyalty.
*   **Software:** "Few games ready" violates the fundamental console business model; hardware is a loss leader for software sales.
*   **Distribution:** Alienating retailers ensures poor shelf placement and active sabotage by sales staff favoring the competitor.

**Risks:** Developer abandonment, brand tarnish, inventory stagnation. These are not merely risks; given the facts, they are certainties under this plan. The proposal sacrifices the critical success factors (price, games, distribution) for a hollow marketing win.

DECISION: NO-GO
