**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Safe, but cedes the open-top niche entirely and misses a potential high-margin halo effect.
*   **Alternative (Heavy GT):** Higher cost, higher risk, directly competes with established luxury brands. Fails the "affordable" constraint.
*   **Fit:** Assumes the north star is brand revitalization and margin improvement via low-volume, high-visibility products.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if safety compliance destroys the cost base or if the "enthusiast" base is too small to sustain production lines.
*   **Evidence:** The proposal states mass-makers view the segment as dead, implying low competition. However, it admits the segment is shrinking.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The lack of heritage is a marketing hurdle, not a factual blocker to production. Shrinking sales are a trend, not an immediate stopper if volume targets are low.
*   **Risks:** Regulatory costs eroding margins; limited total addressable market; novelty wear-off.

**Kill Criteria Check:**
The risks concern core demand and regulatory feasibility. However, the proposal's "lightweight" and "affordable" constraints inherently mitigate the cost risk. The "shrinking segment" is the primary threat, but a small player can survive where mass-makers cannot due to lower break-even points. No fact provided proves the unit economics fail immediately. The safety risk is manageable via design, not a show-stopper.

**Decision:**
The plan beats "do nothing" by capturing a monopoly in a neglected niche with low entry barriers. The risks are executional, not fatal based on the provided facts. The lack of heritage is irrelevant to the mechanical feasibility of a simple roadster.
