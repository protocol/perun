First call: NO-GO. Launching a premium-priced tablet with a tiny app catalog against a dominant incumbent, while the OS has negligible share and leadership is unstable, ignores the primary driver of tablet adoption: ecosystem depth.

**Steelman:** The brand's loyalty and heavy marketing can overcome the app gap temporarily, securing enough early adopters to attract developers before the market solidifies.

**Attack:**
1.  **Ecosystem Moat:** Tablets are platform plays, not just hardware. Users buy apps, not just devices. A "small fraction" of the leader's catalog means missing critical "must-have" applications. At a premium price, consumers have zero incentive to tolerate this deficit when a superior alternative exists at the same cost.
2.  **Developer Chicken-and-Egg:** Heavy investment in outreach cannot instantly manufacture a viable ecosystem. Developers follow users; users follow apps. Without a unique hardware advantage or a significantly lower price to de-risk the switch for users, developer interest will remain tepid despite outreach spend.
3.  **Leadership & Timing:** Leadership transition introduces strategic drift exactly when ruthless execution and long-term commitment are required to fight an entrenched monopoly. The "unproven" perception combined with internal instability signals high risk to both consumers and partners.
4.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" option preserves capital. A "cheaper path" would be licensing the OS to multiple hardware makers to build share first, or pricing aggressively below the leader to force trial. The current plan pays a premium price for a sub-par experience.

**Blockers:**
*   **Value Mismatch:** Pricing "in line with the market leader" while offering a fraction of the value (apps) creates an immediate economic disincentive for adoption.
*   **Strategic Instability:** Leadership transition during a monopoly assault is a fatal timing flaw; the competitor will exploit this uncertainty.

**Risks:**
*   Developer apathy despite spending.
*   Brand damage from a failed premium launch.

**Kill Criteria:** If the app catalog does not reach 40% of the leader's key categories within 6 months, or if market share fails to hit 5% in year one, the project must pivot to a low-cost strategy or cease hardware production.

DECISION: NO-GO
