First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines a premium price with a fragmented ecosystem and a gimmicky differentiator in a saturated two-platform market, a recipe for commercial failure.

**Steelman:** A device leveraging unique 3D hardware and shopping integration can carve a niche among loyal customers despite the app gap.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Value:** The core value proposition (shopping) does not justify a flagship price when the device lacks mainstream apps. Users pay for ecosystem utility, not just hardware novelty. A "smaller app store" is a fatal disadvantage at the high end.
2.  **Alternatives:** A low-priced tablet succeeded by removing friction; this phone adds friction (exclusive carrier, no apps, high cost). The "do nothing" or "low-cost niche phone" alternative offers better risk-adjusted returns.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The device launches to initial curiosity but fails to retain users due to daily app unavailability. The 3D feature is viewed as a novelty, not a utility. Carrier exclusivity caps total addressable market immediately. Inventory piles up within two quarters.
4.  **Evidence:** The market is dominated by two platforms; history shows third OS attempts fail without massive ecosystem parity or radical price disruption. This proposal has neither.
5.  **Blocker:** The combination of "unsubsidised-equivalent price in line with leading flagship phones" and "without the leading platform's own mainstream apps" is a structural contradiction. Premium pricing requires premium ecosystem completeness. The proposal's own facts confirm the app gap and saturation.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The risk of "app gap" concerns the core claim (that users will buy). The facts support this will happen (dominance of two platforms). The commitment (manufacturing, carrier deal) is large and irreversible. This is a blocker.

**Decision:**
Blockers: Premium price paired with incomplete app ecosystem contradicts market reality for flagship adoption.
Risks: Carrier exclusivity limits reach; 3D feature may be perceived as gimmick.

The plan commits to a high-cost structure without the necessary ecosystem foundation to support it. It bets on a hardware novelty overcoming a software deficit at a price point where consumers are least forgiving of missing features.

DECISION: NO-GO
