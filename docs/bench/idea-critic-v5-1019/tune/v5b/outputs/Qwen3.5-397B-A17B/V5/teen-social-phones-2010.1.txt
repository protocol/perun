First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts basic market mechanics for the stated demographic.

**Steelman:** Launching a carrier-locked, data-plan-dependent social device without apps or games will succeed by leveraging the acquired team's niche appeal despite high costs and internal friction.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economic Mismatch:** Targeting price-sensitive youth with a device requiring a full smartphone data plan (costing as much as the hardware) creates a prohibitive total cost of ownership. This ignores the primary constraint of the buyer.
2.  **Value Deficit:** A "social phone" in 2010 without an app store or games offers significantly less utility than a standard smartphone launching later that year by the same company. It asks users to pay a premium for a restricted experience.
3.  **Cannibalization & Timing:** Launching this limited device shortly before the main platform creates internal competition and confuses the market, likely depressing sales of the upcoming flagship.
4.  **Execution Risk:** Known internal conflict between teams, combined with a recent underlying software switch that already caused delays, suggests high probability of further slippage or quality issues.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids capital waste and brand confusion; preserves focus for the main platform launch.
*   **Integrate Features:** Port the social features to the main platform launching later, capturing the youth market without a separate, costly hardware line.
*   **Prepaid/Light Model:** If a separate device is essential, remove the full data plan requirement (e.g., Wi-Fi only or prepaid data), though this still struggles against the main platform's imminent arrival.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price Sensitivity vs. Cost Structure:** The proposal's own facts state buyers are price-sensitive and the plan cost equals the phone cost. This mathematically precludes adoption at scale.
*   **Feature Gap:** No apps/games vs. imminent full smartphone competition makes the value proposition negative.

**Risks:** Internal conflict delaying launch further; carrier rejection due to poor projected uptake.

**Kill Criteria:** If projected subscriber acquisition cost exceeds lifetime value given the plan price, stop. (Already indicated by current facts).

The proposal fails the fundamental test of product-market fit on price and features. It requires a complete strategic pivot, not approval as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
