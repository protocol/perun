First call: GO. The proposal attacks a clear market gap with a structural solution (separate brand) that directly addresses the primary risk of brand dilution.

**Steelman:** Creating a distinct luxury entity with a dedicated network and an all-new flagship allows the parent company to capture high-margin market share without compromising its economy reputation, leveraging cost advantages to undercut entrenched European rivals on price while matching quality.

**Attack & Analysis:**
The core fear—that economy heritage prevents luxury sales—is valid but solved by the proposed firewalls: a separate brand name and dealer network. Without these, the venture would fail; with them, the parent's reputation becomes irrelevant to the luxury buyer. The high cost of a new network is a barrier to entry that protects the venture from smaller competitors, justifying the heavy development spend already incurred. A single flagship model is a standard, low-risk entry strategy for luxury segments (focus on halo effect), mitigating the "thin line-up" risk. The price advantage creates a compelling value proposition against incumbents whose prestige relies on exclusivity and high margins, which are vulnerable to a high-quality disruptor.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal's own facts (separate brand, separate network, engineered from scratch) neutralize the stated risks. The heavy sunk cost argues for completion, not abandonment, provided the product matches the quality claim.

**Risks:** Brand perception leakage if the separation is not absolute; execution risk in building a dealer network from zero; potential price wars from incumbents. These are managed via the kill criteria of maintaining brand separation and monitoring initial sales velocity against the lower price point.

**Decision Logic:** The plan beats "do nothing" (ceding the luxury segment entirely) and "badging an economy car" (which would fail). It is a two-way door in terms of brand architecture (the brand can be killed if it fails, though the network cost is high), but the potential upside of establishing a new profit center in a high-margin segment outweighs the calculated risks. The proposal contains its own solution to its biggest weakness.

DECISION: GO
