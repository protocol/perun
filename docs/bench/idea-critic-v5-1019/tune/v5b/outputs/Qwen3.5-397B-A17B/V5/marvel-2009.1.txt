First call: NO-GO. The price is speculative relative to proven cash flow, and core assets are encumbered.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the publisher instantly solves the boy-demographic gap and internalizes film upside before competitors do.

**Attack:** The proposal admits the target's film slate is "short and unproven" and its "most famous characters" are locked in long-term deals with rivals. Paying $4 billion primarily for future potential while missing current crown jewels is financially unsound. The "cyclical nature" risk compounds this; entering at a peak valuation with limited immediate control over revenue drivers creates a massive downside if the next self-financed film fails. The "creative-control disputes" risk suggests the very talent needed to execute the strategy may resist integration, stalling the turnaround.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids overpaying for encumbered assets; seek organic growth or smaller, unencumbered IP.
2.  **License Specific Characters:** Target boy-demographic IP without buying the whole distressed studio.
3.  **Wait:** Let the target prove its film model and let licensing deals expire, then acquire cheaper.

**Fit:** While it addresses the demographic gap, it violates financial prudence by betting the conglomerate's liquidity on unproven execution.

**Pre-mortem:** The acquisition closes, the next film flops, stock drops, and the company is stuck with $4B debt, no control over top characters, and hostile creative leadership.

**Evidence:** The proposal itself states the films are unproven and top characters are licensed to rivals. No data supports the $4B valuation covering these deficits.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Encumbrance:** Core revenue drivers (famous characters) are unavailable due to rival licenses.
2.  **Valuation Mismatch:** $4B price tag lacks support given the "short and unproven" slate and missing top IP.

**Risks:** Creative exodus, film cyclicality, integration friction. These are secondary to the factual blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** If the top 3 characters remain licensed to rivals post-close, or if the next self-financed film underperforms by >20% vs. projections, initiate divestiture.

DECISION: NO-GO
