First call: GO. The request comes from the largest customer, addressing immediate capacity constraints with a clear economic benefit (lower cost per seat). The design features (raised cockpit) explicitly mitigate long-term obsolescence risk by enabling freighter conversion.

Steelman: Building a massive, convertible wide-body jet secures market dominance against both subsonic growth and potential supersonic displacement, leveraging a single anchor customer to justify the scale.

Alternatives analysis:
1. Do nothing: Cedes capacity growth to competitors and ignores the primary customer's strategic need.
2. Supersonic only: High technical uncertainty and unproven economics; ignores the immediate subsonic volume surge.
3. Stretch existing model: Insufficient to meet the "two and a half times" capacity requirement; inefficient for long-haul density.

Fit: Aligns with the vision of dominating commercial air travel through scale and versatility.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the engine supplier fails to deliver high-bypass technology or if the single customer cancels before certification. However, the freighter conversion capability provides a salvage path if passenger demand shifts. The "one airline" risk is mitigated by that airline being the largest in the market; their commitment signals strong initial viability.

Evidence check: The proposal relies on the customer's growth projection and the supplier's engine roadmap. While the engine is unproven, high-bypass technology is the logical next step for efficiency. The financial outlay is massive but proportional to the market dominance at stake.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (cost, engine, supersonic threat, single customer) are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The freighter conversion directly addresses the supersonic threat risk. The scale matches the customer's specific request.

Risks: Engine development delays, capital overextension, market shift to supersonic. These are managed via the convertible design and the anchor customer's commitment.

Kill criteria: If the engine supplier cannot meet thrust/spec targets within 24 months, or if the anchor airline reduces its order by more than 50% before first flight.

DECISION: GO
