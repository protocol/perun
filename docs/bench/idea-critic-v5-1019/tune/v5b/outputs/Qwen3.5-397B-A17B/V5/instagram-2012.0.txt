First call: NO-GO. Paying $1 billion for a dozen employees with zero revenue and no business model violates basic capital discipline, regardless of strategic fear.

**Steelman:** The acquisition is a defensive necessity to neutralize the primary mobile threat before the IPO, buying immediate market leadership and talent that cannot be built internally fast enough.

**Attack:**
1.  **Valuation:** $1 billion for ~12 people implies ~$83 million per head. Even with tens of millions of users, the lack of a monetization path makes this a speculative bet on "potential" rather than an asset purchase. The multiple is unjustifiable by any standard financial metric.
2.  **Execution Risk:** Integrating a distinct culture into a company with a known weak mobile product often results in talent flight or product dilution, destroying the very value being purchased.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or a smaller talent acqui-hire costs significantly less. Building a competitive feature set internally, while slower, avoids the massive premium paid for panic.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: Regulatory blocks delay integration, key founders leave due to culture clash, and the core product remains weak because the acquisition was a substitute for fixing internal engineering deficits.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Fact:** The target has **no revenue** and **no business model**. A $1 billion price tag lacks a mathematical basis for ROI or payback. This is a factual mismatch between cost and current economic reality.
*   **Strategic Fact:** The buyer admits its own mobile product is weak. Pouring $1 billion into an external fix without a proven internal ability to execute mobile strategy suggests the root cause (engineering/product culture) remains unaddressed.

**Risks:** Regulatory rejection, culture destruction, user churn post-acquisition, opportunity cost of $1B cash.

**Kill Criteria:** If user growth stalls by >20% within 6 months post-close, or if key founding team departs within 12 months, the thesis fails.

**Decision:** The absence of revenue and a business model, combined with an astronomical valuation based solely on strategic fear, constitutes a financial blocker. This is a panic buy, not a sound investment.

DECISION: NO-GO
