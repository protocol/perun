First call: GO. The market collapse is a distribution and perception failure, not a demand extinction; the proposed repositioning directly addresses the root cause of retailer refusal.

**Steelman:** By masking the console as an "Entertainment System" with non-game toys (robot, light gun) and assuming inventory risk via buy-backs, the company bypasses the "video game" stigma to secure shelf space, while the lockout chip prevents the software glut that caused the 1983 crash.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Cedes the recovering market to home computers; brand remains irrelevant in the West.
*   **Direct Launch:** Selling explicitly as a "video game console" guarantees retailer rejection based on current sentiment.
*   **Cheaper Alternative:** Software-only publishing fails without hardware installed base; hardware without quality control (no lockout) repeats the 1983 saturation error.

**Fit:** The strategy aligns with the immediate constraint: retailer hostility. It treats the console as a toy category entry rather than a computer peripheral.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the toy bundle feels cheap compared to dedicated robots, or if the lockout chip creates a software drought. However, the buy-back promise removes the retailer's financial risk, forcing the trial. The cost of delay is total loss of first-mover advantage against Commodore/Atari remnants.

**Evidence & Logic:** The proposal correctly identifies that the 1983 crash was driven by retailer loss of confidence due to unregulated software quality and market saturation. The lockout chip directly solves the quality/saturation variable; the rebranding solves the confidence variable. The take-back promise is the necessary price of admission for shelf space.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (developer anger, return costs) are operational trade-offs, not factual contradictions. Developer restrictions are required to prevent the very crash that just occurred. Return costs are capped by initial order volume, which will be conservative given retailer skepticism.

**Risks:** High operational cost of returns if the product fails; potential developer backlash. These are managed by the strict quality control the lockout enables.

**Kill Criteria:** If retailer orders do not cover manufacturing setup costs within 6 months, or if the attach rate of software per unit sold drops below 1.5, pivot to direct mail or halt production.

DECISION: GO
