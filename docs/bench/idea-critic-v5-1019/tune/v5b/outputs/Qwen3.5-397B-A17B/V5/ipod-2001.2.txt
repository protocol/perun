First call: NO-GO. The plan targets a tiny installed base with a premium price in a category where cheaper options already struggle, without the key enabler (legal downloads) to drive demand.

**Steelman:** A premium, high-capacity device could define a new standard if it offers a vastly superior user experience that justifies the price, eventually expanding beyond the initial computer base.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Fit:** Selling a $400 device only to owners of a niche computer ignores the mass market. Cheaper players failed due to poor UX and limited content, not just price; raising the price without solving content (legal downloads) exacerbates the adoption barrier.
2.  **Sourcing Risk:** Building a custom hard-drive solution in under a year is operationally extreme for a company with no consumer electronics supply chain experience. Component shortages or delays would miss the holiday window, killing momentum.
3.  **Content Void:** Without licensed downloads, the device relies on users ripping CDs. While possible, this friction limits appeal compared to the promise of a seamless library, which the industry has not yet enabled.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A software-only play or licensing the OS to existing hardware makers reduces execution risk and expands the potential user base immediately.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches late, costs $450 due to rush fees, sells 50,000 units to existing loyalists, and is discontinued after two years as competitors bundle better software with cheaper flash storage. The brand dilutes its premium status with a flopped hardware experiment.

**Blockers:**
*   **Addressable Market:** The stated constraint (own computers only) caps the potential volume below the threshold needed to justify the supply chain investment and R&D for a first-time hardware entrant.
*   **Ecosystem Gap:** The core value proposition (easy music management) is crippled by the lack of a legal download store, a dependency outside the company's control and not part of this proposal.

**Risks:** Supply chain failure, price resistance, slow adoption of digital music.

The proposal fails because it attempts a high-risk hardware launch into an unproven mass market while artificially restricting the customer base to a niche, without solving the content distribution problem that defines the category's value.

DECISION: NO-GO
