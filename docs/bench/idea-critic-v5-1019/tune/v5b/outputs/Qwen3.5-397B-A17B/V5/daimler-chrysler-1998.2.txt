First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a cyclical peak and ignores fatal cultural friction.

**Steelman:** Combining a premium German engineer with a profitable US truck maker creates a full-spectrum global giant, sharing platforms to cut costs while accessing the mass market.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cyclical Trap:** The US partner's profitability hinges entirely on a light-truck boom. Buying at this peak means acquiring maximum liability just as the cycle turns. The "merger of equals" valuation is likely distorted by temporary earnings, not structural value.
2.  **Cultural Incompatibility:** "Very different corporate and labour cultures" is a euphemism for operational paralysis. German hierarchical engineering precision clashes with US flexible, volume-driven management. Integrating supply chains and decision-making will stall, eroding the very synergies promised.
3.  **Brand Dilution:** A premium brand cannot effectively manage a mass-market volume strategy without diluting its core value proposition. The "complementary brands" argument fails if the combined entity cannot govern them under distinct operational philosophies.
4.  **Governance Lie:** A "combined board" with such divergent interests often leads to deadlock or a de facto takeover disguised as a merger, causing talent exodus from the acquired side.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve the German firm's premium margins and avoid integrating a cyclical, culturally alien partner at peak valuation.
*   **Strategic Alliance:** Share specific platforms or engines via contract without equity merger, limiting exposure to cultural and labor friction.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-merger, the truck cycle crashes, wiping out the US partner's profit contribution. Cultural infighting prevents cost-cutting integration. The German brand's quality reputation suffers due to mass-market association. The stock price collapses below the pre-merger level of the German firm alone.

**Blockers:**
*   **Valuation Mismatch:** Acquiring based on cyclical peak profits guarantees overpayment.
*   **Integration Risk:** Unresolved cultural and labor differences block the execution of platform sharing.

**Risks:** Market cycle reversal, brand dilution, governance deadlock.

**Kill Criteria:** If the US partner's truck sales drop >15% within 12 months, or if key engineering leadership from either side resigns within 6 months, the merger must be unwound or paused.

DECISION: NO-GO
