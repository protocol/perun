First call: NO-GO. The combination of a historically hostile buyer, a loss-making target without leadership, and explicit community distrust creates a high probability of immediate value destruction through developer defection, rendering the $7.5 billion price unjustifiable on current facts.

**Steelman:** The acquisition secures the dominant development platform before a free competitor scales, leveraging the buyer's resources to stabilize the target while preserving its brand to calm the community.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cultural Collision:** A company known for hostility cannot instantly convincingly pivot to stewardship. The community's vocal distrust is a leading indicator of active defection, not just sentiment. If developers leave, the platform's network effect—the sole source of its value—collapses.
2.  **Financials:** Paying a premium for a loss-making asset with no CEO implies the buyer is overpaying for potential rather than reality. Without a clear path to profitability independent of the parent, this is a cash burn.
3.  **Alternatives:** The existence of a free, trusted competitor provides an immediate exit ramp for dissatisfied users. The "run independently" promise is often viewed as a transitional lie by skeptical communities.
4.  **Regulatory:** Dominant player acquisition invites antitrust scrutiny that could stall integration or force divestiture, creating uncertainty that accelerates user churn.

**Blockers:**
*   **Community Trust:** The proposal admits vocal distrust. Without a concrete, verified mechanism to guarantee independence (beyond a statement), the core asset (developers) is at risk of immediate migration to the free alternative.
*   **Leadership Void:** Acquiring a company without a permanent CEO means buying a strategy vacuum. The buyer inherits the cost of finding leadership while fighting a culture war.
*   **Value Proposition:** The price ($7.5B) assumes dominance continues. If the "hostile" reputation triggers even moderate defection, the revenue multiple becomes indefensible.

**Risks:** Regulatory delay, integration failure, brand contamination. These are secondary to the primary blocker: the asset walks out the door if the community rejects the buyer.

**Kill Criteria:** If developer activity drops >5% within 90 days post-announcement, or if the free competitor gains >10% market share in that window, the thesis fails.

The risks are not merely theoretical; they are inherent in the stated facts of hostility, lack of leadership, and community sentiment. The plan lacks a verified mechanism to prevent the very defection that destroys the asset's value.

DECISION: NO-GO
