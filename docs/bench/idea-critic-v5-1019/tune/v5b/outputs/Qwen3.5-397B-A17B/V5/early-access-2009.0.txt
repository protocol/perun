First call: NO-GO. Releasing an unfinished, unsupported game to a paying public without marketing or a support plan invites immediate reputation damage and refund churn that a solo developer cannot survive.

**Steelman:** Early revenue and user feedback will fund and guide development faster than working in isolation, validating demand before full completion.

**Attack & Options:**
The "do nothing" option (finish privately) avoids public failure but risks building the wrong product. The proposed "paid alpha" fails the blocker test: the proposal admits zero capacity for support and zero marketing. In 2009, "early access" models were not standardized; players expected finished goods or free demos. Selling a broken product without a support channel creates a negative feedback loop: angry reviews kill sales, reducing funds for the very support needed.

A superior alternative exists: release a **free, limited demo** to gather feedback and build a mailing list, then pre-order the full version. This captures the "feedback" and "validation" benefits without the reputational suicide of selling a broken product unsupported.

**Pre-mortem:** The game launches; players encounter bugs and lack features. With no support team, forums fill with rage. Steam/news sites pick up the "scam" narrative. The developer, overwhelmed and broke, abandons the project. The brand is toxic forever.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Support Mismatch:** The proposal explicitly states the solo dev "cannot support a large audience," yet selling publicly guarantees an influx of support tickets that will halt development entirely.
2.  **Market Timing:** In 2009, the "pay for alpha" social contract was not established. Selling an unfinished product is perceived as fraud, not collaboration, without significant existing trust or influencer backing (which is absent here).
3.  **Revenue Illusion:** Without marketing, sales will be too low to fund development but high enough to generate damaging negative reviews.

**Risks:** Running out of money before completion; feature creep based on vocal minority feedback.

**Kill Criteria:** If a free demo does not generate 1,000 wishlists or 10% conversion to a mailing list within 3 months, the project lacks market fit.

**Decision:** The plan as proposed guarantees failure due to the support vacuum and 2009 market expectations. It must pivot to a free-demo/pre-order model first.

DECISION: NO-GO
