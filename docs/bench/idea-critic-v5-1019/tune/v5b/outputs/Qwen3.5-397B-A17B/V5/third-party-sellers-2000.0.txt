First call: GO. The immediate need is selection and liquidity, not margin protection, given the lack of profitability.

Steelman: Aggregating third-party supply transforms the site from a limited retailer into a comprehensive marketplace, increasing customer retention and transaction volume despite lower per-unit margins.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing: Retain control but cap growth at inventory capital limits; likely path to continued unprofitability.
2. Exclusive partnerships: Slower scale, higher legal complexity, same cannibalization risks without the volume upside.
3. Separate used section: Fragments the user experience and reduces price transparency, lowering conversion.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of becoming the primary destination for all goods. Inventory constraints currently block this; third-party capital solves it.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if bad actor sellers drive away core customers faster than new selection attracts them. This is a two-way door; sellers can be suspended, and policies tightened if trust metrics dip. Cost of delay is high: every month without expanded selection cedes ground to competitors who offer "everything."

Evidence & Math: The proposal admits cannibalization and undercutting. However, for an unprofitable retailer, selling a used item via a competitor (earning a fee) is superior to not selling the item at all (earning zero). The fee structure turns fixed inventory risk into variable revenue. Supplier objection is a political risk, not a mathematical blocker; the long-term value of a liquid marketplace outweighs short-term channel conflict.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (undercutting, quality, cannibalization) are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. They are inherent to the model's success mechanism (price competition and selection depth).

Risks: Brand dilution and supplier retaliation. These become kill criteria: if customer return rates from third-party items exceed internal benchmarks by >20% or if top-tier suppliers formally boycott, the program pauses.

Kill Criteria: Monitor "Third-Party Defect Rate" and "Net Promoter Score" weekly. If either degrades beyond 5% of baseline within the first quarter, suspend new seller onboarding.

The strongest attack—that competitors will steal sales—fails because the alternative is no sale. Capturing a fee on a rival's transaction is the only path to positive unit economics given current inventory limits.

DECISION: GO
