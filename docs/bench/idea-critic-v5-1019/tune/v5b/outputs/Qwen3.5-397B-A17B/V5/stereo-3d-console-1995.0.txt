First call: NO-GO. The product sacrifices core usability (comfort, visual fidelity) for a gimmick (stereoscopic 3D) that introduces health risks (eye strain), directly contradicting the goal of a successful bridge product.

Steelman: A low-cost ($180) experimental device leveraging existing tech to fill a market gap between handheld and home consoles with a unique 3D experience.

Options:
1. **Proposed:** High risk of user rejection due to discomfort and poor visuals; low software support.
2. **Do Nothing:** Wait for the next home console; avoid brand damage from a flawed intermediate product.
3. **Enhanced Handheld:** Iterate on the existing portable line with better battery/screen, a proven path with lower health risks.

Fit: Fails to move the north star of "accessible, enjoyable gaming" if the core experience causes physical pain. A bridge product must strengthen the brand, not weaken it with negative user experiences.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, users experience immediate headaches, reviewers pan the red monochrome display as archaic, and the device becomes a cautionary tale of form over function. Sales flatline; the brand suffers. This is a one-way door for reputation; once users associate the brand with nausea, recovery is hard. Cost of delay is low since the home console arrives soon.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits critical flaws: "neither portable nor comfortable," "eye strain or headaches," "red-on-black visuals only." These are not hypothetical risks but stated characteristics of the design. The lack of games confirms a weak ecosystem launch.

Blockers:
1. **Health/Safety:** Stated risk of eye strain/headaches is a direct blocker for a consumer entertainment device.
2. **Usability:** "Neither portable nor comfortable" defeats the purpose of a "handheld-to-home" bridge.
3. **Value Proposition:** Red monochrome 3D is a severe downgrade in visual quality compared to contemporaries, offering no compensating benefit.

Risks: Limited game library, brand confusion, cannibalization of the upcoming home console.

The stated facts confirm the product fails on basic ergonomics and safety. No amount of pricing strategy ($180) offsets a device that physically hurts the user.

DECISION: NO-GO
