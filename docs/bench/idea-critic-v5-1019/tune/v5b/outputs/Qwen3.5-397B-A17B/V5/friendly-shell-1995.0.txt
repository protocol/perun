First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a high-end hardware niche with a $100 price point while ignoring an imminent, likely cheaper, first-party OS update that will address usability for the mass market.

**Steelman:** A cartoon-house shell makes computing intuitive for novices, justifying a premium price on high-end machines before the next OS release.

**Attack:**
1.  **Hardware Mismatch:** The proposal admits most home PCs lack the required memory. Selling a $100 add-on that requires a new computer purchase destroys the addressable market. The "home user" segment in 1995 is extremely price-sensitive; this solution costs more than the hardware it runs on for many.
2.  **Imminent Obsolescence:** A major OS release with a new interface is coming "within months." Any third-party shell released now faces immediate devaluation. Consumers will not pay $100 for a temporary fix when a native, integrated solution is imminent.
3.  **Ecosystem Void:** With "few third-party add-ons," the rooms in this cartoon house are empty. A shell without applications offers no utility beyond novelty, failing to solve the core problem of "hard to use" regarding actual tasks.
4.  **Brand Risk:** For a company leading PC operating systems, selling a patronizing, cartoonish layer that conflicts with the upcoming professional OS direction creates brand confusion and cannibalizes the impending upgrade cycle.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Wait for the major OS release. Cost: $0. Risk: Competitors might fill the gap (unlikely given hardware constraints). Reversibility: N/A.
*   **Integrate, Don't Sell:** Incorporate "guide" features into the upcoming OS release. Cost: Development time. Risk: Delays OS. Reversibility: High.
*   **The Proposal:** Sell a niche, expensive, hardware-dependent shell. Cost: High support/marketing. Risk: Total market rejection. Reversibility: Low (reputation damage).

**Blockers:**
*   **Market Size:** The intersection of "new home users," "high-end PC owners," and "willingness to pay $100 for a shell" is negligible.
*   **Timing:** The imminent OS release renders the product obsolete before it can gain traction.
*   **Utility:** Lack of add-ons means the shell provides no functional value.

**Risks:**
*   User rejection due to patronizing design.
*   Confusion with the upcoming native interface.

The proposal fails on market fit, timing, and utility. It solves a problem for a non-existent customer segment at the wrong time.

DECISION: NO-GO
