First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets $1B+ on unproven demand for premium short-form subscriptions amid known fatigue and abundant free alternatives.

**Steelman:** A high-fidelity, mobile-native video service with A-list talent and orientation-agnostic tech can carve a profitable niche despite free competitors by offering unmatched quality and convenience.

**Attack:**
1.  **Demand Contradiction:** The proposal admits "no proven demand" and "subscription fatigue" while requesting massive capital for a *subscription* model. Free, ad-supported short video already dominates the exact format (under 10 mins). Charging a premium for what users currently get free, without a validated willingness to pay, is a fundamental market mismatch.
2.  **Feature vs. Value:** The core technical differentiator (vertical/horizontal adaptability) is a hygiene factor, not a value proposition worth a new subscription fee. Competitors can replicate this easily; it does not solve the content demand gap.
3.  **Cost Structure:** "High production budgets" and "A-list talent" for sub-10-minute episodes create an unsustainable unit economics model compared to user-generated or lower-cost professional content flooding the market. The burn rate to acquire subscribers in a fatigued market would be catastrophic.
4.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "partner with existing platforms" options offer lower risk. A cheaper alternative is a targeted pilot with a fraction of the budget to validate price sensitivity before raising $1B.

**Pre-mortem:** The service launches, burns through cash on content, fails to convert free users to paid due to fatigue, and shuts down within 18 months as studios pull funding.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** No proven demand for premium short-form subscriptions exists (stated in proposal).
*   **Fact:** Subscription fatigue is rising (stated in proposal).
*   **Contradiction:** The plan requires massive scale immediately to justify A-list costs, but the market signal suggests users will not pay.

**Risks:** Competition for attention, high churn, content cost overruns. These are secondary to the core blocker of unvalidated demand.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot cannot demonstrate >5% conversion from free to paid at the target price point within 6 months, stop.

The absence of validated demand for the specific monetization model (subscription) in the face of abundant free substitutes is a fatal flaw for a $1B launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
