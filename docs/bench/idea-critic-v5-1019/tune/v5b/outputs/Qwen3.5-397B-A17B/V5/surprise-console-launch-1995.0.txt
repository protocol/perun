First call: NO-GO. Launching early with high price, scarce software, and alienated retailers contradicts the fundamental requirements for a successful console launch: ecosystem momentum and channel support.

**Steelman:** An early May launch secures mindshare before the competitor, establishing a "first-to-market" premium narrative despite limitations.

**Attack:**
1.  **Ecosystem Failure:** Consoles live or die by software. Launching with "few games" while developers are unprepared for the complex 3D architecture guarantees a barren library. Consumers will not pay a $100 premium for hardware they cannot use.
2.  **Channel Mutiny:** Shipping only to "a few retailers" explicitly invites retaliation from excluded partners. In 1995, retail shelf space is the primary bottleneck; alienating major chains cedes the market to the rival who launches broadly in September.
3.  **Price-Value Mismatch:** A $399 price point for a 32-bit machine with no games, against a $299 competitor with a full lineup, is indefensible. The "premium" argument collapses without exclusive killer apps.
4.  **Developer Trust:** Surprising developers with an earlier deadline damages long-term relationships, slowing future title availability even after the initial launch window.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing (Wait for September):* Aligns with developers, ensures full retail distribution, and allows a competitive price response.
*   *Limited "Developer" Release:* Ship units only to press/devs in May, not consumers. Builds hype without risking consumer backlash or retail wars.

**Pre-mortem:** By Christmas 1995, the system is labeled a "failure" due to lack of games and high price. Retailers refuse to stock it during the critical holiday season, preferring the competitor's broader, cheaper, game-rich alternative. The brand reputation for reliability is shattered before the generation truly begins.

**Blockers:**
*   **Software Void:** The proposal admits developers are not ready; launching without games violates the core value proposition.
*   **Retail Hostility:** Intentionally excluding partners creates an immediate distribution blockade that cannot be overcome by a small head-start.
*   **Price Disadvantage:** No justification exists for a 33% price premium on inferior (in terms of content) hardware.

**Risks:** Being second to market; losing early adopter buzz. These are manageable; launching a broken product is not.

DECISION: NO-GO
