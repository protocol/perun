First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores direct market validation where two potential partners rejected the concept, and the company lacks critical consumer experience.

**Steelman:** A low-cost, randomized robot can succeed by prioritizing affordability and simplicity over perfect coverage, creating a new market segment unaware they need automation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Signal:** Two floor-care experts rejected early prototypes. This suggests the value proposition (random cleaning) fails to meet professional standards or consumer expectations for reliability. Ignoring this signal is dangerous.
2.  **Capability Gap:** The company has zero consumer-product experience. Manufacturing, distribution, support, and branding for mass-market hardware differ vastly from robotics prototyping. This is a fatal execution risk.
3.  **Trust Deficit:** Buyers are skeptical of robots in homes. A "dumb" random mover exacerbates fears of inefficiency or damage compared to "smarter" mapping rivals, even if expensive.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" avoids burning capital on a likely failure. Partnering with an established consumer brand (instead of launching alone) mitigates the experience gap, though the initial rejection makes this hard.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, gets returned due to missed spots and erratic behavior, the brand is tarnished, and the company runs out of cash trying to support a product line they don't understand.

**Blockers:**
*   **Negative Validation:** Direct rejection by target industry players indicates the core feature set (random movement) is unacceptable to the very market intended to validate it.
*   **Missing Competency:** No consumer experience is a hard blocker for a direct-to-consumer hardware launch; this cannot be learned quickly enough to prevent disaster.

**Risks:** Brand damage, inventory bloat, warranty collapse. These are severe but secondary to the blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot with 100 non-expert users shows >20% return rate due to cleaning performance, stop immediately. However, the current blockers prevent even reaching this stage safely.

The proposal demands a massive leap into an unknown market with a disputed product and no relevant team history. The rejection by floor-care companies is a leading indicator of consumer apathy. Without a partner or a pivot to address the coverage guarantee, launching alone is reckless.

DECISION: NO-GO
