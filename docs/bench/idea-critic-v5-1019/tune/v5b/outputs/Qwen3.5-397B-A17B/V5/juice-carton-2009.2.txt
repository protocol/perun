First call: NO-GO. The proposal discards the brand's primary shelf cue for an untested aesthetic, risking immediate sales collapse among a loyal, older demographic without a safety net.

**Steelman:** A modernized design will attract new buyers and signal quality, outweighing temporary confusion among existing customers.

**Attack:** The proposal admits the old image is the "main shelf cue" and the base is "older." Removing the single visual anchor for this demographic in a "launch all at once" strategy is not a risk; it is a guaranteed failure mode. Older consumers rely heavily on established visual patterns for rapid identification. A vertical rotation and minimalist approach remove the orange—the category signifier—replacing it with a glass and a cap shape, which are secondary cues.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Retain shelf recognition; zero redesign cost.
2.  **Staged pilot:** Test the new design in a limited region to measure recognition drop-off before national rollout.
3.  **Hybrid:** Evolve the current design gradually, keeping the orange prominent while modernizing typography.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales have plummeted 20%. Shoppers, particularly the core older demographic, walked past the cartons believing the brand was out of stock or had been discontinued because they could not instantly locate the familiar orange image. The massive ad spend failed to retrain muscle memory fast enough to prevent lost trials.

**Evidence & Logic:** The proposal's own facts contradict its execution. If the orange image is the main cue, removing it violates the fundamental constraint of shelf visibility. The "all stores at once" approach eliminates the ability to detect this failure early. There is no pilot, no metric for recognition retention, and no fallback plan to revert packaging if sales dip. The cost of delay (waiting to test) is negligible compared to the cost of a failed national relaunch.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Contradictory Facts:** The plan removes the stated "main shelf cue" for the specific demographic most reliant on such cues, ensuring recognition failure.
2.  **Irreversibility of Scale:** A simultaneous national launch with no pilot prevents early detection of the predicted recognition failure, making the error catastrophic rather than correctable.

**Risks:** High cost of reprinting; alienation of loyal base. These become kill criteria only if a pilot were proposed. Without a pilot, they are blockers.

DECISION: NO-GO
