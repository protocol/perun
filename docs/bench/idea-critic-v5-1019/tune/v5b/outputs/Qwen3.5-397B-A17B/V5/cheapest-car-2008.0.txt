First call: NO-GO. The reliance on a single, contested factory site and a brand identity anchored solely to "cheapest" creates immediate, unmitigated blockers that threaten the entire venture before production begins.

**Steelman:** A dedicated high-volume factory enables the $2,500 price point, unlocking car ownership for millions of scooter users who currently cannot afford entry-level vehicles.

**Attack:**
1.  **Site Blocker:** The proposal admits the factory site faces active farmer protests. In land acquisition disputes, delays are indefinite, not calendar-bound. A "new dedicated factory" with "high volume targets" cannot launch if the ground is contested. This is not a risk; it is a stopper.
2.  **Brand Blocker:** Marketing explicitly as the "cheapest car in the world" invites a safety backlash. For families transitioning from scooters, safety is the primary upgrade driver. Positioning the vehicle as a cost-cutting miracle signals compromised structural integrity, deterring the exact demographic (families) the car targets.
3.  **Alternative Failure:** The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" or "used cars." A used car offers proven safety and status at a similar price point without the stigma of being a "budget experiment." The cost-cutting required to hit $2,500 likely erodes the quality needed to compete with reliable used inventory.

**Pre-mortem:** The factory is delayed two years by litigation; the brand launches with a "death trap" reputation after a single high-profile accident; sales stall as families choose used sedans over a new, stigmatized micro-car.

**Evidence Check:** The proposal admits the land protests and safety doubts as facts, not possibilities. These are internal contradictions: you cannot promise high volume from a blocked site, nor sell family safety while boasting of extreme cost reduction.

**Blockers:**
*   Land acquisition is actively contested (protests), making the "dedicated factory" timeline impossible.
*   The "cheapest" positioning directly undermines the "family safety" value proposition required to switch from scooters.

**Risks:**
*   Quality perception vs. used car market.
*   Regulatory scrutiny on safety standards.

Without a secured site and a value narrative beyond price, the plan fails on its own stated facts.

DECISION: NO-GO
