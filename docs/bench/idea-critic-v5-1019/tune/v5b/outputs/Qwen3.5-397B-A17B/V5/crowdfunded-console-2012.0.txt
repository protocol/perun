First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a two-sided marketplace (players and developers) with zero initial liquidity, no marketing budget to solve the cold-start problem, and a history of hardware execution failure in the target timeframe.

**Steelman:** A crowdfunded, open-platform console can succeed by leveraging community enthusiasm to bypass traditional gatekeepers, offering a low-cost alternative to established giants if the team can ship a minimal viable product within a year.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Reality:** Established consoles and smartphones already dominate. Without a marketing budget, the project cannot reach the critical mass of users required to attract third-party developers.
2.  **Developer Incentive:** Developers will not build for a new platform with no installed base. The "open software" angle is a feature, not a business model, and does not guarantee content.
3.  **Execution Risk:** Hardware delays are the norm, not the exception. A one-year ship date from a standing start, funded only by pre-sales, is statistically improbable. If the hardware is delayed, backer trust evaporates, and the campaign fails.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" avoids financial loss. A software-only approach (emulator or store) on existing hardware would validate demand without the capital risk of manufacturing.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because the hardware ships six months late with no exclusive games. Backers demand refunds, the team runs out of funds, and the brand is tainted before launch.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Path to Liquidity:** The proposal admits no marketing budget yet requires attracting both users and developers simultaneously. This is a fundamental contradiction in a two-sided market.
*   **Unrealistic Timeline:** Shipping custom hardware within a year of a crowdfunding campaign, without prior manufacturing experience or capital reserves, contradicts industry norms for hardware development.

**Risks:** Competition, developer apathy, supply chain issues. These are severe but secondary to the structural blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** If a prototype cannot be demonstrated with committed game titles before the campaign launches, the project must stop.

The lack of a marketing strategy to solve the chicken-and-egg problem and the high probability of hardware delay constitute fatal flaws in the current plan.

DECISION: NO-GO
