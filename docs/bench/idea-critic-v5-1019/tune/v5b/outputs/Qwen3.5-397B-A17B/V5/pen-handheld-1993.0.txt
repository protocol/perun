First call: NO-GO. The core value proposition (reliable handwriting recognition) is explicitly stated as unproven and unreliable in testing, while the price point is high for an unproven category with no software ecosystem.

**Steelman:** Launching a $700 pen-based device now establishes category leadership and brand vision before competitors, betting that publicity will drive early adoption despite technical immaturity.

**Attack:**
1.  **Core Failure:** The primary input mechanism fails its basic function ("unreliable in testing"). Releasing a product where the main feature does not work guarantees negative reviews and returns, destroying the brand credibility needed to lead a category.
2.  **Economic Mismatch:** A high price ($700 in 1993 is significant) combined with "little software" and "unproven category" creates a value vacuum. Early adopters tolerate bugs, but not at a premium price for a device that cannot reliably take notes.
3.  **Form Factor:** If it is "too big for a pocket," it fails the "handheld" and "personal" utility test, limiting use cases to desk-bound scenarios where laptops already excel.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A "pilot" approach (limited release to developers) is superior to a full public launch backed by heavy CEO publicity, which raises stakes too high for an unready product.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to fanfare, reviewers immediately demonstrate recognition failures, sales stall due to price and lack of apps, and the company is forced to discount heavily, killing margins and the category's reputation for years.

**Blockers:**
*   **Functional Blocker:** The stated fact that handwriting recognition is "unreliable in testing" directly contradicts the requirement for a usable primary input method. This is not a risk; it is a current defect.
*   **Market Blocker:** High price + no software + unproven category = no viable early market segment identified in the proposal.

**Risks:** Category failure, competitor entry with better tech, brand damage.

**Conclusion:** The proposal asks to bet the brand on a feature that does not work yet, at a price the market cannot justify, in a category that needs software it doesn't have. This is a classic "vaporware" trap. The plan must be revised to fix the recognition engine, reduce the form factor, and secure software partners *before* a public launch.

DECISION: NO-GO
