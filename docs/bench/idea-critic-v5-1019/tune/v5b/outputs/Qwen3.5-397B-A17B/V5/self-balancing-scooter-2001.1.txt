First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to massive fixed costs (40k unit factory) and a high price point ($5,000) while admitting the legal status is unclear, use cases are unproven, and social stigma ("conspicuous") is a barrier.

**Steelman:** The device is a revolutionary breakthrough that will instantly create a new urban transport category, justifying early premium pricing and scale to meet inevitable demand.

**Attack:**
1.  **Legal Blocker:** If the legal status on sidewalks and roads is "unclear," mass adoption is impossible. Municipalities move slowly; a $5,000 toy cannot wait years for legislation. Without explicit legal clearance, the product is unsellable to the mass market.
2.  **Market Fit:** The admitted risks (high price, no proven use beyond institutions, social stigma, low utility) directly contradict the requirement for 40,000 units/year. Institutional buyers alone cannot absorb this volume at $5,000/unit without proven logistics use cases.
3.  **Scale Mismatch:** Building a factory for 40,000 units before validating demand, legal standing, or social acceptance is catastrophic capital misallocation. A "better way" is a pilot run of <1,000 units to test legal waters and user behavior before committing to industrial scale.
4.  **Unit Economics:** At $5,000, the addressable market is tiny. If the device cannot carry loads or commute comfortably without stigma, the value proposition collapses against bicycles or cars.

**Pre-mortem:** The company builds the factory, produces 20,000 units, then cities ban the device from sidewalks due to safety concerns. Sales stall at 2,000 units. The company faces insolvency due to fixed overhead and unsold inventory.

**Blockers:**
*   **Legal Uncertainty:** The proposal admits the legal status is unclear. This is a factual blocker; you cannot sell a vehicle where it is illegal to operate.
*   **Unproven Demand vs. Fixed Cost:** Committing to 40k capacity without proven consumer demand or institutional contracts is a financial failure mode.

**Risks:** Social stigma, high price sensitivity, limited utility. These are severe but secondary to the legal and capacity blockers.

**Conclusion:** The plan fails the basic test of selling a product where its legality is unknown and scaling to 40,000 units without evidence of demand. The commitment to a large factory is premature and dangerous.

DECISION: NO-GO
