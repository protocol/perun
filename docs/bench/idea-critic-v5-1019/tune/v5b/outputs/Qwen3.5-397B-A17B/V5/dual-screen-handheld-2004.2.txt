First call: GO. The proposal avoids a direct hardware war against a superior graphic engine by pivoting to a unique form factor and price point, targeting an untapped demographic rather than fighting for the same core users.

Steelman: By leveraging a dual-screen touch interface at a lower cost, the company creates a distinct market category that renders the rival's graphical superiority less relevant, expanding the total addressable market while maintaining a budget entry via the legacy device.

Alternatives: Matching the rival's specs would likely result in a higher price and a "me-too" product with no clear advantage. Doing nothing cedes the premium segment entirely without a defensive play for the mass market. The proposed path offers the highest reversibility; if the new form factor fails, the legacy device remains a revenue stream, whereas a failed direct competitor would cannibalize sales without gaining share.

Fit: This aligns with a strategy of market expansion and differentiation. It assumes that software innovation can drive hardware adoption even without top-tier graphical fidelity.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if developers cannot easily adapt to the dual-screen paradigm, resulting in a software drought. However, the lower price point mitigates consumer risk, and the "gimmick" concern is neutralized if the touch interface enables intuitive gameplay inaccessible to the rival. The cost of delay is high, as the rival is already well-funded and marketing their superior specs; waiting allows them to cement consumer expectations around graphics alone.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the verified fact that the rival dominates on graphics and media. The counter-strategy relies on the assumption that price and novelty can offset raw power, a valid historical pattern in consumer electronics when targeting non-core demographics.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (developer confusion, unproven touch mechanics) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. They do not prove the plan fails as proposed; they define the work required to succeed. The plan explicitly addresses the cost risk by aiming for a lower price point and mitigates market risk by keeping the older unit on sale.

Risks: Developer adoption speed and consumer perception of the second screen. These become kill criteria: if major third-party support isn't secured within the first two quarters, or if attach rates for touch-specific titles remain below 20% by the first holiday season, the strategy must pivot.

DECISION: GO
