First call: NO-GO. The initial four-city test demonstrated little demand, and the proposal offers no evidence that a name change or a different city demographic will reverse this fundamental lack of product-market fit.

**Steelman:** Renaming and targeting a specific office niche via free samples could uncover a latent need missed by the general consumer launch, validating a pivot before national spend.

**Attack:**
1.  **Evidence Contradiction:** The proposal admits the first test showed "little demand." Changing the name does not alter the product's utility or the core problem that users "may not see a need." Without a change to the adhesive mechanism or the value proposition, the root cause of failure remains unaddressed.
2.  **Flawed Metric:** Success is defined as "most recipients say they would buy it." Verbal intent from free sample recipients is a notoriously poor predictor of actual purchase behavior, especially for a product already deemed unnecessary by the market. This metric invites false positives.
3.  **Cost vs. Insight:** Distributing free samples incurs material and logistics costs. Given the high probability that the "need" gap persists, this expenditure buys low-quality data (intent) rather than solving the high-risk assumption (utility).
4.  **Better Way:** Before spending on samples and renaming, the company must validate if the "re-positionable" feature solves a specific, painful problem for office workers that current solutions do not. A cheap, qualitative inquiry (interviews) in the target offices should precede any physical distribution.

**Blockers:**
*   **Core Claim Failure:** The proposal assumes a name change and sample distribution will generate demand where a prior test proved none existed, without identifying *why* the first test failed or how this plan fixes it. The stated fact ("little demand") contradicts the path to upside.
*   **Invalid Success Criteria:** Relying on stated intent ("say they would buy") instead of actual transactions or a validated problem statement renders the pilot's results unreliable for a national rollout decision.

**Risks:** Wasting budget on a national rollout based on inflated intent metrics; continuing to push a product with no clear market need.

The plan fails because it attempts to market a solution to a problem the market has already indicated it does not have, using a measurement tool (verbal intent) known to be unreliable in this context.

DECISION: NO-GO
