First call: NO-GO. The core value proposition (reliable handwriting recognition) is explicitly stated as unreliable in testing, and the form factor fails the primary constraint for a "handheld" device (pocketability).

Steelman: Launching now establishes category dominance and brand leadership before competitors, betting that software updates will fix recognition and that early adopters will tolerate size and price.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Avoid reputational damage from a flawed launch; wait for technology to mature. Cost: Lost first-mover advantage. Risk: Competitor leads. Reversibility: High.
2. **Delay and refine:** Fix recognition, shrink size, then launch. Cost: Delayed revenue. Risk: Market window closes. Reversibility: High.
3. **Launch as proposed:** High publicity, high price, flawed tech. Cost: Immediate brand damage if it fails. Risk: High. Reversibility: Low (public perception).

Fit: Assumes the vision is "lead a new category," but a broken flagship product defines the category as "toys" rather than "computers," undermining the north star.

Pre-mortem: The device launches with great fanfare. Reviews slam the inaccurate handwriting and bulky size. Developers ignore the platform due to low adoption. The category is declared dead for a decade. The company stock dips. This is a one-way door for brand reputation; you cannot un-launch a failure.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits recognition is "unreliable" and the device is "too big." These are not risks; they are confirmed defects in the minimum viable product. The price ($700 in 1993) is prohibitive without proven utility.

Blockers:
1. **Core mechanism failure:** The primary input method (handwriting) is admitted unreliable. A computer without reliable input cannot function as a "personal digital assistant."
2. **Form factor violation:** It is too big for a pocket, violating the essential definition of the proposed "handheld" category.
3. **Economic mismatch:** High price combined with low utility (broken input, no software) guarantees poor adoption.

Risks: Unproven category, lack of software, execution difficulty. These are secondary to the blockers.

Kill check: Does the unreliable input concern the core claim? Yes. Do the facts support it will happen? Yes, testing already shows it. Is the commitment irreversible? Yes, public brand damage.

DECISION: NO-GO
