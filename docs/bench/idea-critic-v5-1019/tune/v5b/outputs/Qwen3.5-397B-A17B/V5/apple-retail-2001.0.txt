First call: NO-GO. A loss-making manufacturer with single-digit share proposing high-fixed-cost retail in a low-margin sector during a recession violates basic capital preservation.

Steelman: Direct retail is the only way to demonstrate the integrated music strategy and stop third-party staff from sabotaging sales, justifying the lease risk.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Preserve cash; accept continued channel friction. Low risk, low upside.
2. **Shop-in-shop kiosks:** Dedicated demo zones within existing retailers. Lower capex, shares rent, keeps channel peace.
3. **Proposed stores:** High capex, high fixed burn, direct channel conflict.

Fit: While alignment with a "digital music strategy" is logical, the financial structure (losses + recession + high leases) makes this specific execution unfit for the current stage. Survival precedes strategy.

Pre-mortem: The recession deepens; foot traffic drops but leases remain due. Cash runs out in 18 months. Resellers, feeling undercut, drop the brand entirely. The company enters bankruptcy with empty malls.

Evidence: The proposal admits "recent run of losses," "low margins," and "high lease costs." It offers no math showing how a 3-5% share generates enough volume to cover prime mall rents without immediate profitability. The "poor retail track record in the industry" is a stated fact, not a speculation.

Blockers:
1. **Capital Mismatch:** The company is losing money and cannot sustain the high fixed costs of prime leases during a recession. The numbers do not work.
2. **Channel Suicide:** Aggressively competing with the only current sales force (resellers) while having insufficient share to replace that volume is fatal.
3. **Industry Reality:** The stated "poor retail track record" combined with low PC margins suggests the unit economics are negative by definition.

Risks: Inventory obsolescence, brand dilution if stores fail, recession duration.

Kill Criteria: If a pilot kiosk cannot prove a 20% lift in attach-rate for the music strategy within 6 months, abort.

The plan fails the blocker test: it burns scarce cash on fixed costs in a downturn to fight current partners, relying on a model the industry has already rejected.

DECISION: NO-GO
