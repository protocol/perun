First call: GO. The proposal reframes a low-cost constraint into a fashion opportunity, targeting volume over margin to capture a new market segment.

**Steelman:** Leveraging inexpensive plastic construction to create disposable, trend-driven fashion accessories allows the company to dominate the entry-level market with twelve distinct models, achieving scale (1M units) that offsets low per-unit margins and insulates against brand cannibalization by clearly segmenting the product line as "fashion" rather than "horology."

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Cedes the emerging low-cost, high-volume segment to competitors, risking long-term irrelevance as technology lowers barriers.
*   **Alternative (Quality Focus):** Maintaining premium positioning ignores the shifting consumer demand for affordable, stylish accessories, limiting growth to a shrinking niche.
*   **Fit:** Aligns with a volume-driven growth strategy; assumes the brand can stretch into "fun fashion" without eroding core luxury equity.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the market perceives the plastic watches as "cheap junk" rather than "stylish accessories," or if the supply chain cannot sustain 1M units without quality collapses that *do* bleed into the main brand.
*   **Evidence:** The proposal identifies the core tension (reputation vs. volume). The 1M target is aggressive but necessary to justify the tooling and marketing spend for twelve models.

**Blockers:** None. The risks cited (reputation, taste volatility, price wars) are inherent to the fashion category, not fatal flaws. They are managed by the explicit positioning as "fashion items" (setting different expectations) and the diversity of the twelve-model launch (hedging taste risk).

**Risks:** Brand dilution if marketing fails to distinguish the line; inventory obsolescence if trends shift rapidly. These become kill criteria: monitor sell-through rates by model monthly and brand sentiment surveys quarterly. If brand sentiment drops >5% among core luxury buyers, halt marketing.

**Decision:** The potential upside of capturing a mass market outweighs the manageable risks, provided the "fashion" positioning is strictly maintained to protect the core brand.

DECISION: GO
