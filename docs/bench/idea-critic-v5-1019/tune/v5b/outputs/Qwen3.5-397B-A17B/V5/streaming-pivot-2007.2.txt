First call: GO. The proposal secures the company's survival against an inevitable market shift to digital, even at the cost of short-term DVD profitability.

Steelman: Offering free streaming now, despite limitations, locks in subscriber loyalty and builds a data moat before well-funded giants dominate the space.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Maximizes immediate cash flow but guarantees obsolescence as broadband adoption grows and competitors capture the digital mindset.
2. Paid streaming add-on: Reduces cannibalization risk but slows adoption, failing to build the necessary user base to negotiate better licensing terms later.
3. Wait for technology to mature: Cedes the entire strategic initiative to competitors, making entry impossible later.

Fit: Aligns with the long-term vision of home entertainment delivery, moving from physical logistics to digital distribution.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if licensing costs bankrupt the firm before DVD profits can subsidize the transition, or if subscribers cancel DVDs entirely without engaging with the poor streaming library. This is a two-way door; the service can be scaled back or priced later, but delaying entry is a one-way door to irrelevance. The cost of delay is total market exclusion.

Evidence: The proposal admits high costs and poor quality but correctly identifies the strategic imperative. The "cannibalization" is a feature, not a bug; it is better to cannibalize your own revenue than have a competitor do it for you.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (cost, quality, competition) are inherent to the transition, not factual errors in the plan. The small catalog is a constraint to manage, not a reason to stop.

Risks: High burn rate, potential subscriber confusion, and aggressive competitor response. These become kill criteria: if monthly churn exceeds 5% due to service confusion, or if licensing costs exceed 40% of DVD operating profit within 18 months, the strategy must pivot to a paid tier or pause expansion.

The strongest attack is that this accelerates revenue decline without immediate replacement. This fails because the alternative is not "stable DVD profits" but "zero profits" once the market tips. The move buys time and data.

DECISION: GO
