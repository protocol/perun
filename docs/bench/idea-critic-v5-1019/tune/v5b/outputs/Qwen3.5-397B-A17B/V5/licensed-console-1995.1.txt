First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades a dominant brand's long-term equity for negligible, uncapped downside in a market where execution control is surrendered to a partner with no proven track record in this specific category.

Steelman: Licensing accelerates market entry with zero capital expenditure, leveraging a toy partner's distribution to establish a foothold against entrenched console rivals while generating incremental royalty revenue.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the 1995 context. The console market is a "winner-take-all" ecosystem driven by software libraries and marketing spend, not just hardware availability. By ceding marketing and manufacturing to a toy company, the computer maker ensures the product will be perceived as a cheap peripheral rather than a serious computing platform, directly confirming the risk of brand dilution. Small royalties cannot compensate for the opportunity cost of failing to build a dedicated user base or software ecosystem. Furthermore, if the licensee underinvests in marketing—a high probability given their primary focus on toys—the product will fail silently, leaving the computer maker with no recourse and a tainted brand association.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserves brand equity; avoids distraction in a non-core, high-churn market.
2. Internal skunkworks: Build a controlled, premium prototype to test the waters without licensing the core IP to a third party.
3. Joint venture with equity: Retain control over marketing and product definition rather than a passive royalty model.

Pre-mortem: The console launches as a "toy" rather than a computer, fails to attract third-party developers due to low install base, and the toy company discontinues the line after one holiday season. The computer maker is now associated with a failed gadget, harming its core business reputation.

Blockers:
- The royalty model removes all leverage to ensure adequate marketing spend, which is the primary determinant of console success.
- Entrusting a critical consumer beachhead to a non-core partner creates an uncontrollable variable that threatens the parent brand.

Risks: Market dominance by incumbents, low margin per unit, brand confusion.

The lack of control over the customer experience and marketing narrative in a platform business is a fundamental structural failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
