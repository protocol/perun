First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a hardware manufacturer with zero platform experience to enter a duopoly dominated by incumbents with loyal user bases and exclusive content, while simultaneously fighting a new 3D console from one of those incumbents.

Steelman: A CD-based console could succeed by leveraging cheaper media and superior developer tools to attract third-party support, undercutting the cartridge model's cost and capacity limits.

However, the blocker is immediate and fatal based on the stated facts. The proposal admits the company has "no experience as a console platform holder." In a market where success depends entirely on a software ecosystem (famous in-house series, loyal customers), hardware specs are secondary. The two incumbents control the demand side; without a proven track record or exclusive hits, courting outside developers is speculative at best. Developers will not prioritize a new, unproven platform over established ones, especially when one incumbent is launching a competing 3D system simultaneously.

The economic model also presents a blocker: selling hardware at thin or negative margins requires massive volume to recoup losses via software royalties. Without a guaranteed installed base or exclusive content to drive initial adoption, the company faces unlimited downside risk with no clear path to the critical mass needed for profitability. The risk of being squeezed between two entrenched giants while lacking the core competency (platform management and game publishing) is not a manageable risk; it is a structural failure condition.

The "cheaper disc manufacturing" advantage is irrelevant if no one buys the player to spin the discs. The proposal fails to address how to break the network effect of the existing duopoly without the very assets (experience, IP, installed base) it admits to lacking.

Blockers: No platform experience in a software-driven market; simultaneous launch of competing 3D tech by an incumbent; inability to guarantee developer adoption against established duopoly.
Risks: Hardware margin losses; failure to attract third-party devs; strong incumbent loyalty.

DECISION: NO-GO
