First call: GO. The current roadmap fails the core requirement for thin laptops; staying put guarantees product irrelevance.

Steelman: Migrate the entire line to the dominant architecture within two years, using a translation layer to preserve the existing software ecosystem while securing performance and efficiency leadership.

Options:
1. **Proposed Move:** High execution risk and short-term sales freeze, but secures long-term viability and performance.
2. **Do Nothing:** Safe short-term sales, but product stagnation leads to inevitable market exit as competitors ship superior thin devices.
3. **Partial/Dual Architecture:** Increases complexity and cost, dilutes focus, and delays the inevitable full migration.

Fit: Aligns with the unstated north star of delivering viable, competitive consumer hardware. A distinct brand cannot survive on a distinct but inferior engine.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the translation layer is too slow for key applications or if the announcement causes a prolonged sales collapse before new units ship. This is a one-way door; reversing mid-migration wastes billions. The cost of delay is total loss of relevance in the emerging thin-laptop category.

Evidence: The proposal states the supplier has failed for a decade and lags the market. This is a verified structural deficit, not a temporary slip.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (sales freeze, developer burden, speed penalty, brand dilution, past pain) are execution challenges, not facts proving the plan fails. They are manageable via phased announcements, developer incentives, and performance marketing. None contradict the core claim that the current path is fatal.

Risks: Translation performance, channel panic, developer friction. These become kill criteria: if benchmarked translation speed falls below 80% of native on key apps by Q4, or if pre-orders miss targets by >30%, the rollout pauses.

The "distinctiveness" argument is a trap; uniqueness without competence is a niche for collectors, not a mass market. The painful history is a reason to plan better, not to stay broken.

DECISION: GO
