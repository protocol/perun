First call: GO. The proposal trades a rapidly commoditizing cost (storage) for a defensible differentiation (search) and a proven monetization model (text ads), disrupting a complacent market.

**Steelman:** By offering 1,000x the standard storage, the service eliminates the user habit of deleting mail, creating a permanent, searchable data archive that increases switching costs and ad targeting precision over time.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Cost:** Critics cite storage expense, but hardware costs follow Moore's Law; the marginal cost of a gigabyte is falling faster than user acquisition costs. Doing nothing yields zero data leverage.
*   **Privacy:** Scanning for ads is the friction point. However, automated scanning differs from human review. The alternative (subscription fees) fails to achieve mass adoption required for network effects.
*   **Market Maturity:** Incumbents are constrained by legacy infrastructure and low-storage business models. They cannot pivot to "delete nothing" without cannibalizing existing tiers.
*   **Revenue:** Delaying revenue for user growth is standard for platform plays. The "no direct revenue" risk is mitigated by the immediate deployability of text ads once critical mass is reached.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if privacy backlash triggers regulatory bans or if users reject automated scanning. It also fails if storage costs plateau unexpectedly. However, the invitation-only launch controls server load and creates scarcity, mitigating early infrastructure risk.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The math on storage costs vs. ad yield works on a projected curve, not today's spot price. No fact presented proves the model insolvent.
*   **Risks:** Privacy perception is a reputational risk, not a functional blocker; it becomes a kill criterion if user churn exceeds 5% post-launch due to privacy complaints. Storage cost spikes are a financial risk, mitigated by the capped invitation rollout.

**Kill Criteria:** If cost-per-user exceeds ad-yield-per-user by month 18, or if privacy litigation halts content scanning, pivot to a freemium storage model. Leading metric: Daily active users searching archived mail (>6 months old).

The proposal correctly identifies that email's value lies in retrieval, not just delivery. The asymmetry of offering vast storage against entrenched low-storage competitors creates an immediate wedge.

DECISION: GO
