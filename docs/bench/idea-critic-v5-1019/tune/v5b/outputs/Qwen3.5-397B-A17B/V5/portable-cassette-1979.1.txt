First call: GO. The proposal targets an unserved demographic (youth) with a portable, affordable music experience, leveraging existing technology to create a new use case (personal listening) despite channel skepticism.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, portable playback device creates a new "personal music" category for youth, where low price and portability outweigh the lack of recording features or dealer enthusiasm.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Channel Doubt:** Dealers prefer familiar high-margin hi-fi. *Counter:* Youth buy where they congregate; direct marketing bypasses skeptical staff.
2.  **Antisocial Perception:** Headphones isolate. *Counter:* Isolation is the feature, not a bug; it allows music anywhere without disturbing others.
3.  **No Research:** Demand is unproven. *Counter:* Existing sales of bulky portable recorders suggest desire for mobility; research often fails to predict novel behaviors.
4.  **Copycats:** Built from existing parts. *Counter:* First-mover brand association and speed to market matter more than patent moats in fashion-driven electronics.
5.  **Large Run Risk:** High inventory exposure. *Counter:* Essential to hit the youth price point; volume is the strategy, not just a constraint.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Cede the emerging portable market to competitors who might iterate faster.
*   *Add recording:* Increases cost/size, missing the price/portability sweet spot for the target demographic.
*   *Wait for research:* Delays launch, allowing competitors to define the category.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the price misses the youth budget or if the "no speaker" constraint confuses buyers too severely. However, the low component cost supports the price target, and the clear value prop (music on the go) addresses the confusion.

**Blockers:** None. The risks are executional (marketing, distribution), not fundamental flaws in the value proposition. The lack of recording is a strategic simplification, not a defect.
**Risks:** Channel resistance, copycats. These are managed by aggressive marketing and speed.

The proposal correctly identifies a gap between current product offerings (heavy, expensive, recording-focused) and user desire (light, cheap, listening-focused). The constraints (no speaker, no record) are strengths that enable the price and form factor.

DECISION: GO
