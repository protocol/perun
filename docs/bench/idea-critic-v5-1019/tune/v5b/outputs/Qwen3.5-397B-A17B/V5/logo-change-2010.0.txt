First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a high-risk, irreversible brand change with zero validation and a rollout strategy designed to maximize confusion and backlash.

Steelman: A simultaneous, unannounced global rebrand creates a sharp "shock" moment that forces immediate market attention, potentially accelerating the perception of modernity by erasing the past instantly.

However, the claim fails the blocker test. The proposal explicitly admits the new design was "not tested with customers" while acknowledging "loyal customers are attached to the old logo." In retail, alienating the core revenue base to chase a vague "contemporary" feel without data is a fundamental strategic error. The "no advance announcement" clause removes the ability to manage the narrative, guaranteeing that social media backlash will frame the change as an erasure of heritage rather than an evolution.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retains brand equity but fails the modernization goal.
2. Phased rollout with testing: Allows measurement of customer sentiment and adjustment of the design before full commitment. This reaches the same upside (modernization) with significantly lower risk of revenue loss.
3. The proposed "big bang": Highest cost, highest risk, zero evidence of success.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-launch, sales dip 15% as loyalists boycott confused by the unrecognizable branding; social channels are flooded with negative comparisons to competitors; the company is forced to revert or spend millions on damage control. The "one-way door" nature of a simultaneous global switch means there is no graceful retreat once the old assets are destroyed.

Blockers:
1. Zero customer validation on a change explicitly stated to risk alienating the core base.
2. "No advance announcement" strategy ensures maximum confusion rather than managed transition.

Risks:
1. Backlash magnitude unknown but likely high given social media dynamics.
2. Loss of brand recognition equity built over decades.

The absence of a pilot, testing, or communication plan constitutes a failure of basic operational due diligence for a mature retailer.

DECISION: NO-GO
