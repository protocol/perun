First call: NO-GO. The plan trades a functional product for a psychological one based on incomplete data.

**Steelman:** Replacing the formula maximizes immediate taste preference scores, securing relevance against sweeter competitors.

**Attack:** The evidence is fatally flawed. Blind taste tests measure isolated sensory input (a single sip), not long-term consumption or brand relationship. Crucially, the testing protocol withheld the core consequence: the permanent removal of the original. Participants chose "sweeter" assuming the original remained an option; they did not choose "sweeter instead of original." This invalidates the data for a replacement strategy.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Retain share erosion but preserve brand equity and loyalist trust.
2.  **Line Extension:** Launch the sweeter formula as a new variant (e.g., "New Cola") alongside the original. This captures the taste-preference segment without alienating the heritage base.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** High risk of catastrophic backlash due to the "endowment effect"—consumers value what they might lose more than what they gain.

**Fit:** This violates the brand's north star of heritage and continuity. A century-old formula is not just a recipe; it is a cultural constant. Removing it entirely ignores the "emotional attachment" risk explicitly raised in the proposal.

**Pre-mortem:** Launch day triggers outrage. Loyalists feel betrayed not by the taste, but by the loss of choice. Media narratives shift from "taste test winner" to "corporation erasing history." Bottlers face boycotts. The company is forced to reintroduce the old formula months later, admitting defeat and permanently damaging credibility.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Data Contradiction:** The proposal's own cited risk ("emotional attachment") contradicts the blind test methodology which ignored emotion. The test proves nothing about a scenario where the original is removed.
2.  **Missing Fallback:** Withdrawing the original entirely leaves no safety net if the hypothesis fails.

**Risks:** Brand irrelevance, bottler revolt, permanent loss of core demographic.

**Kill Criteria:** If the plan proceeds, it must include a committed date to reintroduce the original if sales dip below baseline within 30 days. However, given the flawed data foundation regarding the removal constraint, the risk is too high to approve as stated.

DECISION: NO-GO
