First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to launch a high-cost, high-friction hardware product into an unproven mass market without solving its primary existential threats.

**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 validates demand and funds iteration before a general release of a revolutionary everyday wearable.

**Attack:**
The "everyday consumer" positioning directly contradicts the identified risks. A $1,500 price point excludes the mass market required for an "everyday" product, while the lack of clear daily utility ensures low retention even among early adopters. The privacy objection is not a minor hurdle but a potential social blocker; a device that records bystanders without clear indicators invites public backlash and regulatory scrutiny, effectively killing social acceptance.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoids reputational damage and hardware inventory risk while the ecosystem matures.
2.  **Enterprise-First Pivot:** Target specific industrial or medical use cases where the $1,500 cost is justified by productivity gains and privacy concerns are manageable. This validates the tech without needing mass social acceptance.
3.  **Developer Kit Only:** Sell strictly to builders at cost to foster software creation, delaying the consumer pitch until "killer apps" exist.

**Pre-mortem:**
The product launches, early adopters wear it for two weeks, then stop due to battery anxiety and social awkwardness. Media narratives solidify around "privacy violation," leading to bans in restaurants and theaters. The company is stuck with unsold inventory and a brand associated with creepiness rather than innovation.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Value Proposition Mismatch:** The proposal claims "everyday consumer" status while admitting "no clear everyday use." This is a fundamental contradiction that no amount of marketing can fix.
2.  **Social/Privacy Hostility:** The stated risk of "privacy objections" combined with "social acceptance" issues creates a hostile environment for adoption that an invitation-only program cannot insulate against once units leave the box.

**Risks:** High price, short battery life, and unproven demand are severe but secondary to the lack of a core use case and the social license to operate.

The plan fails because it asks the mass market to accept a expensive, intrusive device with no defined purpose.

DECISION: NO-GO
