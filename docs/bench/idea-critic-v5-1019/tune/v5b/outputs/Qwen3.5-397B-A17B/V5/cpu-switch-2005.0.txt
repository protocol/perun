First call: GO. The current roadmap fails the core requirement for thin, efficient laptops, and the dominant architecture offers a verified path to close that gap.

**Steelman:** Migrating to the dominant architecture within two years, buffered by a translation layer, is the only viable strategy to regain performance competitiveness and secure long-term supply viability, despite short-term friction.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Guarantees product irrelevance as thermal and efficiency gaps widen. The brand dies with distinctiveness but no sales.
*   **Dual Architecture:** Increases BOM costs and engineering complexity without solving the core thermal deficit of the legacy supplier.
*   **Risks:** Sales freezes and developer friction are real but transient. The "painful earlier transition" is a sunk cost; repeating it is painful, but avoiding it is fatal. Translation slowness is an acceptable tax for compatibility during the bridge period. Brand distinctiveness must shift from "unique chip" to "best user experience," which the current chip cannot deliver.

**Pre-mortem:** The plan fails if the translation layer is too slow for pro users or if the announcement timing cannibalizes Q4 revenue entirely. However, these are execution risks, not strategic blockers. The cost of delay is immediate loss of market share to competitors with superior thermal profiles.

**Blockers:** NONE. The facts state the current supplier *has not* delivered and *lags* the competitor. This is a verified failure of the status quo.
**Risks:** Developer adoption speed, translation overhead, brand perception shift. These become kill criteria: if translation performance drops below 80% native speed for key apps by month 18, or if developer sign-ups lag projections by >30% at the one-year mark, the plan requires a pivot.

The proposal addresses the existential threat with a concrete timeline and a mitigation strategy (translation). The risks are manageable compared to the certainty of decline under the current path.

DECISION: GO
