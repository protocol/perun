First call: NO-GO. The $4 billion price tag against a franchise with recent mixed reviews and an departing creative lead presents an immediate financial and creative mismatch that cannot be justified without a proven revitalization plan.

Steelman: Acquiring the franchise secures a dormant library capable of fueling parks and merchandise for decades, provided the new trilogy successfully re-engages the fanbase.

However, the attack on this claim reveals fatal flaws in the current proposal. First, the "mixed reviews" of recent entries indicate eroding brand equity; paying a premium for damaged goods without a concrete creative turnaround strategy is speculative gambling, not strategic investment. Second, the founder's retirement removes the primary architect of the universe's appeal. Replacing a singular visionary with a committee approach often alienates the core fanbase, directly threatening the "feed all businesses" goal if fans reject the new trilogy. Third, the valuation ignores the constraint of existing merchandise licenses. If much of the value is already locked in long-term contracts, the immediate revenue synergy to justify the $4 billion cash outlay is illusory.

Alternatives: Doing nothing preserves capital for organic growth or acquiring healthier IP. Licensing specific rights for parks or limited film projects offers upside with capped risk, avoiding the $4 billion balance sheet hit.

Pre-mortem: The trilogy launches to fan backlash due to tonal shifts, box office underperforms relative to the massive production and marketing spend required to "fix" the brand, and the stock price drops as the $4 billion investment is written down. The parks see no lift because the brand momentum has stalled.

Blockers: The proposal lacks a verified creative lead to replace the founder, a detailed plan to overcome the "mixed review" trend, and a financial model showing how existing licensing constraints allow for immediate ROI. The price assumes success without proving the mechanism to achieve it.

Risks: Fan rejection, creative vacuum, overpayment for declining assets. These are not merely risks but active blockers because the proposal offers no mitigation for the loss of the founder or the recent quality decline. Without a named showrunner or a pilot script proving viability, the commitment is too large and irreversible.

DECISION: NO-GO
