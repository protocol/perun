First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a distribution problem with forced integration despite three prior failures in the same domain and a market leader protected by massive network effects.

**Steelman:** Integrating social layers into existing utility products (search, mail) leverages current traffic to bootstrap a network, while unique features (circles, hangouts) offer a wedge the incumbent ignores.

**Attack:**
1.  **History as Data:** Three previous failures indicate a structural mismatch between the company's core utility identity and social engagement, not merely an execution error. Repeating the strategy with higher coercion (executive bonuses, forced integration) amplifies the risk of user backlash without guaranteeing adoption.
2.  **Network Effects:** The incumbent's hundreds of millions of users create a barrier that "better features" rarely overcome. Users switch only when the entire graph moves; niche features like circles are insufficient to trigger mass migration.
3.  **Incentive Distortion:** Tying executive bonuses to adoption metrics encourages dark patterns (forced opt-ins, spammy notifications) that degrade the core search and mail products, potentially driving users away from the company's primary revenue generators.
4.  **Forced Integration:** The risk of annoying existing users is not hypothetical; it is a near-certainty when social features are injected into utility workflows. This threatens the core business to chase a speculative secondary market.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserve core product integrity; avoid further brand damage from failed social attempts.
*   *Acquire:* Buy an existing emerging network rather than building from scratch against an incumbent.
*   *Standalone:* Launch a distinct brand without integrating into search/mail to protect core utilities.

**Blocker:** The proposal ignores the empirical evidence of three prior failures and the economic reality of network effects, relying on coercion (integration + bonuses) rather than organic value creation. The plan explicitly risks degrading the core business (search/mail) to subsidize a low-probability venture.

**Risks:** User churn from core products, reputational damage, wasted engineering capital.

**Kill Criteria:** If the beta does not achieve 20% organic retention (excluding forced prompts) within 6 months, or if core product usage drops by >2%, the initiative must terminate.

DECISION: NO-GO
