First call: NO-GO. The plan attempts to enter a saturated market with an unproven business model and a timeline that contradicts the scope of work, creating a high probability of resource waste without strategic gain.

Steelman: Reusing assets to launch a free-to-play last-player-standing mode in two months will capture market share from the rival and generate cosmetic revenue without significantly hurting paid game sales.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** High risk of cannibalization, likely timeline slippage given the "two months" constraint for a complex multiplayer mode, and zero experience in free-to-play economics.
2. **Do Nothing:** Protects the paid game's value and avoids sinking costs into a genre dominated by an entrenched rival.
3. **Paid DLC Expansion:** Leverages existing monetization trust, avoids direct competition with the free rival, and fits the two-month window more realistically.

Fit: The proposal contradicts the studio's current strength (paid cooperative games) and ignores the "north star" of sustainable revenue by jumping into an unfamiliar free-to-play model during a trend peak.

Pre-mortem: The mode launches late (4+ months), fails to retain players against the rival, confuses the existing user base, and cannibalizes 20% of paid sales, resulting in net revenue loss. This is a two-way door, but the cost of delay is low compared to the cost of a failed launch.

Evidence: The proposal admits the studio has "not run a free-to-play business before" and the rival "already owns the genre." The "two months" estimate is an unsupported assumption for a networked competitive mode.

Blockers:
1. **Capability Gap:** The team lacks verified experience in free-to-play economies, a core requirement for the proposed cosmetic revenue model.
2. **Timeline Contradiction:** Building a stable last-player-standing mode (networking, balance, anti-cheat) in two months using existing assets is historically improbable and unstated as a prototype.
3. **Cannibalization Risk:** No evidence is provided that a free mode will not erode the core paid product's value, which is the studio's only proven revenue stream.

Risks: Market saturation, brand dilution, operational distraction.

The lack of free-to-play expertise and the unrealistic timeline constitute factual blockers to the plan's success as proposed. The cannibalization risk remains unmitigated.

DECISION: NO-GO
