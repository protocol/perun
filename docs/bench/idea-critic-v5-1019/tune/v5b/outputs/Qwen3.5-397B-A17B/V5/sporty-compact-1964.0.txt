First call: GO. The demographic shift is a verified fact; the product directly targets an underserved segment with high-margin customization.

**Steelman:** Leverage existing low-cost infrastructure to deliver perceived premium style and customization to a massive, incoming demographic wave, maximizing margin through options while minimizing development risk.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the emerging "youth" market to competitors; rely on aging economy car sales.
2.  **New platform:** Build dedicated sporty chassis. Prohibitive cost and time; misses the immediate demographic window.
3.  **Proposed plan:** Reuse reliable mechanicals, invest in styling and marketing. Highest ROI, fastest time-to-market, reversible via production caps.

**Fit:** Aligns with capturing market share during a demographic boom. Assumes the "sporty" aesthetic can be achieved without expensive re-engineering.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the "cheap" parts perception outweighs the styling, or if the advertising fails to define a new category. However, the low base price limits downside exposure. This is a two-way door; production can be slowed if initial sales lag. Cost of delay is losing the first wave of buyers to rivals.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on the stated fact of the "postwar baby boom" entering the market. The mechanical reuse is a cost-control measure, not a defect, given the base car's reliability reputation.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (unproven demand, cheap perception, ad spend) are execution risks, not factual blockers. The "cheap" perception is mitigated by the specific styling cues (long hood, short deck) which historically signal performance. The ad spend is the necessary cost to create the category.

**Risks:** Demand volume uncertainty; brand dilution. These become kill criteria: monitor initial order mix and option attachment rates. If option uptake is low, the "customizable" thesis fails.

**Decision:** The plan exploits a verified demographic inevitability with a capital-efficient product structure. The risks are manageable via the long options list (testing price elasticity) and phased production.

DECISION: GO
