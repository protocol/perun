First call: NO-GO. The plan to immediately migrate a cult brand with a "quirky" independent distributor network into mass-market channels and corporate advertising ignores the specific assets driving its value: exclusivity and community loyalty.

Steelman: Leveraging the buyer's scale will accelerate growth by fixing distribution gaps and amplifying reach, outweighing the risk of brand dilution.

Attack: The proposal assumes the brand's success is transferable via the buyer's existing playbook (mass ads, supermarket dominance). However, the "cult image" and "independent distributor loyalty" are likely inversely correlated with mass-market exposure. Moving to supermarkets alienates the core niche; corporate advertising destroys the "quirky" authenticity. The risks listed (loyalty, image survival) are not mere possibilities but near-certainties if the integration strategy is immediate assimilation. The "do nothing" alternative (acquire but operate as a standalone subsidiary with distinct distribution) preserves the upside of ownership while mitigating the destruction of the brand's core equity. A cheaper, safer alternative exists: acquire for growth capital and backend support only, leaving front-end distribution and marketing tone untouched for a 24-month probation.

Pre-mortem: One year post-acquisition, sales flatten as core fans reject the "sold out" narrative, independent distributors defect to competitors, and the brand becomes just another SKU on a shelf, failing to compete with established soft-drink giants who have deeper pockets for the very mass-market war the buyer intends to start.

Blockers: The proposal's core mechanism (immediate migration to buyer's distribution/ad model) directly contradicts the stated source of the target's value (cult image, independent loyalty). The plan offers no evidence that this specific consumer segment responds positively to mass-market tactics; historical analogs suggest the opposite. The commitment ($1.7B cash) is too large to risk on an unproven integration hypothesis that threatens the asset's primary differentiator.

Risks: Competition from soft-drink giants is real, but entering their arena (mass market) without a differentiated position guarantees defeat.

Kill criteria: If the brand cannot maintain >15% growth in its original channels within 12 months post-acquisition, the integration strategy is failing.

DECISION: NO-GO
