First call: GO. The offer solves the company's two biggest constraints: capital for unproven technology and distribution access, while leveraging their unique artistic IP.

**Steelman:** Partnering allows the company to pioneer a new medium (feature-length CGI) using their proprietary short-film expertise, mitigating financial ruin through studio backing while retaining creative ownership of a high-concept toy story.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Remain a commercial shop; risk losing the lead animator to the suitor and stagnating as CGI shorts become commoditized.
*   **Self-Finance:** Impossible. Rendering costs and timeline for a feature exceed the company's cash flow; failure means bankruptcy before release.
*   **Wait for Tech:** Rendering speeds will improve, but the window to establish brand identity as *the* CGI feature pioneer will close.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of transitioning from service provider to IP owner. The "toy's point of view" is a scalable franchise hook, not just a film plot.

**Pre-mortem:** The film fails if the story cannot sustain 90 minutes or if rendering bottlenecks delay release until the tech is obsolete. However, the studio partner absorbs the distribution risk. This is a one-way door; once resources shift, there is no returning to the prior small-scale model.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The lack of prior feature experience is a capability gap, not a factual blocker; the studio deal provides the runway to bridge it. The "stronger hand" of the partner is a negotiation risk, not a deal-breaker, given the company holds the only proven CGI storytelling talent.
*   **Risks:** Technical debt in rendering pipelines; creative dilution by the studio. These are managed via milestone-based funding and creative control clauses (kill criteria).

**Kill Criteria:**
1.  **Metric:** Complete a 5-minute proof-of-concept sequence within 6 months proving rendering scalability.
2.  **Stop Condition:** If the studio demands creative changes that void the "toy perspective" core premise, terminate negotiations.
3.  **Leading Metric:** Audience test scores for the short pilot exceeding commercial client benchmarks.

The risk of inaction (losing talent, missing the market shift) outweighs the execution risk. The proposal converts a defensive position into an offensive market creation.

DECISION: GO
