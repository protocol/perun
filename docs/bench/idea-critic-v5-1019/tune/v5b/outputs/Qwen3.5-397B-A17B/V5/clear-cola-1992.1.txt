First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a costly nationwide launch based solely on test market data that the proposal itself admits may overstate demand, while ignoring the high probability of flagship cannibalization and low repeat purchase rates.

Steelman: A clear cola captures a new "purity" segment without eroding the core brand, leveraging positive test results to secure first-mover advantage before rivals react.

Attack:
1.  **Evidence Flaw:** The proposal explicitly cites "test markets can overstate demand" and "novelty buyers may not repeat" as known risks. Proceeding to a *large* national campaign without a mechanism to verify repeat purchase rates in the test phase is financially reckless. Test markets measure trial, not loyalty.
2.  **Cannibalization vs. Growth:** If cola flavor is psychologically linked to color by shoppers (a stated risk), the product fails on taste perception. If it succeeds on taste, it likely steals share from the flagship rather than expanding the total category, especially given "slowing soft-drink growth." The proposal offers no evidence that this is net-new volume.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "limited regional rollout" option is superior. A limited rollout allows verification of the *repeat* rate (the true killer) without the sunk cost of a national marketing blitz. If rivals launch copies, they are copying a potential failure, which is a benefit, not a threat.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** One year post-launch, the product is delisted. Why? Initial trials were high due to curiosity, but repeat purchases collapsed because consumers rejected the taste-color mismatch or realized the "purity" claim was marketing fluff. The massive marketing spend drained resources from the flagship brand during a growth slowdown.

Blockers:
-   **Unverified Repeat Rate:** The proposal jumps to national launch without confirming if novelty buyers convert to regulars. The stated risk "novelty buyers may not repeat" is a known unknown that blocks a major commitment.
-   **Cannibalization Net-Negative:** No data proves this extends the brand rather than fracturing it in a shrinking market.

Risks:
-   Flavor-color psychological barrier.
-   Rapid competitor imitation eroding margins.

Kill Criteria Missing: There is no defined metric for repeat purchase rate required to trigger national expansion, nor a cap on marketing spend pending loyalty verification.

DECISION: NO-GO
