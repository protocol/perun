First call: NO-GO. The compressed timeline and massive inventory commitment create a catastrophic risk profile that outweighs the seasonal opportunity.

Steelman: A single developer can deliver a minimally viable product in five weeks that leverages the film's brand to capture holiday sales, justifying the large production run through volume.

Attack: The five-week constraint is the primary failure point. Normal development cycles are months; compressing this to five weeks for a single programmer guarantees technical debt, bugs, and likely an unfinished product. Poor quality combined with a "hit film" license creates reputational damage beyond financial loss. Furthermore, ordering a "very large" production run before verifying product quality or market reception ignores the basic principle of demand validation. If the game is poor (highly probable) or the film's popularity fades, the company faces massive unsold inventory costs on top of the already high license fee. The risk of total capital loss on inventory and licensing is unmitigated.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Save inventory costs, preserve brand reputation, accept license fee loss.
2. Small batch release: Produce a limited run to test quality and market response before committing to large volumes.
3. Delay launch: Miss Christmas but ensure a quality product for the following year.

The proposal fails because it couples the highest probability of failure (rushed development) with the highest consequence (massive inventory). It beats "do nothing" only if the game is both finished and good within five weeks, which contradicts industry standards. The "large production run" decision must be decoupled from the development start.

Blockers:
- Timeline impossibility: Five weeks is insufficient for a commercial-quality game based on a hit film, making the core deliverable unlikely.
- Inventory risk: Committing to large volumes without a finished, tested product violates basic operational safety.

Risks:
- Brand damage from releasing a broken product.
- Financial ruin from unsold inventory.

Kill criteria: If a playable, bug-free demo is not achieved in two weeks, halt immediately. Production run must be capped at 10% of the proposed volume until sales velocity is proven.

DECISION: NO-GO
