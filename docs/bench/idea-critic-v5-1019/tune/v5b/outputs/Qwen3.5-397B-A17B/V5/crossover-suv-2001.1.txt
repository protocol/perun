First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits the core differentiator (styling) tested poorly with the target demographic, while the platform is aging and compromised.

**Steelman:** A cost-efficient, niche vehicle leveraging existing minivan assets to capture a youthful outdoor market through unique camping utility, accepting polarizing styling as a filter for early adopters.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Fit Failure:** The primary risk is confirmed fact, not speculation: early clinics rated styling poorly. For a "youthful" demographic, aesthetics are the primary purchase driver. An unattractive vehicle with coolers cannot sustain 30,000 annual sales.
2.  **Platform Decay:** Building a "crossover" on an aging minivan platform contradicts the "youthful" positioning. It signals obsolescence before launch.
3.  **Compromised Execution:** Admitted cost cuts during development suggest the final product quality will not support the brand's reputation, increasing warranty risks and lowering resale value.
4.  **False Differentiation:** "No real off-road ability" undermines the camping narrative. If it cannot reach the campsite without a paved road, the utility argument collapses against true SUVs or rugged trucks.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; avoid launching a known stylistic failure.
*   **Refresh Existing Minivan:** Invest in the core platform rather than forcing a square peg into a round hole.
*   **True Crossover:** Develop a dedicated platform if the segment is truly viable (higher cost, higher risk, but honest product).

**Pre-mortem:** The vehicle launches to mockery for its looks, fails to attract youth, alienates traditional minivan buyers, and sells 12,000 units in year one. The division loses millions on tooling and marketing for a compromised asset.

**Blockers:**
*   Confirmed negative customer reception of the defining feature (styling).
*   Contradiction between product constraints (aging platform, no off-road) and marketing promise (youthful, camping).

**Risks:** Brand dilution, inventory write-downs.

**Kill Criteria:** If styling cannot be revised to test positive in clinics before tooling lock, the project must stop. Since the proposal states styling *is* poor and cost cuts *prevented* fixing it, the condition for success is already unmet.

DECISION: NO-GO
