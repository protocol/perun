First call: GO. The core claim is that a mandatory client solves update delivery and cheating, which are existential threats to a live-service PC game business, and the proposed risks are manageable compared to the cost of inaction.

Steelman: A proprietary client is the only viable mechanism to enforce real-time anti-cheat integrity and guarantee patch synchronization for a growing online multiplayer ecosystem, creating a defensible moat that retail distribution cannot provide.

Options analysis shows "do nothing" fails as cheating escalates and fragmentation increases. Relying on third-party portals cedes control and revenue. The proposed path, while risky, captures the platform upside.

Fit is strong: the strategy shifts from pure retail product sales to a recurring service model, aligning with the emerging broadband era despite incomplete penetration. The "mandatory" aspect is the friction required to solve the cheating vector.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the client launches with game-breaking bugs or if broadband adoption stalls longer than expected, causing a retail revolt. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The cost of delay is immediate revenue loss to cheaters and increased support costs for manual updates.

Evidence check: The proposal correctly identifies that boxed media cannot handle frequent balance patches or real-time validation. The risks cited (broadband gaps, retail pushback) are real but do not invalidate the core necessity of the solution; they merely dictate a phased rollout or strong communication strategy. No internal data contradicts the premise that cheating and update latency are critical failures.

Blockers: NONE. The stated risks (user dislike, retail friction, bugs, broadband gaps, copycats) are all *risks*, not blockers. They represent execution challenges or market headwinds, not facts that prove the plan fails as proposed. Broadband is growing; retail can be managed with hybrid SKUs; bugs are solvable; copying validates the market. None of these stop the initiative.

Risks: User churn due to friction, temporary retail shelf loss, initial stability issues. These become kill criteria: if active users drop >15% post-mandate or if two major retailers delist within Q1, pivot to optional client with limited multiplayer features.

The strongest attack—that mandatory access alienates the non-broadband base—fails because the alternative (unpatchable, cheat-ridden games) alienates the entire paying base over time. The proposal addresses the root cause of the business's current struggle.

DECISION: GO
