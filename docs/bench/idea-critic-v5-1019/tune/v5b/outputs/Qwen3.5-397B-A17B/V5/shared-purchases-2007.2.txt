First call: NO-GO. The core mechanism—broadcasting sensitive user purchases to friends—creates immediate, high-severity reputational and legal liability that an opt-out cannot mitigate.

**Steelman:** Sharing purchase data with explicit notification and an opt-out increases ad relevance while respecting user agency through transparency and choice.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Mismatch:** Users do not want sensitive purchases (medical, gifts, financial) broadcasted. An "opt-out" places the burden of privacy on the user, creating a "privacy trap" where silence implies consent. This violates the fundamental expectation of confidentiality in transactions.
2.  **Partner Risk:** Partners face public embarrassment if user reactions to their products are negative and visible in feeds. They will likely refuse participation or demand strict controls that negate the viral value.
3.  **Irreversibility:** As noted in the proposal, this is a one-way door. Once trust is broken by exposing sensitive data, it cannot be restored by simply turning the feature off. The backlash would be instantaneous and catastrophic.
4.  **Alternatives:** Aggregated, anonymized data sharing achieves the advertising revenue goal without exposing individual identities or specific transactions. Targeted ads based on private signals are safer and industry-standard.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fatal Flaw:** The proposal assumes users will actively manage privacy settings for sensitive data. Evidence from similar historical launches suggests the vast majority will not, leading to involuntary exposure of sensitive information.
*   **Contradiction:** The goal is "earn more," but the mechanism risks losing all users and partners, resulting in net negative revenue.

**Risks:**
*   Mass user exodus due to privacy violations.
*   Legal action regarding data protection (even in 2007, basic confidentiality expectations existed).
*   Partner churn.

**Conclusion:** The "notification + opt-out" model is insufficient for high-sensitivity data like purchases. The risk of irreversible brand damage and user loss vastly outweighs the potential ad revenue. The proposal fails the basic test of not harming the core asset (user trust) to monetize it.

DECISION: NO-GO
