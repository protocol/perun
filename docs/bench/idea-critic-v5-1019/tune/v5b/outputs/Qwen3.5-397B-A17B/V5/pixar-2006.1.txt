First call: NO-GO. The $7.4B price for a single-film-per-year studio creates an unsustainable valuation multiple without guaranteed volume expansion, and the all-stock structure dangerously concentrates power by making the studio's founder a major shareholder of the acquirer.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio is the only way to secure its creative talent against rivals and revitalize the conglomerate's failing hand-drawn unit, justifying a premium price for exclusive access to a hit-making machine.

**Attack:**
1.  **Valuation Blocker:** At ~$7.4B for one film annually, the deal implies a valuation per film far exceeding historical box-office returns, even for hits. Without a committed slate increase, the math fails.
2.  **Governance Risk:** The founder becoming a major shareholder creates a conflict where one individual can dictate strategy or block future moves, effectively holding the conglomerate hostage.
3.  **Culture & Dependence:** Placing external leaders in charge of the internal unit risks exiling existing staff without guaranteeing integration success, while tying the entire animation future to a few individuals violates diversification principles.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Risk losing the partnership but avoid $7.4B debt/equity dilution.
*   *Distribution Only:* Secure first-look deals without buying the overhead or governance risks.
*   *Minority Stake:* Gain economic exposure without ceding control or creating a dominant shareholder.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, the next two films underperform relative to the massive acquisition cost, the founder uses their shareholder position to block cost-cutting, and the internal animation unit collapses due to culture clash. The stock price tanks.

**Blockers:**
*   The price relies on perpetual "hit" status with no volume growth; one miss destroys ROI.
*   The governance structure allows the target's founder to overpower the buyer's board.

**Risks:** Culture clash, key person dependence, market shift away from theatrical animation.

**Kill Criteria:** If the studio cannot commit to a 3-year slate of 2+ films pre-close, or if governance caps on the founder's voting rights are not established, the deal must stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
