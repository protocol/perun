First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a massive, irreversible infrastructure build (new dealer network) and a fixed 1957 launch date despite an explicit, admitted risk of recession and a potentially shrinking segment.

**Steelman:** Capturing the growing mid-priced gap requires a distinct brand to avoid diluting existing lines, justifying the dealer investment to compete with the market leader.

**Attack:**
1.  **Timing & Irreversibility:** Launching a capital-intensive dealer network right before a potential recession is fatal. Dealerships are high-fixed-cost assets; if the segment shrinks as feared, these become immediate liabilities. The "fixed 1957 debut" removes the option to wait for economic clarity.
2.  **Better Way:** The proposal ignores cheaper alternatives. Could the gap be filled by upgrading existing models within current brands or sharing dealerships temporarily? Building a whole new network is the most expensive, least reversible option.
3.  **Evidence Gap:** The decision relies on "extensive market research" from a growing market, but admits the market *could* shrink. There is no verified data showing demand will hold during a downturn, nor a plan to pause if early indicators flash red.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** If a recession hits in 1957, the new division will launch into a contracting market with no established loyalty, burdened by underutilized dealers. The parent company's cash flow will be drained supporting a failing network, potentially endangering the core low-priced and premium lines.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fixed Launch vs. Economic Risk:** The commitment to a 1957 debut despite a known recession risk creates a scenario where the company cannot pivot. A sound plan must include a "go-if" economic trigger or a phased rollout that delays dealer construction until demand is proven.
*   **Lack of Staged Entry:** No pilot or capped first step is proposed. The plan is "all-in" on day one.

**Kill Criteria Missing:** There is no defined metric to abort the dealer rollout if pre-launch orders or economic indicators deteriorate.

**Decision:** The plan fails the reversibility test. It bets the farm on a specific economic window while admitting that window might close. Without a phased approach or an economic escape hatch, the risk of catastrophic loss outweighs the potential gain.

DECISION: NO-GO
