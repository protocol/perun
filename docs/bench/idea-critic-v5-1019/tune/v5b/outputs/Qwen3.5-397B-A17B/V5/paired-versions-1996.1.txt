First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a mechanic that requires a second player and hardware accessory during a platform decline, creating a fatal friction point for early adoption.

Steelman: Releasing two versions forces social interaction and doubles the addressable market per user, creating a viral loop that compensates for marketing budget shortages.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Safe, but the game likely fails due to low visibility on a dying console.
2. Single version with AI trading: Removes the hardware barrier but kills the social "must-have" urgency.
3. Two versions (Proposed): Highest risk, highest potential reward via forced socialization.

Fit: Assumes the "collect them all" drive outweighs the hassle of finding a friend and buying a cable. On a declining platform, friction usually kills novelty.

Pre-mortem: The game launches; players buy one version, realize they cannot complete the dex without finding a specific friend with the other version and a link cable. They return the game as "broken" or "incomplete." Sales stall because the core loop requires a critical mass of users that doesn't exist yet. The "price grab" perception alienates the shrinking install base.

Evidence: The proposal admits the publisher is unsure of sales and the platform is aging. There is no evidence of an existing community large enough to sustain the trading economy at launch. The requirement for a second player and cable is a hard dependency, not a soft feature.

Blockers:
1. The core mechanic (trading) is impossible for a solo player without immediate access to a second copy and cable. On a declining platform, the probability of a random buyer finding a trading partner instantly is near zero, rendering the game's primary goal (completing the collection) unachievable for early adopters. This contradicts the value proposition.
2. The "price grab" perception on a dying system may trigger immediate backlash, reducing the already small total addressable market.

Risks:
1. Low install base prevents the network effect from starting.
2. Hardware accessory (link cable) attachment rate is unknown but likely low.

The plan fails because it bets on a network effect that cannot ignite without an initial critical mass, which the aging console cannot provide. The friction is too high for the proposed market conditions.

DECISION: NO-GO
