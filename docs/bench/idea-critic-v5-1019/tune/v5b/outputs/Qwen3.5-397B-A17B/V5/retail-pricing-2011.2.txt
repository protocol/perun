First call: NO-GO. The plan eliminates the core revenue mechanism (discounts) for a customer base explicitly defined as coupon-trained, without evidence they will accept everyday pricing, and commits the entire chain to this unproven model simultaneously.

**Steelman:** The current discount model erodes brand value and margins; a clean everyday pricing strategy with branded shops will attract new, higher-value shoppers and restore profitability, outweighing the loss of legacy coupon users.

**Attack:**
1.  **Contradiction of Facts:** The proposal states most sales historically come from discounted items and customers are "trained to wait for coupons." Removing discounts immediately removes the primary purchase trigger for the existing revenue base.
2.  **Missing Evidence:** There is zero data showing this specific demographic will adapt to everyday pricing. The CEO's success in *electronics* (a different category with different purchase cycles) is not proof this works for *department stores* with this specific audience.
3.  **Fatal Execution Risk:** Rolling out across the entire chain without a pilot removes the ability to detect failure before it becomes catastrophic. If the hypothesis is wrong (high probability given the customer profile), the company faces immediate, unrecoverable revenue collapse before any "new" customer base can be built.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "do nothing" approach preserves current cash flow. A "pilot" approach (testing in 5-10 stores) would validate the pricing shift with limited downside. The current plan beats neither: it risks total failure compared to doing nothing, and offers no learning loop compared to piloting.

**Blockers:**
*   **Revenue Cliff:** The plan removes the known driver of >50% of sales (discounts) for the only known customer segment (coupon-driven) without validated replacement demand.
*   **Irreversibility:** A chain-wide rollout makes mid-course correction impossible once sales tank; the brand damage and cash burn would likely force bankruptcy before a pivot could occur.

**Risks:** Customer alienation, competitor exploitation of the price gap, operational chaos in rebuilding stores. These are secondary to the immediate blocker of eliminating the core business model without validation.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot were run, a >15% drop in same-store sales in month 1 would trigger a stop. Since no pilot exists, the precondition for "GO" (evidence of viability) is missing.

DECISION: NO-GO
