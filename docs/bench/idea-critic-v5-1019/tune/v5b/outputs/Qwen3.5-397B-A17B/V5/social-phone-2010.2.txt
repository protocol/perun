First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores fatal market mismatches for the target demographic.

**Steelman:** Leveraging an acquired brand to launch a closed, carrier-exclusive social phone on a new OS will capture teens despite high costs and no apps, justified by sunk development costs.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economic Mismatch:** Teens are highly price-sensitive. A mandatory data plan costing as much as a full smartphone plan removes the value proposition. If the total cost of ownership rivals premium devices with app stores, the target audience cannot afford the entry.
2.  **Feature Deficit:** Social-networking teens rely on diverse, rapidly evolving third-party applications. Launching with *no* application store against rivals with large catalogs renders the device functionally obsolete for its specific use case on day one.
3.  **Platform Fragmentation:** Building a new in-house OS instead of using the company's existing mobile platform doubles execution risk and delays time-to-market, while offering no compensating benefit to the user.
4.  **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** Two years of sunk development is a reason to cut losses, not to compound them with a go-to-market strategy that contradicts the core needs of the customer.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; avoid launching a product destined to fail against established app ecosystems.
*   **Pivot:** Port the acquired brand's software to the company's existing, proven mobile platform and launch on an open store model, removing the carrier lock-in to lower costs.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because teens refuse the high monthly fee and cannot install the specific social apps they use daily. The new OS lacks developer support, creating a barren ecosystem. The company writes off the hardware investment and damages the acquired brand's reputation.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price:** The data plan cost equals a full smartphone plan, pricing out the teen demographic.
*   **Utility:** Zero app store at launch directly contradicts the "social networking" value proposition.
*   **Strategy:** Ignoring the existing internal mobile platform increases risk without justification.

These are not risks; they are factual contradictions between the proposal's constraints and the market reality. The sunk costs do not validate a flawed business model.

DECISION: NO-GO
