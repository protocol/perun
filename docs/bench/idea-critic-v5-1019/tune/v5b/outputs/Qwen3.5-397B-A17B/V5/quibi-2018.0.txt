First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to commit over $1 billion to a subscription model in a market where the core value (short-form video) is already abundant for free, while ignoring the stated risk of "subscription fatigue."

**Steelman:** A premium, mobile-first studio can succeed by offering exclusive, high-budget short-form content with superior orientation technology that free platforms cannot match, convincing users to pay despite fatigue.

**Attack:**
1.  **Demand Contradiction:** The proposal admits "no proven demand for premium short-form" and that "free ad-supported short video is abundant." Charging a premium for a format users currently expect for free, without evidence of willingness to pay, is a fundamental market mismatch.
2.  **Subscription Fatigue:** With "subscription fatigue" explicitly cited as a rising concern, launching a new, expensive niche service contradicts the macro trend. Users are consolidating, not expanding, paid subscriptions.
3.  **Feature Insufficiency:** "Video looking right vertically or horizontally" is a baseline usability expectation, not a differentiated value proposition worth a billion-dollar bet. It does not solve the content demand gap.
4.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "licensing content to existing giants" options offer similar upside (monetizing talent) with drastically lower capital risk and no need to overcome consumer fatigue alone.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal states there is "no proven demand" for the core product (premium short-form).
*   **Fact:** The market condition ("subscription fatigue") directly opposes the revenue model (new subscription).
*   **Calculation:** A $1B+ commitment requires proven unit economics or desperate strategic necessity; neither is present. The stated facts show the target users likely do not want to pay for this specific format.

**Risks:** High production costs, competition for attention, and unproven pricing power are noted but secondary to the lack of demand validation.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The core claim is that buyers will pay. The proposal's own facts state demand is unproven and fatigue is high. The commitment is massive and irreversible. This creates a blocker: proceeding assumes a market behavior that current evidence suggests does not exist.

DECISION: NO-GO
