First call: NO-GO. The price is 12x the last valuation with no revenue, no monetization plan, and explicit founder resistance to the buyer's core business model (ads).

**Steelman:** The acquisition is a defensive necessity to stop mobile messaging from displacing the buyer's entire ecosystem; the $19B pays for immediate distribution of hundreds of millions of users that cannot be built organically fast enough, valuing growth over current unit economics.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Absurdity:** Paying $19B for 50 employees and minimal revenue violates all standard capital allocation logic. The jump from $1.5B to $19B in one year implies a bubble, not fundamental value.
2.  **Strategic Incompatibility:** The target explicitly refuses advertising, the buyer's only known monetization engine. Integrating them risks destroying the very utility that drives their growth, leaving the buyer with an expensive, non-revenue-generating asset.
3.  **Execution Risk:** Retaining founders who oppose the buyer's strategy is unlikely; losing them post-acquisition often kills the product's momentum.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves $19B cash. Building a clone or partnering costs a fraction of this. The "cost of delay" is hypothetical; the cost of overpayment is certain.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, regulators scrutinize privacy, the founders quit within 18 months, the user base migrates to the next new app because the "no ads" promise is broken or the culture clashes, and the buyer writes down $15B in goodwill.

**Blocker:** The absence of any credible path to monetization compatible with the target's constraints, combined with a price tag that requires perfection in a highly volatile market, constitutes a factual failure of the investment thesis as presented. The proposal admits "no monetisation plan" yet asks for a record-breaking spend. This is speculation, not strategy.

**Risks:** Regulatory backlash, cultural integration failure, technology obsolescence. These are severe but secondary to the primary blocker: buying a business with no defined economic engine at a peak valuation.

DECISION: NO-GO
