First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades a strategic asset (platform control) for negligible financial return in a market where the licensor lacks leverage.

**Steelman:** Licensing accelerates market entry with zero capital expenditure, bypassing the maker's weak consumer presence to establish the platform via a partner's distribution.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** "Small royalty" on a "low-cost" console yields trivial revenue. Without marketing control, volume will likely miss targets, making the total return insignificant compared to the long-term value of the platform IP.
2.  **Strategic Dilution:** In 1995, "internet console" is an unproven category. Handing this to a toy company risks associating a serious computer brand with ephemeral toys, failing to build the intended brand equity. If the product fails due to poor marketing (a high probability given the licensee's primary focus on toys), the platform itself becomes tainted as a "toy" rather than a computing standard.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves the IP for a future, more capable internal launch or a partnership with a stronger electronics distributor. A "joint venture" with shared marketing costs and brand oversight would mitigate the risk of the licensee under-investing in promotion.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sells poorly because the toy company treats it as a niche gadget rather than a computing revolution, and the computer maker is stuck with a devalued platform and no path to recover direct manufacturing rights.

**Blockers:**
*   **Misaligned Incentives:** The licensee (toy company) has no proven track record in sustaining high-tech marketing campaigns, and the contract leaves marketing entirely to them. The proposal admits this risk but offers no mitigation (e.g., minimum marketing spend, co-branding approval).
*   **Value Destruction:** The combination of low unit price, small royalty, and high risk of brand dilution creates a negative expected value for the licensor. The cost of losing control over the platform's trajectory exceeds the potential upside of small royalties.

**Risks:** Market dominance by incumbents is a risk, but ceding control to an ill-suited partner guarantees failure rather than risking it.

The proposal fails because it monetizes the asset at the bottom of its potential value curve while transferring all execution risk to a partner ill-equipped to handle it, with no safety rails for brand protection.

DECISION: NO-GO
