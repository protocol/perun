First call: GO. The proposal directly attacks the three stated barriers to entry: poor prior hardware (solved by focusing on a single "paper-like" use case rather than a general computer), publisher piracy fears (solved by built-in cellular control and closed ecosystem), and low adoption (solved by removing the PC requirement and simplifying discovery).

Steelman: A dedicated, connectivity-enabled device removes friction for non-technical readers and assures publishers of content security, creating a new market rather than fighting for existing hardware share.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Cede the digital future to competitors; print sales eventually decline anyway.
2. **Software-only app:** Fails in 2007 due to fragmented mobile screens, lack of ubiquitous data, and high piracy risk, alienating publishers.
3. **Proposed Hardware:** High upfront cost but captures the entire value chain (device + content) and solves the specific constraints of 2007.

Fit: Aligns with the vision of "any book, anywhere, instantly." The hardware is a means to the end of selling books, not a divergence from the core business.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the device is too expensive, the screen is hard to read, or publishers refuse content. However, the proposal addresses the screen (paper-like) and publisher fear (cellular control/DRM). The cost risk is mitigated by the "low price per title" volume strategy.

Evidence: The proposal correctly identifies that previous failures stemmed from trying to be too many things (general PDAs) and lacking easy content delivery. The "computer-free" requirement is the critical differentiator supported by the cellular inclusion.

Blockers: NONE. The lack of hardware experience is a risk, not a blocker; it necessitates hiring or acquiring talent, which is feasible given the clear specification. The fear of cannibalization is illusory; digital books expand the total addressable market to impulse buyers and travelers who cannot carry print.

Risks: Margin compression on hardware; publisher pushback on pricing. These are managed via the "free cellular" subsidy model (locked ecosystem) and volume sales.

The strongest attack is that hardware is a distraction from retail. This fails because the hardware is the only viable distribution channel for the specific "instant, anywhere" value proposition in this era. Without it, the digital store cannot launch effectively.

DECISION: GO
