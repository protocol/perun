First call: NO-GO. A $1,000 device with an undefined value proposition and no content library cannot survive against cheaper, evolving alternatives.

**Steelman:** A dedicated $1,000 hub bridges the gap between game consoles and PCs by offering superior interactive media, assuming consumers will pay a premium for a specialized living room experience before PCs fully converge.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $1,000, this costs significantly more than game consoles and approaches entry-level PC prices. Unlike a PC, it lacks general utility; unlike a console, it lacks a proven game library. The "multimedia" benefit is abstract compared to the tangible utility of a computer or the entertainment value of a console.
2.  **The Content Trap:** Interactive titles require software support. A small library at launch creates a negative feedback loop: no buyers without hits, no hits without buyers. PCs are gaining multimedia capabilities natively, eroding the unique selling proposition before launch.
3.  **Consumer Confusion:** The proposal admits buyers may not understand the product. Marketing a vague "interactive" category at a luxury price point is a recipe for market rejection. The "do nothing" alternative (waiting for PC convergence or console evolution) carries lower financial risk and leverages existing consumer understanding.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to poor sales due to sticker shock. Developers ignore the platform due to low install base. The unit is discounted heavily within 12 months, destroying brand equity, while consumers continue using PCs for multimedia and consoles for gaming.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price Disadvantage:** The $1,000 price point exceeds the perceived value of a single-function media player when multi-function PCs are available at similar prices.
*   **Missing Ecosystem:** The admitted "small library" of interactive titles is a fatal flaw for a hardware platform dependent on software sales.
*   **Strategic Drift:** The product fights two established categories (PC and Console) without clearly winning either, violating the requirement to beat "do nothing."

**Risks:** Market confusion and rapid PC technological catch-up are secondary to the fundamental economic mismatch.

**Kill Criteria:** If a clear path to securing 10+ major interactive titles at launch cannot be verified, or if a sub-$500 manufacturing cost cannot be achieved to allow a competitive retail price, the project must stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
