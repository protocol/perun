First call: GO. The proposal leverages existing assets (compact platform, converted factory) to address a clear market gap (garage-friendly family van) for a company with limited cash, making it a sound operator's move despite risks.

**Steelman:** Reusing the compact-car platform and a converted factory minimizes capital expenditure, allowing a cash-strapped manufacturer to create a unique, car-like van for families that fits in garages, differentiating it from commercial rivals.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Guarantees continued reliance on government aid and potential collapse as station wagons lose favor.
*   **New Platform:** Financially impossible given the cash constraint; would delay launch and increase risk.
*   **Commercial Van Focus:** Ignores the specific family need for car-like handling and garage fitment, ceding the emerging "minivan" category to competitors.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This aligns with the survival imperative. If it fails, it will likely be because marketing fails to distinguish it from commercial vans or the compact platform compromises cargo utility too severely. However, the "car-like" driving experience is the key differentiator that mitigates the commercial stigma. The risk of rivals copying is secondary; being first to define the category with low overhead is the primary advantage.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The facts state the company has "little cash" and a "compact-car platform." The proposal directly addresses the cash constraint by reusing assets. No factual blocker exists: the lack of a current category is an opportunity, not a contradiction. The risk of buyers seeing it as commercial is real but manageable through positioning, not a reason to halt. The platform limitation is a trade-off for cost and handling, not a dealbreaker.

**Decision:**
Blockers: NONE. The proposal's constraints (cash, platform) are turned into features (low cost, car-like drive).
Risks: Category education, competitor response. These are mitigated by the low-cost entry and first-mover advantage in a new segment.

The plan survives the attack. The lack of a category is the very reason to proceed, and the financial constraints dictate this specific approach.

DECISION: GO
