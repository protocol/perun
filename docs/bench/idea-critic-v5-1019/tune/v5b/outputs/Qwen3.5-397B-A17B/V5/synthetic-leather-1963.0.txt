First call: NO-GO. The proposal asserts a premium launch despite higher costs, unproven comfort, and manufacturer resistance without a plan to overcome these specific deficits.

Steelman: The material's durability justifies a premium price if comfort matches leather and manufacturers can adopt it without retooling.

Attack:
1. **Cost vs. Value:** The synthetic costs more than leather. Shoe manufacturers operate on thin margins; they will not switch to a more expensive input unless it offers a decisive advantage (e.g., significantly lower waste or higher speed). The proposal admits wearers may find it less comfortable, removing the primary consumer pull needed to force manufacturer adoption.
2. **Adoption Friction:** Manufacturers are unwilling to change factories. Introducing a new material often requires adjusting cutting tools, adhesives, or stitching tensions. Without a pilot proving zero retooling costs, the "heavy marketing" spend targets the wrong audience; manufacturers care about production efficiency, not brand hype.
3. **Fashion Risk:** If fashion moves away, the company is left with expensive, durable inventory that no one wants. Leather is biodegradable and traditional; a synthetic failure is permanent waste.

Alternatives:
- **Do nothing:** Avoids capital loss on a product with three known negatives (cost, comfort, adoption).
- **Niche Pilot:** Target a specific non-fashion use case (e.g., industrial boots) where durability outweighs comfort and fashion risks, verifying the "premium" claim before a full shoe launch.
- **Subsidized Transition:** Instead of marketing spend, use funds to subsidize manufacturer retooling or prove the material works on existing lines.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because shoe makers refuse to alter production lines for a material that costs more and feels worse than leather. Marketing cannot bridge a functional deficit in B2B manufacturing.

Blockers:
- **Economic Inversion:** Higher cost + lower perceived comfort = no market demand.
- **Operational Inertia:** No evidence provided that factories can use this without changes.

Risks: Fashion trends shifting; long-term comfort complaints damaging the brand.

The proposal relies on marketing to solve a product-market fit problem defined by negative unit economics and functional drawbacks. It fails the basic test of offering a better or cheaper alternative to the incumbent.

DECISION: NO-GO
