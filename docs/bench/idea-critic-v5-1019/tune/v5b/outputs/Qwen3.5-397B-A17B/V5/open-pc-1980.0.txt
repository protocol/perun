First call: GO. The growth of small-computer makers signals a market shift that the current integrated model cannot address quickly enough; a separate team with off-the-shelf parts is the only viable path to speed.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, isolated team can bypass corporate inertia to deliver a competitive personal computer within a year by leveraging existing ecosystems (software, components, retail) rather than rebuilding them internally.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary fear is that open design invites copying and lowers margins. However, in a nascent market, speed and ecosystem adoption (outside software) create a moat faster than proprietary control. "Do nothing" cedes the emerging personal computing segment to agile rivals. Building internally using standard practices guarantees missing the window; the proposed "separate team" structure mitigates cultural contamination while enabling speed.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits the survival imperative: capture growth or face obsolescence. If we wait for perfect margins or total control, competitors will own the channel and the developer mindshare. The cost of delay is total exclusion from the personal computer market.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal relies on the stated fact of rapid competitor growth and the feasibility of off-the-shelf assembly. No internal facts contradict the ability to form a small team. The risks (copying, supplier dependence, lower margins, no retail experience) are execution challenges, not blockers. They are inherent to the new business model, not flaws in the plan to enter it. The plan explicitly accepts these trade-offs to achieve speed.

**Decision:**
There is no factual blocker. The risks are managed by the structure (separate team) and the strategic necessity of the move. The alternative is irrelevance in the new category.

Blockers: NONE.
Risks: Lower margins, copycats, retail inexperience (accepted as cost of entry).
Kill Criteria: If the prototype cannot be built with off-the-shelf parts within 6 months, or if no major software vendor commits to the platform before launch.

DECISION: GO
