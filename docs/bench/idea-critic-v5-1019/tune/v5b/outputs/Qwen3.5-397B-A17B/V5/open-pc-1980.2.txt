First call: GO. The market signal (small-computer makers growing fast) demands an immediate response that the current integrated model cannot deliver quickly enough.

Steelman: To capture the emerging personal computer market before it consolidates, the company must bypass its slow, proprietary development cycle by creating an autonomous unit using commoditized parts and external distribution, accepting lower margins and IP risks as the cost of speed and relevance.

The "do nothing" option cedes the high-growth segment to agile rivals, likely resulting in long-term irrelevance as the market shifts away from mainframes. Building a proprietary system internally would take too long, missing the window of opportunity. The proposed path trades control for velocity.

Pre-mortem: The project fails if the parent company re-absorbs the team, enforcing standard processes that delay launch until the market moves on, or if the reliance on outside software results in a subpar user experience that damages the brand. However, these are execution risks, not fundamental flaws in the strategy.

The risks cited (copying, supplier dependence, lower margins, no retail experience) are inherent to the new category, not unique failures of this plan. In a commodity market, copying is inevitable; speed to market and brand trust become the differentiators. Supplier dependence is the price of using off-the-shelf parts to achieve the one-year timeline. Lower margins are typical for high-volume consumer hardware compared to specialized mainframes. Lack of retail experience is solved by partnering with existing stores rather than building a new sales force.

No blocker exists in the stated facts. The proposal correctly identifies that the company's usual practices are incompatible with the new market's requirements. The separation of the team and the use of open standards are necessary conditions to succeed, not weaknesses. The alternative—applying the mainframe model to PCs—is the actual path to failure.

Blockers: NONE.
Risks: Execution drag from parent culture, brand dilution if software quality falters, margin compression. These are managed by maintaining team autonomy and setting clear volume targets to offset lower per-unit profit.

DECISION: GO
