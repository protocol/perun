First call: GO. The proposal trades a commoditized constraint (storage) for a defensible moat (search) and a proven monetization path (text ads), disrupting a stagnant market where incumbents rely on user friction (deletion) rather than utility.

**Steelman:** By offering 1,000x the standard storage and native search, the service renders "email management" obsolete, turning the inbox into a permanent, searchable knowledge base. Ad relevance increases with data volume, creating a flywheel where heavy usage improves revenue per user, funding the storage costs that deter competitors.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Cost Risk:** Critics argue 1GB/user is financially suicidal. *Rebuttal:* Storage costs are dropping faster than adoption rates; the "waste" of unused space is lower than the acquisition cost of users forced to delete data on rival platforms.
*   **Privacy Objection:** Scanning mail feels invasive. *Rebuttal:* Users already trade privacy for free services; the value exchange (permanent storage + search) is superior to the status quo. Automated scanning is distinct from human reading.
*   **Market Maturity:** Incumbents dominate. *Rebuttal:* Their dominance relies on artificial scarcity. Removing scarcity changes the game entirely.
*   **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" cedes the future to whoever solves search first. "Paid only" fails to reach the scale required for ad-network effects. "Smaller free tier" repeats the failed models of the past.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if ad relevance remains low despite scanning, or if storage costs plateau unexpectedly. However, the invitation-only launch caps initial exposure, allowing cost validation before full scale.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The math holds: ad revenue per active user in a search-literate ecosystem outweighs marginal storage costs. No fact presented proves the model insolvent.
*   **Risks:** Privacy backlash and high initial burn rate. These are managed via the invitation-only rollout (controlled growth) and clear terms of service. They do not invalidate the core claim.

The proposal correctly identifies that the incumbent's weakness is not price, but utility. By solving the "delete or keep" anxiety, the service creates a lock-in effect stronger than any existing brand loyalty. The lack of direct revenue at launch is a feature of the user-acquisition strategy, not a flaw.

DECISION: GO
