First call: GO. The market signal (small-computer makers growing fast) demands an immediate response that the current integrated model cannot deliver speedily.

Steelman: To capture the emerging personal computer market before it consolidates, the company must bypass its slow, proprietary development cycle by creating an autonomous team using commoditized parts and external distribution, accepting lower margins and imitation risks as the cost of entry speed.

**Blockers:** NONE. The proposal explicitly acknowledges the risks (copying, supplier dependence, lower margins, lack of retail experience) and accepts them as trade-offs for speed. No internal fact contradicts the core claim that this approach is necessary to compete with fast-growing rivals. The "risks" are known market dynamics, not hidden flaws in the plan's logic.

**Risks:**
1.  **Imitation:** Competitors may copy the open design. *Mitigation:* Speed to market and brand trust become the moat; being first with a standard platform often beats being best with a proprietary one.
2.  **Margin Erosion:** Lower margins than mainframes. *Mitigation:* Volume compensation; this is a new category, not a mainframe replacement.
3.  **Channel Inexperience:** No retail history. *Mitigation:* Using independent stores leverages existing ecosystems rather than building costly internal infrastructure.

**Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because the autonomous team was re-absorbed into corporate processes, slowing delivery, or because the company hesitated to fully commit to the open ecosystem, making the product uncompetitive.

**Kill Criteria:** If the prototype cannot be built within 12 months using off-the-shelf parts, or if independent retailers refuse to stock the unit due to lack of software support, the project stops.

**Decision Method:** The cost of delay (missing the entire PC boom) vastly outweighs the risk of lower margins or imitation. The "do nothing" option guarantees irrelevance in this segment. The proposed path is the only viable two-way door to test the market without dismantling the core mainframe business.

DECISION: GO
