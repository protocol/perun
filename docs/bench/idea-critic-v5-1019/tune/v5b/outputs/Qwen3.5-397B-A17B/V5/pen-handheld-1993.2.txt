First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a full launch despite verified testing showing the core mechanism (handwriting recognition) is unreliable, the form factor fails the primary portability requirement ("too big for a pocket"), and the ecosystem (software) is absent.

Steelman: Launching a $700 pen-based device with heavy CEO-backed publicity will define a new handheld category by leveraging first-mover advantage, assuming the market will tolerate early technical flaws for the novelty of digital organization.

Attack:
1.  **Core Mechanism Failure:** The proposal admits handwriting recognition is "unreliable in testing." For a device marketed primarily on pen input, this is not a risk; it is a functional defect that invalidates the primary value proposition. Users cannot rely on a tool that fails its main task.
2.  **Form Factor Mismatch:** A "handheld" computer that is "too big for a pocket" contradicts the essential utility of personal digital assistants: immediate, ubiquitous access. If it does not fit, it will not be carried; if not carried, it will not be used.
3.  **Ecosystem Void:** "Little software" at a $700 price point (a significant premium in 1993) offers insufficient value. Hardware without applications is a toy, not a productivity tool.
4.  **Price vs. Value:** High price combined with unproven category, unreliable tech, and poor portability creates a scenario where the cost of failure (inventory write-downs, brand damage from a flawed flagship) vastly outweighs the potential upside of defining a category that may not exist.

Blockers:
-   **Functional Defect:** Core input method is unreliable (stated fact).
-   **Design Failure:** Device size violates the "pocket" requirement for personal handhelds (stated fact).
-   **Value Gap:** High price with no software ecosystem (stated fact).

Risks:
-   Unproven market category.
-   Execution difficulty in software development.

The stated facts confirm the product fails on functionality, portability, and value. Heavy publicity will only accelerate the exposure of these flaws, leading to a high-visibility failure. The plan does not address these blockers; it attempts to market over them.

DECISION: NO-GO
