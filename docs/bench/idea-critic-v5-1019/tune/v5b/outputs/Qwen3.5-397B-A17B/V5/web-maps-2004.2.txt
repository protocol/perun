First call: GO. The shift from desktop to web, combined with satellite imagery and routing, creates a uniquely defensible position despite 2004 constraints.

**Steelman:** A free, browser-based map with satellite views and routing will become the global standard because accessibility trumps performance, and imagery creates an emotional hook no text-based competitor can match.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Remain a small desktop niche; eventual obsolescence as the web dominates.
*   **Licensed Desktop App:** High friction, limited reach, fails to leverage the acquirer's search distribution.
*   **Web App (Proposed):** High engineering risk (browser speed), high cost (licensing), no immediate revenue. However, "slow browsers" is a transient technical debt, while "global accessibility" is a permanent strategic asset. Established competitors lack the satellite differentiator and the search giant's distribution engine.

**Fit:** Aligns with organizing the world's information; moves the north star from "search results" to "spatial context."

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if licensing costs bankrupt the project before ad-tech matures, or if browser rendering remains too clunky for mass adoption. However, the "no revenue model" risk is mitigated by the acquirer's existing search-ad monopoly; maps become a high-value user retention tool, not a standalone P&L.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The cited risks (cost, speed, competition) are execution hurdles, not factual contradictions. Browser speed will improve; licensing is a solvable negotiation; competitors are vulnerable to the satellite surprise.
*   **Risks:** High burn rate, technical latency, unproven monetization. These become kill criteria: if browser frame rates do not double within 12 months, or if licensing costs exceed 20% of projected user-acquisition value, pivot or halt.

**Decision:** The proposal bets on a platform shift (web over desktop) and a modal shift (visual over textual). The acquirer's unique ability to subsidize losses via search ads neutralizes the "no revenue" blocker. The technical limits of 2004 browsers are a temporary tax, not a dealbreaker.

DECISION: GO
