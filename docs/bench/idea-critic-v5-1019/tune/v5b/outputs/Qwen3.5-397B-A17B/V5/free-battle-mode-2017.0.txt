First call: GO. The proposal leverages existing assets to enter a high-demand genre with a low-cost, reversible experiment that addresses the risk of irrelevance given the rival's dominance.

Steelman: Reusing paid assets to launch a free-to-play last-player-standing mode in two months creates a new revenue stream via cosmetics while mitigating cannibalization by targeting a different market segment (free users) and extending the IP's lifespan.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Retain paid-only model; high risk of losing relevance as the rival dominates the popular genre.
2. **Build new IP:** High cost, high risk, slow time-to-market; fails to leverage current assets.
3. **Proposed plan:** Low incremental cost, fast execution, captures free-market demand; best risk/reward ratio.

Fit: Aligns with survival and growth in a shifting market where the "last-player-standing" genre is proven.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the two-month timeline forces a buggy launch that damages the brand, or if cosmetic sales fail to cover server costs. This is a two-way door; the mode can be shut down if metrics fail. Cost of delay is losing the window to compete with the rival's momentum.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the fact that assets are reusable (reducing dev time) and the genre is popular (validating demand). The two-month estimate is aggressive but plausible given asset reuse.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (cannibalization, lack of F2P experience, rival dominance) are valid concerns but do not constitute factual blockers. Cannibalization is mitigated by the free vs. paid distinction; lack of experience is a learning curve, not a stopper; rival dominance validates the market size.

Risks: Execution risk on the tight timeline; monetization uncertainty without prior F2P data. These become kill criteria: if player retention or cosmetic conversion falls below industry benchmarks within 30 days of launch, the mode is paused.

The plan commits minimal new capital (mostly opportunity cost of dev time). The cap is the two-month development window. Kill criterion: If daily active users do not reach 10% of the paid game's base within 45 days, or if net revenue is negative after server costs by month three, the project stops.

DECISION: GO
