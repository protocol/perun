First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to spend heavily on marketing a product that costs more than the incumbent, offers lower comfort, and faces adoption friction, with no stated upside to justify these deficits.

**Steelman:** Launching now creates a beachhead for a durable material, betting that marketing can override higher costs and inferior comfort to shift manufacturer behavior before fashion trends turn.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** High spend, immediate premium launch. Cost: High. Risk: Catastrophic (negative unit economics, brand damage from comfort issues). Reversibility: Low (sunk marketing spend).
2.  **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital. Cost: Zero. Risk: Missing a future trend. Reversibility: High.
3.  **Niche Pilot:** Target specific non-comfort use cases (e.g., rain boots, work gear) where durability outweighs comfort, without heavy spend. Cost: Low. Risk: Moderate. Reversibility: High.

**Fit:** Assuming the goal is profitable market share, this plan contradicts basic unit economics. A premium price requires superior performance or status; here, performance (comfort) is lower, and status is unproven.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because shoe manufacturers refuse to retool for a material that makes their end-product more expensive and less comfortable, leading to consumer rejection. Fashion shifts to natural fibers, leaving inventory stranded. This is a one-way door for the marketing budget; once spent, it cannot be recovered.

**Evidence:** The proposal's own facts state the synthetic costs *more* and is *less* comfortable. In footwear, comfort is a primary driver of repeat purchase. Marketing cannot permanently sustain a product that is objectively inferior in function and higher in price than the standard.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Negative Value Proposition:** The product is strictly worse (higher cost, lower comfort) than the incumbent leather. No fact suggests a compensating benefit (e.g., waterproofing, ethical sourcing) strong enough to overcome this.
2.  **Adoption Friction:** Manufacturers have no incentive to change factories for a product that reduces their competitiveness.

**Risks:** Fashion volatility and comfort perception are secondary to the fundamental economic blocker.

**Decision:** The plan fails the basic test of offering a better alternative. It attempts to buy traction for a deficient product.

DECISION: NO-GO
