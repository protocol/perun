First call: GO. The demographic shift creates a clear opening for affordable style, and leveraging existing mechanicals minimizes development risk while maximizing margin potential through options.

**Steelman:** Capture the emerging youth market by repurposing proven, low-cost mechanics into a high-style package, using customization to drive profitability and a massive ad blitz to define a new category before competitors react.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Cede the youth segment to rivals; brand ages with current buyers.
2.  **New platform:** Build dedicated sports car mechanics. Prohibitive cost and time; misses the immediate demographic wave.
3.  **Proposal:** Reuse compact mechanics with new styling. Lowest cost, fastest time-to-market, highest margin via options. It beats doing nothing by capturing growth and beats a new platform by preserving capital.

**Fit:** Aligns with the need to refresh the brand image without alienating the core reliability reputation. The "cheap" mechanical base is a feature (affordability), not a bug, provided the styling masks it.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the styling fails to hide the economy roots, making the car look "cheap" rather than "smart," or if the ad spend fails to create a cultural phenomenon. However, the two-way door nature of options allows mid-course correction on features. The cost of delay is losing the entire baby boom cohort to competitors.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on the stated fact of the baby boom entering the market. The risk of "unproven demand" is mitigated by the low base price and the ability to test options elasticity post-launch. The "cheap" perception risk is addressed by the specific design mandate (long hood, short deck) which signals performance regardless of underpinnings.

**Blockers:** NONE. No stated fact contradicts the core claim that style + low price = volume.
**Risks:** Unproven volume demand; perception of cheapness. These are managed by the low base investment (shared parts) and the kill criterion of early sales velocity against ad spend.

The risks are executional, not fundamental. The plan converts a fixed cost liability (R&D) into a variable cost opportunity (options).

DECISION: GO
