First call: GO. The proposal addresses a critical vulnerability: commodity pricing driven by buyer ignorance. If buyers cannot distinguish the core component, the manufacturer loses pricing power to clone makers.

**Steelman:** By funding co-branded advertising, the chip maker shifts demand pull from the computer manufacturer to the end user, forcing computer makers to include the superior chip to satisfy customer requests, thereby securing premium pricing and market share against clones.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Continue selling B2B. Result: Clone makers erode margins using similar part numbers; buyers remain indifferent. This fails to protect the core business.
2.  **Direct Consumer Education Only:** Expensive and lacks the distribution leverage of computer makers' existing ad spend.
3.  **The Proposal:** Risks include channel resentment and high cost. However, the "unprecedented" nature of component branding is the very source of the potential moat. If it works, competitors cannot easily copy the brand equity already established. The cost is high, but the cost of commoditization is existential.

**Pre-mortem:** The campaign fails if computer makers refuse to display the logo or if consumers ignore it. However, the financial incentive (subsidized advertising) aligns interests; computer makers get cheaper ads, the chip maker gets brand placement. Resentment is mitigated by the immediate financial benefit.

**Evidence & Logic:** The proposal correctly identifies that "buyers do not know which chip is in their computer." This information asymmetry is the root cause of the clone threat. Breaking this asymmetry is the only strategic lever available. The risks (budget share, resentment) are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The alternative—remaining an invisible component supplier in a cloning market—is a guaranteed decline.

**Blockers:** NONE. The facts presented show a clear path from buyer ignorance to commodity pricing, and a logical mechanism to reverse it.
**Risks:** High budget consumption and channel friction. These are managed by the program's performance-based nature (paying only when ads carry the logo).

The proposal transforms a B2B weakness into a B2C strength. It forces the market to recognize quality, distinguishing the product from clones. The unprecedented nature of the move is its primary advantage, creating a barrier to entry for competitors who rely on anonymity.

DECISION: GO
