First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits the studio's entire steady income to a single unproven format with a 10x cost increase, advised against by all industry insiders, creating an existential threat to the business.

**Steelman:** A feature film could revolutionize the medium and secure long-term dominance if audiences accept the format.
**Attack:** The core claim—that audiences will sit for a feature—is unproven speculation. The "risk" of audience rejection is actually a near-certain probability given zero precedents. Funding this via short-film profits removes the safety net; one failure destroys the studio. Insiders advising against it represents a verified market signal, not just conservatism.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain thin but steady profits; survive.
2.  **Staged Pilot:** Produce a medium-length special (3-4x cost) to test audience endurance before committing to a full feature.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Bet the company on a 10x cost leap.
The proposed plan does not beat "do nothing" on risk-adjusted returns because the downside is total insolvency.

**Fit:** Assumes the goal is "revolutionize industry" over "sustain business." If the north star is survival, this fails.

**Pre-mortem:** The film releases, audiences laugh at the novelty but refuse to pay feature prices, attendance collapses, and the studio cannot fund the next short, leading to bankruptcy. This is a one-way door; you cannot get the money back.

**Evidence:** No data exists supporting feature-length cartoon viability. All cited evidence (insider advice) contradicts the plan. The budget math (10x cost) vs. current "thin profits" implies a multi-year burn with no revenue bridge.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Funding Structure:** Using operating profits from shorts to fund a 10x speculative venture leaves no runway for errors or delays. The math dictates that a single delay causes insolvency.
2.  **Market Validation:** Zero evidence supports the core revenue assumption (ticket sales for long-form animation). Proceeding without a smaller-scale test violates basic capital preservation.

**Risks:** Budget overrun, audience rejection, talent drain. These are secondary to the structural blocker of funding.

**Kill Criteria:** None exist in the proposal. There is no cap, no pilot, and no stop-loss.

**Decision:** The plan lacks a survival mechanism. It requires a staged approach with a capped budget and a clear pilot before full commitment. As proposed, it is a suicide pact, not a strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
