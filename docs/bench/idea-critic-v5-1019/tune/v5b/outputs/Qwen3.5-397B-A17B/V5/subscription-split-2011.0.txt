First call: NO-GO. Raising prices 60% and immediately fracturing the user experience into two brands, two logins, and two bills treats a pricing shock as a branding opportunity, ignoring the stated risk of customer confusion right after a painful hike.

**Steelman:** Separating a declining physical asset from a growth digital one allows distinct strategies and prevents the legacy cost structure from dragging down the streaming valuation.

**Attack:** The proposal fails the "fit" and "pre-mortem" tests given the specific context of July–September 2011. The core claim is that separation enables strategy, but the *timing* contradicts the stated facts: customers are already reeling from a 60% price increase. Introducing separate billing, accounts, and queues immediately after a massive hike compounds the perceived value loss. The proposal admits "customer confusion" and "two logins" as risks; executing this during a price backlash turns a financial complaint into an existential churn event.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Keep the combined plan. Risk: Streaming strategy constrained by DVD economics.
2.  **Delay Separation:** Implement the price change, stabilize customer sentiment, then separate brands in 6–12 months. Cost: Delayed strategic clarity. Benefit: Avoids compounding negative sentiment.
3.  **Soft Split:** Separate management internally but keep a unified login/bill for users. Cost: Complex backend. Benefit: Strategic focus without user friction.

**Blockers:**
*   **Compound Friction:** The proposal explicitly adds high-friction elements (two bills, two logins, no shared data) immediately after a 60% price hike. The stated facts confirm customers are already reacting negatively to the price; adding operational friction now guarantees amplified churn.
*   **Value Destruction:** Eliminating cross-service recommendations removes a key retention lever exactly when customers are evaluating whether the service is worth the new higher price.

**Risks:** Brand dilution, mass churn, competitor exploitation of the confusion.

**Conclusion:** The strategic logic of separation may be sound in a vacuum, but the execution plan ignores the immediate emotional and financial state of the customer base described in the rationale. It attacks the wrong problem (branding) while exacerbating the real problem (price shock).

DECISION: NO-GO
