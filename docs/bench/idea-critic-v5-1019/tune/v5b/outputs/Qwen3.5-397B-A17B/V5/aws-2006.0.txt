First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer with no developer platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors already oppose its capital intensity.

Steelman: Leveraging spiky internal infrastructure to create a new revenue stream could theoretically improve asset utilization and diversify income beyond thin-margin retail.

However, the claim fails the blocker test. The proposal admits the market for hourly server rental is "unproven" and the company lacks any experience running a developer platform at scale. These are not merely risks; they are fundamental gaps in the core premise that buyers will pay and that the company can deliver. The "spiky" internal need does not guarantee external product-market fit.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids capital dilution and distraction from the core retail business during a period of investor skepticism.
2. Internal optimization only: Solve the spiky infrastructure problem strictly for retail, avoiding the complexity of a multi-tenant external platform.
3. Partnership: Lease excess capacity to an existing vendor rather than building a competing brand and platform from scratch.

The cost of delay is negligible because the external market is undefined; waiting allows competitors to validate (or invalidate) the model without exposing the retailer's balance sheet. The pre-mortem is clear: the company burns capital building a platform that developers reject due to lack of ecosystem, tooling, and trust, while the core retail business suffers from divided leadership attention and reduced reliability.

Blockers:
- No evidence of external demand (market explicitly "unproven").
- No organizational capability (no experience running developer platforms).
- Direct conflict with investor sentiment regarding capital spending.

Risks:
- Distraction from core retail operations.
- Margin dilution.
- Reliability failures damaging the retail brand.

This is a one-way door with high irreversibility. Committing to a distinct business line requires proof of demand that is currently absent. The proposal offers no capped pilot, no specific metric for validation, and no fallback if the external market does not materialize. It asks for a leap based on internal capacity rather than external pull.

DECISION: NO-GO
