First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $4 billion to a franchise with recent mixed reviews, no active creative lead, and significant licensing constraints, failing to demonstrate immediate synergy potential against the high cost.

**Steelman:** Acquiring this franchise secures a legendary IP capable of fueling parks and products for decades, provided the new trilogy revitalizes fan enthusiasm despite the founder's exit.

**Options:**
1.  **Acquire ($4B):** High cost, high risk of creative failure without the founder, uncertain fan reception.
2.  **License Content Only:** Lower cost, avoids integration risk, but yields limited control over parks/products and misses full upside.
3.  **Do Nothing:** Preserves capital; avoids betting on a potentially declining asset.

**Fit:** While the IP fits the "franchise feeding all businesses" strategy, the current state (mixed reviews, retired founder) contradicts the requirement for a reliable engine. The assumption that a new team can replicate past success without the creator is unproven.

**Pre-mortem:** The trilogy flops due to lack of the founder's vision; fans reject the new direction as inauthentic. The $4B price tag becomes a sunk cost with no box office recovery, and existing licensing deals prevent immediate merchandise monetization in parks. This is a one-way door; capital is gone, and the brand may be damaged.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
*   **Blocker 1 (Creative Void):** The proposal admits the founder is retiring and recent films were mixed. There is no verified plan or attached talent to replace the creative lead, making the core product (the trilogy) highly speculative.
*   **Blocker 2 (Valuation vs. Reality):** $4B is predicated on future trilogy success. With no guarantee of quality or fan acceptance, the ROI calculation relies entirely on optimistic assumptions, not current cash flow or guaranteed synergy.
*   **Blocker 3 (Synergy Friction):** Significant merchandise value is already licensed out, delaying the very synergy (consumer products/parks) used to justify the premium price.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The risk of creative failure is central, supported by the "mixed reviews" and "retiring founder" facts. The commitment ($4B) is massive and irreversible. The proposal offers no pilot, no earn-out structure, and no fallback if the trilogy fails.

**Decision:** The plan fails the blocker test. It bets the farm on a revitalization strategy with no attached creative engine and immediate monetization hurdles.

DECISION: NO-GO
