First call: GO. The proposal secures the franchise's survival by migrating the subscriber base to the inevitable distribution medium before competitors lock them out.

**Steelman:** Launch streaming immediately as a free value-add to retain seven million subscribers and gather usage data, treating current limitations (catalog, quality) as temporary constraints to be solved by scale, rather than reasons to delay entry into a shifting market.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Protects short-term DVD margins but guarantees obsolescence as broadband spreads and competitors (tech/retail giants) capture the digital relationship. High risk of total collapse.
2.  **Paid/Tiered Streaming:** Reduces cannibalization risk but slows adoption, failing to build the necessary user base to negotiate better licensing deals or refine the technology.
3.  **Proposed Plan (Free Add-on):** Maximizes adoption, creates a defensive moat around the existing subscriber base, and generates critical data. Best reversibility; can be throttled if costs spike, but cannot be easily started later if the market is lost.

**Fit:** Aligns with the north star of "home entertainment delivery," regardless of format. Delaying contradicts the strategic necessity of owning the customer relationship in the digital age.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if licensing costs bankrupt the firm before DVD profits fund the transition, or if quality is so poor it triggers mass churn. However, the cost of delay (losing subscribers to incoming giants) exceeds the cost of early inefficiency. This is a two-way door; features and pricing can adjust, but market presence cannot be instantly regained once lost.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal admits high costs and small catalog. These are known risks, not hidden blockers. The "cannibalization" fear is a feature, not a bug: it is better to cannibalize your own profitable business than have a competitor do it for you. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal's own terms that prevents execution.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks are execution challenges, not disqualifiers.
**Risks:** Cash burn from licensing, subscriber confusion, technical scaling. These become kill criteria: monitor monthly burn rate against DVD profit decline; set a threshold for subscriber churn attributable to streaming quality.

DECISION: GO
