First call: GO. The proposal trades short-term margin and control for immediate inventory breadth and a high-margin fee stream without capital expenditure, a logical pivot for an unprofitable retailer needing to become the "everything store."

**Steelman:** Transforming from a pure retailer to a hybrid marketplace leverages existing traffic to monetize long-tail demand and competitor inventory, creating a defensible network effect where selection drives traffic, which drives more sellers.

**Attack & Options:**
The primary fear is cannibalization: used items undercutting new sales. However, for an unprofitable entity, losing a low-margin new sale to a high-fee used transaction is superior to losing the customer entirely to a rival site. The alternative, "do nothing," preserves control but caps growth at the limit of current capital and warehousing capacity. A "curated partners only" approach reduces risk but fails to capture the long-tail selection required to dominate the category.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a "selection-first" north star. If we assume failure, it stems from a flood of counterfeit goods or terrible shipping experiences destroying brand trust. This is a two-way door; bad sellers can be removed, and the program capped if trust metrics dip. The cost of delay is high: competitors could lock in exclusive seller relationships or establish the default marketplace status first.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal admits risks (trust, supplier objection) but offers no data that these are fatal. Supplier objection is a negotiation tactic, not a hard blocker; they need the sales channel. Trust risks are manageable via feedback loops and guarantees. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal's logic: fees offset lower margins, and external inventory solves stock-outs without CAPEX.

**Decision:**
No blocker exists. The risks are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The potential to turn fixed costs into variable revenue and expand selection infinitely outweighs the threat of internal competition.

Blockers: NONE
Risks: Brand dilution, supplier retaliation (managed via policy and contracts).

DECISION: GO
