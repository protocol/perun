First call: NO-GO. The plan burns cash on every unit sold while betting on closed capital markets to fund the loss.

Steelman: Aggressively capture dominant market share via IPO proceeds to deter competition, accepting short-term negative margins to secure long-term monopoly pricing power.

Attack: The strategy relies on four simultaneous miracles: a receptive public market for a loss-making entity in the early 2000s (post-dot-com bubble context implied), successful execution of a costly TV blitz, immediate integration of a major competitor, and infinite capital availability to sustain below-cost pricing. The proposal explicitly admits negative gross margins and high acquisition costs. Selling below cost without a verified path to profitability before capital runs out is not a strategy; it is liquidation by marketing. The "risks raised" are not potential hurdles; they are current fatal flaws confirmed by the proposal's own math. If capital markets close (a stated risk), the company collapses immediately due to negative unit economics. There is no "do nothing" baseline comparison that favors this; doing nothing preserves remaining capital, whereas this plan guarantees its total loss.

Blockers:
1. Negative gross margins combined with high shipping costs mean every sale accelerates insolvency.
2. Reliance on IPO proceeds in a volatile market for a non-profitable company is a funding gap, not a plan.
3. No kill criteria or cap on the burn rate are defined; the plan mandates unlimited spending until failure.

Risks: Competitor response, integration failure, brand dilution from discounting. These are secondary to the immediate financial impossibility.

The proposal fails the basic operator test: it spends money it does not have to sell products at a loss, hoping a future event (market dominance) fixes a present mathematical impossibility. A cheaper alternative exists: pause expansion, fix unit economics on core SKUs, and seek private bridge funding based on a path to profitability, not a gamble on a national TV campaign.

DECISION: NO-GO
