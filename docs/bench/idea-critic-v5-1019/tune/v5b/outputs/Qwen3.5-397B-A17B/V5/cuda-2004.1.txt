First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits massive, certain unit-cost increases and engineering distraction against a non-existent market with no committed customers, directly threatening the core business funding the venture.

**Steelman:** Betting the company's marginal efficiency on creating a general-purpose computing market before demand exists, using ubiquity to force adoption.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain cost leadership in gaming; let academics hack existing APIs. Cost: Low. Risk: Missing a future shift. Reversibility: High.
2.  **Targeted High-End:** Add features only to expensive workstation cards. Cost: Moderate. Risk: Limited reach. Reversibility: High.
3.  **Proposal (Mass Market):** Subsidize every consumer unit. Cost: Massive certain loss per unit. Risk: Core business collapse if gaming stagnates. Reversibility: Low (silicon is permanent).

**Fit:** Contradicts the immediate survival need to win the core gaming race. The "north star" of scientific computing is assumed, not stated or funded by current revenue.

**Pre-mortem:** The program drains engineering bandwidth; the rival releases a superior gaming chip; market share collapses; the scientific market fails to materialize fast enough to offset losses; the company is acquired or bankrupted. This is a one-way door with a high cost of delay (none, as the market doesn't exist yet).

**Evidence:** The proposal admits zero large paying customers and niche demand. The cost is applied to *every* unit, guaranteeing margin compression without guaranteed upside.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Economic Suicide:** Raising unit costs on 100% of volume for a feature with 0% current monetization violates basic capital allocation logic for a public company reliant on quarterly gaming performance.
2.  **Strategic Distraction:** Diverting engineering from the primary rival race to build a compiler and ecosystem for a non-customer base creates an immediate competitive disadvantage in the core revenue driver.

**Risks:** Market never forms; academics stick to CPUs; toolchain fails; gamers revolt over price/perf ratio. These are secondary to the blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** If no enterprise customer signs a letter of intent within 12 months, or if gaming market share drops >5% due to performance/cost deficits, the program must be immediately terminated. However, the initial commitment is too large to justify even a pilot without a single anchor tenant.

DECISION: NO-GO
