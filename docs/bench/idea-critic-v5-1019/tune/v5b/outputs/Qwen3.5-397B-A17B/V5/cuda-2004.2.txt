First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits significant capital to build a market that does not exist, raising costs on every unit sold in a hyper-competitive core business without any committed revenue to offset the risk.

**Steelman:** Building a universal compute layer on consumer chips today creates an insurmountable moat tomorrow, turning every gamer into a potential scientific user and locking out rivals who focused only on graphics.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves cash flow for the existential graphics war. A "software-only" approach using existing hacked APIs validates demand before silicing features. The proposal beats neither; it bets the farm on a hypothesis.
2.  **Fit:** The company's survival depends on winning the graphics race. Diverting engineering to compilers and university funding distracts from the core metric: frames per second in games.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** In 2006, the program is canceled. Why? The added silicon area reduced margins on mass-market cards, causing price uncompetitiveness against the rival. Academic interest remained niche; no enterprise buyer emerged to justify the toolchain cost. The core graphics business lost share due to divided engineering focus.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits "no large paying market exists" and "no committed customers." The risks (cost on every unit, distraction) are stated facts, not possibilities.
5.  **Blocker Test:** The core claim requires a future market to materialize to justify present costs. The stated facts confirm zero current demand and high immediate cost. This is a negative cash-flow engine with no off-ramp. The "niche demand" confirmation removes the route to upside in the short term.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The commitment is too large (multi-year, all consumer chips) and too irreversible (silicon area, toolchain build) to stop once started. The risk of enterprise non-adoption concerns the core claim (that buyers will pay). The proposal's own facts support this risk as a near-certainty given the "no market" admission.

**Verdict:** This is a classic "boil the ocean" strategy lacking a beachhead. Without a specific, paying pilot customer or a way to isolate the cost to non-core SKUs, the plan fails the basic test of capital allocation.

DECISION: NO-GO
