First call: NO-GO. The plan removes the core value proposition (discounts) for the existing customer base without evidence they will accept everyday low pricing, while simultaneously undertaking a massive, untested physical rebuild.

**Steelman:** Eliminating coupon chaos will simplify operations and attract a newer, higher-margin demographic, while branded shops modernize the experience.

**Attack:**
1.  **Customer Alienation:** The proposal admits most sales come from discounted items and customers are "trained to wait." Removing discounts immediately destroys the primary purchase trigger for the entire current revenue base. There is no bridge strategy.
2.  **Execution Risk:** Rebuilding stores chain-wide while changing pricing strategy creates two simultaneous, massive points of failure. If the pricing model fails, the capital spent on rebuilding is wasted; if the rebuild stalls, the new pricing lacks the intended environment.
3.  **No Pilot:** Launching this in 2012 without testing in a few stores ignores the specific risk that the "electronics store" success model may not translate to "department store" demographics. The leader's past success is not proof this specific mix works here.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "do nothing" or "slow transition" approach preserves cash flow while testing the new pricing in 5-10 locations. The proposed "all-at-once" approach offers no unique upside that justifies the existential risk of alienating 100% of customers on day one.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Most sales historically come from discounts.
*   **Fact:** Customers are conditioned to wait for coupons.
*   **Contradiction:** The plan removes discounts immediately without a pilot to prove retention or acquisition can offset the guaranteed loss of discount-driven volume. The math of the transition is undefined.

**Risks:**
*   Immediate revenue collapse before new demographics arrive.
*   Capital exhaustion on store rebuilds if the pricing strategy fails.
*   Inability to revert quickly due to chain-wide implementation.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal lacks a measurable threshold for success or a stop-loss mechanism. It commits the entire chain based on an assumption that customer behavior will change instantly.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the blocker test. It contradicts the stated facts about current sales drivers and lacks the necessary validation (pilot) to proceed safely.

DECISION: NO-GO
