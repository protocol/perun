First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer with no developer platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors are already hostile to its capital spending.

Steelman: Leveraging spiky internal infrastructure to create a new revenue stream could theoretically improve asset utilization and diversify income beyond thin-margin retail.

However, the attack on this claim reveals fatal blockers based strictly on the provided facts:
1.  **Capability Gap:** The company has zero experience running a developer platform at scale. Selling to developers requires distinct operational competencies (API reliability, developer support, ecosystem management) that retail logistics do not provide.
2.  **Market Validation:** The market for hourly server rental is explicitly "unproven." Entering an unproven market as a novice against large incumbents is speculation, not strategy.
3.  **Capital Constraint:** Investors are already questioning heavy capital spending. This proposal requires significant further investment to productize internal tools for external consumption, directly contradicting investor sentiment and increasing financial risk.
4.  **Strategic Distraction:** The core business has thin margins. Diverting focus to build a competing business line with high reliability expectations creates a high probability of margin dilution and operational failure in both sectors.

The risks listed (distraction, margin dilution, reliability, competition) are not merely potential downsides; they are near-certainties given the lack of experience and the unproven nature of the market. There is no evidence provided that the internal infrastructure is even suitable for external multi-tenancy or that the company can support the required SLAs.

Alternatives like "do nothing" or optimizing internal costs without external sales avoid the catastrophic risk of failing at two businesses instead of one. A cheaper, staged alternative (e.g., a small pilot) is impossible to validate because the fundamental market demand is unproven and the core competency is missing.

Blockers: Zero relevant experience, unproven market demand, investor hostility to required capex, direct conflict with core business stability.
Risks: Total failure of new unit, degradation of retail operations, loss of investor confidence.

This plan fails the basic test of fitting the company's current capabilities and financial constraints. It attempts to solve a utilization problem by creating a business the company is unequipped to run.

DECISION: NO-GO
