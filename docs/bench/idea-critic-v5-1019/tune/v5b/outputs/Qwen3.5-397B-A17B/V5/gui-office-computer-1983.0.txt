First call: NO-GO. The price is prohibitive, performance is poor, and internal competition exists.

Steelman: This machine pioneers the graphical interface for offices, justifying a premium as a future-proof investment despite current slowness and software scarcity.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Wait for costs to drop and software to mature; risk losing first-mover advantage but avoid immediate failure.
2. Internal cheaper model: Prioritize the developing low-cost alternative; captures the same market upside with better margin potential.
3. Launch as proposed: High risk of rejection due to price/performance mismatch; likely cannibalized by the internal competitor.

Fit: Assumes the market values innovation over utility/cost in 1983. Unverified if office workers will pay 5x premiums for unproven workflows.

Pre-mortem: Sales flatline immediately; CIOs reject the slow speed and high cost; the internal cheaper model launches a year later, making this launch look incompetent and wasteful. One-way door: Brand damage and sunk inventory. Cost of delay: Low, as the market is nascent.

Evidence: Proposal admits price is "several times" competitors, speed is "slow," and software is "little." These are fatal blockers for mass office adoption. The existence of a cheaper internal alternative with similar features makes the current proposal strategically redundant.

Blockers:
1. Price/Cost mismatch: $10,000 is unjustifiable for a slow machine with no software ecosystem compared to established competitors.
2. Internal Cannibalization: A cheaper, similar internal product renders this specific configuration obsolete before launch.
3. Value Proposition: No evidence office workers will pay a massive premium for a slower, empty system.

Risks: Market education failure, developer apathy.

Kill Criteria: If the internal cheaper model can reach 80% of the functionality at 50% of the price within 12 months, this project must pivot or cease.

The proposal fails on basic unit economics and strategic positioning. Launching a slow, expensive product with no software when a better internal option is imminent is fiscal irresponsibility.

DECISION: NO-GO
