First call: GO. The current machine lacks basic business necessities (lowercase, 80-column display), making it non-viable for the target market expansion. Without these features, the company cannot capture business revenue.

Steelman: A dedicated business successor is required immediately to unlock the enterprise market, accepting the risk of phasing out the legacy unit to focus engineering and marketing resources on a viable product.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Remain a hobbyist vendor; eventual revenue stagnation as the market matures.
2. Upgrade existing machine: Likely impossible given the architectural constraints implied by the missing features; delays time-to-market.
3. Build new successor (Proposed): High execution risk, but the only path to business viability.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of becoming a serious computer manufacturer rather than a niche toy maker.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the new hardware is unreliable at launch or if the software ecosystem abandons the platform during the transition. The tight schedule increases the likelihood of bugs. However, delaying launch cedes the business window to competitors.

Evidence: The proposal states the current product *lacks* features essential for business. This is a factual blocker for the stated goal. No evidence suggests the market doesn't want these features; the lack of them is the problem.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (complexity, schedule, migration, reliability) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. They do not prove the plan fails as proposed; they define the difficulty of the task. The risk of hurting revenue by ending the current product is outweighed by the certainty of limited revenue by keeping it.

Risks: Hardware reliability, schedule slippage, ecosystem friction. These become kill criteria: if reliability testing fails two weeks before launch, delay; if software partners refuse to port by Q2, pivot marketing.

The absence of a pilot is not a blocker for a hardware generation shift; the "pilot" is the limited initial rollout. The plan addresses the core deficiency preventing business adoption.

DECISION: GO
