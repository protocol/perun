First call: NO-GO. The plan trades a functional product for an emotional asset based on incomplete data.

**Steelman:** Replacing the formula maximizes immediate taste preference scores, which are the primary driver of beverage consumption.
**Attack:** The evidence cited (blind taste tests, surveys, focus groups) explicitly excluded the critical variable: the permanent removal of the original. Consumers tested a *addition* or *substitution* in a vacuum, not a *loss*. Blind tests measure physical sensation (sweetness), not brand equity, nostalgia, or the psychological reactance triggered by taking away a cultural icon. The proposal conflates "preferred in a sip test" with "preferred as a sole option."

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Retain share erosion but keep the brand asset intact.
2.  **Proposed plan:** Risk total brand alienation for unproven volume gains.
3.  **Better way:** Launch the sweeter formula as a *new* product line alongside the original (a "New" variant), letting the market decide without forcing a binary choice. This captures the sweeter preference segment without triggering the loss aversion of removing the legacy product.

**Fit:** The goal is to stop share loss, but the method ignores the stated risk of "emotional attachment." A strategy that alienates the core base to chase a marginal taste preference mismatch fails the north star of sustainable brand health.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch triggers a public backlash. Loyalists feel betrayed because their favorite product was stolen, not just changed. Sales plummet not because the new taste is bad, but because the act of removal is offensive. The company is forced to reintroduce the old formula, admitting defeat and confusing the market.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fatal Data Gap:** The testing methodology fundamentally invalidates the conclusion. Participants were not informed the original would be withdrawn; therefore, the data does not support the claim that they prefer the *scenario* of the new formula replacing the old. The core premise (consumers want the switch) is unsupported by the provided evidence.

**Risks:** Emotional backlash, bottler revolt, permanent brand damage.

**Decision:** The absence of a fallback and the flawed testing protocol regarding the "removal" variable constitute a blocker. The plan assumes taste is the only variable, ignoring the stated risk of heritage.

DECISION: NO-GO
