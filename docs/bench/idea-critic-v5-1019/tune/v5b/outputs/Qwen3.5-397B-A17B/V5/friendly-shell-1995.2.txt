First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets home users but requires hardware they do not yet own, while a superior, integrated solution is imminent.

Steelman: A cartoon house shell makes computing intuitive for novices on high-end machines, creating an immediate bridge before the next OS arrives.

Options analysis:
1. Do nothing: Wait for the upcoming OS release which will likely address usability natively. Cost: $0. Risk: Low. Reversibility: N/A.
2. Sell standalone shell: Revenue ~$100/unit. Risk: High (hardware mismatch, obsolescence). Reversibility: Low (brand damage).
3. Integrate into next OS: Delays revenue but ensures ubiquity and no hardware friction.

Fit: The strategy conflicts with the "many new home users" premise because the requirement for a "high-end PC" excludes the mass market of 1995. Most home users possess mid-range hardware; demanding an upgrade for a $100 shell creates a prohibitive barrier.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, users realize their machines cannot run it smoothly, and they wait three months for the major OS release that includes similar features for free. Sales flatline, and the "patronizing" critique from power users dominates press coverage, tarnishing the brand's reputation for seriousness.

Evidence & Calculation: The proposal admits the major OS release is "coming within months." Developing, marketing, and distributing a standalone product in this window is operationally tight. If the new OS includes a graphical interface (a logical progression for a market leader), this shell becomes instant vaporware. The "few third-party add-ons" risk confirms a weak ecosystem, placing the entire burden of value on the cartoon metaphor, which is unproven.

Blockers:
1. Hardware mismatch: The target audience (new home users) lacks the required high-end PCs. The addressable market is too small to sustain development costs.
2. Imminent obsolescence: A major OS release with a new interface arriving in months renders a separate $100 shell redundant before it gains traction.

Risks:
1. User perception (patronizing): Mitigated by testing, but secondary to the hardware block.
2. Lack of add-ons: Confirms low stickiness.

The hardware requirement and the timing of the native OS release are fatal flaws. This should not be a standalone product; if the concept has merit, it must be folded into the upcoming OS release, not sold separately.

DECISION: NO-GO
