First call: NO-GO. The combination of a looming recession risk, a shrinking mid-priced segment, and the massive fixed cost of building a new dealer network creates a fatal mismatch between projected revenue and required capital outlay.

**Steelman:** Launching a distinct brand with dedicated dealerships captures the growing mid-market demand before competitors, leveraging extensive research to ensure product-market fit.

**Attack:**
1.  **Macro Timing:** The proposal admits a recession risk and potential segment shrinkage. Launching a capital-intensive venture requiring new dealer infrastructure exactly when consumer discretionary spending contracts is economically unsound. Dealers will struggle to capitalize during a downturn, leading to network instability.
2.  **Cannibalization:** The risk of overlap with existing low and premium lines is high. In a shrinking market, internal competition dilutes sales rather than capturing net new share.
3.  **Fixed Costs:** Building a dealer network is a high-fixed-cost strategy. If the segment shrinks as feared, these costs cannot be scaled down quickly, threatening the parent company's liquidity.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "do nothing" approach preserves capital. A better alternative is to badge-engineer a mid-priced model using *existing* dealerships, eliminating the infrastructure risk while testing the market.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch occurs in late 1957. A recession hits immediately. Consumers trade down to economy cars or hold onto old vehicles. The new dealers, burdened by high overhead and low volume, go bankrupt or demand massive subsidies. The brand becomes synonymous with failure, damaging the parent company's reputation and balance sheet.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Mismatch:** The plan requires expanding fixed costs (dealers) precisely when the target market is predicted to shrink and a recession looms. This violates basic counter-cyclical strategy.
*   **Unproven Necessity:** The "gap" can be tested via existing channels without the irreversible cost of a new dealer network.

**Risks:** Brand confusion, execution delay, marketing inefficiency.

**Kill Criteria:** If the mid-sized segment contracts by >5% in the next two quarters, or if a recession is declared before Q3 1957, the program must be halted.

**Decision:** The reliance on a growing market trend that is explicitly flagged as potentially reversing, combined with the irreversible commitment to new infrastructure, makes this proposal too fragile.

DECISION: NO-GO
