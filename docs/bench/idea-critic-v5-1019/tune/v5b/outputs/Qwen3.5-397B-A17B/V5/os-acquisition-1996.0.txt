First call: GO. The company faces existential decline with failed internal attempts; acquiring a proven, modern core is the only viable path to survival despite high cost and integration risks.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the external modern OS provides an immediate, technically superior foundation that internal teams failed to deliver, securing the platform's future despite integration hurdles.

**Options:**
1.  **Acquire:** High cost, high integration risk, but delivers a modern core immediately. Beats "do nothing" which guarantees obsolescence.
2.  **License:** Lower upfront cost, but cedes long-term control and differentiation; likely insufficient to reverse market share loss.
3.  **Do Nothing:** Certain failure as market share collapses and the dated OS becomes unusable for modern needs.

**Fit:** Directly addresses the critical need for a modern operating system to halt market share erosion. Assumes the strategic goal is survival and regaining relevance.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if developer migration proves too difficult or the founder's return creates internal toxicity, stalling adoption. This is a one-way door with high delay cost; waiting accelerates decline.

**Evidence:** Proposal states internal replacement failed twice. The acquired OS is modern and object-oriented. Revenue mismatch is noted but secondary to strategic necessity.

**Blockers:** NONE. The high price and low revenue of the target are strategic investments, not factual contradictions. The founder's history is a manageable risk, not a disqualifier. Licensing is an alternative but does not offer the same upside of ownership and control required for a turnaround.

**Risks:** Developer migration complexity, founder friction, integration cost. These become kill criteria: monitor developer adoption rates and internal team stability post-acquisition.

**Kill Criteria:** If developer migration tools fail to port 50% of key apps within 18 months, or if internal sabotage halts progress for 6 months, pivot to licensing or exit. Leading metric: Monthly active developers on the new core.

The acquisition offers the only realistic chance to replace the failed internal efforts with a modern core. Risks are significant but manageable compared to the certainty of failure without action.

DECISION: GO
