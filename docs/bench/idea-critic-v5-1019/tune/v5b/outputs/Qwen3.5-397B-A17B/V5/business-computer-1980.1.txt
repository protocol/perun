First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on unproven hardware reliability and a complex design under a tight deadline, creating a high probability of shipping a defective product that damages the brand.

Steelman: A business-ready successor with lowercase and 80-column display is essential to capture the enterprise market, requiring an immediate pivot from the current consumer-focused model.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** Build complex successor, phase out old, launch in <1 year. High risk of failure due to complexity and unproven reliability; alienates existing base.
2. **Do Nothing:** Keep selling the successful current unit. Safe revenue, but misses the business market entirely; eventual obsolescence.
3. **Parallel Launch (Better Way):** Release the new machine as a distinct high-end line without killing the current product yet. Validates reliability and market demand before cannibalizing the only revenue stream.

Fit: Assuming the goal is business expansion, the strategy fits, but the tactic of "phasing out" the only successful product before the new one is proven violates basic portfolio hygiene.

Pre-mortem: The new machine ships late with bugs due to complexity. Reliability issues cause returns. Meanwhile, the old machine is discontinued, leaving zero revenue. The company runs out of cash. This is a one-way door with catastrophic failure modes.

Evidence: The proposal admits hardware reliability is "unproven" and the design is "complex." No evidence suggests the schedule can absorb the inevitable delays of unproven hardware.

Blockers:
1. **Unproven Reliability + Tight Schedule:** Shipping complex, unproven hardware within a year is a factual contradiction for a business-grade product. Business users demand stability over features.
2. **Forced Phase-out:** Ending the only revenue source before the successor proves reliable removes the safety net.

Risks: Software maker adoption, owner migration. These are manageable if the product works, but irrelevant if the hardware fails.

Kill Criteria Check: The core claim is that business users will buy this. The unproven reliability directly negates the "business-grade" requirement. The commitment to phase out the current product is too large and irreversible given the unproven state of the successor.

Decision: The plan to phase out the current machine immediately creates an existential risk. The complexity and unproven reliability make the one-year launch window unrealistic for a business target.

DECISION: NO-GO
