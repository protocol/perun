First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a fixed public schedule for unproven core technologies without a demand signal or regulatory mandate, creating asymmetric downside.

**Steelman:** A bold, fixed-date commitment forces the organization to solve novel battery and transmission challenges rapidly, securing first-mover status before competitors react.

**Attack:**
1.  **Blocker (Technology vs. Schedule):** Nickel-metal-hydride durability is explicitly "unproven." Committing to mass production by 1997 assumes reliability breakthroughs will occur on a fixed timeline. If battery life fails testing in 1996, the public schedule forces either a disastrous recall/warranty crisis or a brand-damaging delay. The plan offers no technical fallback.
2.  **Blocker (Economics):** With cheap fuel and no regulatory mandate, the cost premium of a novel hybrid system cannot be justified to consumers. The proposal admits "no near-term demand signal." Producing a costly vehicle for a non-existent market violates basic capital allocation principles.
3.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "limited pilot" alternative preserves capital while allowing competitors to bear the cost of proving the technology and market. A staged approach (pilot fleet only) tests durability without exposing the entire brand to warranty failure.

**Pre-mortem:** It is 1998. The car launched late due to battery failures. The few units sold suffer catastrophic degradation. Fuel prices remain low, so customers reject the high sticker price. The company faces massive warranty liabilities and reputational damage for rushing an immature product.

**Verification:** The proposal's own facts contradict its viability: "unproven" durability + "cheap fuel" + "no demand" + "fixed schedule" = guaranteed failure. The risks are not hypothetical; they are stated premises that invalidate the business case.

**Kill Criteria Check:**
*   Core claim failure: Yes (buyers won't pay for unproven tech with cheap gas).
*   Evidence supports failure: Yes (proposal admits no demand/unproven battery).
*   Irreversible commitment: Yes (public schedule, mass production tooling).

This is a one-way door bet on uncertain technology with no market pull. The correct move is to delay mass production until battery durability is proven and a demand signal exists.
