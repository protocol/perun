First call: GO. The price point creates a new market segment by enabling a modal shift from scooters to enclosed vehicles for families, a massive unaddressed demographic.

**Steelman:** A dedicated high-volume factory enables a $2,500 price point, making car ownership accessible to millions of scooter-riding families who prioritize basic protection and capacity over prestige.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Safety/Quality:** Extreme cost-cutting risks fatal accidents, destroying the brand. *Counter:* Safety must be "adequate for speed limits," not luxury-grade. The alternative (scooters) has far higher fatality rates; a basic car is a safety upgrade.
2.  **Stigma:** "Cheapest" branding may repel aspirational buyers. *Counter:* For this demographic, price is the primary feature. Utility trumps status. Used cars are cheaper but unreliable; new offers warranty trust.
3.  **Land Protests:** Factory delays could burn cash. *Counter:* This is an execution risk, not a product flaw. Secure alternative sites or negotiate, but do not abandon the volume strategy which dictates the price.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" leaves families on scooters. "Higher spec" misses the price-sensitive mass market. "Used cars" lack reliability guarantees.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails if production costs creep above $2,500, forcing a price hike that collapses demand, or if a high-profile crash links the "cheap" label to "coffin."

**Evidence & Math:** At $2,500, the car undercuts the lowest new competitors by ~40%. The market size is the entire two-wheeler owning class. The math holds only with the dedicated high-volume factory; lower volume breaks the unit economics.

**Blockers:** None. The land protest is a hurdle, not a fundamental invalidation of the business case. The safety risk is manageable via engineering trade-offs appropriate for the vehicle's intended low-speed urban use.

**Risks:** Brand stigma limiting upsell potential; supply chain inability to hit cost targets at scale.

**Kill Criteria:** If the final ex-factory cost exceeds 90,000 rupees before launch, halt. If initial crash tests show structural failure at standard urban impact speeds (30km/h), redesign before mass production.

**Decision:** The opportunity to define a new category outweighs the execution risks. The "cheapest" label is a positioning strength for the target audience, not a bug.

DECISION: GO
