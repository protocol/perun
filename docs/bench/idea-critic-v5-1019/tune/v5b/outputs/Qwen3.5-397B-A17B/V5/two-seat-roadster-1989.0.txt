First call: GO. The market gap for an affordable, lightweight roadster is clear, and the "dead niche" perception by mass-market makers creates a first-mover advantage for a mid-size carmaker willing to take the risk.

**Steelman:** Launching a low-cost, front-engine, rear-drive convertible fills a vacated emotional niche, leveraging existing components to minimize R&D while capturing enthusiast loyalty that mass-market brands ignore.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Safe, but cedes the segment entirely and reinforces the brand as purely utilitarian.
*   **Alternative (Heavy GT):** Higher price, higher margin, but requires new heritage and misses the "affordable" gap.
*   **Fit:** Aligns with a "driver-focused" north star, assuming the company can pivot its brand image.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if safety compliance inflates the price beyond "affordable," or if the two-seat limitation restricts volume below breakeven.
*   **Evidence:** The proposal states the segment has shrunk, but this is based on *current* offerings lacking affordability. The "shrinking" is likely a result of rising costs, not lack of demand.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. No fact provided proves the unit economics fail if "lightweight" and "affordable" are strictly maintained.
*   **Risks:** Safety costs, limited buyer pool, fading enthusiasm. These are execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The lack of heritage is a marketing challenge, not a product blocker.

**Kill Criteria:**
If the prototype cannot meet safety regulations without exceeding the target price by >15%, or if pre-orders from enthusiasts do not cover 20% of initial tooling costs within 6 months of reveal, the project stops.

**Decision:**
The proposal identifies a genuine market void created by competitor retreat. The risks are manageable through strict cost control and niche targeting. The potential reward—owning a unique, emotional segment with low competition—outweighs the risk of moderate sales volume. The "dead niche" is an opportunity for a focused player.

DECISION: GO
