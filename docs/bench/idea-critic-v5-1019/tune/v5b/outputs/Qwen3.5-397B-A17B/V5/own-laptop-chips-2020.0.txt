First call: GO. The company already possesses the core competency in silicon design for its mobile devices, creating a strategic asymmetry against competitors relying on generic suppliers.

Steelman: Leveraging proven mobile silicon architecture for laptops will unify the ecosystem, enable superior performance-per-watt, and capture margin currently lost to suppliers, provided software compatibility is maintained.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Continue dependency on supplier roadmaps, limiting differentiation and margin.
2. Hybrid approach: High-end custom chips only; fails to unify the developer ecosystem or maximize scale.
3. Custom silicon (Proposal): Highest upfront engineering cost but offers long-term control, integration benefits, and supply chain independence.

Fit: Aligns with a strategy of vertical integration and ecosystem lock-in. The mobile design approach is a valid starting point, assuming scalability to laptop thermal envelopes.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if legacy software emulation creates unacceptable performance penalties or if the supply chain cannot meet volume demands during transition. This is a one-way door; once the supplier relationship is terminated, returning is costly. However, the cost of delay is losing the architectural advantage to competitors who might attempt similar moves.

Evidence: The proposal states the company already designs processors for phones/tablets. This validates the technical capability. The risks regarding software and unproven status are inherent to any architecture shift but are mitigated by the company's control over both hardware and operating system.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (software compatibility, engineering cost, supplier loss) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The company's existing success in mobile chips suggests the "unproven" risk is manageable. The end of a supplier relationship is a feature of this strategy, not a bug.

Risks: Software performance gaps, transition friction, buyer confusion. These become kill criteria: if beta testing shows >15% performance degradation on key professional apps, or if pre-orders drop >10% due to confusion, the rollout must pause.

The proposal represents a logical evolution of existing capabilities to secure long-term strategic advantage. The risks are standard for platform transitions and do not constitute a blocker given the stated facts.

DECISION: GO
