First call: NO-GO. The plan commits to a massive, irreversible capital expenditure (130–150 leases) before validating the core claim that the retailer's domestic value proposition translates to a market with different competitors, regulations, and supply chain realities.

Steelman: Rapid saturation prevents the entrenched competitor from reacting, securing market share before prices adjust.
Attack: This assumes the new supply chain and inventory system will work flawlessly at scale immediately. If the system fails or logistics bottleneck, 150 stores become 150 liabilities simultaneously, with no off-ramp.

Options:
1. **Proposal**: High cost, extreme risk, low reversibility (lease obligations).
2. **Do Nothing**: Zero cost, zero risk, forfeits first-mover potential.
3. **Pilot (Better Way)**: Open 5–10 stores to stress-test the cross-border supply chain and bilingual labeling. Lower cost, contained risk, high reversibility.
The proposal does not beat "Do Nothing" because the risk of total systemic failure outweighs the unproven upside. It loses to the Pilot option, which achieves the learning objective (validating logistics and price perception) without the catastrophic exposure of 150 simultaneous failures.

Fit: Expansion aligns with growth, but the *method* (big bang) contradicts basic operational prudence for a first international venture.

Pre-mortem: Two years in, the inventory system cannot handle bilingual SKU complexity, causing stockouts in 80% of stores while lease payments drain cash reserves. The entrenched competitor undercuts prices on key goods, exploiting the retailer's supply chain inflexibility. This is a one-way door; once leases are signed, exit is prohibitively expensive.

Evidence: The proposal admits the supply chain and inventory systems are "new" and "not yet proven." Relying on unproven infrastructure for 100% of the rollout is a factual blocker. The "higher prices on some goods" in the target market suggests the price perception risk is real, not theoretical, yet the plan offers no validation mechanism before full commitment.

Blockers:
1. **Unproven Systems at Scale**: Deploying new logistics and inventory tech across 150 sites simultaneously creates a single point of failure that could bankrupt the venture.
2. **No Validation Step**: The plan skips the necessary step of verifying cross-border viability before signing massive lease obligations.

Risks: Competitor retaliation, regulatory delays, cultural misalignment (all manageable if scaled correctly).

Kill Criteria Check: The risk of systemic failure concerns the core claim (operational viability). The proposal's own facts support that it *could* happen (admittedly unproven systems). The commitment (150 leases) is too large to stop once seen. This elevates the risk to a blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
