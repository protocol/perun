First call: GO. The offer solves the company's single biggest constraint (distribution) while leveraging its unique technical edge, despite unproven scale.

**Steelman:** Partnering with a major studio is the only viable path to fund and distribute a risky, unprecedented feature, trading some control for survival and market entry.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Remain a short-film boutique; risk losing talent to competitors; no path to feature revenue.
2.  **Self-fund:** Impossible given rendering costs and lack of distribution leverage; high probability of bankruptcy.
3.  **The Deal:** High execution risk, but secures funding, distribution, and validates the technology at scale.

**Fit:** Aligns with the vision of pioneering computer animation; moves from niche shorts to mainstream cultural impact.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if rendering bottlenecks stall production indefinitely or if creative conflicts with the studio dilute the unique voice. However, the "toy's point of view" concept is a strong, contained narrative hook that mitigates story risk. The door is effectively one-way regarding the specific partnership, but the technical capability gained is reversible/transferable. Cost of delay is losing the lead animator and the window of novelty.

**Evidence:** The offer itself proves market demand for the talent. No historical data exists for all-CG features (it's a first), making this an assumption-heavy bet. The risk of slow rendering is a known technical constraint, not a showstopper if managed via the studio's capital.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of prior feature experience is a risk, not a blocker, as the studio partner provides the necessary production infrastructure and oversight. The "stronger hand" of the partner is a negotiated reality, not a deal-breaker.

**Risks:** Technical scalability; creative control loss; financial overrun. These become kill criteria: if rendering pipelines cannot scale by the first quarter of production, or if the studio mandates creative changes that destroy the core "toy" perspective, the project must pivot or halt.

**Kill Criteria:** Stop if the rendering cost per minute exceeds the budget cap by 20% within the first six months. Leading metric: Successful completion of a 5-minute test sequence within budget and schedule by Q3 1992.

DECISION: GO
