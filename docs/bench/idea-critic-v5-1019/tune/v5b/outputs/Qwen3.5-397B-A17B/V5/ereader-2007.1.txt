First call: GO. The proposal solves the core friction of digital books (instant access without a PC) while directly addressing publisher piracy fears through a closed, cellular-only device, creating a defensible ecosystem despite hardware inexperience.

**Steelman:** A dedicated, cellular-enabled e-reader removes the need for a computer, making impulse buys instant while the closed system assuages publisher piracy concerns, unlocking content availability that generic devices cannot.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Remain print-only; cede emerging digital market to future competitors.
2.  **Software app only:** Requires users to have PCs/sync; fails to solve "instant gratification"; publishers remain skeptical of DRM on open OS.
3.  **Proposed Hardware:** High upfront cost/risk, but controls the entire user experience and content pipeline.

**Fit:** Aligns with the vision of "every book ever printed" by removing physical distribution limits. Hardware inexperience is a tactical hurdle, not a strategic mismatch.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the device is too expensive, the screen is poor, or publishers refuse content despite the closed system. However, the "free cellular" model removes a key adoption barrier (Wi-Fi complexity) for non-technical readers.

**Evidence:** The proposal correctly identifies that previous e-readers failed due to poor UX (requiring PCs) and lack of content (piracy fears). This plan attacks both simultaneously. The risk of cannibalization is acceptable; better to cannibalize print sales with high-margin digital own-sales than lose them to pirates or competitors.

**Blockers:** NONE. The lack of hardware experience is a risk, not a factual blocker; it can be mitigated via contracting or acquisition (which is implied as a necessary execution step). The margin squeeze is a strategic choice to drive volume, not a mathematical impossibility.

**Risks:** Hardware execution failure; publisher resistance despite protections; slow adoption due to price. These are managed by the "low price" strategy and the unique value proposition of instant, wireless delivery.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot shows <5% attach rate of book purchases to device sales within 6 months, or if major publishers refuse licensing terms, pivot to a software-only model or halt hardware production.

DECISION: GO
