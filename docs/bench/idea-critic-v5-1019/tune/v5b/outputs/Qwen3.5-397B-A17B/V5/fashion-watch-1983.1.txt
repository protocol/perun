First call: GO. The pivot from "quality timekeeper" to "fashion accessory" neutralizes the reputation risk by changing the evaluation criteria from durability to style, while the low cost creates a new market segment rather than cannibalizing existing high-end sales.

**Steelman:** Launching 12 distinct fashion models at a low price point creates an impulse-buy category that leverages existing manufacturing capacity to capture volume from non-luxury consumers, making the 1M unit target achievable through breadth of assortment rather than depth of single-model demand.

**Alternatives:**
1. *Do nothing:* Cede the emerging low-cost plastic segment to competitors; risk irrelevance as quartz technology commoditizes timekeeping.
2. *Single model launch:* Lower tooling risk but fails to address "fashion tastes change quickly"; limits shelf presence and consumer choice.
3. *Premium plastic:* Contradicts the "inexpensive" core advantage; confuses brand positioning.

**Fit:** Assumes the strategic goal is volume growth and market expansion via a new brand perception (fashion vs. horology).

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the company treats these as "cheap watches" instead of "accessories," leading to distribution in discount bins rather than fashion outlets, or if the 12 models are too similar, failing to trigger repeat purchases. The door is two-way; unsold inventory has low liquidation cost due to the low unit price. Cost of delay is high: first-mover advantage in defining "plastic as fashion" is critical before incumbents react.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal's own facts address the primary risks: "fashion items" reframes the reputation concern; "twelve models" mitigates taste volatility. The sales target is aggressive but not a factual impossibility given the global population and low price. No internal contradiction exists between the low cost and the fashion positioning; indeed, low cost enables the fashion turnover rate.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (reputation, taste, competition) are inherent to the strategy but are managed by the specific tactics proposed (fashion positioning, model variety, low price).
**Risks:** Execution error in design distinctiveness; channel conflict. These become kill criteria: if Month 1 sell-through < 15% per SKU, halt expansion.

**Kill Criteria:** If initial retailer orders for the 12-model collection do not exceed 200k units in Q1, pause production ramp. Leading metric: Sell-through rate per SKU by region.

DECISION: GO
