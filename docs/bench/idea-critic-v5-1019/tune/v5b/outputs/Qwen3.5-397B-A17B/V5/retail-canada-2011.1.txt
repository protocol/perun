First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a massive, irreversible capital expenditure (130–150 leases) before validating the core claim that the retailer's domestic value proposition translates to a foreign market with different competitors and regulations.

**Steelman:** Rapid saturation creates immediate scale to amortize the high fixed cost of a new cross-border supply chain, preempting the entrenched competitor before they can react.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Evidence:** The plan assumes domestic loyalty and pricing power transfer instantly across borders. No pilot data exists to support this. The "higher prices on some goods" in the target market may reflect regulatory costs or supply realities the retailer hasn't modeled. Entering with 130 stores is a one-way door; failure means dumping 130 long-term leases in an unfamiliar legal system.
2.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "slow walk" alternative is superior. Open 5–10 stores to test the new inventory system, bilingual labeling, and logistics. If the model works, scale. If it fails, the loss is contained. Buying 130 leases upfront removes the option to learn cheaply.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The new inventory system fails under cross-border complexity. Because 130 stores open simultaneously, the entire network faces stockouts or overstock immediately. Cash flow collapses under lease obligations before the system can be patched. The entrenched competitor lowers prices temporarily to starve the new entrant.
4.  **Blocker:** The proposal explicitly admits the supply chain and inventory systems are "not yet proven." Deploying unproven critical infrastructure at maximum scale (130 sites) simultaneously violates basic operational hygiene. This is not a risk; it is a guaranteed failure mode. The cost of delay (waiting to prove the system) is negligible compared to the cost of a systemic collapse across 150 locations.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal lacks a pilot, a cap on initial exposure, or a fallback if the inventory system glitches. It demands an all-in bet on unverified assumptions.

**Decision:** The absence of a proven supply chain and the refusal to stage the rollout constitute a factual blocker. The plan fails as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
