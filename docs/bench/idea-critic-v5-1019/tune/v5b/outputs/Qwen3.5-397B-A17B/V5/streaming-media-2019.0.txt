First call: GO. The strategic imperative to own the customer relationship and capture long-term lifetime value outweighs the immediate loss of licensing revenue, provided the company can sustain the cash burn.

Steelman: By withdrawing content from rivals and pricing aggressively below the market leader, the conglomerate can leverage its unmatched library to rapidly acquire tens of millions of subscribers, accepting multi-year operating losses as an investment to build a dominant direct-to-consumer platform that secures future profitability and strategic independence.

Options analysis shows "do nothing" preserves short-term licensing cash but cedes long-term control to competitors who are already vertically integrating. Building a proprietary platform is expensive and risky compared to continuing licensing, but it is the only path to capturing the full upside of the IP. The cheaper alternative of hybrid licensing delays the inevitable conflict and dilutes the brand's exclusive value.

Fit is strong for a major content owner in a shifting distribution landscape; the north star is direct subscriber ownership. The pre-mortem reveals failure modes: technology platform instability, inability to retain users after price hikes, and faster-than-expected erosion of cable/theatrical revenue. This is a one-way door; once content is pulled, rebuilding those licensing bridges will be costly or impossible. The cost of delay is high, as competitors are already securing exclusive content and subscriber loyalty.

Evidence relies on the stated fact of a vast library and the market reality of a dominant incumbent. The financial model assumes the company can fund losses long enough to reach scale.

Blockers: NONE. The proposal acknowledges the risks (cash burn, tech execution, cannibalization) but does not present a factual contradiction that makes the plan impossible as stated. The risks are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws in the thesis.

Risks: High cash burn requiring deep pockets; technical failure in streaming infrastructure; slower subscriber growth than projected; accelerated decline in legacy revenue streams. These are managed via kill criteria: if subscriber growth falls below 50% of target by Year 3, or if cash reserves drop below a 24-month runway without a clear path to profitability, the strategy must pivot to hybrid licensing or cost containment.

DECISION: GO
