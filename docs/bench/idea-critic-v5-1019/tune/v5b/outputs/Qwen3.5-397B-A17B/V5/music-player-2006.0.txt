First call: NO-GO. The proposal attacks an entrenched monopoly with a late, incompatible product that alienates existing partners without a clear value advantage.

**Steelman:** A well-funded entrant can displace a dominant player by offering superior hardware (screen, wireless) and a seamless ecosystem, betting that user experience trumps ecosystem lock-in.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve partner relationships and avoid high burn; accept status quo.
2.  **Licensing:** License software to existing hardware makers to build share without device risk.
3.  **Proposal:** Build proprietary hardware/software, drop industry copy-protection (DRM), and spend heavily on marketing.
The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" because it actively destroys current revenue channels (partner stores/devices) to chase a low-probability win against a loyal incumbent.

**Fit:** Assuming the goal is market leadership, this strategy contradicts the reality of the 2006 market where labels control content and the incumbent defines the standard. Dropping DRM, while potentially user-friendly, guarantees label rejection, leaving the store empty.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches with high hype but few songs due to label boycotts over dropped DRM. Wireless sharing is blocked by rights holders. The large screen increases cost without solving the content gap. The marketing budget burns cash acquiring users who cannot find key albums, leading to rapid churn.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the leader has an "enormous share and loyal ecosystem" and that "labels may restrict sharing." It offers no evidence that labels will accept a non-DRM platform from a newcomer. The "no clear price advantage" confirms there is no lever to pry users loose.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Content Availability:** Dropping the industry copy-protection standard without pre-signed label deals means the store launches with a critical content gap. An empty store is a fatal flaw.
2.  **Partner Alienation:** Abandoning the standard promoted to partners destroys existing distribution and licensing revenue before the new venture proves viable.
3.  **Late Entry:** Entering late against a loyal ecosystem requires a 10x advantage or price cut; the proposal offers neither, only "larger screen" and "wireless," which are insufficient to overcome network effects.

**Risks:** High burn rate, brand damage from failure, inability to secure content rights.

DECISION: NO-GO
