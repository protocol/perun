First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines a premium price, a tiny compatible installed base, and an unproven legal music supply chain, creating a near-certain commercial failure under stated facts.

**Steelman:** A high-margin, integrated device could define a new category if the ecosystem lock-in forces superior usability that fragmented competitors cannot match.

**Attack:**
1.  **Addressable Market:** With small computer market share and no consumer electronics footprint, the initial user base is too small to sustain the $400 price point or justify custom 5GB drive sourcing costs. Cheaper players failed due to poor UX and lack of content; this proposal solves neither (no legal downloads yet) while raising the price barrier.
2.  **Supply Chain:** Sourcing a custom 5GB drive for a low-volume product in under a year is operationally implausible without existing vendor relationships, which the company lacks.
3.  **Ecosystem Trap:** Restricting usage to own-computer owners initially caps adoption at the very moment critical mass is needed to pressure the music industry for licensing.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do nothing:* Avoids capital loss in a failing hardware segment.
*   *Software first:* Build the music management software for Windows/Mac to build user base before hardware.
*   *Partner:* License the software to existing player makers.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches to poor sales due to high price and Windows incompatibility. The music industry refuses to license without proven scale. Inventory becomes obsolete as flash memory prices drop. The project drains cash from the core computer business.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Viability:** The stated facts (small share, high price, no content) mathematically prevent the volume needed to amortize custom component costs.
*   **Strategic Fit:** Entering a low-margin, high-volume consumer market contradicts the company's high-margin, low-volume DNA without a clear bridge (like legal music sales, which don't exist yet).

**Risks:** Execution delay, component shortage, brand dilution. These are secondary to the fundamental blocker of market size vs. cost structure.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot cannot secure a committed legal music partner before tooling starts, the project must stop. If initial pre-orders from the existing computer base do not cover 50% of the first production run, cancel.

The proposal fails the "beat do nothing" test. The risks are existential, and the upsides rely on external factors (music licensing) not in the company's control.

DECISION: NO-GO
