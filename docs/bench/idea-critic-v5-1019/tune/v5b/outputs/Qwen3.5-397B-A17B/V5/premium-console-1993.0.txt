First call: NO-GO. The proposed $700 price point is commercially fatal against competitors selling at a loss, and the licensing model creates an unsolvable margin conflict for manufacturers.

**Steelman:** Licensing accelerates market penetration by leveraging existing manufacturing capacity, avoiding capital expenditure while capturing software royalties.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $700, the console costs double or triple existing competitors who subsidize hardware to drive software sales. Early adopters may pay a premium, but not a 200% premium for unproven graphics without an exclusive game library.
2.  **Incentive Misalignment:** The core flaw is the licensing model. Competitors sell hardware at a loss to maximize installed base. This proposal requires licensees to sell hardware *at a profit* plus pay royalties. No manufacturer can match the $700 price point while maintaining margins if competitors are selling at $300–$400. Licensees will refuse to build a product they cannot sell.
3.  **Market Reality:** Established players control the ecosystem. Without first-party exclusives or a subsidized price, third-party developers will not prioritize this platform, killing the software royalty stream.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids certain failure.
*   **Subsidized First-Party Hardware:** Build/sell own hardware at a loss to gain market share (industry standard). High risk, high capex.
*   **Licensing with Subsidy:** License design but subsidize the unit cost to match market pricing. Requires massive upfront capital the start-up likely lacks.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because licensees cannot price competitively. Inventory sits unsold at $700 while competitors dominate at $350. The start-up earns zero royalties because no games are sold on an orphaned platform.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Impossibility:** The requirement for licensee hardware profit + royalty vs. competitor loss-leader pricing makes the $700 unit uncompetitive. The math does not work.
*   **Adoption Barrier:** The price excludes the mass market required to attract game developers.

**Risks:** Strong incumbents, unproven tech.

The proposal fails the basic unit economics test. You cannot license a premium-priced hardware design into a subsidy-driven market without absorbing the loss yourself.

DECISION: NO-GO
