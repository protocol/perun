First call: GO. The stated facts show a dominant search business with no hardware presence facing a future where mobile access could gatekeep its core revenue. The proposal uses a small, fixed cost ($50M) to secure an open-source foundation that aligns with the "free software pushes mobile web" thesis, directly protecting the primary business model against carrier and handset-maker lock-in.

Steelman: Acquiring this specific team provides the only viable path to influence the mobile OS layer without building from scratch or paying exorbitant licensing fees to incumbents, ensuring the search engine remains accessible as the market shifts.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Risk being entirely dependent on incumbent OS vendors who may favor their own search tools or charge prohibitive fees; high strategic risk.
2. Build internally: Too slow; the market is consolidating around existing players; misses the window of opportunity.
3. License existing OS: Loses control over the user experience and search integration; contradicts the "open" strategy needed to disrupt carrier control.

Fit: This moves the north star of "organizing the world's information" by ensuring access points (mobile devices) remain open to indexing. It fits the stage of a cash-rich company needing to buy optionality in a nascent but critical channel.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if handset makers refuse to adopt the OS despite it being free, or if carriers block installation. However, the open-source nature lowers the barrier for adoption compared to proprietary alternatives, and the "free" aspect creates a compelling economic incentive for manufacturers to differentiate themselves.

Evidence: The proposal admits no current revenue model, which is consistent with an infrastructure bet. The $50M price tag is negligible relative to the potential loss of search market share if mobile becomes a walled garden. The six-person team represents a low-integration risk.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (adoption, carrier resistance) are inherent to market entry, not flaws in the acquisition logic itself. The lack of a direct revenue model is a feature, not a bug; the revenue comes from the downstream search ads.

Risks: Long payback period, potential for the OS to remain niche. These are managed by the low acquisition cost and the strategic necessity of having a foothold.

DECISION: GO
