First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a paid subscription and broadband requirement in a 2002 market where competitors offer free online play and broadband penetration is critically low, creating an immediate barrier to entry that contradicts stated user expectations.

**Steelman:** A unified, premium online service creates a sticky ecosystem and recurring revenue, differentiating the console through superior identity and voice features that free, fragmented alternatives cannot match.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Fit:** In 2002, "few homes have broadband" is not a risk; it is a hard constraint. Mandating broadband excludes the vast majority of the installed base, while competitors capture them with free, dial-up compatible (or local) solutions.
2.  **Pricing Power:** Charging for a service users expect to be free, without an existing installed base to lock in, invites immediate rejection. The "annual subscription on top of console price" compounds the cost barrier against rivals who subsidize online play via software sales.
3.  **Operational Capability:** The company has "never run" hosting or support operations. Building this from scratch while launching hardware is a dual-execution failure mode. If the service stalls at launch due to operational infancy, the "premium" promise becomes a liability.
4.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "publisher-led" approach aligns with current market norms and shifts infrastructure risk to game studios. A "freemium" or hardware-subsidized model would better address the adoption barrier than a mandatory paywall.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Broadband adoption is too low to support a "console-wide" mandatory service; the addressable market is insufficient to sustain the required operations.
*   **Fact:** Competitors' free models establish a price anchor of $0 that this proposal cannot overcome with unproven features.
*   **Fact:** Lack of internal operational history creates a high probability of service failure at launch, damaging the brand.

**Risks:** (These are secondary to the blockers) Player backlash, slow subscriber growth, high churn.

The proposal fails the "beat do nothing" test because the mandatory constraints (broadband + payment) shrink the market below viability before the service proves its value. The operational inexperience is a fatal flaw for a launch-critical dependency.

DECISION: NO-GO
