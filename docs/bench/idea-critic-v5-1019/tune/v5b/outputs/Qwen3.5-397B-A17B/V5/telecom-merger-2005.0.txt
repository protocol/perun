First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $36 billion to integrate fundamentally incompatible networks while inheriting legacy equipment that limits data growth, creating a massive integration burden without a clear path to synergy.

**Steelman:** The acquisition secures a sticky, high-value business niche (push-to-talk) that the buyer cannot organically replicate, justifying the premium if the niche can be migrated or isolated profitably.

**Attack:**
1.  **Technology Mismatch:** The core assets are incompatible. Running parallel networks indefinitely destroys margins; migrating the target's customers to the buyer's legacy-incompatible tech risks churning the very high-value base being purchased.
2.  **Data Limitation:** The target's network is "limited for data." In 2005, data growth is the primary value driver for wireless. Buying a technology dead-end for $36 billion locks capital away from future-proof infrastructure.
3.  **Integration Cost vs. Goodwill:** The "large goodwill" implies paying for future value that must be extracted via integration. If integration fails due to incompatibility, that goodwill becomes an immediate write-down. The cost to build a bridge between incompatible tech stacks or replace the target's handsets/network likely exceeds the standalone value of the push-to-talk service.
4.  **Alternative:** A commercial partnership or MVNO agreement to resell the target's push-to-talk service would capture the business customer revenue without the $36 billion balance sheet risk, technical debt, and integration nightmare.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-merge, the networks remain separate due to migration complexity, draining OpEx. Business customers flee during forced handset transitions. The $36 billion price tag results in a massive impairment charge as the "synergies" prove technically impossible to realize at scale.

**Blocker:** The proposal admits the technologies are "incompatible" and the target is "limited for data," yet offers no specific, funded technical plan to migrate the high-value customers to a data-capable platform without churn. The $36 billion price assumes successful integration of incompatible systems, which contradicts the stated technical facts. The risk of losing the core asset (customers) during migration is a structural flaw, not just a risk.

**Kill Criteria:** Without a verified, low-churn migration path for the push-to-talk base to a data-capable network within 18 months, the deal destroys value.

DECISION: NO-GO
