First call: NO-GO. The plan trades high-trust utility (email) for low-trust engagement (social) by violating user consent, inviting regulatory backlash and brand damage that outweighs unproven engagement gains.

**Steelman:** Leveraging the massive existing user base via default opt-in is the only viable path to jumpstart network effects for a social layer within an inbox, overcoming the cold-start problem faster than any voluntary rollout.

**Attack:**
1.  **Trust & Privacy:** Email is a private channel; imposing social visibility by default breaches the implicit contract of confidentiality. This is not a feature tweak but a fundamental shift in service nature.
2.  **Regulatory & Legal:** Automatic enrollment with exposed connections in 2010 invites immediate scrutiny from privacy advocates and regulators, risking fines and mandatory shutdowns.
3.  **Usage Mismatch:** Users check email for tasks, not social browsing. Forcing a social graph onto a utility tool often results in low engagement while poisoning the core product experience.
4.  **Irreversibility:** Once connections are exposed and users feel violated, trust cannot be restored by simply reversing the setting. The reputational damage is permanent.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve high trust and core utility; avoid regulatory risk.
*   **Opt-In Only:** Launch with explicit user consent. Slower growth, but sustainable and safe.
*   **Separate Product:** Build a standalone social network, linking it optionally to email contacts, keeping the domains distinct.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch triggers a user exodus to competitors citing privacy violations. Regulators intervene, forcing a costly rollback. The social feature sees negligible active usage because the context (inbox) is wrong, while the core email service suffers from reduced user confidence.

**Blockers:**
*   **Consent Violation:** The proposal explicitly states "enrolling... automatically" with "default privacy settings that make some connections visible." This contradicts the core expectation of email privacy and creates an immediate, verifiable liability.
*   **Irreversible Harm:** The risk of permanent brand damage from a forced social rollout outweighs the speculative benefit of network speed.

**Risks:** Low adoption, regulatory fines, user churn. These are severe but secondary to the fundamental blocker of non-consensual data exposure.

**Kill Criteria:** If user trust drops by >5% in post-launch surveys or if any regulatory body issues a formal inquiry within 30 days, the project must be immediately terminated and all changes reverted.

DECISION: NO-GO
