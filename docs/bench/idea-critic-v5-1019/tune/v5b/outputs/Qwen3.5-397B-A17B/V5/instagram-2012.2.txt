First call: NO-GO. The price implies a valuation disconnected from any verified revenue or proven business model, violating basic capital discipline.

**Steelman:** The acquisition is a defensive necessity to neutralize the primary mobile threat before the IPO, buying immediate scale and talent that cannot be built internally fast enough.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financials:** Paying $1B for zero revenue and no business model sets a dangerous precedent. The "growth" is on a single platform with a nascent second; user engagement does not equal enterprise value without a path to monetization.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows the threat to mature, but building a competitor or partnering costs a fraction of $1B. The buyer's weak mobile product suggests execution issues that throwing cash at may not fix.
3.  **Cultural Risk:** Integrating a dozen-person culture into a public giant often destroys the very innovation being purchased. If the team leaves, the buyer paid $1B for a dead app.
4.  **Regulatory:** Pre-IPO timing invites intense scrutiny; a blocked deal wastes resources and signals desperation.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, the app's growth stalls post-integration, the team departs within 18 months, and regulators force a divestiture or impose heavy restrictions. The $1B is written off, and the stock price drops on the perception of wasted capital.

**Blockers:**
*   **Valuation Mismatch:** No revenue or business model exists to justify a $1B price tag based on current facts.
*   **Execution Gap:** The buyer's own weak mobile product indicates an inability to leverage the acquisition effectively without further unproven investment.

**Risks:** Cultural destruction, regulatory block, user churn post-acquisition.

**Kill Criteria:** If the target cannot present a credible monetization strategy within 90 days, or if key engineering staff turnover exceeds 20% in the first year, the initiative must be pivoted or shut down.

The absence of a revenue model and the extreme premium relative to any tangible asset constitute a factual failure of the proposal's economic logic. While the strategic threat is real, the cost to eliminate it is unjustifiable without a clearer path to ROI.

DECISION: NO-GO
