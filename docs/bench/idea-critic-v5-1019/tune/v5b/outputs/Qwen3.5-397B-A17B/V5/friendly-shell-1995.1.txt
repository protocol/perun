First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a mass home market but mandates hardware ("high-end PC") that the majority of that market does not own, immediately capping adoption below viability.

Steelman: A cartoon house shell makes computing intuitive for novices, justifying a $100 premium despite hardware constraints.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed:** High barrier to entry, immediate obsolescence risk from upcoming OS, limited ecosystem.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Wait for the major OS release to handle UI improvements natively; avoid fragmenting the user base.
3.  **Alternative:** Release a lightweight "guide" overlay compatible with existing low-end PCs rather than a full shell replacement.

**Fit:** Contradicts the goal of serving "many new home users" by excluding those without high-end machines.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, users realize their current PCs cannot run it, and they wait for the upcoming major OS release instead. Sales flatline; the brand looks out of touch. This is a one-way door with high development cost and low reversibility once marketing spends begin.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal admits the target demographic finds computers "hard to use," implying they are unlikely to have recently purchased "high-end" hardware. The risk that "it needs more memory than most home PCs have" is not a speculative risk; it is a factual blocker based on the 1995 installed base. Furthermore, the imminent major OS release renders a standalone $100 shell redundant before it gains traction. The lack of third-party add-ons confirms the ecosystem cannot support a parallel interface.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Hardware Mismatch:** The requirement for a high-end PC excludes the primary target audience (new home users with standard equipment).
2.  **Imminent Obsolescence:** A major OS update with similar goals is months away, destroying the product's unique value proposition and longevity.
3.  **Ecosystem Void:** No third-party support limits utility, making the $100 price point unjustifiable.

**Risks:**
Patronizing experienced users (mitigated by optional installation), but this is secondary to the hardware blocker.

**Decision:**
The plan fails on its own stated facts regarding the user base's hardware capabilities and the competitive timeline.

DECISION: NO-GO
