First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades certain, high-margin licensing revenue for speculative subscription growth while intentionally burning cash to undercut a dominant incumbent with over 100 million subscribers.

**Steelman:** Withdrawing content forces migration to a proprietary platform, capturing long-term customer lifetime value and data, despite short-term revenue loss.

**Attack:**
1.  **Revenue Math:** Licensing yields immediate billions with near-zero marginal cost. Substituting this with a "low price" subscription requires acquiring tens of millions of users just to match current licensing income, ignoring the massive cost of content production and platform operations. The proposal explicitly plans for multi-year losses, creating a cash drain without a guaranteed path to profitability.
2.  **Competitive Reality:** Entering a market where the leader has >100M subscribers requires immense differentiation or deep pockets. Competing on price alone against an entrenched network effect is a race to the bottom. The target (tens of millions) is a fraction of the incumbent's base, suggesting the company will remain a distant second while bleeding cash.
3.  **Execution Risk:** The company has never run a global streaming technology platform. Building this while simultaneously marketing a new service and managing content withdrawal introduces catastrophic operational risk.
4.  **Cannibalization:** The proposal admits to cannibalizing existing cable and theatrical revenue. Without a clear mechanism to replace this high-margin income quickly, the net present value of this strategy is likely negative compared to the status quo.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Contradiction:** The plan relies on replacing high-margin, guaranteed licensing fees with low-margin, speculative subscription fees while incurring massive new operating costs. The math does not support sustainability without unlimited capital, which is not guaranteed.
*   **Capability Gap:** No evidence suggests the organization can build a competitive tech platform faster than rivals can adapt.

**Risks:** Market saturation, inability to churn-proof the service, and permanent erosion of brand value through price wars.

**Kill Criteria:** If licensing revenue drops by >20% in Year 1 without a corresponding 15% growth in paid subscribers, the strategy fails.

The proposal sacrifices a profitable, dominant market position for a costly, uncertain gamble against a superior incumbent.

DECISION: NO-GO
