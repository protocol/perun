First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and friction profile suggest a classic "solution in search of a problem" with a fatal mismatch between price and value.

**Steelman:** A premium, hassle-free nutrition appliance for affluent early adopters who value convenience over cost, using IoT to ensure safety and automate replenishment.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** At $699, the device costs ~7x a high-end conventional juicer. The proprietary bag model likely carries a 300%+ markup over raw produce. For the subscription to make sense, the convenience premium must outweigh a massive recurring cost differential. In 2016, consumers are already resistant to basic subscription boxes; locking them into a single vendor for perishable goods at a premium is a severe adoption barrier.
2.  **Friction & Connectivity:** Requiring Wi-Fi to verify "freshness" of a physical bag introduces a single point of failure for a basic kitchen task. If the server drops or the user's network changes, the device becomes a brick. This is a negative feature, not a safety guardrail, given that expiration dates can be printed on bags.
3.  **Market Size:** The intersection of users willing to pay $699 for hardware, commit to a costly subscription, and accept proprietary lock-in for a commodity product (juice) is vanishingly small. The "well-funded" status creates a sunk-cost fallacy risk, pushing marketing spend to solve a unit-economic problem.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Sell high-end juicers without subscription lock-in.
*   *Cheaper Path:* Sell the press mechanism as an accessory for existing blenders/juicers; sell bags separately in retail stores without device tethering.
*   *Staged:* Launch the bag subscription first with a manual press to validate demand before engineering $699 of connected hardware.

**Pre-mortem:** The company burns through cash on customer acquisition, churn spikes when users realize the per-juice cost is $8 vs $1.50 DIY, and the "smart" features become support nightmares. Inventory of proprietary bags becomes waste.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unit Economics:** The proposed pricing structure (high CapEx + high OpEx) for a commodity outcome creates a negative value proposition that marketing cannot sustain long-term.
*   **Unnecessary Complexity:** Internet connectivity for freshness checking adds cost and failure modes without solving a real user pain point that a printed date cannot address.

DECISION: NO-GO
