First call: GO. The proposal targets a distinct, emerging demographic (young postwar buyers) with a specific unmet desire (sporty styling at low cost) using a proven, low-risk mechanical foundation.

**Steelman:** Leverage existing compact car reliability and tooling to create a high-margin, style-focused vehicle for a new generation, minimizing development risk while maximizing customization revenue.

**Alternatives:**
1.  *Do nothing:* Cede the emerging youth market to competitors; retain only the "dull" reputation.
2.  *New platform:* Prohibitively expensive and slow; misses the immediate demographic window.
3.  *Upgrade current compact:* Dilutes the economy brand; fails to deliver the "sporty" psychological break buyers seek.

**Fit:** Directly addresses the stated shift in buyer preferences. Reuses 80%+ of existing parts (verified by proposal), ensuring low unit cost.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the marketing fails to separate the car's image from its economy donor, or if the "unproven" demand is actually non-existent. However, the low base price limits downside exposure, and the long options list captures margin from early adopters willing to pay for style.

**Evidence & Math:** The mechanical basis is already paid for (sunk cost). The primary variable cost is the ad campaign. Given the low marginal cost of the shared chassis, the break-even volume is significantly lower than a clean-sheet design. The risk of "looking cheap" is mitigated by the specific styling cues (long hood, short deck) which psychologically distance the product from its donor.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (unproven demand, image dilution, spend) are standard market entry risks, not factual contradictions. The proposal explicitly addresses the cost risk via parts sharing and the revenue risk via options. No internal fact contradicts the existence of the youth boom or their desire for style.

**Risks:** Demand volume uncertainty; brand perception. These are managed by the low base price (limiting inventory loss) and the targeted ad spend.

**Kill Criteria:** If initial pre-orders do not cover the launch campaign cost within 90 days, halt further production expansion. Monitor the ratio of option uptake vs. base model sales as the leading metric for profitability.

The plan beats "do nothing" by capturing a new market segment with minimal incremental engineering risk. The potential upside (defining a new class) vastly outweighs the contained downside (marketing spend on a sellable, reliable car).

DECISION: GO
