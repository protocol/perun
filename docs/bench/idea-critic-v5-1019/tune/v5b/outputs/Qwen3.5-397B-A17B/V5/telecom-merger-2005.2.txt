First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $36 billion to acquire a network explicitly described as incompatible and limited, requiring the buyer to run legacy equipment indefinitely.

Steelman: The acquisition secures a sticky, high-value business segment via a unique push-to-talk feature that the buyer cannot currently replicate, justifying the premium and integration headache as a defensive moat.

Attack: The core claim fails on the stated facts. The target's technology is "incompatible" and "limited for data." In 2005, the industry trajectory is decisively moving toward data convergence (3G/4G), not siloed voice services. Buying a "limited" network forces the acquirer to maintain a costly, divergent infrastructure forever or incur massive, undefined migration costs to move those specific users to the main network. The $36 billion price likely prices in the revenue of the legacy service without adequately discounting the technical debt of maintaining two incompatible networks. The "large goodwill" risk confirms the price exceeds tangible assets, betting entirely on a technology the proposal admits is a dead end ("limited," "incompatible").

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoid $36B outlay and integration chaos; build or license a push-to-talk app on the existing compatible network.
2. Asset buy: Acquire only the customer contracts and brand, migrating users immediately to the buyer's modern network, avoiding the legacy tech debt.
3. The proposed merger: Highest cost, highest risk, locks in obsolete technology.

Pre-mortem: Two years post-merge, integration stalls. The legacy network drains capital needed for data expansion. Business customers churn because the "unique" service degrades during migration or lacks data features competitors offer. The $36B becomes a write-down.

Blockers:
- The target network is "incompatible" and "limited for data," contradicting the strategic need for a unified, data-capable platform.
- The plan offers no credible path to migrate the acquired base off the legacy tech, implying permanent dual-network operations which destroys margin synergy.

Risks: Integration failure, goodwill impairment, technology obsolescence.

The proposal asks to pay a premium for a technological liability. Without a defined migration plan to unify the networks, this is not a merger; it is an anchor.

DECISION: NO-GO
