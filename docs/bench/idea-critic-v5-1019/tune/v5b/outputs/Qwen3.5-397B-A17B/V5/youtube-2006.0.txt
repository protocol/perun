First call: NO-GO. The price is astronomical for an asset with zero revenue, massive liabilities, and no proven monetization path.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant traffic leader instantly solves distribution scale, preempting competitors and capturing the cultural shift to online video before it crystallizes, betting that ad models will mature faster than litigation can shut the platform down.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Continue growing the existing smaller service organically; avoids immediate liability but risks irrelevance if the target locks in network effects.
2.  **Build/Buy Smaller:** Acquire a cheaper, compliant niche player; lower risk but fails to capture the "main stage" audience.
3.  **The Proposal:** Pays a 100% premium for market leadership while inheriting existential legal threats that could bankrupt the combined entity.

**Fit:** Aligns with a "organize the world's information" vision only if video is deemed critical infrastructure immediately. However, the financial structure (stock) dilutes current shareholders for an unproven asset.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-close, major studios win an injunction forcing the removal of 80% of popular content. Traffic collapses, ad revenue fails to cover bandwidth, and the stock price halves due to the wasted capital and ongoing legal fees. The integration distracts engineering from core search profitability.

**Evidence & Math:** The target has ~$0 revenue against a $1.65B price tag. Even with optimistic viral growth, the payback period is infinite without a miraculous shift in advertiser behavior or copyright law. The bandwidth costs are linear with usage, while revenue is speculative. The "largest destination" status is built on infringing content, which is a fragile foundation, not a moat.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Financial Suicide:** Paying $1.65B for negative cash flow and undefined liability violates basic capital allocation principles. The numbers do not work under any conservative scenario.
2.  **Existential Legal Risk:** The core value proposition relies on content the company does not own and is actively being sued for. A single adverse ruling could zero out the asset's value while the debt (in diluted equity) remains.

**Risks:** Ad model failure, bandwidth spirals, culture clash. These are secondary to the primary blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** If legal counsel cannot guarantee a safe harbor or settlement path within 30 days, the deal must die. If projected ad CPMs do not exceed bandwidth cost per view by 3x, the unit economics fail.

DECISION: NO-GO
