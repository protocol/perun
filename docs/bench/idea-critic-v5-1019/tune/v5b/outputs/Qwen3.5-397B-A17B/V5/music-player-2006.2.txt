First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market late, abandons a strategic standard (copy-protection) that secured partner alignment, and relies on unproven label cooperation for its key differentiator (wireless sharing) without a price advantage.

Steelman: A superior hardware form factor (larger screen) and seamless wireless sharing will disrupt the incumbent's ecosystem, overcoming lateness and DRM removal risks through user experience superiority.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids channel conflict and sunk costs; preserves existing DRM partnerships.
2. License software only: Leverages the desktop client on existing hardware; lower capex, no store risk.
3. Niche high-fidelity player: Targets audiophiles with the larger screen; avoids mass-market store war.
The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" because the core value prop (sharing) is blocked by the very partners (labels) whose restriction is cited as a risk.

Fit: Assuming the goal is mass-market share, this contradicts the reality of label power in 2006. If the goal is hardware differentiation, the store dependency introduces unnecessary existential risk.

Pre-mortem: The device launches, but labels block wireless sharing due to piracy fears caused by dropping DRM. The store remains empty or expensive. The large screen cannot justify the premium without the ecosystem. The marketing budget burns cash acquiring users who churn due to lack of content. This is a one-way door with high irreversible cost.

Evidence: The proposal admits the leader has an "enormous share," the company is "late," and labels "may restrict sharing." It offers no evidence that labels will reverse their DRM stance for this specific entrant.

Blockers:
1. Contradictory dependency: The plan drops the industry DRM standard while relying on label cooperation for sharing, despite admitting labels may restrict it. The proposal provides no mechanism to secure this cooperation.
2. No price/value leverage: Entering late against a loyal ecosystem with no price advantage and a potentially crippled feature set (if sharing is blocked) guarantees failure.

Risks: Marketing inefficiency, hardware commoditization, channel alienation.

Kill criteria: If label contracts do not explicitly permit unrestricted wireless sharing before hardware tooling begins, the project stops.

DECISION: NO-GO
