First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades long-term user trust for short-term ad data, risking a backlash that could permanently damage the platform's core value: social connection.

**Steelman:** Sharing user activity via an opt-out model with notifications maximizes data liquidity and ad targeting precision while providing a superficial layer of user control.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Mismatch:** An "option to decline" (opt-out) places the burden of privacy on the user. Most users will not navigate settings to disable this, leading to involuntary exposure of sensitive purchases (e.g., medical, financial, or personal gifts). This is not a feature; it is a trap.
2.  **Social Friction:** Broadcasting purchases to friends transforms a commercial transaction into a social performance. Users will feel surveilled, and friends may judge spending habits. This creates immediate social anxiety, reducing platform engagement.
3.  **Partner Risk:** Partner sites face reputational damage if their association with users becomes a public feed item subject to friend commentary. They may withdraw partnerships to avoid this exposure.
4.  **Irreversibility:** As noted in the risks, this is a one-way door. Once users feel betrayed by involuntary exposure, trust cannot be restored by simply turning the feature off later. The "notification step" is insufficient to mitigate the feeling of violation after the fact.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Maintain current ad models without risking user exodus.
*   **Opt-In Only:** Share data only if users explicitly agree. This yields less data initially but preserves trust and ensures willing participation.
*   **Aggregated Insights:** Provide advertisers with anonymized, aggregated data rather than individual social broadcasts.

**Pre-mortem:** The feature launches, users discover sensitive purchases are visible to colleagues and family, media outlets frame it as a privacy scandal, and a mass deletion campaign ensues. The company is forced to retract the feature, but the brand damage is permanent.

**Blockers:**
*   The mechanism relies on user inertia (opt-out) to function, which inherently violates reasonable expectations of privacy for sensitive data.
*   The stated risk of being "hard to withdraw" confirms the potential for catastrophic, irreversible brand damage.

**Risks:** Partner withdrawal, user churn, regulatory scrutiny.

The proposal fails because it monetizes user relationships without their explicit, informed consent, treating privacy as a setting to be bypassed rather than a covenant to be kept.

DECISION: NO-GO
