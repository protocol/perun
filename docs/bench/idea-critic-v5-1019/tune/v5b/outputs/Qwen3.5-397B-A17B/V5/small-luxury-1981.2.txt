First call: NO-GO. The plan to sell a mass-market compact platform under a luxury badge at a premium price contradicts the core asset of a luxury carmaker: exclusivity and engineering distinction.

Steelman: Capturing a new demographic of American compact seekers by leveraging existing parent-company economies of scale to offer an entry-level luxury product without developing a bespoke chassis.

Attack:
1.  **Brand Dilution:** Luxury pricing requires perceived unique value. A shared mass-market platform creates an immediate cognitive dissonance for buyers. If the "luxury" is merely trim on a common car, the premium price becomes indefensible against rivals offering genuine compact luxury engineering.
2.  **Sales Channel Friction:** Salespeople refusing to use the main brand name indicates an internal recognition that the product undermines the brand promise. This friction will kill conversion rates regardless of marketing spend.
3.  **Risk Realization:** The proposal explicitly admits buyers "may see through" the strategy. In luxury goods, perceived authenticity is binary; once seen as a "fake" or "rebadged" economy car, the brand suffers long-term reputational damage that outweighs short-term volume gains.
4.  **Alternatives:** Doing nothing preserves brand equity. Developing a distinct, smaller platform specific to the luxury division, while costlier, protects the brand's value proposition.

Pre-mortem: The car launches, critics and consumers identify the shared underpinnings immediately. Sales are weak due to high price-to-value ratio. The main brand's prestige erodes as it becomes associated with "cheap" platforms. The model is discontinued within three years, having damaged the brand's resale value and image.

Blockers:
*   **Contradictory Value Proposition:** The plan relies on a "premium price" for a "mass-market platform." The stated fact that buyers seek "luxury" conflicts with the reality of a shared economy chassis. You cannot charge luxury margins for non-luxury engineering without deceiving the customer, which is unsustainable.
*   **Internal Rejection:** The stated risk that salespeople will not endorse the brand confirms the product fails internal quality/brand standards.

Risks: Brand erosion, sales failure, channel conflict. These are not merely possibilities; they are direct consequences of the proposed architecture.

The proposal asks to trade long-term brand equity for short-term market share using a mechanism (platform sharing) that directly destroys the equity being sold.

DECISION: NO-GO
