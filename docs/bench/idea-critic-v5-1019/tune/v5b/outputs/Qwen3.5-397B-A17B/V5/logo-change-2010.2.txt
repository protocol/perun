First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates a high-cost, irreversible change with zero validation against its primary risk: alienating the core customer base.

**Steelman:** A simultaneous, unannounced rebrand creates immediate market shock to force a contemporary perception, betting that visual boldness outweighs loyalty friction.

**Attack:**
1.  **Evidence Void:** The proposal explicitly admits the new design was "not tested with customers." Launching a brand identity without validation contradicts the goal of retaining a "mature" retailer's base while updating it.
2.  **Risk vs. Reversibility:** Rolling out to "every channel at once" with "no advance announcement" creates a one-way door. If backlash occurs (highly probable given stated risks), the brand cannot quietly revert; the confusion itself damages equity.
3.  **Alternatives Ignored:** "Do nothing" retains current equity. A "staged pilot" (e.g., digital-only first) offers the same upside potential with contained downside. The proposal beats neither.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The most likely failure mode is immediate social media backlash framing the brand as "erasing its history," causing loyalists to boycott. The proposal lists this risk but offers no mitigation, relying on silence rather than strategy.
5.  **Blocker:** The absence of customer testing is a factual blocker for a decision involving high reputational risk. You cannot approve a strategy that explicitly ignores its own stated primary failure point.

**Decision Logic:**
*   **Blockers:** No customer validation data; "all-at-once" approach prevents iterative learning; no communication plan for a change explicitly predicted to cause backlash.
*   **Risks:** High probability of alienating loyal customers; irreversible brand damage if the new mark fails to resonate.

This is not a "go-if" scenario because the missing element (testing) fundamentally invalidates the "all-at-once" execution method. You cannot test and launch simultaneously. The plan must be revised to include a validation phase and a staged rollout before any approval is possible. Approving this as proposed is negligence.

DECISION: NO-GO
