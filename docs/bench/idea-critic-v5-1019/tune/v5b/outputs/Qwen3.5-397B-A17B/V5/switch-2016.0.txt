First call: GO. The hybrid form factor directly addresses the eroding handheld line while leveraging existing IP without requiring a costly hardware arms race.

**Steelman:** A hybrid device leverages the maker's strong first-party software to create a unique market position that rivals cannot match with raw power alone, recapturing lapsed handheld users and offering a distinct home experience.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Fit:** The proposal pivots from failing at both pure home and pure handheld markets to dominating the niche where neither phones nor high-power consoles compete. It aligns with the need to stop financial bleeding by reducing R&D risk on cutting-edge silicon.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" guarantees continued erosion by phones. Building a more powerful home console repeats the previous failure mode (financial loss) and ignores the handheld crisis. A cheaper, weaker handheld alone cannot command the proposed price point or solve the home presence issue.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if third-party support collapses entirely or if the "confusing positioning" narrative sticks, causing consumers to wait. However, the maker's own studios provide a sufficient launch baseline to drive early adoption, mitigating the thin lineup risk.
4.  **Evidence:** The financial losses from the previous home console and the erosion of the handheld line are verified facts supporting the need for a radical departure. The modest chip choice is a strategic constraint to ensure profitability at the proposed price point, not a technical oversight.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (specs, positioning, third-party support) are execution challenges inherent to any new platform launch, not factual contradictions of the plan's core logic. The "weak specs" are a feature of the cost/strategy, not a bug.

**Risks:** Third-party abandonment and consumer confusion. These become kill criteria: if pre-orders from key partners fall below a specific threshold by launch minus 3 months, or if marketing tracking shows >40% confusion on the "hybrid" concept, the launch marketing spend must be paused for repositioning.

**Decision:** The proposal correctly identifies that competing on power is a losing battle given recent history. It bets on unique utility and first-party content, which are the maker's proven strengths. The risks are manageable through aggressive first-party scheduling and clear marketing, whereas the alternative is stagnation.

DECISION: GO
