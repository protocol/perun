First call: NO-GO. Doubling production while sales lag by 60% during a recession with rising interest rates destroys cash without a verified buyer.

Steelman: The claim is that flooding the market now creates unstoppable momentum, forcing dealers to absorb stock and securing market share before competitors react, assuming demand is merely suppressed by visibility, not affordability.

Alternatives:
1. **Do nothing/Reduce:** Match production to sales (3,000 run rate) to preserve cash; risk losing shelf space but survive longer.
2. **Incentivize Demand:** Use limited cash for consumer rebates or low-rate financing to clear the existing 4,500 unit overhang before building more.
3. **Proposal (Double Output):** Increases unsold inventory from 4,500 to potentially 9,000+ units rapidly.

Fit: This contradicts basic survival logic for a cash-constrained startup in a high-rate recession. The "north star" must be liquidity preservation, not volume accumulation.

Pre-mortem: The company doubles output. Dealers, already strained by high interest rates, refuse further financing. The startup burns its remaining cash on parts and labor for cars that sit in lots. Interest costs on inventory financing spike. The company faces insolvency within months due to cash exhaustion, not lack of product.

Evidence & Math:
- Current Stock: 7,500 produced - 3,000 sold = 4,500 unsold.
- Sales Rate: ~3,000/year (approx. 250/month).
- Proposed Action: Double monthly output.
- Result: Inventory growth accelerates while sales stagnate or drop due to recession.
- Financing: Rising interest rates make holding inventory more expensive, directly attacking the dealer's ability to stock more.

Blockers:
1. **Cash Flow Mismatch:** The proposal requires immediate cash outflow for production against delayed or non-existent inflow from sales. With "limited cash" stated, this is a direct path to insolvency.
2. **Dealer Capacity:** High interest rates explicitly reduce dealer financing capacity; forcing more stock onto them is operationally impossible if they cannot borrow to buy it.
3. **Demand Contradiction:** The proposal ignores the stated fact that the economy is sliding into recession, meaning demand is falling, not waiting to be unlocked by volume.

Risks: Total capital depletion, receiver appointment, brand collapse.

Kill Criteria: If cash reserves cannot cover 6 months of operations at current burn without new sales, this plan fails immediately. The leading metric is "Days Cash Remaining," which this plan accelerates toward zero.

DECISION: NO-GO
