First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to transplant a successful electronics retail model onto a mid-market department store with a fundamentally different, coupon-conditioned customer base, without any validation.

Steelman: Eliminating coupons and rebuilding stores into branded shops will modernize the brand, simplify operations, and attract higher-margin shoppers, replicating the success of the CEO's previous electronics chain.

However, the attack on this claim reveals fatal flaws in the stated facts:
1.  **Customer Incentive Mismatch:** The proposal admits most sales historically come from discounted items and the base is "coupon-driven." Abruptly removing the primary purchase trigger for the core revenue source ignores inverted incentives. These customers are trained to wait; they are unlikely to accept "everyday pricing" without a transitional period or proof of value they currently do not perceive.
2.  **Unverified Analogy:** The strategy relies entirely on the CEO's past success in *electronics*, a category with different margin structures, purchase frequencies, and customer expectations than mid-market apparel/home goods. What worked for branded electronics may fail catastrophically for general merchandise.
3.  **Irreversible Scale:** Launching chain-wide without a pilot ignores the "pace of change" risk. If the hypothesis is wrong (i.e., customers leave rather than adapt), the company faces immediate, unrecoverable revenue collapse across all locations before a correction can be made.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Most sales come from discounted items. **Contradiction:** The plan removes discounts immediately, directly attacking the stated revenue engine without evidence that the remaining volume at full price will compensate.
*   **Fact:** No pilot test. **Contradiction:** The plan commits the entire chain to a high-risk behavioral shift based solely on an analogy to a different industry.

**Risks:**
*   Existing customers defect en masse before new demographics are acquired.
*   Capital expenditure on store rebuilds yields negative ROI if traffic plummets.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The proposal lacks a "stop" condition. If sales drop 20% in month one, is there a revert plan? The absence of a pilot means there is no early warning system. The risk concerns the core claim (that buyers will pay full price), the facts suggest it could fail (historical dependence on coupons), and the commitment is total and hard to reverse (chain-wide rollout). This meets the threshold for a blocker.

The plan fails the "do nothing" comparison: maintaining the status quo preserves current cash flow while allowing for a tested, staged transition. The proposed "all-in" approach bets the company on an unproven hypothesis in a declining sales environment.

DECISION: NO-GO
