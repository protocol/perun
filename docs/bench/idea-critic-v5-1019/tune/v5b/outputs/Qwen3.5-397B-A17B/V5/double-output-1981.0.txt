First call: NO-GO. Doubling production while sales lag by 60% during a recession with rising interest rates destroys cash without a verified buyer.

**Steelman:** The claim is that flooding the market now secures future dominance or forces dealer absorption before a predicted recovery, betting that inventory costs are lower than the cost of lost market share later.

**Options:**
1.  **Double Production:** Maximizes cash burn; relies on dealers financing unsold stock they cannot move. Highest risk of insolvency.
2.  **Do Nothing/Reduce:** Aligns output with demand; preserves cash. Risks losing manufacturing momentum but ensures survival.
3.  **Incentivize Demand:** Use limited cash for consumer rebates or dealer financing subsidies instead of building more units. Addresses the root cause (demand/cost of capital).

**Fit:** In a recession with high interest rates, liquidity is the north star. This proposal directly contradicts survival logic by converting scarce cash into depreciating inventory that dealers explicitly cannot finance.

**Pre-mortem:** The company runs out of cash in six months. The doubled inventory sits on lots, accruing storage and financing costs. Dealers refuse delivery due to credit constraints. The company enters bankruptcy with double the unsold assets and zero liquidity. This is a one-way door to failure; the cost of delay (waiting for recovery) is lower than the cost of immediate insolvency.

**Evidence:** The proposal's own facts state sales are 3,000 vs. 7,500 produced. Interest rates are rising, increasing the cost to hold inventory and decreasing consumer affordability. Dealers lack financing capacity. There is no evidence of pent-up demand or a mechanism to move the additional 15,000+ annualized units.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Cash Flow Mismatch:** Limited cash cannot support doubled production costs while revenue remains flat.
2.  **Distribution Bottleneck:** Dealers explicitly cannot finance more stock; building more cars creates a physical logjam with no outlet.
3.  **Demand Contradiction:** Recession and high rates suppress demand; increasing supply ignores the stated market reality.

**Risks:** Inventory depreciation, brand damage from distressed assets, total capital loss.

**Decision:** The plan fails the basic test of matching supply to verified demand and liquidity constraints. It accelerates failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
