First call: GO. The proposal targets a specific, underserved segment (low-cost, simple utility) that incumbents are ignoring, turning a technical limitation (random movement) into a cost advantage.

**Steelman:** A functional, affordable robot vacuum using randomized navigation can capture mass-market adoption by prioritizing price and simplicity over perfect coverage, validating the category before competitors dominate.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Cedes the low-end market to future entrants; high risk of being locked out as a niche player.
2.  **Mapping Rivals:** High cost, complex tech, slow time-to-market. Fails the "affordability" test for early adopters.
3.  **Fit:** Aligns with a "land grab" strategy for consumer robotics. The lack of consumer experience is a risk, but the simple product design minimizes operational complexity compared to mapping units.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if random navigation leaves visible debris clusters, causing immediate returns. However, the "low cost" premise absorbs higher return rates better than premium models. The "dumb" robot is a feature for price-sensitive buyers, not a bug.

**Evidence & Kill Criteria:**
The disinterest from floor-care companies is a positive signal; they are biased toward professional-grade, high-margin equipment and likely underestimate the consumer "novelty/utility" overlap. Their rejection validates the need to bypass traditional distribution and sell direct or via mass retail.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (trust, coverage, experience) are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws in the value proposition. The randomized approach is a deliberate trade-off for price, not an oversight.

**Risks:** Brand damage from poor cleaning performance; inventory buildup if adoption lags.
**Kill Criteria:** If initial pilot sales show a return rate >20% due to "poor cleaning" complaints within 90 days, halt production and pivot to a hybrid model or licensing. Leading metric: Units sold per week vs. return rate.

The proposal correctly identifies that waiting for perfect technology or partner validation will kill the opportunity. The market needs a "good enough" solution now.

DECISION: GO
