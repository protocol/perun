First call: GO. The removal of a legacy port creates an immediate void; filling it with a premium, ecosystem-locking accessory is a standard high-margin recovery strategy.

**Steelman:** Launching proprietary wireless earbuds at $159 converts a customer pain point (dongle dependency) into a recurring revenue stream while deepening hardware integration via the custom chip.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Cedes the accessory market to third parties and leaves users with a fragmented experience.
*   **Cheaper Alternative:** Selling a basic Bluetooth headset lacks the seamless pairing "magic" required to justify the phone's premium positioning.
*   **Risks vs. Reality:** Ridicule over aesthetics is transient; functionality drives adoption. Loss risk is mitigated by the charging case (which also solves battery limits). The $159 price point anchors the product as premium, distinct from the "free" wired option which is now obsolete for this device. Anger over the jack removal is inevitable but irrelevant if the wireless alternative is the only viable path forward.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs only if the custom chip pairing is buggy or the battery life is significantly worse than advertised. The proposal assumes the chip works as intended.

**Evidence & Metrics:** The decision rests on the assumption that the proprietary chip delivers a user experience superior to generic Bluetooth. Without this, the price premium collapses.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (looks, loss, price, anger) are execution challenges or market reactions, not factual contradictions of the business case. The "free" wired earphone is no longer a valid comparison point once the jack is removed.

**Kill Criteria:** If initial pairing success rates fall below 95% in beta testing, or if pre-order conversion from phone buyers is under 15%, the launch strategy must pivot to include a dongle bundle or price reduction.

**Leading Metric:** Attachment rate (earbuds sold per new phone unit) in the first quarter.

DECISION: GO
