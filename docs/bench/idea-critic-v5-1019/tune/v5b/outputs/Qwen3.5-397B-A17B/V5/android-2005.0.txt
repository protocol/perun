First call: NO-GO. A search-ad company with no hardware DNA spending $50M+ on a 6-person secret OS project in 2005 contradicts core competency and market reality.

**Steelman:** Acquiring this OS is the only defensive move to prevent search irrelevance if mobile browsing displaces desktop, using open-source to bypass carrier/handset gatekeepers.

**Options:**
1.  **Acquire:** High cost ($50M+), extreme risk (adoption failure), low reversibility.
2.  **Partner/License:** Lower cost, moderate risk, higher reversibility.
3.  **Do Nothing:** Zero cost, risk of long-term displacement, fully reversible until market shifts.
The proposal fails to beat "Do Nothing" or "Partner" on stated facts. Carriers and handset makers have zero incentive to adopt a newcomer's OS; distribution is blocked.

**Fit:** Assumes mobile search will mirror desktop behavior immediately. This contradicts the stated constraint that carriers control distribution and handset makers are entrenched. The strategy relies on a hypothetical future (mobile web dominance) rather than current leverage.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because handset makers refuse to install the OS, carriers block it, and the $50M burns while the core search business loses focus. It is a one-way door with a multi-year payback and no revenue model.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "no revenue model," "handset-maker adoption" risks, and "carrier resistance." The $50M price tag for six people implies a speculative premium unsupported by current traction.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Distribution Impossibility:** Stated facts confirm carriers and handset makers control access and have "no reason to adopt." Without a path to installation, the bet on "pushing mobile web use" is mathematically zero.
2.  **Core Competency Mismatch:** A software/ad firm acquiring an OS/hardware-adjacent asset violates the "no hardware business" constraint without a defined bridge.
3.  **Economic Failure:** No revenue model combined with a $50M+ outlay and multi-year payback fails the basic viability test for a non-core asset.

**Risks:** Long payback, team retention, open-source fragmentation. These are secondary to the blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** If handset adoption is <5% within 24 months, terminate. Leading metric: Pre-installs signed.

The plan commits massive capital to a distribution channel that explicitly rejects it, based on a revenue model that does not exist.

DECISION: NO-GO
