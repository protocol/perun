First call: NO-GO. Launching a business-centric device without native email, calendar, or contacts directly contradicts the company's core strength and value proposition, effectively disarming its primary competitive advantage.

**Steelman:** The 7-inch form factor targets a specific portability niche, leveraging a newly acquired OS to diversify beyond phones, with the hypothesis that phone-pairing mitigates the lack of native apps initially.

**Attack:**
1.  **Strategic Contradiction:** The proposal admits the company "leads in business email," yet the device ships without built-in email unless paired. For a standalone tablet, this is a fatal usability gap for the target demographic. Business users will not tolerate a workflow dependency on a phone to access their primary tool.
2.  **Pricing Failure:** Matching the price of a rival's *larger* tablet with a smaller screen, fewer native apps, and an untested OS offers zero value. The rival's large app store further widens the gap.
3.  **Platform Risk:** An untested OS with uncertain developer commitment creates a "ghost town" risk. Without native apps or a robust ecosystem, the hardware is a commodity with no software moat.
4.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "delay" option is superior until native email/calendar apps are ready. Alternatively, license the rival's OS or delay launch to build the app ecosystem, rather than releasing a crippled product that damages the brand's business reputation.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, reviewers pan the lack of email, business customers reject the phone-dependency, developers ignore the barren OS, and the product is discontinued within a year, wasting the OS acquisition cost.

**Blockers:**
*   **Core Value Mismatch:** Shipping a "business" device without native email contradicts the stated market leadership and user needs.
*   **Price/Value Disparity:** Equal price for inferior hardware (smaller) and software (no apps, untested OS) ensures immediate market rejection.

**Risks:** Developer abandonment, brand erosion in the enterprise sector.

The proposal fails the basic test of delivering value consistent with the brand's strength. It asks business users to pay a premium for a device that lacks their essential tools.

DECISION: NO-GO
