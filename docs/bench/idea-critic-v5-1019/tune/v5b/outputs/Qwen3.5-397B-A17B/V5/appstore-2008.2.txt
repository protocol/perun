First call: GO. A closed, curated store turns untrusted code from an existential risk into a managed product feature, aligning developer incentives (70% revenue) with platform stability.

**Steelman:** A single, company-run store with mandatory review and a 30% fee is the only viable path to scale third-party innovation while preserving the device's reputation for polish and security, directly countering carrier fears of bypassed services.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Store:** High control, high trust, immediate revenue share. Beats "do nothing" by capturing the ecosystem value rivals already enjoy.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Cedes the entire application market to competitors; the device becomes a web-only terminal, likely failing against rival ecosystems.
3.  **Open Sideloading:** Maximizes developer freedom but invites malware and instability, validating carrier objections and destroying the "polish" differentiator.

**Fit:** Moves the north star from "web apps are sufficient" to "best mobile experience," acknowledging that native apps are required for competitive parity.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the review process becomes a bottleneck, starving the store of content, or if the 30% fee drives key developers to web apps. However, the cost of delay is total market irrelevance; waiting a quarter allows rivals to lock in users and developers permanently.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits rival platforms *already* have ecosystems. The stated concerns (security, stability, carrier relations) are solved *by* the proposed controls (review, single store), not arguments against them. The 30% fee is standard for providing distribution and trust.

**Blockers:** NONE. The concerns listed are risks, not factual contradictions. The plan explicitly addresses security and stability via review, and carrier objections via the walled garden.
**Risks:** Review bottlenecks, developer revolt over fees. These become kill criteria: if app approval takes >2 weeks or top 50 developers refuse launch, pivot.

**Kill Criteria:** If the store launches with fewer than 500 quality apps in six months, or if a security breach occurs due to review failure, halt expansion and audit the process. Leading metric: Number of approved apps vs. submissions ratio.

DECISION: GO
