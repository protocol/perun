First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets $1B+ on unproven demand for premium short-form subscriptions amid known fatigue, without a pilot or evidence that users will pay for what they currently get free.

**Steelman:** A high-budget, mobile-native service with superior tech and A-list talent can carve a profitable niche by offering quality short-form content that justifies a subscription despite free alternatives.

**Attack:**
1.  **Demand Contradiction:** The proposal admits "no proven demand" and "subscription fatigue" while asking for massive capital. Free, abundant short-form video (UGC and ad-supported) dominates the exact use case (mobile, <10 mins). Converting users from free to premium for the same format is a historic failure mode, not a proven opportunity.
2.  **Cost vs. Value:** High production budgets and A-list talent drastically increase the break-even point. Short-form content typically relies on volume and low marginal cost; this model inverts that, requiring immense recurring revenue from a format users are conditioned to consume for free.
3.  **Differentiation Illusion:** "Video looks right vertically or horizontally" is a baseline expectation, not a defensible moat. It does not solve the core value gap: why pay?
4.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "acquire existing niche player" options offer lower risk. A cheaper alternative is a capped pilot ($50M) to test willingness-to-pay before raising $1B. The current plan skips validation entirely.
5.  **Pre-mortem:** The service launches, acquires few subscribers due to price resistance, burns cash on expensive content that fails to retain, and collapses within 18 months as studios pull funding. The "tech feature" is irrelevant to the churn.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fatal Assumption:** The plan assumes users will pay a premium for short-form professional video despite abundant free substitutes, contradicting the stated market reality of "subscription fatigue."
*   **Scale Mismatch:** Raising $1B+ before validating unit economics or demand is a structural failure. The commitment is too large for the level of uncertainty.

**Risks:** Competition for attention, high churn, content cost inflation. These are secondary to the fundamental lack of product-market fit evidence.

**Kill Criteria:** None exist in the proposal; none can be applied post-$1B spend effectively.

The absence of a pilot, the contradiction between "no proven demand" and the requested scale, and the availability of free substitutes create an insurmountable blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
