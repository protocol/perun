First call: GO. The proposal leverages existing assets to address a clear market gap for families needing garage-friendly utility, turning a survival situation into a category-defining opportunity.

**Steelman:** By adapting a proven compact-car platform and converting an idle factory, the company can launch a low-cost, car-like van that creates a new household segment, utilizing government backing to bridge cash constraints.

**Attack & Analysis:**
*   **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" risks continued irrelevance as station wagons saturate. Building a new platform is impossible given the cash shortage. Reusing the compact platform is the *only* viable path, not a limitation; it ensures cost parity with cars while offering van utility.
*   **Fit:** This directly targets the stated buyer behavior (families buying wagons/vans) by offering a hybrid solution. It aligns with the survival stage: low capital expenditure, high potential upside.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure would stem from marketing the vehicle as a commercial tool rather than a family hauler, or if the compact platform proves too cramped. However, the "garage-fit" requirement validates the size constraint as a feature, not a bug.
*   **Evidence:** The government guarantee provides the necessary liquidity bridge. The lack of an existing category is an opportunity for first-mover advantage, not a risk of non-existence. Rivals copying the idea validates the market once established; being first allows brand ownership of the "minivan" concept.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited are speculative or manageable through positioning. The cash constraint is mitigated by the loan guarantee and platform reuse.
**Risks:** Commercial perception (mitigated by marketing focus); competitor response (mitigated by speed to market).

**Kill Criteria:** If initial consumer testing shows >60% perceive it strictly as a commercial vehicle, pivot messaging or halt. Leading metric: Pre-order conversion rate from family-oriented demographics within 3 months of reveal.

The proposal represents a classic "two-way door" decision with asymmetric upside. The constraints (cash, platform) force the discipline required for success. The absence of a category confirms the blue-ocean potential.

DECISION: GO
