First call: GO. A component seller facing indistinguishable clones and ignorant buyers must shift power to the end-user; branding is the only lever to force computer makers to prefer your chip over identical alternatives.

Steelman: By making the invisible visible, you transform a commodity into a demanded feature, compelling computer makers to participate or lose sales to rivals who do.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Remain a hidden commodity; price becomes the only differentiator; margin collapses as clones proliferate.
2. Trade promotions only: Incentivizes makers but fails to create consumer pull; makers still swap chips for pennies.
3. Consumer brand campaign (Proposed): High cost and channel friction, but creates irreversible demand pull that locks makers in. It beats "do nothing" because the status quo guarantees commoditization.

Fit: Assumes the goal is margin protection and market dominance, not just unit volume. If the north star is being the preferred engine of the PC revolution, this fits; if it is merely shipping silicon, it is overkill.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if computer makers form a cartel to reject the logo, or if the message confuses consumers who think they are buying the whole computer. The door is two-way initially (ads can stop), but brand perception, once shifted, is hard to reverse. Cost of delay: High. Every month without branding allows clones to cement the idea that "all chips are the same."

Evidence: The proposal states buyers currently do not know which chip they have and clones use similar numbers. This factual baseline confirms the commodity trap. The risk of "unprecedented" action is not a blocker; it is the price of creating a new category. The risk of maker resentment is mitigated by the financial incentive (co-op advertising); those who refuse will face consumers asking for the competitor's logo.

Blockers: NONE. The facts presented show a classic commodity trap where the only escape is demand-pull branding.
Risks: Channel conflict, high budget share, unproven medium. These are managed by the co-op structure (makers opt-in) and the existential threat of doing nothing.

DECISION: GO
