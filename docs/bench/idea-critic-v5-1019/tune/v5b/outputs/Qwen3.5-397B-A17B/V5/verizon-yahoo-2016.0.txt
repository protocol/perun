First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a scale deficit by acquiring a second declining asset, creating a larger entity with shrinking revenue and no proven path to growth.

Steelman: Combining two distressed media portfolios creates sufficient scale to negotiate better terms and consolidate technology, potentially stabilizing cash flow long enough to pivot to mobile.

However, the attack on this claim reveals fatal flaws. First, the "better way" analysis fails: doing nothing preserves capital, while acquiring a second falling knife doubles the integration burden without addressing the core issue—relevance in a mobile-first market dominated by two entrenched giants. The target has falling traffic, falling revenue, and unstable leadership; these are not temporary dips but structural declines.

Second, the fit is non-existent. The buyer is a wireless carrier with zero history in consumer media. They lack the cultural DNA, product instincts, and operational playbooks required to turn around a creative, fast-moving ad business. Adding a second distressed asset to a company with no prior success in the domain increases execution risk exponentially.

Third, the pre-mortem is clear: one year post-close, the combined entity faces deeper revenue declines as integration distractions stall innovation. The $4.8 billion price tag locks capital into depreciating assets while competitors accelerate. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of a failed integration; waiting allows the market to clarify further without burning billions.

Evidence check: The proposal admits the target's metrics are negative and the buyer lacks experience. These are not risks to be managed; they are blockers that invalidate the core thesis that scale alone creates competitiveness. No kill criteria or pilot can fix a strategy that relies on reversing multi-year structural declines in a sector where the buyer has no competency.

Blockers:
1. Buyer has zero relevant operating experience; target has multi-year structural decline.
2. $4.8B commits massive capital to depreciating assets with no turnaround plan.
3. Scale does not equal competitiveness against dominant platforms when the underlying product is shrinking.

Risks: Integration failure, continued revenue erosion, cultural clash.

DECISION: NO-GO
