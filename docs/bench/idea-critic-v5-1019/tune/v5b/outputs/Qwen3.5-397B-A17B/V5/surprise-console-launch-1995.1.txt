First call: NO-GO. Launching early with no games, high price, and alienated retailers contradicts the core requirement for a successful console launch: a robust ecosystem.

**Steelman:** An early May launch at $399 secures mindshare and shelf space before the competitor's September arrival, accepting limited SKUs to establish a foothold.

**Attack:**
1.  **Content Void:** Consoles are useless without software. Shipping with "few games" guarantees negative word-of-mouth. Early adopters will buy, play the single title, and return the unit or let it gather dust, killing momentum before the competitor arrives.
2.  **Price Disadvantage:** At $100 above the rival, the value proposition collapses without a superior library. Consumers will wait for the cheaper, better-supported September option.
3.  **Channel Hostility:** Excluding major retailers to favor a few creates permanent enemies. In 1995, retail shelf space is the primary bottleneck; retaliatory exclusion from key chains during the critical holiday season would be fatal.
4.  **Developer Friction:** Forcing an early date on developers struggling with complex 3D tools results in broken, unpolished launch titles, reinforcing the "hard to program" reputation and deterring future support.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing (September Launch):* Aligns with developers, maximizes game count, matches competitor timing, and preserves retailer relationships.
*   *Pre-order Campaign:* Announce in May, ship in September. Captures mindshare without the operational disaster of empty shelves.

**Pre-mortem:** By December 1995, the early launch is viewed as a desperate failure. Retailers have blacklisted the brand, reviewers have panned the barren library, and the competitor has captured the market with a full slate of games at a lower price. The early move burned cash and goodwill without securing market share.

**Blockers:**
*   **Insufficient Software:** The proposal admits "few games ready." This is a factual blocker for a hardware platform launch.
*   **Retailer Retaliation:** High probability of channel exclusion based on the proposal's own risk assessment.
*   **Price/Value Mismatch:** $399 for a limited library vs. $299 for a competitor with full support.

**Risks:** Developer burnout, brand damage, inventory stagnation.

**Kill Criteria:** If launch library < 10 titles, or if >20% of major retailers refuse stock, the launch fails.

DECISION: NO-GO
