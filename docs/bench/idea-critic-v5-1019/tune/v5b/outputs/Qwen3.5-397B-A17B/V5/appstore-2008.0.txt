First call: GO. The proposal converts a "web-only" constraint into a controlled ecosystem, directly addressing the risk of rivals locking in developers while mitigating security and stability through a single review gate.

Steelman: A curated native app store creates a defensible moat by offering developers monetization and users superior performance, while the 30% cut and review process fund the platform and ensure quality, outweighing the operational burden.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing (Web apps only): Lowest cost, but cedes the high-value native ecosystem to rivals; likely fatal long-term.
2. Open sideloading: Maximizes developer speed but invites security chaos and carrier backlash; threatens product polish.
3. Proposed Store: Balances control with openness; captures value while managing risk.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of creating a superior, differentiated consumer product. Web apps alone cannot match native performance or hardware access, which are critical for the "polish" the CEO values.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the review process becomes a bottleneck, stifling innovation, or if carriers block the phone due to bypassed services. However, the 30% revenue share offers a lever to negotiate with carriers, and a centralized store is faster to scale than fragmented sideloading support. The cost of delay is losing the developer mindshare to established rivals permanently.

Evidence: The proposal acknowledges existing rival ecosystems, confirming market demand. The 30% model is standard for value exchange (distribution + payment). Security risks are managed by the "review and approve" clause, turning a vulnerability into a quality filter.

Blockers: NONE. The concerns (security, carrier objections, workload) are operational challenges inherent to the strategy, not facts proving the plan fails. The plan explicitly includes the mechanism (review) to address security and polish.

Risks: Review backlog slowing innovation; carrier pushback. These are managed via the kill criteria: if app approval time exceeds 7 days or carrier deals stall launch, pivot to a tiered review system or revenue-sharing negotiations.

The "web apps are sufficient" stance is invalidated by the rival native ecosystems; sticking to it guarantees irrelevance. The proposed store is the only viable path to compete.

DECISION: GO
