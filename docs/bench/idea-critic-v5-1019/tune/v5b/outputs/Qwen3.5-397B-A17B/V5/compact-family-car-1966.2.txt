First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a high-fixed-cost dealer network and a single engine size without evidence that buyers want this specific combination over existing cheap small cars or larger alternatives.

Steelman: A dedicated 1100 cc compact sold through a proprietary network will capture families needing more space than current small cars but less cost than large ones, utilizing the new plant's capacity.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoids dealer costs but leaves the new plant underutilized.
2. Shared network: Sell through existing channels to reduce upfront capital risk.
3. Multi-engine strategy: Offer a range of engines to hedge against shifting buyer preference.
The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" or "shared network" because the dedicated network cost is a massive, irreversible bet on unproven demand.

Fit: Assumes the north star is market share growth via a new segment, but the execution method (dedicated network) contradicts the constraint of competing against low-price rivals.

Pre-mortem: The plant runs below capacity because the dedicated network takes too long to build, while buyers choose established rivals' cheaper models or wait for a larger car. This is a one-way door with high delay cost; waiting allows testing demand without building dealers first.

Evidence: No data supports that buyers specifically want an 1100 cc engine over available options, nor that a dedicated network is required to sell this car. The risks raised (costly network, rival pricing, capacity needs) are acknowledged but not mitigated by the plan.

Blockers:
1. The commitment to a costly dedicated dealer network before validating demand for the specific 1100 cc segment creates a financial trap if sales lag.
2. No alternative distribution method is considered despite the explicit risk of high network costs.
3. The single engine size ignores the risk that buyers may prefer cheaper or larger options, with no fallback.

Risks: Competitor price wars, slow network rollout, misaligned product-market fit.

Kill criteria: None proposed. A capped pilot using existing dealers or a temporary sales channel should precede full network construction. Without a cheaper, reversible path to validate the 1100 cc family segment, the plan is too brittle.

DECISION: NO-GO
