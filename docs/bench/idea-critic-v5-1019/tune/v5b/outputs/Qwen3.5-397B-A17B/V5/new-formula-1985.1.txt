First call: NO-GO. Replacing a heritage asset based solely on taste metrics ignores the brand's emotional equity.

**Steelman:** The data proves consumers prefer the sweeter taste; swapping formulas maximizes immediate sensory satisfaction and halts share loss to rivals.

**Attack:** The testing methodology is fatally flawed. Participants preferred the sweeter taste *given a choice*, but the proposal removes the choice entirely. Blind tests isolate flavor from brand identity, yet cola purchases are driven by heritage, nostalgia, and ritual. By withdrawing the original, the plan converts "preference for sweetness" into "loss of identity," triggering a backlash no taste metric can predict. The risk of alienating loyalists is not theoretical; it is the direct outcome of removing their option.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Retain status quo; continue slow share erosion.
2.  **Line Extension (Better Way):** Launch the sweeter formula as "New Cola" while keeping the original. This captures the sweet-preference segment without betraying heritage.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Replace original entirely. High risk, irreversible in the short term.

**Fit:** This contradicts the north star of brand loyalty. A brand that discards its history to chase a metric loses its reason for existing.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales plummet not because the drink tastes bad, but because consumers feel betrayed. Competitors capitalize on the outrage by marketing themselves as the "authentic" choice. Bottlers face riots; the company is forced to reintroduce the old formula at great cost, looking indecisive.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits testing did not inform participants of the removal. This omission invalidates the results for a *replacement* strategy. The stated risks (emotional attachment, no fallback) are confirmed blockers because the plan offers no mitigation other than hope.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Methodology Failure:** Taste tests measured preference in isolation, not reaction to removal. The core claim (swapping works) rests on data that does not support the specific action of withdrawal.
2.  **No Fallback:** Removing the only alternative guarantees that any negative reaction has no pressure valve.

**Risks:** Emotional backlash, bottler revolt, permanent brand damage.

**Kill Criteria:** If a line extension captures 50% of the projected gain without removing the original, the replacement plan is void.

DECISION: NO-GO
