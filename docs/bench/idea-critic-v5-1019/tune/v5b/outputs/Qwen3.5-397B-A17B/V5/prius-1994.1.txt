First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a fixed public schedule for unproven core technologies (battery durability, novel transmission) without a demand signal or regulatory mandate, while facing cheap fuel alternatives. This creates massive downside risk with no immediate upside.

**Steelman:** A bold, fixed-schedule launch of a novel hybrid powertrain by 1997 will secure first-mover dominance and define the future of automotive efficiency before competitors react.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital until regulators force the issue or battery tech matures. A "technology demonstrator" program validates durability without the warranty exposure of mass production. The proposal fails to beat "do nothing" on risk-adjusted returns given cheap fuel and zero demand signals.
2.  **Fit:** The strategy assumes a market shift that current facts (cheap fuel, no mandates) explicitly contradict. Betting the corporate program on an unproven consumer preference is a mismatch with the stated environment.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume the 1997 launch failed. Cause: Battery packs degrade rapidly in real-world cycles, triggering catastrophic warranty claims that exceed the vehicle's margin. The novel transmission suffers teething issues, delaying volume and tarnishing the brand. With no regulatory stick or consumer pull, sales flatline, and the program becomes a financial sinkhole.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits battery durability is "unproven" and there is "no near-term demand signal." Committing to mass production based on assumptions rather than data is a fundamental planning error.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Core Technology:** Mass production commitment relies on battery durability and transmission reliability that are explicitly stated as unproven. This is a factual gap in the execution plan.
*   **No Economic Case:** Cheap fuel and lack of regulatory mandates mean the product has no cost advantage or legal necessity for the consumer, contradicting the assumption of viable demand.

**Risks:** Warranty exposure from battery failure; brand damage from reliability issues; capital misallocation in a low-demand market.

**Kill Criteria:** If battery cycle testing does not demonstrate 5-year durability equivalent to conventional powertrains within 12 months, the program must pivot to R&D only.

The absence of a pilot, validated demand, or proven technology constitutes a blocker for a mass-production commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
