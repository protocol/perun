First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a tiny installed base, an unproven legal music supply, and a premium price in a market where cheaper devices have already failed to gain traction.

**Steelman:** A premium, high-capacity device could create a new category if it offers a seamless end-to-end experience that fragmented competitors cannot match, leveraging the brand's design reputation to justify the price.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Reality:** Cheaper players exist but haven't sold well. This suggests the problem isn't just capacity or price, but the lack of a legal, easy way to get music. Without licensed downloads, this device is an expensive hard drive for ripped CDs, a niche within a niche.
2.  **Addressable Base:** Restricting use to owners of a small-share computer line drastically caps the initial market. At $400, the value proposition for this tiny group is weak compared to waiting for broader compatibility or cheaper alternatives.
3.  **Execution Risk:** Sourcing a 5GB drive and building a reliable player in under a year is aggressive. Any delay pushes launch further into a period where competitors might solve the software/licensing bottleneck first.
4.  **No Alternative Considered:** The proposal ignores the "do nothing" or "license-first" approach. Building hardware without securing the music ecosystem is putting the cart before the horse. A cheaper, software-only solution or a partnership model should be explored before committing to hardware.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Music Ecosystem:** The core value (easy access to music) is missing. The music industry has not licensed downloads at scale. This is a factual blocker based on the proposal's own admission.
*   **Tiny Market:** The addressable market (small-share computer owners) is too small to support the R&D and sourcing costs for a $400 device, especially when cheaper alternatives exist.

**Risks:** Price sensitivity, component sourcing delays, brand damage if the product fails.

The absence of a legal music pipeline and the severely restricted compatibility make this venture highly likely to fail. The risks are not just potential; they are grounded in the current market realities described.

DECISION: NO-GO
