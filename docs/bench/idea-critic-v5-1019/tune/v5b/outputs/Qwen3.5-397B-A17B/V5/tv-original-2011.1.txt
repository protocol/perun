First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits a large share of the content budget to an unproven format (straight-to-series, two seasons) without the industry-standard de-risking mechanism (a pilot), relying solely on internal data that has never predicted success for original prestige drama.

Steelman: The service's unique viewing data reveals a specific, underserved demand that traditional pilot testing would miss, justifying a bold, differentiated release strategy to secure exclusive talent and market leadership immediately.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current plan:
1.  **Data Validity:** The viewing data reflects consumption of *licensed* content. There is zero evidence this data predicts willingness to pay for or engage with *original* scripted drama from this specific provider. The leap from "users watch friends" to "users will subscribe for this specific expensive drama" is unsupported.
2.  **Catastrophic Exposure:** Ordering two full seasons (26 episodes) upfront eliminates all off-ramps. If the first few episodes fail to resonate, the company is contractually bound to fund and release the remainder, burning the majority of its content budget on a potential failure with no chance to pivot.
3.  **Alternative Ignored:** The "do nothing" or "pilot first" options are superior. A single-season order or a traditional pilot reduces financial exposure by over 50% while still testing the core hypothesis (that original content drives subscriptions). The proposed "all-at-once" release is a distribution tactic, not a justification for doubling production risk.

The risks are not merely "uncertain"; they are existential given the budget concentration. The proposal treats unverified assumptions about data predictive power as facts. A blocker exists: the plan commits irreversible capital based on a mechanism (data predicting original hit potential) that has never been proven to work for this asset class.

Blockers: Commitment of >50% content budget to an unproven content type based on data with no established predictive validity for originals; no off-ramp after episode 1.
Risks: Talent mismatch, audience rejection of binge model, competitor response.

Kill criteria would require a validated correlation between current viewing patterns and original drama interest, plus a phased funding structure. As proposed, the financial risk outweighs the strategic benefit.

DECISION: NO-GO
