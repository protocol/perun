First call: NO-GO. The proposal bets the entire community on an unproven redesign while explicitly alienating the core user base that drives traffic.

**Steelman:** A complete rewrite with a publisher-focused algorithm and immediate cutover is the only way to reverse traffic decline and modernize the platform before a rival captures the market.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit:** The proposal contradicts the stated fact that the "core community values the existing voting mechanics." Changing this mechanic to favor publishers directly attacks the loyalty of the only asset the site has.
2.  **Options:** "Do nothing" preserves the current loyal base. A staged rollout or A/B test allows validation without total exposure. The proposal rejects these safer paths for a "big bang" launch.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume the launch failed. Why? The loyal community revolted against the new voting mechanics and the perceived bias toward publishers, migrating en masse to the growing rival site. Because the launch was "all users at once" with "no rollback," the damage is instantaneous and irreversible.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the risks: large engineering effort, no rollback, and alienating core users. It offers no pilot, no metric for success, and no fallback plan.
5.  **Blocker:** The combination of "move all users at once" and "no rollback" creates a single point of failure for the entire business. If the new mechanics fail (which is likely given community sentiment), the site cannot revert. This is a one-way door with a high probability of negative outcome based on the proposal's own facts.

**Blockers:**
- Irreversible deployment: "Move all users at once" with "no rollback" means any negative reaction destroys the product permanently.
- Contradictory strategy: Alienating the "loyal community" to chase traffic ignores that the community *is* the traffic source.

**Risks:**
- Engineering failure during rewrite.
- Rival captures users during the transition.

**Kill Criteria:** None proposed. A safe approach would require a capped pilot (e.g., 5% of users) with a strict retention metric before full rollout.

**Decision:** The proposal fails the basic operator test of preserving the core asset while testing change. It demands total commitment to an unverified hypothesis that contradicts known user values.

DECISION: NO-GO
