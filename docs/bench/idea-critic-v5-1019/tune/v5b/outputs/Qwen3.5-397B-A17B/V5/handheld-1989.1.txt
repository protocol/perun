First call: GO. The price point and battery life create a distinct category (portability vs. power) that the color rival ignores, targeting a mass market rather than enthusiasts.

**Steelman:** A sub-$90, long-life device with a killer app creates an uncontested market space, rendering superior hardware irrelevant for the core use case of portable gaming.

**Attack & Options:**
The monochrome screen and modest specs are vulnerabilities only if the goal is graphical fidelity. If the goal is accessibility and uptime, they are features. The "do nothing" option cedes the portable segment to the rival. A "high-end handheld" alternative fails on margin and battery, repeating the company's past single-game failures by targeting a niche too small to sustain the ecosystem. The proposed path beats "do nothing" by unlocking a new demographic; it beats "high-end" on unit volume potential.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
Fits a strategy of market expansion over spec wars. Failure scenario: Third-party developers ignore the platform due to perceived weakness, leaving the library thin. However, the bundled puzzle game mitigates this initially by providing immediate value. The cost of delay is high; the rival could solidify hold on the "portable" definition if not countered with a clearly differentiated alternative.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
The proposal's own facts support the thesis: the rival's strength (color/power) implies its weakness (battery/price). The $89.95 price is a psychological barrier breaker. The $20M marketing spend is the necessary fuel to define the category before the rival reacts.
*Blockers:* NONE. The risks (monochrome, modest hardware) are strategic trade-offs, not factual contradictions. The margin concern is valid but secondary to establishing the installed base required for software revenue.
*Risks:* Developer adoption, screen perception. These are managed via the bundle and marketing narrative, not by changing the hardware specs which would break the price/battery promise.

**Decision:**
The plan survives the attack because its "weaknesses" are the precise mechanisms for its price and battery advantages. No blocker exists in the stated facts.

DECISION: GO
