First call: GO. The proposal addresses a critical vulnerability: commodity pricing driven by buyer ignorance. If buyers cannot distinguish the component, they will not pay a premium for quality, forcing competition solely on price.

Steelman: By shifting demand pull to the end-user, the chip maker transforms its product from an invisible specification into a valued brand attribute, forcing computer makers to include the chip to sell their own units, thereby securing pricing power and shelf space.

Options analysis:
1. **Do nothing:** Continue competing on price and specifications alone; risk margin erosion as clones become indistinguishable.
2. **Trade promotions only:** Pay computer makers directly without consumer visibility; high risk of funds being absorbed as margin without driving specific demand.
3. **Proposed campaign:** Co-op advertising with branding. Highest upfront cost and channel friction, but the only path to creating distinct consumer demand that bypasses the computer maker's control.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of margin protection and market leadership. The strategy assumes consumers care about internal components, a risk mitigated by the simplicity of the "ask for this name" message.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if computer makers collectively refuse the co-op terms or if consumers find the technical distinction meaningless. However, the financial incentive (subsidized advertising) aligns maker interests despite potential resentment. The cost of delay is immediate exposure to clone undercutting.

Evidence: The proposal correctly identifies that "buyers do not know which chip is in their computer." This information asymmetry is the root cause of the commodity trap. The risks cited (unprecedented nature, budget share, channel resentment) are execution hurdles, not fundamental flaws. Unprecedented moves often yield monopoly rents; budget share is justified if it breaks the commodity cycle; channel resentment is manageable when the alternative is lost sales volume.

Blockers: NONE. No fact presented proves the plan fails as proposed. The risks are valid but do not constitute a blocker under the kill criteria; they are uncertainties to be managed, not reasons to abort.
Risks: Channel pushback, consumer apathy, high burn rate. These become kill criteria: if major makers boycott or sales lift is <5% after two quarters, pivot.

DECISION: GO
