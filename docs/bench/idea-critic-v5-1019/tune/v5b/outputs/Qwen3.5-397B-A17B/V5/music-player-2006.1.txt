First call: NO-GO. The proposal enters a saturated market late, dismantles the company's own established copy-protection standard (alienating existing label partners), and offers no price advantage against a dominant incumbent with high switching costs.

**Steelman:** A superior hardware interface and seamless wireless sharing will overcome ecosystem lock-in and convince labels to accept a new DRM-free or relaxed standard due to the company's marketing muscle.

**Attack:**
1.  **Strategic Contradiction:** Abandoning the copy-protection standard the company previously championed creates immediate friction with label partners who relied on that promise. Without explicit, pre-secured waivers from major labels, the store cannot launch legally.
2.  **Ecosystem Moat:** The "loyal ecosystem" of the leader is not just habit; it is likely tied to existing libraries and device integration. A "larger screen" and "wireless sharing" are incremental features, not category-defining shifts sufficient to trigger mass migration in 2006.
3.  **Late Market Entry:** Entering late requires a 10x advantage or a significantly lower price. The proposal admits "no clear price advantage." Wireless sharing is a high-risk feature likely to be blocked by the very labels needed for content.
4.  **Cost vs. Return:** A "large marketing budget" burned against an entrenched monopoly without a unique legal or economic wedge is capital destruction.

**Blockers:**
*   **Partner Revolt:** The proposal explicitly states the plan drops the copy-protection standard promoted to partners. Without evidence that labels have already agreed to this reversal, the content supply chain is broken.
*   **No Wedge:** There is no mechanism described to overcome the "enormous share" and "loyal ecosystem" other than marketing spend and minor hardware tweaks.

**Risks:** Label restrictions on sharing could neuter the primary differentiator; high customer acquisition costs in a mature market.

**Decision Logic:** The plan relies on partners accepting a reversal of a security standard they previously demanded, while offering no economic incentive for users to switch. This is a strategic contradiction, not a viable entry strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
