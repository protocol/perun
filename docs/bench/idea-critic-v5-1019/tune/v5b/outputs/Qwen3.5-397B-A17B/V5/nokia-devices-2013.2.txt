First call: NO-GO. Buying a shrinking handset division to solve an app-ecosystem deficit contradicts the core driver of platform success: developer momentum.

**Steelman:** Vertical integration is the only lever left to prevent the sole hardware partner from defecting to the rival, preserving the OS's remaining foothold.

**Attack:**
1.  **Wrong Cure:** The proposal admits the rival wins on "apps and developers." Hardware ownership does not create apps; it burdens the balance sheet. Developers follow users, not factory ownership. If sales are already "falling," acquiring the source of that decline accelerates losses rather than halting them.
2.  **Market Signal:** Acquiring a struggling partner signals desperation to other handset makers, likely pushing them faster toward the rival platform to avoid being next or dependent on a competitor's hardware division.
3.  **Integration Drag:** Integrating a large, declining workforce diverts critical management attention from the software ecosystem—the actual battleground—to low-margin hardware logistics.
4.  **Opportunity Cost:** €5.4 billion could subsidize developers, buy key apps, or fund a pivot. Instead, it purchases a liability with falling sales.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-acquisition, the OS share drops further. The acquired division requires massive restructuring costs. Competitors cite this move as proof the OS is a "walled garden" threat, solidifying their alliance with the rival. The app gap widens because the capital needed to attract developers was spent on factories.

**Blockers:**
*   **Strategic Mismatch:** The stated problem is an "app-ecosystem problem." The proposed solution is "hardware ownership." There is no causal link provided where owning the factory fixes the app shortage, while the cost directly reduces funds available to fix it.
*   **Momentum:** Buying a business with "falling sales" to stop a "market leader" violates basic turnaround logic unless the asset contains unique, irreplaceable IP not mentioned here.

**Risks:** Workforce integration, competitor backlash. These are secondary to the fundamental strategic error.

**Kill Criteria:** If the acquisition does not result in a net increase in active developers within 12 months, the thesis is false. However, the premise is so flawed that waiting for this metric is too expensive.

The plan trades scarce capital for a declining asset that does not solve the stated bottleneck.

DECISION: NO-GO
