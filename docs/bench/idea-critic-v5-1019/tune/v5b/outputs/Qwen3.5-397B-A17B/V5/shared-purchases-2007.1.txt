First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades long-term user trust for short-term ad data, risking a backlash that could destroy the platform's core value: social connection.

**Steelman:** Sharing purchase data with explicit consent and an opt-out creates a viral marketing loop while respecting user agency.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Mismatch:** Users join to connect with friends, not to broadcast consumption. "Sensitive purchases" (medical, gifts, adult items) create immediate social friction. An opt-out is often ignored or misunderstood; default-on sharing assumes rational actor behavior where emotional privacy concerns dominate.
2.  **Partner Risk:** Partners fear public embarrassment from negative comments on their products appearing in feeds. They may withdraw integration if the sentiment turns toxic, killing the data supply.
3.  **Irreversibility:** Once users feel violated, trust is lost permanently. The "hard to withdraw" risk is actually a certainty: rolling back feels like admitting guilt, cementing the narrative of invasion.
4.  **Alternatives:** Aggregated, anonymized data for advertisers achieves revenue goals without exposing individual behavior. Targeted ads based on private signals preserve the public feed's social sanctity.

**Pre-mortem:** Launch occurs. Users discover a sensitive purchase shared. Outrage spreads faster than the feature. Media labels it "creepy." Mass opt-outs follow, but the damage is done. Advertisers flee the controversy. The company is forced to kill the feature, admitting failure, with trust metrics permanently depressed.

**Blockers:**
*   **Core Contradiction:** The proposal's mechanism (publicizing private acts) directly undermines the product's primary asset (user trust/safety).
*   **Evidence of Failure Mode:** The "sensitive purchase" risk is not hypothetical; it is inherent to the data type. No kill criterion can fix a fundamental value mismatch before launch.

**Risks:** Partner withdrawal, media backlash, user churn. These are not merely risks; they are probable outcomes of the design.

**Kill Criteria:** If any single user complains about a sensitive item being shared, the feature fails its core safety test. The proposed "notification step" is insufficient mitigation for high-stakes privacy errors.

The cost of delay is zero compared to the cost of a trust collapse. Do not launch a feature where the failure mode is existential.

DECISION: NO-GO
