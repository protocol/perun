First call: NO-GO. The plan ignores stated risks of alienating loyal customers and triggering instant backlash by removing the brand's primary recognition symbol without testing.

Steelman: A simultaneous, unannounced rebrand maximizes shock value to signal a decisive break from the past, forcing immediate market attention.

Attack: The proposal fails the "do nothing" and "staged rollout" alternatives. A cheaper, safer path exists: test the new mark in limited channels or regions to validate customer reception before global commitment. The current plan treats a high-stakes, one-way door (erasing decades of equity) as a simple switch. The absence of customer testing is not a minor gap; given the explicit risk that "loyal customers are attached to the old logo," it is a fatal flaw. The "no advance announcement" strategy amplifies the risk of backlash by denying the brand a narrative to manage the transition, relying entirely on the design's inherent acceptance which is unproven.

Pre-mortem: The rollout triggers immediate social media outrage from core demographics who feel the brand identity was stolen. Sales dip as recognition fails in-store and online. The company is forced to revert or spend massively on re-education, having burned the element of surprise for no verified gain.

Blockers:
1. The proposal explicitly admits the new design "was not tested with customers" while acknowledging "loyal customers are attached to the old logo." Proceeding without validation contradicts the basic operational requirement to verify demand before destroying existing equity.
2. The "all channels at once" approach eliminates the ability to pivot based on early feedback, turning a manageable risk into a catastrophic potential failure.

Risks:
- Instant backlash on social media.
- Loss of brand recognition.
- Alienation of the core customer base.

The lack of a pilot, metric, or fallback converts these risks into blockers. A sound operator would demand a tested prototype and a phased rollout before approving such an irreversible change.

DECISION: NO-GO
