First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to commit to full-scale manufacturing and direct sales with no proven demand, no production history, and a price disadvantage against entrenched competitors who can easily replicate the mechanism.

**Steelman:** A superior bagless technology justifies a premium price and can disrupt the market if launched immediately by the inventor.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cost & Distribution:** Entering hardware manufacturing requires massive capital expenditure. Without an existing factory or supply chain, unit costs will be high, forcing the premium price that risks alienating buyers. Incumbents have economies of scale the inventor cannot match initially.
2.  **Replication Risk:** If the technology is viable, incumbents can engineer around patents or release a competing version using their established distribution networks, crushing a new entrant before it gains foothold.
3.  **Unproven Demand:** Buyers have never paid extra for "bagless." Assuming they will without a market test is speculative. The risk of total inventory obsolescence is extreme.
4.  **Execution Gap:** Moving from prototypes to mass production is a distinct competency. Thousands of prototypes do not equal a manufacturable product at scale.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Retain the invention; wait for a buyer.
*   **Licensing:** License the technology to an established manufacturer. This transfers production risk and leverages existing distribution, though it caps upside.
*   **Pilot:** Build a tiny batch to test price elasticity and manufacturing feasibility before founding a company.

**Pre-mortem:** The company launches, burns cash on tooling, fails to secure shelf space due to competitor pressure, and cannot sell enough units at the high price point to cover overhead. The inventor goes bankrupt; the technology is sold for pennies.

**Blockers:**
*   **No Manufacturing Record:** Founding a hardware company without production capability is a fatal operational gap.
*   **Price/Value Mismatch:** No evidence exists that customers will pay a premium for this specific benefit.
*   **Capital Exposure:** The proposal commits to full manufacturing before validating the business model.

**Risks:** Incumbent copying, retail rejection, cash flow collapse.

**Kill Criteria:** This plan lacks a pilot phase or licensing strategy to de-risk the massive capital commitment. Committing to found a company and manufacture immediately, given the lack of production history and unproven price tolerance, is reckless. The safer path is licensing or a minimal viable product test, neither of which is proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
