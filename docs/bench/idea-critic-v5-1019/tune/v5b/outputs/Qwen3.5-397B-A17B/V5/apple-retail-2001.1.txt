First call: NO-GO. A loss-making manufacturer with single-digit share entering a low-margin, high-fixed-cost channel during a recession contradicts basic survival logic.

Steelman: Direct retail is the only way to demonstrate the integrated music strategy and stop third-party staff from sabotaging sales, justifying the burn rate as a marketing expense rather than a profit center.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Preserve cash, accept continued channel friction, likely deepen losses.
2. Shop-in-shop kiosks: Lower lease risk, limited brand control, cheaper but weaker demo.
3. Proposed chain: Maximum control, massive fixed costs, highest bankruptcy risk if adoption lags.
The proposal must beat "do nothing" on survival odds; high fixed costs in a recession make this unlikely.

Fit: Aligns with a "digital hub" vision but assumes the company can survive the cash burn required to prove it. A sound idea that bankrupts the firm before traction is a failure.

Pre-mortem: Two years in, a mild recession deepens; mall traffic drops, leases remain due, inventory becomes obsolete, and resellers retaliate by dropping the brand entirely. The company runs out of cash before the store concept proves viable. This is a one-way door with extreme delay cost (immediate cash drain).

Evidence: The proposal admits "recent run of losses," "low margins," and "high lease costs." No evidence suggests the company has the balance sheet to sustain years of negative unit economics while waiting for a cultural shift. The "poor retail track record in the industry" is a stated fact, not a risk to be mitigated.

Blockers:
1. Financial mismatch: Loss-making entities cannot absorb high fixed costs in a cyclical downturn without a guaranteed immediate ROI, which is not evidenced.
2. Contradictory premises: Relying on a "new strategy" to justify stores while admitting the industry has a "poor retail track record" creates a fatal gap between hope and historical data.

Risks: Channel conflict, inventory obsolescence, recessionary pressure on discretionary spending.

The financial structure (losses + high fixed costs + recession) creates an immediate liquidity trap that no amount of brand demonstration can solve fast enough.

DECISION: NO-GO
