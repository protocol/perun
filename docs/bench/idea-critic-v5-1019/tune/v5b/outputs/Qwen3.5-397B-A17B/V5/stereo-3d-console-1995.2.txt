First call: NO-GO. The proposal sacrifices core usability and comfort for a gimmick that contradicts the company's strength in portable or immersive home experiences.

**Steelman:** A low-cost ($180) bridge product leveraging existing tech to capture market share between generations via unique stereoscopic 3D.

**Attack:**
1.  **Usability Blocker:** The requirement for an eyepiece negates "tabletop" social play, while red monochrome graphics severely limit game appeal compared to contemporaries.
2.  **Health Risk:** Documented potential for eye strain and headaches is a critical blocker for a mass-market gaming device, risking brand reputation.
3.  **Market Fit:** It fails the "portable" test of the handheld line and the "comfort" test of the home line, satisfying neither user base.
4.  **Content Gap:** "Few games at launch" guarantees poor adoption, creating a negative feedback loop for developers.
5.  **Cannibalization:** With a true next-gen home console due in two years, this product risks confusing consumers and diluting resources without offering a compelling long-term value proposition.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserve brand equity and focus resources on the upcoming home console and true handheld iterations.
*   *Delay:* Wait for color stereoscopic tech or true portable 3D solutions.
*   *Software Bridge:* Release enhanced games for existing handhelds rather than new hardware.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, users experience headaches within 30 minutes, sales stall due to poor word-of-mouth, and the company is forced to discount heavily, damaging the premium brand image before the next generation arrives.

**Blockers:**
*   Physical discomfort (eyepiece/strain) contradicts core gaming requirements.
*   Red-only visuals are inferior to standard market offerings.
*   Lack of software support at launch.

**Risks:** Market confusion, brand dilution, financial loss on hardware subsidies.

The stated risks are not merely potential; they are inherent design flaws that prevent the product from succeeding as a mainstream gaming device. The "bridge" strategy fails because the bridge leads nowhere desirable for the user.

DECISION: NO-GO
