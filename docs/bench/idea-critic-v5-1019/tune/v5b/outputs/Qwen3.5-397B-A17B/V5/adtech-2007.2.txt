First call: NO-GO. The $6 billion cash price for a fast-moving technology asset, paid at a large premium without verified synergies or retention guarantees, presents an immediate capital destruction risk that outweighs the strategic urgency.

Steelman: Acquiring the platform is the only viable path to rapidly scale display capabilities and prevent the search leader from monopolizing the ad-serving infrastructure, thereby securing the company's long-term revenue diversification.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain cash but cede the display market to the competitor, risking long-term irrelevance in non-search ads.
2. Build internally: Lower cost and no conflict, but too slow to catch the competitor's imminent move.
3. Partner/licensing: Cheaper and reversible, but fails to secure the proprietary technology needed to compete directly.

Fit: Aligns with the goal to compete with the search leader, but the "cash-only" structure and "large premium" contradict prudent capital allocation in a volatile tech sector.

Pre-mortem: The acquisition fails because key engineering talent leaves post-merger due to culture clash, the technology becomes obsolete within 18 months due to market shifts, and existing agency clients defect to competitors due to perceived conflicts of interest, leaving the buyer with a $6 billion stranded asset.

Evidence: The proposal admits to a "fast-changing market" and "conflicts with existing customers" but offers no mitigation. The price is a stated fact ($6B cash), representing a massive, irreversible outlay for an unproven integration.

Blockers:
1. Capital Misallocation: Paying a "large premium" in all-cash for a technology asset in a "fast-changing market" creates an immediate negative expected value if the technology shifts or talent flees. The proposal offers no earn-out or retention mechanism to de-risk this.
2. Channel Conflict: The stated "conflicts with existing agency customers" threatens the core business revenue stream without a defined separation strategy.

Risks: Technology obsolescence, integration failure, regulatory scrutiny.

The lack of structural protections (e.g., earn-outs, retention bonuses, firewalls for agencies) against the admitted risks makes the deal financially reckless as proposed.

DECISION: NO-GO
