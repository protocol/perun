First call: NO-GO. The proposal demands full-price data plans for price-sensitive youth while stripping the device of apps and games, the primary drivers of smartphone adoption for that demographic.

Steelman: Launching a locked-down, carrier-exclusive social phone leverages an acquired team's expertise to capture the youth market quickly before the main platform arrives.

Attack: The value proposition is inverted. Asking cost-conscious users to pay a premium for a "dumber" phone (no app store, no games) contradicts basic market logic. The data plan cost equals a full smartphone, yet the device offers a fraction of the utility. Competitors will offer full smartphones at similar price points. The internal conflict history suggests execution risk, but the product-market fit failure is immediate and fatal.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve capital; avoid launching a product likely to fail.
2. Delay until main platform launch: Unify efforts, reduce internal friction, and offer a competitive device.
3. Cheap feature phone: If price is the constraint, drop the data plan requirement rather than inflating the cost.

Fit: This does not move the north star of platform adoption; it risks brand damage by associating the company with an overpriced, under-delivering product.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because teens refuse to pay smartphone rates for a restricted experience. Sales are negligible; the carrier drops support; the acquired team is disbanded.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the target is price-sensitive and the cost is high. It admits there are no apps or games. These facts alone contradict the strategy.

Blockers:
1. Economic mismatch: Price-sensitive users cannot/will not pay smartphone data rates for a non-smartphone experience.
2. Feature deficit: No app store or games removes the primary incentive for youth to adopt a new phone OS.

Risks: Internal conflict, timing clash with main platform. These are secondary to the fundamental product flaw.

Kill criteria: If early carrier feedback indicates resistance to the price-to-feature ratio, halt immediately. However, the current facts already trigger a stop.

DECISION: NO-GO
