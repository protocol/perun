First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a costly nationwide launch based solely on test market data that the proposal itself admits may overstate demand, while ignoring the high probability of non-repeat purchases and flagship cannibalization.

Steelman: A clear cola can capture a specific "purity" niche if the novelty sustains beyond the initial trial, justifying the marketing spend despite cannibalization risks.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Preserve flagship margins; risk appearing stagnant.
2. **Limited regional rollout:** Test repeatability and cannibalization in a controlled zone before national spend.
3. **Proposed national launch:** High cost, high risk of fleeting novelty, potential brand dilution.

Fit: Assuming the goal is sustainable growth, not just headline-grabbing novelty, a full launch misaligns with the stated risk of non-repeat buyers.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because the "purity" narrative does not overcome the consumer expectation that cola must be dark. Novelty buyers try it once, find the taste lacking or the experience strange, and do not return. Meanwhile, loyal flagship drinkers switch to the clear version, reducing overall margin if the new product is priced similarly but costs more to market. Rivals copy the gimmick, fragmenting the niche further. The massive marketing spend yields a short-term spike followed by a steep cliff, leaving the brand with a weakened flagship and a failed extension.

Evidence: The proposal's own facts cite "novelty buyers may not repeat" and "test markets can overstate demand." These are not hypotheticals; they are admitted weaknesses in the current data set. Proceeding nationally treats uncertain test data as proven fact.

Blockers:
1. **Unproven Repeat Purchase:** The core business case relies on retention, yet the proposal explicitly flags that buyers may not repeat. Without data proving retention beyond the initial novelty, the ROI calculation is invalid.
2. **Cannibalization without Net Gain:** If the clear cola merely shifts existing volume from the flagship while incurring higher marketing costs, the net result is value destruction.

Risks: Flavor-color association mismatch; rapid competitor copying.

The plan fails the kill check: The risk of non-repeat purchases concerns the core claim (that buyers will sustain the brand), the proposal's own facts support this likelihood, and the commitment (national launch) is too large to stop once seen.

DECISION: NO-GO
