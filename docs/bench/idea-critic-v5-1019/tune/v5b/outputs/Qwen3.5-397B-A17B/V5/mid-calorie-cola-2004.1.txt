First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to split the market with a product that likely satisfies neither core segment, while charging a premium for a potentially fleeting trend.

**Steelman:** A mid-calorie option captures health-conscious young men who reject the taste of diet cola but fear full-sugar carbs, creating a new category before rivals do.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Demand:** The core conflict is unresolved. If low-carb dieters avoid sugar, they choose diet (zero). If taste is paramount, they choose regular (full). A "half-sugar" product risks being the worst of both: too many carbs for dieters, artificial aftertaste for traditionalists. The proposal admits this risk but offers no evidence that a specific demographic exists to bridge it.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows the diet segment to grow naturally without cannibalizing high-margin regular sales. A cheaper alternative is reformulating existing lines or limited-time tests rather than a full "large campaign" launch.
3.  **Evidence & Trends:** The low-carb craze (Atkins era) is volatile. Basing a permanent SKU and premium pricing on a potential fad is dangerous. The rival's similar move suggests a land-grab, not validated demand; following them into a crowded, unproven niche increases failure probability.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 12 months. Why? The product sat on shelves because dieters didn't trust the carb count, and regular drinkers hated the taste. The premium price prevented trial, and the trend collapsed, leaving inventory write-offs.
5.  **Blocker:** The proposal states as a fact that "people who want fewer calories already buy the diet version." This directly contradicts the core assumption that these same people (or enough others) will buy a mid-calorie version. Without proof of a distinct segment that *refuses* diet but *fears* full sugar, the unit economics cannot work, especially at a premium price point during a possible trend crash.

**Decision:** The risks are not merely executional; they strike at the fundamental value proposition. The "half-way" solution solves no urgent pain point better than existing options.

Blockers: Contradictory consumer behavior (dieters already have a solution); unproven segment size; high risk of trend collapse.
Risks: Cannibalization; price resistance; competitor saturation.

DECISION: NO-GO
