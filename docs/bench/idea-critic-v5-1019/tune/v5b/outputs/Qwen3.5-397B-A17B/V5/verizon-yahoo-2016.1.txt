First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a scale deficit by acquiring a second declining asset, compounding execution risk without addressing the core lack of carrier expertise in consumer media.

**Steelman:** Combining two struggling legacy portals creates sufficient scale to compete with dominant ad platforms by leveraging the carrier's distribution.
**Attack:** This assumes scale alone drives competitiveness in a market defined by data superiority and product innovation, not just inventory volume. The acquirer has no history in consumer media, yet plans to integrate a target with falling traffic, recurring management instability, and shrinking revenue. This is not a turnaround; it is a merger of two weakening positions.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve capital; avoid integrating a culturally unstable, declining business into an inexperienced organization.
2.  **Build/Buy Expertise:** Acquire a smaller, growing mobile-first tech firm or hire leadership with proven ad-tech success before attempting large-scale M&A.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Spend $4.8B on a declining asset. High cost, extreme integration risk, low reversibility.

**Fit:** The strategy aims for a "mobile media business," but the tactic (buying a dying portal) contradicts the north star of competing with dominant, innovative platforms. A sound idea must move toward growth, not arrest decay through consolidation.

**Pre-mortem:** The integration fails because the carrier's culture cannot absorb the target's instability. Ad revenue continues to slide as key talent departs post-acquisition. The combined entity remains too small to challenge the duopoly, resulting in a massive write-down. This is a one-way door; the $4.8B is sunk with no exit.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the target's traffic and revenue are falling, management is unstable, and the buyer lacks relevant experience. These are not risks to be managed; they are factual blockers indicating the core claim (that this combination will succeed) is unsupported by the stated premises.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Incompetence Gap:** The buyer has zero history in consumer media, yet the plan requires mastering complex integration of a distressed asset.
2.  **Negative Momentum:** Acquiring a business with multi-year declines in traffic and revenue contradicts the goal of building a scaled, competitive future business.
3.  **False Premise:** Scale via consolidation of legacy assets does not equal competitiveness against data-driven dominators.

**Risks:** Further revenue decline, culture clash, talent flight.

The plan fails the basic operator test: do not spend billions to acquire problems you lack the skills to fix.

DECISION: NO-GO
