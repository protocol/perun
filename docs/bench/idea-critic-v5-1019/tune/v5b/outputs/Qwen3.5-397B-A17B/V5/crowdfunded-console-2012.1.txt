First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on unproven developer adoption and ignores the capital intensity of hardware, creating a fatal mismatch between the "low-cost" constraint and the reality of manufacturing.

Steelman: A niche, open-source console can succeed by targeting underserved hobbyists rather than competing directly with major studios, using crowdfunding to validate demand before mass production.

However, the attack reveals critical flaws. First, the "chicken-and-egg" problem is unresolved: without established developers, the platform has no content; without content, developers will not build. Crowdfunding backs ideas, not empty ecosystems. Second, the timeline is delusional. Shipping hardware within a year of a campaign ignores supply chain realities, certification processes, and the high probability of component shortages or quality control failures, which typically delay first-run hardware by 6–18 months. Third, the "low-cost" constraint contradicts the need for competitive performance against 2012-era smartphones and consoles; cutting costs here likely results in a device too weak to run modern games, rendering it obsolete at launch.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids capital loss but misses the niche opportunity.
2. Software-first approach: Build the OS/emulator for existing PC/mobile hardware to grow the developer community before committing to silicon. This is cheaper, reversible, and validates the core value proposition.
3. Partner with an existing OEM: License the OS rather than building custom hardware, reducing risk and capex.

Pre-mortem: The campaign succeeds, raising $2M. Six months later, the team cannot source chips at the target price point. Delays stretch to 18 months; backers demand refunds. The brand is tainted by failure before public launch.

Blockers:
- The one-year shipping timeline is incompatible with hardware development cycles, making the core promise impossible to keep.
- No strategy exists to secure game libraries, which is the primary driver of console adoption.

Risks:
- Market saturation by mobile gaming.
- Insufficient marketing budget to reach the niche audience.

The proposal fails because it treats hardware manufacturing as a simple extension of software development, ignoring the specific operational blockers of supply chain and ecosystem building. The lack of a software-first validation step is a fatal omission.

DECISION: NO-GO
