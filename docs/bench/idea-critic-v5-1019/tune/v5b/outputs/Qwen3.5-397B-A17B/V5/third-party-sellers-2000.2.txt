First call: GO. The proposal transforms a capital-intensive inventory model into an asset-light marketplace, directly addressing the core constraint of non-profitability by monetizing traffic without bearing inventory risk.

Steelman: Aggregating all offers (new/used, internal/external) on a single page maximizes selection and liquidity, creating a "everything store" experience that draws more customers than a limited first-party catalog, while generating high-margin fee revenue.

Options analysis:
1. Do nothing: Remain unprofitable with limited selection; high risk of being outpaced by broader competitors.
2. First-party only: Safe but capital-constrained; cannot scale selection without deepening losses.
3. Marketplace (Proposal): Unlocks infinite selection and immediate fee revenue; risks price competition and quality control are manageable trade-offs for survival and growth.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of becoming the primary destination for commerce by prioritizing selection and customer choice over protecting marginal first-party sales.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if external seller fraud destroys trust or if suppliers boycott. However, the "fee per sale" model ensures revenue even if first-party sales dip due to undercutting. The cost of delay is continued cash burn and limited catalog growth.

Evidence & Blockers: The proposal admits risks (undercutting, quality, cannibalization) but these are operational challenges, not fundamental flaws. Cannibalization is acceptable if the fee revenue exceeds the lost margin on first-party sales. Supplier objection is a risk, but the value of access to the retailer's customer base may outweigh it. No factual blocker exists in the text; the math of "fee revenue + expanded selection" vs. "inventory cost" favors the shift for an unprofitable entity.

Kill Criteria: If external seller defect rates exceed internal benchmarks by >20% within 6 months, or if net revenue per page (fees + first-party margin) declines vs. baseline.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited are inherent to the model but do not invalidate the strategic necessity of shifting to a marketplace to achieve profitability.
Risks: Supplier retaliation, quality variance, brand dilution. These require monitoring, not rejection.

DECISION: GO
