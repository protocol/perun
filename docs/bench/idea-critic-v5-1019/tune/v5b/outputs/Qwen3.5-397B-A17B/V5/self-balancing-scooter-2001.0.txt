First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to massive fixed costs (40k unit factory) and a high price point before resolving fundamental market and regulatory viability.

**Steelman:** A breakthrough personal transporter with strong backing can create a new urban mobility category if launched decisively to capture first-mover advantage.

**Attack:**
1.  **Regulatory Blocker:** The legal status is "unclear" for sidewalks and roads. Deploying 40,000 units without defined legality invites immediate bans, confiscations, or liability suits that could kill the product post-launch. You cannot scale a vehicle where it is illegal to drive.
2.  **Market Mismatch:** At $5,000, the price is prohibitive for mass adoption, yet the use cases beyond "institutional buyers" are unproven. A factory sized for 40,000 units assumes mass consumer demand that contradicts the high price and limited utility (low cargo, conspicuousness).
3.  **Better Way:** A staged launch targeting verified institutional buyers (security, tourism, large campuses) with a smaller production run validates demand and legal frameworks without the catastrophic risk of an oversized factory. This reduces capital exposure while solving the "unproven use" risk.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: Cities ban the device due to safety/legal ambiguity; inventory piles up as the niche institutional market saturates quickly, unable to absorb 40k units; the high price prevents pivot to consumers.

**Blockers:**
*   **Regulatory Void:** Operating a mass-market vehicle with undefined legal status is a factual failure condition for the proposed scale.
*   **Capacity vs. Demand:** Building for 40k units contradicts the admitted lack of proven consumer use cases and the high price barrier.

**Risks:** Social stigma ("conspicuous"), limited utility. These are secondary to the regulatory and capacity mismatches.

**Decision Logic:** The proposal demands a "one-way door" decision (huge factory capex) based on unproven assumptions and unresolved legal threats. The "do nothing" or "pilot" alternative offers the same upside potential (validating the tech) with drastically lower ruin risk. The plan fails because it scales before validating the two most critical constraints: law and market fit.

DECISION: NO-GO
