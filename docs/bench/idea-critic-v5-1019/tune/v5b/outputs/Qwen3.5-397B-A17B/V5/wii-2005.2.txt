First call: GO. The market leader and runner-up are fighting a specs war for hardcore gamers, leaving the casual mass market underserved and price-sensitive.

**Steelman:** By abandoning the losing battle for graphical fidelity and targeting non-gamers with an intuitive, low-cost motion interface, the console can create a blue ocean, achieving volume through hardware penetration rather than per-unit margin.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Continue chasing leaders in HD power; likely remain a distant third with shrinking margins.
2.  **High-Def Pivot:** Attempt to match rivals; requires massive R&D, higher price, and still leaves us third in line for developer attention.
3.  **Proposed Strategy:** Lower hardware barrier, unique input, family focus. Highest risk of developer skepticism but only path to market expansion.

**Fit:** This ignores the stated "north star" of traditional console wars (graphics/power) to redefine the market stage entirely. It fits a volume-over-margin strategy.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if third-party publishers refuse to support the unusual architecture, leaving a software drought, or if the "toy" perception prevents adoption by the very casual audience targeted. However, the cost of delay is catastrophic; waiting for HD maturity cements the current hierarchy.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
*   *Blockers:* NONE. The proposal acknowledges the risks (skepticism, no HD, thin margins) but these are strategic bets, not factual contradictions. The math works if volume compensates for thin margins.
*   *Risks:* Third-party abandonment, "toy" stigma, untested demand. These are mitigated by first-party software launches and the sheer price differential.

**Kill Criteria:**
If hardware sales do not reach 5 million units within 12 months, or if third-party title count remains below 20 significant releases by launch +6 months, pivot to a hybrid model or cease hardware production. Leading metric: Weekly attach rate of motion controllers to software sales.

The proposal correctly identifies that winning the current game is impossible; the only move is to change the game. The risks are real but manageable compared to the certainty of losing a specs war.

DECISION: GO
