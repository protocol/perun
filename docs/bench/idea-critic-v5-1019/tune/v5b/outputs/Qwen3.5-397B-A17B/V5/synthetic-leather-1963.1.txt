First call: NO-GO. The proposal's own facts state the synthetic costs more than leather while offering unproven comfort and facing high switching costs for manufacturers.

Steelman: Launching now captures early market share before fashion shifts, leveraging durability to justify a premium price despite higher unit costs.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids capital loss but cedes potential innovation leadership.
2. Target niche (e.g., rain boots): Lower switching costs, tests durability claim without premium pricing pressure.
3. Subsidize factory retooling: Increases upfront burn to overcome manufacturer resistance.

Fit: Assumes "premium" aligns with 1963 consumer desire for synthetics over natural leather; this is unverified. Fashion volatility is a stated risk that directly threatens the "premium" positioning.

Pre-mortem: The product fails because shoe makers refuse to retool for a material that costs more and feels worse, leaving the chemical company with unsold inventory as fashion trends revert to natural leather. This is a one-way door with high sunk costs in marketing and production scaling.

Evidence: The proposal explicitly admits the synthetic costs more than leather, wearers may find it less comfortable, and manufacturers are unwilling to change factories. These are not risks to be mitigated; they are current, stated facts that contradict the core claim of viable market entry. The "durability" advantage does not offset higher cost and lower comfort in a premium fashion segment where aesthetics and feel drive purchases.

Blockers:
1. Unit economics: Higher cost than the incumbent (leather) with no confirmed willingness to pay a premium.
2. Adoption barrier: Manufacturers explicitly unwilling to retool; no incentive provided in the plan.
3. Product-market fit: Potential comfort deficit contradicts "premium" positioning.

Risks: Fashion trend reversal, execution difficulty in marketing.

The plan commits to heavy marketing spend and a full launch despite three fundamental flaws cited as facts within the proposal itself. A cheaper, safer alternative is to pilot in a non-fashion, high-durability niche (e.g., industrial workwear) to prove comfort and demand before targeting premium shoes. The current plan bets the farm on overcoming cost, comfort, and inertia simultaneously with no evidence of demand.

DECISION: NO-GO
