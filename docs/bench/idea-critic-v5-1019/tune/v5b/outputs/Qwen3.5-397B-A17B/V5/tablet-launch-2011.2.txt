First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a premium price for an unproven product with a tiny app catalog in a market dominated by a single incumbent, while the company faces leadership instability.

Steelman: Leveraging the acquired OS creates a unique ecosystem, and heavy marketing can overcome initial app scarcity if the hardware quality is exceptional.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High risk. Premium pricing requires parity or superiority in apps/ecosystem, which is explicitly absent. Leadership transition distracts from the intense focus needed for launch.
2. **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital. Wait for OS maturity or leadership stabilization. Risk: Missing the window, but avoids immediate failure.
3. **Low-Cost Entry:** Launch at a significantly lower price point to incentivize adoption despite the small catalog. Risk: Brand dilution, but builds user base.

Fit: The strategy assumes users value the OS over the app ecosystem. In 2011, tablet utility is almost entirely app-dependent. A small catalog is a fatal flaw at a premium price.

Pre-mortem: The tablet launches, reviewers praise the hardware but pan the lack of apps. Consumers, confused by the leadership change and seeing no reason to switch from the dominant leader, reject the high price. Inventory piles up; the OS division is sold off.

Evidence: The proposal admits the app catalog is a "small fraction" of the leader's and the OS has "almost no market share." History shows network effects dominate platform markets; users do not pay premium prices for empty ecosystems.

Blockers:
1. **Price/App Mismatch:** Pricing "in line with the market-leading tablet" while offering a "small app catalog" contradicts basic value perception in platform markets. Users pay for apps, not just hardware.
2. **Leadership Distraction:** Launching a complex new category during "leadership transition" invites execution failure when precision is required.

Risks: Market dominance of competitor, unproven shopper sentiment. These are severe, but the pricing strategy turns them into blockers.

Kill Criteria: If the app count does not reach 50% of the leader's within 6 months, pivot to a budget device or abandon the hardware line.

DECISION: NO-GO
