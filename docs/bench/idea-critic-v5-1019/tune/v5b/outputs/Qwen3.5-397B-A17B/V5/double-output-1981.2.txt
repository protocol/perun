First call: NO-GO. Doubling production while sales lag by 60% during a recession with rising interest rates destroys cash without creating demand.

**Steelman:** The claim is that flooding the channel now secures future market share or forces sales through volume, assuming the recession is brief and dealers can absorb inventory.

**Attack:**
1.  **Cash Burn:** Producing 4,500 additional unsold units ties up massive capital in raw materials and labor. With high interest rates, the cost of carrying this inventory skyrockets. The company has "limited cash"; this move accelerates insolvency.
2.  **Demand Mismatch:** The core constraint is demand, not supply. A recession reduces consumer purchasing power; high interest rates make car loans expensive. More cars on lots do not create buyers.
3.  **Channel Capacity:** Dealers are already struggling to finance existing stock. Forcing more inventory onto them risks breaking dealer relationships and their financial stability, collapsing the distribution network.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves cash. Reducing production aligns costs with reality. A cheaper alternative is incentivizing sales of existing stock, not building new units.

**Pre-mortem:** The company runs out of cash in six months. Dealers reject delivery of new units. The firm enters bankruptcy with a warehouse full of depreciating assets and no liquidity to restructure.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** Sales (3,000) are less than half of production (7,500).
*   **Fact:** Recession and high rates suppress demand and increase financing costs.
*   **Fact:** Limited cash reserves cannot support doubled production costs without immediate revenue, which is absent.
*   **Contradiction:** The proposal increases the exact liability (inventory) that is already causing strain, while ignoring the stated lack of cash and demand.

**Risks:** Dealer bankruptcy, total liquidity crisis, brand collapse.

**Kill Criteria:** If cash reserves cannot cover 3 months of doubled production costs without new sales, the plan fails immediately. The leading metric is "cash burn rate per unsold unit."

This plan ignores the fundamental economic reality presented: a demand-side crisis treated with a supply-side solution. It guarantees failure by exhausting the only resource (cash) needed to survive the downturn.

DECISION: NO-GO
