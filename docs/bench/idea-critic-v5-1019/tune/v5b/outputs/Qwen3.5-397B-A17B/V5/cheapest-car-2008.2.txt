First call: GO. The arithmetic of moving millions from two wheels to four at a price point previously thought impossible creates a category of one, provided the unit economics hold.

**Steelman:** A dedicated factory and ruthless value engineering can deliver a safe, compliant vehicle at $2,500, unlocking a massive latent market of scooter families who cannot afford used cars, making volume the only path to profitability.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Cedes the "first cheap car" narrative and volume advantage to competitors; leaves the target demographic trapped in unsafe two-wheelers.
*   **Used Car Market:** Unreliable, high maintenance, and lacks the status of "new." Cannot guarantee safety standards at scale.
*   **Scooters:** Unsafe for families, weather-exposed, and limited cargo capacity. The upgrade desire is proven; only price is the barrier.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This aligns perfectly with the vision of motorizing the masses. If this fails, it will be because land protests delay the factory (killing volume targets) or because "cheapest" is misinterpreted as "unsafe" rather than "efficient." The cost of delay is high; first-mover advantage in this price bracket is absolute.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal cites specific risks (land, safety perception, alternatives). None are factual blockers *yet*; they are execution risks.
*   *Land Protests:* A logistical hurdle, not a product flaw. Mitigate via government negotiation or site diversification.
*   *Safety/Quality:* Addressed by meeting regulatory minimums, not exceeding them. The kill criterion is regulatory approval, not luxury standards.
*   *Branding:* "Cheapest" can be reframed as "Most Accessible." The market for $2,500 new cars is currently zero; creating it outweighs stigma.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks are manageable execution challenges, not fundamental flaws in the thesis. The alternative (waiting for perfect conditions) guarantees missing the window.

**Kill Criteria:** If regulatory safety certification is denied, or if land acquisition delays push launch back by more than 18 months, halt. Monitor monthly production ramp-up vs. target; if <50% of target by Month 6, pivot pricing or features.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The claim that "buyers won't want the cheapest" was tested against the reality that the target audience currently owns *nothing* comparable. The desire for family safety and status overrides the stigma of low price when no other new option exists.

DECISION: GO
