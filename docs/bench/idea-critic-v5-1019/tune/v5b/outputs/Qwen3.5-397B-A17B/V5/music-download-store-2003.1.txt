First call: GO. The company leverages its dominant hardware footprint to solve a critical ecosystem threat (piracy) that devalues its core product.

Steelman: A legal, frictionless, per-song store creates the only viable alternative to theft, securing the hardware business even if software margins are thin.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited are execution challenges, not factual contradictions.
**Risks:** 
1. **Free alternatives:** Mitigated by offering superior convenience and integration.
2. **Label withdrawal:** Addressed by securing licenses *before* launch; labels need revenue more than they hate digital.
3. **Thin margins:** Accepted as a loss-leader strategy to protect high-margin hardware sales.
4. **Platform lock-in:** Initially a feature, not a bug, to drive hardware adoption.
5. **Subscription rivalry:** Unproven model in 2003; users prefer ownership.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the interface is clunky or if labels demand prohibitive fees. However, the company's market leverage makes label cooperation likely. The cost of delay is catastrophic: continued piracy erodes the perceived value of the music player itself.

**Evidence:** The proposal relies on the stated fact that the player is "selling well." This existing user base provides immediate distribution. The 99-cent price point is a psychological anchor against "free," offering a legal middle ground.

**Kill Criteria:** If major labels refuse licensing terms within 6 months, or if initial attach rates (songs per user) remain below 1.0 after launch, the model fails.

**Decision Logic:** The alternative ("do nothing") guarantees the erosion of the hardware business by piracy. The proposed path monetizes the ecosystem, locks in users, and forces the industry to standardize around a paid model. While margins are tight, the strategic necessity of saving the hardware franchise outweighs the software risk. The "subscription" alternative is speculative; the per-song model matches current consumer behavior.

DECISION: GO
