First call: GO. The proposal secures the franchise's future by transitioning customers to an inevitable digital model before competitors force obsolescence.

**Steelman:** Launching a free, limited streaming service now leverages the existing subscriber base to gather critical usage data and build a digital habit, creating a defensive moat against incoming tech giants while the high-margin DVD business funds the transition.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maximizes short-term profit but guarantees extinction as broadband普及s and studios favor new digital entrants.
2.  **Paid standalone streaming:** Fails due to low content value and high friction; adoption would be too slow to matter.
3.  **Proposed (Free add-on):** Lowest friction adoption, immediate data generation, and perceived value increase without immediate revenue risk.

**Fit:** Aligns with the long-term vision of home entertainment delivery, even if it temporarily dilutes the core DVD metric. The north star is "viewing hours delivered," not "discs shipped."

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if licensing costs spiral before user engagement proves value, or if the poor quality drives users to pirates instead of retaining them. However, the cost of delay is total market irrelevance; waiting for perfect quality or universal broadband cedes the customer relationship to Netflix's future competitors.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal admits high costs and small catalogs. These are known constraints, not hidden blockers. The "cannibalization" risk is actually a feature: it is better to cannibalize your own profitable business than have a competitor do it for you. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal; the risks are strategic bets, not operational impossibilities.

**Kill Criteria:** If streaming costs exceed 15% of DVD profits within 18 months without a corresponding increase in total subscriber retention, pause expansion. Leading metric: Percentage of subscribers activating streaming monthly.

The absence of a perfect catalog or universal broadband is not a reason to wait; it is the reason to start now while the DVD cash cow still funds the experiment.

DECISION: GO
