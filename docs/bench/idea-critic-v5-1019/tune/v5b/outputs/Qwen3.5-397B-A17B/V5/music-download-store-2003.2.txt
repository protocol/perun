First call: GO. The company leverages its dominant hardware position to solve the industry's existential threat: chaos. Free sharing offers no reliability; subscriptions in 2003 face bandwidth and catalog fragmentation. A unified, licensed store legitimizes the market.

**Steelman:** A curated, legal ecosystem converts pirates into paying customers by offering superior convenience and reliability, securing hardware dominance through exclusive software integration.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Cede the music experience to fragmented, illegal networks; hardware value erodes as users flock to incompatible PC solutions.
*   **Subscription Model:** Premature for 2003 dial-up/early broadband limits; consumers prefer ownership.
*   **The Risk:** 99 cents yields thin margins. **Rebuttal:** The store is not the profit center; it is the catalyst for high-margin hardware sales. The "loss" is a marketing subsidy.
*   **The Constraint:** Initially Mac-only. **Rebuttal:** This creates a "halo effect," driving computer sales first. Windows expansion follows once the model proves viable.
*   **Label Risk:** Labels may withdraw. **Rebuttal:** They are desperate for revenue and control; they have no better alternative than this structured pipeline.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the interface is clunky or the catalog sparse. However, the proposal mandates licensing from *all* major labels, solving catalog depth. The simplicity of per-song ownership beats the complexity of early streaming.

**Evidence & Logic:** The core claim rests on users valuing convenience over "free." History shows friction kills piracy when a legal alternative is seamless. The margin argument is a category error; the goal is ecosystem lock-in, not per-track profit. The "rivals" backing subscriptions are betting on infrastructure that does not yet exist at scale.

**Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented proves the model cannot work; the risks are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. The thin margin is a feature, not a bug, designed to move metal.
**Risks:** Slow adoption, label renegotiation. These are managed via the hardware tie-in and first-mover advantage.

**Kill Criteria:** If major labels refuse licensing within 6 months, or if attach rates (songs per device) remain below 10% after one year, pivot to a subscription model or wholesale licensing.

DECISION: GO
