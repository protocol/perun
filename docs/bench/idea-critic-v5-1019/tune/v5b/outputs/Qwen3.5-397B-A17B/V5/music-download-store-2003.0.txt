First call: GO. The company leverages its dominant hardware position to solve the industry's existential piracy problem with a legal, convenient alternative.

**Steelman:** A closed ecosystem selling low-margin, protected singles creates the only viable legal path for mass-market digital music, securing hardware sales while appeasing labels.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Skeptic:** Margins are thin, and platform exclusivity limits reach. However, the goal is not store profit but hardware differentiation. Without this, hardware becomes a vessel for stolen content, eroding brand value.
*   **Better-way:** Subscriptions were the rival path, but 2003 bandwidth and catalog licensing made them impractical for mass adoption. "Do nothing" cedes the market to piracy. Per-song sales match consumer psychology better than rentals.
*   **Fit:** Aligns perfectly with a hardware-first strategy. The store is a feature, not a standalone business.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if labels withdraw licenses or users reject the price. Yet, labels have no better alternative to recover revenue; 99 cents is the psychological anchor for a "single."
*   **Evidence:** The proposal relies on the fact that current piracy offers poor user experience (searching, viruses) versus instant, reliable access.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. No factual contradiction exists in the proposal. The margin concern is a feature of the strategy (loss leader), not a flaw.
*   **Risks:** Label rebellion, competitor subscriptions, platform lock-in. These are manageable execution risks, not fundamental flaws. The "platform lock-in" is actually the strategic moat.

**Kill Criteria:** If major labels refuse licensing within 6 months, or if attach rate (songs per device) remains below 10% after one year, pivot to negotiation or subscription models.

**Decision:** The proposal correctly identifies that convenience beats free when the free option is broken. It monetizes the hardware ecosystem rather than the content directly. The risks are inherent to the market, but the proposed path is the only one that aligns hardware strength with label needs.

DECISION: GO
