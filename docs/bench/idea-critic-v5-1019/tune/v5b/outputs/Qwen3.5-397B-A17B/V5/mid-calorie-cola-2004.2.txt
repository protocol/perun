First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a shrinking demographic with a product that satisfies neither core need (taste vs. calorie reduction) at a higher price point during a potential fad.

**Steelman:** A mid-calorie option captures young men wanting taste without the "diet" stigma, leveraging a premium trend before it fades.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Demand:** The low-carb trend is explicitly flagged as a potential fad. Betting a major campaign on a transient trend for a permanent SKU is capital inefficient. If the trend dies, the product has no standalone value proposition.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows the diet segment to grow organically without cannibalizing full-sugar sales. A cheaper alternative is reformulating existing lines or limited-time offers (LTOs) to test the "mid-calorie" hypothesis without a full premium launch.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 18 months. Cause: The "half-sugar" compromise alienates taste-purists (who buy regular) and calorie-counters (who buy diet). The premium price creates a high barrier for a trial product with an unproven value prop. The rival's similar launch suggests a crowded, low-differentiation space where the first mover doesn't guarantee wins if the category is flawed.
4.  **Evidence Gaps:** The proposal admits risks regarding consumer satisfaction ("satisfy neither group") and price sensitivity but proceeds anyway. There is no data showing young men specifically demand *exactly* half-sugar at a *premium*.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Value Prop:** The core claim relies on a segment that may not exist (those wanting half-sugar but not diet). The proposal's own risk assessment admits this product may satisfy neither existing group.
*   **Trend Dependency:** The entire volume projection likely hinges on the "low-carb" trend continuing. If it is a fad (as noted), the volume collapses, leaving a premium-priced orphan product.
*   **Pricing Mismatch:** Launching an unproven, compromised product at a *premium* contradicts basic trial economics. Trial requires low friction; premium pricing increases friction.

**Risks:** Cannibalization of core brands, brand dilution, wasted campaign spend on a fad.

**Kill Criteria:** If a blind taste test shows <40% preference over diet or regular, or if low-carb search trends drop >15% in Q1, abort.

The plan fails the "beat do nothing" test because the risks are inherent to the product definition, not just execution. The compromise positioning is a strategic weakness, not a niche.

DECISION: NO-GO
