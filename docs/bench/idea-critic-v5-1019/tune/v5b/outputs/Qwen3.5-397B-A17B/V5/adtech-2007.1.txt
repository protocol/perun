First call: NO-GO. The proposed $6 billion cash outlay for a display-ad platform in 2007 represents an unchecked strategic pivot into a volatile market where the buyer lacks core competency, driven primarily by reactive competition rather than verified organic demand.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the platform provides immediate scale to challenge the search leader's emerging display dominance, bypassing years of internal R&D in a fast-moving sector.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Blocker:** The price is a "large premium" in cash, the company's largest ever. In 2007, display ad technology was fragmented and unproven compared to search. Paying a peak-market premium for a commodity-like infrastructure creates massive goodwill risk if the market shifts (which it did with the rise of programmatic and mobile later).
2.  **Strategic Conflict:** The proposal admits conflicts with existing agency customers. Alienating the current revenue base (agencies) to chase a speculative display future undermines the core search business. The "conflict" is not a risk; it is a guaranteed revenue churn mechanism.
3.  **Reactive Motive:** The primary driver is the competitor's move ("which just agreed to buy"). Basing a $6B capital allocation on mimicry rather than unique unit economics is a classic value-destroying error. The "fast-changing" nature of the tech suggests the asset could be obsolete quickly, making the irreversible cash spend indefensible.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or partner/invest minimally preserves cash while the display market matures. Building internally or acquiring smaller, specialized tech later avoids the integration nightmare and premium pricing of a market-top deal.

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-acquisition, the platform is technologically outdated, agency clients have left for neutral providers, and the $6B cash reserve is gone, leaving the company vulnerable during the 2008 financial crisis with no ROI from the display segment.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** $6B cash premium for unproven, fast-changing tech.
*   **Fact:** Direct conflict with existing, profitable customer base (agencies).
*   **Fact:** Strategy is reactive (competitor mimicry), not based on verified internal capability or demand.

**Risks:** Market shift to mobile/programmatic rendering the acquired tech obsolete; integration failure; culture clash.

The commitment is too large, too irreversible, and based on flawed premises (reactive strategy, ignoring customer conflict).

DECISION: NO-GO
