First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and market timing fundamentally contradict the capital structure.

**Steelman:** A premium, niche service for global travelers can generate sufficient margin to service debt before terrestrial networks achieve ubiquitous coverage.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Shrinkage:** The target segment (international business travelers) is shrinking rapidly as ground cellular networks expand globally. The "worldwide" advantage decays monthly.
2.  **Price Elasticity:** At $3,000 per handset and several dollars per minute, the addressable market is too small to support $5 billion in debt. Subscriber growth targets required to meet debt payments are mathematically impossible within this price bracket.
3.  **Product Deficit:** Bulky handsets with poor indoor performance fail the basic utility test for a premium product, increasing churn risk.
4.  **Go-to-Market Gap:** Sales channels are not ready, guaranteeing a slow launch that conflicts with immediate debt service obligations.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital; avoid catastrophic default.
*   **Pivot to B2B/Government:** Target specific verticals (maritime, aviation, defense) where price sensitivity is lower and indoor use is less critical.
*   **Delay/Restructure:** Pause commercial launch until handsets improve and debt is refinanced (though high leverage makes this unlikely).

**Pre-mortem:** The company launches, misses subscriber targets by 60% due to price and device quality, cannot service interest payments, and declares bankruptcy within 18 months while terrestrial networks capture the remaining roamers.

**Evidence & Logic:**
*   Debt service requires massive, immediate scale.
*   High price + poor UX + shrinking market = insufficient scale.
*   The proposal admits all critical failure modes (price, device quality, channel readiness, competitor speed) without offering mitigations.

**Blockers:**
*   **Financial Mismatch:** The projected revenue from the defined niche cannot cover the debt service of $5B borrowed capital.
*   **Timing:** The window of exclusivity is closing faster than the sales ramp-up.

**Risks:** (All become blockers given the financial constraint) Market adoption speed, device iteration cycles, competitor expansion.

**Kill Criteria:** If subscriber acquisition costs exceed $2,000 or if average revenue per user (ARPU) does not hit $500/month within 6 months, the model fails. Given the pricing, even maxed-out ARPU may not suffice.

This plan commits to a high-fixed-cost structure with a low-probability revenue stream. It is a bet on a vanishing monopoly.

DECISION: NO-GO
