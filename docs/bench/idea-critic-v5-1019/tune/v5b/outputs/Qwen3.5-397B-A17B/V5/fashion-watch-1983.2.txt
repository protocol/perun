First call: GO. The proposal reframes a low-cost commodity into a high-volume fashion category, directly addressing the risk of obsolescence by competing on style rather than heritage.

**Steelman:** Leveraging existing manufacturing to create a new, disposable fashion segment allows the company to capture volume from competitors while insulating its core luxury brand through distinct positioning.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Reputation Risk:** Valid only if the plastic line is marketed as "luxury." Positioning explicitly as "fashion items" creates a separate brand architecture. The risk is mitigated by clear segmentation, not by abandoning the product.
2.  **Aggressive Target (1M units):** At ~83k units/month globally, this requires massive distribution but is feasible if the price point creates impulse-buy behavior. The target is a stretch goal, not a blocker, provided the marketing budget matches the ambition.
3.  **Fashion Volatility:** Launching twelve models diversifies aesthetic risk. If tastes shift, the low unit cost of plastic allows for rapid pivots without the inventory write-downs associated with precious metals.
4.  **Competitor Price Cuts:** Established brands cannot easily match the cost structure of a dedicated plastic movement maker without diluting their own premium pricing. This is a cost-leadership moat.

**Blockers:** None found in the stated facts. The proposal acknowledges the risks and offers a strategic pivot (fashion vs. timekeeper) that neutralizes the primary threat to reputation. The sales target is ambitious but serves as a necessary north star for a volume play; missing it is an execution risk, not a fundamental flaw in the strategy.

**Risks:** Inventory misalignment if specific models flop; brand confusion if marketing fails to distinguish the lines. These are managed via the twelve-model diversification and clear positioning.

**Kill Criteria:** If first-quarter sell-through is below 15% of the pro-rated annual target, halt production expansion and re-evaluate the fashion positioning. Leading metric: Weekly sell-through rate by model.

The proposal turns a potential weakness (cheap materials) into a strategic advantage (accessible fashion). It beats "do nothing" by opening a new market segment that protects the core business from low-end disruption.

DECISION: GO
