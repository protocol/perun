First call: NO-GO. The plan relies on a weak revenue model and cedes control of the brand's only consumer touchpoint to a partner with misaligned incentives.

**Steelman:** Licensing allows rapid, low-capital entry into the consumer market by leveraging a toy company's distribution, generating royalty revenue while avoiding manufacturing risk.

**Attack:**
1.  **Economics:** "Small royalty per unit" in a low-cost console market likely fails to cover the opportunity cost of the platform license. Without volume guarantees, the upside is negligible compared to the risk of brand dilution.
2.  **Incentives:** The licensee (toy company) lacks the core competency to market an "internet console" against established giants. Their incentive is to move hardware volume, not to build a sustainable ecosystem for the licensor. If marketing fails, the licensor earns nothing but retains the reputational damage.
3.  **Strategy:** Entering a dominated market with a "low-cost" derivative via a third party is a losing position. It confirms the licensor as a secondary player rather than an innovator. The risk that "consumers may not connect the product with the licensor's brand" is a certainty if the licensee controls marketing; this wastes the brand equity.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves brand integrity. A direct, focused pilot or a joint venture with shared marketing control would be superior, though still risky.

**Pre-mortem:** The console launches, sells poorly due to weak marketing, and is perceived as a cheap toy accessory. The licensor gains no foothold in the internet market, earns trivial royalties, and damages its computer brand association.

**Blockers:**
*   The economic model (small royalty, low cost) does not support the investment of a valuable platform license.
*   Ceding marketing control guarantees the brand connection risk cited in the proposal remains unmitigated.

**Risks:** Market dominance by incumbents, licensee execution failure. These are severe, but the structural flaws in the deal terms are the immediate blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** Any deal where the licensor does not control marketing messaging or where projected royalties do not exceed a minimum threshold based on conservative volume estimates.

DECISION: NO-GO
