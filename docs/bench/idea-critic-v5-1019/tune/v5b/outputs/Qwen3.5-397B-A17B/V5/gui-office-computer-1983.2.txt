First call: NO-GO. The price is an order of magnitude too high for the stated market, and internal competition exists.

**Steelman:** A premium, mouse-driven workstation offers unmatched usability for specialized high-value tasks, justifying a niche luxury price point despite speed and software limitations.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Market:** At $10,000, the unit costs several times more than leading business PCs. Office workers (the target) operate on standardized budgets; this price excludes the mass market entirely.
2.  **Internal Cannibalization:** A cheaper, similar machine is already in development internally. Launching the expensive version first undermines the upcoming cost-effective solution and confuses the sales channel.
3.  **Value Mismatch:** Slow performance and scarce third-party software negate the usability benefits of the graphical interface for general office work. The "premium" feels like a defect, not a feature, at this price point.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" allows the cheaper internal project to launch later at a viable price point. Waiting captures the market without the brand damage of an overpriced, underpowered release.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sells negligible units due to price, and is recalled within a year as the cheaper internal model arrives, branding the company as out of touch with business reality.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price:** $10,000 is prohibitive for "office workers" compared to established competitors.
*   **Internal Conflict:** A superior (cheaper) alternative exists within the same company, making this launch strategically redundant.
*   **Performance/Software:** Slow speed and lack of software make the high price indefensible for productivity.

**Risks:**
*   Market rejection of GUI (mitigated by targeting niche, but price blocks even niche).
*   Execution delays on the cheaper internal model (does not justify launching a flawed expensive one).

**Conclusion:** The proposal fails on basic unit economics and strategic fit. It targets a mass audience ("office workers") with a luxury price tag while offering inferior performance and software support compared to existing options and future internal plans.

DECISION: NO-GO
