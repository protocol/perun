First call: GO. The proposal targets an unserved gap: luxury quality at a non-luxury price, leveraging existing engineering competence to undercut incumbents.

**Steelman:** A distinct brand identity, separated physically and visually from the economy parent, can overcome brand stigma while delivering superior value through shared underlying engineering efficiencies, capturing margin-conscious luxury buyers.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed Plan:** High capex (new dealers, R&D), high risk (brand rejection), high upside (new segment dominance). Reversible only at great loss.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain status quo; cede luxury margin to Europeans; risk brand stagnation as economy margins compress.
3.  **Badge Engineering:** Launch luxury trim under existing brand. Lower cost, but fails to overcome the "economy car" stigma; likely lower upside.

**Fit:** Moves the north star from "volume leader" to "full-spectrum manufacturer." Assumes buyers prioritize value/refinement over heritage if the badge is distinct.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the separate dealer network fails to attract foot traffic due to lack of brand pull, burning cash before volume is reached. Or, if cost-cutting to maintain the price advantage compromises the "match European leaders" quality claim, destroying credibility instantly.

**Evidence & Math:** The proposal admits heavy sunk costs. The critical math is whether the price gap vs. Europeans covers the new dealer operating costs given projected low initial volume. The "single model" risk is acute; if the flagship misses, there is no fallback.

**Blocker Test:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The risks (brand stigma, dealer cost, thin lineup) are severe but not factual impossibilities. They are execution risks, not proof of failure. The "heavy development cost" is sunk; the decision rests on future ROI.
*   **Risks:** Brand perception inertia; cash burn from standalone dealers; single-point failure of the flagship.

**Kill Criteria:** If the first 12 months of sales do not achieve 60% of the breakeven volume required to sustain the separate dealer network, the brand must be folded or merged into existing channels.

**Decision:** The potential to redefine the market hierarchy outweighs the execution risks, provided the brand separation is absolute.

DECISION: GO
