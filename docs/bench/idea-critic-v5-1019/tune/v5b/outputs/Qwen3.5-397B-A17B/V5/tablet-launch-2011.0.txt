First call: NO-GO. Launching a premium-priced tablet with a tiny app catalog against a dominant incumbent, while the OS has negligible share and leadership is unstable, ignores the primary driver of tablet adoption: ecosystem depth.

Steelman: The brand's hardware prestige can temporarily offset OS weakness if developer momentum is ignited immediately through heavy investment.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High price, small catalog, heavy ad spend. Risk: High burn, low conversion. Reversibility: Low (brand damage).
2. **Do Nothing:** Wait for OS maturity or catalog growth. Cost: Delayed entry. Risk: Missing the window.
3. **Subsidy Strategy:** Sell hardware at cost/loss to drive OS share, monetize later. Cost: Margin hit. Risk: Cash flow.

Fit: Assumes users prioritize brand over apps. In 2011, tablet utility is almost entirely app-dependent. A small catalog is a functional blocker, not a marketing challenge.

Pre-mortem: The device launches to strong initial sales due to brand loyalty, but users return it within 30 days due to "app gap." Developer outreach fails to gain traction because the installed base remains too small to justify porting costs. The transition in leadership causes strategic drift, halting the subsidy needed to compete.

Evidence: The proposal admits the app catalog is a "small fraction" and OS share is "almost none." Market data (general knowledge 2011) confirms the leader's dominance is entrenched by network effects. No evidence suggests heavy advertising converts users when core utility (apps) is missing.

Blockers:
1. **Utility Deficit:** A premium price requires parity in core utility (apps). The proposal admits a "small app catalog," which contradicts the pricing strategy.
2. **Ecosystem Chicken-and-Egg:** Heavy developer outreach fails without an installed base; an installed base requires apps. The plan offers no mechanism to break this cycle other than "invest heavily," which history shows is insufficient without a installed base hook.

Risks: Leadership transition causing execution failure; brand dilution from a failed premium product.

Kill Criteria: If app count does not triple in 6 months, halt hardware production. Leading metric: Daily active users per device.

DECISION: NO-GO
