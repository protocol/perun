First call: GO. Removing the jack forces a wireless future; owning the chip and case solves pairing and battery anxiety, creating a new high-margin category before competitors react.

Steelman: The jack removal is a forced migration that only succeeds if the wireless alternative offers seamless connectivity and portable power that wired ears cannot match, justifying the $159 price through superior convenience rather than audio fidelity alone.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Keep the jack, delay wireless transition, cede premium accessory margin to third parties, and maintain clutter.
2. Launch cheap wireless buds: Lower price but likely suffer pairing friction and poor battery, reinforcing "wireless is worse" bias.
3. Proposed plan: High price funds R&D for custom silicon (instant pairing) and case (portable charging), directly neutralizing the top two functional objections (battery, convenience) while monetizing the forced switch.

Fit: Aligns with a north star of minimalism and ecosystem lock-in. The custom chip ensures the experience is exclusive to this phone, driving ecosystem value.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the "odd look" triggers lasting cultural ridicule that outweighs utility, or if losing one bud renders the $159 investment useless. However, the charging case mitigates battery loss, and the custom chip mitigates pairing friction. The cost of delay is losing the definition of the "modern phone" to no one; hesitation validates the jack's necessity.

Evidence: The proposal admits risks (looks, loss, price, anger) but offers specific technical counters (custom chip, case) for the functional deficits. The "anger" is a transition cost, not a product flaw.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited are market reception and behavior changes, not factual contradictions or impossible engineering constraints. The price is high, but justified by the unique integration (chip) and utility (case) not present in free wired options.

Risks: Cultural ridicule, unit loss, price resistance. These are managed by the kill criteria below.

Kill criteria: If pre-orders for the buds are <10% of phone pre-orders by launch month end, or if return rates due to "fit/looks" exceed 15%, pivot to a lower-cost, generic-chip alternative for the next cycle. Leading metric: Attach rate of buds per phone unit sold in Q1.

DECISION: GO
