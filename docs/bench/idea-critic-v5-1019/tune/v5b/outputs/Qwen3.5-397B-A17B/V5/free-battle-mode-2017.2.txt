First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to enter a saturated market with an unproven business model while threatening existing revenue, all within an unrealistically short timeline.

**Steelman:** Reusing assets allows a rapid, low-cost entry into the popular last-player-standing genre to capture users via a free-to-play model, diversifying revenue beyond the initial paid sale.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Fit:** A rival already dominates the genre. Entering late requires a unique value proposition, which "reusing assets" from a cooperative game does not provide. The core loop of cooperation conflicts mechanically with last-player-standing dynamics, suggesting the reused assets may not fit the gameplay needs.
2.  **Cannibalization:** Offering a free version of similar content directly undermines the value proposition of the paid cooperative game. Without a clear differentiation strategy, this risks eroding the studio's stable, proven revenue stream for a speculative one.
3.  **Operational Risk:** The studio lacks experience in free-to-play economics (retention, monetization balance, live ops). Building a sustainable economy in two months is operationally implausible; rushed launches in this sector typically fail due to balance issues or exploit vulnerabilities.
4.  **Timeline:** Two months is insufficient to design, test, and balance a competitive multiplayer mode that can challenge an established incumbent, especially while integrating a new monetization backend.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Protect current paid sales and avoid brand damage from a low-quality clone.
*   *Pivot:* Invest time in designing a unique hybrid mode that leverages the studio's cooperative strength rather than copying the rival's formula.
*   *Delay:* Extend the timeline to properly build the free-to-play infrastructure and test the economy before launch.

**Blockers:**
*   The two-month timeline is factually insufficient for a balanced competitive launch.
*   Lack of internal expertise in free-to-play operations creates a high probability of economic failure.
*   Direct competition with a genre owner using recycled cooperative assets offers no clear path to user acquisition.

**Risks:** Revenue cannibalization, brand dilution, wasted development resources.

The proposal fails to address how it will succeed against an incumbent with inferior assets and no operational experience in the target model.

DECISION: NO-GO
