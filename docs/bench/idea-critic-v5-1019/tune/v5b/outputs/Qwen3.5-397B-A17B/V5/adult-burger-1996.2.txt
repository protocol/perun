First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the brand's core identity and customer value proposition without a mechanism to overcome them.

**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can temporarily override brand perception, forcing adults to trial a premium burger despite the chain's "kids and value" reputation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Brand Inertia:** The "child-centered image" is a structural barrier, not a marketing gap. Adults associating the brand with toys and low prices will likely reject a high-priced item regardless of ad volume. Advertising amplifies existing associations; it rarely inverts them instantly.
2.  **Economic Mismatch:** The core demographic expects value. A price point "above the core menu" alienates regulars without guaranteeing new adult buyers who have established premium alternatives.
3.  **Irreversible Cost:** Committing the "largest advertising budget" before validating demand violates basic capital discipline. If the product fails (likely due to brand fit), the sunk cost is catastrophic.
4.  **Better Way:** Test the product in a limited market with modest spend to verify if adults will actually pay a premium at *this* specific counter. If the brand image blocks sales, no amount of advertising fixes the underlying disconnect; a sub-brand or acquisition would be required instead.

**Blockers:**
*   **Contradictory Premise:** The plan assumes ad spend can overcome a deeply entrenched brand identity ("child-centered") that directly conflicts with the target attribute ("premium adult"). History shows brand pivots of this magnitude rarely succeed via advertising alone.
*   **Fatal Economics:** Betting the "largest budget" on an unproven hypothesis where the primary risk is fundamental brand rejection is financially indefensible.

**Risks:**
*   Alienating the core family base.
*   Wasting massive capital on a product the market rejects due to brand perception.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   Do not proceed without a pilot test proving adults will pay the premium price at current locations.
*   Cap initial marketing spend at <5% of the proposed budget until unit economics are proven.

The proposal demands an all-in bet on a hypothesis that the brand itself disproves. The mismatch between "kids/value" and "adult/premium" is a strategic chasm, not a messaging problem solvable by budget.

DECISION: NO-GO
