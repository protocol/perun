First call: NO-GO. The proposal demands committing the entire company to a single unproven product line while lacking the capital for essential infrastructure (factory, batteries) and facing a non-existent charging network.

**Steelman:** A focused bet on electric vehicles (EVs) allows a newcomer to define a premium niche without legacy combustion constraints, leveraging a purchased factory to accelerate timeline.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capital Mismatch:** The proposal admits the company lacks funds for the factory and batteries yet recommends buying a large factory immediately. This creates an immediate liquidity crisis before production begins.
2.  **Market Readiness:** In 2008, scarce charging infrastructure severely limits the addressable market to early adopters with home charging, insufficient to sustain a full-size sedan mass-production line.
3.  **Execution Risk:** Mass production is notoriously difficult for startups; combining this with unproven battery supply chains and a single-product strategy removes all margin for error.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "do nothing" or "limited pilot" approach preserves capital. A cheaper alternative is contracting assembly (avoiding factory purchase) or launching a high-end limited roadster first to prove technology and brand before committing to a mass-market sedan.

**Pre-mortem:** The company exhausts its cash reserves retrofitting the factory and securing battery contracts. Without a widespread charging network, consumer adoption stalls. Established makers, watching from the sidelines, undercut prices or release competing models once the path is cleared. The company cannot pivot because it has committed to "only" EVs and sold its future on one large factory.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The company explicitly lacks the capital for the factory and batteries required by the plan.
*   **Fact:** The 2008 charging infrastructure is insufficient to support mass-market full-size sedan sales.
*   **Kill Check:** The commitment is total ("only battery-electric," "large factory"), irreversible (buying physical plant), and the core claim (viable mass production) contradicts the stated lack of capital and infrastructure.

This is a classic over-extension: betting the farm on unproven technology and infrastructure without the necessary runway. The plan fails on its own stated financial constraints.

DECISION: NO-GO
