First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits the entire company to a single, unproven product line while lacking the capital to build the necessary factory and battery supply, creating an immediate solvency risk before production begins.

**Steelman:** Committing exclusively to EVs in 2008 captures a first-mover advantage in a future-dominated market, leveraging a purchased factory to accelerate scale faster than organic construction.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capital Mismatch:** The proposal admits the factory and batteries require "far more capital than the company has." Committing to *only* EVs eliminates revenue diversification that could fund operations. Buying an existing large factory from established makers is likely prohibitively expensive and may involve outdated tooling unsuited for unique EV architectures, burning cash reserves immediately.
2.  **Market Infrastructure:** With charging stations scarce in 2008, demand for a full-size electric sedan is artificially capped. Without a parallel massive investment in charging infrastructure (not mentioned), the product addresses a market that functionally cannot use it at scale.
3.  **Execution Risk:** A new carmaker with "little experience" attempting mass production of a complex, novel powertrain in a purchased facility invites catastrophic quality and ramp-up failures. Established competitors can easily undercut price or leverage their superior supply chains.
4.  **Strategic Rigidity:** The "only battery-electric" constraint removes the ability to pivot to hybrids or ICE vehicles if battery costs or adoption rates lag, turning a calculated risk into an existential gamble.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing/Wait:** Preserve capital until battery costs drop and charging infrastructure matures.
*   **Hybrid Approach:** Launch a limited EV run while maintaining flexible manufacturing for other powertrains to ensure cash flow.
*   **Asset Light:** Contract manufacture instead of buying a factory to reduce upfront CAPEX.

**Pre-mortem:** The company buys the factory, depletes cash on retooling, fails to ramp production due to inexperience, and cannot sell units due to lack of chargers. Bankruptcy occurs within 18 months.

**Blockers:** The explicit admission of insufficient capital for the core requirements (factory + batteries) combined with the "all-in" strategic constraint makes the plan financially impossible as stated.

**Risks:** Infrastructure scarcity, competitor response, production inexperience. These are severe but secondary to the funding gap.

DECISION: NO-GO
