First call: GO. The proposal avoids a direct hardware arms race against a well-funded rival by leveraging asymmetry: dual screens and touch input to target a broader, non-core audience at a lower price point, while maintaining legacy support.

**Steelman:** Differentiation via novel interaction (touch/dual-screen) and price will expand the total addressable market faster than matching graphical fidelity, securing the ecosystem against a superior-spec competitor.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Compete solely on graphics. *Result:* Likely failure due to rival's funding advantage and established lead in media features.
2.  **Match Specs:** Build a stronger machine. *Result:* Higher price, delayed launch, alienating the core base without guaranteeing new users.
3.  **Proposed Path:** Accept lower graphical power for unique gameplay and cost efficiency. *Risk:* Developer confusion and "gimmick" perception. *Mitigation:* Keeping the older handheld on sale preserves the core library and revenue stream while the new device cultivates new genres. The "unproven" nature of touch is the very opportunity to define a new category where the rival's graphics advantage is irrelevant.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This fits a strategy of market expansion rather than consolidation. If it fails, it will be because first-party software did not demonstrate the unique value of the second screen, causing developers to ignore the platform. However, the lower price point reduces the barrier to entry, making the "gimmick" risk a marketing challenge rather than a fatal economic flaw.

**Evidence & Blockers:** The proposal relies on the factual premise that a lower price and novel input can offset graphical inferiority for a "broadened audience." No internal contradiction exists. The risks (developer confusion, unproven tech) are execution challenges, not blockers. The presence of a fallback (keeping the old system) removes the existential risk of alienating the current base.

**Decision:** The plan successfully sidesteps a losing battle of specifications to fight a winnable battle of utility and price. The risks are manageable through software leadership and do not invalidate the core strategic pivot.

Blockers: NONE.
Risks: Developer adoption speed, consumer perception of novelty vs. utility.

DECISION: GO
