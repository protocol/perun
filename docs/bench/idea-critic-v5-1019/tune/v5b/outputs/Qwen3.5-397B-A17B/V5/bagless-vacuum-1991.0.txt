First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to full-scale manufacturing and direct sales without evidence that buyers will pay a premium for an unproven bagless mechanism, despite established incumbents holding distribution monopolies and the inventor lacking any manufacturing record.

Steelman: A superior cyclone technology justifies a premium price and can disrupt the market if the inventor vertically integrates to capture full value.

Attack: The plan fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests. The core claim relies on buyers paying extra for a feature (bagless) that has no proven market demand in 1991. The risks are not merely potential; they are structural facts: higher unit costs, zero distribution leverage, and no manufacturing history. The proposal offers no pilot, no capped initial run, and no kill criteria to test demand before committing to the massive capital expenditure of founding a manufacturing company. A cheaper, safer alternative exists: license the technology to an established manufacturer to validate the premium price point and performance claims at scale before attempting vertical integration. The current plan bets the entire venture on the assumption that "better technology equals immediate market adoption," which contradicts the stated reality of incumbent dominance and consumer inertia.

Blockers:
1. No evidence supports the claim that buyers will pay a premium for bagless vacuums; the proposal admits this is a risk but proceeds as if it is solved.
2. The inventor has no manufacturing record, making the proposed self-manufacturing route a high-probability failure point before sales even begin.
3. A cheaper alternative (licensing) reaches the same upside (market validation) with significantly lower risk and capital requirement, blocking the current "build everything ourselves" approach.

Risks: Incumbents copying the idea, retail distribution rejection, cash flow collapse due to high production costs. These are severe, but the blockers above make the plan untenable as proposed. The commitment is too large and irreversible given the unproven core assumption.

DECISION: NO-GO
