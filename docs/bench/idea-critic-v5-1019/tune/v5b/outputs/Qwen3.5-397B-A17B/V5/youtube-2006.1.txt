First call: NO-GO. The price ($1.65B) vastly exceeds any verified revenue or asset value, and the core mechanism (hosting copyrighted content) invites existential litigation.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the dominant traffic leader now secures the category before competitors do, betting that ad monetization and legal settlements will eventually justify the premium.

**Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Avoid immediate liability; risk losing the video market entirely if the target survives lawsuits.
2.  **Build/Buy Smaller:** Grow internal video service or buy a compliant, smaller rival. Lower cost, but likely fails to capture the network effect.
3.  **Acquire Target:** Highest cost and risk, but guarantees market leadership if legal/ad hurdles clear.

**Fit:** Assumes the strategy is "dominate online video immediately." If the north star is profitability or legal safety, this fails.

**Pre-mortem:** The deal closes, but media lawsuits result in a judgment exceeding the company's cash reserves or force the removal of all popular content, leaving a worthless shell with massive bandwidth bills. The ad model fails to cover bandwidth costs per view.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits "no meaningful revenue," "large volume of copyrighted clips," and "threatened lawsuits." The math does not work: $1.65B for zero profit and negative legal standing is speculation, not investment. The bandwidth costs alone could bankrupt the division without a proven ad CPM.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Financial:** The price relies on future ad revenue that is explicitly "unproven" in the proposal.
2.  **Legal:** The core asset (library) contains "large volume" of infringing material with active legal threats. This is not a risk; it is a stated fact that threatens the buyer's entire existence.
3.  **Alternative:** A cheaper path exists (build or buy compliant tech) that avoids the existential legal threat, even if growth is slower.

**Risks:** Bandwidth scaling costs, failure to monetize views, integration friction.

**Kill Criteria:** If legal counsel cannot quantify a settlement path under $500M within 30 days, or if projected bandwidth costs exceed ad revenue by >2x after 6 months.

The proposal asks to bet the company on unproven economics and active illegality. No amount of "market leadership" justifies an uncapped liability where the asset itself is the lawsuit.

DECISION: NO-GO
