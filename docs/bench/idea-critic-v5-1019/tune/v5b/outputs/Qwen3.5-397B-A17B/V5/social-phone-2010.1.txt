First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores basic market fit: teenagers need apps and low costs, neither of which are offered.

Steelman: Leveraging a popular messaging brand to capture teens via an exclusive, data-centric device could work if the social experience is unmatched and the price is right. However, the current plan fails on both counts.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Avoid sinking more capital into a likely failure; preserve brand equity.
2. **Pivot to existing OS:** Port the messaging experience to established platforms (iOS/Android) where teens already live and apps exist. Lower risk, immediate reach.
3. **Proposed Plan:** High cost, no apps, new OS, exclusive carrier. Highest risk, lowest likelihood of adoption.

Fit: The strategy contradicts the "north star" of teen adoption. Teens live on social networks defined by third-party apps. A phone with no app store is functionally broken for this demographic in 2010.

Pre-mortem: The launch fails because parents refuse the high data plan, and teens reject a phone that cannot run popular games or social apps available on competitors' devices. The sunk cost of two years' development becomes a total loss. This is a one-way door with massive inventory risk.

Evidence: The proposal admits rival smartphones have large app catalogs and the data plan is "high for teenagers." These are not risks; they are fatal flaws confirmed by the proposal's own facts. The decision to use a new in-house OS instead of the company's existing mobile platform further fragments resources without clear benefit.

Blockers:
1. **No App Store:** Teens require apps; competitors have them. This removes the route to the upside (social network dominance).
2. **Price:** Data plan cost equals a full smartphone plan, pricing out the target demographic.
3. **Platform Fragmentation:** Ignoring the company's own mobile platform for a new OS adds unnecessary technical risk and delay.

Risks: Execution difficulty, carrier exclusivity limiting reach. These are secondary to the blockers.

The sunk cost of two years is irrelevant to the future viability. Committing to manufacturing and marketing now guarantees a larger loss.

DECISION: NO-GO
