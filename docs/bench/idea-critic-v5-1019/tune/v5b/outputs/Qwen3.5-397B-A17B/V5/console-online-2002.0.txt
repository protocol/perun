First call: NO-GO. The proposal mandates broadband in 2002 when penetration is low, charges for a feature rivals offer free, and requires operational capabilities the company lacks.

Steelman: A unified, high-quality paid network creates a sticky ecosystem and revenue stream that free, fragmented publisher solutions cannot match, justifying the hardware loss leader.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Cede online play to publishers; risk becoming a offline-only niche.
2. **Publisher-led free model:** Match rivals; zero infrastructure cost but no unified identity or quality control.
3. **Proposed paid service:** High upfront cost, high risk of adoption failure due to price and connectivity barriers, but highest upside if successful.

Fit: Assumes a vision where online is central, not peripheral. However, the market infrastructure (broadband) does not yet support a "console-wide" mandate.

Pre-mortem: The service launches to silence because 90% of owners lack broadband and refuse to pay $50/year for what competitors offer free. The company burns cash on idle servers and support staff it cannot manage, damaging the brand. This is a one-way door with high sunk costs; delay allows broadband adoption to mature.

Evidence: The proposal admits "few homes have broadband" and the company has "never run" these operations. These are not risks; they are current facts contradicting the "console-wide" and "operational readiness" assumptions.

Blockers:
1. **Infrastructure Mismatch:** Mandating broadband in 2002 excludes the vast majority of the installed base, making "console-wide" adoption impossible.
2. **Pricing vs. Expectation:** Charging for online play when the market standard is free creates an immediate adoption barrier without proven exclusive value.
3. **Operational Void:** Launching a complex service without prior experience or stated hiring/partnering plan guarantees failure in uptime and support.

Risks: Player base fragmentation, high churn, brand damage.

The plan fails as proposed because it mandates conditions (ubiquitous broadband, willingness to pay) that do not exist, while ignoring the company's operational inability to deliver the service. A pilot, cap, or partnership strategy is absent.

DECISION: NO-GO
