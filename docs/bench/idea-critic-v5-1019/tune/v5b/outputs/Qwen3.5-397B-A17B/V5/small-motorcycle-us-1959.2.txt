First call: NO-GO. The proposal targets a market segment (small commuter bikes) that the evidence suggests does not exist in the US, where motorcycles are culturally defined as large machines, while simultaneously exposing the firm to catastrophic mechanical failure on US highways and severe financial strain via foreign currency depletion.

**Steelman:** Entering the US with small bikes via non-traditional channels creates a new commuter category, bypassing the "rough" motorcycle image and leveraging existing retail networks to minimize setup costs.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Fit:** The premise that Americans will commute on 50cc step-throughs contradicts the stated fact that the US market consists of "large machines" with a "rough image." There is no evidence of demand for small bikes; the proposal assumes a cultural shift rather than serving an existing need.
2.  **Product Viability:** The proposal admits the company's larger models (the only alternative if small bikes fail) "may not hold up" at US highway speeds. Entering a market where your primary product may be culturally rejected and your secondary product is mechanically unfit is reckless.
3.  **Financial Exposure:** Tying up "scarce foreign currency" to establish a subsidiary for an unproven product in a hostile market violates basic capital preservation. The cost of delay is negligible compared to the cost of failure.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserves capital and reputation; avoids mechanical liability.
*   *Export Only:* Test larger models via existing distributors without subsidiary overhead (though mechanical risk remains).
*   *Licensing:* Partner with a US manufacturer to adapt designs for US speeds before direct investment.

**Pre-mortem:** The subsidiary opens, sales of small bikes are zero because Americans view them as toys, larger bikes seize on highways causing lawsuits, and the parent company faces a liquidity crisis due to the drained foreign currency reserves.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Mechanical Mismatch:** Stated fact that larger models may fail at US speeds removes the fallback option.
2.  **Cultural Mismatch:** No evidence supports the existence of a small-bike commuter market; the stated norm is "large machines."
3.  **Capital Constraint:** "Scarce foreign currency" makes this high-risk bet unsustainable compared to domestic needs.

**Risks:** Brand unknown, distribution channel mismatch. These are secondary to the fundamental product-market and mechanical blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** If a pilot test of 100 units in a single US city does not achieve 80% sell-through in 3 months without heavy discounting, terminate immediately. However, given the mechanical and cultural blockers, even a pilot is ill-advised without first verifying engine durability at US speeds.

DECISION: NO-GO
