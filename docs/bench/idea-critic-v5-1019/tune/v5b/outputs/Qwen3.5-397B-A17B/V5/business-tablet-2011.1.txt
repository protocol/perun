First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to enter a competitor's market with an inferior product (smaller screen, no native email) at an equal price point, while relying on an untested operating system and uncertain developer support.

Steelman: The company aims to leverage its new OS to create a distinct ecosystem, betting that future app growth and phone integration will eventually outweigh the initial lack of parity with the rival's established tablet.

However, the attack reveals fatal flaws in the current plan:
1.  **Value Mismatch:** The target demographic is "business email" users. Shipping a device without built-in email, calendar, or contacts (unless paired) directly contradicts the core value proposition of the brand.
2.  **Pricing Error:** Matching the price of a rival's larger, feature-rich tablet with a smaller, feature-poor device offers no economic incentive for adoption.
3.  **Ecosystem Risk:** Developers are unlikely to commit to an untested OS for a niche device, creating a vicious cycle where the lack of apps further reduces hardware appeal.
4.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "wait" option is superior to launching a product that actively damages the brand's reputation for business utility. A cheaper, safer alternative would be to delay launch until native email apps are ready or to price significantly lower to offset the feature deficit.

The pre-mortem is clear: The device launches, business users reject it due to the lack of email, general consumers reject it due to the high price and small app library, and the company writes off the OS acquisition cost.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal states the device lacks built-in email/calendar/contacts.
*   **Contradiction:** The company leads in "business email," making this omission a direct negation of its brand promise.
*   **Fact:** Price matches the rival's larger tablet.
*   **Contradiction:** No rational consumer chooses a smaller, less capable device at the same price.

**Risks:** Untested OS, developer apathy, and competitor momentum are significant but secondary to the immediate product-market fit failure.

This plan fails the basic test of offering a compelling reason to buy. It attacks the company's strength (email) while failing to compete on the rival's terms (apps, screen size, price).

DECISION: NO-GO
