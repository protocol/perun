First call: GO. The core claim is that a mandatory online client solves update delivery and cheating while creating a new distribution channel.

Steelman: A proprietary client is the only viable mechanism to enforce real-time anti-cheat verification and guarantee patch consistency, turning a logistical weakness into a proprietary storefront monopoly.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Cheating erodes the player base; retail updates remain slow. High risk of product death.
2. Patch via website: Fails to stop memory-editing cheats running alongside the game.
3. Third-party platforms: Non-existent or immature in 2003; cedes control and margin.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of long-term player retention and recurring revenue. Assumes broadband adoption will outpace the friction of mandatory installation.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the client launches with critical stability bugs, causing a retail revolt before network effects kick in. This is a one-way door for architecture but reversible for business model (can revert to boxed-only if client fails). Cost of delay is high: every month without anti-cheat accelerates community churn.

Evidence: The proposal admits broadband is not universal, but the target market (PC gamers with popular titles) skews early-adopter. The risk of retail pushback is real but manageable if the boxed version includes the client installer.

Blockers: NONE. The stated risks (dislike of accounts, broadband gaps, competitor copying) are execution hurdles, not fundamental flaws. Competitor copying validates the model; being first with a large installed base creates the moat. The "no universal broadband" fact does not block the plan because the minimum viable user segment already has connectivity.

Risks: Retail channel shrinkage, initial user friction. These become kill criteria: if retail sales drop >20% in Q1 or client adoption <60% of new users, pivot to hybrid offline mode.

Strongest attack survived: The argument that "players will reject mandatory online" was tested against the alternative (unplayable games due to cheating). Users prefer friction over a broken game; the value of a cheat-free environment outweighs the login hassle.

DECISION: GO
