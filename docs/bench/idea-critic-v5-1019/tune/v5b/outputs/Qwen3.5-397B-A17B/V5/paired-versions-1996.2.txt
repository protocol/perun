First call: GO. The mechanic directly solves the core engagement problem (completion) by manufacturing a social dependency that the hardware uniquely supports, turning a limitation (aging console, single-player focus) into a viral distribution engine.

**Steelman:** Releasing two distinct versions forces social interaction to achieve 100% completion, leveraging the installed base's desire to collect everything to drive double-unit sales per social circle, effectively making players the marketing channel.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Risk of the game disappearing into the library of a dying platform; low upside.
*   *Single Version + DLC/Updates:* Technically impossible on 1996 cartridge hardware without recalls or new SKUs anyway.
*   *Price Grab Perception:* Valid risk, but mitigated if the "missing" creatures are genuinely desirable and trading is seamless. The "greed" narrative only sticks if the content feels thin; if the collections feel substantial individually, the dual release feels like variety, not extraction.
*   *Declining Hardware:* The link cable is the *only* remaining unique value prop of this handheld against home consoles. This proposal leans into the device's specific strength rather than fighting its age.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the version differences are perceived as arbitrary or if the link cable failure rate is high. If trading is frustrating, the completion goal becomes annoying rather than engaging.

**Evidence & Fit:** The proposal relies on the psychological drive for completion (Pokédex effect) and the physical constraint of the link cable. No external data is needed; the logic holds on the mechanics alone. The "uncertain sales" fear is addressed by doubling the addressable market per player (friends need the other version).

**Blockers:** None. The risks (popularity, price grab, cable requirement) are inherent to the platform and genre, not flaws in this specific strategy. In fact, the strategy *requires* the cable and the collecting mindset to work. The "price grab" is a feature, not a bug, provided the value proposition per cartridge remains high.

**Kill Criteria:** If playtesting shows players feel cheated rather than motivated to trade, pivot to a single version with time-limited events (though technically hard in 1996).

**Decision:** The dual-version strategy transforms the hardware's isolation into a social necessity, creating a self-sustaining ecosystem that a single release cannot achieve.

DECISION: GO
