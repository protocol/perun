First call: GO. The current distribution model fails at its core functions: delivering updates and preventing cheating. A mandatory client directly solves these existential threats, while the store expansion offers a high-upside secondary revenue stream.

Steelman: A dedicated client is the only viable mechanism to ensure game integrity and timely patches for popular PC titles, creating a defensible ecosystem that can later scale into a broader distribution platform.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Cheating erodes the player base; update failures increase support costs and churn.
2. Patcher-only: Solves updates but fails to address real-time anti-cheat or unified purchases.
3. Retail-only updates: Logistically impossible for frequent patches; too slow for cheat mitigation.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of maintaining game quality and player retention. The shift from pure retail to hybrid digital is inevitable given the technical constraints of boxed media.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if early client bugs drive players away before stability is reached, or if retail partners retaliate by delisting boxes before digital adoption hits critical mass. Broadband limits may exclude a segment of users, but the core audience for popular PC games likely has access. This is a one-way door for the specific games requiring it, but reversible for the company if the client is abandoned (though damaging).

Evidence: The proposal states the current struggle with updates and cheating as fact. No data contradicts the necessity of an online component to solve these specific issues.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (user dislike, retail friction, bugs, broadband, competition) are execution challenges, not fundamental flaws. They do not prove the plan fails as proposed; they define the kill criteria.
Risks: User adoption friction, retail channel conflict, initial software instability.

Kill Criteria: If the client causes a >15% drop in active users within 6 months due to stability or access issues, or if retail partners drop >30% of shelf space within 1 year, pivot to an optional patcher model. Leading metric: Daily Active Users (DAU) on the client vs. boxed-only launches.

The risks are manageable compared to the certainty of failure in the current model. The "better way" of sticking to boxes cannot solve the stated problems.

DECISION: GO
