First call: NO-GO. The plan attempts to solve two hard problems (cross-border logistics and unproven consumer behavior) simultaneously across multiple continents with finite capital, creating a single point of failure in funding.

Steelman: A massive, simultaneous global launch creates an unassailable brand presence and network effect before competitors can react, justifying the extreme burn rate as a defensive moat.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Retain capital but cede the emerging online channel to future rivals.
2. Single-market pilot (e.g., UK only): Validates logistics and demand with 10% of the cost; reversible; delays global dominance but proves the model.
3. Proposed plan (Global): Highest upside if successful, but total loss if logistics break or cash runs out; effectively irreversible once offices are leased and staff hired.

Fit: Aligns with the "largest in the world" vision but ignores the survival constraint required to reach it.

Pre-mortem: The company runs out of cash 18 months in. Cause: Logistics costs in the US were 3x estimates due to returns, while European marketing spend failed to convert due to cultural mismatches. The burn rate across five offices drained the Series A before unit economics were positive in any single region. This is a one-way door; closing offices incurs massive legal and reputational costs.

Evidence: The proposal admits online fashion buying is "unproven" and acknowledges that multi-country operations multiply costs. There is no data showing customers will buy fashion online, nor evidence that the team can manage complex international supply chains. The risks listed (logistics, delay, unproven market, cash runway) are not mitigated; they are amplified by the scale.

Blockers:
1. The core claim relies on unproven consumer behavior (online fashion) while simultaneously executing the most expensive possible distribution strategy (multi-country). The proposal's own facts state the market is unproven; scaling unproven demand across multiple high-cost regions guarantees capital exhaustion before product-market fit is found.
2. No cheaper alternative reaches the same learning velocity. A single-market launch tests the core hypothesis (will they buy?) without the confounding variable of international logistics failure.

Risks: Venture money running out, logistics complexity, delayed launch. These are treated as blockers because the proposal offers no cap, pilot, or metric to stop the bleed if the unproven assumptions fail.

DECISION: NO-GO
