First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to commit to full-scale manufacturing and direct sales without evidence that buyers will pay a premium for an unproven category, despite explicit risks of high cost, zero brand equity, and aggressive incumbent retaliation.

Steelman: A superior bagless technology justifies a premium price and can disrupt the market if launched immediately as a standalone manufacturer.

Attack:
1.  **Economic Viability:** The proposal admits the unit cost is "noticeably more" than established brands. Without a proven willingness to pay, launching at a premium against cheaper, trusted incumbents is a capital destruction strategy. The math fails before the first unit ships.
2.  **Distribution & Copying:** Incumbents have retail lock-in and can copy the mechanical design faster than a startup with "no manufacturing record" can scale. If the idea works, giants will undercut the price; if it fails, the startup bears the entire R&D and tooling loss.
3.  **Unverified Demand:** The core claim—that buyers will pay extra for "bagless"—is an assumption, not a fact. Thousands of prototypes prove engineering feasibility, not market fit. Launching a company based on prototype count rather than paid pre-orders is speculation, not strategy.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing/License:** License the technology to an incumbent. This avoids manufacturing risk, leverages existing distribution, and generates immediate royalty revenue without needing customer education.
*   **Pilot:** Sell a limited batch via direct mail to validate the premium price point before founding a full manufacturing entity.

Pre-mortem: The company launches, burns cash on tooling, fails to secure shelf space against incumbent pay-to-play fees, and folds when early adopters return the complex, expensive machines. The incumbents then release a cheaper version using the now-public concept.

Blockers:
*   **Negative Unit Economics:** High cost + no brand premium = immediate loss per sale.
*   **Unproven Core Premise:** No evidence exists that the target market values "bagless" enough to overcome the price hurdle.

Risks: Incumbent copying, supply chain failure, consumer rejection. These are fatal without a validated price point.

The proposal demands an irreversible, high-cost commitment based on unverified assumptions. It must first prove someone will actually pay the premium.

DECISION: NO-GO
