First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores fatal mismatches between the product's constraints and the stated goal of becoming an "everyday consumer product."

**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 will validate demand and refine the device for a mass-market release by filtering for tech-tolerant users.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Positioning:** $1,500 is a professional/enterprise price point (2013 context), not "everyday consumer." This creates a brand identity of exclusivity and utility that contradicts the mass-market vision. It cannot beat "do nothing" for the average user when the cost exceeds a high-end smartphone.
2.  **Privacy & Social Acceptance:** The core function (always-on camera) directly triggers the stated risks of privacy objections and social rejection. Without a proven technical or policy mitigation (e.g., visible recording indicators, strict data limits) included in the plan, the product invites regulatory backlash and public hostility before scale.
3.  **Use Case Void:** The proposal admits "no clear everyday use for most people." Selling a solution without a defined problem to early adopters relies on them inventing the use case, which is a high-risk gamble for a hardware launch with high unit costs.
4.  **Battery Life:** Short battery life renders an "everyday" device unusable for a full day, causing immediate churn upon general release.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do nothing:** Avoids reputational damage from a failed public launch.
*   **Enterprise Pivot:** Target specific industrial use cases (logistics, manufacturing) where the $1,500 price is justified, privacy is controlled, and utility is clear. This matches the constraints better than the consumer proposal.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, media outlets ban it due to privacy concerns, early adopters return it due to lack of utility and poor battery life, and the brand becomes synonymous with "creepy tech," killing the mass-market dream permanently.

**Blockers:**
*   The stated price ($1,500) contradicts the "everyday consumer" positioning.
*   The admitted lack of "clear everyday use" means the core value proposition is unproven.
*   No mitigation exists in the proposal for the confirmed privacy/social risks.

**Risks:** High price sensitivity, battery fatigue, regulatory bans.

**Decision:** The plan fails the basic test of product-market fit and pricing strategy for the stated goal.

DECISION: NO-GO
