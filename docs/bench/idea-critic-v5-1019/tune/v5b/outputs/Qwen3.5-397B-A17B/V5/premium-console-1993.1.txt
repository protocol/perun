First call: NO-GO. The proposed $700 price point is prohibitively high compared to existing consoles, and the licensing model prevents the subsidy strategy required to compete.

Steelman: Licensing shifts manufacturing risk to partners while securing royalty streams, leveraging their scale to penetrate the market with superior graphics.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** High price ($700), licensee hardware profit, dual royalties. Risk: Zero volume due to price; licensees refuse uncompetitive terms.
2. **Do Nothing:** Avoid immediate capital burn but forfeit market entry.
3. **Alternative (Subsidy Model):** Start-up absorbs hardware loss (or forces licensees to) to lower street price to ~$300, recouping via game royalties only. Risk: High upfront capital requirement; complex licensee accounting.

Fit: Aiming to "set the standard" requires massive install base. High pricing targets a niche, not a standard. The model contradicts the north star of market dominance.

Pre-mortem: The console launches at $700. Consumers buy established $200 competitors. Licensees, unable to sell the overpriced units, cancel production. The start-up earns zero royalties and collapses. This is a one-way door; brand perception as "luxury niche" is hard to reverse. Price sensitivity in 1993 is extreme; $700 is a PC price, not a toy price.

Evidence: The proposal admits the price is "far above existing consoles" and competitors "sell hardware at a loss." The core claim (setting the standard) relies on volume, which the stated price mathematically precludes.

Blockers:
1. **Price/Volume Contradiction:** The $700 price point guarantees insufficient install base to "set the standard," directly contradicting the primary goal.
2. **Incentive Misalignment:** Licensees cannot profit on hardware at a competitive price point if the start-up demands royalties on both hardware and games while refusing to subsidize.

Risks: Strong incumbents, execution difficulty.

The plan fails its own objective. To set a standard, you need ubiquity; to get ubiquity in this market, you need a low entry price. The proposed financial model makes a low price impossible.

DECISION: NO-GO
