First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to launch a high-friction, high-cost hardware product into a mass market without solving for core utility or social viability, relying on an "invitation-only" phase that does not mitigate the fundamental flaws in the value proposition.

**Steelman:** An invitation-only launch at $1,500 allows the company to validate demand among tech enthusiasts, gather privacy feedback, and refine the product before a general consumer release, positioning it as a futuristic everyday device.

**Attack:**
1.  **Utility Gap:** The proposal admits there is "no clear everyday use for most people." Selling a $1,500 device without a defined killer app is not a strategy; it is a gamble on users inventing utility post-purchase. Early adopters buy novelty, but the path to "everyday consumer product" requires immediate, obvious utility which is explicitly absent.
2.  **Social & Privacy Blockers:** The risks of "privacy objections" and "social acceptance" are not teething issues; they are existential threats for a always-on camera. An invitation-only program does not solve the "Glasshole" phenomenon; it may amplify it by creating an perceived elite class wearing recording devices in public spaces.
3.  **Price vs. Value:** At $1,500 (2013 dollars), the price point competes with high-end laptops and tablets which have established utility. Without a clear use case, the price is unjustifiable regardless of the "early adopter" label.
4.  **Battery Life:** Short battery life renders an "everyday" product unusable. If the device cannot last a day, it fails its primary positioning.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids brand damage from a failed public rollout and privacy backlash.
*   **Enterprise Pivot:** Target specific industrial or medical use cases where the camera and hands-free operation have clear ROI, bypassing social acceptance issues.
*   **Developer Kit Only:** Sell strictly to developers at cost to build the ecosystem before attempting a consumer product sale.

**Conclusion:** The proposal fails the "must beat do nothing" test. The stated risks (privacy, social acceptance, lack of use) are not merely potential hurdles; they are confirmed deficits in the current plan that the proposed launch strategy does not resolve. Launching this as an "everyday consumer product" is premature and likely to result in significant financial loss and reputational harm. The lack of a clear use case is a blocker, not a risk to be managed later.

DECISION: NO-GO
