First call: NO-GO. The plan relies on selling products below cost to buy growth while burning cash on TV and acquisitions, a strategy that fails if capital markets tighten or shipping costs remain high.

Steelman: Aggressively capture market share now to establish a monopoly, using public funds to subsidize losses and eliminate the largest rival before competitors do.

Options:
1. **Proposal:** High burn, negative margins, massive debt/equity dilution. Irreversible if markets close.
2. **Do Nothing:** Preserve cash, fix unit economics, grow organically. Reversible.
3. **Niche Focus:** Target high-margin segments only. Lower risk, slower scale.
The proposal does not beat "do nothing" because it assumes infinite capital availability despite explicit risks of market closure.

Fit: Assumes the north star is "size at all costs." If the true north star is "sustainable business," this fails immediately.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-IPO, the stock crashes as investors realize each sale loses money. The TV campaign fails to convert profitable long-term users. Warehouses sit half-empty. Capital markets freeze, preventing a secondary raise. The company cannot pay suppliers or shipping bills and collapses. This is a one-way door; once public and leveraged, retreat is fatal. Cost of delay: Minimal; competitors are also bleeding cash and may fail first if they overextend.

Evidence: The proposal admits to "negative gross margins" and "high customer-acquisition cost." Selling below cost guarantees that revenue does not cover variable costs, let alone fixed costs like warehouses and TV ads. The math dictates that scaling this model accelerates bankruptcy unless new capital arrives continuously.

Blockers:
1. **Unit Economics:** Selling below cost with heavy shipping expenses means every additional customer increases total losses. No path to profitability is shown.
2. **Capital Assumption:** The plan requires continuous capital inflow for ops and ads, yet explicitly lists "capital markets could close" as a risk. If markets close, the company insolvates immediately.
3. **Margin Structure:** Negative gross margins cannot be fixed by volume; they worsen it.

Risks: Competitor retaliation, shipping cost volatility, ad inefficiency. These are secondary to the fundamental math failure.

Kill criteria: Any plan requiring negative gross margins and continuous capital raises in a volatile market without a proven path to positive unit economics is a blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
