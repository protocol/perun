First call: GO. The stated facts show a dominant library, a clear path to undercut the market leader on price, and a management willingness to fund losses to capture tens of millions of subscribers. The strategic logic of capturing direct customer relationships and recurring revenue outweighs the known risks of transition.

Steelman: Leveraging an unmatched content moat to disrupt the market via aggressive pricing will force rapid scale, making the temporary loss of licensing revenue and high cash burn a necessary investment for long-term dominance.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain licensing fees but cede direct consumer ownership to rivals, risking irrelevance as the market shifts to direct-to-consumer.
2. Hybrid model: Keep some licensing while launching a premium service. This dilutes the value proposition and slows subscriber growth, failing to reach the critical mass needed to compete.
3. The Proposal: Beats "do nothing" by securing the future business model; beats hybrid by maximizing speed to scale.

Fit: Aligns with the north star of owning the customer relationship in a shifting distribution landscape.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if technology execution collapses under load or if content costs spiral faster than subscriber acquisition. This is a two-way door regarding price, but a one-way door regarding content withdrawal. Delay costs immediate market share to entrenched competitors.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the stated fact of a "major library" and "billions" in current licensing. The math holds if the lifetime value of a direct subscriber exceeds the net present value of licensing fees, which is the core bet.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (cash burn, tech execution, cannibalization) are inherent to the strategy, not factual contradictions. The company has the capital to fund the loss; the tech risk is executable via hiring/building; cannibalization is the intended mechanism of value capture.

Risks: Execution complexity and speed of adoption. These become kill criteria: if subscriber growth misses targets by >20% in Year 2, or if tech downtime exceeds 1%, pivot to a hybrid model.

The strongest attack—that losing guaranteed licensing revenue for uncertain subscriptions is financially reckless—fails because the status quo guarantees long-term decline as rivals build their own libraries. The only path to preserving the asset's value is to control its distribution.

DECISION: GO
