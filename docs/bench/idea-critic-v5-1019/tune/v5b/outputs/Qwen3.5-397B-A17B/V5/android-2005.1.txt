First call: NO-GO. A $50M acquisition of a six-person team with no revenue, no hardware, and no carrier relationships contradicts the core competency of a search-advertising company in 2005.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the OS secures the future mobile search interface before competitors, using open-source leverage to bypass carrier gatekeepers and drive long-term ad volume.

**Attack:**
1.  **Strategic Fit:** The company sells ads, not operating systems. It lacks the DNA to manage a multi-year OS development cycle, hardware partnerships, or carrier negotiations. The distraction from the core search business is immediate and severe.
2.  **Market Reality:** In 2005, carriers and handset makers (Nokia, Motorola) hold absolute power. They will not install an unproven, ad-centric OS from a search vendor on their devices. The "open source" angle does not solve the distribution bottleneck; it merely gives the code away without guaranteeing adoption.
3.  **Financials:** $50M for six people is an astronomical valuation for a pre-revenue project with no clear path to monetization other than a theoretical future increase in search queries. The payback period is indefinite.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A cheaper alternative is to build a mobile-optimized search website, which works on existing phones immediately without needing an OS or carrier approval. This captures the ad revenue opportunity without the massive operational overhead of building a phone platform.
5.  **Pre-mortem:** The project fails because handset makers refuse to pre-load the OS, carriers block it as bloatware, and the team burns cash for five years while the core search business loses focus. The result is a write-off and a stalled mobile strategy.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capability Mismatch:** The acquirer has zero experience in OS development or hardware supply chains, making successful integration and execution highly improbable based on stated facts.
*   **Distribution Impossible:** The proposal admits carriers and handset makers have "no reason to adopt" the platform; without distribution, the bet on increased search volume cannot materialize.

**Risks:** Multi-year burn, cultural clash, opportunity cost of ignoring web-based mobile search.

The proposal relies on a chain of optimistic assumptions (adoption, carrier cooperation, successful OS build) that contradict the stated market realities. The cheaper, safer path to mobile ad revenue is optimizing for existing browsers, not buying an OS.

DECISION: NO-GO
