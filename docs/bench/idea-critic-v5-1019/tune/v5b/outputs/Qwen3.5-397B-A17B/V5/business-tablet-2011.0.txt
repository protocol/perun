First call: NO-GO. The proposal abandons the company's core strength (business email) in its most vulnerable moment, offering a premium-priced device with a critical functional deficit against a dominant rival.

**Steelman:** Launch a niche, phone-tethered 7-inch tablet on an untested OS to establish a new form factor quickly, betting that hardware differentiation outweighs the lack of native enterprise apps and ecosystem maturity.

**Attack:**
1.  **Strategic Contradiction:** The company leads in business email, yet the device ships without built-in email, calendar, or contacts unless paired with a phone. This alienates the exact customer base the company dominates. A business tablet without native PIM (Personal Information Management) tools is non-functional for its target market.
2.  **Pricing Failure:** Matching the price of a rival's larger, established tablet with a mature app store, while offering a smaller screen, an untested OS, and missing core features, creates no value proposition. Customers pay more for less.
3.  **Ecosystem Risk:** Developers are unlikely to commit to an untested OS for a niche device, especially when the primary use case (business) is crippled by missing native apps. This creates a death spiral: no apps mean no users, which means no developers.
4.  **Better Way:** The "do nothing" or "wait" option is superior to launching a flawed product that damages the brand's enterprise reputation. Alternatively, delay launch until native email/calendar are integrated, or price significantly lower to compensate for the ecosystem gap.

**Pre-mortem:** The device launches, reviewers pan the lack of email and high price, enterprise IT departments reject it due to management hurdles, developers ignore the platform, and the project is discontinued within a year, wasting the OS acquisition cost.

**Blockers:**
*   **Core Feature Missing:** Shipping a business-oriented device without built-in email/calendar contradicts the company's market position and customer needs.
*   **Price/Value Mismatch:** Pricing equal to a superior competitor (larger screen, mature OS, full app suite) ensures zero competitive advantage.

**Decision:** The proposal fails on its own stated facts. It surrenders the company's primary moat (email) while charging a premium for an inferior, untested product.

DECISION: NO-GO
