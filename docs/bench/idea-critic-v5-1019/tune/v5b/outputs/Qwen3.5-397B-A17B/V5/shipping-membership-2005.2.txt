First call: NO-GO. The proposal admits unproven economics and a large short-term margin hit without a defined cap or pilot to validate the loss ratio.

Steelman: A $79 annual fee unlocks sufficient order frequency and loyalty to offset the marginal cost of free two-day shipping, creating a net positive lifetime value despite immediate margin compression.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Unproven Economics:** The proposal explicitly states "economics are unproven" while committing to a "large short-term margin hit." Launching a company-wide financial liability without a validated unit model is a factual failure of the plan as proposed.
2.  **Irreversibility Risk:** The plan acknowledges the offer is "hard to reverse" once sold. Committing to an irreversible path with unproven math violates basic capital preservation.

**Risks:**
*   Heavy buyers exploiting the fee, causing losses to exceed revenue.
*   Inability to pivot if adoption curves differ from projections.

**Kill Check:**
*   *Core Claim:* Will buyers pay enough extra in frequency to cover shipping? **Unproven.**
*   *Evidence Support:* Does the proposal show data supporting profitability? **No**, it states the opposite.
*   *Irreversibility:* Is the commitment too large to stop? **Yes**, annual memberships create contractual obligations that cannot be easily revoked mid-term without reputational damage.

Three yeses make this a blocker. The proposal asks to bet the margin on an unproven hypothesis with high reversibility costs. A sound operator would demand a capped regional pilot or a provisional term limit before a full launch. Since the proposal offers neither a pilot nor a cap, and explicitly flags the economics as unknown, it fails the blocker test.

The absence of a "test phase" in the proposal is the fatal flaw. You cannot approve a permanent structural change to the P&L based on a hypothesis that admits it is unproven. The correct move is to design a test, not launch the product.

DECISION: NO-GO
