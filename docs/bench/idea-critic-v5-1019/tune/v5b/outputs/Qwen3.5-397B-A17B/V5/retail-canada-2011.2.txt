First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a massive, irreversible capital expenditure (130–150 stores) before validating the core claim that the retailer's domestic value proposition translates to a foreign market with different competitors and regulations.

**Steelman:** Rapidly securing 150 sites creates an immediate scale advantage against the entrenched competitor, leveraging existing lease availability to lock out rivals before they can react.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capability Mismatch:** The retailer admits it must "learn" cross-border logistics, regulation, and bilingual labeling. Simultaneously launching a complex new supply chain and inventory system while learning these fundamentals creates a high probability of operational collapse. You cannot learn to run a marathon while inventing new running shoes.
2.  **Unproven Demand:** The plan assumes the "loyal domestic following" extends across the border or that price sensitivity works identically in a market with different incumbents. No pilot data exists to prove customers will switch from the entrenched competitor.
3.  **Alternative:** A "beachhead" strategy opening 5–10 stores would validate the supply chain, regulatory compliance, and price perception with <10% of the capital risk. If the model works, expansion follows; if it fails, the company retains 90% of its capital. The current plan offers no such reversibility.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** In 24 months, the company has opened 100 stores but faces chronic stockouts due to unproven logistics, regulatory fines for labeling errors, and an inventory system that cannot handle local nuances. Cash flow turns negative as stores underperform against the entrenched rival's pricing power.

**Blockers:**
*   **Operational Inversion:** The plan requires mastering unknown variables (logistics/regulation) at maximum scale immediately. This contradicts basic operational hygiene.
*   **Lack of Validation:** Committing to ~150 leases before proving the unit economics in even one store is a bet on luck, not strategy.
*   **System Risk:** Deploying a "new inventory-management system" concurrently with a new international supply chain introduces coupled failure points that cannot be isolated or debugged during a blitz rollout.

The absence of a pilot, the coupling of unproven systems with maximum scale, and the lack of evidence that the value prop transfers constitute fatal flaws.

DECISION: NO-GO
