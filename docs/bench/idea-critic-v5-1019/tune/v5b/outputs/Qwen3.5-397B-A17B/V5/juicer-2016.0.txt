First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and behavioral friction of a $699 connected juicer relying on proprietary, subscription-only inputs create an insurmountable barrier to mass adoption in 2016.

**Steelman:** A premium, hassle-free health appliance where the recurring revenue from proprietary pods subsidizes hardware R&D, justified by freshness verification and convenience.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price & Value Mismatch:** At $699, the device costs more than high-end blenders and competitors' juicers that accept *any* produce. The subscription model locks users into a per-serving cost significantly higher than grocery store produce, violating basic consumer rationality for a commodity category.
2.  **Unnecessary Complexity:** Requiring Wi-Fi to verify "freshness" of a physical bag introduces a single point of failure (network outage, server shutdown) for a mechanical function. This creates anxiety rather than convenience.
3.  **Market Reality:** The "single-serve pod" model works for coffee (low hardware cost, high margin on stale beans) but fails for fresh food where users value visual inspection and price flexibility. The addressable market for users willing to pay a 300% premium for pre-chopped fruit is likely too small to sustain a "well-funded" hardware startup's burn rate.
4.  **Alternatives:** A non-connected version accepting generic bags or fresh produce would lower the price point and remove the "walled garden" resentment, increasing total addressable market.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Viability:** The proposed price structure (high CapEx + high OpEx) contradicts established consumer behavior in the kitchen appliance sector.
*   **Feature Risk:** Mandatory connectivity for a core safety/freshness feature is a reliability liability that offers no compensating upside to the user.

**Risks:** Supply chain complexity for fresh produce; high churn if subscription costs accumulate; rapid obsolescence if the app service ends.

**Kill Criteria:** If pilot data shows customer acquisition cost exceeds $300 or if monthly churn exceeds 5% due to pod pricing, the model is invalid.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The argument that "convenience justifies premium" was tested against the $699 entry barrier; convenience rarely overcomes a 3x-4x price premium in commoditized food prep without a patented health outcome, which is not claimed here.

DECISION: NO-GO
