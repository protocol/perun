First call: NO-GO. The proposed budget (10x average short) exceeds the studio's entire steady-but-thin profit capacity, creating an immediate liquidity crisis where a single failure triggers insolvency.

**Steelman:** A feature film unlocks a new, higher-margin revenue tier that shorts cannot access, justifying the risk if the studio can survive production.

**Attack:**
1.  **Financial Blocker:** Funding a 10x cost project from "steady but thin" earnings implies diverting 100% of cash flow for years. This halts short production, killing the very revenue stream funding the gamble. There is no working capital left for operations or marketing.
2.  **Market Risk:** No precedent exists for feature-length animation. If audiences reject the format (a high probability given insider advice), the studio loses its entire reserve with no fallback.
3.  **Better Way:** The proposal ignores staged validation. A medium-length special (3-4x cost) could test audience endurance and distribution channels without betting the entire company on the first attempt.
4.  **Fit:** While the vision aligns with growth, the execution method (all-in funding from operations) contradicts basic survival constraints.

**Pre-mortem:** The project reaches 80% completion; funds are exhausted; shorts have ceased production; the studio cannot finish the film or pay staff, leading to bankruptcy before release.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capital Insufficiency:** The funding model (internal cash flow) mathematically cannot support a 10x cost spike without ceasing operations.
*   **Single Point of Failure:** No alternative financing or partial-release strategy is offered to mitigate the "all-or-nothing" risk.

**Risks:** Audience rejection, cost overruns, talent drain.

**Kill Criteria:** Proceed only if external financing covers at least 50% of the budget, or if a pilot special proves audience retention >60 minutes. Without these, the risk is existential, not strategic.

The proposal fails because it treats a venture-capital-scale risk with a small-business balance sheet. It demands a "go" decision on a plan that guarantees operational collapse before the product reaches the market. The lack of a phased approach or external capital makes this a suicide mission, not a bold strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
