First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a scale deficit by acquiring a second declining asset, compounding execution risk without addressing the core lack of carrier expertise in consumer media.

**Steelman:** Combining two distressed media portfolios creates sufficient scale to compete with dominant ad platforms by leveraging the carrier's distribution, despite the target's decline.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Capability:** The acquirer has "no history in consumer media" and is attempting to integrate a target with "repeated management changes" and falling revenue. Adding a second failing culture to an inexperienced operator increases integration failure probability, not success.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. The "cheaper alternative" is investing in the carrier's own native ad tools or partnering, rather than buying a $4.8B liability. A declining asset rarely provides the technology or talent needed to leapfrog incumbents.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The integration drags on due to cultural clashes and legacy tech debt. Ad revenue continues to fall faster than synergies can be realized, leaving the carrier with a massive write-down and no competitive moat against the two dominant platforms.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the target's traffic and revenue are "falling for years." Buying a falling knife to fix a lack of expertise is a speculative gamble, not a strategic bridge.

**Blockers:**
*   **Capability Mismatch:** The acquirer lacks the fundamental domain expertise ("no history") required to turn around a complex, failing media business.
*   **Negative Momentum:** The target is in structural decline with unstable leadership; the plan offers no specific mechanism to reverse this trend other than "scale," which is unproven in this context.

**Risks:**
*   Integration failure due to cultural/technical debt.
*   Continued ad-revenue decline outpacing synergies.
*   Distraction from core wireless business.

**Kill Criteria:**
*   If ad revenue does not stabilize within 12 months post-close, initiate divestiture.
*   If key engineering talent retention drops below 80% in Q1, halt integration spending.

The plan relies on hope that two negatives make a positive, ignoring the acquirer's inability to manage the asset class.

DECISION: NO-GO
