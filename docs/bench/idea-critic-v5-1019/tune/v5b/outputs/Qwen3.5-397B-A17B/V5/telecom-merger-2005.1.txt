First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits $36 billion to acquire a network explicitly described as incompatible and data-limited, requiring the buyer to operate a legacy burden that contradicts the industry's forward trajectory toward data convergence.

Steelman: The acquisition secures a sticky, high-value enterprise segment via a unique push-to-talk feature that the buyer cannot currently replicate, justifying the integration headache as a defensive moat against churn.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoid $36B debt and integration risk, but cede the enterprise niche to competitors who might buy the target instead.
2. Build/Buy Feature Only: License or develop push-to-talk on the buyer's native network. Cost is lower, risk is technical execution, but it lacks the immediate installed base.
3. The Proposal: Highest cost, highest technical risk (dual networks), maximum goodwill exposure. It fails to beat "do nothing" on capital efficiency because the core asset (the network) is a liability, and the core value (customers) can potentially be migrated only if the feature works on the buyer's tech—a premise unproven here.

Fit: In 2005, the North Star for wireless is data adoption (3G/4G transition). Acquiring a "data-limited" network creates a strategic drag, forcing capital expenditure on maintaining obsolete technology rather than advancing the unified data vision.

Pre-mortem: The integration stalls due to technical incompatibility; the legacy network degrades service for both bases; the $36B price tag creates a debt overhang that prevents competitive pricing or infrastructure upgrades, leading to mass enterprise churn once the service instability becomes apparent. The "unique" feature fails to migrate, rendering the customer base worthless.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the technologies are "incompatible" and the target is "limited for data." No evidence suggests the push-to-talk service can be replicated on the buyer's superior network.

Blockers:
1. Strategic Misalignment: Buying a "data-limited" network in 2005 directly opposes the industry's critical pivot to data, creating an immediate technical debt trap.
2. Unproven Migration: The value rests on retaining customers via a feature tied to incompatible hardware; without proof this feature can survive the migration, the $36B pays only for a transient subscriber list likely to churn during integration.

Risks: Integration cost overruns, goodwill impairment, dual-network operational complexity.

DECISION: NO-GO
