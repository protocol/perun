First call: GO. The proposal targets an unserved mass market (casual/non-gamers) by removing the primary barrier to entry (high cost and complexity) rather than fighting a losing battle for hardware supremacy against entrenched leaders.

**Steelman:** By sacrificing raw power and hardcore appeal, the console creates a new, blue-ocean category where it faces no direct competition, relying on volume sales of a unique, intuitive interface to offset thin hardware margins.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Remain a distant third in a saturated high-definition race; likely eventual exit from the market.
2.  **Compete on power:** Attempt to match rivals' specs; requires massive R&D spend with high risk of still finishing third due to developer ecosystem lock-in.
3.  **Proposed path:** Low-cost, motion-based entry. Highest risk of cultural rejection, but the only path to market leadership via expansion rather than displacement.

**Fit:** Aligns with expanding the total addressable market beyond the shrinking pool of traditional gamers. Assumes the "casual" segment is large enough to sustain the business model.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the motion controller is perceived as a gimmick rather than a genuine input method, or if third-party developers refuse to build software despite the installed base, leaving the system with no "killer app" beyond the launch title. This is a two-way door; the hardware can be discounted further or pivoted, but brand perception as a "toy" may be hard to reverse.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the hardware margin is thin, implying a razor-and-blades model dependent on software attachment rates. The skepticism from hardcore gamers is irrelevant if the target demographic does not overlap with them.

**Blockers:** NONE. The risks cited (limited third-party support, toy perception) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The lack of high-definition output is a deliberate feature to reduce cost, not a flaw. No existing fact proves the casual audience will not buy; it is untested, which is the nature of the bet.

**Risks:** Developer adoption lag; inability to shed "toy" label later; low software attach rate.

**Kill Criteria:** If software attachment rates fall below 3 titles per console within 12 months, or if the installed base does not reach 20 million units within 18 months, the strategy must pivot to cost-cutting or licensing the controller technology.

DECISION: GO
