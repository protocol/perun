First call: GO. The price point and battery life create a distinct category (portability/affordability) rather than fighting a spec war the company previously lost.

Steelman: Win by offering a durable, ultra-portable, affordable device with a killer app, ignoring the "color/high-power" arms race to capture the mass market.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Cede the portable market to the rival; risk long-term brand erosion.
2. High-spec color handheld: High cost, poor battery, direct competition with the rival's strengths; likely repeat of past single-game failures.
3. Proposed monochrome device: Low entry price ($89.95), superior battery, bundled software drives adoption. Beats "do nothing" by establishing a new ecosystem; beats high-spec by avoiding a losing hardware battle.

Fit: Aligns with a strategy of mass-market penetration and software attachment rates over hardware prestige.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if third-party developers ignore the platform due to perceived hardware weakness, or if the "single-game" stigma prevents perception of it as a true system. The $20M marketing spend is the door-opener; if it fails to shift perception from "toy" to "system," the hardware specs become irrelevant. This is a two-way door; a failed launch allows a pivot or exit with limited long-term damage compared to a costly high-spec failure. Cost of delay: The rival solidifies its user base and developer loyalty, making future entry prohibitively expensive.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the assumption that $89.95 is a psychological threshold for impulse buys and that battery life outweighs screen quality for the target demographic. The bundled puzzle game is critical; without it, the hardware is just a screen.

Blockers: NONE. The risks (monochrome, modest specs) are features of the cost strategy, not contradictions of it. The "thin margins" risk is mitigated by the software bundle and volume sales model inherent to the low price.

Risks: Developer adoption; consumer perception of "cheap" vs. "smart." These become kill criteria: if software library does not grow within 6 months, or if sales miss 50% of forecast in Q1, the project must pivot or cease hardware production.

DECISION: GO
