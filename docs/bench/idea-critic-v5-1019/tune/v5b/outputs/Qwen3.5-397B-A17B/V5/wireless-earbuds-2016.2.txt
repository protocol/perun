First call: GO. The removal of the headphone jack creates an immediate, forced market for a wireless alternative; delaying the solution cedes the ecosystem to third parties and validates customer anger without offering a path forward.

Steelman: Launching a proprietary, seamless wireless audio ecosystem at the exact moment the legacy port is removed transforms a perceived loss (the jack) into a premium upgrade, locking users into the hardware ecosystem through convenience rather than just necessity.

Alternatives considered:
1. Do nothing: Leaves customers with no official audio solution, guaranteeing backlash and churn to competitors with jacks or better accessories.
2. Sell wired dongles only: Admits defeat, frames the phone as broken, and fails to monetize the transition or drive ecosystem stickiness.
3. Delay launch: Allows third-party competitors to establish the standard before the official solution arrives.

Fit: This moves the north star of ecosystem integration by making the audio experience exclusive to the brand's devices.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if the battery life (5 hours) is perceived as unusable for daily commutes or if the "odd" design becomes a cultural punchline that overshadows utility. However, these are execution risks, not strategic blockers. The cost of delay is losing the narrative control of the transition.

Evidence: The proposal states the jack is being removed regardless of this product's status. The $159 price point is high but justifiable by the proprietary chip's pairing speed and the charging case utility, which solves the battery anxiety.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (design ridicule, loss, price, battery) are inherent to pioneering a new category, not facts that prove the plan fails. The anger over the jack is inevitable; the only variable is whether the company provides the solution.

Risks: High price sensitivity and battery anxiety. These become kill criteria: if pre-orders miss targets by >40% due to price, or if return rates exceed 15% due to battery loss, pivot to a lower-cost tier or bundle immediately.

The strongest attack survived is the price objection: while $159 is steep compared to free wired ears, the value prop is the *only* seamless wireless experience on the new device. Without this product, the "free" wired option is gone entirely, making the comparison invalid.

DECISION: GO
