First call: NO-GO. The $1,000 price point creates an immediate market mismatch against cheaper consoles and increasingly capable PCs, while the value proposition remains undefined to the consumer.

**Steelman:** A premium, dedicated hub for interactive living room media is viable if it offers a unique experience PCs and consoles cannot match, justifying the high entry cost.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $1,000, the device costs significantly more than game consoles and competes directly with full personal computers. Without a killer app library, the hardware is an expensive solution to a non-existent problem.
2.  **Category Confusion:** Positioned "between" a console and a PC, the device risks appealing to neither. Gamers will choose cheaper dedicated consoles; productivity users will choose versatile PCs. The "interactive disc" format lacks a clear identity compared to established music or photo standards.
3.  **Content Chicken-and-Egg:** A small library of interactive titles provides insufficient incentive for early adopters to spend $1,000. Developers will not invest in a niche platform with low install bases, creating a death spiral.
4.  **Substitution Threat:** As PCs rapidly gain native multimedia capabilities, the unique selling proposition of a dedicated player erodes monthly. Waiting six months might yield a PC that does this and more for a similar total household cost.

**Blockers:**
*   **Economic Mismatch:** The stated price ($1,000) exceeds the perceived value of a single-function media player when multi-function alternatives (PCs) exist at comparable prices.
*   **Undefined Audience:** The proposal admits buyers "may not understand what the product is for." Launching a premium hardware product with an unclear value proposition is a guaranteed failure mode.

**Risks:** Small title library, rapid PC commoditization, high manufacturing costs preventing price drops.

**Kill Criteria:** If a market survey shows less than 15% of target users can articulate the device's primary use case without prompting, or if fewer than 50 "killer app" titles are committed for launch, the project must stop.

**Strongest Attack Survived:** The argument that "premium positioning creates a new category" fails because the price gap is too wide to bridge without a clearly understood, indispensable utility, which the proposal explicitly admits is missing.

DECISION: NO-GO
