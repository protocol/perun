First call: GO. The friction of per-order fees directly suppresses repeat purchase frequency, which is the primary lever for lifetime value in retail. Removing this friction via a fixed upfront fee aligns customer incentives with volume, even if marginal shipping costs rise.

**Steelman:** An annual prepaid fee converts variable transactional friction into a sunk cost for the customer, psychologically encouraging maximum order frequency to "amortize" the membership, thereby locking out competitors and increasing total gross profit despite lower per-order margins.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary objection is that heavy buyers will cause shipping costs to exceed the $79 revenue. However, this assumes the retailer cannot negotiate better carrier rates based on the guaranteed volume surge the membership creates. The alternative of "doing nothing" guarantees continued friction and churn to any competitor who solves it. A "free shipping threshold" (e.g., orders over $50) is a weaker alternative; it still penalizes small, frequent top-up orders which are common in retail, whereas the membership captures the entire share-of-wallet.

**Pre-mortem & Door:**
If this fails, it is because the uptake rate is too low to generate the volume needed to renegotiate carrier contracts, leaving the retailer paying premium shipping rates on frequent small orders without the offsetting revenue scale. This is a one-way door for the launch year; once customers pay $79, revoking the benefit mid-year is impossible without massive reputational damage. The cost of delay is high: if a competitor launches first, they capture the loyal segment permanently.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
There are no factual blockers in the proposal. The "unproven economics" is a risk, not a blocker, as the $79 fee provides immediate cash flow to offset initial margin hits. The risk of heavy usage is actually the desired outcome for inventory turnover and customer lock-in. The only missing element is a kill criterion: if the attachment rate is below 5% after six months, or if the loss per member exceeds $20, the program must be restructured.

**Decision:**
The strategic necessity of removing friction outweighs the short-term margin risk. The model shifts revenue recognition upfront and secures loyalty.

Blockers: NONE
Risks: High uptake leading to negative unit economics if carrier rates aren't renegotiated; irreversibility within the contract year.

DECISION: GO
