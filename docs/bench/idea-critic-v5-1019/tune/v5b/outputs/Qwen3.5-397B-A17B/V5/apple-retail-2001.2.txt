First call: NO-GO. A loss-making manufacturer with single-digit share proposing high-fixed-cost retail in a low-margin sector during a recession violates basic capital preservation.

Steelman: Direct retail is the only viable channel to demonstrate the integrated music strategy and stop third-party staff from steering customers to cheaper rivals, potentially saving the brand.

Options:
1. **Proposed:** Build mall stores. High cost, high risk, irreversible leases, negative immediate cash flow.
2. **Do Nothing:** Continue losing share to resellers; brand erodes slowly but cash burn remains low.
3. **Better Way:** Create "shop-in-shop" branded zones within existing retailer footprints or launch a direct online configuration tool. Both demonstrate the product without massive lease liability or channel warfare.

Fit: While the "digital music strategy" requires demonstration, the proposed vehicle (own stores) contradicts the company's current financial fragility. A strategy requiring solvency cannot be funded by a company nearing insolvency.

Pre-mortem: The recession deepens; foot traffic drops. Fixed leases become untenable while inventory sits unsold. The company burns through remaining cash reserves on rent rather than R&D, forcing bankruptcy within 18 months. Resellers, feeling threatened, deprioritize the brand further.

Evidence: The proposal admits "recent run of losses," "low margins," and "high lease costs." Mathematically, adding high fixed costs to a loss-making operation with volatile demand is a solvent killer. No evidence suggests the margin lift from direct sales offsets the immediate cash drain of build-outs and leases in a downturn.

Blockers:
1. **Financial Mismatch:** The company is currently losing money; the plan adds massive fixed overhead (leases) in a cyclical downturn. This creates an immediate liquidity crisis.
2. **Channel Suicide:** Alienating the only current sales force (resellers) without a guaranteed replacement volume invites immediate revenue collapse.

Risks: Execution inexperience, inventory obsolescence, recessionary demand shock.

The financial structure of this plan guarantees failure given the stated losses and macro environment. The strategic goal (demonstration) is valid, but the tactical vehicle (own stores) is fatal.

DECISION: NO-GO
