First call: NO-GO. Releasing an unfinished, unsupported game to a paying public without marketing or a support plan invites immediate reputational damage that a solo developer cannot recover from.

Steelman: Early revenue funds development while real user data prioritizes the most valuable features, creating a community-driven loop.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Finish the game privately. Risk: Building features nobody wants. Cost: Time.
2. Free closed beta: Recruit testers without financial transaction. Risk: Less committed feedback. Cost: Management overhead.
3. Proposed paid alpha: Immediate cash flow. Risk: High churn, negative reviews, support overload.

Fit: Assumes "survival via cash" outweighs "long-term brand viability." In 2009, the "Early Access" model was not yet normalized; players expected finished products or distinct demos, not broken paid alphas.

Pre-mortem: The game launches. Players encounter bugs and lack of content. Unable to support the influx, the developer ignores forums. Negative reviews accumulate on distribution platforms. The game is flagged as "abandoned" or "scam," destroying future sales potential even if completed later.

Evidence Check: The proposal admits the game is "unfinished" and the developer "cannot support a large audience." These are stated facts, not risks. Selling a product you cannot support is a fundamental breach of the buyer-seller contract, especially pre-2013 when consumer protection in digital distribution was stricter regarding "finished" goods.

Blockers:
1. **Capacity Mismatch:** The proposal explicitly states the developer "cannot support a large audience" yet proposes a public sale which inherently invites one. This is a self-contradiction.
2. **Market Timing:** In 2009, the market tolerance for paid unfinished games was near zero outside of specific niche communities not mentioned here.
3. **Irreversible Reputation:** Negative reviews from a botched paid alpha are permanent and will suppress sales of the final product.

Risks: Running out of money before completion; feature creep based on vocal minority.

The lack of a support plan and the explicit admission of inability to handle audience scale makes a public paid release fatal. A free closed beta or seeking a publisher must precede any sale.

DECISION: NO-GO
