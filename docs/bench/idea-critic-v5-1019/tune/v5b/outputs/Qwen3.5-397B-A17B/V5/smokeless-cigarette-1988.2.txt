First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to launch and promote widely based on test markets that have not yet demonstrated consumer acceptance, while acknowledged risks directly threaten the core value proposition.

**Steelman:** A heated tobacco product offers a cleaner alternative with less smoke, potentially retaining smokers seeking reduced harm without quitting nicotine, justifying the sunk development costs if adoption occurs.

**Attack:** The claim fails the blocker test on current facts. The proposal admits smokers "may dislike the taste" and find the lighting mechanism "complicated." Since taste and ease of use are the primary drivers of cigarette adoption, a product inferior on these dimensions cannot succeed in a wide launch. Promoting a complex, bad-tasting product widely invites immediate market rejection and wastes further capital. Furthermore, promoting "health" benefits invites regulatory scrutiny that could ban the product entirely before it gains traction. The "very large sum" already spent is a sunk cost; throwing good money after bad by launching a flawed product is irrational.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Preserve capital, avoid regulatory backlash, and accept the sunk cost loss.
2.  **Iterate:** Fix the taste and lighting mechanism before any launch.
3.  **Niche Pilot:** Test in a single jurisdiction without health claims to gauge real-world usage.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches, smokers try it once, hate the taste and complexity, and return to standard cigarettes. Regulators sue over implied health claims. The brand suffers reputational damage, and the remaining inventory is written off.

**Blockers:**
*   **Core Claim Failure:** The proposal admits the product may fail on taste and usability, which are essential for repeat purchase. You cannot launch a consumer good that the target audience likely dislikes.
*   **Regulatory Trap:** Wide promotion of health benefits without explicit approval is a legal liability that could kill the category.

**Risks:** High execution complexity, consumer rejection, regulatory ban. These are not merely risks; the admitted potential for taste/usability failure is a blocker to a *wide launch*.

**Decision:** The plan to "launch and promote widely" is premature and dangerous given the admitted flaws. The sunk cost is irrelevant to the forward-looking decision.

DECISION: NO-GO
