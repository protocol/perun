First call: GO. The proposal targets an unserved demographic (youth) with a portable, affordable music experience, leveraging existing parts to minimize R&D risk despite high initial tooling costs.

**Steelman:** A dedicated playback-only device creates a new "personal music" category for youth, where portability and price outweigh the lack of recording or social sharing features.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **Dealer Skepticism:** Dealers project current hi-fi norms (recording/social) onto a new use case. This is a distribution education problem, not a product flaw.
2.  **Antisocial Concern:** Headphones isolate, but they also enable music consumption in previously impossible contexts (commuting, walking), creating value distinct from home hi-fi.
3.  **No Market Research:** No data exists because the category does not yet exist. Waiting for research cedes first-mover advantage.
4.  **Copycat Risk:** Using existing parts invites copying, but being first allows brand ownership of the "personal stereo" concept before competitors react.
5.  **Large Run Requirement:** High volume is necessary for the target price point. This is a binary bet: commit fully or don't enter.

**Pre-mortem:** The product fails if the price misses the youth budget or if the battery life/portability promise is not met. The lack of recording is actually a feature (smaller, cheaper, lighter), not a bug, once the value proposition shifts from "utility" to "lifestyle."

**Blockers:** None. The objections are based on extrapolating past behavior to a novel context, which is invalid for disruptive innovation. The risks (copying, dealer doubt) are manageable through speed to market and marketing.

**Risks:** Competitors copy quickly; inventory overhang if the trend fails. These are mitigated by the low cost of goods (existing parts) and the potential for high volume sales to offset margins.

**Kill Criteria:** If the first production run does not sell through to consumers (not just dealers) within 6 months, or if the unit cost cannot be maintained at the target price due to component scarcity, the line is killed.

**Decision:** The proposal creates a new market rather than fighting in an old one. The risks are inherent to innovation but do not block the attempt.

DECISION: GO
