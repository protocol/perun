First call: NO-GO. Releasing an unfinished, unsupported game to a paying public without marketing or a support plan invites immediate reputational damage and refund waves that a solo developer cannot survive.

**Steelman:** Early revenue funds development, and player feedback ensures market fit before over-building.
**Attack:** This assumes players will tolerate brokenness without support. In 2009, "paid alpha" was not a normalized contract; it was often viewed as selling vaporware. Without a publisher or budget, there is no buffer for the inevitable surge of bug reports. A solo dev fixing bugs cannot simultaneously build features, causing the "unfinished" state to persist longer, validating negative reviews.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Finish the game privately. Risk: Building features nobody wants.
2.  **Free Beta:** Release unfinished version for free to stress-test servers and gather feedback without financial transaction expectations.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Paid alpha. Risk: High. If the first 100 buyers hate the rough state, word-of-mouth kills the project permanently before it improves.

**Fit:** The goal is a successful game launch. This plan jeopardizes the brand before the product exists.

**Pre-mortem:** The game launches. Players encounter game-breaking bugs. They demand refunds. The developer is overwhelmed by support tickets, halting all coding. Steam/forum reviews turn negative within 48 hours. Sales stop. The project is abandoned.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits the game is "unfinished," the dev is "solo," and there is "no support" capacity. These are internal contradictions to a commercial release.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Support Capacity:** A solo dev cannot support a "large audience" of paying customers expecting a functional product. The proposal admits this inability.
2.  **Market Expectation:** Selling an unfinished product without a established trust framework (common in 2009) creates a high probability of immediate rejection.

**Risks:** Running out of money; building the wrong features. These are mitigated by a free beta, not a paid one.

**Kill Criteria:** If a free beta cannot retain 20% of users for one week, do not sell.

The lack of a support plan and the historical context of 2009 make a paid release fatal.

DECISION: NO-GO
