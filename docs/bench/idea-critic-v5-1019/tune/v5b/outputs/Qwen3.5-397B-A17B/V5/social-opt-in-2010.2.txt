First call: NO-GO. The plan trades core user trust for speculative engagement by violating established expectations of inbox privacy.

**Steelman:** Leveraging the existing user base via automatic enrollment with visible defaults is the only way to achieve critical mass quickly enough to compete with dedicated social networks before the window closes.

**Attack:**
1.  **Context Mismatch:** An inbox is a private utility, not a public square. Users expect confidentiality. Making connections visible by default violates the psychological contract of email, likely triggering immediate backlash and churn rather than engagement.
2.  **Regulatory & Reputational Risk:** Automatic enrollment without explicit consent invites regulator scrutiny and class-action risks regarding data usage. The cost of defending privacy violations exceeds the potential revenue from a social layer.
3.  **Reversibility Failure:** The proposal admits the launch is "hard to reverse once connections are exposed." Once private graphs are made public, the damage to brand trust is permanent, even if the feature is disabled. A two-way door is required for experimental features; this is a one-way door with negative upside.
4.  **Alternatives:** A "do nothing" approach preserves the core product's value. An opt-in model with strict private defaults tests demand without betting the company's reputation. The proposed "default visible" strategy assumes users want social discovery in email, a premise contradicted by the nature of the medium.

**Blockers:**
*   **Trust Violation:** The core mechanism (automatic enrollment + visible defaults) directly contradicts the primary value proposition of webmail (privacy).
*   **Irreversibility:** The inability to undo exposure of user data makes the risk profile unacceptable.
*   **Regulatory Exposure:** High probability of enforcement action based on the described data handling.

**Risks:** Low adoption, brand damage, user exodus. These are secondary to the blockers.

**Kill Criteria:** If user trust drops by >1% or regulatory inquiries occur within 30 days, terminate immediately. However, given the structural flaws, prevention is required over cure.

The plan fails because it attempts to force a social behavior onto a private utility using coercive defaults, risking the entire business for an unproven feature.

DECISION: NO-GO
