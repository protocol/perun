First call: GO. The proposal converts a philosophical stance ("web apps are sufficient") into a defensible market position by controlling the ecosystem while capturing value, directly addressing the risk of rival platforms outpacing feature velocity.

**Steelman:** A curated, revenue-sharing native app store is the only viable mechanism to scale functionality beyond web limits while maintaining brand polish and mitigating security/carrier risks through centralized review.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Reliance on web apps cedes the "native experience" advantage to rivals, likely resulting in rapid market share erosion as competitors leverage richer ecosystems.
2.  **Open Distribution:** Matching rivals' open models invites immediate security breaches and carrier backlash, potentially bricking devices or voiding network partnerships before the brand establishes trust.
3.  **The Proposal:** The 30% cut funds the review infrastructure required to ensure stability. Centralized approval acts as a quality filter, directly countering the "hurt product polish" concern. While review workload is high, it is a linear operational cost versus the exponential risk of unvetted code.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
This fits a stage where the hardware is proven, but the software ecosystem is the new battleground. If this fails, it will be because the review process becomes a bottleneck, stifling developer enthusiasm, or because the 30% fee is perceived as predatory compared to emerging alternatives. However, waiting a quarter to "perfect" the web experience guarantees losing the developer mindshare war. The cost of delay is total ecosystem irrelevance.

**Blockers & Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The concerns listed (security, carrier objections, workload) are operational challenges solved by the proposed review process, not factual contradictions. The existence of rival ecosystems validates the demand, making "do nothing" the riskiest path.
*   **Risks:** Review bottlenecks slowing time-to-market; developer revolt over fees; carrier friction. These are managed via the kill criterion of "time-to-approval" and revenue share adjustments if adoption lags.

The proposal correctly identifies that controlling the distribution channel is more valuable than the immediate margin of the 30% fee. It turns the CEO's previous skepticism into a strategic pivot without abandoning the core value proposition of a polished device.

DECISION: GO
