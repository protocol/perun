First call: NO-GO. The stated price is prohibitive relative to competitors, and performance is acknowledged as poor.

Steelman: This machine offers a revolutionary interface that could redefine office productivity if the market values intuition over raw speed or cost.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** Launch at $10k. Cost: Extreme. Risk: High (price/speed). Reversibility: Low (brand damage).
2. **Do Nothing:** Wait for hardware costs to drop. Cost: Zero. Risk: Miss first-mover advantage. Reversibility: High.
3. **Internal Pivot:** Adopt the cheaper internal alternative. Cost: Low. Risk: Cannibalization. Reversibility: High.

Fit: Assuming the goal is mass office adoption, a $10k price point excludes the vast majority of target users, especially when faster, cheaper business PCs exist.

Pre-mortem: The product launches and sits unsold. Offices refuse to pay a 3-4x premium for a slower machine with no software library. The internal cheaper project launches shortly after, making early adopters feel foolish and devaluing the brand.

Evidence: The proposal itself admits the price is "several times" higher than leaders, speed is "slow," and software is "little." These are not assumptions; they are stated deficits. No data suggests office workers will pay a massive premium for a mouse when text-based systems are faster and cheaper.

Blockers:
1. **Price vs. Value:** The cost is objectively non-competitive without a compensating advantage (speed/software), which the proposal admits is missing.
2. **Internal Alternative:** A cheaper, similar internal machine exists. Launching the expensive version first is fiscally irresponsible.
3. **Software Ecosystem:** Business utility depends on software; "little third-party software" renders the hardware useless for most office tasks.

Risks: Market rejection, brand damage, inventory write-downs.

The combination of high price, low performance, and lack of software creates a product that cannot compete. The existence of a cheaper internal alternative makes this specific configuration untenable as a launch product.

DECISION: NO-GO
