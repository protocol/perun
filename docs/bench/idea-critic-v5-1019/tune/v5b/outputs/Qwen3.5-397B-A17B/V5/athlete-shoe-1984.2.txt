First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits a large share of the marketing budget to an unproven rookie with high injury risk and regulatory uncertainty, violating basic capital preservation principles for a public company.

**Steelman:** Secure a generational talent before market pricing reflects his value, using controversy to generate free media coverage that offsets the high upfront cost and creates a distinct brand identity in basketball.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain status quo; likely continue losing share to established basketball brands with lower risk.
2.  **Sign established veteran:** Higher certainty of performance, lower "hype" potential, likely higher immediate cost, less narrative control.
3.  **Proposed Rookie Deal:** Highest upside if the player becomes a star; total loss if he fails or gets injured immediately.

**Fit:** Assumes the company's north star is "disruptive growth" rather than "stable market participation." If the latter, this fails immediately.

**Pre-mortem:** The player suffers a career-ending injury in the first month. The company is left with $5M+ in sunk costs, zero inventory movement, and no narrative. Alternatively, the league bans the shoes immediately, and the "controversy" fizzles without generating sustained sales, leaving the brand associated with rule-breaking rather than performance.

**Evidence:** No data exists on this specific player's professional viability (he hasn't played). The claim that "signature shoes rarely sell beyond a season" is a stated fact in the proposal that directly contradicts the long-term (5-year) investment thesis. The financial commitment is described as a "large share" of the budget, creating a single-point failure mode.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Contradictory Logic:** The proposal acknowledges signature shoes rarely last a season yet commits to a 5-year deal. This mismatch between historical data and the proposed term is a fundamental flaw in the financial model.
2.  **Capital Concentration:** Allocating a "large share" of the budget to a single unproven asset violates diversification basics unless the "do nothing" scenario is confirmed as existential bankruptcy (not stated).

**Risks:** Injury, league fines, consumer rejection of non-conforming colors, rookie underperformance. These are severe but manageable *if* the base logic held; however, the term-length contradiction blocks the deal as structured.

**Kill Criteria:** If the player does not play 60% of games in Year 1, or if the league issues a permanent ban on the specific colorways within 30 days, the deal must be renegotiated or terminated.

DECISION: NO-GO
