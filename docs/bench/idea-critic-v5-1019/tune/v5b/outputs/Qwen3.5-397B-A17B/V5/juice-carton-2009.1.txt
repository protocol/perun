First call: NO-GO. The proposal discards the brand's primary shelf cue—the orange with a straw—in a single, irreversible nationwide launch, directly contradicting the stated risk that shoppers rely on this image for recognition.

**Steelman:** A modernized design will attract new buyers and signal quality without alienating the core base if supported by heavy advertising.

**Attack:** The plan fails the "fit" and "evidence" tests. It explicitly acknowledges the buyer base is "loyal and older" and that the old image is the "main shelf cue," yet offers no pilot to verify if the new minimalist design works for this specific demographic. Replacing a proven visual anchor with an abstract one (glass, cap, rotated text) removes the fastest path to purchase for the existing revenue engine. The "large advertising campaign" is a costly assumption, not evidence that shelf recognition will survive the change.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Retain shelf recognition; miss potential modernization.
2.  **Proposed (Big Bang):** High risk of immediate sales collapse if recognition fails; unrecoverable cost of printed cartons.
3.  **Staged Pilot:** Test new design in limited regions/stores to measure recognition and sales lift before national commitment.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, sales have dropped 15%. Investigation reveals older loyalists walked past the cartons, failing to identify the brand quickly amidst competitors. The massive print run makes reversal impossible.

**Blocker:** The proposal admits the old image is the main cue and the base is older/loyal, yet mandates a simultaneous nationwide swap. This contradicts the core requirement of maintaining brand recognition for the existing revenue source. A "cheaper, safer alternative" (staged pilot) reaches the same upside (validation) without the catastrophic downside of a failed national launch.

**Decision:** The lack of a pilot for a high-risk visual change targeting a loyal, older demographic constitutes a blocker. The commitment is too large and irreversible given the stated risks.

**Kill Criteria (for future):** If a pilot is run, stop if week-4 repeat purchase rate drops >5% vs control.

DECISION: NO-GO
