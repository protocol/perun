First call: NO-GO. The proposal ignores the fundamental contradiction between "cola" and "clear," risking brand confusion and flagship cannibalization without a mitigation plan.

**Steelman:** A clear cola can succeed as a distinct "purer" sub-brand if it avoids flavor expectations tied to color and secures repeat buyers beyond novelty.

**Attack:**
1.  **Flavor-Color Association:** Consumers strongly associate dark color with specific cola taste. A clear liquid may be perceived as weak, flavored water, or a different category entirely, leading to immediate rejection upon trial despite positive test market curiosity.
2.  **Cannibalization vs. Extension:** Without distinct flavor profiling, this will likely steal share from the flagship rather than expand the total market. The proposal admits this risk but offers no strategy to prevent it.
3.  **Novelty Trap:** Test markets often overstate demand for novel visuals. If the product fails to deliver a unique taste experience matching the "pure" claim, repeat purchases will collapse once the visual novelty fades.
4.  **Competitive Response:** A nationwide launch invites immediate copycats. If the value proposition is solely visual, rivals can neutralize the advantage quickly, leaving the company with a fragmented portfolio.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve flagship dominance; avoid brand dilution.
*   **Limited Regional Rollout:** Test repeat purchase rates and flavor perception in a larger, diverse market before committing to national marketing spend.
*   **Repositioning:** Launch as a new beverage category (e.g., "clear citrus-cola blend") rather than a direct "clear cola" to manage flavor expectations.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch fails because consumers buy once for the look, hate the taste mismatch, and tell friends it's "watered-down cola." Sales plummet after Q1, damaging the flagship's premium image.

**Blockers:**
*   **Flavor-Color Mismatch:** The core claim (purer alternative) relies on visual differentiation that contradicts established sensory expectations for cola. No evidence is provided that the taste profile overcomes this.
*   **Cannibalization Risk:** The proposal admits this but proceeds without a firewall strategy.

**Risks:** Novelty fade, competitive copying, test market bias.

**Kill Criteria:** If repeat purchase rate in test markets is <20% after 60 days, or if flagship sales drop >5% in test regions, halt expansion.

DECISION: NO-GO
