First call: NO-GO. The unit economics and market trajectory described make success impossible under the proposed terms.

**Steelman:** This is the only viable solution for global business travelers who require connectivity in remote regions where ground cellular cannot reach, justifying a premium price for a first-mover monopoly.

**Attack:**
1.  **Market Obsolescence:** The proposal admits ground cellular is "spreading fast." The target demographic (international business travelers) primarily operates in urban centers and major transport corridors where ground networks are expanding fastest. The niche for "everywhere" coverage shrinks daily, while the competitor's cost basis approaches zero marginal cost for the user.
2.  **Fatal Unit Economics:** A $3,000 handset and multi-dollar-per-minute calls create an insurmountable barrier. With $5 billion in debt, the project requires massive, immediate scale to service interest. The high price caps the Total Addressable Market (TAM) to a tiny fraction of the user base needed to cover debt payments. You cannot scale a luxury niche fast enough to service billions in debt.
3.  **Product-Market Fit:** Bulky handsets that fail indoors contradict the primary use cases of business travelers (conferences, offices, hotels). If it doesn't work where the customer spends 90% of their time, the "global" feature is a novelty, not a utility worth $3,000.
4.  **Execution Risk:** Sales channels are "not ready." Launching a complex, expensive hardware ecosystem without established distribution is a recipe for missing the critical mass required to avoid default.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid sinking further capital into a depreciating asset against a rapidly improving competitor.
*   **Pivot to Niche:** Abandon mass business travel; target only specific verticals (maritime, aviation, government) with subsidized hardware, acknowledging the consumer market is lost.
*   **Delay:** Wait for handset miniaturization and cost reduction, though this risks ceding the window entirely to ground networks.

**Blockers:**
*   **Debt vs. Adoption Mismatch:** The stated debt load requires rapid mass adoption, but the price and product limitations restrict adoption to a slow-growing niche. The math does not work.
*   **Competitive Trajectory:** The core value proposition erodes every month ground networks expand.

**Risks:**
*   Indoor signal failure.
*   Channel readiness delays.

The financial structure demands volume the product cannot deliver.

DECISION: NO-GO
