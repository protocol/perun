First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks to "promote widely" based on unverified assumptions about taste, usability, and regulatory acceptance, despite admitting these are significant risks.

**Steelman:** A heated tobacco product offers a cleaner alternative to combustion, potentially retaining smokers who want reduced smoke without quitting nicotine entirely.

**Attack:**
1.  **Taste & Usability:** The proposal admits smokers may dislike the taste and the lighting mechanism. If the core experience (taste/ritual) is inferior to the incumbent (ordinary cigarettes), wide promotion will accelerate rejection, not adoption. A "complicated" ignition process breaks the primary value proposition of convenience.
2.  **Regulatory Trap:** Promoting it as a "cleaner way to smoke" invites immediate regulatory scrutiny on health claims. Without pre-approved evidence, this marketing angle is a legal liability that could halt the launch entirely.
3.  **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** The "very large sum" already spent is irrelevant to the forward-looking decision. Launching a flawed product to recoup development costs compounds the loss with brand damage and inventory write-offs.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves capital. A limited, controlled pilot (not wide promotion) is the only safe path to validate taste and usability before committing to a full launch.

**Pre-mortem:** The product launches widely; smokers reject the taste and complexity; regulators issue cease-and-desist orders on health claims; the company faces massive write-offs and reputational harm.

**Blockers:**
*   **Unproven Core Value:** The proposal states users *may* dislike the taste and method. Launching without resolving this is fatal.
*   **Regulatory Exposure:** Wide promotion of health benefits without explicit regulatory clearance is a blocker.
*   **Complexity:** A product harder to use than the competitor cannot succeed via wide promotion alone.

**Risks:** High development cost (sunk), market rejection, regulatory fines.

**Kill Criteria:** If pilot data shows <20% repeat usage due to taste or complexity, or if regulators reject health claims, the project stops.

The plan fails because it skips the necessary validation of taste, usability, and regulatory standing before "wide promotion." The sunk cost is not a reason to proceed; it is a reason to be more rigorous.

DECISION: NO-GO
