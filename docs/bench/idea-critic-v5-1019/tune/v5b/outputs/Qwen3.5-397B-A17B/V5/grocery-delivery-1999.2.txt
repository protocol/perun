First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits irreversible capital before validating the core business model in a single market.

Steelman: Rapidly building a nationwide moat of automated warehouses secures market share before competitors can react, leveraging cheap capital to dominate a fragmented industry.

Attack: The plan fails because it treats unproven assumptions as facts. Groceries have notoriously thin margins and high spoilage rates; unit economics in one city (June 1999 launch) cannot predict performance across diverse demographics without data. The "30-minute slot" promise requires extreme customer density to be profitable, which does not exist yet. Spending $1 billion on fixed infrastructure (warehouses, trucks) before proving variable costs (pick/pack/delivery) creates massive operating leverage on a negative contribution margin. If the first city fails to reach profitability due to low density or high churn, the company is locked into billions of dollars of useless capacity in a dozen other cities.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve cash, iterate on the single-city model until unit economics are positive.
2. Staged expansion: Build one additional warehouse only after the first meets specific density and margin targets.
3. Asset-light: Lease existing space and use third-party logistics to test new markets before constructing custom facilities.

Pre-mortem: The company runs out of cash within 18 months. The automated warehouses sit half-empty because customer acquisition costs are higher than projected and retention is lower. The fixed cost burden prevents pivoting to a sustainable model.

Evidence: The proposal admits "no operating track record" and "risks raised" regarding margins and density. There is zero evidence that the current model works, let alone that it scales profitably.

Blockers:
1. Irreversible capital commitment ($1B) based on zero validated unit economics.
2. Expansion into new cities before solving density constraints in the pilot city.
3. Fixed infrastructure build-out for a low-margin business with unproven demand elasticity.

Risks: Capital intensity, spoilage, competition, execution failure. These are secondary to the fundamental blocker of scaling a broken or unproven model.

Kill criteria: Do not proceed until the first metropolitan area demonstrates positive unit economics for three consecutive months at current scale.

DECISION: NO-GO
