First call: NO-GO. The proposal combines maximum engineering risk with maximum deployment risk while alienating the core user base that drives traffic.

Steelman: A complete rewrite with a new algorithm and publisher focus, deployed instantly to all users, is the only way to reverse traffic decline and compete with a rival.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retains community trust but likely loses ground to the rival; reversible.
2. Iterative updates: Modify surfacing logic on the existing codebase; lower engineering risk, allows rollback.
3. Staged rollout: Test the new design on a small user segment; validates publisher focus without endangering the whole community.

Fit: Assuming the north star is sustainable traffic growth, alienating the "loyal community" contradicts the asset needed to achieve it.

Pre-mortem: The rewrite takes too long, the new algorithm depresses engagement among existing users, and the "big bang" launch triggers a mass exodus to the rival with no ability to revert. The site dies because the core community felt unheard and the technical debt of a rewrite delayed critical fixes.

Evidence & Calculation: The proposal admits the community values current mechanics and that a full rewrite is a large risk. It offers no data that users want publisher-favored content. The "all at once" move eliminates the safety net.

Blockers:
1. The deployment strategy (move all users at once) explicitly removes the ability to rollback if the "large engineering risk" materializes or the community rejects the changes. This is a fatal flaw in execution logic.
2. The core value proposition (community voting) is being altered to favor publishers without evidence of demand, directly threatening the "loyal community" cited as the site's strength.

Risks: Engineering overrun, community revolt, rival acceleration. These are compounded by the lack of a pilot.

Kill Criteria Check: The risk of losing the core community concerns the core claim (that users will engage). The proposal's own facts support this risk (users value existing mechanics). The commitment (full rewrite, total migration) is too large to stop once seen. This elevates the risk to a blocker.

The plan fails the basic operational test of risk management. A capped, time-boxed pilot with a staged rollout is required before any full commitment.

DECISION: NO-GO
