First call: GO. The strategic necessity of securing a proven hit-making engine outweighs the premium price, given the internal unit's failure and the imminent loss of the partner to a rival.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the studio is the only viable path to instantly restore animation dominance, leveraging their proven creative leadership to fix the conglomerate's broken internal unit, despite the high upfront cost.

**Analysis:**
The "do nothing" option is catastrophic: the partnership ends, the rival gains the asset, and the conglomerate's own unit continues to underperform. The $7.4 billion price tag is steep for one film a year, but you are buying a monopoly on specific creative talent, not just a production slate. The alternative—building a new culture from scratch—has already failed multiple times.

Culture clash and dependence on key leaders are real risks, but the proposal explicitly mitigates the latter by putting those leaders in charge. The shareholder dilution from the all-stock deal is a financial engineering problem, solvable if the acquired asset generates the projected box-office returns. The "one film a year" critique ignores the long-tail revenue of animation hits (merchandising, streaming, parks), which likely justifies the multiple.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the creative leaders leave immediately post-acquisition or if the conglomerate's bureaucracy stifles their output. However, granting them operational control directly addresses this. The cost of delay is losing the asset to a competitor forever; there is no "wait and see" option here.

**Blockers:** None. The high price is a feature of a competitive auction for a unique asset, not a flaw in the logic. The risks are manageable execution challenges, not fundamental deal-breakers. The internal failure rate proves organic growth is not a viable alternative.

**Kill Criteria:** If the creative leadership team resigns within 12 months, or if the first two films under new ownership underperform the studio's historical average by >20%, initiate a strategic review of the unit's structure.

DECISION: GO
