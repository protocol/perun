First call: NO-GO. The proposal contradicts the brand's core identity and customer value proposition without a validated path to overcome them.

**Steelman:** A massive ad spend can temporarily override brand perception, forcing adults to trial a premium burger despite the chain's "kids and value" reputation.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit:** The strategy clashes with the "child-centered image" and "value" north star. Adults currently view the brand as fast/cheap; a premium price creates cognitive dissonance, not aspiration.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves margin. A "better way" would be testing premium items in adult-heavy locations with localized marketing before committing to an industry-record budget.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The campaign launches, adults try the burger once due to ads, find the environment (toys, noise) mismatched with a "premium" experience, and never return. The high price alienates the core value-seeking base. The company burns cash on acquisition with zero retention.
4.  **Evidence Gap:** The proposal admits risks (brand perception, price sensitivity) but offers no evidence that ad spend alone fixes them. It assumes awareness equals demand.
5.  **Blocker:** The plan commits to the "largest advertising and promotional budget the industry has seen" *before* demonstrating demand. This is an irreversible financial commitment based on unproven assumptions about brand elasticity. The stated facts (customers come for kids/value) directly contradict the core claim that they will pay premium prices.

**Kill Criteria Check:** The risk of brand dilution and financial loss is central to the claim. The proposal's own facts support that this failure mode is likely (adults see chain as fast/not premium). The commitment (largest budget ever) is too large to stop once seen. This constitutes a blocker.

**Verdict:** The plan fails the blocker test. It bets the company's liquidity on overturning deep-seated brand perception without a pilot. The correct move is a capped, time-boxed test in specific markets with a clear kill metric (e.g., repeat purchase rate <15% after 3 months) before scaling spend.

DECISION: NO-GO
