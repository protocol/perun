First call: GO. The proposal leverages existing core competencies (music, design) to defend against erosion while flipping the carrier power dynamic via revenue sharing.

**Steelman:** A premium, keyboard-less device integrating a proven music ecosystem and full web browsing can disrupt the market if carrier constraints on design are removed via an exclusive revenue-share deal.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Carrier-led music phones erode the flagship music player business. Status quo is a slow decline.
*   **Alternative (License OS):** Safer, faster reach, but cedes control of the user experience and hardware integration, diluting the brand premium.
*   **Fit:** Aligns with the north star of integrated user experience; "assumed" strategy is that control drives value.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if consumers reject the lack of a physical keyboard or if the single carrier cannot support the premium price point. However, the "no keyboard" risk is mitigated by the superior touchscreen interface potential, and the price is justified by the unique value proposition (music + web + design).
*   **Evidence:** The company's dominance in music players proves demand for high-quality portable media. Carrier frustration with low-margin handset subsidies creates an opening for a revenue-share model.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The lack of phone experience is a risk, not a factual blocker, as the core tech (touch, OS, music) is already owned. The "no third-party apps" limitation is a strategic choice for V1 stability, not a fatal flaw.
*   **Risks:** Market rejection of virtual keyboard; limited initial reach due to exclusivity; execution risk in a new category. These are managed by the premium positioning (targeting early adopters) and the exclusivity deal (securing carrier marketing spend).

**Kill Criteria:** If the carrier refuses the revenue-share model or demands design control, the claim fails. Since the proposal states the carrier *agrees*, this condition is met.

**Decision:** The proposal transforms a defensive need into an offensive platform shift. The risks are inherent to innovation but are outweighed by the cost of inaction and the unique leverage of the music brand. The exclusivity and revenue share are necessary trades to secure the required control.

DECISION: GO
