First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer with thin margins and no developer platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors are already hostile to its capital spending.

Steelman: Leveraging existing spiky infrastructure to create a high-margin revenue stream that smooths demand and diversifies beyond retail.

Options analysis reveals the "do nothing" path preserves capital and focus, whereas this plan introduces massive execution risk. The "cheaper alternative" of optimizing internal usage first is ignored. The fit is poor: the company's north star is retail dominance, not becoming an infrastructure utility. The pre-mortem is clear: the project fails due to reliability gaps that enterprise developers will not tolerate from a retailer, causing brand damage and capital drain. The cost of delay is negligible because the market is explicitly stated as "unproven"; waiting for validation costs nothing but preserves cash.

Evidence check: The proposal admits zero experience in this sector, unproven market demand, and active investor skepticism regarding capital expenditure. These are not assumptions; they are stated facts that directly contradict the feasibility of success.

Blocker Test:
1. **Capability Gap:** The company has no history running a developer platform at scale. This is a stated fact, not a risk.
2. **Market Validation:** The market is unproven. Entering now is speculation, not strategy.
3. **Capital Constraint:** Investors are already questioning heavy spending. This plan requires more.
4. **Strategic Distraction:** Core margins are thin; splitting focus threatens the primary business.

These are not mitigatable risks without a pilot, which the proposal does not include. The plan commits to a full business line launch despite lacking the core competencies, market proof, or financial goodwill to survive early failures. A "go-if" is impossible because the foundational premises (market existence, internal capability) are currently false or unknown.

The strongest attack survived is the theoretical margin upside, but it fails because it relies on assumptions contradicted by the company's current operational reality and investor sentiment. Without a proven market or internal expertise, the probability of catastrophic failure outweighs the speculative reward.

DECISION: NO-GO
