First call: GO. The proposal leverages a structural cost advantage (falling storage prices) to solve the primary user pain point (full inboxes) while creating a unique data asset for ad targeting, turning a commodity into a differentiated product.

**Steelman:** By offering 1,000x the standard storage, we force incumbents into a margin-crushing price war they cannot win without cannibalizing paid tiers, while our ad-revenue model monetizes the increased engagement and data richness that massive storage enables.

**Alternatives:**
1. *Do nothing:* Cede the search-data feedback loop to competitors; email remains a siloed utility.
2. *Paid tier only:* Fails to achieve the network effects and data volume required to train superior ad-matching algorithms.
3. *Standard free tier:* Indistinguishable from incumbents; guarantees failure against entrenched brands.
Only the 1GB proposal breaks the market equilibrium.

**Fit:** Assumes the strategic north star is organizing information to make it universally accessible and useful. Email is the highest-value, most structured personal dataset; indexing it is essential to the mission.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if privacy backlash triggers regulatory bans or mass user abandonment before ad revenue scales. However, the "invitation-only" launch acts as a pressure valve, allowing controlled exposure and narrative shaping before general availability. The cost of delay is high: every month waits for a competitor to realize storage costs have collapsed.

**Evidence & Math:** In 2004, disk costs are plummeting (~$1/GB wholesale). While 1GB/user seems lavish compared to 4MB competitors, the marginal cost is negligible against the lifetime value of a user generating searchable intent data. The "privacy" objection is a feature, not a bug: users trade scanning for infinite free storage and superior utility.

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks (cost, privacy, incumbents) are execution challenges, not factual contradictions. The math works because the unit economics of storage have shifted fundamentally since incumbents set their limits.
**Risks:** Privacy backlash; ad-revenue lag. These become kill criteria: if opt-out requests exceed 5% in the first quarter or CPMs fall below $X, pivot to enterprise licensing.

The proposal correctly identifies a market discontinuity (storage cost collapse) and exploits it to enter a mature market.

DECISION: GO
