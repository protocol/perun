First call: GO. The request comes from the largest customer with a clear economic driver (cost per seat) in a growing market, despite massive capital requirements.

**Steelman:** Building a scalable, convertible wide-body jet secures market dominance against supersonic threats by maximizing current subsonic efficiency for the primary buyer.

**Options:**
1.  **Proposed:** High risk/cost, but captures immediate scale economics and future freight utility.
2.  **Do Nothing:** Cedes the efficiency race to competitors; risks losing the anchor customer.
3.  **Supersonic Only:** Ignores current subsonic demand growth; technology is premature for mass adoption.
The proposal beats "do nothing" because the anchor order validates the immediate need for scale.

**Fit:** Aligns with the vision of dominating commercial airliner capacity. The freight conversion feature hedges against the stated supersonic risk.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the single airline cancels before certification, leaving unmatched capacity and debt. However, the raised cockpit design ensures residual value as a freighter even if passenger demand shifts. This is a one-way door with high reversal costs, but the cost of delay allows competitors to lock in the new standard.

**Evidence:** The proposal rests on the airline's explicit request and growth projections. No internal data contradicts the feasibility of a larger airframe or new engine partnership.

**Blocker Test:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The size of the outlay is a risk, not a factual contradiction. The unproven engine is a development challenge, not a disqualifier. The supersonic threat is speculative and mitigated by the freighter conversion capability.
*   **Risks:** Single customer dependency, capital exhaustion, engine reliability. These are managed by the freighter fallback and the strategic necessity of the move.

The "supersonic replacement" fear is a known industry variable, not a blocker, especially given the subsonic growth trajectory. The freighter conversion is the critical kill criterion safeguard: if passenger demand collapses, the asset retains utility. The scale matches the customer's specific demand to cut costs.

DECISION: GO
