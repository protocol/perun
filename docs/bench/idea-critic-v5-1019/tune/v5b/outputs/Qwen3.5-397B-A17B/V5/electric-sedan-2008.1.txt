First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a capital-intensive strategy with no verified funding path for the factory or battery supply, creating an immediate solvency risk.

Steelman: The company should bet its entire existence on a single electric sedan produced in a purchased legacy factory to dominate the emerging EV market before incumbents react.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Preserve cash, wait for charging infrastructure to mature, avoid bankruptcy risk.
2. Contract manufacturing: Partner with an existing maker to build the car without buying a factory, reducing upfront capital needs.
3. Staged entry: Build a limited prototype run to validate demand before committing to full-scale production and factory acquisition.

Fit: Assuming the goal is market disruption, this approach aligns but ignores the survival constraint. A strategy that bankrupts the company before the first unit sells fails the primary objective.

Pre-mortem: The company buys the factory, exhausts capital on retooling and battery development, fails to secure a reliable battery supply chain, and cannot scale production. Charging scarcity limits customer adoption. Incumbents flood the market with competitive models using their established supply chains. The company runs out of money within 18 months. This is a one-way door; once the factory is bought and the commitment made, retreat is impossible.

Evidence: The proposal admits the company lacks capital for the factory and batteries. It admits charging is scarce. It admits no mass production experience. These are not risks; they are stated facts that directly contradict the feasibility of the plan as written.

Blockers:
1. Capital mismatch: The plan requires far more capital than the company possesses for its two largest cost centers (factory, batteries).
2. Experience gap: No mass production experience combined with a "production only" commitment creates a high probability of execution failure.
3. Infrastructure dependency: Scarce charging stations directly limit the addressable market for a full-size electric sedan in 2008.

Risks: Incumbent response, battery technology immaturity, supply chain establishment.

The proposal asks for an irreversible, total commitment despite admitting it lacks the money, experience, and market conditions to succeed. A cheaper, staged alternative exists that validates the concept without betting the company.

DECISION: NO-GO
