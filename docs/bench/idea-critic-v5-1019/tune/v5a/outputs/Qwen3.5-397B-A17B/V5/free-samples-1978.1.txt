First call: NO-GO. The initial four-city test demonstrated weak market demand; repeating the experiment with a different name and a free sample tactic does not address the fundamental lack of product-market fit or the specific need office workers have for this item.

**Steelman:** Renaming and sampling in a single mid-sized city acts as a low-cost pivot to validate if "awareness" rather than "utility" was the barrier, potentially unlocking national viability if conversion is high.

**Attack:**
1.  **Demand vs. Awareness:** The first test showed "little demand," suggesting a utility problem, not an awareness problem. Free samples generate usage, not necessarily perceived value. If the product solves no problem, sampling only accelerates waste.
2.  **Metric Flaw:** The success criterion is "most recipients say they would buy." Verbal intent from free recipients is a notoriously poor predictor of actual purchase behavior, especially for a product already flagged as unnecessary by the market. This creates a high risk of false positives leading to a costly national failure.
3.  **Cost vs. Insight:** Giving away inventory incurs material and distribution costs without guaranteeing the data is real. A "no" on a survey after using a free item is still cheaper than a national rollout based on a "yes" that doesn't convert to cash.
4.  **Alternatives:** The "do nothing" or "kill project" option preserves capital. A cheaper alternative would be a paid pilot in one city with the new name, forcing real market validation before sampling.

**Blockers:**
*   **False Positive Trap:** The proposed metric (stated intent) cannot reliably distinguish between politeness and actual demand, directly contradicting the goal of validating a national rollout which requires hard sales data.
*   **Ignored Evidence:** The proposal ignores the primary finding of the first test (little demand) by assuming a name change fixes a utility deficit.

**Risks:** Wasting budget on a national rollout based on inflated survey results; depleting inventory on users who will not repurchase.

**Kill Criterion:** If less than 15% of sample recipients make a repeat purchase within 30 days without further prompting, the project stops. However, relying on "say they would buy" as the gate is a structural failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
