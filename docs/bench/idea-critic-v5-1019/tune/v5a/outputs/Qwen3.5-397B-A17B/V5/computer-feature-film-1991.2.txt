First call: GO. The offer solves the company's two hardest problems—capital for unproven tech and access to distribution—while the "toy perspective" naturally limits scope to match current capabilities.

**Steelman:** Partnering allows the company to leverage its unique technical moat to create a new market category (all-CG feature) while offloading financial risk and distribution complexity to an established player, using a story concept that aligns perfectly with their short-form experience.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
The primary fear is execution failure due to rendering costs and inexperience with long-form叙事. However, "doing nothing" guarantees stagnation as larger studios inevitably catch up technologically. The risk of the studio holding the "stronger hand" is real but mitigated by the studio's desperation to hire the lead animator away; they need this specific team more than the team needs them, creating a symbiotic rather than predatory dynamic. **Pre-mortem:** If this fails, it will be because the production timeline ballooned due to rendering bottlenecks, exhausting the budget before completion. This is a two-way door failure (financial loss), not an existential one, as the core IP and tech remain. The cost of delay is total irrelevance; waiting for tech to improve cedes the first-mover advantage to competitors.

**Blockers:** None. The proposal acknowledges risks but offers the only viable path to surmount them. The lack of prior feature experience is a risk, not a blocker, addressed by the partner's production infrastructure.
**Risks:** Rendering overruns, creative interference. These are managed by the specific story choice and the partner's vested interest in success.
