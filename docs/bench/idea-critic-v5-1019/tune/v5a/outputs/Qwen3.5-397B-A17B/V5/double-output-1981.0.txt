First call: NO-GO. Doubling production while sales lag by 60% during a recession with rising interest rates accelerates cash burn without addressing demand.

**Steelman:** The claim asserts that flooding the market with double the inventory will force a breakthrough in sales volume or supply chain efficiency that outweighs the cost of holding unsold assets during an economic downturn.

**Attack:**
1.  **Demand vs. Supply:** The core failure is lack of demand (3,000 sold vs. 7,500 made), not supply shortage. Doubling output ignores the recession and high interest rates that are actively suppressing buyer ability to finance purchases.
2.  **Cash Flow:** Producing cars requires immediate cash for parts and labor. With 4,500 unsold units already tying up capital, doubling production will exhaust limited cash reserves before any additional revenue is realized.
3.  **Channel Capacity:** Dealers cannot finance more stock amidst rising rates. Forcing more inventory onto them risks breaking the distribution network entirely.
4.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves cash longer. Reducing production aligns costs with actual sales, extending the runway. The proposal beats neither alternative on survival probability.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The company has "limited cash." Increasing production increases cash outflow immediately, while the "large and growing" unsold stock proves revenue cannot keep pace. This mathematically guarantees insolvency sooner than the current trajectory.
*   **Fact:** Interest rates are rising. This increases the cost of financing both the manufacturer's operations and the dealers' inventory, directly contradicting the strategy of leveraging debt to build stock.

**Risks:**
*   Total liquidity collapse before the recession ends.
*   Dealer network insolvency due to forced inventory loading.
*   Asset devaluation of unsold cars as the model year ages.

**Kill Criterion:** If cash reserves drop below the cost of one month's reduced production run, the company fails. This proposal accelerates reaching that point.

The proposal attempts to solve a demand-side crisis with a supply-side surge, ignoring the stated constraints of cash, interest rates, and dealer capacity. It creates an immediate, fatal liquidity event.

DECISION: NO-GO
