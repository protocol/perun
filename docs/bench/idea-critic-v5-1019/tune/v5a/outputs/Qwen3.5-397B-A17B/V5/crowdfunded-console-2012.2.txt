First call: NO-GO. The proposal relies on a two-sided market (players and developers) without a mechanism to ignite either side, while facing entrenched incumbents with deep pockets and existing ecosystems.

**Steelman:** A lean team can exploit an open software platform to offer unique, community-driven games that closed consoles ignore, using crowdfunding to validate demand before heavy manufacturing spend.

**Attack:**
1.  **The Chicken-and-Egg Blocker:** The proposal admits developers may not build for a new platform. Without games, players won't back; without backers, developers won't build. Crowdfunding requires a playable prototype or signed exclusive titles to succeed, neither of which is mentioned. Relying on "open platform" appeal is insufficient against established ecosystems (Sony, Microsoft, Nintendo) and the rising mobile tide (iOS/Android in 2012).
2.  **Hardware Reality:** The team acknowledges hardware delays are common but sets a rigid "ship within a year" deadline tied to crowdfunding promises. Missing this deadline due to supply chain issues (common for first-time hardware founders) triggers refund cascades and reputational death before public sales begin.
3.  **Market Timing:** In 2012, the "Ouya" experiment (similar premise) demonstrated that low-cost hardware alone does not compel developer support without a massive installed base or unique technical advantage. The "no marketing budget" constraint ensures the campaign remains invisible amidst noise.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids certain capital loss.
*   **Software First:** Build the open platform as an emulator or PC distribution layer to gather users before committing to hardware.
*   **Accessory/Kit:** Sell a development kit only, not a consumer console, to validate developer interest without mass-manufacturing risk.

**Pre-mortem:** The campaign raises 20% of the goal because the game library looks thin. The team rushes manufacturing to meet the year deadline, shipping buggy hardware. Backers demand refunds; the brand is toxic before public launch.

**Blockers:**
*   No strategy to secure developer commitment prior to the campaign.
*   No contingency for hardware delays despite acknowledging them as a primary risk.
*   Zero marketing budget in a crowded 2012 market makes campaign visibility impossible.

**Risks:** Incumbent price wars, mobile dominance, supply chain failure.

**Kill Criterion:** If a playable prototype with three signed indie exclusives is not ready before the campaign launch, the project cannot validate the core value proposition.

DECISION: NO-GO
