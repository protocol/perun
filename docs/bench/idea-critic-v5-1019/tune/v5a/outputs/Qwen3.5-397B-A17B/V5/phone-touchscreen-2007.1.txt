First call: GO. The proposal attacks the core weakness of current handsets (carrier-controlled, fragmented software, poor media) by leveraging existing dominance in music and design, trading initial reach for control and margin.

**Steelman:** A premium, carrier-subsidized device that functions as a pocket computer and music player, bypassing keyboard conventions via touch, secures revenue share and design autonomy through exclusivity.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
1.  **No Keyboard/Business Market:** Competitors rely on physical keyboards for business users. *Counter:* A full web browser and email on a large touchscreen offer superior utility than cramped keyboards, appealing to a broader consumer base beyond enterprise.
2.  **No App Ecosystem:** Launching without third-party apps limits utility. *Counter:* First-party integration (music, web, email) provides immediate "good enough" value; an ecosystem can be added later once the hardware base is established.
3.  **Exclusivity/Reach:** Limiting to one carrier caps sales. *Counter:* Exclusivity forces the carrier to market heavily and subsidize costs, while revenue sharing offsets lower volume. It prevents carrier bloatware, preserving the user experience.
4.  **No Phone Experience:** The company is new to phones. *Counter:* They dominate the adjacent music player market which competitors are encroaching upon; waiting grants competitors time to solidify their foothold.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:** This aligns with defending the music player franchise and expanding into high-margin computing. Failure would stem from the touchscreen being unusable or the price being too high even with subsidy. However, the "do nothing" option guarantees erosion of the core music business. The cost of delay is ceding the mobile music and internet market to rivals.

**Blockers vs. Risks:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. The facts show a clear path to revenue share and design control, which are critical differentiators.
*   **Risks:** Market rejection of touch-only input; limited initial addressable market due to exclusivity; lack of apps. These are manageable risks given the potential upside and the existential threat to the music player line.

The proposal trades short-term volume for long-term strategic control and margin, directly addressing the threat of commoditization by carriers and competitors.

DECISION: GO
