First call: GO. The proposal targets an unmet need for portable, affordable personal music among youth using existing technology, despite internal skepticism.

**Steelman:** A low-cost, portable cassette player focused exclusively on playback via headphones will unlock a new mass market of young consumers who value personal mobility over recording capabilities or shared listening.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain status quo; cede the emerging portable segment to future competitors; miss the youth demographic.
2.  **Full-featured device:** Include recording/speaker; higher cost excludes the target price point; delays launch.
3.  **Proposed approach:** Strip non-essentials (recording, speaker) to hit the youth price target; high volume required to offset low margin.

**Fit:** Aligns with expanding access to music; assumes youth prioritize portability and privacy over social sharing or recording.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if dealers refuse to stock due to "no recording" bias, or if the "antisocial" perception stalls adoption. However, these are education hurdles, not product flaws. The lack of market research is a risk, but the use of existing parts mitigates technical failure.

**Evidence:** No external data exists yet (marked assumed). The core logic rests on price elasticity: existing units are too expensive/bulky for teens. The large initial run is a financial blocker only if the price cannot be achieved otherwise; the proposal states large runs *are* required to reach the price, creating a binary bet on volume.

**Blockers:** NONE. The objections (dealer doubt, social stigma, lack of research) are risks, not factual contradictions. The "no recording" feature is a deliberate cost/size trade-off, not an oversight. The "antisocial" angle may actually be the unique selling proposition (personal escape).

**Risks:** Dealer resistance; competitor copying; inventory overhang if the youth market does not materialize.

**Kill Criterion:** If pre-orders from key youth-oriented retailers do not cover 40% of the first production run within 60 days of announcement, halt mass production and pivot to a smaller batch.

**Decision:** The plan survives the attack. The risks are manageable commercial challenges, not fundamental flaws. The potential to create a new category outweighs the conservative objections of traditional dealers.

DECISION: GO
