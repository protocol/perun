First call: GO. The proposal converts a "web-only" limitation into a controlled ecosystem, directly addressing the risk of rivals locking in developers while mitigating security and stability concerns through a single review gate.

**Steelman:** A curated native app store creates a defensible moat by offering developers monetization and users superior performance, while the 30% fee and mandatory review fund platform integrity and prevent the fragmentation seen in open rivals.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Retain web apps only. Risk: High. Developers will flock to rivals with native APIs, rendering the hardware a commodity.
2.  **Open sideloading:** Match rival openness. Risk: High. Unvetted code threatens the "polish" and stability the CEO prizes; carrier objections regarding bypassed services will intensify without a central choke point for approval.
3.  **Proposed Store:** Balances openness with control. It appeases carriers by retaining approval power over network-affecting apps and protects the brand via curation.

**Fit:** Moves the north star from "sufficient web access" to "indispensable platform." Without native apps, the device cannot compete on utility.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the review process becomes a bottleneck, stifling innovation, or if the 30% fee drives key developers away. However, given the lack of alternative distribution for native code on this closed hardware, developers have little leverage. The cost of delay is existential; waiting a quarter allows rivals to cement network effects that cannot be undone.

**Evidence:** The proposal acknowledges existing rival ecosystems, proving demand. The concerns (security, stability) are solved by the proposed mechanism (review), not reasons to abort.

**Blockers:** NONE. The concerns listed are operational challenges inherent to the solution, not facts proving failure. The 30% cut is standard for providing distribution and trust.
**Risks:** Review latency, carrier friction. These are managed via the kill criterion: if approval times exceed 7 days or top-tier developers reject the terms within 6 months, the process must be streamlined or fees adjusted.

The plan beats "do nothing" by securing the platform's future and beats "open distribution" by preserving the product's core value proposition of stability.

DECISION: GO
