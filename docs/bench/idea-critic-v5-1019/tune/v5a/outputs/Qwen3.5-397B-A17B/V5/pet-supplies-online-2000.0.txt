First call: NO-GO. The plan burns cash on every sale while betting on infinite capital availability in a volatile market.

**Steelman:** Aggressively buy market share via IPO proceeds to achieve monopoly scale before competitors, accepting short-term losses as an investment in long-term dominance.

**Attack:**
1.  **Unit Economics:** Selling below cost on heavy, low-margin items creates negative gross margins. Shipping costs exacerbate losses. Growth amplifies bleeding; every new customer increases the deficit. Scale does not fix broken unit economics; it accelerates insolvency.
2.  **Capital Reliance:** The strategy requires continuous capital inflow for ads, acquisitions, and infrastructure. If capital markets close (a stated risk), the company cannot fund operations or debt, leading to immediate collapse.
3.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" or optimizing margins preserves runway. A cheaper alternative is targeting niche, high-margin segments rather than a blanket price war. The proposed path must beat "survival," which it fails to do.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The company IPOs, spends heavily on ads and acquisitions, but a market downturn prevents follow-on funding. With negative margins per unit, revenue growth increases total losses. Cash runs out before profitability is reached. Competitors with better margins or deeper pockets survive; this firm goes bankrupt.
5.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits to negative gross margins and high acquisition costs. These are not hypotheses; they are stated facts that contradict the viability of a loss-leading strategy dependent on external funding.

**Blockers:**
*   **Negative Unit Economics:** Selling below cost on heavy items guarantees loss per transaction. No volume solves this without a future price hike that may drive customers away.
*   **Funding Dependency:** The plan fails if capital markets close, a plausible scenario given the risks cited. There is no fallback to profitability.

**Risks:** Competitor retaliation, execution complexity of mergers, warehouse overbuild.

**Kill Criterion:** If cash reserves drop below 6 months of operating expenses without a path to positive gross margin, stop all expansion and advertising immediately.

The plan relies on optimistic market conditions to sustain a fundamentally broken economic model. It fails the basic test of viability under the stated risks.

DECISION: NO-GO
