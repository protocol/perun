First call: NO-GO. The plan to forcibly migrate a cult brand with a "quirky" independent distributor network into a mass-market supermarket channel and corporate advertising model ignores the specific assets driving its value: exclusivity and authenticity.

Steelman: The acquisition leverages the buyer's scale to accelerate distribution and awareness, capturing growth before competitors dominate the tea category.

Attack: The proposal assumes the brand's success is transferable via the buyer's existing playbook. This is a category error. The "cult image" and "independent distributors" are not obstacles to be optimized; they are the product's core differentiators. Mass-market advertising and supermarket saturation typically destroy the scarcity and community loyalty that define premium cult brands. The risk of alienating the existing distributor network is not a side effect; it is an immediate threat to current revenue. Furthermore, entering a space where "large soft-drink companies" are already launching teas suggests a crowded market where being "just another" corporate-owned SKU is a death sentence.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Avoids $1.7B cash outlay and integration risk; preserves capital for organic sports drink defense.
2. Strategic partnership: Secure distribution rights without full ownership or operational overhaul, testing channel fit without brand destruction.
3. Acquire and isolate: Maintain the independent distributor network and marketing voice as a wholly separate subsidiary, resisting the urge to "fix" what works.

Pre-mortem: One year post-acquisition, the brand's sales flatten as core fans reject the "sold out" perception, independent distributors drop the line due to conflicting mass-market terms, and the product becomes indistinguishable from competitor launches on supermarket shelves. The $1.7B is sunk, and the brand equity is erased.

Blockers: The plan explicitly states an intent to change the very distribution and marketing mechanisms that constitute the brand's value proposition. There is no evidence provided that this specific consumer segment responds to mass-market tactics; indeed, the "cult" descriptor implies the opposite. The strategy guarantees the loss of the asset being purchased.

Risks: Brand dilution, distributor revolt, failure to differentiate against incumbents.

DECISION: NO-GO
