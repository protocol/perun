First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits irreversible capital before validating the core business model in a single market.

Steelman: Rapid nationwide warehouse construction creates an unassailable logistics moat, securing market share before competitors can react.

Options:
1. **Proposed Plan:** $1B+ spend, 12+ cities. Cost: Extreme. Risk: Catastrophic if unit economics fail. Reversibility: None (sunk infrastructure).
2. **Do Nothing/Wait:** Operate in current city only. Cost: Low. Risk: Losing first-mover advantage. Reversibility: High.
3. **Staged Expansion:** Validate unit economics in City 1, then expand to 2-3 similar cities. Cost: Moderate. Risk: Slower scale. Reversibility: Moderate.

Fit: While speed aligns with venture growth mandates, it contradicts the fundamental constraint of "no operating track record" and "low-margin" realities.

Pre-mortem: The company runs out of cash within 18 months because customer density in new cities is insufficient to fill the massive automated warehouses, leaving fixed costs unbearable while margins remain negative. The assets are too specialized to repurpose. This is a one-way door; once built, the capital is gone. The cost of delay (waiting for data) is negligible compared to the cost of failure (total insolvency).

Evidence: The proposal explicitly admits "no operating track record" and "risks raised: capital intensity, thin margins." There is zero evidence that the unit economics work in the first city, let alone twelve.

Blockers:
1. **Fatal Premise Mismatch:** The plan requires scaling a model that has not yet proven viable. Expanding before learning unit economics in a low-margin business guarantees magnified losses.
2. **Irreversibility without Validation:** Committing $1B to specialized infrastructure before validating demand or operational efficiency violates basic capital allocation logic. There is no stop condition; the plan is "all-in" immediately.

Risks: Customer density failures, margin compression, execution complexity.

Kill Check: If the company proceeds, the kill criterion is cash runway exhaustion. Since the plan commits full scale before any metric can be read, there is no working stop condition to cap losses. This is a blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
