First call: NO-GO. The proposed $700 price point is materially higher than competitors while the business model prevents the start-up from subsidizing hardware to drive software adoption, a proven industry standard.

**Steelman:** Licensing shifts capital risk to partners, allowing rapid market saturation if manufacturers accept lower margins for long-term royalty streams.

**Attack:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At $700, the console targets a niche luxury segment, contradicting the goal to "set the standard" which requires mass-market volume. Competitors selling at a loss can undercut this price by 40-50%, making the proposed hardware uncompetitive for the core gaming demographic.
2.  **Incentive Misalignment:** Licensees must profit on hardware sales. Unlike integrated rivals who lose money on boxes to recoup via game royalties, these partners face high unit costs and cannot lower prices to stimulate demand without eroding their immediate margin. They lack the incentive to engage in a price war.
3.  **Channel Conflict:** Multiple licensees building similar high-cost hardware will cannibalize each other rather than expanding the total market, diluting marketing efforts against entrenched competitors.
4.  **Adoption Barrier:** High hardware prices drastically reduce the installed base. A smaller installed base makes the platform less attractive to third-party game developers, reducing the software library, which further depresses hardware demand—a death spiral.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids certain failure in a price-sensitive market.
*   **Integrated Model:** Start-up manufactures hardware, sells at a loss, and recoups via software. This matches competitor tactics and controls the user experience.
*   **Single Partner:** License to one major electronics giant to consolidate marketing and absorb initial hardware losses, rather than fragmenting efforts.

**Pre-mortem:** The project fails because the high price limits the installed base to early adopters only. Competitors release cheaper, comparable hardware, securing exclusive game deals. Licensees, unable to lower prices due to profit requirements, halt production. The start-up earns negligible royalties and collapses.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price Disadvantage:** The $700 price is unsupported by a unique value proposition strong enough to overcome established, cheaper competitors.
*   **Margin Structure:** Requiring licensee profit on hardware prevents the aggressive pricing necessary to gain market share against rivals subsidizing hardware.

**Kill Criterion:** If the projected installed base at $700 is less than 50% of the nearest competitor's base within 18 months, the model fails.

The proposal ignores the fundamental economics of the console business: hardware is a loss leader for software. Fragmenting this across profit-seeking licensees at a premium price guarantees market irrelevance.

DECISION: NO-GO
