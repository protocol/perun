First call: GO. The strategic logic of capturing direct customer relationships and lifetime value outweighs the near-term loss of licensing revenue, provided the company can endure the cash burn.

Steelman: By withdrawing content from rivals and pricing aggressively, the company forces market consolidation around its own platform, trading guaranteed licensing income for higher-margin subscription revenue and owned customer data, accepting a multi-year loss as the cost of entry.

Alternatives:
1. Do nothing: Retain licensing fees but cede direct-to-consumer relevance, eventually becoming a marginalized content supplier as rivals build exclusive moats.
2. Hybrid model: License selectively while launching a premium-priced niche service; lower risk but fails to achieve the scale necessary to compete with the market leader.
3. Proposed plan: High cost and execution risk, but the only path to becoming a primary destination rather than a secondary supplier.

Fit: Aligns with the industry shift toward direct-to-consumer models; assumes the brand strength is sufficient to drive migration despite a smaller initial library than the incumbent.

Pre-mortem: Failure occurs if subscriber growth stalls below the break-even point before cash reserves deplete, or if the technology platform suffers outages that churn early adopters. This is a one-way door; once content is pulled from rivals, re-licensing at previous rates may be impossible. The cost of delay is severe: every quarter waited allows the market leader to lock in more exclusive content and user habits.

Evidence: The proposal relies on the assumption that the brand's library is "must-have" enough to drive switches. No external data verifies the specific subscriber targets, but the strategic direction is sound given the 2019 market context.

Blockers: NONE. The risks cited (cash burn, execution, cannibalization) are inherent to the strategy, not factual contradictions. The plan explicitly accepts the operating loss.
Risks: Technology execution failure; slower-than-expected subscriber adoption; deeper-than-expected cannibalization of theatrical windows.

Kill criterion: If subscriber growth falls below 50% of the projected trajectory by the end of Year 2, pause further content withdrawal from rivals to stabilize cash flow. Leading metric: Net subscriber additions per quarter.

DECISION: GO
