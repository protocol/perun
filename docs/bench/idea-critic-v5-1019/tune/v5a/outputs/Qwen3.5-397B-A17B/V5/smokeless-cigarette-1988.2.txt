First call: NO-GO. The proposal assumes smokers will accept a complicated, different-tasting product despite high switching costs, and that regulators will permit broad "cleaner" claims without rigorous proof, both unverified.

Steelman: A heated tobacco product offers reduced smoke and could retain smokers seeking a less offensive alternative if the ritual and taste are close enough to combustion.

Attack:
1.  **Consumer Fit:** Ordinary cigarettes are valued for simplicity and specific sensory feedback. A "complicated" ignition and altered taste directly attack the core utility. If users must learn to light a carbon tip differently, adoption friction is extreme. The proposal admits users "may dislike" these fundamental traits; launching widely before resolving this is reckless.
2.  **Regulatory Trap:** Promoting it "widely as a cleaner way to smoke" invites immediate regulatory scrutiny. Without pre-approved health claims or definitive comparative data, this marketing strategy risks bans or forced withdrawals. The proposal treats regulation as a risk to manage, not a gate to pass first.
3.  **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** The "very large sum" spent is irrelevant to future success. Doubling down via a wide launch to recoup costs increases exposure if the product fails the taste/usability test.

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoids reputational damage from a failed launch and regulatory backlash.
*   **Niche Pilot:** Restrict to a small segment to validate taste/usability before wide promotion.
*   **Re-engineer:** Fix the ignition and taste before any market exposure.

Pre-mortem: The product launches, smokers reject the complex lighting and taste, regulators block "cleaner" ads as unsubstantiated, and the company faces massive write-offs plus brand damage.

Blockers:
*   **Unverified Demand:** The proposal admits smokers may dislike the core experience (taste/lighting). No evidence suggests they won't.
*   **Regulatory Premise:** Marketing as "cleaner" assumes regulatory approval that is not stated as secured.

Kill Criterion: If <20% of test users switch from regular cigarettes due to taste/complexity, stop.

The plan fails because it proposes a *wide launch* and *wide promotion* despite admitting fundamental flaws in user experience and uncertain regulatory standing. The sunk cost does not justify bypassing product-market fit verification.

DECISION: NO-GO
