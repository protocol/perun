First call: NO-GO. The compressed timeline and massive inventory commitment create a catastrophic risk profile that outweighs the seasonal opportunity.

**Steelman:** A single elite developer can leverage a strong IP to rapidly produce a passable game, and flooding the market before Christmas maximizes holiday sales velocity to recoup the high license fee immediately.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capacity Mismatch:** Five weeks is insufficient for a commercial-quality title, regardless of programmer skill. This guarantees a broken or shallow product, damaging the brand and the newly acquired license value.
2.  **Inventory Trap:** Ordering a "very large" run based on a rushed, low-quality product ignores the direct correlation between quality and sell-through. If the game fails (highly probable given the timeline), the company faces massive warehousing costs and write-offs on unsold cartridges.
3.  **Single Point of Failure:** Relying on one programmer creates a critical bottleneck. Illness, technical debt, or scope creep immediately misses the Christmas window, rendering the inventory worthless.
4.  **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** The high license fee pressures the team to rush, but throwing more money (production run) at a flawed execution plan compounds the loss rather than mitigating it.

**Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Preserve capital, though the license fee is lost.
*   **Staged Release:** Build a minimal viable product in five weeks for a limited run to test quality, then scale production in Q1 if successful.
*   **Delay:** Miss Christmas but release a high-quality title in Q1, ensuring better long-term sales and brand health.

**Pre-mortem:** The game launches in December with game-breaking bugs. Retailers return unsold units. The company is left with warehouses full of defective cartridges and a tarnished reputation, having lost both the license fee and the production budget.

**Blockers:**
*   The five-week timeline makes quality assurance impossible, directly contradicting the requirement for a "hit" product to justify the license.
*   Committing to a large production run before verifying product quality is financially reckless.

**Kill Criterion:** If a playable, bug-free build is not verified by an external party within three weeks, production must be capped at a pilot run of 5,000 units.

DECISION: NO-GO
