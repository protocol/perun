First call: NO-GO. Buying a loss-making, leaderless asset hated by its users for $7.5B during an antitrust crackdown is financial and reputational suicide.

**Steelman:** Only acquiring the dominant platform now prevents a free competitor from locking in the next decade of development, justifying the premium as a defensive monopoly play.

**Attack:** The math fails immediately. A $7.5B price tag for a company losing money with no CEO implies betting entirely on future monopoly rents. However, the user base is actively hostile and vocalizing defection to free alternatives. If even 20% of high-value users leave post-announcement due to distrust, the revenue base collapses, making the multiple indefensible. The "run independently" promise rings hollow given the buyer's historical hostility to open source; developers will not wait to be proven right.

**Alternatives:** 
1. *Do nothing:* Avoids immediate $7.5B cash/stock burn and antitrust scrutiny.
2. *Partner/Invest:* Gain influence without assuming total liability for losses or community backlash.
3. *Build/Buy smaller:* Acquire niche tools to chip away at dominance without triggering a regulatory nuclear response.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-deal, regulators freeze the merger citing anti-competitive consolidation. Simultaneously, a mass migration to the free competitor occurs, citing "cultural incompatibility." The stock price drops 15% on dilution and failed integration. The target remains leaderless and loss-making, now a toxic subsidiary.

**Evidence Check:** The proposal admits the target is loss-making, leaderless, and distrusted. These are not risks; they are current facts. The price ($7.5B) contradicts the fundamental state of the asset. No kill criteria or caps are offered to limit exposure if defection accelerates.

**Blockers:** 
1. **Valuation Mismatch:** Paying a growth premium for a loss-making, leaderless entity violates basic capital allocation logic.
2. **Community Revolt:** The core asset (the network) is volatile and explicitly hostile to the buyer; acquisition triggers the very defection it aims to prevent.
3. **Regulatory Wall:** Buying the dominant player in a concentrated market while under antitrust sensitivity is a guaranteed legal blockade.

This is not a calculated risk; it is a violation of the "do no harm" principle for shareholders and the product ecosystem.

DECISION: NO-GO
