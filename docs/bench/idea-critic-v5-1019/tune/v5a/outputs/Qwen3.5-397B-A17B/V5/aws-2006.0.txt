First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer with no developer platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors are already hostile to its capital spending.

Steelman: Leveraging existing spiky infrastructure to create a high-margin revenue stream that smooths demand and diversifies beyond low-margin retail.

Options:
1. Do nothing: Avoids distraction and capital risk; misses potential upside.
2. Internal optimization only: Reduces costs without external reliability demands or market risk.
3. Proposed external cloud: High capex, unproven demand, direct competition with giants, severe execution risk.

Fit: Contradicts the immediate constraint of investor skepticism regarding heavy capital spending. Diverts focus from the core retail business during a critical infrastructure build-out.

Pre-mortem: The project fails because reliability SLAs required by enterprise developers exceed the retailer's operational maturity, causing reputational damage to the core brand. Capital overruns trigger investor revolt. This is a one-way door; exiting means writing off specialized hardware. Cost of delay is low; the market is unproven, so waiting for validation reduces risk.

Evidence: The proposal admits the market is "unproven," the team has "no experience," and investors "question heavy capital spending." These are stated facts, not assumptions.

Blockers:
1. Capital contradiction: The plan requires heavy capital expansion exactly when investors are questioning such spending.
2. Experience gap: No internal capability to run a developer platform at scale, creating an immediate reliability deficit against incumbents.
3. Unproven market: No evidence of demand to justify the risk of entering a competitive field with thin margins.

Risks: Margin dilution, distraction from core retail, brand damage from outages.

Kill check: The plan commits to a full-scale build-out without a pilot to validate the "unproven" market or the team's ability to meet enterprise reliability. There is no capped, time-boxed first step. Committing to full scale before validating demand or operational capability is a blocker.

DECISION: NO-GO
