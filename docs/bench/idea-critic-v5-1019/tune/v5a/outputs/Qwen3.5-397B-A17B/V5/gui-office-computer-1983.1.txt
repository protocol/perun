First call: NO-GO. The stated price ($10,000) is several times higher than leading competitors while offering inferior speed and a barren software ecosystem, making it commercially non-viable as proposed.

**Steelman:** This machine pioneers the graphical interface for business, justifying a premium for future-proofing despite current slowness and limited software.

**Attack & Options:**
1.  **Price vs. Value:** At "several times" the cost of leading PCs, the value proposition collapses. Office workers need productivity; a "slow" machine with "little software" actively reduces it compared to cheaper, faster text-based rivals.
2.  **Internal Cannibalization:** A cheaper internal alternative with a similar interface is in development. Launching the expensive, slow version now guarantees immediate obsolescence and brand damage when the cheaper unit arrives.
3.  **Do Nothing/Wait:** Delaying launch allows the internal cheaper project to mature, avoiding a failed premium launch. The "first mover" advantage is nullified if the product is objectively worse and vastly more expensive than existing options.

**Fit & Pre-mortem:**
The proposal fails the basic economic test: high cost + low performance + low utility = no sales. Assuming it launches, the pre-mortem is immediate: sales teams cannot justify the $10k price tag against faster competitors, early adopters return the "slow" units, and the subsequent release of the internal cheaper model renders this launch a strategic blunder that wastes marketing capital.

**Blockers:**
*   **Price Disparity:** The $10k price point is indefensible given the "slow" performance and "little software" admitted in the proposal.
*   **Internal Alternative:** The existence of a cheaper, similar internal project destroys the urgency and logic of launching this specific high-cost unit now.

**Kill Criteria:**
The plan commits to a full launch without a pilot to test price sensitivity against the admitted performance deficits. There is no stop condition for the obvious risk that no one will buy a slow, expensive computer with no software.

**Decision:**
The proposal asks to launch a product that is slower, has less software, and costs multiples more than the competition, while ignoring an imminent cheaper internal alternative. This is a guaranteed failure.

DECISION: NO-GO
