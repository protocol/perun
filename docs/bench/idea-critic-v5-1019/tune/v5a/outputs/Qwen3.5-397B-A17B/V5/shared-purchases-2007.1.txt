First call: NO-GO. The core mechanism converts private financial behavior into public social content, creating immediate reputational and legal liability that an opt-out cannot mitigate.

**Steelman:** Sharing purchase data drives ad revenue by leveraging social proof, with user consent managed via notifications and decline options.

**Attack:**
1.  **Incentive Mismatch:** Users value privacy for financial transactions; partners value brand safety. Publicly broadcasting sensitive purchases (e.g., medical, adult, debt-related) violates both. An "option to decline" places the burden on the user to protect themselves against a default setting that exposes them, guaranteeing a subset of accidental leaks.
2.  **Irreversibility:** Once private data is broadcast to a social graph, it cannot be un-seen. The "hard to withdraw" risk is a certainty, not a possibility. A notification step does not prevent the initial breach if the user misses it or misunderstands the scope.
3.  **Better Way:** Aggregated, anonymized data for ad targeting achieves the revenue goal without exposing individual user behavior to their peers. This preserves partner trust and user privacy while still monetizing the data.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** The feature launches, a high-profile user's sensitive purchase is mocked in their feed, mainstream media picks up the story as a "privacy scandal," partners demand immediate removal to protect their brands, and regulators launch an investigation. The cost of withdrawal exceeds the initial revenue gain due to churn and legal fees.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fact:** The proposal admits the feature is "hard to withdraw."
*   **Contradiction:** You cannot launch a system known to be irreversible that handles sensitive data with a safety mechanism (opt-out) that relies on user action to prevent harm. The stated risk of "invasion of privacy" is a guaranteed outcome for any user who fails to opt out immediately.
*   **Alternative:** Anonymized aggregation reaches the same upside (ad revenue) without the existential risk.

**Kill Criterion:** If any single sensitive transaction category (health, finance, adult) is exposed to a user's network, the program must terminate immediately. Since the proposal lacks a mechanism to guarantee this never happens on day one, it fails.

DECISION: NO-GO
