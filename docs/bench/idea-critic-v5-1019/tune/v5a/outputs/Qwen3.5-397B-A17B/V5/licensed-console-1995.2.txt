First call: NO-GO. The proposal trades a dominant brand's equity for negligible royalties in a saturated market without controlling marketing or user experience.

**Steelman:** Licensing offers a capital-light entry into consumer gaming, leveraging an existing partner's manufacturing to bypass high upfront costs while generating incremental royalty revenue.

**Attack:**
1.  **Fit & Strategy:** A "small royalty" and delegated marketing directly contradict the need to build consumer brand connection. If consumers do not link the product to the licensor, the strategic goal of brand expansion fails. The proposal admits this risk but offers no mitigation.
2.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" preserves brand integrity. Entering via a low-cost, uncontrolled channel risks diluting the premium perception required to compete with established console makers. A cheaper, safer alternative is a tightly controlled pilot or co-branded accessory where the licensor retains marketing oversight.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** The product launches, sells moderately due to low price, but is perceived as a "cheap toy" rather than a serious computer extension. The licensee cuts marketing spend to protect margins; the licensor earns pennies per unit while suffering long-term brand damage. The door is two-way (exit the license), but the reputational cost is high.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal itself states the risks: dominant competitors, small royalties, weak marketing incentive, and brand disconnection. These are not hypotheticals; they are admitted flaws in the current plan. No data suggests the licensee can overcome market dominance without significant shared investment, which the royalty model precludes.

**Blockers:**
*   **Strategic Contradiction:** The mechanism (passive licensing) cannot achieve the stated implicit goal (brand building/consumer presence) because it cedes control of marketing and user perception.
*   **Economic Misalignment:** Small royalties do not justify the risk of brand dilution against entrenched competitors. The unit economics fail to support the necessary marketing spend to win.

**Risks:** Market rejection, brand dilution, licensee under-performance.

**Kill Criterion:** If the licensee cannot commit to a minimum marketing spend equal to 20% of projected wholesale revenue within 90 days, the deal kills the brand value faster than it builds revenue.

The plan as proposed guarantees low returns and high brand risk. It must be revised to include shared marketing control and higher economic stakes before proceeding.

DECISION: NO-GO
