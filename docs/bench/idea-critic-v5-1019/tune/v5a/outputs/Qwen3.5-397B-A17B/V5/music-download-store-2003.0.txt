First call: GO. The company leverages its dominant hardware footprint to solve a critical ecosystem threat (piracy) that devalues its core product.

**Steelman:** A curated, legal, per-song store integrated with the market-leading player converts pirates to payers by offering superior convenience and reliability compared to fragmented file-sharing, securing the hardware moat.

**Attack & Options:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Piracy erodes the value proposition of owning a dedicated music device; hardware sales eventually collapse.
*   **Subscription Model:** Premature for 2003 broadband limits and consumer psychology; high churn risk.
*   **Free/Ad-Supported:** Impossible without label cooperation; labels hold the content monopoly.
*   **Risks:** Low margins are acceptable if the primary ROI is hardware retention, not software profit. Platform lock-in (own computers only) is a feature, not a bug, to drive device upgrades. Label withdrawal is unlikely as they desperately need a revenue model to replace dying CD sales.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if labels demand unsustainable royalties or if the copy protection frustrates users enough to return to piracy. However, the "light" protection and 99-cent impulse price point directly address these friction points.

**Evidence & Fit:** The "North Star" is selling more portable players. Free sharing cannibalizes the need for a dedicated device. A legal store validates the hardware purchase. The 99-cent price aligns with the psychological "single" cost, unlike album-only purchases.

**Blocker Test:**
*   **Blockers:** NONE. No fact presented proves labels will refuse (they are desperate), nor that margins must be high immediately. The platform restriction aligns with the strategy of hardware dominance.
*   **Risks:** Margin compression, competitor subscriptions. These are managed by treating the store as a loss-leader for hardware sales.

**Kill Criterion:** If major labels refuse licensing within 6 months, or if attach rate (songs per device) remains below 1.0 after one year, pivot to a subscription model or negotiate revenue-sharing hardware bundles.

**Decision:** The proposal directly secures the core business against an existential threat using the company's unique leverage (hardware integration). Risks are manageable; the cost of delay is losing the music ecosystem entirely.

DECISION: GO
