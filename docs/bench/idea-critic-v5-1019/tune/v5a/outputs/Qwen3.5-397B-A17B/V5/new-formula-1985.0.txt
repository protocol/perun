First call: NO-GO. The plan trades a functional product for an untested emotional gamble by removing the incumbent entirely based on taste data that explicitly excluded the consequence of loss.

**Steelman:** Replacing the flagship with a sweeter formula maximizes immediate taste preference scores, assuming flavor is the sole driver of brand loyalty and that removing the original will not trigger a backlash.

**Attack:** The evidence is fundamentally flawed. Blind taste tests measure isolated sensory input, not brand equity. By withholding the fact that the original would be withdrawn, the research failed to measure the value of scarcity, heritage, or consumer autonomy. Flavor preference in a sip does not equal preference for permanent replacement. The proposal ignores the "endowment effect": consumers value what they possess more than what they are offered. Removing the original converts a product choice into a forced migration, inviting resentment rather than adoption.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain share erosion but preserve brand trust.
2.  **Line Extension:** Launch the sweeter formula as a new variant alongside the original. This captures the "sweeter" preference segment without alienating heritage loyalists.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Total replacement. Highest risk, irreversible without significant cost.

**Pre-mortem:** The launch triggers a public outcry framed as corporate arrogance. Loyalists feel betrayed because their choice was removed, not just outperformed. Media narratives shift from "taste" to "loss of heritage." Bottlers face boycotts. The company is forced to reintroduce the old formula, admitting the new one failed, damaging credibility twice.

**Blockers:**
*   **Fatal Research Gap:** The core evidence (taste tests) explicitly excluded the primary variable (removal of the original). The data does not support the specific action proposed (total withdrawal).
*   **No Fallback:** The plan lacks a stop-loss mechanism. If the reaction is negative, there is no immediate path to restore the status quo, guaranteeing prolonged market instability.

**Kill Criterion:** If focus groups told the truth about removal and showed >20% intent to boycott, the plan fails. Since this was not tested, the risk is unquantified and unacceptable.

**Decision:** The plan relies on data that contradicts the proposed action's scope. Taste wins sips; heritage sells cases. Do not remove the original.

DECISION: NO-GO
