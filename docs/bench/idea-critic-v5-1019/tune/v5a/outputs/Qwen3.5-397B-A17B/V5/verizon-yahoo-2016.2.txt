First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to solve a scale deficit by acquiring a second declining asset, creating a larger entity with falling revenue rather than a growing one.

**Steelman:** Combining two distressed media portfolios creates the critical mass needed to negotiate better rates and share technology, halting decline through synergy.

**Attack:**
1.  **Options:** The "do nothing" alternative avoids sinking $4.8B into depreciating assets. A cheaper alternative exists: invest the capital in building native mobile capabilities or acquiring smaller, growing tech firms rather than a legacy portal with repeated management failures. The target is a "falling knife"; catching it does not stop the fall.
2.  **Fit:** The carrier has no history in consumer media. Integrating a second failing culture with a legacy acquisition from last year compounds execution risk. The stated aim is to compete with dominant platforms, but the inputs (declining traffic, falling ad revenue) move in the opposite direction of the required output (scale and growth).
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure in 24 months. Cause: The combined entity fails to arrest revenue decline because the core products are obsolete, not just under-scaled. Integration distractions cause further talent exodus. This is a two-way door only if the asset can be sold quickly; however, buying a declining business at a premium makes exit costly.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits traffic and revenue are falling for years and management is unstable. No evidence suggests this specific combination reverses those trends. The $4.8B price tag for a "declining" business suggests paying for past glory, not future cash flow.
5.  **Blocker:** The core claim is that scale will enable competition. However, scale in a shrinking, mismanaged category is a liability, not an asset. The plan fails as proposed because it buys decline expecting growth without a verified mechanism to reverse the fundamental product obsolescence. The lack of carrier experience in media makes the integration risk a near-certain failure point.

**Kill Criterion:** If combined ad revenue does not grow within 12 months, the thesis is dead. The proposal offers no cap on losses before this metric is known.

Blockers: Paying a premium for declining assets with no proven turnaround mechanism; acquirer lacks domain expertise.
Risks: Cultural clash, continued talent drain, faster-than-expected market shift to mobile-native competitors.

DECISION: NO-GO
