First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to a capital-intensive path without addressing the funding gap for the factory and batteries, which are explicit constraints.

**Steelman:** Committing exclusively to EVs and buying a large factory creates a focused, scalable platform that leverages existing infrastructure to accelerate production, betting on first-mover advantage in a nascent market.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capital Mismatch:** The proposal admits the company lacks funds for the factory and batteries yet recommends proceeding. Buying a "large factory" from established makers contradicts the "young carmaker" status; established makers rarely sell viable large-scale capacity without premium pricing or hidden liabilities.
2.  **Infrastructure Dependency:** With scarce charging stations, demand for a full-size electric sedan in 2008 is artificially capped. Producing only EVs ignores this market reality, risking unsold inventory.
3.  **Execution Risk:** Zero mass production experience combined with a "large factory" purchase creates a high probability of operational failure. A smaller pilot or contract manufacturing would mitigate this; buying a plant amplifies it.
4.  **Competitive Response:** Established makers have deeper pockets to flood the market or lower prices if threatened, squeezing a cash-poor entrant.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Preserve cash, wait for charging infrastructure to mature.
*   *Contract Manufacturing:* Lease capacity instead of buying a factory to reduce upfront capex.
*   *Hybrid/Smaller EV:* Launch a smaller, cheaper vehicle to test the market before committing to full-size sedans.

**Pre-mortem:** The company buys the factory, drains its cash reserves on retooling, cannot secure battery supply contracts due to lack of credit, and faces zero sales due to range anxiety and lack of chargers. Bankruptcy occurs within 18 months.

**Blockers:**
*   **Funding Gap:** The plan explicitly states the company cannot afford the core requirements (factory, batteries) but proposes doing them anyway. This is a mathematical impossibility without an unstated financing miracle.
*   **Market Readiness:** Scarce charging infrastructure makes a "full-size" EV (high battery need) commercially unviable in 2008.

**Risks:** Competitor retaliation, production inexperience, supply chain failure.

**Kill Criterion:** If financing for the full factory purchase and battery inventory is not secured in writing before signing the purchase agreement, the plan must stop. No partial funding allows for a "large factory" strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
