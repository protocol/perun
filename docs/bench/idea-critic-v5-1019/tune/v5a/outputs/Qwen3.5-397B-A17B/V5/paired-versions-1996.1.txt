First call: GO. The mechanic directly solves the core engagement problem (completion) by manufacturing social dependency, turning a single-player limitation into a network effect.

**Steelman:** Releasing two distinct versions forces interpersonal trade to achieve 100% completion, leveraging the existing install base of link cables to create viral growth despite declining hardware sales.

**Attack & Analysis:**
1.  **Alternatives:** "Do nothing" yields a standard RPG with no viral loop. A single version with unlockable creatures removes the social imperative. The two-version approach is the only path to mandatory peer-to-peer interaction.
2.  **Fit:** Aligns with the "collect them all" north star. The aging console is actually an asset here; the installed base of cables is already high, reducing friction for early adopters.
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the version differences are perceived as cosmetic or if the second version launches too late, breaking the trading window. The "price grab" perception is mitigated if the creature rosters are meaningfully distinct and marketed as essential experiences.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal relies on the assumption that players value completion over convenience. This is a known psychological driver in collection mechanics. The risk of needing a second player is the feature, not a bug; it drives software sales (two copies) and hardware accessory utilization.
5.  **Blocker Test:** No factual blocker exists. The publisher's uncertainty is a baseline condition for all new IP, not a specific flaw in this architecture. The "declining popularity" is countered by the strategy's reliance on deep engagement from the remaining dedicated user base rather than mass market reach.
6.  **Kill Criteria:** If pre-orders for the second version do not reach 60% of the first version's volume within the launch window, the trading ecosystem will fragment, and the strategy fails.

**Decision:** The risks are executional (marketing the difference, timing), not structural. The mechanism creates a self-reinforcing sales loop that no other option provides. The potential upside of creating a cultural phenomenon through forced social interaction outweighs the risk of perceived greed, provided the content differentiation is robust.

DECISION: GO
