First call: NO-GO. Buying a shrinking handset division to solve an app-ecosystem deficit contradicts the core dynamic of platform markets: developers follow users, not corporate ownership structures.

**Steelman:** Vertical integration is the only remaining lever to prevent the manufacturer's defection to the rival platform, preserving the installed base long enough to attract developers through guaranteed hardware distribution.

**Attack:**
1.  **Ecosystem Mismatch:** The proposal admits the rival platform dominates apps and developers. History shows that acquiring failing hardware cannot manufacture ecosystem vitality. Developers write for where the users are; they do not follow a corporate merger. The €5.4 billion buys a declining asset, not a solution to the "app gap."
2.  **Signal to Partners:** Acquiring a major manufacturer signals to all other handset makers that the OS vendor is now a direct competitor. This will likely accelerate their exit to the rival platform, shrinking the total addressable market for the OS rather than expanding it.
3.  **Financial Drag:** Integrating a large, struggling workforce with falling sales creates a massive operational distraction and financial drain. Capital spent here is capital not spent on the software experience or developer incentives, which are the actual drivers of platform success.
4.  **Root Cause:** The problem is defined as an "app-ecosystem problem," yet the solution is a hardware purchase. This is a category error. Hardware ownership does not force developers to port apps if the user base remains small and unprofitable for them.

**Blockers:**
*   **Strategic Contradiction:** The plan attempts to fix a software/network-effect problem (lack of apps) with a hardware acquisition, despite admitting the hardware sales are falling. The stated facts confirm the target cannot deliver the required upside (developer interest) because its market share is collapsing.
*   **Partner Alienation:** The move inherently threatens the remaining partner ecosystem, likely causing the very defection it seeks to prevent among other manufacturers.

**Risks:** Integration failure, continued sales decline, accelerated partner exodus.

**Kill Criterion:** If partner OEMs announce platform switching within 6 months of deal closure, the strategy has failed to secure the ecosystem.

The cost of acquiring a dying business to solve a network effect problem outweighs the temporary control gained. The capital is better deployed elsewhere, or the hardware division should be allowed to fail while focusing purely on software differentiation.

DECISION: NO-GO
