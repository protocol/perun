First call: NO-GO. Buying a shrinking handset maker to solve an app-ecosystem deficit contradicts the core dynamic: developers follow users, not ownership structures.

**Steelman:** Acquiring the division secures the primary hardware outlet for the OS, preventing an immediate switch to the rival platform and buying time to fix the app gap through tighter integration.

**Attack:** The proposal treats hardware ownership as a lever for ecosystem growth, but the handset maker's sales are already falling due to the lack of apps. Integrating a large, declining workforce adds massive fixed costs and distraction without addressing the root cause: the rival's network effect. Other handset makers, fearing competition from the OS owner, will likely accelerate their own exit or deepen ties with the rival, shrinking the total addressable market for the OS further. The €5.4 billion cost creates a "burning platform" where failure requires writing off billions, not just pivoting.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do nothing/License only:** Let the handset maker fail or switch, focusing resources on making the OS so good it runs on *other* hardware or waiting for a different partner.
2.  **Strategic Partnership:** Pay for exclusivity or optimization without the burden of manufacturing and headcount.
3.  **The Proposal:** Highest cost, highest risk, solves the wrong problem (supply vs. demand for apps).

**Pre-mortem:** Two years post-acquisition, the combined entity is bloated, the OS share has dropped further as other manufacturers defected, and the app gap remains because user numbers didn't spike. The €5.4 billion is written down.

**Blocker:** The core claim—that hardware ownership fixes an app-ecosystem problem—is unsupported by the stated facts. The market leader wins because of apps; buying a failing hardware vendor does not create apps, it only captures a shrinking slice of existing demand while alienating other hardware partners. The falling sales indicate the hardware is already losing the war; owning the loser does not change the battlefield.

**Kill Criterion:** If smartphone sales do not grow within 12 months post-acquisition, the thesis is broken. However, the upfront risk of alienating other manufacturers and the massive integration overhead constitutes a present blocker to the logic that this *will* work.

DECISION: NO-GO
