First call: NO-GO. The proposal attempts to enter a mature market with a device that compromises the core utility of a phone (ergonomics, power continuity) while failing to match the incumbent's primary advantage (game library) or price point.

Steelman: A hybrid device leverages existing mobile distribution to offer casual gaming at a premium, creating a new category where phone utility subsidizes gaming hardware.

Attack:
1.  **Fundamental Usability Failure:** The requirement to remove the battery to change games is a catastrophic design flaw. It creates friction impossible for a consumer product, effectively killing software sales and user retention.
2.  **Ergonomic Contradiction:** A device "awkward to hold for calls" fails its primary function. Users will not accept a degraded phone experience for secondary gaming features, especially when dedicated alternatives exist.
3.  **Economic Disadvantage:** At $300, the unit is significantly more expensive than the market leader's dedicated console, yet offers fewer games due to lack of publisher commitment. Without a library, the hardware has no value proposition to gamers; without gaming superiority, it has no value proposition to phone buyers over cheaper phones.
4.  **Market Reality:** The "huge game library" of the leader creates a network effect this proposal cannot breach without exclusive titles, which are explicitly noted as "uncommitted."

Alternatives:
*   **Do Nothing:** Avoid sinking R&D into a device that alienates both phone and gamer segments.
*   **Software Only:** Pre-install simple games on standard phones without hardware modifications or card slots.
*   **Dedicated Device:** Build a proper console without phone compromises (though this ignores the "mobile maker" strength).

Pre-mortem: The device launches, reviewers pan the battery-swap mechanic and awkward form factor. Publishers refuse to port games due to low install base. Sales stall at 10% of forecast. Inventory is liquidated at a loss within 18 months.

Blockers:
*   **Design Blocker:** Battery removal for media swapping is functionally unacceptable.
*   **Content Blocker:** No publisher commitment means no software ecosystem at launch.
*   **Price Blocker:** Higher cost than the superior incumbent with no differentiating value.

Risks: Brand damage from releasing a flawed flagship; inventory write-downs.

Kill Criterion: If a functional prototype cannot swap games without power loss or ergonomic failure by the next quarter, the project terminates.

DECISION: NO-GO
