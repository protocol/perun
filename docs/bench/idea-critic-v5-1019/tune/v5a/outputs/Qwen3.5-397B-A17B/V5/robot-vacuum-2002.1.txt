First call: GO. The proposal targets a specific, unmet need (affordable automation) by sacrificing a non-essential feature (perfect mapping) to solve the primary barrier to entry (cost), despite valid execution risks.

**Steelman:** A low-cost, randomized robot creates a new mass market by prioritizing "good enough" cleaning at an accessible price point, bypassing the expensive complexity that stalled B2B interest.

**Attack & Alternatives:**
*   **Do Nothing:** Cedes the emerging category to high-end mapping rivals, limiting robotics to niche luxury status.
*   **Mapping Rivals:** Higher cost, longer development, and unproven consumer demand for perfection.
*   **Fit:** Aligns with creating a new consumer category rather than competing in an existing industrial one.
*   **Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if random cleaning is perceived as "broken" rather than "simple," or if safety incidents erode trust. However, these are risks, not blockers.
*   **Evidence:** The lack of B2B interest confirms the industrial model is flawed, validating the pivot to direct-to-consumer. The "no experience" risk is mitigated by the simplicity of the proposed mechanism (randomized vs. mapping).

**Blockers:** NONE. The stated risks (coverage gaps, perception, inexperience, trust) are uncertainties inherent to innovation, not factual contradictions. The proposal explicitly accepts the trade-off of random movement for lower cost, addressing the coverage concern by design. No evidence suggests the target users *cannot* be found or that the unit economics fail at the proposed price point.

**Risks:** Consumer rejection of random patterns; safety liabilities; supply chain inexperience. These become kill criteria: halt if early adopter return rates exceed 15% due to cleaning performance, or if any safety incident occurs.

**Decision:** The plan survives the attack. The alternative (waiting for perfect mapping or forcing B2B sales) is more likely to fail than a focused, low-cost consumer launch. The risks are manageable via a capped pilot.

DECISION: GO
