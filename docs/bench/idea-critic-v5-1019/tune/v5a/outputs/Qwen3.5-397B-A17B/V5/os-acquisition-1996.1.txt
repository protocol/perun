First call: GO. The company faces existential decay with no internal path to a modern OS; buying a proven, object-oriented core is the only viable shortcut to survival, despite the cost and integration risks.

**Steelman:** Acquiring this specific modern OS provides an immediate technological foundation that internal teams failed to build twice, while repatriating the founder secures critical architectural knowledge and signals a decisive break from past failures to the market.

**Alternatives:**
1.  *Do nothing:* Continued market share erosion leads to irrelevance or bankruptcy.
2.  *License another vendor:* Perpetual royalties, loss of differentiation, and dependence on a competitor's roadmap.
3.  *Internal rebuild:* Already failed twice; time-to-market is too slow given falling shares.

**Fit:** Directly addresses the "dated operating system" bottleneck blocking all future product viability.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if the technical migration alienates the existing developer base or if the founder's return triggers internal cultural collapse. However, the cost of delay (continued share loss) outweighs these execution risks. The "few hundred million" price tag is high relative to the target's current revenue but cheap relative to the acquirer's survival and market cap restoration.

**Evidence & Blockers:**
*   *Blockers:* NONE. The low sales of the target are expected for a niche player and do not negate the code quality. The "difficult history" is a management risk, not a technical blocker, and is mitigated by the "advisor" role rather than immediate full operational control.
*   *Risks:* Migration friction and cultural friction. These are managed via kill criteria (e.g., if developer adoption drops >20% in Q1 post-launch, pivot strategy), not reasons to abort the acquisition.
*   *Kill Criterion:* If the core OS cannot run 80% of legacy apps within 12 months, halt full migration and maintain dual-track support.

The proposal beats "do nothing" and "license" by offering ownership and speed. The risks are real but manageable compared to the certainty of decline without action.

DECISION: GO
