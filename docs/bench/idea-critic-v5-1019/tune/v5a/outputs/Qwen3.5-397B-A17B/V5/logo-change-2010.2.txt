First call: NO-GO. The proposal demands an irreversible, high-blast change with zero validation against its own stated risks.

**Steelman:** A simultaneous, unannounced global rebrand will instantly modernize the brand image, outweighing any transient customer confusion.

**Attack:**
1.  **Alternatives:** The proposal ignores "do nothing" and cheaper, safer alternatives like phased rollouts, A/B testing in select markets, or soft launches. A "big bang" approach is the highest-risk option available.
2.  **Fit:** While "contemporary" is a valid goal, alienating the loyal base (the core revenue source) contradicts the fundamental business need of a "mature retailer."
3.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure: Loyal customers perceive the change as erasing heritage, triggering an immediate social media backlash. Without prior testing, the company cannot distinguish between noise and a genuine exodus until sales drop.
4.  **Evidence:** The proposal admits the new design was *not tested*. It explicitly lists "loyal customers are attached" and "backlash instant" as known risks but offers no mitigation.
5.  **Blocker:** The lack of a pilot or testing phase is a factual blocker. You cannot claim to reduce risk for a mature brand while executing the riskiest possible launch strategy (no announcement, all channels) on untested assets. The stated facts confirm the target users (loyal customers) may reject it, yet the plan proceeds as if this is impossible.
6.  **Kill Criterion:** If the brand loses >5% of repeat customers in Q1 post-launch, the strategy fails. The current plan has no "stop" condition; the rollout is total before any metric can be read.

**Verdict:**
This is a classic "one-way door" decision made with two-way door speed. The absence of a pilot, cap, or metric is not just a gap; it is a refusal to manage the specific risks admitted in the text. A sound operator would demand a limited market test before committing the entire brand identity. As proposed, this is negligence, not strategy.

DECISION: NO-GO
