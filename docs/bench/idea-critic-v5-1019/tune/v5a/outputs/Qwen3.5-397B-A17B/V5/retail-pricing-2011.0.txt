First call: NO-GO. The plan discards the core revenue mechanism (discounts) for a customer base explicitly defined as coupon-trained, without evidence they desire everyday low prices, and commits the entire chain before validating demand.

Steelman: Eliminating coupon clutter and creating branded shops will modernize the brand, attract higher-margin shoppers, and simplify operations, offsetting the loss of discount-driven volume.

Options:
1. **Do nothing:** Retain declining sales but preserve cash flow from the existing loyal base.
2. **Pilot first:** Test the new pricing and shop-in-shop concept in 5–10 stores to measure elasticity and churn before a chain-wide rollout.
3. **Hybrid:** Reduce coupon frequency while introducing branded zones, allowing a gradual transition.
The proposal rejects the pilot (Option 2), demanding an immediate, total switch.

Fit: While modernization aligns with a "north star" of profitability, the strategy assumes the current customer base will adapt rather than leave. Given the demographic is "older" and "coupon-driven," this assumption contradicts the stated facts of their behavior.

Pre-mortem: Six months post-launch, sales collapse because the loyal base stops buying waiting for non-existent coupons, while new customers do not arrive fast enough to fill the void. Reversing the pricing model after a public announcement destroys brand credibility. This is a one-way door; once trust in the pricing model is broken, it cannot be easily restored. Cost of delay: Minimal compared to the risk of total revenue failure.

Evidence: The proposal admits most sales come from discounted items and customers are trained to wait for coupons. No data suggests these customers will accept higher everyday prices.

Blockers:
1. **Contradictory Evidence:** The plan relies on a customer segment defined by price sensitivity to accept a model removing their primary purchase trigger, with no pilot to prove otherwise.
2. **No Stop Condition:** The rollout is "entire chain at once" with no test. If the hypothesis is wrong, the company faces catastrophic loss before any correction is possible. There is no capped, time-boxed first step.

Risks: Customer churn, brand alienation, inventory mismatch for branded shops.

Kill Criterion: If a pilot in 5 stores shows a >15% drop in transaction volume within 60 days without a corresponding increase in margin dollars, the rollout stops. Since no pilot exists, the condition cannot be met, making the current plan unsafe.

DECISION: NO-GO
