First call: NO-GO. The proposal commits to high-volume manufacturing and a premium price point before resolving fundamental regulatory and market-fit uncertainties.

**Steelman:** A breakthrough personal transporter with strong backing can create a new urban mobility category if launched decisively at scale to capture early adopters and establish brand dominance.

**Attack:**
1.  **Regulatory Blocker:** The legal status is "unclear" for sidewalks and roads. Launching 40,000 units invites immediate regulatory crackdowns or bans, stranding inventory and users. You cannot sell a vehicle where it is illegal to operate.
2.  **Market Fit:** At $5,000, the addressable market is tiny. With "unproven" uses beyond institutions and high social conspicuity, consumer demand is speculative. Building factory capacity for 40,000 units assumes demand that hasn't been validated.
3.  **Better Way:** A staged launch is superior. Produce a limited pilot run (e.g., 500 units) for institutional trials and specific geo-fenced urban zones. Use this to clarify regulations, prove utility, and refine the product before committing to mass production. This reduces capital risk and allows price elasticity testing.
4.  **Pre-mortem:** Assume failure. The likely cause is a patchwork of city bans rendering the device unusable in major markets, combined with low consumer adoption due to price and stigma, leaving the company with massive fixed costs and unsold inventory.
5.  **Evidence:** The proposal itself admits the legal status is unclear and consumer use is unproven. These are not risks to be managed post-launch; they are prerequisites for launch.

**Blockers:**
*   **Regulatory Uncertainty:** Selling a device without confirmed legal operating status is a fundamental failure mode.
*   **Unvalidated Demand:** Committing to 40,000 units/year capacity without proven consumer demand or clear use cases contradicts basic operational prudence.

**Risks:** High price sensitivity, social stigma, limited utility.

**Kill Criterion:** If pilot testing in three major cities does not result in clear regulatory approval pathways within 6 months, the project must pivot or stop.

**Decision:** The plan as proposed ignores critical blockers. A "go" requires resolving legality and validating demand before scaling factory capacity.

DECISION: NO-GO
