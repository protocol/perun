First call: GO. The proposal targets an unmet need for portable, affordable personal music among young people, leveraging existing parts to minimize R&D risk despite competitive copying concerns.

**Steelman:** A low-cost, portable cassette player without recording or speakers creates a new "personal music" category for youth, relying on volume sales to offset low margins and deter copycats through brand and speed.

**Options:**
1.  **Do nothing:** Maintain status quo; cede the emerging portable market to competitors who might iterate faster.
2.  **Full-featured portable:** Add recording/speaker; higher cost, higher price, misses the specific "youth/affordable" wedge.
3.  **Proposed (Playback only):** Lowest cost, highest volume potential, creates a new behavior (personal listening).

**Fit:** Aligns with the vision of democratizing high-fidelity sound, shifting from stationary hi-fi to personal mobility.

**Pre-mortem:** Failure occurs if dealers refuse to stock it due to "no recording" bias, or if the "antisocial" stigma prevents adoption. However, the low price point reduces the barrier to trial. The lack of market research is a risk, but the product creates its own market rather than serving an existing one.

**Evidence:** Based on stated facts: existing parts available, large run required for price target. No contradictory data provided.

**Blockers:** NONE. The concerns raised (dealer doubt, antisocial perception, lack of research, copycats) are *risks*, not factual blockers. They describe market education challenges and competitive dynamics, not impossibilities. The requirement for a large production run is a strategic commitment, not a failure condition, provided the price point is achieved.

**Risks:** Dealer resistance, slow initial adoption due to unfamiliar form factor, rapid commoditization by competitors. These are mitigated by the low entry price and the novelty of the experience.

**Kill Criterion:** If pre-orders from key youth-focused retailers do not cover 40% of the first production run within 60 days, halt further manufacturing and pivot to a bundled strategy.

**Decision:** The proposal passes the blocker test. The risks are inherent to category creation but do not invalidate the core logic of low-cost access via existing technology.

DECISION: GO
