First call: NO-GO. A 60% price hike followed immediately by a forced split into two brands, two logins, and two bills treats loyal customers as adversaries, guaranteeing mass churn and reputational destruction.

**Steelman:** Separating a declining legacy asset (DVD) from a growth engine (streaming) allows distinct strategies and prevents the legacy cost structure from dragging down the growth valuation.

**Attack:** The proposal ignores the timing and execution. Implementing a complex operational split (separate sites, accounts, billing) mere months after a massive price increase compounds the friction. Customers perceive this not as strategic clarity, but as a "double dip" designed to extract more money while degrading service utility. The loss of shared ratings and queues destroys the core value proposition of a unified entertainment library. The risk of "customer confusion" is understated; the likely outcome is active hostility and cancellation.

**Alternatives:**
1.  **Do Nothing:** Maintain the combined plan. Risk: Legacy costs obscure streaming growth metrics.
2.  **Soft Separation:** Keep one login/bill but itemize costs internally. Risk: Accounting complexity remains.
3.  **Proposed Plan:** Maximum friction, maximum customer alienation.

**Pre-mortem:** Six months post-launch, subscriber counts plummet 20%, stock price collapses, and the brand becomes a case study in tone-deaf execution. The "separate management" benefit is outweighed by the revenue cliff.

**Evidence:** The proposal admits to a recent 60% price hike. Historical data on subscription services shows that price increases coupled with reduced utility (splitting libraries/logins) trigger exponential churn, not linear attrition.

**Blockers:**
1.  **Compound Friction:** Executing a hard split immediately after a 60% price increase creates an insurmountable barrier to retention. The combined shock guarantees a backlash that threatens the viability of the streaming business itself.
2.  **Value Destruction:** Eliminating cross-service recommendations and unified queues reduces the product's value while increasing its effective price, violating basic retention logic.

**Risks:** Brand erosion, competitor exploitation of the confusion, operational failure in spinning up a new billing stack quickly.

**Kill Criterion:** If customer service contact volume regarding billing confusion exceeds 50% of baseline within week one, the split must be paused. However, given the current trajectory, the failure is structural, not just operational.

DECISION: NO-GO
