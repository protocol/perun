First call: NO-GO. The proposal asks a retailer with no developer platform experience to enter an unproven market against entrenched incumbents while investors already oppose heavy capital spending.

**Steelman:** Leveraging spiky internal infrastructure to create a new revenue stream could amortize fixed costs and diversify beyond low-margin retail, provided the market materializes.

**Attack:**
1.  **Capability Gap:** Running a reliable, multi-tenant developer platform is fundamentally different from internal infrastructure management. The proposal admits zero experience at scale, creating a high probability of reliability failures that would damage the brand before product-market fit is found.
2.  **Capital Contradiction:** Investors are already questioning heavy capex. This plan doubles down on the exact constraint causing friction, risking a collapse in confidence and stock value before any external revenue is proven.
3.  **Unproven Demand:** The market for hourly server rental is explicitly stated as "unproven." Entering a greenfield market against established hardware/software vendors without a validated customer base is speculation, not strategy.
4.  **Distraction:** Core retail margins are thin. Diverting focus to build a complex B2B sales, support, and engineering organization threatens the primary business engine.

**Alternatives:**
*   *Do Nothing:* Avoids capex backlash and distraction; preserves core focus.
*   *Internal Optimization:* Use spiky capacity to improve retail resilience or lower internal costs, avoiding external SLA risks.
*   *Partnership:* License excess capacity to an existing vendor rather than building a competing platform.

**Pre-mortem:** The platform launches, suffers an early outage due to inexperience with multi-tenant isolation, and alienates early adopters. Simultaneously, retail operations degrade due to talent diversion. Investors pull funding citing the doubled-down capex error, forcing a fire sale of the new unit.

**Blocker:** The combination of "unproven market," "no experience," and "investor opposition to capex" creates a fatal mismatch. The plan requires spending more money on the very thing investors are rejecting, to enter a market that doesn't exist yet, without the skills to execute. This fails the basic test of capital allocation discipline.

**Kill Criterion:** If the company cannot secure internal alignment on capex *before* proposing external sales, the business model is dead on arrival.

DECISION: NO-GO
