First call: NO-GO. Launching early with high price, scarce software, and alienated retailers contradicts basic market entry logic for a hardware platform dependent on ecosystem support.

**Steelman:** An early, limited launch secures mindshare and tests the channel before the competitor's September arrival, accepting short-term friction for long-term positioning.

**Attack:**
1.  **Ecosystem Failure:** Consoles live or die by software. Launching with "few games" while developers are unprepared guarantees a barren library at launch. Early adopters buying a $399 device will find nothing to play, generating negative word-of-mouth that will poison the well before the full launch.
2.  **Channel Warfare:** Shipping only to "a few retailers" explicitly invites retaliation from excluded major chains. In 1995, retail shelf space is the primary bottleneck. Alienating key distributors four months before the main competitor arrives hands them leverage to favor the rival or demand punitive terms later.
3.  **Price Disadvantage:** Being $100 more expensive than the rival is a severe handicap. Without a robust game library to justify the premium, the value proposition is nonexistent. The price signal says "luxury," but the product delivery says "incomplete."
4.  **Technical Debt:** If the machine is already "hard to program," rushing developers for an early date exacerbates quality issues, leading to buggy launch titles that further damage the brand's reputation for technical competence.

**Pre-mortem:** By September, the console is labeled "overpriced and empty." Retailers refuse to stock the full launch due to prior slights. Developers miss the real launch window because they were burned by the rushed May deadline. The competitor launches with a full slate of games, lower price, and full retail support, capturing the entire holiday season.

**Blocker:** The combination of an incomplete software library and active retailer alienation creates a self-inflicted wound that cannot be healed before the competitor's September launch. The proposal sacrifices the critical mass needed for success (games + distribution) for a symbolic early start that yields only negative momentum.

**Kill Criterion:** If launch titles score below 70% in press previews or if top-10 retailers refuse carry-over stock by June, the strategy has failed; however, the current plan guarantees these outcomes.

DECISION: NO-GO
